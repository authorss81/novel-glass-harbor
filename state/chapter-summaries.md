### 1. THE TEN DAYS, ONE LINE EACH, AND THEIR COUNTED LENGTHS

**861, MONDAY THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF NOVEMBER, *d* ONE. A rota day and the two hundred and fifth charge, fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of it. A run of fifty days beginning on a Monday is said out loud to carry seven Sundays in it and not eight, and this harbor has a figure for the hundred and fortieth time that wall came down and one for the hundred and forty-first and none at all for an eighth. One question goes into the column of the asked, being what the seventh Sunday of that run is for, and the keeper says out loud that a run of days is a run of days and that he will not give it a number until somebody tells him what the number is for, and nobody in that shed answers. The man of about thirty-two writes ten days out on the back of a board off a broken crate and reads it twice. COUNTED LENGTH 1,031.**

**862, TUESDAY THE TWENTY-NINTH, *d* TWO. The hundred and ninth wage Tuesday, twenty envelopes in a locked chest from about ten until about four, twenty-six lines at one hundred and four marks unmoved, the hundred and tenth week fallen due at the close. A man of about thirty-one of that market says out loud that this is the ninth Tuesday running and that nobody has asked what the ninth is for, and nobody asks him. About four of that nine say out loud that they had stopped counting those Tuesdays in their own heads, and the keeper says he will not add that up in front of people. Nothing whatever is put to anybody in a question. COUNTED LENGTH 894.**

**863, WEDNESDAY THE THIRTIETH, *d* THREE. Line one worked at about eight with the fifth of December on the face of it for the day it settles, being seven hundred and ninety-four pence in five parts with the multiplication printed at every one. Five men out at about eight and in wet at about half past three, the fifth of them nineteen and on his first working week. One question goes into the column of the asked, being what those five men were paid, and the keeper answers it off the line and does not say who hired them, and neither the man who hired them nor the man who set that rate is in that shed. The man of about thirty-two says out loud that a line with a day on it is checked by one man and not by nine. COUNTED LENGTH 1,107.**

**864, THURSDAY THE FIRST OF DECEMBER, *d* FOUR. A rota day and the two hundred and sixth charge at about ten past ten, and the old man of about sixty-eight does ten of those fourteen again. About four of that nine say out loud that there is a figure for the hundredth of those charges and one for the two hundredth and nothing at all for the two hundred and sixth, and the keeper says a cistern is a cistern and that he charges it on the days it comes. A man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place stops at that reeve's bench on his way up the lane and goes on, and the wall behind it has mortar showing on one face of it and there is no day on any page in this harbor on which it began showing and nobody in that lane asked. Nothing whatever is put to anybody in a question. COUNTED LENGTH 962.**

**865, FRIDAY THE SECOND, *d* FIVE. Line one settles at about four at seven hundred and ninety-four pence and the whole of that foot is read after the settlement and not before it, standing at one hundred and thirty-three thousand nine hundred and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and twenty-three thousand three hundred and forty-two pence short. The wooden slate is in the man of about thirty-two's coat and does not come out of it, and about four of that nine do not know it is there and one of that nine knows. While the reading is still going on the man of about thirty-two says out loud, to nobody in particular, that he does not know who puts that wall up, and about four people hear all of it and nobody asks him how long he has been counting it. Two questions go into the column of the asked, being whether that week's coal is the coal of last month and whether a line worked on a Wednesday has ever settled on a Saturday, and neither is answered. COUNTED LENGTH 1,018.**

**866, SATURDAY THE THIRD, *d* SIX. Line two worked at about eight with the eighth of December on the face of it, being one thousand and sixty-eight pence in five parts, and a new glass for the lamp at the head of that mole at two marks out of the yard's own working money and not out of the bag. At about one a man of about twenty-seven of that second lay puts down a length of coil and says out loud that that wall was quicker last year, and about four people agree with him out loud and about four of those four say they had not counted it themselves and are going on believing him. Not one of those four has a figure for last year and not one of them ever had one. One question goes into the column of the asked, being by how much it was quicker last year, and he says out loud that he did not know and did not measure it and was not going to. The man of about thirty-two hears all of it and says nothing. COUNTED LENGTH 877.**

**867, SUNDAY THE FOURTH, *d* SEVEN. THE ONLY WALL FIGURE OF THESE TEN DAYS. That wall came off at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-nine minutes and went back at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-two minutes off and fifty-two back, one hundred and forty-three the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-fifth time, and both ends ran a minute longer than the ends of the last one ran, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it. About four people stand in front of it counting, and one of them is a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place who has now stood in that lane on five Sundays and said nothing on any of the five, and one of them is the man of about thirty-two, who says out loud, to nobody in particular, that it was a minute each end again. A man is in that lane for the half hour it takes him and speaks to none of the four and none of the four speaks to him, and about four people in that lane could not tell this afternoon what he looks like. Three questions go into the column of the asked, being whether that bench is due to be painted, whether that cistern's covers come off at four or at half past four this time of year, and whether the tide turns before that wall goes up or after, and none of the three is answered and none of the three is about that wall. COUNTED LENGTH 1,152.**

**868, MONDAY THE FIFTH, *d* EIGHT. A rota day and the two hundred and seventh charge, and line two settles at about four at one thousand and sixty-eight pence and the foot is read after that settlement, standing at one hundred and thirty-five thousand and fifty pence out and one hundred and twenty-four thousand four hundred and ten pence short. The keeper says out loud that a column gets added up once and in writing and a foot gets read once and out loud, and that this shed has been mixing them up since September. One question goes into the column of the asked, being whether that two hundred and seventh charge is written down anywhere, and he says out loud that it is on a rota and that a rota is a rota and not a page, and about four of that nine say they had not known that. About four of that nine say out loud that a foot read before a settlement and a foot read after one are two different numbers and that nobody in that lane has ever known which one the figures on that wall were counted against, and a man of about thirty-one of that market says out loud that he does not know which of those two they were, and nobody answers him and he does not ask again. COUNTED LENGTH 906.**

**869, TUESDAY THE SIXTH, *d* NINE. The hundred and tenth wage Tuesday, twenty envelopes in a locked chest, the column of twenty-six lines unmoved at one hundred and four marks, being sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence, and the hundred and eleventh week fallen due at the close. About four of that nine ask the keeper how many wage Tuesdays there have been since the wages stopped, and he says out loud that he will add it up once, in writing, and is not going to say the number out loud to anybody this month and is not going to read it at that stone standing up. A man of about thirty-one of that market says out loud that he does not know who counted the figures on that wall and that he had never once asked anybody about it, and about four of that nine say it is nobody's business and one of that nine says it is nobody's job, about himself and about three other people, and nobody asks him which. The man of about thirty-two comes in at about three and stays until about five and says nothing about any of it. ONE QUESTION ON THIS DAY. COUNTED LENGTH 1,013.**

**870, WEDNESDAY THE SEVENTH, *d* TEN. Line three worked at about eight with the twelfth of December on the face of it, being one thousand two hundred pence in five parts, and the keeper does not add it to the foot of that sheet and says out loud why not. At about one a man of about fifty-eight of that market asks, in about nine people and in the ordinary way, whether that wall is a figure on a page anywhere in this harbor, and is told out loud that it is not, and that the man telling him knows every figure on every page in that room and that this one is on none of them. About four of that nine say out loud that a figure nobody wrote down is not a record and one of that nine says out loud that a figure somebody has written down on a piece of wood in his own coat is not a record either, and nobody says which of those two is right. About four of those nine then ask who it is that puts that wall up, and about four of that nine say out loud that they do not know, and the man of about thirty-two says nothing at all to that and nobody asked a second man the same thing. About four people in that shed say out loud that a wall does not get counted and a bench does, and nobody answered that either. AT ABOUT SIX THE KEEPER ADDS UP THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED ONCE, IN WRITING, AT THE BACK OF THAT LEAF, AND IS NOT GOING TO SAY THE NUMBER OUT LOUD TO ANYBODY THIS MONTH AND IS NOT GOING TO READ IT AT THAT STONE STANDING UP. TWO QUESTIONS ON THIS DAY. COUNTED LENGTH 1,086.**
