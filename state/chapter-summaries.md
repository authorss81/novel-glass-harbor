# Chapter Summaries

Thirty finished chapters now exist for Volume 01, Batches 0001–0003. Summaries record what the prose actually did, not what the card planned.

## Format for every completed chapter

Each entry is two to five sentences and records: what the chapter's scene changed, what new canon it established, what it cost, and which thread it closed or opened. Entries appear in chapter order under a volume heading, and the most recent twenty chapters stay available for voice continuity.

## Volume 01, Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10) — complete

### Chapter 1 — Salt in the Second Pump

An ordinary paid pump repair goes wrong in the honest way: Calder refuses a cosmetic patch, grinds the web back and seats a sleeve, finds a second crack he cannot charge for, and eats the difference while the chandler's note goes to eleven weeks into six. A paid chain raise on the mole's inner face goes sideways when the fouling gives all at once, and on the walk back he finds a transparent fragment wedged under a two-fathom drifted storm anchor at eight feet, with four feet of fouled ground line seized into the glass. Chiselling the last shell off it makes the glass answer with a bell note that comes back a hand's width late, felt in the hand as much as the ear. He records the position, tags the drifted anchor with a lead line and a rag, and leaves the iron bedded; Kest is on the mole head at dark with three terms and a morning deadline. Establishes: ordinary work, debt, physical danger of worn gear, the shard as unclaimed glass of Registry interest, the drifted mole anchor as separate municipal iron, and Calder as a line diver. Cost: a cracked pump housing and most of the day's safe diving margin.

### Chapter 2 — The Claim Window

In the reeve's floating office Kest reads out his own written record of the mole work, makes Calder sign the chain's valuation and a pending-claim line, and refuses the paragraph that would let the glass be lifted on his word alone. Mara Venn enters as a Lantern Registry tide conservator, measures the fragment — including a narrow band ground flat along its lower cut face, curved, entered as a measurement with the words *purpose not stated* — and refuses to certify any origin for it, which puts the claim on the record as a claim over an object of undetermined origin and undetermined age. Calder gives her the day sheet, the position mark, and the three names the claim is entered against, because a position with no hour and no wage on it is a story. She reads Calder's rope lay, gives him an eastern district, and asks him twice what he recognized down there; he gives her a family answer and she writes *personal association, stated as unverified, not material to provenance* and tells him she chose to leave it. Reversal: the fragment has no physical origin at all, and a fouled line seized into it is the only lead. Cost: Calder's private motive becomes an official document, and the yard's three working names are now on a Registry page.

### Chapter 3 — Work Before Proof

Nessa puts three conditions on her gauges and labor — log it as ordinary salvage, the position goes on the public wall the day they go out, and Bevin is on the watch and not the bottom — and Calder signs them on the slate. Tobias Wren looks at the fragment and warns against diving for an answer; Alden refuses to discuss the plate note, refuses again when asked about the ferry's survey, and sends Calder out of the yard. Calder reads the fouled line as eastern make; Sera Mott identifies the drifted anchor as the lost ground tackle of the unclaimed shoal coaster *Alder Reach*; and a trip out to the inner edge sights the *Alder Reach*'s frames, a second bedded anchor, and a pale line of something lying on the bottom on a dead straight to the west, plus a repairer's shop mark of eleven years back — the middle of the Gannet decade, in plain reckoning — on the wreck's ironwork, written in a notation Calder recognizes from Oren's own pressure logs and tells the crew is only a shop hand of that decade. Kest writes a work permit naming the inner edge for two dates, inside the harbor line, for a forty-mark bond, and says aloud that it does not reach the wreck's outer marks or the cutter's recorded mooring ground. Cost: the remaining air, the pump time, most of the bond, and one working day of paid work.

### Chapter 4 — What the Anchor Caught

On the first of the two permit dates the crew rigs a doubled bell hose over a cracked one and works the inner edge in the fifty-five minutes between slack water and the set, finding the pale run is not a dropped panel but a transparent keel rib with a continuous keel line and eleven keys running west. The second clamp takes the load, the rib settles two or three inches, a silt curtain takes four minutes and the visibility with it, and Calder pulls the clamp off the outer third and gives up the outer part of the rib. He measures the plate in the dark, frees the bedded shoal iron with a sacrificial line, and comes up with the weathered transparent hull plate off the rib's foot in his other hand as the current makes, one pressure-bell cycle gone and a new clamp lost overboard. The shackle has a bright saw kerf in it. Ends with Mara on the slip asking what came up, what came off, and not what it means. Cost: one of the two paid working days, a pressure-bell cycle, a replacement clamp, and the outer third of the rib.

### Chapter 5 — Older Than the Registry

The second permit date is spent at the bench and earns nothing. Mara works three incompatible repair histories into the plate: a keyed keel seam filled with the good grey compound Calder's own family uses, a patch in iron from a mill that closed two generations before the Accord that kills the short chain Calder wanted, and a Crown-shop bolted flange over a sealed aperture with darker, older glass under the lid. Turning the plate over finds a builder's mark of nine joined lines struck into the underside, predating every Registry mark, describing a thing held together from more than one place rather than owned; Mara refuses to read it as consent, refuses to name a builder, and refuses to let the lid be lifted in a shed. Tobias supplies secondhand memory of the Veyr cutters, Nessa builds the cradle, air limit, and cutoff, and the cutoff word is fixed as **Enough**. Calder's anchor is revealed as Oren's brass rigger's hand bell, and Mara records it as family property with the association *not established*, in writing, in front of him. Batch midpoint: the plate is pre-Dominion civic construction; the shard's own origin stays undetermined. The chapter closes when the seated anchor settles and the shard pulses in Calder's forearm in the shape of Oren's pressure-notation hand.

### Chapter 6 — One Witness, One Angle

Over the Free Keel platform at three fathoms inside the mole, Nessa reads the six conditions aloud in order, Calder goes into the shard, and gets one scene from one position: low, seated on a coil, a sealed case on his right shoulder, one of four men carrying it, the near end because he could not be seen from the rail. A man with a paper reads numbers and a phrase containing *standing empty* twice and turns the paper over while the case goes past him; shouted words arrive from behind and above and come apart in the middle; a gloved hand with a caulking-iron callus on the two middle fingers closes on a fitting on a tagged keel-line and goes out of frame with the fitting before anything is shown to be cut. The frame tears into a doubled, hard-rimmed edge he does not force, and he surfaces when Nessa says *Enough*, with seventy minutes of air left and a rope burn on his forearm that was not there before. Practice trace: the body knows the carry, the low angle, and the tagged line. Ends with him answering Nessa in Oren's call mark and Nessa walking to the far end of the platform to hold the mooring post.

### Chapter 7 — What the Bell Borrowed

Mara tests the hearing and finds Calder turning to the wrong bell: the harbor's tide bell with the loading bell from the echo sitting a third of a second under it, so that he cannot reliably tell which sound belongs to the room. She documents it, forbids any bell entry until two clean daylight looks, and writes the contamination note — a possible seam that must travel a keel-line like a splinter — as a stated limit on handling. Nessa produces the slate of the six conditions in Calder's own writing with her own line added, a slate Mara Venn signed at the platform after Nessa made her sign it out loud, in which the tender's decision stands without discussion while the diver is attached and each of them is right about one half of it; Calder reads it twice and signs it again in the infirmary in front of the witnesses. He is off the water for twenty-four hours on Nessa's measurement and gives Mara every number, the burn, and the hour it started, while withholding the Oren mark for the third time. Cost: diving access for a night and a day, Nessa's explicit shutdown authority, and a written record of three places where he did not tell her something.

### Chapter 8 — The Shard Leaves the Deck

Bevin proposes hiding the shard under a loose floor and Nessa picks up a flat chisel to take four inches off the plate's keyed corner; Calder stops both, because either act would make the crew unable to say a word about the objects ever again. Kest serves a Registry seizure order, states aloud that a reeve cannot seize glass, and reads the seal line: *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*, the first appearance of the honorific, over a ground of transparent mineral of undetermined origin presumed Crown material. The long middle is the four-line surrender, signed without prejudice, and Kest's own clause releasing all claim to both irons including the tagged drifted mole anchor for a proper lift. Mara makes the public provenance duplicate out loud — measurements, hours, the three repair histories with the iron's *ceiling*, the nine-line mark with her note that a mark is not a witness, the fouled line as a material comparison and not a chain of custody, the shard as one object of undetermined origin with its ground band entered as a measurement whose purpose is not stated, and every name that touched either object. Both objects go to the Morrow circuit custody room as two separately entered items under two numbers; the freed shoal iron stays on the yard stack. Ends with Calder and Mara laying the *Vigil*'s public mooring record over the yard's sounding drawing and finding they lie the same way. Cost: both pieces of glass, and the investigation reduced to copies.

### Chapter 9 — The Three-Second Gap

In the circuit copying room Mara duplicates four documents at the office's expense and keeps the custody log. The ferry's public pressure log carries a deliberate three-second gap and a heavier mark after it, which proves only that a hand was elsewhere; the Navy report, Alden's signed harbor salvage report, and the Registry's uncontested log still do not contradict each other on their faces. The survey's ninth page gives number four as a clear space of eight foot four by six foot nine, and the shard's keyed cut edges and its ground band, measured in the circuit copying room from the figures Mara took by hand on Wednesday evening, match a *public* Crown yard specification for mnemonic case plating, so a bellwright can give a case length and breadth as a range — which makes the manifest's *empty* label testable and not yet proved, because nothing has been measured at the site. Three independent bearings and the *Vigil*'s public mooring place the shard's origin ground on the same bearing as the cutter. Calder writes a second, separate account of the entry, headed as unverified from himself, and keeps it apart from the physical findings; he writes one false line into it, that the personal association was confirmed and not disclosed, and does not tell Mara the Oren mark. Alden refuses twice, in sentences that give Calder nothing usable, and warns him not to go into another one. Ends with the *Vigil* taking a berth at the naval pile and Kest's notice nailed to the market wall.

### Chapter 10 — A Bell Out of Time

Kest's limited inspection accepts the chain of labor in writing, offers a twelve-day disputed-claim defense and preserves the public duplicate, and refuses to bargain; he then tells Calder the *Vigil* has lodged a naval salvage claim over the whole shoal and that the permit's two dates are spent, and makes him say aloud that he will not put a boat on the ground. On the mole at half past two Calder hears the delayed keel-line note and, under it, Oren's call answering, and cannot decide whether it is his memory, the glass, or his own aftertaste shaped like what he wants; he keeps the discipline and does not write it down, and instead enters his hearing impairment in the crew's own book where Bevin and Nessa can read it. The *Vigil*'s officer's paper forbids approach within four hundred yards of the outer marks, a naval tender sets floats on the bearing of the pale run, and Calder states his decision aloud in front of the crew: he keeps investigating under naval scrutiny with no glass, no permit, no second entry and a bell he cannot trust, gives Nessa the standing shutdown, and gives Bevin the job of logging anything he calls a voice. The bell answers him out of the bottom of the harbor one last time, Nessa's only answer is to make him say it again in the morning, and Calder adds the standing formula to the crew's book: *Note entered by Marr. Stated to the crew.* — **eight words, and Chapters 10 and 20 both call it "four words" on the page, which is a Batch 0002 miscount left standing rather than rewritten from a fix phase.** Ends on Calder's own written and spoken choice, with the tender's line as the price rather than the closing image.

## Volume 01, Batch 0002 (Chapters 11–20) — complete

### Chapter 11 — The Poor Week
Saturday 15th. Calder seeks ordinary harbor work and is refused twice because a seizure crew is too dear to hire; Sella Brigg of the *Low Lantern* enters a technically good Free Keel shared-tools claim to a third of the freed shoal iron's eventual sale via Tobias's borrowed strop, on the Free Keel charter's rule that a borrowed tool earns a share of what it lifts. Establishes the batch engine: being right about glass made the yard expensive. Cost: no job, iron now encumbered, a third already entered with the harbor clerk.

### Chapter 12 — Weather Work
Sunday 16th gale. Ordinary surge dive inside the line on the *Merrit* for fifteen marks; Calder mishears the working bell for the echo bell, reaches wrong, and Nessa exercises the standing shutdown with work undone. The burn reopens on barnacles, the short hose is reduced to washers, the pump weeps, and the master pays eleven. Cost: four marks short, hose, packing, arm. Hearing loss now operational.

### Chapter 13 — The Name on the Paper
Monday 17th. The harbor clerk refuses a week's ordinary scrubbing countersign for Calder as Alden Marr's son without a Registry mark, offering a higher bond and raised share or a statement against his father's report. Calder refuses the statement and loses the job for lack of eight marks, then tells the two coasters' foremen himself that no crew is coming; Alden separately refuses to stand as a work witness, costing the same job for a non-report reason. Cost: the week's wages. That night four of the *Merrit*'s eleven marks go to the chandler and seven are kept for air; the note stands at **eleven weeks into six**, four marks lighter and no week forgiven.

### Chapter 14 — Kest's Terms
Tuesday 18th. Kest distinguishes claim window (spent), work permit (two dates spent), and one-season charter (distant hearing grant) and offers twenty days further on thirty marks plus an eighth of future share, asking the duplicate be taken down; Calder refuses the takedown and keeps the wall, and Kest does the arithmetic aloud in front of him — seven in the bag, twelve promised, a third of the iron not his to offer. In the same scene Kest orders the harbor's own lift of the drifted mole anchor for **Thursday's slack water** and bars Calder from the mole while it is done (hostile), then hands him a five-mark pump-packing order on the reeve's account (not hostile). Cost: the bond gap named in figures.

### Chapter 15 — The Free Keel
Wednesday 19th net loft. Harl Dace of the *Grey Widow* puts the question to nine crews: will crews stand rescue cover for a yard the Registry has touched. Calder pledges the *Kittiwake*'s November slip tides free and a written promise not to draw other crews onto the naval line, winning inside-the-line rescue cover from every crew in the room, Sella last. Tobias prices the gift on the slip — a hard beach takes the bilge keels, then the garboards, and March means cutting out oakum — and Calder cannot argue with the man who has to mend it. **Batch midpoint turn:** the batch stops being a defense of work and becomes a crew that can be trusted to turn up. Sella holds her third but will not press a forced sale. Cost surviving winter: slip given, cradle deferred to spring, hull wear accepted.

### Chapter 16 — Honest Work
Thursday 20th. The harbor's own buoy comes to the yard after three crews refuse it: a one-day job, slack water to dark, twenty-six marks with cord and fitting found by the harbor, paid on the buoy master's count and the clerk's slip. Bevin, twenty-two, takes the bottom twice under Nessa with Calder on the tender's cleat, comes up on the call rather than on his own readiness, and argues the number at the ladder; the harbor bell rings three clear strokes and Bevin's hand and voice keep Calder from turning to the wrong sound. The crew eats on the punt. Payment counted in a closed room: 8 out in rope and food, 18 split 4 air, 2 chandler, 12 into the tin for the bond, and Bevin reads the air line aloud — a third gone on the 15th, eleven marks bought, and a pot of a hundred marks a third, so there is no margin in it at all. That afternoon the harbor lifts the drifted mole anchor at its own cost while the whole crew watches from the fish dock rail, Calder barred from the wall by Kest's own order, with the *Vigil*'s tender a quarter mile off holding the near end of her line. Gain: air, food, a started bond, and Bevin able to work.

### Chapter 17 — Mara's Certificate
Friday 21st. Mara shows a Registry query on her forty-mark certificate expense, duplicate witnessing, and three *not stated* entries, and states what it actually costs: while it stands every entry she writes goes to a circuit officer to be countersigned, so any certificate she issues takes three weeks instead of one, and she is spending the standing that lets her refuse. Calder asks her to certify the bearing sheet as provenance and write a custody-room line; she refuses both and is right — bearing is direction, not provenance, and a line would spend the same credit twice — offering witness-to-method instead. Refusal stands; forty marks remain owed after food. Cost: deniable institutional friction begun, and it is priced in weeks, not in feeling.

### Chapter 18 — What a Record Serves
Saturday 22nd. The crew takes a tide job off the harbor's work board at first light — a Lowfen lighter with a parted hawser on the inner mud, twenty-four on the board, less the harbor's charter share, three for the harbor's pumped hire, two for rope and food, sixteen in the bag — because an owner in a hurry does not ask what the yard's paper says. Bevin takes the bottom under Nessa and Calder holds the line, and the tender's number goes in the book whatever Bevin thinks he did. Then Bevin's coast-wide printing plan: fifty copies at two marks, a hundred the yard does not have, or ten for twelve with the drawing left out. Mara refuses her name on carried copies — a harbor-wall witness is not a coast-wide warrant — and Calder resets to crew-only copies, paying twelve out of Saturday's wage and **not out of the bond tin**, which Nessa names as the only money in the yard with a date on it. First consent form: a record serves working hands, not a crowd. Cost: twelve marks, four of the bag to air, and the bond money untouched because there was no other money.

### Chapter 19 — The Narrow Extension
Monday 24th sitting. The *Vigil*'s officer puts the yard's connection to the 13-year-old *Gannet* salvage report into the room and asks whether the harbor authority will found an instrument on the figures of that yard; Calder refuses to defend his father's report and answers with the countersigned labor log and the harbor's own paid tender, so Kest takes the labor as proved and writes the officer's question into the record as the officer's question. The bearing sheet is admitted as a drawing only. The clerk puts a value on what the crew holds — twenty marks for the iron at a fair sale, thirteen after Sella's third, and the glass worth nothing to Calder today — and then the bond: **twelve marks out of the tin onto the table**, yard gear held to a clerk's generous eighteen, the iron's sale pledged to the balance, twenty days to the 14th of next month, harbor's eighth off any future raising, four-hundred-yard bar and echo-look rule entered, duplicate stays public. Tobias offers the pump; Nessa refuses and wins the argument. Mara witnesses method. Cost: real share gone before earned, pump and gear in pawn, and a naval note about the yard's name.

### Chapter 20 — The Leash
Tuesday 25th. Calder writes the full terms in the crew's book before the crew and reads them aloud with Bevin calling the numbers back, then says the two things the book had been avoiding: the air pot is a third gone and was a third gone on the 15th, because fifteen marks buys fifteen marks against a pot of a hundred a third, so there is no margin left in it; and he gives up the bottom till the burn takes a glove, till spring, unless a sitting lifts it, with Bevin as the bottom man and himself on the tender. He states the money mark by mark — *Merrit* eleven to chandler and air, the buoy's eighteen to air, note, and tin, Saturday's twenty-four to the harbor, the pump, the rope, the printer, and the last four to air — refuses to buy the shoal measurement he wants, puts Mara's forty first after food against her own objection, and beaches the *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle under her. Ends on his written and spoken bill, not a threat: *Note entered by Marr. Stated to the crew.*

## Volume 01, Batch 0003 (Chapters 21–30) — complete

### Chapter 21 — What May Be Measured

Wednesday the 26th. With no boat, no pump, no money and an air pot that must be paid for before a job starts, Calder asks Kest the only question available: what may be done to ground he is not allowed to touch, and Kest answers it as four lists in his own hand — look, read the office's own books, take a bearing from harbor ground, and ask what any person has seen; sound it in a boat inside the line on a booked and pre-paid job; **nothing** approached or sounded from; take an object only under a claim window; tell the reeve first about the mole's toe. The shoal book comes up out of the stern locker in two parts and is a survey of the whole shoal run on a stated course at stated intervals by the same man for forty years, **and it has a dated gap**: the last figure is a spring sounding, then a line in a clerk's hand saying the outer ground was discontinued on the officer's paper of the 14th, then one further line run four days later out of pure professional stubbornness, with one station two fathoms off the flat. Kest will let the figure be read in the room and will not let it be copied, will not name it, and puts the yard's whole bar into four lines Calder must write into his own book. Cost: two bearings taken from bad stations, with the pig iron written into the record, and the plan itself, which Calder reads aloud at four o'clock and which ends *nothing said out loud about what the ground is for until there is a man on this coast who can be asked*. Closes: the question of whether the yard may work at a distance. Opens: why the harbor's own measurements stopped.

### Chapter 22 — The Space That Was Never Emptied

Thursday the 27th. To read the harbor authority's thirteen-year-old file Calder must first file a **written purpose**, which Kest reads into the hearing record with the door shut, which commits the yard publicly to *placed and not scattered* with no measurement behind it, and which the circuit office will later hold in its hand. Kest distinguishes four documents in one warning speech and permits the file to be read in the barge, in daylight, with the clerk, not copied. Inside the *Aster Gannet*'s wreck entry: she came in in pieces over four days; against standing space four, *grating in place, not cleared, contents not removed*, in the survey's own hand, initialled. **Kest is the second witness**: he remembers the reeve of the day being asked and declining to go out to her, admits he was thirty-one and had been two years in this office in the year after the Cut — **fifteen years in it now** — and states the answer nobody had wanted, *there is no figure in any file in this harbor of that space measured as found, and I have looked*. His whole objection is to the sentence in which the yard says it has **no measurement of that assertion**, and that is the sentence the notice of the 31st leaves out. Cost: the purpose on the record, Mara's read of *not removed* as custody language in a harbor's hand, and Tobias Wren's missing offset chisel, which Calder enters in the book as **not chased** with the reason that a man who asks a witness where her steel is stops believing the rest of her. Adds the fourth document and the reason nobody measured. Proves nothing.

### Chapter 23 — The Number at the Ladder

Friday the 28th. No air and no boat, and the yard cannot start a job it cannot pay for, so Calder goes to the board and Bevin Rook has already been there since six, and the job is the harbor's own: fouled ring and shackle at the mole's third and fourth seams and the toe to be looked at where the office's own lead has come up short for two years. **Sella Brigg advances six marks of air against the crew's share on her own slate**, and says aloud that her master should be off that water by ten and that if he is not, her crew's rescue cover is not offered again this winter. **Nessa refuses the inner face** — eight feet of glare against a wall that reflects the swell, no way off the line — and they work the outer face in nine and ten feet with a quarter mile of clear water. Bevin finds a pale bedded seam running out from under the mole's toe and *along* the mole under the rubble, three keys, deeper at the north end; he says numbers and not meanings. Under load Calder cannot stop his head turning and **Nessa tells him what the bell was instead of asking him**, and he does not turn again for the rest of the tide. Calder enters the tender's count, declines the diver's own count, and writes a rule that the boy's own number goes in if he says it aloud. Kest enters the toe and the depth and his own lead's figure and **refuses to enter *pale material*, *bedded*, or *bedded in the mole's foundation***, and states the 400-yard bar and his own reason for it, and moves the sitting to the seventh. First point.

### Chapter 24 — The Chain Comes Tight

Saturday the 29th, southwest four increasing. Twenty-six marks on the board for the mole's ninth seam, and Sella Brigg states all three conditions before the crew leaves: her master will take the boat out, he should be off by ten, and if he is not then the cover is gone. Nessa gives Bevin twelve minutes against Calder's instinct for more, because a boy who comes up with the answer is worth more than a boy who has to be pulled off a stone. At thirteen minutes the *Low Lantern*'s master holds the chain's lead off the foundation stone, the boat comes up on a sea, the chain comes tight, and **Bevin Rook's left hand is crushed in the bight** — fourth and third — and he comes up nineteen seconds later on the tender's number with his arm held out and not one word of complaint, and Calder is the one who cannot see it from the cleat. Nessa puts the boy in a sling and sends Calder in with the boat. The stove goes in on the way up the slip and is Sella's own cost. The clerk pays **eighteen of twenty-six, for work not completed, obstruction of the authority's own masonry**, and Kest writes what the tide gang saw and not what Calder saw. Sella states the cover is withdrawn and that the boy choosing nine with the hand already caught will be worth more to the yard than the nine marks, and asks for it in the book before it becomes a story. At the top of the make Calder hears the delayed note with nobody holding a pencil and does not write it, and recognises that a record in one man's head which no other man can check is the thing this whole batch has been about.

### Chapter 25 — What a Poor Yard Pays

Sunday the 30th. The bonesetter on the fish dock sets the hand for a mark and the wood across the knuckles so that a hand that healed low would be lost in six weeks, and a surgeon off the north coaster writes the hour, the cause, the man who held the cleat and the man who gave the call for another mark: **two marks out of nine, and the whole of what the law can be made to do for a hurt hand in Morrow Quay, and it does not pay a single week's wages.** Bevin's mother asks three questions, all of them about money, and asks for the original in her apron and to be told the day the defense ends in a room, out loud, by a man in a coat. Nessa reorganizes the yard in front of him: the tender's cleat is hers, Marr holds the pump and the fall, Wren takes the boat, and Rook reads the board and writes the book — for his own good, and she will not argue about it in April. She then makes the yard worse on purpose, in writing, in front of the boy, so nobody can say afterward that they pretended. **Alden Marr re-binds the hand higher, says the cold arithmetic about twenty-four marks of wages and about carrying a boat and a boy at once, notices that his son has asked him nothing, and tells one story about a man off the *Kittiwake* in 1961 whose hand went under a chain and who was back in the water in nine weeks and was not the same man for a year.** He says nothing about the report and nothing about the ferry. Cost: two marks, six weeks of a boy's wages, and a yard with no tender, no pump, no bottom man and no boat.

### Chapter 26 — The Office Reads Her Back

Monday the 31st. Three sheets from the Morrow circuit office, served on the reeve, quoting **Mara Venn's handling note of the 14th word for word in the circuit's own type** — *held separated, no keel-line contact without a written plan, a join travels along the line to the next panel and the next panel's witness is not asked* — and drawing four clauses from it: the mole's toe and inner face from the **third seam to the eleventh** closed to all work and approach by all persons; no transparent mineral into any shed; **no person who handled it in the water inside the harbor on the authority's work without a plan countersigned by a circuit officer**; and the argument, that the yard's own filed purpose asserts a continuous line, that a line of joined construction is not a scatter, and that material which travels is not material a crew that has handled it may approach — with the sentence in which the purpose said **the crew had no measurement of the assertion it was making**, **omitted** — that sentence, and not the purpose's closing one. The note it quotes is hers of **the 14th of last month**, written on a bench about one loose fragment. A one-page request from the *Vigil's* captain comes in on the same boat and asks for nothing, because she has no authority in this harbor, and Calder cannot tell whether it is a courtesy or a hand on the back of Kest's neck. Kest points out the gap in the reasoning — a join travels along a *joined* line, and a line lying in a row on a bottom is not thereby one — and serves the paper anyway, at eleven, in four clauses, naming her, and adds enforcement in his own words that the notice does not contain. Bevin copies it out fair on a yard board and Nessa insists the difference between a copy and a publication be written on it. **The thing that has kept Calder off the water is now the thing used to keep him off the water**, and Mara tells him in the road that she wrote it, that it is correct, that four hours is what it took her to find out she can live with being quoted by her own office, and that she is going to write nothing today because a second thing of hers in a Registry coat is exactly what that office wants.

### Chapter 27 — What the Note Does Not Say

Tuesday the 1st. Calder has the clerk copy the four clauses out exactly, without putting a word in of his own, and Mara reads out the four paragraphs of her second note: it names no water, gives no distance, and says that **a join travels along a joined line and a line of glass lying in a row is not thereby a joined line**, and that handling limits are not a quarantine. The cost is a date. While her query stands the note cannot be countersigned by her, and it will carry the date of the **22nd** — eight days after the defense lapses and fifteen days after the hearing — and the yard has nothing at all before the seventh. **She refuses to witness the three points**, says a witness is not an advocate, and says she will not write *safe* about joined construction either, because she has no instrument that would tell her, which is the true reason and the first reason is that the next paper would be quoted and the one after that would be quoted wrongly. Calder's resentment of the word *seam* is stated and she answers it exactly: she used it because it is correct, he has not used it, and the thing that has gone wrong is not the word but that there is no mechanism in the Dominion for a limit to stay a limit once it has a name on it. Cost: a quarter of her standing, a document eight days too late, a refusal Calder wanted argued. The chapter closes on a fish market boy reading the notice aloud and asking whether it is talking about a chain or a line, and on Bevin Rook telling Calder that a note dated the twenty-second is for the next man, so the next six days are not waiting for her.

### Chapter 28 — Three Points and One Line

Wednesday the 2nd. The shoal book, read in the office with the clerk in the room and not copied. **Station six of the harbor authority's annual course reads fifteen fathoms and two corrected, the wire having been paid out to eighteen fathoms and two, against a firm-clay flat at sixteen three and sixteen four — a foot and an inch, in one figure out of thirty-six on two miles — and the reason it has been invisible for sixty years is that an uncorrected line puts the station within two fathoms of its neighbours and nobody has ever compared a station to its neighbours on a course that is flat, because a man does not doubt a flat.** Kest rules: he will hear a figure out of a book his office wrote, he will not hear a bearing taken toward it, and **no line may be drawn between the three points**, because a line between three points on a chart is a route and a route is a claim about where to put a boat on ground a naval vessel has claimed. The bench arithmetic gives **a mile and two hundred yards from the mole's toe to the shoal's inner edge and a mile and two hundred and ten yards from there to the station, a difference of ten yards**, and Bevin supplies the objection nobody wanted before the hearing does. **Nessa overrules the ambition**: a conclusion can be taken apart in a room in eleven minutes and everything under it goes with it, so the yard writes the numbers and writes *what it is for is not known to this crew, and no person in this harbor has been asked* — and the word *placed* is struck out of the yard's copy of the purpose while left standing in the hearing record, dated, so that both are legible. The book comes up on the **Wednesday the 2nd and not the Thursday the reeve named**, and the chapter says why. Calder takes the bearing from the mole head on the strength of Kest's signed station note of the 28th and nothing else that is written, hears the delayed note again at the top of the make, and does not go back up. The three sheets go on the market wall with no line drawn on them, and the unverified memory slate stays on its own shelf with its one false line, unadded to. Order shown; purpose withheld.

### Chapter 29 — The Panel at the Fifth Seam

Thursday the 3rd, **a fortnight past the equinox**. Tobias goes up the mole for cradle timber that does not exist, and Kest comes with them, and at the third step from the bottom on the **inner face at the fifth seam — ground he served closed on the 31st and has never read once to himself — a plate of glass comes up into the slop at eleven feet at the top of the tide**, bedded into the mole's own foundation stone with three keys and a Crown-shop bolted rim and darker glass inside the rim. **Kest breaches his own served notice to look, says so out loud, enters it in his own book naming the three witnesses who were standing there, and knows exactly where it puts him and does it on purpose.** He then produces a six-clause instrument he has had in his coat since the 31st — one supervised look, one diver, no tools, no lead line, no bell, the authority's tide-gang on the wall, a stated reason entered first, the finding posted on the market wall, dated the 8th — **and it is void on its face**, because clause three of the 31st requires a plan countersigned by a circuit officer, and Kest has no power to countersign, and the yard asks for it from the office that has just argued the ground is dangerous. He says the answer to Calder's request to have his own bottom restored will be yes, and that he does not enjoy having thought about it. Bevin argues for himself as the diver and is refused, and Calder refuses on the record that a twenty-two-year-old would be the only witness for the rest of his life and that the crew's own book is worth nothing the first time it fails to stop its own author. Then the arithmetic of the night: a hired punt, a top of tide at two in the morning, eleven feet of water, a line on the rim. **He writes down that he thought about it and did not go, and gives three reasons, and the second is the real one — the day he marks that panel in his own book, every number after it is a number somebody has to take on trust from him.** Cost: the dive he wants, for the batch and for the term. The panel is not lost, is not hidden, and is not gone; it is there on the next tide and in March, and the yard has written down where it is and has not gone.

### Chapter 30 — The Bill for the Interval

Friday the 4th. Kest's one precise leniency, read out by the clerk twice: on the harbor authority's own chart, at the mole's inner face between the third and the eleventh seams, *ground claimed by the harbor authority, pending examination, no other claim may be entered, the claim is the authority's and is not the Free Keel yard's, and the Free Keel yard has asked for nothing, has been given nothing, and has declined to ask* — **a fence round his own ground, priced in the timber fenders, four sets of moorings and two herring berths that come out of service in March**, and it protects the panel from the *Vigil*, from the circuit office, and from the reeve. The yard writes its first honest balance sheet: nine marks of air bought, twenty-six marks of work in ten days, twenty-one of it gone, nothing on the chandler's note, nothing on the certificate, five marks in the bag, and a list of what it does not have that includes a boat, a pump, a bottom man, a tender, a permit, glass, and any place in the water on the authority's work. Then **ten written terms for the sitting on the seventh**, sealed against addition after the sixth, of which the two that matter are that the crew's ability to work be restored without asking for clause three to be withdrawn — the yard is not asking for the clause lifted, it is asking to be *heard* saying what the clause is — and that the bottom be restored to the holder of the crew's book, with a stated reason, a tender who is not the diver, and a cutoff, asking for no date and no place. **Bevin finds no term about his own hand, and Calder explains why: a reeve would rather give a man his own grief than argue with him, and then that would be what the hearing was.** The two signatures go on the page together — **ten terms, one of them cut by Nessa, ten going in** — and the page goes into the bench chest with the five rolled copies of the duplicate, which comes back on the 14th whether they can pay or not. At the top of the make the delayed note goes under the harbor's three strokes and Bevin logs it and Calder does not write it. Ends on *Note entered by Marr. Stated to the crew* — eight words, the third time in the volume — and a fish market wall carrying five documents, a tender twenty days on station a quarter of a mile off the mole, the outer marks a mile and a quarter out with nobody's name on them, and the pale run about two and a half miles out on a dead straight, going west-southwest, toward seventy fathoms and one old man's bad advice. **None of the ten days bought a claim. All of it is on the wall, and it will be there in the morning.**
