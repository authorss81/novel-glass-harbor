# Open Threads

**Volume 01, Batch 0001, Chapters 1–10, is written.** Every promise below is marked **PAID** (a chapter settled it), **PART** (a chapter moved it and named it on the page), **CARRIED** (unchanged and still owed), or **OPEN BY DESIGN**. A thread is closed only when a written chapter pays it off. The Batch 0001 block is followed by the standing volume-by-volume and character promises, which are unchanged.

## Opening batch promises (Volume 01, Batch 0001, Chapters 1–10) — as paid

- **PAID — Ordinary work and cost (Chapters 1–3).** Chapter 1 opens on a paid pump repair that Calder refuses to patch, with a second crack he eats, a chandler's note eleven weeks into six, and a fouled chain that takes his shoulder. The mole haul, the anchor hook finding a real edge, and the fouled ground line give the ordinary work its whole weight before any glass is named.
- **PAID — Legitimate claim (Chapters 2–3).** The shard arises from a paid job; Calder cannot buy the claim and does not try to. He submits a labor log, an anchor position mark, and crew names, and Kest grants a **four-day disputed claim window** because Calder's work generated the recovery, not because Kest trusts him. **That window closed when the objects were surrendered.**
- **PAID — Jurisdiction on paper (Chapters 1–4).** Every dive in the batch is inside the harbor line under a named instrument: the claim window, and a separate **work permit** naming the inner edge of Gannet Shoal for **two dates** with a forty-mark bond. Kest says aloud in Chapter 3 that it does not reach the wreck's outer marks, the cutter's recorded mooring ground, or the keel line beneath the shoal. Chapter 4 pays that off: the marker line stops Calder a few fathoms short of the rib's outer end and he turns back. **The permit is spent.**
- **PAID — The first transparent hull (Chapters 3–4).** The pale run on the shoal bottom is established as a vessel-scale transparent hull with a continuous keel line and eleven keys, not a dropped panel. It is **not dated or attributed in Chapter 4**; its age and civic origin are Chapter 5's discovery and belong to the **plate** alone. The plate lifted in the same dive is transparent glass from that structure, not *Gannet* iron and not a *Gannet* artifact.
- **PAID — Two anchors, never fused (Chapters 1, 4, 8, 10).** The drifted mole anchor that held the shard is tagged and left bedded, marked with Kest's own float mark, and released back to the harbor authority for a proper lift. The freed shoal iron is a different piece, is ordinary, and stays on the Free Keel stack. Calder writes the distinction into the record in Chapter 4 (*anchor, shoal, port quarter of the Alder Reach — not the mole iron*) and both irons are named separately wherever either appears. **Neither is glass and neither was affected by the seizure.**
- **PAID — Two objects, two provenances (Chapters 5, 8, 9).** The **nine-line builder's mark is on the plate** (Chapter 5) and the **joinery match to a Crown yard case specification is on the shard's cut edges** (Chapter 9). The public provenance duplicate states both separately, and Chapter 9's second document is a separate, labelled, unverified account of the echo, kept physically apart from the physical findings. The shard is never declared pre-Dominion civic glass.
- **PAID — The two paid working days (Chapters 4–5).** One dive day and one bench day, the bench day being the permit's second date, spent building the cradle, the air limit, and the cutoff while two men were out slinging and the yard earned nothing. Both are on the record in Mara's book and in the crew's book.
- **PART — First echo cost (Chapters 6–10).** All six preconditions are spoken aloud in order, a named tender, an independent line, a fixed air limit, a seated physical anchor, a pullable cutoff, and a written reason. The costs are paid and are still active: rope-burn reenactment, tinnitus with a second bell a third of a second late, hearing confusion that survives into Chapter 10, and a line in the crew's book. **Still owed:** the two clean daylight looks before any further entry, and the twenty-four-hour minimum off the water.
- **PAID — Mara's independence (Chapters 2, 5, 8, 9).** She cannot enter an echo, certify truth, or become an assistant, and she does not. She produces the eastern rope district, refuses to certify a tidy provenance, refuses to read a builder's mark as consent, refuses to let the aperture be opened in a shed, holds the Registry's original custody log while Calder holds only a marked public copy, and writes her own stated reason for the entry. **Her refusal to certify has not yet cost her anything on the page; that cost is still owed and belongs to a later volume.**
- **PAID — Batch loss (Chapter 8).** Both glass objects leave the yard in one witnessed surrender and are entered separately in the Morrow circuit custody room. The crew keeps the freed shoal iron, the measurements, the repair-seam records, the scaled drawing, the anchor-bearing sheet, the names, the countersigned labor log, and the public provenance duplicate. **No glass stays in Calder's hands past Chapter 8, and the batch contains no loophole.**
- **CARRIED — The unaccounted plate note (Volume 4 pay-off).** The repairer's note about a plate cut from the *Gannet*'s own iron plating remains public in the copy of Alden's signed report, and Calder has now read it twice in front of his father. Alden gave one sentence in Chapter 9 — that he saw many things leave the pile in a storm in the dark and could not tell one plate from another — and no more. It is never fused with the transparent plate, and it still closes in Volume 4 with the removed file page.
- **OPEN BY DESIGN — First echo witness.** The *Vigil* rating who carried the case is still unnamed, unlocated, and uninterviewed, and the gloved hand with the caulking callus is still something the witness saw. Calder may believe it was Oren's and may not show it; the prose makes him choose not to write the heel-note down at all. See the deferred decisions list below.
- **PAID — No teacher for the entry (Volume 1 lock).** The six conditions were assembled in the yard out of Tobias Wren's secondhand memory of the Veyr cutters thirty-one years ago, Nessa's engineering, and Mara's paperwork, and the prose says so in Calder's and Nessa's own voices. No master arrived. The entry worked because the conditions were met.
- **PAID — The *Vigil* arrives as paperwork (Chapters 8–10).** A Registry seizure order, a filed naval salvage claim over the whole shoal, a cutter taking a berth at the naval pile, an officer's paper with a four hundred yard limit, and a tender setting floats on the bearing. There was no boarding, no gunfire, no physical seizure, no face-to-face meeting with Holt, and no vessel-to-vessel action of any kind.
- **CARRIED — The compartment measurement (Volume 1 midpoint pay-off).** Chapter 9 put the survey's ninth page, the public case specification, and a case length and breadth given as a **range** in the same room for the first time, laid two bearings over a public mooring line, and read the three-second gap out loud. The contradiction is now available to anyone holding four documents. **It is not proved, and no earlier chapter may treat it as proved.** The measurement at the site belongs to the Volume 1 midpoint, and the ground is under a naval claim.
- **PAID — Earned forward pull (Chapter 10).** The batch ends on **Calder's own spoken decision**, in front of Nessa, Bevin, Tobias, and Mara, after the cutter's order has arrived: he keeps investigating under naval scrutiny with no glass, no permit, no second entry, and a bell he cannot trust, gives Nessa the standing shutdown and Bevin the logbook job, and prefers to be slow and straight about it. The tender's line is the price in the scene; the last beat is his own pencil and his own sentence.
- **CARRIED — Route clue.** A broad west-southwest bearing only. Three independent bearings agreeing inside four degrees, the *Vigil*'s public mooring line, and Orison named once by Tobias at a hundred and thirty fathoms down. **No map, no route, and no purpose.**

## Threads opened by the batch and now owed

These are new. Each names the volume that must close it, per thread hygiene.

1. **The two custody numbers.** The courier wrote them on the back of the second sheet and they are deliberately not quoted in the prose. They may be given digits when a later volume needs them on the page, and must not be invented retroactively. Carries until Volume 4.
2. **The nine-line mark's meaning.** The batch establishes that it describes a thing held together from more than one place and that no office in Veyr can interpret it. The **Nine Harbors Compact as a compact about custody closes in Volume 5**, and until then the mark may not be named as theirs.
3. **The aperture under the Crown flange.** A chamber with something settled in it, darker and older glass, lid never lifted, held under a written handling plan. Volume 1's later dives should return to it; the general question of how a forced or fused record is examined belongs to Volume 3's faultreader work and Volume 6's descent.
4. **The saw kerf in the shoal anchor's crown shackle.** Somebody sawed it nearly through and it was loaded again. Ordinary iron, nobody's claim, no consequence yet. Open until the chain is examined as evidence rather than as tackle; the natural home is the Volume 1–3 wreck-field work.
5. **The repairer's mark of the year eleven on the *Alder Reach*'s inside framing.** Calder has told the crew it is a shop hand of that decade and has told nobody that the notation is written the way Oren's own pressure logs were written. This is a private suspicion with no evidence, it is **not** a *Gannet* fitting, and it must not be resolved by a convenient document. Open across Volume 1 and into Volume 3, where the chain's order becomes legible.
6. **Nessa's condition and the cost of Calder's silence to her.** Mara has three *not stated* entries and has told him in writing that one of them will eventually be the reason his evidence is set aside. This pays in **Volume 4**, when Calder hands her the evidence and the original anchor, and it is the professional spine of the slow burn.
7. **The taper of the hearing.** Two clean daylight looks, then a slow recovery, then Volume 14's temporary loss and Volume 15's permanent damage. The Volume 1–13 stages must not let the tinnitus vanish silently between books; every volume should name it once.

## Character promises

- Alden's original repair log and partial confession remain unrevealed until the middle volumes: the partial confession begins in Volume 9 and the original repair log is handed over publicly in Volume 15. When he confesses, the reader must be able to distinguish his signed harbor salvage report from the custody copy Kest later alters in Volume 9, and the unaccounted plate note must have been paid off by the removed file page in Volume 4 rather than by his confession.
- Oren's stored choices must remain incomplete and occasionally resistant to Calder's preferred interpretation; no resurrection, no obedient final message, and no echo that speaks a command.
- Calder's full Second Bell license is not a Volume 1 payoff. Volume 1 settles the first recovery claim; the Volume 4 inquiry removes his eligibility for the full examination.
- The Mara relationship progresses through professional equality and public accountability. The Volume 12 marriage is a chosen life, not a plot reward.
- Tobias Wren is injured during the Orison investigation and later trains crews from shore. He is not killed to validate the protagonist.
- Ilya Serr's fear must stay credible: he is not defeated by being shown irrational, but by a coast that shares risk through many custodians instead of surrendering authorship.

## Institutional and physical promises

- The bell-buoy core recovered at the end of Volume 1 answers with the same delayed note Calder hears in the continuing keel line at the end of Chapter 10. That single signal is the motif the finale pays: the bellwright's answer becomes a working route signal rather than a relic.
- The three Gannet documents (harbor salvage report, Navy seizure report, Registry custody log) must stay distinct in the reader's mind from the first mention onward.
- A recovered echo can be stable and still be wrong. At least one later volume must show a sincere witness who was deceived, not only a liar.
- Consent marks (private, family, route use, public witness, sealed) are honored as written. Emergency access requires two independent custodians, a recorded reason, limited use, and a restored seal.

## Author-level decisions deferred to a later outline phase

These are known seams in the long plan. They are recorded here so no batch closes them by improvisation, and each must be fixed when the volume that depends on it is planned.

1. **Captain Holt's position after Volume 3.** She loses her ship and command in Volume 3, yet she is the officer whose pressure makes Kest alter the harbor authority's custody copy of the Gannet salvage report in Volume 9. Her posting after her defeat, and the channel by which a disgraced officer reaches a Morrow Quay reeve, are unchosen.
2. **The first echo's witness.** Deliberately unlocated and, after Batch 0001, more so: the prose gives him a body (smallest of four men, near end of the case, could not be seen from the rail) and gives Calder the sound of his own voice saying *I'll take the near end, I'm smallest*, and no name, no face, no rank, and no way to trace him. The volume that first needs him decides whether he is findable and what finding him would cost. He must not be resolved by a convenient anonymous letter or a surviving crew list.
3. **The abandoned Oren lead in Volume 5.** Calder saves a living Lowfen bell archive and gives up a lead on Oren to do it. The lead's content and whether it resurfaces are unchosen.
4. **Lira Venn's volume.** She is canon in the bible and must be used before her later work duplicating public records matters, but the volume that gives her that work is not selected. She may not be invented into a plot after the fact.
5. **The teaching question for later stages.** Bellwright, Keelwright, and Chorus Diver work in Volumes 5, 9, and 11 are team crafts in the plan. The person who teaches each is not fixed, and the Volume 1 lock against a convenient master still applies to all of them.

## Flagged for the controller, not fixable from a writer phase

These are defects in controller-owned files. A writer or reviewer phase must not edit `.opencode/agent/`, `AGENTS.md`, `PHASE_SYSTEM.md`, `scripts/`, or `.github/workflows/`, so they are recorded here for the controller to repair.

- **`AGENTS.md` §Systems and In-World Interfaces, "The System" line.** The writer agent definition at `.opencode/agent/novel-writer.md` instructs the writer to "Use the System naturally. Keep panels rare, readable, and connected to the fictional world." That contradicts `AGENTS.md`, `bible/power-system.md`, `bible/premise.md`, `bible/terminology.md`, `outline/series.md`, and batch hard rule 1, all of which forbid any game-style interface in this world. The writer agent is the process that actually produces the chapters, so it is the highest-leverage place a panel could enter the novel. **Mitigation in force meanwhile:** both `workspace/volume-01/batch-0001/PROMPT.md` and the new `workspace/volume-01/batch-0002/PROMPT.md` state the ban unconditionally and enumerate the permitted world-native substitutes, so neither batch can introduce one even while the agent file stands. **Batch 0001 was written under that mitigation and came out clean:** a mechanical scan of `chapters/volume-01/` for panel and software vocabulary returned nothing, and the only message-like objects in the ten chapters are a lead-line tag and rag float mark, a crew's book entry, a reeve's slate, a fish market notice, Registry book entries, a custody record, a separate written condition sheet, and one spoken cutoff word, none of which tells Calder what to do next. The agent line should be rewritten as a conditional keyed to the approved fiction bible, and the same check is worth making for every novel in the fleet, since the line is shared.
