## Opening batch promises (Volume 01, Batch 0001, Chapters 1–10)

- **Ordinary work and cost:** Chapter 1 must show a paid repair job, debt pressure, and why safe equipment matters before the glass mystery takes over.
- **Legitimate claim:** the shard must arise from physical salvage labor; Calder cannot win the claim by stealing Crown property or by relying on Oren's private connection.
- **Jurisdiction on paper (Volume 1 lock):** every dive in this batch happens inside the harbor line under a harbor-authority instrument. Chapter 2 wins a claim window for the recovery. Chapter 3 buys a **work permit** naming Gannet Shoal's inner edge and two dates, with a bond the yard can barely meet, and Kest states that it does not reach the wreck's outer marks, the cutter's recorded mooring ground, or the keel line beneath the shoal. The permit is a mandatory visible cost under batch hard rule 8, and it is the mechanism that closes the crew out in Chapter 11. **No chapter may put Calder past the imperial line without the instrument that licenses it,** and no chapter may quietly widen a permit past the site and dates it names.
- **The first transparent hull:** Chapter 4 must establish that the shard came from a vessel-scale transparent hull, not a loose panel. The structure is **not dated or attributed in Chapter 4**: its age, its builder's mark, and its civic origin are Chapter 5's discovery, and only the plate carries them. The weathered hull plate lifted in the same dive is transparent civic glass from that structure, not *Gannet* iron and not a *Gannet* artifact.
- **Two anchors, never fused:** the drifted iron at the mole that held the shard and the freed shoal iron lifted in Chapter 4 are two separate pieces of ordinary iron from the same mundane wreck. Name which one in every sentence; never lift the mole anchor in this batch, and never call the shoal iron the iron the shard was found under.
- **Two objects, two provenances:** the nine-line builder's mark belongs to the plate (Chapter 5) and the joinery match to the *Vigil*'s sealed cases belongs to the shard (Chapter 9). The provenance diagram must show them separately, and the shard is never declared pre-Dominion civic glass inside Volume 1.
- **The two paid working days:** Chapter 4 spends a dive day and Chapter 5 spends a bench day in which the crew builds the anchor, cutoff rig, and written reason while earning nothing. Those two days are also the shoal permit's two dates, so the permit runs out spent rather than used. `bible/power-system.md` names the bench day, so the compression into one afternoon is a cost error, not a pacing choice.
- **First echo cost:** the initial tide-ear entry must use all six preconditions, a named tender on the surface, an independent line, a fixed air limit, a seated physical anchor, a cutoff the tender can pull without permission, and a written reason for the entry. Aftertaste and hearing confusion must affect Chapters 6–10.
- **Mara's independence:** she can test glass provenance but cannot enter the echo, certify truth, or become Calder's assistant. Her refusal to certify a tidy story must cost her something later.
- **Batch loss:** Kest holds the original shard and the weathered hull plate for a Registry courier, who takes both to the Morrow circuit office as separately entered items. The crew preserves the freed shoal iron, measurements, repair-seam records, a scaled drawing of the plate, names, and a public provenance duplicate rather than keeping either object through a convenient loophole. No glass stays in Calder's hands past Chapter 8.
- **The unaccounted plate note (Volume 4 pay-off):** the repairer's note about a plate cut from the *Gannet*'s iron plating is public in the copy of Alden's signed harbor salvage report, and Calder has read it. It is a fact about the ferry's hull iron, it must never be fused with the transparent hull plate from Chapter 4, and it cannot authenticate that plate as a *Gannet* artifact. It closes in Volume 4, where the page removed from the Gannet file accounts for the plate that was never accounted for. Alden's own partial confession is a separate thread that begins in Volume 9 and is completed in Volume 15 with the original repair log.
- **First echo witness (open by design):** the first echo is the frame of a *Vigil* rating who helped carry the sealed case, so the corroboration comes from a participant in the seizure. He is never named, located, or interviewed in Batch 0001, and Calder may believe the gloved hand was Oren's without being able to show it. Whether he can be found later, and what finding him would cost, is an author-level decision reserved for the volume that first needs him; it must not be resolved by a convenient anonymous letter or a surviving crew list.
- **No teacher for the entry (Volume 1 lock):** the six tide-ear conditions are assembled in the yard from Tobias's memory, Nessa's engineering, and Mara's paperwork. Any later volume that teaches Calder a further technique must preserve the principle that progress comes from practice, cost, and people, never from a convenient master who arrives to explain the rules.
- **The *Vigil* arrives as paperwork, not violence (Volume 1 lock):** a filed naval salvage claim, a Registry order, and a cutter taking a berth. No boarding, no gunfire, no physical seizure, and no face-to-face meeting with Holt in Batch 0001, so the batch's pressure stays administrative and the escalation into open conflict remains available for later volumes.
- **The compartment measurement (Volume 1 midpoint pay-off):** the *Gannet* survey's figures are public from the opening, but proving the compartment was too large to have been empty needs the sealed case's own dimensions, which sit in Navy-held custody papers. Chapter 9 supplies the case's type and size and makes the contradiction testable; the Volume 1 midpoint supplies the physical measurement that proves it. No earlier chapter or document may treat the compartment as proven.
- **Earned forward pull:** the *Vigil* arrives with Magister Ilya Serr's senior examiner seal, placing Calder's evidence under naval seizure pressure while the shard's old route remains unexplained. The batch must end on **Calder's own costly decision**, spoken aloud to his crew: he keeps investigating with no glass, no permit, and no second entry. The *Vigil*'s order to stay off the wreck and its tightening salvage line are the price of that decision, not the closing beat. `bible/premise.md` and `bible/themes.md` both require the pull to be a decision with a price attached, not a warning or a threat handed to him.
- **Route clue:** the batch may establish a broad physical bearing toward Orison. It may not establish a map, a route, or a purpose.

## Character promises

- Alden's original repair log and partial confession remain unrevealed until the middle volumes: the partial confession begins in Volume 9 and the original repair log is handed over publicly in Volume 15. When he confesses, the reader must be able to distinguish his signed harbor salvage report from the custody copy Kest later alters in Volume 9, and the unaccounted plate note must have been paid off by the removed file page in Volume 4 rather than by his confession.
- Oren's stored choices must remain incomplete and occasionally resistant to Calder's preferred interpretation; no resurrection, no obedient final message, and no echo that speaks a command.
- Calder's full Second Bell license is not a Volume 1 payoff. Volume 1 settles the first recovery claim; the Volume 4 inquiry removes his eligibility for the full examination.
- The Mara relationship progresses through professional equality and public accountability. The Volume 12 marriage is a chosen life, not a plot reward.
- Tobias Wren is injured during the Orison investigation and later trains crews from shore. He is not killed to validate the protagonist.
- Ilya Serr's fear must stay credible: he is not defeated by being shown irrational, but by a coast that shares risk through many custodians instead of surrendering authorship.

## Institutional and physical promises

- The bell-buoy core recovered at the end of Volume 1 answers with the same delayed note Calder hears in the continuing keel line at the end of Chapter 10. That single signal is the motif the finale pays: the bellwright's answer becomes a working route signal rather than a relic.
- The three Gannet documents (harbor salvage report, Navy seizure report, Registry custody log) must stay distinct in the reader's mind from the first mention onward.
- A recovered echo can be stable and still be wrong. At least one later volume must show a sincere witness who was deceived, not only a liar.
- Consent marks (private, family, route use, public witness, sealed) are honored as written. Emergency access requires two independent custodians, a recorded reason, limited use, and a restored seal.
