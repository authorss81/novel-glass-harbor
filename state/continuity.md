## Institutions and jurisdiction

- The **Morrow Quay harbor authority**, working from the reeve's office on the west basin, issues claim windows, work permits, and one-season provisional working charters, and can order a wreck held for inspection. It cannot certify a glass witness or overrule a Registry seizure.
- **Three boundaries, never blurred:** the **harbor line** runs along the mole and the inner shoals and lets harbor crews work any wreck inside it for a share; the **imperial line** is the Crown's outer-shelf boundary, beyond the harbor line, and working past it needs the full Second Bell license; the **one-season provisional working charter** is the harbor authority's wider seasonal permission and is the first instrument that lets a crew work beyond a single claim. In Volume 1, every dive stays inside the harbor line under a claim window or a **work permit** that names a site and dates. No chapter in Volume 1 may put an unlicensed Calder past the imperial line, because the one-season charter is not granted until Movement 5 and the full license is a Volume 4 question. The *Vigil*'s naval salvage claim in Movement 2 bites precisely because it sits over the same shoal and past the line the crew cannot cross.
- The **Lantern Registry** certifies provenance and custody, not truth. Mara works from the **Morrow circuit office**, whose copying room holds the public pressure log and the copies.
- The **Crown Navy** asserts salvage authority at sea. Keep the Registry, the harbor authority, and the Navy distinct in every scene, with distinct documents.
- The **Free Keel** shares tools, rescue plans, and legal risk among Morrow Quay's independent crews. It has no leader and is frequently broke.
- The **Office of Returning Memory** is the ministry; Ilya's seal represents it in Volume 1 through a seizure order, not through his presence.

### The plate's three repair histories, in order

1. A **keyed keel seam** down the long side with a good grey compound of mica, pine resin, and ash. Calder states as his own testimony that this is a **Morrow Quay yard mixture** used by his father, his grandfather, and his great-grandfather, and possible in only four harbors on this coast. It is the only fact in the examination that ties the plate to a *place* rather than a *date*.
2. A **patch in the upper quarter** in iron from a mill that closed its furnace two generations before the Serein Accord. This kills the short chain Calder wanted: the plate was already broken and being repaired long before the Gannet. Mara gave this as a **ceiling, not a date**, and that wording is in the record.
3. A **Crown-shop bolted flange** over a cut aperture in the lower corner, with stamped studs on a jig spacing, and **darker, older glass under the lid** that has not gone the way the rest of the plate has. Mara calls the aperture a **chamber** with something settled in it and refuses to let the lid be lifted in a shed.

### The nine-line mark

The **custody mark of nine joined lines** is on the **underside of the plate**, clear of fouling, standing about two inches proud, cut with a tool rather than a mould, made of harder grey glass. It predates every Registry mark. Mara's reading, which is binding: it is a **builder's mark, not a claim seal, not a cargo seal, and not a mark of consent**; it describes a thing **held together from more than one place** rather than a thing owned; the plate's own construction is **older than the Serein Accord**; she will not name a builder, and she has asked and been told no and believed them. The **Nine Harbors are not named anywhere in the batch and must not be named in Volume 1.**

### The shard's provenance, kept separate

- The **shard's ground band**, a band about a thumb wide ground flat the whole length of its lower cut face, with the worked lines running down into it and stopping dead at its edge, and curved rather than straight because the thing it was cut from was round. Mara measured its width and its curve by hand in **Chapter 2** and entered it as *ground* with *purpose not stated* under it, and it went into the Chapter 8 public duplicate in the same terms. **Chapter 9** identifies it as the flange allowance of a Crown yard case specification. It is a Crown-shop working practice on the **shard** and has nothing to do with the **plate's** bolted flange and sealed aperture; the two must never be described in one clause.
- The shard's **cut edges and flange form match a public Crown yard specification for mnemonic case plating** filed with a refit account for another vessel. This is a *compatibility, not an identification*. Any yard that had the pattern could have cut a case.
- The case's **own dimensions are not known and cannot be known from public papers.** A bellwright can give a **length and breadth as a range within a foot either way**, from the plate thickness and the flange diameter plus the two-hundred-year-old workshop rule about flange allowance. It is recorded as a range, not a number.
- The shard's **origin ground** is placed on the same bearing as the *Vigil*'s recorded mooring, by the three bearings and the public mooring line. The plate's builder's mark tells the crew nothing about the shard, and the shard's cut edges tell the crew nothing about the plate. Every document, drawing, and measurement keeps them as two objects with two provenances.
- The **ferry's compartment is not proved.** The survey's ninth page gives number four as eight feet four by six feet nine clear and eleven feet two deep; the case range is between seven and eight feet in its longest face; the manifest says the hold stood empty. The two numbers are now in the same room for the first time and the contradiction is **available to anybody who holds all four documents**, but nothing has been measured at the site and the site is past the harbor line. **The physical measurement that proves it belongs to the Volume 1 midpoint.**
- The **public pressure log's three-second gap** is now read by Calder and Mara together: a hand was elsewhere for three seconds and then returned, and the mark after the gap is heavier. It proves nothing about motive and is not a lie, because nobody composed it.

### The named-tender cutoff, as a physical method

A wire runs from the diver's harness up inside the hose to the tender's hand, made fast to a cleat on the working deck with a good long turn and a half hitch. It means nothing until the tender puts her weight on it and goes over the fall; then the clip comes off the shoulder and the diver is a man on a line with a man on the surface. The tender's word is **"Enough,"** said **once** and not twice, because twice is a conversation a man can join. **Nessa Pike was the named tender, held the line, and called it**, and Calder surfaced with seventy minutes of air unspent. A slate signed at the platform in Chapter 5 and again in Chapter 7 records that the tender's decision stands without discussion while the diver is attached, and that the diver is right about the going down and the tender is right about the going up. **This is the same procedure used in Volumes 14 and 15.**

### What the crew found in the water off the south end of the mole

- **The claim window of the 27th of November** names the mole's face at the south end **beyond the eleventh seam** and the water off it inside the harbor line, for one date, for a look, with a hand lead and a slate. **The notice of the 31st closes the third to the eleventh seams only, and has never closed water.** This is the volume's answer to *how a man gets a steel on that ground* and it is a form from a man in a barge.
- **The plate.** Ferry iron, three-eighths, lying on the new mole's rubble slope in **about nine and a quarter feet** at the mole face, with the depth **eight and a quarter inside the coaming** by a lead off a boat and not better than a foot on either figure. It is **not glass and it is not the plate in the custody room** and it has nothing to do with the transparent keel on the shoal. It is the *Aster Gannet*'s own iron.
- **The coaming.** Four foot by three foot **outside**, measured from a boat on the 27th; **three foot nine by two foot four clear inside**, measured with a steel rule in the hand on the 8th of December. The bellwright's reading: a stores hatch, not a crew's hatch; a crew's hatch is two foot square.
- **The grating.** Out of its seat and in its seat, the rebate worn bright on the **inboard side only**, a stowage board in it that is not on the survey, and a hasp broken at the stem. The survey is the ferry's own and gives the grating in place before she sailed.
- **The aperture.** Low on the outboard side, a hand's breadth and a half, edges dressed with a bevel on both sides, **a circle of fourteen small holes at an even spacing**, half-inch iron, cut to a template from outside. **The fouling in the holes and on the bevel is continuous with the fouling on the plate outside and gives no hour, no man and no direction.** Nobody in the crew has touched it, nobody can say who cut it or when or from where, and it may have been cut from the water or from the deck and no instrument on this coast will tell it.
- **The measurement and its exact shape, which is the volume's reversal and not its climax.** The survey gives standing space number four, port side aft, as **eight foot four by six foot nine clear and eleven feet two deep under the grating**; the manifest says the hold stood empty. The measured opening is three foot nine by two foot four. The space lies under a grating in a deck, so **the hatch is the only way anything has ever gone into that space or come out of it**, and nothing of the case's seven-to-eight-foot described size could pass it in or out, so **the space cannot have been cleared**; and this office's own thirteen-year-old file of the wreck, in a harbor's hand, gives that space as *number four, port side aft, grating in place, **not cleared, contents not removed***, which is a harbor saying in writing that the space stood uncleared and that it was never cleared on any record here. So a paper saying the hold stood empty at the moment of the seizure is not describing the space at that moment whatever else is true. The yard wanted to prove the room was occupied and has proved the room cannot have been cleared, which is worth more because it is a figure and not a sentence. **What stands in the room is still unknown and is not asked for.** **Note the order of the argument, because the Batch 0005 review found the chapter had it the other way round:** the measurement excludes *this case* through *this opening* and does not by itself establish that the space was never cleared of anything else. The missing step — that the hatch is the only opening, and that the harbor's own file records the space as not cleared — is now in Chapter 47 on the page, and it must be carried into any later volume that repeats the finding.

### The bell-buoy core, as a working thing and not a device

- Recovered on **Tuesday the 6th of December** in **forty-one minutes** at the top of the flood, in about eight and a half feet, fouled off in slabs. Iron, forged, a hand's breadth and a half, flat foot, curved back, a forged yoke and a shackle, a clapper on a gudgeon with **the tongue half worn through**, and **four pieces of worked glass leaded in a band in the waist** with lead gone white.
- **It answers.** A bell in the ground answers another bell in the same ground through the keel-line between them, and the answer arrives **a hand's width of a second late**. This is the whole of the volume's motif: the delayed note has come up off the mole since Chapter 1 and has arrived with every iron in the harbor — the tide bell, the mole's chain alarm of the 28th of October, the buoy's clapper of the 20th of October — and the crew had never once struck anything.
- **The craft, as the bellwright states it and as the chapter obeys:** the sound is the iron, and the glass in the waist is not decoration, it is what makes a bell a *signal* instead of a noise, and nobody on this coast knows why and nobody claims to. **The trade is the going out**: fouling is not the enemy, a wet gudgeon writes the bell off, water under the lead clouds four pieces of glass for good, and a bell that is not looked after stops answering and starts being a rock. A new tongue is a day's work every four or five years for as long as it hangs.
- **It cannot be worked in a shed.** The second clause of the notice of the 31st keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in the harbor, the yard has never argued it, and therefore **the core is hung or it rusts.** The bellwright will not open a bell in the wet. Nothing was refitted.
- **It is hung** on Sunday the 25th of December on a harbor shackle, a harbor chain and a short moor, on the authority's own ground line, in eight and a half feet off the south end, with a chart notation in Kest's own hand: *maintained by the Free Keel yard, not a mark and not a light and not a danger, a signal kept by hand, at the cost stated in this office's hearing record of the twenty-eighth of November.* It has not been taken off and will not be. **The twenty-fifth was a Sunday; the Batch 0005 review corrected the chapter from Saturday.**

**Debt, unchanged and unpaid.** **Forty marks to Bevin Rook** — sixteen for the reading of the book from the fourteenth of October and twenty-four for the hand from the twenty-ninth — stand whole and are not reduced and are not asked to be reduced. **Forty marks to Mara Venn**, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and **not paid in this batch.** **A certificate of method is six marks and three weeks** (Chapter 52), which is the price a conservator with no standing charges, and nobody pays it. **Twenty marks of shoal iron** on the stack, a third of the sale entered to **Sella Brigg for February**, a saw kerf in one crown shackle, the whole stack the charter's bond at a clerk's thirteen. **The air pot is a third gone** and four marks of air bought in this batch have not put a mark back into it. **A pump with a cracked housing, not fitted before the spring, and therefore not hireable, and therefore four months of not being able to hire one out.** The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle.

**Money that is entered and has not arrived: the first of February.** Kest entered in the authority's own book that the nineteenth of January is **paid as a day at eighteen marks** for a crew of three and a boat, out of the authority's own account, with the eighth and the share to come off anything raised and **nothing to be raised**, and the authority's bills in that quarter go out on the first of the month, so **the yard's fourteen marks arrive on the first of February, which is the same day the boy's first wage falls due.** Calder does not tell the boy, because a boy told his wage is coming on a date is a boy given a date, and the yard has spent four months taking dates out of it.

### 4. The bellwright's name, and what he said

**CORVIN SARN, the harbor's own bellwright of Morrow Quay, about fifty, with a blacksmith's forearms, in that yard since the twentieth of October, NAMED IN CHAPTER 55, in Nessa Pike's mouth, and spoken again by Calder Marr when the man comes down the steps.** He is the harbor's and not the Free Keel's, and he says so. **He teaches nobody.** He states, in his own terms, that a bell is iron, and that the whole of the craft is the going out and not the making, and that fouling is not the enemy and must not be cleaned, and that water in a gudgeon writes the bell off and water in the lead clouds four pieces of glass for good with a Crown furnace two hundred miles off as the only repair. **He refuses the crew's share of the day in one sentence** — a man who sells his going out is a man whose going out belongs to somebody — and both the offer and the refusal are in the book. **He gives the mechanism, and it is a mechanism and not a mystery:** the core's seaward face carries a pale scour because thirteen years of fouling has been taking gravel off it at every flood, and the plate under the mole's root has had nothing moving over it since the year of the Cut, and *a bell only speaks while somebody keeps the weather working on it*. **The costs he states: a rope a year, a man's day every four or five years for the tongue, and a person in a boat in the weather the rest of the time.** The day's hire in the book is **eight marks and a day of a boat**, and the chapter says it will be the same again in a year and the year after.

### 5. The dated positions of the charter, the berth, the ninth seam and the wages

- **The one-season provisional working charter** is unchanged: it runs to the last day of March, it is inside the harbor line bounded by the outer marks, **it may be suspended after an inspection, for cause, by one man with a key and a book**, and the reeve said out loud on the twenty-sixth of December that he has no cause and will not, and has said nothing since and has not been asked. The suspended-instrument warning is not repeated in this batch. **The notice of the thirty-first is not lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and the third clause is not reached, and the yard does not ask for it to be reached.**
- **The north berth is still out of service** and is the one that takes a northeast blow. On the first of March, forty herring boats, one berth short.
- **The ninth seam's mooring: bought back, not worked.** On the seventeenth Kest spends the authority's own money on a **fourth set of moorings** and on **the herring fleet's winter warp for the season**, cut and laid by Neale's gang in the fortnight before the first of March. **This is the cost that lands on third parties:** the gang loses three men in the last week of February, the money that went on moorings and warp is money that was not going to be spent on the nineteen winters, so **the disputed and unpaid nineteen winters stay disputed and unpaid**, and Voke's claim is extinguished in practice and not on paper and he cannot show anybody. **The mooring is not yet worked**: the stone was shifted four inches on the twelfth and the shackle was not freed, and the work must be done in a hard month by a hired diver, and the diver is very likely Tam Slee again. **Kest states the price out loud before he grants the thing**, as he did in November about the charter, and says the thing the yard wants and the thing the town pays for are not the same thing. **The cost of the twelfth is the thirteenth's entry: seven marks the authority paid, and the boy's wrist, which Kest says is not his to pay and does not pretend it is.**
- **The wages.** Nothing has been paid. **A wage at four a week from the first of February is entered in the crew's book in the boy's own right hand**, in addition to the forty marks, on a day the yard can name and cannot pay until it raises something, and **the first four weeks of it fall in February**, the same month the authority's fourteen marks arrive. **The word *wages* is not put back into the book**, and the boy asks for it to be written longhand on the wall copy as well, without the word.
- **The coast office** is still unanswered. It asked for a **statement of the authority's capacity to moor the fleet**, in four lines dated the twenty-eighth of November, and the harbor authority promised an answer in a hearing room on the same day and has not written it in seven weeks. **Voke will write the statement himself for nothing**, and now Kest has also promised the coast office a statement of what the authority spent, and both will be read by the same man in Veyr.

### 6. The bodies and the crew

- **Calder Marr**, 32, a line diver with an unstable tide-ear capacity used twice and not used again in this batch. **No dive of his own in this batch and no possibility of one**, because the notice's third clause names him. **His tinnitus is named once, in Chapter 57**: he loses the harbor's crane, cannot place it, asks for a sentence to be said again, and is told it is the fourth time since the fourteenth of October and there will be a fifth and there will be a twentieth, and that no paper in this harbor or in Veyr takes it out of a man's head. He loses a sound and recovers by asking, not by pretending.
- **Nessa Pike**, overrules him for the sixth time since the twenty-fifth of October and wins the sixth, and the sixth cost a boy a hand. She calls *Enough* at nineteen minutes on the twelfth, does not apologize, does not soften it and does not look away. She holds the cleat on the nineteenth with no diver to tend. She reads the money for the boy when he cannot. She signs three things away in four months and is thanked for none of them.
- **Bevin Rook**, 22, left arm in a sling, left hand crushed in October, **left wrist crushed again on the twelfth of January when a chain came tight and he put the bad arm into the fall.** The bonesetter's word in January is different from November's: *it will close, it will not be worth what it was, and I will not promise you June; what is in my book is a boat hand in June, and that is not a promise and it is not a lie.* **June is not resolved.** His mother is told the truth once in a front room and once more in a shed and is not to be told again in a room. ### 7. New entries in the delayed note, and what they are not

**Logged in the flat form — hour, state of the water, the harbor's three strokes, the delay of a hand's width of a second, no source, not written as a voice — and read back twice.**

- **The ninth: the eleventh of January.** Top of the flood, south end, a light southerly, the harbor's three at ten minutes past ten, the answer out of the core under the third. **There is no call in it.** Bevin enters the absence of the call as a fact.
- **The tenth: the nineteenth of January.** Top of the flood, a light air off the north, the harbor's three at four minutes past ten, the same second as the eleventh and the sixth and the twenty-sixth of December, the note only. **That is the third logged occasion running with no call in it, against three earlier occasions with one, and the batch enters the count of the three and nobody in the boat asks him a question about it.**

**Span still begins on the twenty-sixth of October. The answer inside the note is not approached, not named and not resolved, and is reserved to the Volume 15 midpoint.** The two unwritten occasions of Volume 01 stand.

### 8. The delayed note's mechanism, which is craft and not mystery

**The core answers because the flood scours its seaward face and the fouling is what does the scouring; the plate under the mole's root never answered because nothing has moved over it since the year of the Cut. A thing only speaks while somebody keeps the weather working on it.** The delay is **a hand's width of a second** and it is a figure, written down, and as of Chapter 60 it is **a figure in the yard's own book as well as the harbor's**, and it has not become an instrument. The bell still reads nothing, settles nothing and says no word, is still fouled, has a tongue still half worn through, and cannot be worked in a shed because of the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first.

### 9. What Chapter 60 found, and what it may not be used for

**The run's near end is not at the mole and it is not at the shoal. It goes landward, under the mole's root and under the quay, and the figures are ordinary.** South end **nine and a quarter**; forty feet north **eight and a half**; at the mole's root **seven and three-eighths**; then **five and a quarter, landward, under a stone quay**, sounded four times, coming up at about a foot in thirty. **The harbor authority has no chart of what is under the town because the town was built on it.** **No diver was in the water and the reason entered in the reeve's book names a hand lead and a slate.** **Calder stops the day at twenty past eleven at a figure that is not the figure the yard went out for**, and gives the reason aloud in the boat: *a route that no paper has ever been written for cannot be asked about in a hearing room, and a man who cannot ask is a man with no instrument.* **What is under the town is not explained, is not named, and is not to be pursued by a night party or a punt.** The **re-eving's ruling that no line may be drawn between the three points stands and is not lifted**, and nothing in this batch draws one.

### 10. The costs this volume may not waive again

- **The note, re-written in a creditor's own hand at nineteen marks and now at thirteen**, and the first money on it since the eighteenth of November was a mark, and a fortnight's food went on it instead. **A yard's living goes on a note and not out of a bag, and that is where the money goes.**
- **A promise with a date on it, broken out loud, thirty-three days late, and not repaired.** Volume 2 may not quietly make Calder a man who keeps such promises.
- **A wage named on a date the yard cannot pay, by a boy who asked for a worse bargain than he could have asked for.** It is not paid and may not be paid early out of a scene.
- **A wrist, four months after a hand, and a bonesetter who has changed her word and will not promise June.** June is a sentence in a book and not a promise and not a lie.
- **A reeve's price paid in a third party's winter** — the tide gang's three men, a brother's-in-law's boat, nineteen winters that are still unpaid because the money went on warp. **Kest's authority may be spent again and it may always cost somebody who is not the yard.**
- **Eleven copies of the public record instead of ten**, and a naval slate holding the old number, and a man in a net loft asking *who is it for* with no answer. **The consent question has begun to cost records.**
- **The first time a reason written for a municipal book was short a purpose, the omission was entered in the yard's own book, and the crew of the boat agreed not to ask the question.** That agreement is a cost on all of them and is not free.
- **Four marks in a bag, nothing in a tin, a note at thirteen, a pump that cannot be hired, a boat on the hard with no cradle, and fourteen marks and a first wage both falling due on the first of February.**

### 12. Two deliberate reconciliations, flagged rather than hidden
