## Central pressure

**A party that has proved, on the face of a copy and on the face of an acknowledgment, that a line of the *Aster Gannet*'s manifest was altered, and that no paper in its own hands can say by whose hand, is now in a position it has never been in: it holds a true record and it cannot check the record, and an office in a city has said in writing that it released the transcript and will be read in four years as an office which released it. The volume's job is to make that position cost what it costs, which is that a party which has asked for a copy of a record has asked to be listed, and the listing is in a city, and the only answer available to a poor working harbor to an office that says only Veyr can safely hold a testimony is to go and build somewhere else to put it — and building somewhere else is the whole of the craft and none of the money.**

**The Crown's position is the volume's antagonist and it is not a man.** The Crown's claim is that testimony is safe in one place and nowhere else, that a registry that has kept records for three hundred years is the only body fit to hold a disputed record, and that a yard of two and a half people on a mole in a working harbor is not a custody. **That claim is true enough to be dangerous, which is the only kind of claim this book has ever let an institution make, and the volume must not once make it false.** The volume's pressure is therefore not a villain and not a cutter: it is a question with an institution's answer on it, and the yard's answer has to be a place and a set of hands and not a speech.

**AND THE SECOND HALF OF THE PRESSURE IS THE ONE THE VOLUME OPENS ON AND DOES NOT ANSWER: what an office that has kept records for three hundred years thought it was guarding against at the point at which it decided what it was allowed to lose.** A man of the first lay asked that in a market in this harbor on Wednesday the second of December in about forty people's hearing and was told *this yard does not know*, with the reason of the thirteenth of August and a bare second-column fact with no name in it. **The volume may not answer it in a room, may not answer it with a document, may not name a compact as the answer, and may not turn a bare second-column fact with no name in it into a document. What the volume may do is put four or five documents of the wrong shape beside it on a table, one at a time, and let the shape of the thing do the work.** That is the volume's method and it is the method Volume 04 ended on.

**No power stage is bought in this volume's first half. What this volume can pay for is the thing a faultreader cannot do and a listening-bouy trade cannot do either: getting four people who have never met to keep the same page in the same order, and being the party that did not write it.**

## Escalation sequence

Five movements. Each resolves a local problem and creates a larger one. Chapter ranges are the writing schedule's boundaries and the movements are written to them.

### Movement 1, a party that has asked to be listed, and a form with no date on it (Chapters 201–210)

**The volume's first morning is Saturday the sixth of December 2026 and the copy is to be produced to a court in a city on the fourth of January.** The Crown's position arrives as a form, or as a set of terms, or as a man in a room who repeats it: **testimony is safe in one place and nowhere else, and this harbor is not that place, and the harbor authority agrees.** **The yard is not charged with anything. It is asked whether it will hold a copy of a record for the coast, and the asking is the danger, because a party that agrees to hold a record has agreed to be the record's address, and an address can be found.**

**Turn:** a yard that has spent four volumes saying that a record a man can check is the only kind worth having discovers that being checkable is also being findable, and that the instrument it built to make itself credible is the instrument that will make it locatable.

### Movement 2, the work that cannot be sold while a coast is being argued about (Chapters 211–220)

**Ordinary harbor work does not stop because a letter has come up a hill, and the volume's second movement is the cost of doing both.** A day at a seam at about three marks. The compressor's gland, paid for in May and useless now, and the hose after it and this harbor has no hose. **A buoy, because a buoy has to be built out of something and the only thing this yard has is the gear it hires and the two hands it has.** The boy in a June berth that nobody in that crew has gone to see. And the coast road's own figures, in type in a city, and the refusal of the licence that stands unreversed, and the eleven-line list's item eleven, which is now a thing the yard has said out loud it cannot put in front of anybody.

**Turn:** the party is asked to hand its one true record to a place that will keep it safe, and refuses, and finds out that a refusal to hold is read as an intention to keep, and that the only way to be read correctly is to have somewhere to put it that is not a hold.

### Movement 3, the four documents of the wrong shape, and the word on the ninth of them (Chapters 221–230)

**THE VOLUME'S MIDPOINT, AND IT IS NOT A DISCOVERY. IT IS A READING.** The party gets hold of, or is shown, or is lent, four records kept by four communities on this coast about four things that happened to them, **and the four are incompatible, and all four are true, and putting them side by side on a bench in a shed produces a fifth thing that none of them says.** **And the ninth of them is a record of an agreement, and the agreement has a name, and the name is a name of a compact and not a name of a government.** **Nobody reads it out in a room. It is read at the top of a bench by two people, and the clerk is not there, and that is the volume's method and its cost.**

**Turn:** the party learns that the record-keeping it has been demanding of everybody else was once somebody's ordinary practice, and that the practice had a name, and that the name is not on any page of any office in this country.

### Movement 4, a person who cannot be examined, and a room that is not free (Chapters 231–240)

**A man who is the best reader of joins in this harbor is asked to look at a thing that is not a join, in front of people who do not need him to be right.** The Second Bell form of the fourth of December is answered — in a market, in about forty people's hearing, and **the answer is not a speech and is not a defense and does not put the writ anywhere near it.** **The hearing room over the tide-gang store is used again, the tide gang loses another morning, and the price is named out loud before the yes by a man who is not a friend of this party.** And a plate in eleven feet stays where it is, and a note against it stands open, and the volume's own mechanism for ever reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter from a shed.

**Turn:** the party discovers that a document it has been calling evidence is, in the hands of the people it came from, a set of instructions about who may be asked what, and that the four communities whose records it read will each read its copy.

### Movement 5, a living archive and a paper that takes the charter (Chapters 241–250)

**THE VOLUME'S CLIMAX.** A thing comes up the coast that is a person's own account of their own place, held by people who are alive, in water no instrument in this harbor reaches, on a day when the yard could be somewhere else chasing a lead on its own dead. **Calder chooses the living archive and lets the lead go, and the lead does not come back, and nobody in the shed improves on that.** **And a Crown patrol takes the crew's legal charter — not the writ of the first of May, which is a municipal instrument of this harbor and is not theirs to take, and not the Second Bell license, which this holder has not got and cannot be examined for; a charter is a different thing and there is one, and it goes on a form with a name on it.** **And the volume's resolution is that the four communities and this harbor and about nine other places put a page in a drawer in five towns and agree who may read it, and the agreement has no name, and the reason the agreement has no name is on the page.**

**RESOLUTION:** the Crown cannot make the yard say it altered a manifest and cannot make any of the four communities hand over a record, **and the yard cannot make any community trust it, and it does not try, and the four lines of a limit that four people of this coast wrote and signed are the reason a chain of custody works at all.** **The Second Bell eligibility is not restored and is not applied for, and a party which is not examined is not a party which has been refused, and that sentence is said out loud in a market in about forty people's hearing and is the last true thing anybody says in this volume.** A question is asked in a market about what an office was guarding against, and it is asked a second time, and it is still not answered, **and the yard's answer the second time is a second fact with a reason against it and no name in it, and the yard is still not going to call a fact an answer.**
