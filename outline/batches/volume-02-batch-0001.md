## Batch goal

**Open the leash and do not resolve it.** In ten chapters the Free Keel yard learns what a charter is worth and who it is paid for, watches a conservator lose the last of the standing that made her useful, takes ordinary work inside the line that pays badly enough to be honest and too badly to live on, gets a real injury and a real cost on a crew whose boy was a boat hand in June and is not one now, starts the going out in the weather that a hung bell requires, has a reeve spend authority that costs somebody who is not the yard, and goes out on the nineteenth of January at the mole's toe and finds that the run does not stop at the mole or the shoal but goes under the town, where the harbor authority has no chart because the town was built on it.

**The batch does not resolve the leash and does not begin the volume's climax.** The first of March is eleven weeks off at the closing and is named with a figure against it.

## Starting condition

**Five marks in a bag and nothing in the tin, and nothing has been put into either since the first of December.** A pump with a cracked housing and no new one to be had before the spring. A boat on the hard with her bilge blocked in and no cradle. A chandler's note written up in the chandler's own book as *eleven weeks into six* while the yard's own count says thirteen, with six marks put on it in three days in November and none since. Forty marks owed to Bevin Rook and forty to Mara Venn, none of it paid. A chandler's promise made on the first of December and not kept. One herring berth short, the ninth seam's mooring out of service, the north berth out of service, nineteen winters of dues disputed and unpaid. A charter to the last day of March that one man can stop after an inspection. A bell hung on the town's own ground that somebody has to go out and look after. Three custody items that do not come back and a reason for the seizure that is on no paper this harbor has ever been handed.

## Starting relationships

Calder and Mara are two people who have both refused to be useful and kept the refusal on the record. Calder and Kest have spent authority four times in one month and every one of the four cost somebody else a berth, a rope or a hearing. Bevin Rook is owed forty marks, is owed none of it, and reads the book. Alden Marr is at the far bench and has not spoken since the thirteenth of November. Nessa Pike holds the cleat, the pump, the fall and the standing shutdown.

## Starting power level

**Line Diver, with an unstable tide-ear capacity used twice** and one permanent memory gone — the lift at the end of a childhood call. Off the bottom except on dates the reeve names. Not a faultreader. The bell-buoy core is not a power.

## Batch midpoint

**Chapter 55, the going out.** Before it, the yard has work it cannot be paid for and an office that has asked a question whose refusal is a delay. **In it, the yard learns what keeping a signal actually costs** — a rope a year, a man's day every four or five, a fouled waist that cannot be cleaned from a boat, and a public chart notation that has committed them to all of it in front of a town. The object stops being a discovery and becomes a job with a man's name against it. **After it, the crew goes into the water in weather that is wrong for it, and pays.**

## Batch climax

**Chapter 60, the nineteenth of January, a Thursday, at the mole's toe.** A lead, a slate and the bell hung, at the top of the flood, with the reason in the reeve's book at eight in the morning and Pike on the cleat and no diver unless that reason says so. The near end of the run is traced **inland, under the town**, and the harbor authority has no chart of what is under the town because the town was built on it. The batch ends with the leash named as a leash, the first of March named with a figure against it, and a question no paper has ever been written to answer.

**Immediate goal:** Find out what the yard can be paid for in the first week of the year, and find out what the chandler's book says about the note, before anybody else in the harbor asks.

**Resistance:** There is nothing to be paid for yet, the pump has a cracked housing and no new one can be had before the spring, the *Kittiwake* is on the hard, and **the harbor authority's clerk wiped the hearing room's slate wall at eight on Monday the fifth of January, which is tomorrow, so any figure chalked on that board tonight is gone in the morning.** Mara Venn's standing went off her file on the date this chapter opens on.

### Chapter 0052 — The Second Request

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** The Morrow circuit office copying room, a long cold box with a counter down the middle and four stools. **Thursday the fifth of January.**

**Immediate goal:** Find out what the circuit office wants this time and what it will cost to say no, before the yard is asked to do anything at all.

**Resistance:** **The request is worded so that a refusal is a delay**, and Calder can see that it is, and cannot say so in a room that belongs to the office that wrote it. The fee is three marks and the yard has five and has just asked a chandler to write down a worse figure. Mara Venn has no standing to refuse anything and the yard has no money to pay for anything.

**Information revealed:** The full text of the second request, read out with the door of the room's own business open, in the office's own type, and **the sentence that makes a refusal a delay.** It asks for something narrower than the first request and much more useful to the office, and it asks for it in a form that a person who says no has to explain in writing. **Mara Venn is not the addressee and is named in the copy as a person who has already declined a related request**, which means the yard cannot spend her twice. She says so herself, in the road, in about four sentences, and is not thanked.

**Action or decision:** Calder does not buy anything and does not refuse anything, and pays the fee to have the request **entered and dated** — which is the cheapest thing an office sells and the only thing in it that is a fact. He reads the date back into the crew's book in his own left hand and Rook reads it back twice. **He then asks Mara Venn a question he has no right to ask, which is what a certificate costs, and she gives him the figure because a figure is not a refusal.**

**Major turn:** The office's clerk tells him, in the flat voice of a man filling in a box, that a request unanswered is returned with a second seal on it in a fortnight, **and that the second one will be worded so that a refusal is a delay.** Calder has the second one already, in his hand, on the fifth of January. **The instrument of Mara Venn's Volume 4 break is on the page in its first form, and nobody in the room knows yet what it is for.**

**Emotional change:** Calder is told the price of the thing he wants by the one person who cannot charge it, and does not pay it, and does not resent her for the figure.

**Power or resource change:** **Three marks out of five.** The bag is **two** after the copying room, and the chapter says so, and the boy reads it back.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** **Mara Venn does not break with the Registry in this chapter, in this batch, or in this volume.** Four *not stated* entries stand against the same man and the fifth is not filled in. No custody object is asked for and none is requested. The first request's four correct grounds are not re-argued. **The Oren mark is not mentioned and is not told.**

**Ending type:** decision, with a bill attached.

**Next-chapter pull:** Two marks is a day's air, and there is a herring skipper's warp on the north wall that has to move one berth along, and the north berth is out of service because of a notice this yard did not write.

### Chapter 0054 — What a Boy Is Owed in June

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** A cottage row behind the fish market, a fish market stall, and the yard. **Monday the ninth to Tuesday the tenth of January.**

**Immediate goal:** Tell a mother what the yard owes her son, in her own front room, in daylight, with the book open, because she asked to be told and because a boy cannot be the one who asks twice.

**Resistance:** Sixteen marks for the reading from the fourteenth of October and twenty-four for the hand from the twenty-ninth, forty in all, and **the first four weeks at four a week begin in January and the yard has two marks.** The bonesetter's word in January was *hold, and leave it* and the word in November was something else, and the boy's own report of what his hand can do is better than anybody's, and he is the only one in the room who is not being protected.

**Information revealed:** **The hand, four months on.** It will be a boat hand in June. A mother asks three questions and one of them is about the fifth seam.

### Chapter 0055 — The Going Out

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** A boat off the south end of the mole at the top of the flood, and the yard. **Wednesday the eleventh of January**, a fine day, which is the point.

**Immediate goal:** Get somebody out to a hung bell with a lead and a slate and a line, because a bell that is not looked after stops answering and starts being a rock, and because the chart notation on the harbor's own chart says the cost is in the hearing record of the twenty-eighth of November and somebody has to begin paying it.

**Resistance:** The fouled waist cannot be cleaned and must not be. The water under the lead clouds four pieces of glass for good and there is a furnace two hundred miles off that is Crown work. A wet gudgeon writes the whole bell off. **The yard's pump is cracked and cannot pump, the harbor's would cost a hire, and the harbor's crane is paid by the harbor and is not available for this.** There is a rope due and a line of oilcloth and no marks.

**Information revealed:** **The cost of a signal, said by the man whose trade it is, in his own terms, and the man is named: Corvin Sarn, the harbor's own bellwright of Morrow Quay, about fifty, with a blacksmith's forearms, who has been in that yard since the twentieth of October and has never been asked a question by anybody in it.** He is asked one. He does not teach anybody anything. He states what a bell is made of and what a glass in a waist is for and what the going out costs: a rope a year, a man's day every four or five years for the tongue, and a person in a boat in the weather the rest of the time, **and the whole of the craft is the going out and it is the one nobody wants.** He will not touch the bell in the wet and gives the reason.

**Action or decision:** The yard goes out anyway, on a hired line at a cost the chapter states, and Sarn comes because he is the harbor's bellwright and this is the harbor's ground, **and Calder offers him the crew's share and Sarn refuses it in one sentence, and the reason he gives is about a man who does not sell his going out.** The fouling is read off and the delay is read off and the book is read back, and **the delay is a hand's width of a second and is written down as a figure and not as a wonder.**

**Major turn:** They find that the fouling on the core and the fouling on the *Gannet's* plate are the same thirteen years of it, and that the core's seaward side has a clean pale patch where the gravel has come off it every tide and been not allowed to stay, **and that this is why it answers and the plate never did** — a bell only answers while something moves over it, and a plate under a mole's rubble never had anything moving over it. **The motif acquires a mechanism that is not a mystery and costs nothing to keep: a thing only speaks while somebody keeps the weather working on it.**

**Emotional change:** A man the yard has been ignoring for four months is asked a question and answers it, and the yard's whole regard for him changes, and he does not appear to notice and is not thanked.
