## Batch goal

**Fund one recovery out of three and find out that the thing you have is the thing you cannot sell.** In ten chapters a glass storm puts a yard with no reader in weather with a bucket it may not take indoors; the harbor authority's cistern is charged on a rota by a man it cannot pay to walk out to a sluice, and the town starts paying in the only currency it has — a mooring, a berth, a warp, a boy's summer, a cistern; three recoveries are costed and two of them cannot be funded; a man of fifty-one with a bad wheel is better on a lead than a boy of twenty-two and the yard finds out it may not use him without asking him what he hears; a pilot out of Saltmarch comes into the roads and asks the batch's question, which is who keeps a route; a herring skipper's warp comes up off the bottom for the yard at a price the yard cannot pay; **a boat hand's berth for June is offered to a boy who left the stone on the fifteenth and the batch does not resolve it**; a recovery raises worked glass that belongs to nobody and has to go into a return; **and on the last day a party who is not an office asks the yard to sign something that would make the road usable by strangers, and the yard refuses, and learns in the same hour that a route nobody else can use is not a route.**

**The batch takes no power stage, spends no acknowledgement, names the condition at most once if a chapter earns it, does not ask Sera Mott, does not resolve the coast road, does not connect it to anything under the town, and does not begin the volume's climax or put the *Vigil* in the water.**

### Chapter 0112 — The Rota on a Monday and a Thursday

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

### Chapter 0120 — The Signature

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** The shed, the mole's steps, and the fish dock. **Wednesday the fourteenth and Thursday the fifteenth of June.**

**Immediate goal:** Hear what the man with the paper wants, and find out what it costs, and decide before the end of the day.

**Resistance:** **The offer is good and it is not a trap, and that is what makes it hard.** A party who is not the Crown, not a court, not the circuit office and not the harbor authority — a master of boats with two boats and a following, who trades past this harbor and has a copy of the sheet and has read it, and who wants **the right to use the road: a licence, in exchange for money that would fund the third recovery outright and a berth for a herring boat's gear and a line in a book that is not the yard's.** He is not a villain, he is not an institution, he is a man with two boats who has done the arithmetic and thinks the road is worth more to him than to three and a half people. **And he is right about the arithmetic and wrong about the road, and the yard has to say which, out loud, without saying the word for what the road is.**

**Information revealed:** **The batch's turn, paid for: a route nobody else can use is not a route.** A road kept by three and a half men and a boy who has gone is a secret, and a secret is a thing one person loses, and the man with two boats has understood exactly that and is offering to be the person who does not lose it, **and that is a real offer and it is made in good faith by a man who has read a fisher's document and a shop's refusal and a court's form and has put them together correctly.** The yard's answer is that a licence to use a road cannot be given by the people who found it, because the people who found it are not the people who keep it, **and the man with two boats asks the obvious next question, which is who does keep it, and there is nobody in that shed who can answer it except a pilot from Saltmarch who is four days away.**

**Action or decision:** **The yard does not sign, and says no in about nine sentences, in the shed, in Calder Marr's own hand on the back of the paper, and gives the paper back.** And the yard says, in the same breath and without being asked, what it would take instead: **a person who will go and look at it, twice a year, for nothing, and does not have to be told why.** The man with two boats thinks about it and says that is a thing a man says when he has nothing, and he leaves with his paper and does not threaten anybody and does not become a villain, **and the chapter does not have him come back angry and does not have anybody put a mark against his name in any book.**

**Major turn:** **The refusal costs the town its third recovery.** The money that would have funded the warp, or the ground tackle, or the thing on the mole's toe, came from this offer, and the yard has turned it down on a principle it cannot prove and cannot fund, **and the chapter's last page is a woman with a warp at the bottom of the harbor whose debt is now on a market wall with her name against it and no money behind it, and who is not told for a week.** And the yard does not improve on that in a speech.

**Emotional change:** **A man of thirty-two refuses money he cannot replace, in a shed, to a man who was polite, and goes out and stands at the top of the mole's steps, and the chapter does not tell anybody what he is thinking and does not need to.**

**Power or resource change:** **No money in and no money out, and a recovery that is not funded and is named as not funded, and a berth and a boat's gear that do not come.** **And on Saturday the seventeenth of June a return falls due about a shard of worked glass that belongs to nobody, in a crate under a tarpaulin on a mole's crown, and the yard has three days to write it and no boy to read it back.**

**Continuity fact to preserve:** **The road is not resolved, not explained, not named a network, and not connected to anything under the town. The word *network* is not put in the yard's hand. No paper out of a city is produced in this chapter and none may be — the coast office's answer is Movement 3's.** **The nine final routes are not named in the prose. Orison is a place seventy fathoms down.** **The refusal is to a private party and creates no antagonist, no institution and no final enemy, and the man with two boats may reappear in this volume as an ordinary man with a grievance and may not be given an office, a ship, a uniform or a title.** **A custody object does not return. Nothing is claimed. No custody number's digits are quoted.**

**Ending type:** political pressure, and a refusal that costs somebody who is not the yard and is in the yard's own book.
