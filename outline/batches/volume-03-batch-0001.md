## Batch goal

**Run the clock once and pay for it, and lose the boy.** In ten chapters the Free Keel yard discovers what a four-day reporting clause is by letting a return fall two days past the day it was due, and then having to make one that is a confession of its own failure; works out what a day is worth on ground it has been forbidden to touch for five months; buys the gland and the hose with a page it cannot re-write; refuses a man who wants to be hired and is not on the paper, which is the moment the batch learns that four names is a leash and a crew at the same time; **puts Bevin Rook off the stone on Monday the fifteenth of May and keeps the third column with nobody to read it back**; watches a correct refusal land on the man who carried the paper up the coast; produces the writ on a mole in the open air; and goes out to a bell with no tongue on its frame.

**The batch does not begin the volume's climax, does not take the faultreader stage, and does not resolve the coast road or anything under the town.**

### Chapter 0101 — Four Days

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** The Free Keel yard on the fish dock, the mole's steps, and the head of the market wall. **Thursday the fourth of May**, morning. **The hearing room's slate wall is wiped at eight that morning and there is no sitting.**

**Immediate goal:** Work out what the writ's four days are counting from, and get the yard's first return made, because the clerk is coming up the hill at nine with a form and the yard has a bucket it has been carrying for a week.

**Resistance:** **The four days run from the finding or the raising, and nobody in that yard knew there had been one.** On Friday the twenty-eighth of April the yard raised four hundred and forty pounds of old iron off the mole's face between the third and fourth seams — ground the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first closes, on an office instrument, under a claim window that has been spent and logged — and a lump of green glass came up with the rubble and went into a bucket and was not looked at. **The return was due on the second of May and today is the fourth, and it is late, and the yard and the clerk count the four days differently and the clause does not say which way.** The pot of air is gone, the note is at twenty-three, the tin is empty, and the boy is still on the stone and is the only person who can read a position back.

**Information revealed:** **The exact terms of the reporting clause, in the yard's own mouth, as the yard works out what they mean** — four days, place, hour, the person in whose keeping, the limits stated on it, a copy to the harbor authority, and the sentence nobody in the shed has yet said out loud: **where a finding is disputed between persons, the harbor authority may seat one of them and one other and take such finding as it is given.** **And the first fact of the batch, which is that the yard's first return under a paper it asked for is two days late and a return about its own bucket.** The lump of green glass has been in a boat in a shed for six days. **The second clause of the notice of the thirty-first keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in the harbor, and nobody in this yard is going to argue that clause in a shed, and so the object cannot go anywhere to be looked at.**

**Action or decision:** Calder Marr asks the clerk, in a doorway, one question — not what the form is for, but **from which hour the four days are counted and whether the day of the finding is one of them** — and the clerk says the office's own practice is the day of the finding and not the day it was written down, and that **a return entered late is a return entered late and is not a different instrument and is not a smaller one.** **The yard decides to make the return on the fourth day and to say in it that the finding was the twenty-eighth of April and that the return is the fourth, in its own words, with the two days between them on the page and not in a margin, and to put the lump of glass in it as an object in the yard's keeping that the yard has not looked at and will not describe.**

**Major turn:** **The first cost of the writ is that the yard's own silence is now the reportable thing.** A woman on the market wall can be asked what a bar of gravel is worth; a lump of glass in a bucket cannot be, and a clause asks for a finding and a finding is not a conclusion, and the yard has said that in a written objection that went up the coast on the second of May and is now the only sentence in the world that helps it.

**Emotional change:** The boy asks whether the late return is his fault and is told no, in Nessa Pike's voice, in about nine words, and then is told that the four days were nobody's fault either, and that a thing nobody knew about is not a thing a man of twenty-two should have known about.

**Power or resource change:** No money moves. **A page and a boy's whole morning, and a copy to the harbor authority's ledger in the clerk's own hand, and the yard's first entry in a book that is not its own.**

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The writ runs to the first of March next, is not the Second Bell license, is not a charter, renews nothing, and its ground is the mole from the first seam to the twelfth, the dump, the north wall, the first and second seams, all ground landward of the third seam, and private ground within the line. **The reporting clause does not reach Mara Venn.** The four names are Calder Marr, Nessa Pike, Bevin Rook, and Tam Slee of the *Grey Widow*, hired and not of this association. The first of May was a Monday and the wall was wiped at eight and Kest's chalk figure was good for a week and is good until Monday at eight.

**Ending type:** institutional pressure, and a decision with a date on it.

**Next-chapter pull:** A lump of worked glass is in a boat in a shed, and the second clause of the notice says it cannot go in a shed, and it has to be looked at, and the only ground in this harbor where a thing can be looked at in daylight is a mole.

### Chapter 0102 — What Is in the Bucket

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** The mole's crown between the third and ninth seams, in the open air, in daylight, with the tide making. **Saturday the sixth and Sunday the seventh of May.**

**Immediate goal:** Look at a lump of green glass without putting it in a shed, without putting a diver in the water, and without making it a claim, and get a set of figures a man on a mole can check.

**Resistance:** **The second clause of the notice of the thirty-first.** The yard may look at it in the open air on a mole and nowhere else in this harbor, and the yard has not argued the clause in eight months and is not going to start on a Monday. The boy is not on a boat and cannot check anything. A lump of glass that has been in a bucket in a wet boat for six days is wet glass, and **wet glass is not a thing a conservator will put a name to**, and there is no conservator with an office in this harbor. And the mole's crown between the third and the ninth seams is ground the notice closes, and **if the yard stands on it with a bucket, that is a seventh thing the harbor authority has to acknowledge, and only the harbor authority can acknowledge it, and only the reeve can pay for it.**

**Information revealed:** **What the lump is and what it is not, in figures and in two columns.** It is a lump, about a hand's length, green at the fracture and cloudy through the body, with **a scalloped edge that is not a fracture and not a mould line and is a thing worked glass does that Calder Marr has never seen and cannot account for**; it is not a panel, it is not a plate, it is not the ferry plate, and **it is not the worked panel the yard gave back in a market in April and it is not connected with it.** It carries no claim seal. It is in no office's book and has no number anywhere. **What it is not established: how old it is, who worked it, and whether it was ever attached to anything.** The boy puts the mass and the sound and the look of it in the second column with a reason against each, and puts the edge and the fracture and the place it came out of in the first.

**Action or decision:** **The yard does the thing it did in April, deliberately, with the reason said out loud: the object is looked at in daylight on a mole's crown and not indoors anywhere, the finding is in the yard's own book in its own words, and it is not a claim, not a seal, not a survey, and it binds nobody.** And Calder Marr enters **the seventh of the reeve's acknowledgements**, at the yard's asking for the third time in three months, in the reeve's own hand, and the cost of it is Kest's and is never free.

**Major turn:** **The lump is not interesting and the batch says so.** It is a piece of worked glass in a bucket and it is worth nothing to anybody and **that is the point, and a man from a coast office in a market two days later will make the yard prove that it is worth nothing, and the yard will have to say it in public.** The yard does not know that yet and the chapter does not say it; the chapter says only that a man who comes back for a thing twice is a man who thinks it is worth something.

**Emotional change:** A man looks at a piece of glass for an hour and a half and finds out that the most interesting thing about it is that nobody wants it, and **Nessa Pike says the thing the batch needs said, in her own trade's words, in a road**: a yard that has found a thing worth nothing has bought a season, and a yard that has found a thing worth something has bought a quarrel.

**Power or resource change:** **No money moves. A day of a boat and a man's day and the seventh acknowledgement, and the yard's own second consecutive day given to a thing that raised nothing.**

**Continuity fact to preserve:** **The second clause of the notice stands and is not argued.** The bellwright is not of this party and is not of the Free Keel. The lump is in no office's book, carries no seal, and is not connected to the panel in Dace's keeping, to the plug in the chest, to the ferry plate, or to anything under the town. The writ's ground may not be widened, and the yard is on the mole as the owner of hired gear and not as a workman.

**Ending type:** discovery, and a deliberate flatness.

**Next-chapter pull:** There is no air in this harbor, there is a gland gone and a hose gone, and a day on ground the yard is now allowed to stand on would pay a wage on Tuesday.

### Chapter 0103 — A Day Rate

**POV:** Calder Marr, close third person.

**Location/time:** The shed, the mole, and the second lay. **Monday the eighth and Tuesday the ninth of May.**

**Immediate goal:** Turn a writ into a day's work at the yard's own rate, and pay a boy four marks on Tuesday without telling him it was coming.

**Resistance:** **A writ is a leash and it is also the only paper in this yard that pays, and those two facts are the same fact.** Work landward of the third seam needs no instrument and takes the eighth and the share on a trade argument; work on the mole's crown needs an instrument, and **the yard has one, and using it means every raising off that ground is a raising that has to be reported inside four days, which is the whole of what Chapter 101 cost.** There is no air. There are two hired men of the *Grey Widow* and a harbor-authority tender who is not a diver and is not of the party, and **the notice's third clause means none of the three names on the writ can be on the authority's work**, so the tender has to be somebody else and the yard has to ask for him.

**Information revealed:** **What a day on the writ's ground is worth, in a column a man can check.** Gross, the eighth, the share, air at the yard's own two marks, the two hired men at the rate they are worth, and a figure at the end that is smaller than the figure the same job would have paid in April under a claim window and larger than anything the yard has earned since the twenty-ninth of April. And the sentence that carries the batch: **the eighth and the share come off a day rate, and the yard has never argued either, and a yard that argues the eighth off a day rate on the authority's own ground is a yard that is arguing the mole out from under itself.**

**Action or decision:** **The yard takes the day, funds it before anybody touches a boat, and pays the boy on the Tuesday out of it** — and does not tell him on the Monday that it is coming, because a boy told his wage is coming on a date is a boy given a date, and this yard has spent four months taking dates out of it. The entry is in two columns, the diver is not of the party and fixes his own limit at the boat, the tender is the harbor's and not a diver, and the cutoff is his to pull without asking anybody.

**Major turn:** **The yard's first money off its own paper is also its first obligation off its own paper, and both arrive on the same page.** Bevin Rook reads the position back and, for the first time since the twenty-ninth of April, reads a figure that is not going down, and then says the thing nobody asked for, in the middle of a list and not at the end of it, which is that the yard has five days' air bought for a day and one pot, and that the pot is the whole of the air in this harbor, and that a yard that spends its whole air on a Tuesday has nothing on a Wednesday.

**Emotional change:** A man of fifty does a day's work he was paid for in April out of a hat, and at the end of it a younger man makes him a line that he has to mend, and neither of them says anything about it. And **Corvin Sarn is asked one question — not in a room — and answers it in his own words, and says the thing he said in a shed doorway in April and says there is no end of it in a book: a bell in the dark is a bell that is not there, and the mole-head light is not on, and the third lay went dark on the nineteenth of March, and the repair account has been empty since the middle of February, and somebody in a room ought to be told.**

**Power or resource change:** **A going out done, a figure in the first column, a figure in the second, and a day of a boat and a man's day paid out of a market's money two months ago.** **A hand lead's line made in the net loft at night and right the second time**, and the boy's line in the book about a man making a line at night, with the reason that no man on a mole could check how a line was made and that in four years somebody might want to know.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** **The tongue is not on the frame and the phrase "a tongue half worn through" may not be revived.** The bell reads nothing, settles nothing and says no word, and it is not an instrument, and the going out is the whole of the craft. **The count of logged occasions is eleven and the two empty answers are two and neither is advanced.** **No occasion is entered.** The condition may be named once in this volume and is not named in this chapter. **A lead plug with a number in it is in a chest in a shed, in no office, and the bellwright will not say what the number is, and he is not asked in a shed.**

**Ending type:** craft, and a cost that recurs.

**Next-chapter pull:** **A returning of the circuit office's question about the bar of gravel, a coast office that has not answered a fisher's document since the first of March, a claim in a hearing record over the ground the yard is now standing on with a bucket, and the first of June four weeks and a day off with a crew of two and a half and a third column nobody can read back.**
