## Batch goal

**Run the clock twice and pay for it, and lose the boy.** In ten chapters the Free Keel yard discovers what a four-day reporting clause is by letting a return fall two days past the day it was due, and then having to make a second one four days after a look at a lump of glass on a mole's crown; works out what a day is worth on ground it has been forbidden to touch since the thirty-first of October, which is six months and not five; buys the gland and cannot buy a hose to match, with a page it cannot re-write, and lets the pump out of the shed for the first time since the fifth of April; refuses a man who wants to be hired and is not on the paper, which is the moment the batch learns that four names is a leash and a crew at the same time; **puts Bevin Rook off the stone on Monday the fifteenth of May and keeps the third column with nobody to read it back**; watches a correct refusal land on the man who carried the paper up the coast; produces the writ on a mole in the open air; **and puts one boat off the south end to a bell with no tongue on its frame, which is not a going out and is not paid for as one, and does not ask the man of fifty the one question the batch has to leave alone.**

**The batch does not begin the volume's climax, does not take the faultreader stage, and does not resolve the coast road or anything under the town.**
