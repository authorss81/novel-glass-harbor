# Volume 01 Outline: The First Bell

Chapter range: 1–50

## Central pressure

Calder's crew must prove a legitimate claim to the first transparent wreck and secure a defensible provisional working charter and salvage share before Kest and the Lantern Registry seize the recovery. The *Aster Gannet* clue makes success dangerous: the same evidence that can clear the Marr family also puts Calder's crew under Ilya Serr's authority and draws the *Vigil* toward Morrow Quay.

This volume resolves the legitimacy of the first recovery, not Calder's full Second Bell license. The broader license remains a later goal and is explicitly deferred after the Volume 4 inquiry.

## Starting state

Calder is a thirty-two-year-old line diver with practical salvage skill, family debt, and one failed attempt short of the full Second Bell examination. He works under the Free Keel's loose protection and accepts the official account of Oren's death only outwardly. No echo ability has been activated in the story, and the crew has no legal or practical route into Orison Deep.

## Starting relationships

Calder trusts Alden's silence and Bevin's eagerness more than he should. Mara Venn enters as a Registry tide conservator assigned to audit the first transparent shard; Calder wants her away from the crew because he does not know whether she will report him. Nessa and Tobias know Calder's work habits well enough to challenge unsafe decisions, but not yet well enough to stop him.

## Starting power level

**Line Diver.** Calder is competent with rope, pumps, pressure bells, hull seams, and weather instruments. His sensitivity to glass faults comes from long practice. He has no practiced control of an echo, no aftertaste history, and no independent power that can solve a legal or physical problem.

## Major locations

- Morrow Quay's public dry dock, fish market, Free Keel yard, and the reeve's office on the west basin
- Gannet Shoal and the ordinary wreck entangled with the old glass chain
- The *Vigil*'s outer mooring and blockade line
- The first Orison shelf, the deeper stage of the same chain that the volume's later dives reach
- The harbor authority's hearing room in Morrow Quay, where local salvage claims, holds, and provisional working charters are decided
- The Morrow circuit office copying room, where the public pressure log and provenance duplicate are read

## Major factions

- **Free Keel:** independent salvage crews sharing tools, labor, and legal risk
- **Morrow Quay harbor authority (reeve's office):** dependent on salvage income but liable if imperial glass enters the harbor without permission; it issues claim windows, work permits, and one-season provisional working charters and can order a wreck held for inspection, but it cannot overrule a Registry seizure
- **Lantern Registry:** able to certify provenance and restrict handling, but not to certify truth
- **Office of Returning Memory:** Ilya Serr's office, represented locally by seizure authority and senior examiner Ilya's seal
- **Crown Navy and the *Vigil*:** a late external pressure rather than an active combat force for most of the volume

## Escalation sequence

The volume moves through five escalating movements. Each one resolves a local problem and creates a larger one. Chapter ranges are approximate; the batch boundaries are fixed by the writing schedule, not by the story.

1. **Movement 1, the legitimate find (Chapters 1–10):** an ordinary paid job exposes a transparent hull fragment. Calder wins only a disputed claim window, spends his crew's remaining air and pump time proving salvage labor, takes one supervised echo entry that leaves him with tinnitus and a rope-burn aftertaste, and loses the physical shard to a Registry seizure bearing Ilya Serr's senior examiner seal. He keeps measurements, names, and a public provenance duplicate, and the *Vigil* arrives at Morrow Quay.
2. **Movement 2, the leash (Chapters 11–20):** the disputed claim window and its provisional extension are treated by Kest as revocable favors rather than victories, because the one-season charter does not exist yet. Calder must fund weather-exposed recoveries, hold a crew together through a poor month, and prove the recovered material's value in a harbor authority hearing where his family's Gannet connection is used against him. A rival crew claims part of the first wreck's value. The crew wins a narrow extension and a bond they can barely meet, but loses Calder's remaining air margin and part of its share.
3. **Movement 3, the ordered chain (Chapters 21–30):** Calder traces the wreck chain from Gannet Shoal toward the first Orison shelf and finds that the transparent vessels are positioned, not scattered. A bad descent injures Bevin's hand and costs the crew its tender. Mara's contamination report is used by the Registry to argue the chain is contagious. Calder chooses to keep the evidence public and loses the chance to reach a deeper panel alone.
4. **Movement 4, the hearing (Chapters 31–40):** the harbor authority hearing on the first recovery becomes a public contest over custody. Calder must place the *Vigil*'s claim and the shard on the same shoal using physical measurements and the provenance duplicate, not the echo. The Navy's salvage claim arrives mid-hearing. Mara's position as a Registry tide conservator is the only reason the crew's chain of labor survives, and refusing to certify a convenient custody story earns her an internal reprimand and a note in her file. Calder still hides the Oren-specific mark.
5. **Movement 5, the bell-buoy core (Chapters 41–50):** Calder's crew recovers a usable bell-buoy core and a fragment holding Oren's first minutes of the seizure. Surfacing it through a public route rather than keeping it privately costs him a specific childhood memory of Oren's voice and earns the Crown's attention. The one-season provisional working charter and the salvage share are granted; the seizure's reason is not.

### Costs the volume may not waive

- Money: air, pump repairs, a replacement clamp, a cracked housing, a bond, and harbor legal fees come out of the crew's share.
- Body: at least one injury to a working crew member, one bad descent, and Calder's tide-sickness onset.
- Standing: Calder loses examination eligibility, crew wages, and the shard; Mara spends internal credit.
- Time: each recovery spends a safe diving day the crew cannot replace, so every later dive is worse weather or worse gear.

## Midpoint reversal

Around the volume midpoint, later physical pressure records corroborate the limited first echo from Batch 0001. The *Gannet* compartment the manifest calls empty is too large to have been empty, the sealed case crossed it during the seizure, and the *Vigil*'s mooring line was cut from shore after the seizure began. The transparent shard is therefore neither an ordinary wreck panel nor a private keepsake: the discrepancy reflects coordinated action rather than a lone thief or clerical error. The evidence still does not identify who ordered the lie or what happened after the seizure, and no forum rules on it in this volume.

## Volume climax

Calder's crew recovers a usable bell-buoy core and a fragment containing Oren's first minutes during the Gannet seizure. Calder chooses to surface the fragment through a public route rather than keep it as a private answer. The act costs him a specific childhood memory of Oren's voice and alerts the *Vigil*. The Crown can no longer dismiss the shard as an isolated wreck panel.

## Concrete resolution

The crew secures a one-season provisional working charter, retains its agreed salvage share, and preserves the provenance chain through Mara. The public evidence establishes that the *Vigil* was present at the Gannet scene and that the official manifest is unreliable. Ilya Serr's name and senior Registry examiner's seal appear on the current seizure order, but his role in the Gannet lie and the reason for the order remain undisclosed. The immediate claim to the first recovery is legally defensible; the mystery it opens is not resolved.

## New-volume question

Why are the transparent vessels arranged along a deliberate route toward Orison instead of lying as scattered ordinary wrecks, and who controls the missing links?

## Final chapter image

At slack tide, the recovered bell-buoy core answers with the same delayed note Calder heard in the keel line at the end of Chapter 10, while the transparent fragment sits behind Mara's mixed seal. Calder hears an adult version of a childhood call he can no longer remember and chooses the next licensed run rather than a private descent.

## Planned relationship and power change

Calder becomes a tide-ear through supervised practice, failure, and a visible aftertaste cost; he is not yet a faultreader and cannot treat an echo as proof. Calder and Mara move from mutual suspicion to limited professional trust. Calder still hides the Oren-specific mark at the volume boundary, so their intimacy and accountability remain incomplete.

## Antagonist movement in this volume

1. **Ors Kest** enforces the harbor authority's permits, holds the shard for a Registry courier, and threatens the claim window. He is the volume's active obstacle and remains a pressured local official rather than a villain.
2. **Captain Veyra Holt and the *Vigil*** arrive as a late external pressure and claim naval salvage authority over the anchored wreck. Holt does not explain the seizure's real reason.
3. **Ilya Serr** appears only through his office, his senior examiner's seal, and the seizure order. His name is on paper; his face does not need to appear in this volume, and his historical role in the Gannet lie must not be revealed.

## Reveal discipline for Volume 1

Volume 1 may establish the existence of the Gannet Cut, the glass fleet, the *Vigil*'s claim over the shoal, a physical alignment between the shard and the cutter's recorded mooring position, and the first hint of a route toward Orison. It must not explain the Quietening, identify the Crown Seal, name the Nine Harbors as a compact, prove who altered the ferry manifest, or reveal what the sealed glass case contained. Every later answer must be earned by a physical artifact, a conflicting testimony, a labor-based expedition, or a political document whose limitations are understood.

## What Volume 1 must not do

- Do not let Calder become a faultreader or claim to read seams.
- Do not let a recovered echo count as a complete or official record.
- Do not give Calder the full Second Bell license; the one-season charter is the payoff.
- Do not let Mara certify truth, enter an echo, or become Calder's assistant.
- Do not let the crew keep the physical shard past the seizure.
- Do not let a second or speculative batch file be implied; only the ten cards in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md` exist.

## Handoff to Chapter 11

By the end of Batch 0001 the crew has: a public provenance duplicate, a twelve-day disputed-claim defense, an uncertain bearing toward Orison, a lost physical shard, a hidden Oren-specific mark, and a tide-ear capacity Calder cannot safely repeat. Chapter 11 begins with a legal dispute under naval pressure and Calder's hearing still altered.
