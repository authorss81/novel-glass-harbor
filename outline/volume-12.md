## Central pressure

**The Crown begins activating the Crown Keel, making one controlled echo appear through seized bells and forcing communities to accept a stabilising history.**

The mechanism is not a battle and it is not a monster and it is not a voice in anybody's head. **IT IS A SET OF SEIZED BELLS AND VESSELS THAT ANSWER EACH OTHER, AND WHAT IT PRODUCES IS AN ACCOUNT THAT SOUNDS LIKE A WITNESS BECAUSE SOMEBODY'S WITNESS IS IN IT.** A community that has spent two winters refusing to be told what it remembers is offered, for the first time, an account that is stable, that does not contradict itself, that anybody can repeat, and that has a man in an office behind it. **AND THE ACCOUNT IS NOT FALSE. THAT IS THE WHOLE OF THE PRESSURE AND IT IS WHY NOBODY IN THIS VOLUME CAN SIMPLY REFUSE IT.** A thing that is a lie is easy to argue with in a room. A thing that is technically accurate and complete and has a seal on it is not, and the people who argue with it are made to look like people who want a coast where children are frightened by what they cannot verify.

**AND THE PARTY IS IN THE WORST POSITION IT HAS EVER BEEN IN.** Its own strongest argument is a sheet of figures a stranger can check with a pencil, and a stabilised account is not a figure and cannot be checked with a pencil. Its buoys are not its buoys and the crews that hold them are four named men on four tacks on the boards of a market. Its best result is a night in which three points were worked by three crews and the whole of the coordination was a page of hours and a bell on each of three marks, and **a man with a boat of about fourteen tons has said out loud, in eleven words, in a room, that this coast can be cut in one place at a time, and every place the Crown begins to speak from is another such place.** It has a sea under martial custody. It has a proof in a locked case that nobody has ever read out. It has four heads on a wall and a fifth is not to be added. It has twenty-eight pence in a bag.

**AND THE DECISION IN FRONT OF THE PARTIES IS NOT WHETHER TO RESIST. IT IS WHETHER A COMMUNITY THAT IS OFFERED A STABLE ACCOUNT AND A COMMUNITY THAT IS OFFERED NOTHING ARE GOING TO BE ABLE TO STAY IN THE SAME COALITION, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS WRONG.** One of those communities will be believed. **The Crown's account is put in front of a room in full, with its seal and its limits on the face of it, and it does not contradict itself and nobody can knock it down.** A careful man tries and cannot, and the reason he gives is a woman who said that she had been in the room and refused in her own mouth to say a thing she did not know. **The midpoint is paid in this movement, in a room, out loud, and is not answered by a speech, and the person who pays it is not thanked.**

### Movement 3, every bell on that coast answering one signal, and the thing a sealed record does that a permitted one cannot (Chapters 571–580)

**The Crown Bell branch relay begins to answer itself, and a local crew is offered a Crown instrument for its own mark in exchange for a copy of its own crossings, and the offer is refused, and the refusal costs the offerer nothing and the refuser a season's work.** A community that has sealed everything is compared, on the page and not in a speech, with a community that has permitted everything, **and the sealed one is the one that holds.**

### L31. THE DRAUGHT

**A close-sea line is a line of boats and a paper and a price. It is not a wall, and the volume's argument is that a coast held by people who live on it is harder to close than a coast held from an office, AND THAT A COAST HEARD FROM ONE PLACE IS EASIER TO SILENCE THAN A COAST HEARD FROM SEVERAL. Nothing in this volume may make the blockade total, permanent, or unanswerable, and the concrete resolution is many communities offering a permitted account at the same time with the refusals left visible.**

### L32. THE SOUND

**A bell on a buoy, a bell in a room over a fish market, a bell in a shed, AND A SIGNAL AT A REGULAR INTERVAL THAT NO HAND RANG.** The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for a figure and is not asked in this volume, and his chest stays shut and the eight marks and a day of a boat stay unargued. **NO CHAPTER MAY GIVE THE CROWN ACCOUNT A VOICE, A TONE, A NAME OR A FACE, AND NO CHAPTER MAY HAVE IT ANSWER A QUESTION.**

### L33. THE WORKING DAY

**A wage Tuesday is the day a harbor finds out what it owes and the finding out is not a payment. A Sunday the wall behind a reeve's bench comes down bare and goes up again unrepaired and not whole. A Monday and a Thursday the cistern is charged and walked on. A day asked for by name is paid at the figure in chalk whether anybody is on the water or not. The work is visible, including in the climax, and the climax of this volume is paid in days, hours, coal, air, a lost instrument, and a bill.**

### L34. THE APPARATUS, AND WHERE THE HOUSE FINDS ITS ESCALATION

Each chapter carries, in this order: the chapter number and a title; an italic subtitle made of that day's own figures and hours; the dateline paragraph; a bold block of the day's figures and of what is standing; the scene, in paragraphs of two to six sentences, separated by rules; a bold closing block; a rule and a rule; and then the leaf's *first column* and *second column* entries in the yard's own mouth, in italics, with an hour against each. **The second column takes five lines a day and is not checkable from a mole, and a reason entered in the second column of one chapter is not restated in the second column of another. No two chapters of a batch may carry the same standing block, and the standing paragraph carries the day-counters and the hearing and two or three standing items and nothing else.**

### L35. THE SWEEPS A LATER PHASE MUST RUN AGAIN

Quote parity even on every file; the second person at zero in the narration and in the apparatus; **no four-digit year on any file**; a counted word-length, line-count, name-count, day-count or sheet-count on a printed document passes; every clock evaluates; **the duplicate-run sweep ACROSS THE SCENE PROSE OF EVERY CHAPTER IN THE BATCH AND NOT ACROSS THE SECOND COLUMNS ALONE, BECAUSE A SWEEP SCOPED TO THE APPARATUS CANNOT SEE A DUPLICATED SCENE PARAGRAPH**; and no named prohibited word in any mouth or any narration. **THE PROHIBITION LIST IS VOLUME 11'S L35 AND THE ITEM 12 LIST EXPANDED AND NOT WEAKENED, AND *ORISON* IS THE ONE NAME REMOVED FROM IT AND ONLY FOR THIS VOLUME, PER § *Divergences* ITEM 3.**

### L36. THE SENTENCES THAT MAY NOT BE SAID TWICE

**A named term, a refusal, a settlement, a sound, a bell, a named argument, a reveal, a location, a favour, a wound, a threshold and a death are each used once in the volume and not restated in the same words.** A reason written in the second column of one chapter is not written in the second column of another, and the sweep is run.

### L37. THE ENDING OF THE VOLUME, AS IT MUST STAND

**Chapter 600 ends with: the four columns at three hundred and thirty-three, one thousand five hundred and thirty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, with the second column's arithmetic printed on the face of the reading; the fitting-out sheet at a figure a stranger can take three numbers off; the proof still in a case on a table in that harbor; a buoys list with local crews' names at the head of it and one man's name nowhere on it; a hole in a Tuesday that is still a hole; a bag with twenty-eight pence in it; the visible gaps, each carrying what is not said, who is not saying it, what they are entitled to keep, and what would have to happen for it to be opened; and the new question asked once at a working place and answered by nobody. The closing block is inside the world, may not say what is coming, and may not name this chapter or this volume.**
