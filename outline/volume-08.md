## Central pressure

**The Crown offers Calder legal amnesty and protection in exchange for handing over Mara's public archive, and refusing it brings the Navy directly to Morrow Quay.** The offer is not a bribe and it is not a threat. It is an instrument with a seal on it and four things named as its price, and the fourth of them is a statement that the Crown's account of the Gannet seizure is true. Every one of the four is refused, in public, by the people who own the four things and not by one man, and the refusal is what the Navy comes for.

**The blockade of Volume 07 has become a political weapon, and the coalition has proof and no leverage at sea.** A page of forty lines is the strongest document on that coast and it cannot lift a gun. A harbor poorer on the fourth day of July than on the fourteenth day of May is asked, in the second week of July, to say no to a Crown amnesty in the strongest voice available to a government that has never once withdrawn a notice from a wall.

## Escalation sequence

### Movement 1, a paper with a seal on it and a price on it that is not money (Chapters 351–360)

**A sealed instrument arrives at the shed naming four things as its price, and the fourth of them is a statement that the Crown's account of the Gannet seizure is true. Calder will not answer it in a shed. He puts the four prices where a person can look at them, eleven people who hold the eleven leaves are asked in eleven rooms, nine say no, one says no with a reason that is about a line of writing and not about a person, and one says nothing. ### Movement 2, a room in Veyr where nobody with a title is in it, and a clause that has never been tested (Chapters 361–370)

**An inquiry sits, on terms drawn by the Crown, and Calder and Mara go into it as the only two people on that coast who can be shown the thing. The negotiation turns on a clause that has been on the books since the first of May and has never once been used: a civilian convoy under municipal papers is not a vessel returning from beyond the line, so the sixth clause's door is open to it as well as to the others. A woman of no office makes him say out loud, in a room with a clerk in it, what a refusal costs, and he says the four things and does not improve on them. HE APOLOGISES TO HER IN THIS MOVEMENT, IN HIS OWN VOICE, ONCE, AND SHE DOES NOT FORGIVE HIM AND DOES NOT THANK HIM, AND WHAT SHE ASKS FOR INSTEAD IS A PROCEDURE.**

### Movement 3, a convoy of confiscated memory vessels, and a policy written on a page (Chapters 371–380)

**MIDPOINT. The closed-sea squadron has been lifting unsealed testimony out of civilian hulls for eleven weeks, under a form, and the form is the policy. Calder can read the form from the inside of a convoy, and the convoy is a Crown convoy, and getting at it means being on the water inside the line's own arrangements. THE REVERSAL: the taking is not a raiding policy invented by an admiral. It is a standing instruction dated before the blockade and countersigned with a Registry seal, which means the Crown has been building one history out of civilian testimony while a harbor four days up the coast has a page of forty lines on a wall saying that a thing on the water has to be looked at by somebody who is not being paid by the person who wants it looked at. He does not get the form. He gets the shape of it, and a date, and a seal, and a slip of paper with seven thousand on it and no name on it. He comes up with two foot of water on a remembered line's arithmetic wrong by the same two foot, and it is nobody's.**

### Movement 4, a patrol and a transport, and boats that were not a fleet (Chapters 381–390)

**The coordination work. Calder begins the repair and convoy work that makes him a keelwright in the next volume, and the condition is on the face of it: a convoy is usable only where somebody maintains it, and maintenance is a wage and a day and a person who is there on Tuesday. The man of about fifty-one's page on the mole is what a boat looks like when nobody is maintaining anything. Six masters of the third lay are taken in at the outer water on a papers question, and the papers question is answered, and the answer is the first legal thing anybody has won in this volume and it is a foot and not a fleet. The Crown uses the convoy work as its occasion: a patrol is sent to a civilian transport carrying flour because of it, and Calder is the one who can see the patrol coming and is the one who knows a route out.**

### Movement 5, he stopped it (Chapters 391–400)

**THE CLIMAX. Calder uses a coordinated convoy to bring the eleven weeks of lifts and the countersigned form into a public record, and his timing causes a patrol to attack a civilian transport. He stops the naval action rather than chase the evidence. He admits the cost in a room with a clerk in it and on a mole, in his own voice, once, and is not comforted.
