## Central pressure

**A line of boats has been put outside the mouth of that harbor and has not been taken away, and a coast that cannot send anything out cannot be shown to be a coast, and a party that has proved a thing on a trench floor twenty-two fathoms down now has to prove it between two buoys in four fathoms in front of people who need to eat.**

The Assembly is still a sheet of about three feet on the wall of a room over a fish market with four rule-heads, four standing lines, eleven names at the foot of it and eleven people who did not sign. **It has no jurisdiction, no head, no seat, no roll and no money, and a cordon is a thing made of the second kind of problem and not the first.** It cannot negotiate, it cannot refuse anybody anything, and it cannot decide where a boat goes. What it can do is hold a record and say out loud what a thing does not show, and this volume is about what happens when that is the only thing anybody has.

**AND THE OTHER HALF OF THE CENTRAL PRESSURE IS THAT THE QUESTION ASKED AT A MOLE HEAD ON THE SECOND DAY OF DECEMBER IS NOW A WORKING PROBLEM.** *If they are putting that old thing back together on that floor down there, how many harbors have to be joined up to it before a man can make it work?* A cordon is the answer being built in front of the party, by people with boats and a paper, and the party cannot stop it and cannot out-run it and cannot ignore it, because a thing that is a hole in a floor with a paper in it is now being described to every master on that coast as a reason to stay inside somebody else's line.

**THE CREW IS A CREW AND NOT A VESSEL.** About nine people are in it. Four are of that yard and are paid. Four are hired by the day and are paid in advance. **A machine is a tool, and it was built by people, and it does not settle anything by being understood.** It is six centuries old and it is on the bottom and the Crown's marks are around it and nobody in that harbor has been given it and nobody has been stopped from knowing about it, and **that is the whole of the machine in this volume: it is a reason somebody else has, and the party is not going to be the only people who have one.**

### L29. THE LIVES THAT MAY NOT BE USED

**The man of about twenty-nine is not a motive. The man of about thirty-eight's kept thing is not a mystery to be opened. The man of about thirty-four's Tuesday is not a puzzle. The man of about thirty-two's hands and aftertaste and unplaceable afternoon are not a condition to be fixed. The pump hand of about forty-four is not a mascot and is not a mouth. The woman of thirty of no office is not the party's.**

### L30. THE TWO SHEETS AND THE COUNTS THAT MAY NOT BE MADE

**The four heads and the fitting-out sheet are never in one clause, one column, one room or one mouth without both being named out loud separately. Neither carries a count of persons. No list of the people who put nothing in is kept by anybody in that harbor and no chapter may keep one, name one, or count one.**

### L31. THE DRAUGHT

**A close-sea line is a line of boats and a notice and a price. It is not a wall, and the volume's argument is that a coast held by people who live on it is harder to close than a coast held from an office. Nothing in this volume may make the blockade total, permanent, or unanswerable, and the concrete resolution is the coast moving people and supplies without a Crown fleet.**

### L32. THE SOUND

**A bell on a buoy, a bell in a room over a fish market, a bell in a shed. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for a figure and is not asked in this volume, and his chest stays shut and the eight marks and a day of a boat stay unargued.**

### L33. THE WORKING DAY

**A wage Tuesday is the day a harbor finds out what it owes and the finding out is not a payment. A Sunday the wall behind a reeve's bench comes down bare and goes up unrepaired. A Monday and a Thursday the cistern is charged and walked on. A day asked for by name is paid at the figure in chalk whether anybody is on the water or not. The work is visible, including in the climax, and the climax of this volume is paid in days, wages, coal, rope, bells and two lost boats.**

### L34. THE APPARATUS, AND WHERE THE HOUSE FINDS ITS ESCALATION

Each chapter carries, in this order: the chapter number and a title; an italic subtitle made of that day's own figures and hours; the dateline paragraph; a bold block of the day's figures and of what is standing; the scene, in paragraphs of two to six sentences, separated by rules; a bold closing block; a rule and a rule; and then the leaf's *first column* and *second column* entries in the yard's own mouth, in italics, with an hour against each. **The second column takes five lines a day and is not checkable from a mole, and a reason entered in the second column of one chapter is not restated in the second column of another. No two chapters of a batch may carry the same standing block, and the standing paragraph carries the day-counters and the hearing and two or three standing items and nothing else.**

### L35. THE SWEEPS A LATER PHASE MUST RUN AGAIN

Quote parity even on every file; the second person at zero in the narration and in the apparatus; **no four-digit year on any file**; a counted word-length, line-count, name-count, day-count or sheet-count on a printed document passes; every clock evaluates; **the duplicate-run sweep across the second columns of every pair of chapters in a batch returns no passage of prose**; and no named prohibited word in any mouth or any narration.

### L36. THE SENTENCES THAT MAY NOT BE SAID TWICE

**A named term, a refusal, a settlement, a sound, a bell, a named argument, a reveal, a location, a favour, a wound, a threshold and a death are each used once in the volume and not restated in the same words.** A reason written in the second column of one chapter is not written in the second column of another, and the sweep is run.

### L37. THE ENDING OF THE VOLUME, AS IT MUST STAND

**Chapter 550 ends with: the four columns at two hundred and eighty-three, one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, with the second column's arithmetic printed on the face of the reading; the proof still in a case on a table in that harbor; a buoys list with local crews' names at the head of it and one man's name nowhere on it; a shipyard rebuilt at a figure that is on a sheet; a hole in a Tuesday that is still a hole; a sea declared and a coast that can be cut again on a Tuesday; and the new question asked once at a working place and answered by nobody. The closing block is inside the world, may not say what is coming, and may not name this chapter or this volume.**
