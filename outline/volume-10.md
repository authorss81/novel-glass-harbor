## Central pressure

**A working expedition has to go down to a shelf nobody in that harbor has a chart of, to look at a blank hull, before somebody with a warrant and good boots gets there first — and the money has to come out of eleven people who cannot afford it, out of a sheet that is not the Assembly and is not signed by anybody who has not asked, and the only instrument in that harbor that could pay for it binds nobody and decides nothing.**

The Assembly is a sheet of about three feet on the wall of a room over a fish market with four rule-heads, four standing lines, eleven names at the foot of it and eleven people who did not sign. **It has no jurisdiction, no head, no seat, no roll and no money, it did not ask for money and cannot receive any, and this volume's central pressure is what happens to a thing with rules on it when a real job has to be paid for on the morning it has to be paid for.** So the money does not go in through the four heads. It goes in through a second sheet, bought blank at a penny a sheet at a counter in a room off that market, with its own head, its own refusals, its own hours, its own arithmetic, and a line at the foot of it that is neither a cost nor a name nor a figure. **The two sheets are never put into one clause, one column, one room or one mouth, because the first sheet is rules and the second sheet is money, and a harbor that cannot tell those apart is a harbor that will be told what to do by whichever of them is written in the larger hand.**

**THE EXPEDITION IS SENT BY NOBODY.** It is not sent by the Assembly, which cannot send anything. It is not sent by the harbor authority, which has a list and a berth fund and pays one day with a boat and two men at a figure in chalk. It is not sent by the Crown, which has a warrant and is three weeks ahead of it. It goes because nine people put four marks each into a sheet, four men put in a day apiece in advance, two people put in a mark each, and a boat's owner put in eight marks; and because eleven people were asked one at a time in the same eleven sentences written at the head of a page before anybody was asked, and two of them said no with a reason in their own mouths, and the two refusals stand on that sheet at the same size as the nine.

**AND THE OTHER HALF OF THE CENTRAL PRESSURE IS A CREW AND NOT A VESSEL.** About nine men go. Four are of that yard and are paid. Four are hired by the day and are paid in advance. One is a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors who is in nobody's crew and is a member of nothing, who asks what happens when the plan fails, and who is not a mascot in this volume, is not lost in it, and is not thanked.

### Movement 3, MIDPOINT, a hull built to take testimony out of the living, and a tool that was civic before it was the Crown's (Chapters 471–480)

**THE REVERSAL, AND IT IS PAID IN THE SAME CHAPTER IT IS STATED. The blank hull is not a store and not a grave and not a monument. It is a machine for removing testimony from a living crew, and the parts are on the floor in racks, and they are civic, and they are six centuries old, and the frame of the machine is old and the fittings on it are not. The source of the Crown Seal is not a god and not an ancient monster and not a curse: it is a civic tool, built by people who wanted to stop nine communities arguing with each other about a disaster, rebuilt by the Serein Crown's engineers three hundred years later with the same geometry and a different purpose, and a setter of marks on that shelf is being paid to set marks around it. The cost of the reversal is that every account the party has of what the Crown is doing is now worse and more specific, and that the party has said out loud on the twenty-third day of November in a room over a fish market that it is not going to be able to keep a thing like that out of a public record, and nobody in that harbor believed it at the time.**

### Movement 4, a cache, a strip, and a crew that comes up different (Chapters 481–490)

**A cache of old civic records is found, in racks, in condition, behind the machine, and it is the best thing this party has ever had in its hands, and it is also the only thing in this volume that can be used as a key to anything. ## What this volume must not do

**These are the volume's own, they are written here before the five movements are broken into chapters, and they are binding on every chapter of Volume 10. A prohibition in this house is kept by putting on the page what happened and not by printing a list of what did not.**

1. It may not answer the question Volume 09 asked — *can a thing like this speak for people who cannot speak safely, and for people whose own account of what happened to them is being held under a lie?* — and that question is not asked again in this volume and is answered nowhere in it. **Neither is the question about who gets into the big Crown wrecks.**
2. It may not ask its own new question before Chapter 500, may not answer it in this volume, and no character may say the word *singular* of a person.
3. It may not open the drawer on the hill, may not take the piece of worked lead out of that building, may not enter the custody room in Movement 1, and may not put a digit of any of the three numbers in that room on any page.
4. It may not name who altered the ferry's manifest, at whose instance or with what intent; may not name the compartment; may not restate the compartment; may not make the ferry's documents into four; and may not reach the plate in eleven feet, measure it, touch it, ask about it or name it.
5. It may not enter the Chorus Diver stage, may not take the Keelwright stage again, and may not improve the Faultreader, Bellwright, Storm-Reader or Tide-Ear stages. **A stage in this volume is never taken by being named and never settles anything and never prices anything.**
6. It may not improve the harbor's poverty on any of its items, and it may not end in a victory that improves a price list. **The figure at the foot of the board in the front street does not come down, the mole-head light stays out, and there is still no flour in that market on the fiftieth day of this volume.**
7. It may not pay, advance, reduce, re-date or improve any standing debt, and it may not re-date the three marks to a man of Ashtide.
8. It may not fill the third column, appoint anybody to it, scratch the boy's hand out of the head of it, put his surname on a page, or put him on the water.
9. It may not put a minister in a room, may not put a man in a room who is only ever a name, a seal or an office, and may not make the Crown's man on the shelf a face, a plan or a speech. **He is a working man doing a job under a warrant and he is named.**
10. It may not kill anybody, may not name a body, and may not print a casualty figure; and it may not lose a sympathetic character to prove the protagonist's growth.
11. It may not let a description of a thing stand in for the thing without that being contested, recorded and paid for in the same scene.

**Eleven names stand at the foot of the sheet on the wall in the room over a fish market, and eleven people did not sign it, and nobody in that harbor can add the two figures together and get anything.** The fitting-out sheet carries a line for each contribution with a name and an hour, carries the two refusals as lines at the same size, **and carries no count of persons at all, so that the count of eleven is on the Assembly's sheet and is on no other sheet in this volume, and the two sheets are never counted together, never added, and never spoken of as one group.** A stranger with a pencil may put the two side by side and will find that one of them has a number of people on it and the other one has a number of shillings, and that is not a sum of anything.

### L31. THE DRAUGHT

**The volume's work is a descent. The draught in the trench is stated once and is the same on every day it is stated: the water comes up about two feet in the twenty minutes after a flood tide and does not go down again inside the working day.** No chapter of this volume puts anybody on the bottom of that trench in the wrong hour of the tide, and no chapter has a man walk into a place the water has just left and be surprised by it.

### L32. THE SOUND

**Sound in this volume is the first thing a diver loses and the first thing a machine takes.** A man who has been in a bell on that floor describes what he heard and the account is kept, and where two accounts of the same sound do not agree, both are kept and neither is settled, and **the count of accounts that do not agree with each other in this volume is added to the six in the last volume's leaf and is never settled, and the six are never settled either.**

### L33. THE WORKING DAY

**About a hundred and five minutes of ebb in twenty-four hours at that harbor and about two hours of usable it, and a company of seven boats and about thirty men is worth exactly that and no more. The tide gang is four men short. A person is the thing that costs and a day for a person has to be written on a paper and asked one man at a time.** No chapter of this volume sends thirty men out on a shift the harbor cannot pay for, and no chapter of this volume improves the tide gang.

### L34. THE APPARATUS, AND WHERE THE HOUSE FINDS ITS ESCALATION

**Each chapter carries the day's figures, the scene, a closing block, and the leaf's first and second columns in that yard's own mouth, and the second column takes five lines a day and is not checkable from a mole.** The house's own measurement places the escalation of this material in the apparatus, and this volume does not repair that and does not hide it: **the weight of a chapter is allowed to sit in the printed record, and a chapter that moves a person is allowed to move them in the columns, and the body is not required to be the whole of the effect.** Both definitions come out of the same mouth in the same words at every read-back.

### L35. THE SWEEPS A LATER PHASE MUST RUN AGAIN

**Across all fifty chapters and on all four places: the twelve named terms, the widened pass, the year, the second person, the quote parity, the italic fourth-column line, the book-structure forms, the separation of dates, the counted figures, the spelled arithmetic and the money. NOT ONE COUNTED FIGURE IN THIS VOLUME IS TO BE FOUND BY LOOKING.**

### L36. THE SENTENCES THAT MAY NOT BE SAID TWICE

**The six things an account has to carry. The five reasons the coin came out of a bag. The four heads and the four standing lines. The reason the fourth column took no line, entered once, on the twenty-eighth day of August.** **Each of these is said once in this volume and no paragraph of this volume restates one of them in different words, and where a restatement is wanted the wanted thing goes in the scene and not in the narration.**

### L37. THE ENDING OF THE VOLUME, AS IT MUST STAND

**Chapter 500 closes with: the proof in a case on a table in that harbor; a leaf read back with both definitions on the face of the reading; the four columns at two hundred and thirty-three, one thousand and thirty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, being the base of one hundred and eighty-three and seven hundred and eighty-nine at the close of Chapter 450 with fifty entries against the first column and two hundred and fifty against the second, and the second column's arithmetic printed on the face of the reading; the new question asked once and answered by nobody; a hole in a Tuesday; a man who will not hear out of one side until the fourth of December; and a machine that is still on the bottom with a Crown man's marks set around it and a chamber with a stove in it standing over the top of it.**

**THE FOUR FIGURES AT THE CLOSE OF CHAPTER 500, RE-DERIVABLE BY ANYBODY WITH A PENCIL: 183 + 50 = 233, 789 + 250 = 1,039, the third takes none, and the fourth takes none and stands at 125. At the hour of that day's reading the first is two hundred and thirty-two and the second is one thousand and thirty-seven, and both definitions are on the face of the reading.**
