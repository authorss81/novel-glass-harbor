## Central pressure

**The charter is a leash and not freedom.** The one-season provisional working charter granted in Morrow Quay's hearing room on Monday the 28th of November runs to the last day of March, is inside the harbor line, is bounded by the outer marks and by four hundred yards off them, and **can be suspended by the harbor authority at any time after an inspection, for cause.** It is not the Second Bell license, it does not lead to one, and no man in Morrow Quay can hand one out. The crew has work to do and no money to do it with, a notice it cannot lift closing eight seams of the town's own wall, a chain that may be pulled from under them by one man with a key and a book, and a Crown cutter at a cable's length off the mole with a claim in the hearing record and a tender on the seaward end of a run of glass.

The volume's job is to make the leash felt in the bodies of people who are not in the yard — a herring skipper with a berth out of service, a mooring on the ninth seam that cannot be worked, a coast office that answered a letter with the word *capacity*, nineteen winters of dues that are disputed and unpaid, and a town with one berth short on the first of March — **and to end with the crew holding a provisional writ that comes with a reporting clause attached, which is a longer leash with a name on it.**

The license is not earned in this volume. What is earned is the right to keep working, at a price, in a place that is poor and busy and short of a hard.

## Starting state

**Five marks in a bag and nothing in the tin, and nothing has been put into either since the first of December.** That is the whole of the yard's liquid position at the volume's first page, and it is the state every figure in this volume is measured against. Also standing at the opening: a pump with a cracked housing and no new one to be had before the spring; a boat on the hard with her bilge blocked in and no cradle; a chandler's note written up in the chandler's own book as *eleven weeks into six* while the yard's own count says thirteen, with **six marks put on it in three days in November and none since, and the chandler has never been asked to write a new figure and neither has Calder**; forty marks owed to Bevin Rook and forty owed to Mara Venn, none of it paid; a stack of shoal iron worth twenty marks at a fair sale with a saw kerf in one crown shackle, a third of the sale entered to another crew to be paid in February, and the whole stack entered as the charter's bond at a clerk's thirteen to the crew.

Calder Marr is thirty-two, a line diver and an unstable tide-ear, off the bottom except on dates the reeve names, and **the public duplicate hangs on the market wall and the crew's book has a hundred and forty entries in it and the two custody numbers are still not quoted.** The bell-buoy core is hung on the town's own ground and answers the harbor's tide bell through the ground a hand's width of a second late. The three custody items do not come back. The reason for the seizure of the fourteenth of October is on no paper this harbor has ever been handed.

## Starting relationships

**Calder and Mara** are two people who have both refused to be useful to each other and have kept the refusal on the record. He told her what the Oren mark is and she wrote none of it and will write none, and on the twenty-sixth of December she refused out loud, in the open, to say the thing she has believed since the fourteenth of October, because a thing she said would make her a witness. He does not thank her. She does not want to be thanked. **On the fourth of January her standing goes and she becomes a trade rather than an office**, and the professional spine of the relationship changes from *can this office be made to hold a line* to *can two people who can both refuse still work together when neither of them can refuse anything any more.*

**Calder and Kest** have spent authority four times in one month and every one of the four cost somebody other than the yard a berth, a rope, or a hearing. Kest has said out loud in the open air that he may suspend the charter, that he has no cause, and that he will not. **That promise is now a debt with a date on it**, and this volume is the first one in which the reeve's own spending can be spent against him.

**Calder and Bevin**: the boy is in the water, is owed forty marks, is not being paid, and has a hand that will be a boat hand in June and is not one now. He reads the book. He is the only person in that yard who can be relied on to say the thing nobody asked for.

**Calder and Alden**: a man of sixty-one at the far bench of the same shed who has not spoken to anybody since the thirteenth of November and is not asked for anything. **He does not confess in this volume.** The partial confession begins in Volume 9 and the original repair log is handed over in Volume 15. The plate note closes in Volume 4 with the removed file page, and this volume must not open it.

**Nessa Pike** holds the tender's cleat, the pump, the fall, the board and the slate, and the standing shutdown, and has now overruled Calder five times and won five times. **The fifth cost her the count of ten printed copies signed in her own hand.** She is the only person in that yard who can be trusted to do it a sixth time.

## Starting power level

**Line Diver, with an unstable tide-ear capacity used twice and a first permanent memory gone.** The first entry was the thirteenth of October, eleven minutes forty inside, surfaced on the tender's word, costing a rope-burn reenactment, continuous tinnitus with a second sound a third of a second late, and emotional residue strong enough to answer a person in Oren's call mark without deciding to. The second was the eighth of December, eleven minutes inside a limit of forty, under the same six conditions read aloud, and it cost him the lift at the end of a childhood call: **not muffled, not forgotten, absent, and no person in Morrow Quay can restore it and none will be asked.** He carries a practice trace from a loading space. He is off the bottom except on named dates.

- **Morrow Quay harbor authority (reeve's office)**: issues claim windows, work permits and one-season provisional working charters, can order a wreck held, **can suspend the charter after an inspection for cause**, and cannot overrule a Registry seizure

## Escalation sequence

Five movements. Each resolves a local problem and creates a larger one. Chapter ranges are the writing schedule's boundaries and the movements are written to them.

### Movement 1, the leash opens (Chapters 51–60)

The first of March is a Wednesday and is **eight weeks** off on the fourth of January, and the yard has five marks. Mara Venn's query comes off her file on **Wednesday the fourth of January** and a conservator with nothing left to refuse is a conservator who is expensive to employ. The circuit office's next request arrives, **worded so that a refusal is a delay.** Ordinary paid work inside the line under the charter pays badly and there is more of it than there is money for. The crew goes out on **Thursday the nineteenth of January** at the mole's toe with a lead and a slate and the bell hung, and traces the near end of the run **inland, under the town**, where the harbor authority has no chart because the town was built on it. **A body**: a real injury and a real cost, on a working crew, with Bevin's hand a boat hand in June and not a boat hand now. And the going out in the weather begins, because somebody has to look after a hung bell, and the bellwright is named in the chapter that does it.

**Turn:** the run is not a road out to sea. It goes under the town, and the office that would have to rule on it has no instrument for ruling on it.

### Movement 2, weather and the capacity of a harbor (Chapters 61–70)

Three storm-exposed recoveries that have to be funded before they can be attempted, a supply problem that takes two chapters to be understood and one to be paid for, a rival crew, and the first of March arriving as a **date with forty boats in it.** The charter is used the way a leash is used: for the harbor's benefit as much as the crew's, and the difference is priced in somebody else's winter.

**Turn:** the coast office's word *capacity* is answered, and the answer is a document the yard did not write and cannot fix.

### Movement 3, the paper that asks for the book (Chapters 71–80)

The circuit office's request is answered and re-asked. Naval confiscation of a working boat happens in daylight and is done with a form. The crew's own public record is turned against it in a room it cannot leave, and the ten copies that were the yard's only leverage become the thing a crew is asked to hand back.

**Turn:** the yard learns that a public record is a thing other people can now use, including against the yard, and cannot become private again without losing the only protection it has.

### Movement 4, the forged seal (Chapters 81–90)

**The volume's midpoint.** A fisher's account of a coast road that no current chart includes. Mara Venn proves the claim seal on it is forged — a material and institutional finding, in her own competence, without a diver and without a ship — **and cannot explain who made the glass or why the road is not on any chart.** The crew is on the wrong side of a document that is competent and false, which is the volume's argument about certification turned inside out.

**Turn:** the seal is forged and the witness is real, and a record can be a lie about provenance and a true account of a road, and the two facts have to be carried together.

### Movement 5, the rescue and the writ (Chapters 91–100)

**The climax.** Calder abandons a valuable official route to rescue a trapped fishing crew during a glass storm. The rescue proves his local reliability and **destroys the equipment needed to claim a legal salvage award**, so that the thing which makes him trustworthy in Morrow Quay is the thing that costs him in Veyr. The crew earns a provisional writ through a public hearing **and the Crown attaches a reporting clause to it**, and the missing coast road becomes evidence of a concealed network.

**Resolution:** a longer leash with a name on it, a boy whose wages begin to be paid out of somebody else's pocket, and a question about charts.

### Costs this volume may not waive

- **Money.** Five marks in a bag at the opening and nothing in the tin. Air, rope, food, pump hire, the chandler's note, wages, a replacement for the cracked housing, and the harbor's eighth and share off anything raised. **The note must be re-written or it must stay unwritten, and either is a scene.** A poor crew does not get rescued and a charter is not money.
- **Body.** At least one injury to a working crew member with a real cost, one bad descent, and Calder's tide-sickness continuing. **No volume may let the tinnitus vanish silently; name it once in this volume.**
- **Standing.** A custody object does not return. A named plan is refused again. Somebody on the harbor's own ground loses a berth, a rope, a warp, or a hearing as a direct result of something the yard asked for.
- **Time.** Every recovery spends a safe diving day the crew cannot replace, so every later dive is worse weather or worse gear.
- **Labor stays visible.** The forty marks twice; the first four weeks of wages falling in January and February; **Sella Brigg's third in February**; the withdrawn cover; the note nobody has re-written; the cracked pump; the *Kittiwake* on the hard; the harbor's crane and barge, which the harbor pays for and the yard does not; **the north berth and the ninth seam out of service**; **Elias Voke's statement of capacity**; the hard at Ashtide. A charter that pays badly is still a charter that pays.

## What this volume must not do

- **Do not let Calder become a faultreader.** No testing an object for one with an answer, and no chapter in which he looks at a thing and is told what it means. **The two things found on the plate on the sixth of December — a dressed aperture, and a fouling that gives no hour — were not seam work and must not be re-read as seam work.** The word *seam* came from Mara Venn's mouth and not from his.
- **Do not re-use the compartment as a reveal.** The Volume 1 midpoint is spent. The Volume 2 midpoint is a fisher's account of a coast road and a forged claim seal.
- **Do not give Calder the full Second Bell license.** Losing eligibility for the examination is a Volume 4 cost.
- **Do not let Mara Venn certify truth, enter an echo, or become Calder's assistant.** Her competence must act on its own. Her break with the Registry is Volume 4 and **may not happen in this volume**, however badly she is treated.
- **Do not let the crew keep any custody object.** None of the three returns.
- **Do not let a concealment be told a second time for nothing.** A secret may be told once, to one person, in a private scene, at a price. **The volume must close with the Oren mark still hidden from every office, from Kest, and from his father**, and the concealment's three parts stand: the false line on the unverified slate, the boy with the book who will not write what came out of a man's mouth and was not asked, and the woman with no standing left who knows what is in the anchor and has never been asked to say it.
- **Do not name the Nine Harbors, the Quietening or the Crown Seal.** **The nine-line mark is not explained.** It is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction that is not a claim seal and not a mark of consent, and no office in Veyr can interpret it, and it closes in Volume 5.
- **Do not let Alden Marr confess.** The partial confession begins in Volume 9 and the original repair log is handed over in Volume 15. The plate note closes in Volume 4 with the removed file page.
- **Do not resolve the answer inside the delayed note.** **It is reserved to the Volume 15 midpoint.** The call in it on the twenty-sixth of December is a grown man's, unhurried, further off, saying a thing to somebody who is not answering, and it went out into the water and did not come back. **Nobody in Morrow Quay or Veyr can place it, and Bevin Rook's book is never going to resolve it.**
- **Do not introduce a second and speculative batch file.** The cards for Chapters 51–60 are `outline/batches/volume-02-batch-0001.md` and no other Volume 2 batch file exists.
- **Do not write a game-style interface.** No panels, stat blocks, character sheets, status messages, floating text, quest text, skill trees, level-ups, or software vocabulary. The only message-like elements are claim seals, Registry notations, bell marks, written limits, gauges, the reeve's chart, the hearing room's slate wall, the crew's book, and physical warnings.

## Jurisdiction inside this volume

The harbor line runs along the mole and the inner shoals. The **imperial line** lies beyond it, and working past it needs the full Second Bell license, which this volume does not grant and does not approach. Every dive therefore happens inside the harbor line under a named instrument the harbor authority issues: the **one-season provisional working charter** for general work on the authority's own ground, a **claim window** for a specific object and date, and a **work permit** naming a site and dates on a bond. The charter is wider than anything the yard has held and it is revocable after an inspection.

**Ordinary harbor work landward of the third seam is not inside the closure.** The notice of the thirty-first of October closes the mole's toe and inner face from the third seam to the eleventh and does not reach the twelfth, the dump, or water, and **no notice has ever closed water.** A crew the notice bars from the authority's work may still take private work on harbor ground inside the line, and the third clause catches only the authority's work. **This is the only reason the yard has money at all and it stays true in this volume.**

**The notice is not the harbor authority's to lift.** Two proper plans for the fifth seam are owed to Volumes 2 and 3, and the mechanism by which the fifth-seam plate is ever reached is **a change of politics in an office, not a better argument from a shed.** A plan written and lodged is the correct form and it will be refused again, correctly, and the refusal has to cost something.

## Midpoint reversal

**A fisher's account of a coast road that no current chart includes, and a claim seal Mara Venn proves is forged.**

Around the volume's midpoint, in the middle of Movement 4, a working fisher describes a road along a coast that no current chart of this coast includes — a line of marks, a bottom, a way of getting in out of weather that he has used and that the authority's own soundings do not carry. He is not a mystic and he is not lying about the water. The account is physically checkable and the crew checks part of it. It arrives attached to a piece of worked glass bearing a **claim seal**, and **Mara Venn proves the seal is forged** — a material and institutional finding, in her own competence, without a diver and without a ship. She can say what a forged seal is. **She cannot say who made the glass, and she says so in her own words before anybody asks.**

**This is the reversal because it turns the volume's competence against it.** A record can be a lie about provenance and a true account of a road, and the two facts have to be carried together, and a conservator whose entire value is that she can tell a mark from a forgery now has to say *I cannot explain this glass* in a room where the glass is the only evidence.

**It is not the compartment and the compartment must not be re-used as a reveal.** It is a different object, a different document, a different trade, and a different failure.

## Volume climax

**Calder abandons a valuable official route to rescue a trapped fishing crew during a glass storm.**

The choice is the volume's whole argument about usefulness: the official route is the thing that would have paid, and the fishing crew is the thing that is in the water. He goes to the crew. **The rescue proves his local reliability in Morrow Quay and destroys the equipment needed to claim a legal salvage award**, so the act that makes him trustworthy at home is the act that costs him in Veyr, and both are true and neither cancels the other. The bell-buoy core's own ground answers during the storm, at the delay, and nobody can say what is in the note.

## Concrete resolution

**The crew earns a provisional writ through a public hearing, and the Crown attaches a reporting clause to it.** A writ with a reporting clause is a longer leash with a name on it, and the yard takes it, because a yard with no paper cannot work and a yard with a reporting clause knows what it has agreed to. The missing coast road becomes evidence of a concealed network, entered on a public wall in the yard's own hand with the limits written inside the rules and six copies carried out by hand to the crews that keep the ground.

Also paid: **a Kest payment that costs somebody who is not the yard** — a berth, a rope, a warp or a hearing — because that is the reeve's established pattern and the volume must not soften it; the first four weeks of Bevin Rook's wages out of somebody else's pocket, at least in part, and the yard's own debt to Mara Venn **not** paid; the chandler's note either re-written or still not re-written, said out loud.

## New-volume question

**Who edits the sea charts, and why do all the missing roads converge on Orison?**

Opened by the coast road that is not on any chart and by the fact that a road with no chart is a road somebody has had removed from one. Not answered. **The nine final routes are named in the bible and none of them may be named in the prose of this volume**, and the phrase *Orison* may be spoken only as a place seventy fathoms down.

## Final chapter image

At the first of March, a Wednesday, in a harbor with one herring berth short and a mooring on the ninth seam out of service and nineteen winters of dues still disputed, **a writ with a reporting clause is read into a hearing record and a coast road that no chart carries is read onto a market wall beneath a naval claim, and the bell answers the harbor's tide bell through the ground a hand's width of a second late, and a person goes out in a boat in the weather to look after it because that is the whole of the craft and the one nobody wants.** The boy with the book writes the hour and the state of the water and the delay, and reads it back twice, and the line says *no source* and *not written as a voice*, because that is the only honest form of the line and it is the line.

## Planned relationship and power change

**Calder and Mara** move from two people who refuse each other to two people who have refused each other in public and keep working. **Her break is Volume 4 and not before.** He learns in this volume that a conservator with no standing is a conservator he cannot spend, and that asking her to certify something after the fourth of January is asking her to be useful rather than right, and that is the difference he has been making with her for four months.

**Calder and Kest** reach the point where the reeve's own spending can be spent against him, and the volume resolves it with a reeve who is not a hero and who chose to tell the truth only after understanding what silence had cost. **He does not become a friend. He becomes a man whose promises have dates on them.**

**Calder and Bevin** move from a boy who is owed forty marks to a boy whose claim is being paid out of somebody else's pocket in part, and who still cannot close his hand, and who is still the only person in the yard who can be relied on to say the thing nobody asked for.

**Calder and Alden** do not move. A man at a far bench who is not asked for anything, and Calder does not go. **That not-going is the relationship and it is not a plot point and it is not paid off in this volume.**

**Power: unchanged in rank, changed in method.** Calder is still a line diver with an unstable tide-ear capacity and **he is not a faultreader and does not become one.** What changes is that he anchors to a schedule and a tender and a fixed limit and a stated reason, and that he does it without being the only person in the room who knows why. **A formal Bellwright stage, Keelwright stage or Chorus Diver stage is not taken in this volume.** What the volume pays is the maintenance of one hung bell, on the ground, in the weather, by a person with a name.

## Antagonist movement in this volume

1. **Ors Kest** enforces the charter, can suspend it, and spends authority that costs other people. He is a pressured local official with a date on his promises and is not a villain.
2. **Captain Veyra Holt** is the active antagonist's ship and signature. **She may be named, may sign, may send a paper, and is described from a distance only. She does not appear in a room in this volume and no face-to-face meeting occurs.**
3. **The circuit office** is the institutional pressure inside the story: a request worded so that a refusal is a delay, a custody log re-entered with no reason, a coastal office that answers with a question.
4. **Magister Ilya Serr appears as name, seal and office only**, always as *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner* on a seal line, and **never in a room, through Volumes 1–8.** No minister, no ministry naming, no room presence, and no Gannet-lie reveal. The office of Returning Memory is not named.
5. **Magistrate Halven Rook** is the circuit's magistrate and the Volume 4–6 institutional antagonist. **He may be named on paper and must not appear in a room.**
6. **No new antagonist, no new institution, no new final enemy.**

## Reveal discipline for Volume 2

Volume 2 may establish the existence of a coast road that no current chart includes, the fact that a claim seal on this coast can be forged and competent, the *Vigil's* claim as a live instrument rather than a lodgment, and the first hint that missing roads converge. It **must not** explain the Quietening, identify the Crown Seal, name the Nine Harbors as a compact, explain the builder's mark of nine joined lines, prove who altered the ferry manifest, reveal what the sealed glass case contained, name the Office of Returning Memory, or put a senior examiner in a room. Every later answer must be earned by a physical artifact, a conflicting testimony, a labor-based expedition, or a political document whose limitations the characters understand.

**The clear opening in the coaming is three foot nine by two foot four. The space is eight foot four by six foot nine clear and eleven feet two deep under the grating. The sealed case is seven to eight feet in its longest face. The hatch is the only opening. The harbor's own thirteen-year-old file gives the space as *grating in place, not cleared, contents not removed*.** Therefore **the space cannot have been cleared**, and a paper saying the hold stood empty at the moment of the seizure is not describing the space at that moment. Three steps, in that order, and **any later volume that restates the finding restates all three.** **No chapter may state or imply that the sealed case was in standing space number four.** The three Gannet documents — the harbor salvage report, the Navy seizure report, the Registry custody log — stay three documents.

### L7. The ferry, the mole and the ground.

**The *Aster Gannet's* wreck lies in pieces under and beside the south end of the mole, and the mole was built on her in the year she went down**, on the harbor authority's own account of its own works, *the foreshore at the south end being mixed, containing iron and timber*. This is **the only lawful ground on which the compartment could be measured inside the harbor line.** No custody object returns. Nothing on claimed ground is touched. The *Gannet's* ferry iron and the transparent keel are two different objects and are never fused.

### L8. The bell, which is not an instrument.

**Hung on Sunday the 25th of December** on harbor gear in eight and a half feet off the south end, with Kest's chart notation that has never been taken off: *maintained by the Free Keel yard, not a mark and not a light and not a danger, a signal, kept by hand, at the cost stated in this office's hearing record of the twenty-eighth of November.* **It reads nothing. It settles nothing. It says no word.** It answers the harbor's tide bell through the ground a hand's width of a second late, and it answers because four pieces of worked glass are leaded into its waist, and **nobody on this coast knows why and nobody claims to.** Fouled waist, tongue half worn through, a year's rope. **A new tongue is a day's work every four or five years for as long as that bell hangs, and the whole of the craft is the going out: a person, in a boat, in the weather.** A bell that is not looked after stops answering and starts being a rock. **It may never become an instrument that answers a question.**

### L9. Custody and what does not come back.

**Three items in the Morrow circuit custody room under three numbers, digits not quoted, and none of them returns.** A supervised look was answered no, or heavily conditional: the six-clause plan for the fifth seam returned uncountersigned on four grounds, the first of which is that the office will not countersign an instrument putting a person inside the closure its own notice created. **The four-hundred-yard bar off the outer marks stands.** The reeve's six-clause instrument of the third of November is conditional, its condition has failed, and it is held open at his pleasure. **The municipal claim on the third item cannot be withdrawn without a request, and the yard will not make one.** The custody log re-entry of the twentieth of December gives **no reason**, and **no paper in this harbor or in the circuit office's copying room states what a municipal claim does to a presumed Crown material**, and the *Vigil* is the second office in this harbor that has never been told. **That sentence is a live instrument and may not be answered by a clerk.**

### L10. Who may appear, and how.

**Ilya Serr appears as name, seal and office only**, always as *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner* on a seal line, and **never in a room, through Volumes 1–8.** No minister, no ministry naming, no room presence, no Gannet-lie reveal. **Halven Rook** is the circuit's magistrate and the Volume 4–6 institutional antagonist; **he may be named on paper and must not appear in a room.** **Captain Veyra Holt** may be named, may sign, may send a paper, and is described from a distance only. **Captain Holt's posting after Volume 3 and the channel by which a disgraced officer reaches a Morrow Quay reeve are Volume 03's decision and may not be invented here.**

### L11. The Crown's next move, and the shape of Volume 2's midpoint.

**The Crown's next move is a request from the circuit office worded so that a refusal is a delay.** It is the instrument of **Mara Venn's Volume 4 break, which may not happen in Volume 2.** **The Volume 2 midpoint is a fisher's account of a coast road that no current chart includes, and a claim seal Mara Venn proves is forged.** It is not the compartment and the compartment must not be re-used as a reveal.

### L12. The nine-line mark, and what is not explained.

**The builder's mark of nine joined lines is not explained in this volume.** It is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction, it is not a claim seal and not a mark of consent, four books in the Registry list every mark a Serein office may strike into glass and it is in none of them, and no office in Veyr can say what it means. **Do not name the Nine Harbors, the Quietening, or the Crown Seal.** The mark closes in Volume 5.

### L13. Calder does not become a faultreader.

**No testing an object for one with an answer.** No scene in which he looks at a thing and is reliably told what it means. **The two things found on the plate on the sixth of December — a dressed aperture, and a fouling that gives no hour — were not seam work and must not be re-read as seam work.** The word *seam* came from Mara Venn's mouth and not from his, and he has not claimed to read one.

### L14. The answer inside the note is reserved.

**The answer inside the delayed note is reserved to the Volume 15 midpoint.** The call in it on the twenty-sixth of December is a grown man's, unhurried, further off, saying a thing to somebody who is not answering, and it went out into the water and it did not come back. **No man in Morrow Quay, no man in Veyr and no woman in a Registry coat with four *not stated* entries against one of them can say whose it is, and it is not a boy's and it is not his.** Nobody may name it in this volume.

### L15. Labor stays visible, and it is the volume's spine.

The forty marks twice; the first four weeks of wages falling in January and February; **Sella Brigg's third of the iron, in February**; the *Low Lantern's* rescue cover withdrawn for the winter on her own slate and Harl Dace's cover the only cover the yard has; the chandler's note nobody has re-written; the cracked pump and no new housing before the spring; **the *Kittiwake* on the hard with her bilge blocked in and no cradle**; **the harbor's crane and the harbor's barge, which the harbor pays for and the yard does not**; **the north berth out of service and the ninth seam's mooring out of service**; **Elias Voke's statement of the authority's capacity to moor the fleet, which he has offered to write himself for nothing and in which he will put the north berth, the ninth seam and the boy's name**; the hard at Ashtide, unanswered. **A poor crew does not get rescued and a charter is not money.**

### L16. Alden Marr does not confess.

**The partial confession begins in Volume 9 and the original repair log is handed over in Volume 15.** **The plate note closes Volume 4 with the removed file page.** He is at the far bench, is not asked for anything, and Calder does not go. **No fragment, no report talk, no second story, in this volume.**

### L17. The bellwright is named here, at the end of Movement 1.

He has been the harbor's own bellwright since the twentieth of October, about fifty, with a blacksmith's forearms, and he has never been asked a question by anybody in the yard. **He is named in Volume 02, in Movement 1, in the chapter that carries the first of the going out — the chapter in which somebody goes out in a boat in the weather to look after a hung bell, or the one immediately before it — and the name is spoken in a person's mouth and not written in a narrator's list.** **He teaches nobody.** He is a tradesman with a day's work every four or five years and an opinion about fouling, and he is the harbor's bellwright and not the Free Keel's. He is not a Stage 5 mentor; the formal Bellwright stage is Volume 5 and requires a crew, and Volume 1's lock against a convenient master applies to every later volume.

### L18. Volume 2 must not take a formal power stage.

**No Bellwright, Keelwright, Storm-Reader, Chorus Diver or Openhand stage is taken in this volume.** What is paid is the maintenance of one hung bell, on the ground, in the weather, by a person with a name. Calder's growth in this volume is method, not rank: he anchors to a schedule, a tender, a fixed limit and a stated reason, and he does it without being the only person in the room who knows why.

### L19. The first echo's witness stays where he is.

Unnamed, unlocated, uninterviewed, and not to be resolved by a convenient anonymous letter or a surviving crew list. The prose gives him a body — the smallest of four men, near end of the case, a lead tag with the first two figures and not the third — and gives Calder the sound of his own voice saying *I'll take the near end, I'm smallest*. **The volume that first needs him decides whether he is findable and what finding him would cost, and that volume is not Volume 2.**

### L20. Sera Mott's half, and who is left to go out.

*There was more of them out there. This one has been heard answered from the westward, and always late. The ones that answered from the westward were kept, and somebody went out in a boat to keep them, and the ones she knows about stopped, because the men who kept them stopped going out, and the men stopped because there was nobody on the far banks by the time anybody asked.* **It is a road with nobody on it and not a mystery.** She has been asked by archivists and has said no and believed them, and says she will say no one more time in about two years. **Who is left to go out is Volume 11's argument in embryo; the first half of the answer is Volume 5.** She may be asked once more in this volume and may say no again, and **nobody may have her name entered in a record without her asking for it.**

### L21. The first of March, and the harbor's own calendar.

**The first of March is a Wednesday.** One herring berth is short, the ninth seam's mooring is out of service, the north berth is out of service, and nineteen winters of dues are disputed and unpaid. **The charter runs to the last day of March and must be renewed, and it may be suspended after an inspection, for cause, by one man with a key and a book.** The harbor authority's clerk **wipes the hearing room's slate wall every Monday at eight** whether there is a sitting or not; the wall is a convenience and the ledger is the record, and a figure on the wall survives one week.

### L22. Length and shape, carried from Batch 0005's finding.

**Aim at 2,200–3,200 words a chapter.** **No chapter is two rooms.** Alternate the pressure types deliberately: local work, relationship pressure, training, investigation, political or institutional pressure, the larger mystery, recovery, and the volume climax and resolution, in roughly that order and adapted to this volume. **A chapter that wants to be a hearing room *and* a market is split across two days with a body or a market or a boy in one of them.** Ten speeches are not ten chapters. **Where a scene will not compress, let it run rather than drop a cost the crew pays** — and note that a chapter which runs long because it is one complete room is a different fault from a chapter which runs long because it is two.

### L23. The two custody numbers, and what may not be quoted.

The two numbers the courier wrote on the back of the second sheet are deliberately not quoted in the prose. **They may be given digits when a later volume needs them on the page, and must not be invented retroactively.** The third number is the fragment's, and the digits of all three are still not spoken in a hearing room.

### L24. The notice's own closure, and the reeve who works inside it. Added on the Batch 0001 review, 26 September 2026.

**The first clause of the notice of the thirty-first closes the mole's toe and inner face from the third seam to the eleventh to all work and all approach by all persons, "which includes this office, this yard, and any other crew in this harbor," and the office's own moorings and fenders on that ground are not to be worked.** The bedded seam in the mole's own foundation at the third and fourth seams is inside that closure, and the first point, the ninth seam's fouling, and the run under the town are all inside it. **The harbor authority has no power to lift that notice.**

**What the chapters of Movement 1 established is that the harbor authority has done it anyway, twice, knowingly, and has entered the fact in its own book in its own hand.** The permit of the eighth of January puts a man inside the closure; the reason entered on the twelfth of January says so in its own sentence, and Calder Marr puts the reeve's own November ground — that this office will not put a person inside a closure its own notice created — to him in the room, and Kest orders the clause written on the sheet and read back unsoftened. The claim window of the seventeenth names ground the same notice closes, and Kest says so in his own voice in his barge and says he wants the yard to be holding it as well as he is. The line Kest writes under the reason on the nineteenth says it a third time and says a man in Veyr will find two such entries in one ledger.

**Binding on every later chapter of this volume: any instrument of the harbor authority that puts a person, a boat or a lead inside that closure must be accompanied, on the page and in the record, by the reeve's acknowledgement that his office has no power to lift the notice and is acting inside it. A chapter may not treat the closure as lifted, narrowed, or irrelevant, and may not have the yard work that ground without that acknowledgement. The cost of the acknowledgement is Kest's alone and it is never free.**
