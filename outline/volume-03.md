## Central pressure

**Reconstructing the old fleet route becomes a race against rival salvage crews and a naval cutter that wants the map erased.**

The Free Keel yard of Morrow Quay has a paper at last and the paper is a leash: **a provisional writ of salvage working, granted on Monday the first of May, running to the first of March next, with four names on it and a Crown reporting clause on it that runs a four-day clock and lets the harbor authority seat one of two disputants and take what a person says as it stands.** There is no charter. There is no second season, and the writ's own last line says the second season is not the harbor authority's to give and will arrive out of a city from a man who has never been inside this harbor. There is **no air in this harbor**, no compressor gland, no delivery hose, a hand lead's line made badly at night, thirteen marks in a bag, twenty-three on a creditor's page, forty marks owed to a boy and forty to a conservator and eight shillings and tenpence to a woman, and a boy who is to be off the stone on the fifteenth of May whatever his own arithmetic says.

**The volume's job is to make the map cost what a map costs.** A route that no chart carries is the only thing this coast has that an empire cannot simply take away, and the moment a crew of three and a half can start drawing one, three other people want it: a rival crew with better gear, a coast office that answers with a question, and a cutter with a claim in a hearing record and an officer in the road who wants the sheet that says what is under the town. **And the volume's job is to end with the route public, which means the yard loses the only advantage it ever had, which is a thing nobody else knew.**

**The faultreader stage is not earned by a dive in this volume's first half and may not be bought.** It is taken late, badly, at a price, and Calder Marr is worse at it afterwards than he was before, because a seam identifies difference and not truth and Volume 03 is the volume in which that sentence costs somebody.

## Escalation sequence

Five movements. Each resolves a local problem and creates a larger one. Chapter ranges are the writing schedule's boundaries and the movements are written to them.

### Movement 1, the first return and the first bill (Chapters 101–110)

The writ's four-day clock runs for the first time and the yard discovers what a return costs: **the return was due on the second of May, the finding was the twenty-eighth of April, and the yard makes it on the fourth and says on the page that it is two days late** — a finding it must report, a finding it will not characterize, and a man who is not one of the four names asking to be one. **The boy goes off the stone on Monday the fifteenth of May, on a date the yard named in a room in front of a clerk of the circuit office, and the yard loses its only reader.** The work has to be paid for: no air, no gland, no hose, and a chandler's page at twenty-three. **The eighth-clause plan for the fifth seam, lodged on the seventeenth of January and never answered, is answered in this movement — refused, correctly, and the refusal costs somebody.** And a rival crew that is not a villain puts the yard on notice that a road no chart carries is a thing a man can sell.

**Turn:** the yard learns that a reporting clause is not a tax on findings, it is a price on **the yard's own silence**, and that the first thing the yard will be made to report is a thing it cannot report without saying what it does not know.

### Movement 2, weather and the work that cannot be sold (Chapters 111–120)

Three recoveries that have to be funded before they can be attempted and cannot all be funded. **A glass storm, with no cause named for it, and a man of fifty-one with a bad wheel in it.** Saltmarch entered from a distance and a pilot who keeps a channel by hand and wants nothing from a diver. The town pays in the only currency it has: a mooring, a berth, a warp, a boy's summer, a cistern charged on a rota that a man does not walk.

**Turn:** the yard is asked to sign something that would make a route usable by strangers, and refuses, and finds out that a route nobody else can use is not a route.

### Movement 3, the chart and the two men who want it (Chapters 121–130)

The coast office answers. The cutter asks for the harbor authority's chart a second time. **The reeve is offered something, or threatened with something, and the price is named in front of the room and paid in somebody else's winter.** A man in a city reads a writ with a reporting clause on it and writes back a question about a bar of gravel.

**Turn:** a public route is a thing other people can now use, and one of the things they can do with it is take it, and **the yard's own eleven copies and six hand-copies become the reason a rival crew's map looks better than the yard's.**

**RESERVED TO THIS MOVEMENT AND NOT TO BE SPENT EARLIER: the coast office answers, and a man in a city reads a writ with a reporting clause on it and writes back a question about a bar of gravel.** A man in a market in Movement 1 or 2 may ask who edits the charts, may be a man of this coast, and may want to buy a copy of a sheet; **he may not produce, quote, or refer to a document that has come back out of a city, and no chapter before the hundred and twenty-first may put the coast office's answer on the page.** The circuit office's own fourth request of the sixteenth of March is a different paper and may be referred to at any time.

### Movement 4, what the glass is joined to (Chapters 131–140)

**The volume's midpoint.** A physical join, found by labor and not by an argument: a place where a keel-line ran between two things that were never made to be joined, with a witness who was not coerced and a joiner who was. **Mara Venn proves something about the join and cannot say who made it, and Calder Marr reads a seam for the first time and gets it wrong in front of people who trusted him.** The vessels are not a scatter and not a single thing: they stand in a chain that nine communities kept in some way, and the memories in them are civic records — a fisher's storm, a child's evacuation, a builder's account of a wall.

**Turn:** the fleet's formation is legible, and it is legible as a **public** fact, and the yard cannot unread it and cannot make it private again, and Mara Venn says the sentence that ends her time on the crew.

### Movement 5, the cutter and the bell-archive (Chapters 141–150)

**The climax.** The *Vigil* moves on the crew while Calder Marr is trying to raise the *Hearthwake*, a working vessel of one of the nine communities with a bell-archive in her that is the only local copy of a community's own account of itself. **He saves the vessel's living crew and loses the chance to carry the full bell-archive to the surface, and the two are the same choice and neither cancels the other.** The rescued crew testify about their community's part in the old Compact, in their own words, on their own terms, with a limit written on it.

**Resolution:** a longer leash with a name on it, a boy who cannot do the thing he was kept for, a conservator who is not on the crew any more, and a question about who changed a rule.

At least one injury to a working crew member with a real cost and a real bill, and one bad descent, and the tide-sickness continuing. **The condition is named once in this volume and no more, and it is not cured and it is not a debt that may be paid off quietly.**

## What this volume must not do

- **Do not let Calder become a faultreader who is told what things mean.** A seam is a physical or perceptual break where incompatible records have been joined. It **identifies difference, not truth.** The stage is taken late in this volume, it is taken badly, **it may not be used to settle an argument, to prove a motive, or to convict anybody of anything, and a Volume 03 chapter may not have a seam tell Calder what happened.** Calder's growth this volume is method and consequence, not perception.
- **Do not re-use the compartment as a reveal.** The Volume 1 midpoint is spent. The Volume 2 midpoint is a fisher's account of a coast road and a forged claim seal. **The Volume 3 midpoint is a physical join and a civic chain.**
- **Do not give Calder the full Second Bell license.** Losing eligibility for the examination is a Volume 4 cost and the writ is expressly not it.
- **Do not let Mara Venn certify truth, enter an echo, or become Calder's assistant.** **She leaves the active crew in this volume and the Registry in Volume 4, and the two are different events.** Her competence must act on its own. She is expensive and right and she does not break in this volume.
- **Do not let the crew keep any custody object.** None of the three returns.
- **Do not let a concealment be told a second time for nothing.** **A concealment may be told once, to one person, in a private scene, at a price. It was told once, in Chapter 38. The volume closes with the Oren mark still hidden from every office, from Kest and from his father.**
- **Do not name the Nine Harbors as a compact, the Quietening, or the Crown Seal.** The nine-line mark is not explained. **The nine final routes are named in the bible and none of them may be named in the prose.** The mark closes in Volume 5.
- **Do not let Alden Marr confess.** The partial confession begins in Volume 9, the original repair log is handed over in Volume 15, and the plate note closes in Volume 4 with the removed file page.
- **Do not resolve the answer inside the delayed note.** It is reserved to the Volume 15 midpoint. **Eleven logged occasions since the twenty-sixth of October and two empty answers, and neither count may be advanced by arithmetic or by a measurement.**
- **Do not name the coast road a network.** The word was used once, by a rival, and refused in public. **The road may be described, published, doubted, half-believed and argued about. It may not be explained, it may not be connected to anything under the town, and the two may never be fused.** Orison may be spoken of only as a place seventy fathoms down.
- **Do not explain what is under the town, name it, or send anybody after it at night.** It is a bearing and a bottom and nothing else. **Nine and a quarter feet is the *Aster Gannet's* ferry plate in a coaming beyond the eleventh seam and is a different object at a different part of the mole, and the two may never be fused.**
- **Do not open the lead plug's number.** It is in a chest in a shed, in no office, in no book, and the bellwright has said he will say it in a room if somebody asks him in a room and not before. **It is not a custody object, it is not one of the three items, and it is not connected to the ferry plate or to anything else in this harbor.**
- **Do not let the bell become an instrument.** It reads nothing, settles nothing and says no word. Fouled waist, a frame cut through the casting, two parts of a chain in a lead plug where the tongue was, a year's rope, **a new tongue a day's work every four or five years for as long as that bell hangs, and the whole of the craft is the going out: a person, in a boat, in the weather.** Eight marks and a day of a boat, paid once, and it recurs.
- **Do not give the glass storm a cause and do not let it speak.** It is a physical and psychological hazard, it is in the second column with a reason against it, **no cause may be named for it in any later volume**, and it may never become a voice that tells anybody anything.
- **Do not ask Sera Mott again.** She was asked once, on Sunday the ninth of April, and refused, and her name is not in the yard's book at her asking. **Volume 03 inherits the refusal and not the question.**
- **Do not write a game-style interface.** No panels, stat blocks, character sheets, status messages, floating text, quest text, skill trees, level-ups, or software vocabulary, and **no second person in narration and no prompt.** The only message-like elements in this world are claim seals, Registry notations, bell marks, written limits, gauges, the reeve's chart, the hearing room's slate wall, the crew's book, the market wall, the copying clerk's counter, the harbor authority's ledger, the bonesetter's writing, and physical warnings. **Nothing knows anything.**
- **Do not introduce a new antagonist, a new institution, or a new final enemy.**
- **Do not have the crown's reporting clause reach Mara Venn.** She is not a holder, not of the yard, and has said so in a room where it was entered.
- **Do not draw a line between the three points.** The reeve's ruling stands and is not lifted, and Volume 03 may not draw one.

## Jurisdiction inside the volume

The harbor line runs along the mole and the inner shoals. **The imperial line lies beyond it, and working past it needs the full Second Bell license, which this volume does not grant and does not approach.** Every dive therefore happens inside the harbor line under a named instrument the harbor authority issues — **a claim window, a work permit on a bond, or the provisional writ of the first of May with its ground, its four persons and its reporting clause** — **or on a man's own account landward of the third seam, where no office instrument is required and the yard keeps the practice in its own book anyway and will not have the two confused.**

**Ordinary harbor work landward of the third seam is not inside the closure.** The notice of the thirty-first closes the mole's toe and inner face from the third seam to the eleventh and does not reach the twelfth, the dump, or water, and no notice has ever closed water. Clause three catches only the authority's work. **This is still the only reason the yard has money and it stays true in this volume.**

**The writ is not the Second Bell license, is not a charter, and renews nothing.** It runs to the first of March next, is inside the harbor line and bounded by the outer marks, **the four-hundred-yard bar off the outer marks stands and is not narrowed by it**, and its ground is the mole from the first seam to the twelfth, the dump, the north wall, the first and second seams, all ground landward of the third seam, and private ground within the line. **No later volume may widen the writ's ground past what it names, may not treat it as a charter, and may not have any office other than the harbor authority renew it.** **The second season is a named absence and a Volume 03 chapter may name the absence and may not manufacture a renewal.**

**Because the closure is the harbor authority's own, any instrument of that office that puts a person, a boat or a lead inside it carries the reeve's acknowledgement on the page and in the record, at the reeve's cost, and it is never free.** There are **six** of these in that office's ledger now, and the sixth was the second one a party asked for. **A seventh and an eighth may be entered in this volume, the seventh at a party's asking and the eighth on the reeve's own motion, and the cost of both is Kest's alone.**

**The notice is not the harbor authority's to lift.** The mechanism by which the fifth-seam plate is ever reached is **a change of politics in an office and not a better argument from a shed**, and Volume 03 is where the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January is answered and refused.

## Midpoint reversal

**The vessels form a civic chain linking nine coastal communities, and their memories are civic records and not the private collection of a drowned city's rulers.**

Around the volume's midpoint, in the middle of Movement 4, a place is found — by labor, in weather, with a lead and a slate and a faultreader who has been wrong once already — where a keel-line ran between two things that were never made to be joined. The join is physical and checkable: a material, a repair, a fouling, a figure. **A witness was not coerced and a joiner was, and the record proves the shape of the system and not the name of anybody who built it.** What the join shows is that the fleet was **kept by people**, by hand, in several places, over a long time, and that what it carried was **witness** — a fisher's storm, a child's evacuation, a builder's account of a wall, a family leaving a village — and not an order, and not a man's possession.

**This is the reversal because it turns the volume's argument against the volume's fear.** A volume that has spent five months saying *a record can be a lie about provenance and a true account of a road* arrives at a chain that is true about nine communities and gives no name, and the honest man has to publish it anyway. **Mara Venn's finding is the hinge and it is her competence acting on its own, and she says the thing that ends her time on the crew in her own words and before anybody asks it**, and the finding is about glass and not about the sea, and the account is about the sea and not about the glass, and neither is a reason to believe the other.

**It is not the compartment and the compartment must not be re-used. It is not the coast road and the coast road must not be resolved. It is a different object, a different document, a different trade, and a different failure.**

## Volume climax

**The *Vigil* moves on the crew while Calder Marr is trying to raise the *Hearthwake*, and he saves the vessel's living crew and loses the chance to carry her full bell-archive to the surface.**

The choice is the volume's argument about usefulness: the bell-archive is the only local copy of a community's account of itself and it is worth more to that community than anything else in the water, and the people in the *Hearthwake* are worth more than the archive to the people who own her. **He goes to the crew.** The act proves the crew's reliability to nine communities at once and destroys the thing they were going to prove it with, and both are true and neither cancels the other, and **this time the volume's argument is a sentence a fisher said in Volume 02 and the sentence still holds**: both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other.

## Concrete resolution

**The rescued *Hearthwake* crew testify about their community's part in the old Compact, in their own words, with a limit written on it and the limit entered.** The fleet's formation is public, and the Crown can no longer pretend the wrecks are isolated salvage targets, **and the yard has lost the only thing it had: a map nobody else knew about.** A boy is off the stone, a conservator is not on the crew, a reeve has spent his last authority, a chandler's note is either re-written or still not re-written and said out loud, and **a question is asked in a market about who changed a rule, and it is not answered, and the refusal to answer it is on the page in the yard's own hand.**

## New-volume question

**If the fleet was a civic network, who changed the rule that made it an empire's property?**

Opened by the rescued crew's testimony, which is a local account of a local practice and not a history, and by the plain fact that a chain kept by nine communities for six centuries cannot have been seized three hundred years ago without somebody having had to decide to do it. ### L21. The two-column convention, and the third column, and what may not be done to either.

**The two-column convention is the yard's written practice and is binding on Volume 03.** What a man on a mole or a boat can check goes in the first column; what he cannot goes in the second, **with a reason against every line of the second.** **Both stand, neither is scratched out, and a request to strike one out is refused.**

**The third column was created on Tuesday the second of May 2026 for what the boy said when he was not in the boat, and carries the time it was read on the steps and the time it was written in the book.** **It is a method and not a rule, it is of the yard's book and of no office's, and it may not be applied retroactively to any date before the second of May 2026.** Used in Chapters 63, 66, 68, 69, 74, 78, 82, 88, 90, 91, 93, 95, 96, 98 and 99.

### L22. Jurisdiction, and the shape of the work.

**Every dive is inside the harbor line under a named instrument the harbor authority issues — a claim window, a work permit on a bond, or the writ of the first of May with its ground, its four persons and its reporting clause — or on a man's own account landward of the third seam, where no office instrument is required and the yard keeps the practice in its own book anyway and will not have the two confused.** **The writ is not the Second Bell license, is not a charter, renews nothing, and its ground may not be widened past what it names, and no office other than the harbor authority may renew it.** **No chapter may put an unlicensed Calder past the imperial line, and the full Second Bell license is not granted and not approached.**

**A person on a lead and a person in the water are two different kinds of day and the yard keeps them apart, and a man with no cutoff is not given an invented one.**

### L23. Length and shape, as the last batch actually taught it.

**Aim at 2,200–3,200 words a chapter.** **THE RULE "no chapter is two rooms" IS WITHDRAWN, and single-day multi-room chapters are now allowed and are not a fault.** The three chapters that were withdrawn on that rule — **Chapter 92 (the shed and the market), Chapter 98 (the net loft, the mole's steps and the shed) and Chapter 100 (the boards and the dock)** — are each *one day* in two or three rooms, and the rule that replaced the withdrawn one permits every one of them, and they stand as the precedent. The rule that replaces it: **no cost is paid twice, and every date in a chapter belongs to one day.** A chapter that wants to be a hearing room **and** a market is split across two days, with a body or a market or a boy in one of them. A chapter that is a rescue **and** a hearing is split across two days. **The hearing room and the market wall are never the same day, and the clerk reads the ledger in the hearing room over the tide-gang store and the ledger is the record and the wall is a convenience.** **A man may be in three rooms in one day. A man may not be paid for two days in one chapter, and no day may be in a chapter twice.** **Where a scene will not compress, let it run rather than drop a cost the crew pays** — and note that a chapter which runs long because it is one complete room is a different fault from a chapter which runs long because it is two. **Alternate the pressure types deliberately. Ten speeches are not ten chapters.**

### L26. Power. What is taken in this volume and what is not.

**No Bellwright, Keelwright, Storm-Reader, Chorus Diver or Openhand stage is taken in this volume.** The **Faultreader stage is taken in this volume, late, badly, and at a price:** it perceives incompatible testimony and false joins as perceptual or pressure seams. **A seam identifies difference, not truth.** Calder may compare a seam with a written record, another witness and the physical hull. **The more he relies on the seam the more likely he is to experience conflicting aftertastes, and severe use can make ordinary conversation feel like several conversations at once.** **A Volume 03 chapter may not have a seam settle an argument, prove a motive, convict anybody, or tell Calder what happened, and the volume must not let him off the consequences of getting one wrong.** The stage is not a rank and does not put a crew behind a man.
