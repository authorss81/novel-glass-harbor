# Power System: Echoes, Glass, and Dives

## Design purpose

The power system is a working maritime technology made from glass, pressure, witnesses, and human decisions. It creates useful abilities without making Calder all-powerful. Growth changes what he can attempt and who can attempt it with him; it does not erase the need for equipment, corroboration, maintenance, or consent.

There is no game-style interface in this novel. Banned interface elements are: status panels, stat blocks, character sheets, skill trees, level-ups, quest text, notifications, floating text, prompts that address the diver in the second person, and any software or programming vocabulary used to describe the magic. If a draft needs a brief message-like moment, it must be rendered as an in-world bell mark, Registry notation, written limit, gauge reading, or physical warning, and it must not tell Calder what to do next.

## The basic materials

### Tideglass

Tideglass is a clear, abrasive mineral found in deep basalt shelves and old pressure seams. It is not magical by itself. Raw tideglass can hold the shape of an object, but it cannot reliably hold a memory. Harvesting it requires diving, cutting, and grinding; the material is dangerous to lungs and pump seals.

### Echo-glass

Echo-glass is tideglass that has been worked in saltwater while a person deliberately witnesses an experience in its presence. The craft requires a bellwright, a clean pressure space, a physical object, and enough time for the glass to settle. It preserves what the witness could perceive, not what the witness privately knew.

Echo-glass is transparent because the memory pattern is distributed through the panel rather than stored as a visible image. Looking through a panel normally shows ordinary water and room light. Under pressure, with a proper anchor, a trained diver can enter the **echo**.

### Keel cord

A keel cord is a braided metal-and-glass line that links panels and vessels. It carries a coordinated pattern through a hull and allows a community to assemble several testimonies into one navigable record. Keel cords are structural, fragile, and politically important. Breaking a cord can stop a cascade but can also lose the memories on both sides.

A vessel made from echo-glass is a **memory vessel** in common speech and an **echo hull** in technical Registry language. The terms do not imply different classes of ship; they describe a vessel whose cargo is witnessed experience.

## How memory is stored

A memory vessel records an **echo** only when these conditions are met:

1. A living witness intentionally describes or relives a specific scene while touching a prepared panel.
2. The scene contains sensory information available to that witness: sight, sound, touch, smell, pressure, or bodily feeling.
3. A physical **anchor** is present. The anchor is usually an object, tool, garment, door, or piece of the original site, but any object strongly associated with the scene can work.
4. The glass has time to settle in saltwater. A rushed panel is unstable and may mix moments from adjacent witnesses.

### Living anchors

A living person can serve as a temporary **living anchor** only when the person is conscious, explicitly consenting, physically connected to the route, and protected by a named tender. A living anchor can hold a route or give a chorus a temporary center; it cannot supply an unlimited memory, be used to extract testimony, or be made into a permanent substitute for a physical panel. The person must be able to withdraw before the route reaches full synchronization, and withdrawal may leave the network unstable.

The cost is immediate and personal: pressure pain, shared aftertaste, loss of time, and the risk that the anchor cannot distinguish their own memory from the route's. A forced living anchor produces an unbound echo and can erode the anchor's sense of self. Volume 14 rehearses Calder offering his body as a temporary gap-anchor; Nessa exercises the named-tender cutoff before synchronization, and the aftermath is severe but reversible: acute aftertaste, joint pain from a pressure bell that should not have touched him, three days of poor sleep, and temporary hearing loss that clears before the finale. Calder's informed decision to remain connected in the finale is still a distinct choice, and it requires the same consent and tendering as any other dive. The permanent cost of that final connection is a permanent escalation, not an extension of the rehearsal. Ilya's attempt to bind himself permanently to the Crown Keel is a violation of this rule and leaves him trapped as the mechanism's sole living custodian.

The result is an embodied scene. It has duration and perspective, but no automatic access to motives, unseen events, or later interpretation. A witness can be wrong about cause, miss a person behind them, or faithfully record a lie told by someone else.

A witness is always a situated person: someone with a reason to have been in that place, a stake in the scene, and a partial view of it. Before any echo is used in a chapter, the writer should be able to answer who was watching, from where, and what that person wanted. A record with no situated witness is a mood rather than a testimony, and a story built on one has nothing to corroborate.

A stable echo can be real and still be morally misleading. A panic-filled child can accurately show a frightened child without identifying which adult caused the danger. A carefully prepared official can sincerely remember a record that was altered before the scene began.

## Consent and forced testimony

A willing witness may be sealed, witnessed, and later opened only within the uses the witness agreed to. Consent to make an echo is not automatically consent to publish it, display it to a family, or carry it along a trade route. A living witness may revoke future use, except when a narrowly defined emergency procedure permits temporary access to locate a person or prevent imminent physical harm. Emergency access requires two independent custodians, a recorded reason, limited use, and restoration of the seal afterward. It never authorizes propaganda or a permanent public copy.

A forced witness can also be recorded, especially through torture, coercion, or a magical pressure chamber. The result is called an **unbound echo**. Unbound echoes often have hard edges, recurrent panic, and poor continuity. They are physically unstable and can spread through a keel-line like a splinter of glass. They may be retained as forensic evidence of coercion, but they cannot be presented as a voluntary witness or used to authorize a route without independent review.

Consent is not a magic morality button. A willing witness can lie, omit, or be deceived. An unbound echo is not automatically truthful. The practical problem is that unbound testimony is dangerous to carry and unreliable to use; the moral problem is that the Crown has learned to call it necessary in emergencies.

A **claim seal** is a public mark placed on a panel after a recognized witness or assembly accepts responsibility for it. The seal records custody, provenance, and any stated limits on use; it does not certify moral truth. A panel marked “private,” “route use,” “public witness,” or “sealed” must be handled according to that mark. Multiple independent seals strengthen a record's usefulness, but two copies can still be copied.

If the witness is dead, no one may invent a new living consent. A family, local assembly, or mixed group of custodians may choose a limited public use, preserve the record as restricted, or refuse access, but a claim to historical importance does not grant unlimited permission. A posthumous decision can be revisited when new evidence changes the circumstances; it cannot be treated as an unquestionable command from the dead.

## Entering an echo

A diver enters an echo through a controlled procedure:

1. Lower a pressure bell or a tethered hard-suit to the hull.
2. Establish a real air supply, a tender, and a physical line.
3. Place the anchor against the panel and lower internal pressure until the glass resonates.
4. Enter with a second line and a handhold. The diver experiences the recorded scene as if embodied, but cannot pause, rewind, or choose a better angle.
5. Surface before the diver's air, line, or body fails. The diver must return through the same scene, even when the scene ends in danger.

The diver's body can be affected by the echo. Cold becomes real cold, a blow leaves a bruise, smoke produces a cough, and a recorded fall can injure a joint if the diver is poorly braced. The most common injury is not a magical wound but a body trying to protect itself from a danger that is no longer present.

### Surface knowledge

A diver can surface with a **practice trace**: a remembered action, route, or sensory fact that the witness performed or perceived. It is not a complete skill download. A person who once watched a bellwright tie a pressure knot may surface with the feel of the sequence and one useful correction, but must still practice the craft. A person who witnessed a calculation can provide a clue, not an infallible formula.

Emotional content remains more difficult to set down. A calm memory can leave a diver overconfident; grief can follow them for hours; a witnessed scream can remain audible in a quiet room. This residue is called **aftertaste**.

## The rules that do not bend

- The magic cannot read a person's private thoughts.
- It cannot reveal a future event.
- It cannot prove that a witness understood what happened.
- It cannot grant a complete profession, bloodline ability, or divine authority.
- It cannot safely remove a memory from a living person. “Extraction” is coercive, damaging, and leaves psychological or physical scars.
- It cannot make a dead person a new conscious person. An echo may preserve testimony, habits, and emotional patterns, but it is not automatically the person.
- It cannot erase an event by destroying one panel. Physical fragments, related witnesses, and the diver's own memory can preserve traces.
- It cannot make the sea obey a human command. At most, a crew can read a remembered route and choose whether to risk it.
- A memory vessel is not a treasure chest. Lifting it can require more labor, air, and money than the crew can safely spend.

## Diving and salvage limits

### Single dives

At the beginning, Calder can work as an ordinary salvage diver with pumps, tools, and rope. He can inspect a wreck, cut a cable, replace a seal, and bring up a fragment. His experience makes him competent, but no magic is required for the job.

### Tide-ear

A tide-ear is a diver who has learned to sense a resonance in echo-glass and enter one shallow, anchored scene. The ability is physically available to any trained diver with a compatible nervous response; it is not a unique bloodline. Most divers can learn it after exposure and practice. Calder is unusually good at recognizing pressure faults because of years of line work, not because he was chosen.

A tide-ear can:

- sense a panel that holds a scene;
- enter one echo with a personal anchor;
- surface a limited practice trace;
- recognize when a panel is overloaded or contaminated, though not precisely why.

A tide-ear cannot:

- read several panels at once;
- tell whether a scene is true;
- remember the echo while carrying another;
- safely open an unbound echo without a crew and containment plan.

The first cost is aftertaste, physical reenactment, and ordinary decompression risk. Repeated use can cause **tide-sickness**: chronic hearing loss, panic, poor sleep, blurred vision, and temporary confusion between the diver's own past and the echo. Severity depends on depth, duration, consent, anchor quality, and the diver's health.

### The first entry, as used in Volume 1

Calder's first echo entry in Chapters 6–7 is the template for every later dive, and later volumes must pay for every exception to it. The entry requires all of the following, in this order: a named tender on the surface, an independent line, a fixed air limit, a physical anchor seated against the panel, a cutoff the tender can pull without asking permission, and a recorded reason for the entry. Calder surfaces when air, pain, or the tender's decision says he surfaces, not when he is satisfied.

The first entry produces exactly one scene, from one witness's position. It leaves Calder with a limited practice trace plus aftertaste: a rope burn that his body re-enacts hours later, ringing ears that make the harbor's working bells hard to place, and emotional residue that briefly answers other people in the dead rhythm of the echo. He cannot enter a second echo for a full day, cannot judge truth, cannot rewind the scene, and cannot open the contaminated edge. Nessa holds the cutoff authority in that scene and uses it, which establishes the standing rule for the rest of the series: a tender's stop is not a request.

A first successful entry is not mastery. Calder's hands shake for two days. He cannot reliably tell, afterward, whether a memory he has is his own. The ability is available to other trained divers, and Volume 1 makes clear that other people can learn it from him, which is why the growth engine later moves from private skill to public method.

### Faultreader

A faultreader can perceive a **seam** where incompatible witnesses have been forced into one keel pattern. A seam appears as pressure that does not match the scene, a sound arriving before its source, a hand that changes angle across a cut, or an object that carries two incompatible histories.

The ability identifies difference, not truth. Calder can compare a seam with a written record, another witness, and the physical hull. The more he relies on the seam, the more likely he is to experience conflicting aftertastes. Severe use can make ordinary conversation feel like several conversations at once.

### Bellwright

A bellwright can work echo-glass into a practical listening device, pressure-safe case, or **bell buoy**. A bell buoy can transmit the presence of a stable echo across a local route. It cannot transmit the entire experience; it gives a trained operator a signal, timing, and sometimes a practice trace.

The first bellwrights can maintain a small number of linked buoys. Every link is a physical line that must be repaired, and every operator must be trained. A bellwright cannot create a memory from nothing or use a buoy to bypass the consent of the original witness.

### Storm-reader

A storm-reader can follow the pressure pattern recorded by several old vessels. The pattern is a memory of a past route, not a forecast. A storm may have changed, a channel may have collapsed, and a witness may have been lost. A storm-reader can improve a crew's odds or identify a remembered obstruction; they cannot command the storm, predict an unrecorded event, or guarantee a safe crossing.

### Keelwright

A keelwright can repair a coordinated vessel or convoy enough to use a shared keel pattern. The work requires ordinary shipwright skill, compatible glass panels, and a crew that accepts the route's risks. A keelwright does not own the vessel's memories and cannot legally or magically erase an unwanted witness by severing a line.

### Chorus diver

A chorus diver can coordinate several witnesses who each hold one channel of a shared event: sound, movement, pressure, or aftermath. This permits a more complete reconstruction, but the result is never a single objective recording. Each witness may be biased, and a chorus diver risks carrying everyone's aftertaste at once.

A chorus dive requires consent from living participants, physical anchors, a named tender, and a place to surface. A crew can choose not to participate. The ability cannot compel an unwilling witness.

### Openhand

An openhand can connect independent community routes without turning the network into a private instrument. This is the final power stage, not a larger personal spell. It requires several independent custodians, a minimum of distributed anchors, working bells, and people willing to carry responsibility when one route fails. The openhand cannot read every memory or make all communities safe. The ability changes the question from “what can Calder control?” to “what can strangers agree to carry together?”

## Growth requirements

Each stage requires all of the following:

- practice in ordinary salvage work;
- at least one failure or refusal that changes Calder's decision;
- a relationship or crew member who teaches or challenges the rule;
- equipment, money, and time that are lost or redirected;
- a moral choice about who may access the resulting record.

Calder does not advance by collecting a new vessel. He advances when he can use a new kind of memory safely, and the story makes the safer use harder than the dramatic shortcut.

Every stage also carries a bill in money and time, because the story is a working maritime one. Calder's first tide-ear entry costs the crew a night's air, a bench day in which Nessa builds the anchor, cutoff rig, and written reason, and a safe diving day the yard cannot give back. Faultreading costs the concentration a paying job needs. Bellwright work costs glass, furnace fuel, and unpaid crew wages. Storm-reading costs a supply crossing. The final openhand connection costs him his hearing. Do not let a stage advance while every bill is quietly waived.

## The eight visible stages and their costs

| Stage | Primary gain | Main cost | Earliest story range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line Diver | Physical salvage, pressure work, seam reading by hand | Injury, debt, ordinary labor limits | Opening |
| Tide-Ear | Enter one anchored scene and surface a practice trace | Aftertaste, reenacted injury, tide-sickness | Volume 1 |
| Faultreader | Detect incompatible testimony and false joins | Competing perspectives, concentration loss | Volume 3 |
| Bellwright | Build and operate local listening bells and buoys | Maintenance, dependence on a crew, public exposure | Volume 5 |
| Storm-Reader | Navigate a remembered storm route with a crew | Outdated data, weather risk, no command over the sea | Volume 7 |
| Keelwright | Repair and guide coordinated vessels | Shared liability, costly materials, cannot erase a panel | Volume 9 |
| Chorus Diver | Coordinate multiple witnesses without forcing one synthesis | Shared trauma, consent failures, loss of personal certainty | Volume 11 |
| Openhand | Connect distributed routes under shared custody | Must share control; permanent personal damage and vulnerability | Volume 13 |

## Examples of use and failure

- **Useful memory:** A drowned pilot's echo shows a hand moving a brass marker before the current changed. Calder surfaces with the route trace, but the buoy team must still test the water because the current may have moved.
- **False certainty:** A registry clerk's echo clearly remembers signing an order. It proves the clerk remembers signing, not that the order was lawful or that the clerk understood what the sealed case carried.
- **Forced testimony:** An unbound military echo repeats a threat and creates a pressure seam. It may expose an old crime, but carrying it into a city risks panic and further contamination.
- **Consent conflict:** A family wants a sealed panel opened for a public memorial; a surviving witness refuses. The community can preserve a restricted record or omit the echo, but it cannot make refusal meaningless by declaring the memory public property.
- **Failure under pressure:** A diver follows a remembered route into a collapsed arch. The echo is genuine, the water is not, and the crew must choose the ordinary escape over the proof.

## Rules for future drafts

- Give the magic a cost in the scene that uses it, not only in a later summary.
- Do not let a recovered memory function as a universal answer. Identify what it does not show.
- Use practical salvage work alongside power growth.
- Let relationships change what information can safely be shared.
- Keep the System absent. No panel, stat block, status message, floating text, or software vocabulary belongs in the prose, and no later volume is planned to introduce one. A future change to this rule would be a recorded canon decision in `state/continuity.md`, not an improvisation in a chapter.
