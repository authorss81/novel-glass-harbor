## Shape of the ten chapters

Spine, not cage; move a beat a chapter if prose improves, provided all land. The volume's opening pressure is **the leash and the first of March**, and the batch's job is to make a man in a poor yard understand what a paper is worth and what it is not:

1. **The fourth of January, and what a conservator is with no standing.** Mara's query comes off her file. She has a trade and no power to refuse anybody, she is not thanked, and the yard is poorer without the thing she has been doing for it. The first week of a charter, on the board, with no money.
2. **The nineteenth of January.** The named date, the lead and the slate and the hung bell, at the mole's toe, on the bedded seam in the mole's own foundation. **The run does not stop at the mole or the shoal: it is under the town**, and the harbor authority has no chart of it because the town was built on it, and no office has an instrument for a route and no forum will rule on one.
3. **The Crown's next move.** The circuit office sends the request that is worded so that a refusal is a delay. The yard is now a **charter holder** rather than a crew with a claim window, and that is a different animal in a different file, and the notice that catches three of its people has still not been lifted.
4. **A work that pays, badly.** Ordinary harbor work inside the line, under the charter, on the yard's own account, with a cracked pump and a hired one at three a tide, and the point of the chapter is that a charter lets a crew take work it could not take before and does not pay any of it.
5. **A body.** A bad descent, a real injury, a real cost, and a crew reorganized around it. Bevin's hand is a boat hand in June and is not a boat hand now. Calder's forearm is ungloved and his hearing is compensated by four people and by a book.
6. **An office.** The copying room, the custody log, the three numbers, and a second request or a refusal with a date on it that the yard cannot reach. Mara may refuse and may be refused.
7. **A family, a boy, or a market.** One of these, and not a room. Bevin's mother, the wages falling in January and February, Sella Brigg's third in February, Elias Voke's statement of capacity, or the herring fleet and the north berth.
8. **An argument in a room, with a cost in somebody else's berth.** Kest pays for things in moorings and berths, and this batch should show one more thing he pays for, and it should be somebody who is not the yard.
9. **Weather, and the coast getting busy.** The first storm-exposed work of the season, the three recoveries that have to be paid for out of nothing, and the long business of a supply problem — rope, glass, lead, a crane, a tongue for a bell.
10. **The first of March, approached.** Slack water, the north berth, the ninth seam, the coast office's silence or its answer, and a choice of what the crew does next under a paper that has to be renewed.

**Do not let the ten chapters be ten speeches.** Batch 0004's finding was that seven of ten were rooms, ledgers and noticeboards; Batch 0005 corrected the shape and its finding is recorded in `state/open-threads.md`'s deferred decisions, item 14: **no chapter is two rooms, and the pressure types alternate.** Carry that instruction forward. Batch 0005 was six chapters past the 3,200-word guideline; **do not repeat that.** If a scene wants to be two rooms, split it across two days and let a body or a market or a boy be in one of them.
