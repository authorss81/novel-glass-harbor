## Shape of the ten chapters

Use this as a spine, not a cage. Each chapter must earn its own goal and its own ending beat, and you may move a beat a chapter earlier or later if the prose is better for it, provided all ten land and the batch closes on Movement 2's own resolution.

1. **The letter and the poor week.** Calder tries to convert a good record into ordinary paid work and discovers that a crew with a Registry seizure behind it and no permit is a crew nobody wants to hire. A rival crew makes this concrete by claiming part of the first wreck's value on a document that is technically good. The immediate goal is one paid job; the resistance is that having been right about the glass has made the yard expensive.
2. **Weather-exposed work.** A job that has to be done in bad weather to be worth doing, with air money that is not there. This is where Calder spends what is left of his margin and where the crew finds out what it costs to keep him diving. Let a real accident or near-accident cost something physical and un-recuperable. Not a dive for glass: a dive for somebody else's boat.
3. **The family connection used against him.** A hearing-room or an office scene in which Calder's name as Alden Marr's son is the reason something is refused. He must choose between attacking the document and accepting the price, and the price is real. This is the batch's first political pressure and it should not be a fight.
4. **Kest's terms.** The twelve-day defense, the sitting, the bond, and the gap between what the crew can meet and what it must meet. Kest is not a villain and does not enjoy this. Whatever Calder refuses to give here must be something he is actually able to refuse in Chapter 9 of the volume outline, and whatever he gives must cost a share.
5. **Rival crews and ordinary politics.** The Free Keel's loose association tested in the cheapest possible way: shared tools, a shared rescue plan, and whether other crews will stand with a yard the Registry has touched. Introduce no new institution; the Free Keel already exists and has no leader.
6. **Recovery, not discovery.** A chapter in which the crew earns money and eats. This batch needs one chapter that is simply work going right, so that the chapters where it goes wrong have somewhere to fall from. A bell in a practical context is allowed here: harbor working bells, buoy bells, a bellwright's fitting, nothing that advises anyone.
7. **Mara's position.** Her certificate, her cost, her three *not stated* entries, and the beginning of a professional consequence she has to carry herself. Do not spend her Volume 4 break early; spend a small, deniable piece of institutional friction that nobody can call a break.
8. **The public record under pressure.** An attempt to get the duplicate, the bearing sheet, or the drawn plate into wider public use, and the cost of doing it. The consent question appears here in its first Volume 1 form: what may be published about a wreck, and who a public record of a wreck actually serves.
9. **The narrow extension.** The batch's legal climax: a hearing, a bond, and a term that gives the crew a little more time and a little less of its share. Something must be conceded here that Calder does not want to concede, and it must be a real share, not a feeling.
10. **The aftermath and the leash.** The chapter where the batch's resolution is felt rather than declared. The crew has an extension it can barely meet, a share reduced, a hearing still ahead, and a naval claim on the only ground that matters. It ends on a decision or a cost with a bill attached, spoken or written by Calder in front of his crew, not on a threat received.
