## What Movement 5 is for, from `outline/volume-02.md`

**Movement 5, the rescue and the writ, Chapters 91–100. This is the volume's climax and its resolution.**

1. **The climax. A fishing crew is trapped during a glass storm, and Calder abandons a valuable official route to go and get them.** The official route is the thing that would have paid, and the fishing crew is the thing that is in the water. He goes to the crew. **The rescue proves his local reliability in Morrow Quay and destroys the equipment needed to claim a legal salvage award, so that the act which makes him trustworthy at home is the act that costs him in Veyr, and both are true and neither cancels the other.** The point is the volume's whole argument about usefulness, and **it is not a choice between a good deed and a bad one. It is a choice between two ways of being useful, and the yard's own method is what makes the choice possible: a reason entered before anybody goes, a named tender who is not the diver, a fixed limit on the slate, a cutoff the tender can pull, and a two-column record afterward.**
2. **A glass storm is a local pressure event in which sound, light and perspective reorder around overloaded glass.** People may hear voices from the wrong direction or lose the ability to distinguish an echo from their own thought. **It is a physical and psychological hazard and not a monster, and it is not a second person and not a voice that tells anybody anything.** Write it as pressure, sound, light, and what a body does when it cannot tell a remembered thing from a present thing.
3. **The bell-buoy core's own ground answers during the storm, at the delay**, a hand's width of a second, and **nobody can say what is in the note.** The answering is the climax's instrument, not a solution. It is a bell that has been looked after by somebody going out in a boat in the weather, and that is the whole of the craft and the one nobody wants.
4. **The crew earns a provisional writ through a public hearing, and the Crown attaches a reporting clause to it.** A writ with a reporting clause is a longer leash with a name on it, and **the yard takes it, because a yard with no paper cannot work and a yard with a reporting clause knows what it has agreed to.** The reporting clause is a document; it is read aloud; the clerk enters it; and the yard's objection to it goes in the record unsummarized.
5. **The missing coast road becomes evidence of a concealed network, entered on a public wall in the yard's own hand with the limits written inside the rules, and six copies carried out by hand to the crews that keep the ground.** **Six hand-carried copies, to named crews, in this batch.** The word *network* may be used **only** as the volume's own new question and only in a form that does not explain it — a question about charts, asked in a room or on a wall, **and Orison may be spoken of only as a place seventy fathoms down, and the nine final routes are named in the bible and none of them may be named in the prose of this volume.**
6. **The resolution pays, in this order and not another:**
   - **the eight marks owing to the harbor's bellwright;**
   - **sixteen or more marks of arrears of a boy's wage out of somebody else's pocket, at least in part, and the yard's own debt to Mara Venn not paid;**

9. **The volume closes on the volume's own image.** `outline/volume-02.md`'s final chapter image is available and may be used as a guide to the register and not as a template: **a writ with a reporting clause read into a hearing record, a coast road that no chart carries read onto a market wall beneath a naval claim, the bell answering the harbor's tide bell through the ground a hand's width of a second late, a person going out in a boat in the weather to look after it because that is the whole of the craft and the one nobody wants, and the boy with the book writing the hour and the state of the water and the delay and reading it back twice, and the line saying *no source* and *not written as a voice*, because that is the only honest form of the line and it is the line.** **Note that the image's date in the outline is the first of March, which is where the volume's clock began and not where it ends; the chapters are the authority on the date and this volume ends in late April or on the first of May.**

## Method

- Write **finished prose**, not outlines and not beat lists. Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences, varied rhythm. A one-line paragraph only as a deliberate dramatic beat.
- **No duplicated paragraphs and no exact repeated sentences across the batch.** Check before you finish.
- **Every character acts from motive.** Kest is not a villain and does not enjoy harm. Sella Brigg is not a woman who offers. **Mara Venn is not an assistant and does not become one, and she is expensive and right and does not break.** Elias Voke is a herring skipper and not a plot device. **Noll Tarner is a rival crew and not a villain and does not become one, and his boat is at the pile and his men may go ashore twice a day.** Edric Pringle is a woman who has been difficult about money and says the cause out loud first. **Bevin Rook is a boy of twenty-two who is owed money and is owed none of it, with a hand that will not close and a wrist that will not either, and a bonesetter's sentence that says a boat hand in June and June is two weeks off at the end of this batch and must not be resolved as anything better or worse.** **Harl Dace is a man who hires on the strength of a piece of paper, has lost two men on a day nobody wrote down, has been right for thirty-one years, and is a witness and not a party and is never paid and never asked to be right.** The harbor authority's clerk is a man who fills in a box, keeps a page, says what a box can hold, and is not an antagonist and must not become one. Corvin Sarn is a tradesman and does not teach.
- Do not repeat a mechanical filler word as a tic. Vary *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded*.
- **Ground the sensory detail** — a lead going over, cold hands on a line, tar, weed, the weight of a warp on a wet wall, the smell of a wet rope, a wet stone under a knee, the noise of a shed full of rain on a tin roof, a glass storm as pressure and wrong sound and borrowed sight — without loading every paragraph with description.
- **Read what you have written against the jobs and the locks, and check every figure and every date against the calendar above before you call the batch done.**
