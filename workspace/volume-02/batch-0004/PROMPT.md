## What Movement 4 is for, from `outline/volume-02.md`

**Movement 4, the forged seal, Chapters 81–90. This batch is the volume's midpoint.**

1. **A working fisher describes a coast road along a coast that no current chart of this coast includes.** A line of marks, a bottom, a way of getting in and out of weather that he has used, and that the authority's own soundings do not carry. **He is not a mystic and he is not lying about the water.** He is a man with a trade and a schedule and a reason to be right, and the best version of him is a man who has been right for thirty years and has never had anybody ask him.
2. **The account is physically checkable and the crew checks part of it.** Not all of it, and not the whole coast. **Part of it, on the ground, in daylight, with a boat and a lead, and the check has to cost something** — a day, a wage, a hire, a tide, a risk, or a figure the yard does not have.
3. **The account arrives attached to a piece of worked glass bearing a claim seal.** A claim seal is a Registry instrument and the Registry is the office that wants the record single. The glass is not a custody object, it is not one of the three items, and **it is a different object in a different trade from the compartment and it must not be confused with it.**
4. **Mara Venn proves the claim seal forged, in her own competence, without a diver and without a ship.** A material and institutional finding, in her trade, on the object, in front of people. **She can say what a forged seal is. She cannot say who made the glass, and she says so in her own words before anybody asks.** That sentence is the chapter's hinge and it may not be softened, and it may not be made to sound like modesty.
5. **The turn: a record can be a lie about provenance and a true account of a road, and the two facts have to be carried together.** This is the volume turning its own competence against itself. A conservator whose entire value is that she can tell a mark from a forgery now has to say *I cannot explain this glass* in a room where the glass is the only evidence, and a crew that has just spent a month learning that a public record can be used against it now has a document that is false in the one respect that matters and true in the other.
6. **The batch's consequences, all of which must be on the page:** the yard has no instrument and this document is either an instrument or a weapon; the circuit office's fourth request of the sixteenth of March is unanswered and the copy count in its own type is eleven and it may now want the glass as well; the *Sea Wren* is still at the pile and the eleven copies are still the yard's only leverage; **Neave Tarrant is still not found and is NOT to be found and is NOT to be opened in this batch** — that is Movement 5's; and **Alden Marr is at the far bench, silent, not asked for anything, and Calder does not go.**
7. **The volume's climax is NOT in this batch.** It is Chapters 91–100: the rescue in a glass storm, the destroyed equipment, the provisional writ with a reporting clause attached. **Do not begin it.** Do not put a trapped fishing crew in the water and do not destroy the equipment.
8. **Mara Venn is a trade and not an office, and her break is Volume 4.** She must be **expensive and right** and must not break here, however badly she is used. **She may be refused work, refused payment, and refused a hearing, and she does not break.**
9. **Ask her for a figure and not a decision, and let her refuse the asking.** She has done this twice already, in a hearing room and in a shed, and a third time must be different in its detail and identical in its principle.
10. **The second season remains unknown.** The charter is gone; whatever comes back from Veyr is a date the yard does not control, and a chapter may name the absence.

**Who the fisher is.** He must be **a working seaman and not a clerk, not a reeve, not a conservator and not a mystic**, and he must not be Elias Voke, Sella Brigg, Edric Pringle or Noll Tarner, who are all on the page and all in a record. **Harl Dace of the *Grey Widow* is the strongest available existing man** — a working skipper of about thirty-one winters who asked in a net loft in January who the record was for, who hired the yard in March on the strength of a sheet of paper, and who is not a fisher of this harbor and not a Free Keel man. **A man of the *Sea Wren*'s old crew, or a man out of the roads in a boat who comes into Morrow Quay in April, is also available.** **A man of the third lay is available and must not be used, because he drags the Tarrant thread into the midpoint and that thread is Movement 5's.**

**The coast road may be described and partly checked, and the account is not to be resolved.** It may be doubted, set aside for want of a boat and a tide, or half-believed. **It may not be explained, named as a network, or connected to anything under the town.** The harbor authority still has no chart of what is under the town because the town was built on it, and the two may never be fused.

## Method

- Write **finished prose**, not outlines and not beat lists. Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences, varied rhythm. A one-line paragraph only as a deliberate dramatic beat.
- **No duplicated paragraphs and no exact repeated sentences across the batch.** Check before you finish.
- **Every character acts from motive.** Kest is not a villain and does not enjoy harm. Sella Brigg is not a woman who offers. Mara Venn is not an assistant and does not become one. Elias Voke is a herring skipper and not a plot device. **Noll Tarner is a rival crew and not a villain and does not become one, and his boat is at the pile and his men may go ashore twice a day.** Edric Pringle is a woman who has been difficult about money and says the cause out loud first. **Bevin Rook is a boy of twenty-two who is owed money and is owed none of it, with a hand that will not close and a wrist that will not either, and a bonesetter's sentence that says a boat hand in June.** **Harl Dace is a man who hires on the strength of a piece of paper and has lost two men on a day nobody wrote down.** The harbor authority's clerk is a man who fills in a box, now keeps a page, and says what a box does; he is not an antagonist and must not become one. Corvin Sarn is a tradesman and does not teach.
- Do not repeat a mechanical filler word as a tic. Vary *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded*.
- **Ground the sensory detail** — cold hands on a lead, tar, weed, fouling, the weight of a warp on a wet wall, the smell of a wet rope, a wet stone under a knee, the noise of a shed full of rain on a tin roof — without loading every paragraph with description.
- **Read what you have written against the jobs and the locks, and check every figure and every date against the calendar above before you call the batch done.**
