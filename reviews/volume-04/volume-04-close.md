# Part 1 — Volume 04 against its own outline, clause by clause

## 1. Central pressure — *the inquiry, and what an inquiry costs*

**RULED: RESOLVED, and resolved in a form the outline did not name. Nothing was decided, nobody was charged, and the inquiry convicted the yard of nothing. The cost was paid and it was paid in the yard's own method.**

The outline's central pressure has two halves and the fifty chapters rule on them separately.

**The first half — a formal inquiry into the Free Keel yard and into the party that went past its own outer marks on the eighth of August — is PAID, and the inquiry was held.** It is held twice and it is the same sitting: **Wednesday the twenty-eighth and Thursday the twenty-ninth of October** in the hearing room over the tide-gang store (Chapters 181–185), in front of a clerk of the circuit office who is a mouth of clauses and not an antagonist, **with the party un-represented** (Ch 184: *The party was not represented. It had instructed nobody and had retained nobody and had entered that on the twentieth of September and had not improved on that entry*). **The party was told to produce its book and the book was produced, whole and unshortened, from the first of May to the last page, and the holder of the writ read it out himself for three hours and fifty minutes** (Ch 184). The notice of the tenth of September that the whole of that sitting is built out of is put on the schedule and a join is put on it in front of the clerk (Ch 184, the Thursday). The acknowledgment of the twenty-ninth of November, the release of the copy record and the market read-out of the first of December are all downstream of that sitting and are all acts of the same pressure.

**The second half — the yard's work being used to validate somebody else's account, and the woman who can check glass having left the crew and being owed forty marks — is PAID IN A DIFFERENT FORM AND THE FORM IS STRONGER.** The Registry's use of Mara Venn's work is not paid off; it is **made visible, made checkable, and given a number.** The mechanism is Leaf 62 and Leaf 88 (Chapters 171, 175, 176, 177): her own refusal of the eleventh of November, in her own hand, with her trade and a date, is withdrawn from the *Aster Gannet* file, copied, and a slip is put in saying the copy is to be read with **leaf eighty-eight, which she has never seen and which nobody in this harbor has seen.** She comes up this hill unasked on the twelfth of October, says about four minutes' worth in a doorway, refuses the line, and says *I am not a door.*

**THE COST, AND IT IS NOT WHAT THE OUTLINE AIMED IT AT.** The outline says the cost is that a party which has published itself has to be cross-examined about the publishing. **What the chapters give is that a yard which spent two volumes saying that a record a man can check is the only kind worth having came out of a room less able to be checked, by its own method, in public, with a clerk's own entry against it.** Three specific prices are on the page and are checkable by either brother or by any man who can get off a mole:

1. **The join went on a notice and told him only that a document has been in two hands at two times.** He said *I do not know* three times in a room and would not say it a fourth time in a different way (Ch 184). **The clerk of the circuit office then said he had had that paper since the tenth of September and could see the two inks himself, and entered the fact in that order — the clerk first and the holder second — and gave the reason: if the fact is left out, then in about four years the only thing in the bundle anybody is proud of is a thing a man of this harbor put there** (Ch 184). **That is the volume's whole argument turned inside out on its own page, by a clerk, in a cold room, for nothing.**
2. **The market of the eighteenth of July was read back to him in his own words, in a flat voice, by a stranger who was not doing a part**, out of the yard's own second column, and the clerk said the words of the question and not the yard's (Ch 184).
3. **The third column was given up to a clerk in a flat voice, and the clerk entered that nobody was going to be asked about it afterwards and gave the reason: the only person alive who can explain those fourteen lines is the boy whose hand wrote them, he is not in this room, and this office cannot make him come** (Ch 184).

**AND THE INQUIRY'S OWN CONCLUSION IS ENTERED BY THE CLERK IN HIS OWN WORDS: *Nothing was decided. Nobody was convicted. Nobody was charged with anything.*** (Ch 196, the sitting of the twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh of November, which is a different sitting in a different room over the same store and is the volume's climax, not the inquiry.) **No office in this harbor is a villain this week and I have put that on the first line of the record as well** (Ch 196).

**Is the central pressure the inquiry or the publication's consequences? RULED: it is BOTH, and the volume resolves it in the publication's consequences, and the inquiry is the instrument that extracts the price.** The outline's own sentence — *a party which has published itself has to be cross-examined about the publishing* — is the mechanism. **The volume's own argument, that a reason does not travel and that the first column is checkable and the second is not, is NOT the argument the outline asked for and is a better one, and the outline is not amended.** The outline asked for an inquiry that costs money and standing; the chapters give an inquiry that costs a party's *standing as a source*. After the sitting, a man of thirty-six of the first lay says out loud at the head of this dock that a yard's figures are worth what a clerk's are, and the holder enters that sentence against himself in his own book, in his own hand, with a reason against the line (Ch 186, and again in Ch 196). **What it cost: nothing convicted, nothing charged, a statement of capacity in the yard's ledger and no instrument behind it, and a book that used to be worth something and is now worth what a clerk's are.**
