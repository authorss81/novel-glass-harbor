## Part 1 — Volume 01 against its own outline, clause by clause

### 1. Central pressure

`outline/volume-01.md`: *Calder's crew must prove a legitimate claim to the first transparent wreck and secure a defensible provisional working charter and salvage share before Kest and the Lantern Registry seize the recovery.*

**PAID. Chapters 1–50, resolved at Chapter 45, not deferred.**

The pressure is on the page in every one of the five movements and it is the same pressure in all five, not five different problems wearing the same coat. It is on the page as a *legitimate* claim: the labor chain is found proved and countersigned on the twenty-fourth of October (Ch3, Ch40), the permit names a site and two dates and is spent (Ch3–5), the claim window closes when the courier takes the box down the slip (Ch1–8), and the *Vigil's* claim is entered over the same shoal on the eighth of November with the yard's three-word objection on the same page (Ch32). It is on the page as the Registry's reach: the seizure order of the fourteenth of October (Ch8, Ch26, Ch40, Ch48), the notice of the thirty-first of October built out of Mara's own handling note (Ch26), and the custody-log re-entry of the twentieth of December that gives no reason (Ch49).

The volume also holds the *qualifier* the outline sets out, and holds it correctly: the pressure is **inverted** on the ground rather than resolved head-on. Calder does not win a hearing on the seizure. He wins an undertaking lifted (Ch45), a share kept (Ch45), three custody items that do not come back (Ch47–48), and a body of paper that the offices in Veyr will argue over for years (Ch49). **The volume resolves the legitimacy of the first recovery, and it does not touch the full Second Bell license**, which the outline explicitly defers and which `outline/series.md` makes a Volume 4 cost. Both are honoured.

**The pressure is on the page for the whole volume.** It is named in Calder's own mouth in the last two chapters as a leash rather than a liberty (Ch50: *A leash, Marr. A longer one than the leash you had, and I put it on you*), and the Chapter 50 ledger of what the yard does and does not have is the volume's own closing argument that paper is not money.

### 3. The midpoint reversal — **RULED**

`outline/volume-01.md` states it as the compartment *the manifest calls empty is too large to have been empty*. The chapters deliver *the space cannot have been cleared*.

**Ruling: the three-step form in Chapter 47 is ratified as the volume's finding, and the outline's two-step phrasing is a reading rather than a specification. This is proposed-canon item 12, deferred twice, and it is now closed.**

The outline's wording does not commit the volume to *was occupied*; it commits it to *is too large to have been empty*, which is a statement about the fit of a measurement and a thing. The chapter's three steps are the mechanical content of that sentence:

1. The space lies under a grating in a deck, **so the hatch is the only way anything has ever gone into it or came out of it**, and the clear opening measured with a steel rule in the hand is **three foot nine by two foot four**, while the sealed case is **seven to eight feet in its longest face** — so it cannot go in that way, and nothing of that size can be taken out that way.
2. A room nothing can be taken out of **is a room that has not been cleared** — this is the step the Batch 0005 review found missing. The first draft argued only that *this case* could not have passed *this opening*, which does not establish that the space was never cleared of anything else.
3. **This office's own thirteen-year-old wreck file, in a harbor's hand, gives number four as *grating in place, not cleared, contents not removed*.** A harbor has been recording in writing that the space stood uncleared since the year the ferry went down.

**All three are now on the page** — in the entry of the eighth of December (Ch47), in the reason entered in the reeve's book at eight that morning (Ch47), and in Bevin Rook's read-back, which is the version a clerk in Veyr would eventually read (Ch47). The argument is now the yard's own premise rather than its conclusion, which is why it survives.

**Why this form is better and is the one that must be carried forward.** *Too large to have been empty* is a claim about the compartment at the moment of the seizure. *Cannot have been cleared* is a claim about a **document**, and it is a figure: eight foot four by six foot nine clear and eleven feet two deep under the grating, against three foot nine by two foot four of opening, against a file that says *not cleared, contents not removed*. The yard wanted to prove the room was full and has proved the room cannot have been cleared, and a room that cannot be cleared is a room the manifest is not describing when it says the room stood empty. The chapter says this in Bevin's mouth and it is the volume's most durable sentence. It proves the manifest unreliable and **proves nothing whatever about what was in the space**, which is the distinction the whole series turns on.

**Binding on every later volume, written into `outline/volume-02.md`:** no chapter may state or imply that the sealed case was in standing space number four; the three Gannet documents — the harbor salvage report, the Navy seizure report, the Registry custody log — stay three documents; and any volume that restates the finding restates **all three steps**, never the two-step form. `outline/ending.md` promise 4 pays this in Volume 15, and a later volume that compresses it to two steps will have made the finale's payoff shorter than the setup.

### 5. The new question that motivates Volume 02, and Sera Mott's half of it

`outline/volume-01.md` asks: *Why are the transparent vessels arranged along a deliberate route toward Orison instead of lying as scattered ordinary wrecks, and who controls the missing links?*

**PAID as an opening, and it is opened twice, in two different registers.**

The first opening is the yard's own: three places on one bearing where the bottom comes up and nowhere else on two miles of a course the harbor authority itself sounded (Ch28, Ch39, Ch41), with the word *placed* struck out of the yard's own copy of its purpose and the purpose's *order shown, purpose unknown* entered in a hearing record. Kest's own words on the seventh of November rule that **no line may be drawn between the three points** (Ch31, Ch39), and the ruling is not lifted at the fourteenth (Ch40) or at the twenty-eighth (Ch45). So the volume gives the reader a route and refuses, in a municipal hearing room, to let its own crew draw a line on it. That is the whole thesis of the series in one ruling.

**Sera Mott's half is paid in full and is the better half.** On the twenty-first of December, at a mole head, with the tide going, standing next to the object on its cradle under a sail (Ch49), she says the other half out loud: there was more of them out there; she has heard this one answered from the westward and always late; **the ones that answered from the westward were kept, and somebody went out in a boat to keep them, and the ones she knows about stopped, because the men who kept them stopped going out, and the men stopped because there was nobody on the far banks by the time anybody asked.** *It is a road with nobody on it.* She has been asked by archivists, has said no, has believed them, and says she will say no one more time in about two years. Bevin Rook writes nothing down on purpose and says so at the time, and then enters the one line that says she asked for nothing to be written (Ch49).

That is the volume's new question and it is not a mystery, it is a **fact about people and about labor**, which is the register the entire series is written in. It is also, structurally, the seed of Volume 11's argument in embryo, and it is now on the record as such. The seaward end of the run is under a naval claim with a tender at a cable's length and nobody has asked the cutter what it is for (Ch50).

**Volume 02 opens the pressure and does not resolve it.** The leash, the first of March, and the first of March is a **Wednesday**, nine weeks and two days from the Monday the volume closed on (Ch50).

### 7. The final chapter image

`outline/volume-01.md` states: *At slack tide, the recovered bell-buoy core answers with the same delayed note Calder heard in the keel line at the end of Chapter 10. **Three clauses paid, one read rather than followed, and a judgment recorded.**

- **Slack tide.** Paid. *There was slack water on the mole face on the twenty-sixth of December a little after four in the afternoon, and the tide that had been going off since before noon stood still for the best part of ten minutes, and then began to come back.* The chapter is titled *Slack Water* and the word appears in the last third.
- **The core answers with the same delayed note.** Paid, and strengthened: the answer on the twenty-sixth is at **the same second as the sixth**, and Bevin Rook says so out loud, which makes the motif a *measured, repeated figure* and not a rhyme. The delay is **a hand's width of a second**, the same words as Chapters 10, 40 and 46, and the answer is *flatter than anything a harbor grows, because nothing on that ground was growing. It was hanging.*
- **The fragment in custody under a claim that cannot be withdrawn.** Paid in the ledger of the last third, with the third item, the third number, *not compared by this office with any other item*, the municipal claim, the second clause of a correct notice, and *would not withdraw the claim*.
- **The public provenance duplicate hangs in the Free Keel yard with the crew's own names on it.** **Read rather than followed, and the ruling is that the chapter is the better image.** The duplicates are not one object hanging in one shed. There are **ten printed crew copies**: two on the market wall beside the original, three on the walls of three other Free Keel crews, and five rolled in oilcloth in the bench chest — a count the yard published on the twenty-third of November, in Bevin Rook's right hand, with Nessa Pike's countersign and a sixth line excluding other crews' paper, and a number the *Vigil's* man wrote on his own slate and did not ask for (Ch 43). The public record on the market wall is in **three parts in four hands** with the limits written inside the rules and six copies carried to the herring skippers, the net loft and the *Low Lantern* (Ch 49). **The outline's clause describes a single artifact in a single shed; the chapters describe a record that is in ten sheds, two markets and a boat's head, and whose only currency is that people carried it themselves.** Chapter 50's own inventory of the market wall names the names on the public record — *Marr and Rook and Pike and Wren and Mott and Venn* — which is the outline's clause, arrived at by a better road. **The outline is not wrong; it is smaller than what was built, and the built thing is the argument of the whole series in one object.** No repair is due and the outline is not edited.
- **He hears an adult version of a childhood call he can no longer remember.** Paid, and the chapter is careful about the order: the *memory* is lost on the morning of the ninth of December (Ch 48) and the *call* arrives out of the ground on the twenty-sixth (Ch 50), **a grown man's, not a boy's, not his own, and a man in a boat further off saying a thing to somebody who was not answering, and it went out into the water and it did not come back.** He does not turn round.
- **He chooses the next licensed run rather than a private descent.** Paid, and the licensed run is named: **the nineteenth of January, a Thursday**, the mole's toe, the top of the flood, a lead and a slate and a bell hung, and **no diver unless the reason is in the reeve's book at eight in the morning and Pike is on the cleat.** The last line has him going up the steps to write down the hour, because that was the job, and the book on the bench and the pencil on top of it and the boy of twenty-two putting it in front of him without being asked.

**Judgment, recorded and not acted on: Chapter 50 is the better image and the outline is not amended.** The chapter ends on three people signing a year's work out in a shed with the stove going and the bell answering outside, which is the volume's actual last fact. **The chapter is not rewritten and `outline/volume-01.md` is not edited** — the outline's image is satisfied in substance and exceeded in one clause, and a volume is closed on the page, not on the plan.
