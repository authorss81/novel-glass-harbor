## Part 1 — Volume 02 against its own outline, clause by clause

### 1. Central pressure — *The charter is a leash and not freedom*

**RULED: RESOLVED, and resolved in the opposite direction to the opening. Not deferred.**

The outline's sentence is that the volume must end with the crew holding a provisional writ that comes with a reporting clause attached, **which is a longer leash with a name on it**, and that the license is not earned and what is earned is the right to keep working at a price. Every word of that is on the page.

The leash did not shorten. **It lengthened, acquired a name and four persons, and now carries a four-day reporting clock and a Crown signature.** The proof is in Chapter 97, and it is proved three ways in one room: the writ names four persons, of which the fourth is a hired diver asked in person in a shed on the afternoon of the first of May and told, in his own words on the page, *I am content to have it* (Ch 98); the reporting clause is a Crown attachment carrying **three names on paper and no face in the room**, requested in writing by the magistrate of that circuit, signed by the master of a cutter holding a claim in this harbor, and sealed by **Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner**; and the instrument's last line, in Kest's own hand and read back by the clerk, says **that this office is not the office that can renew it, that the second season is not this office's to give, and that if there is one it will arrive out of a city from a man who has never been inside this harbor** (Ch 97).

**The volume's own sentence for it is the yard's sentence in the hearing room of the first of May**, and it is on the page in Calder Marr's own mouth: *We are the most reliable crew in this harbor and it has cost us a hand lead, a hose and a claim window, and the writ we got for it is a leash with our names on it, and I am going to take it, and I am not going to stand in a room and tell anybody that the two things are a bargain, because they are not a bargain, and both of them are true, and neither of them cancels the other* (Ch 97). Elias Voke says the other half in the same room, as a fisher and not as a man making a point: **that is the leash, then. It is a longer one. It has our names on it** (Ch 97).

**Ruling on the sentence itself: it is the volume's argument and it is a better sentence than the outline's, and the outline is not amended.** The outline says a leash; the chapter says a leash that is also a receipt, and a man who has been reliable for money and gear and a claim window, and those two facts are not negotiable against each other. That is the whole series thesis in one paragraph of dialogue, and it is spoken by a character who cannot afford to be consoled. A Volume 03 chapter may quote it, cite it, or be embarrassed by it; it may not improve on it.

**What the pressure cost the volume:** the charter ended at midnight on Friday the thirty-first of March and was not renewed (Ch 80), the yard held **no instrument, no claim and no paper of any kind** for the whole of April, a job at the mole's toe was refused for want of an instrument on the same morning a job on the second seam was taken because no instrument was needed (Ch 81), and the thing the yard got in place of the charter costs a mooring and a tide-gang man and a four-day clock.

### 3. Starting relationships — PAID, all four visible in scenes

**Calder and Mara.** The outline's prediction was that her standing goes on the fourth of January and she becomes a trade rather than an office, and that the professional spine changes from *can this office be made to hold a line* to *can two people who can both refuse still work together when neither of them can refuse anything any more*. **Paid, and further than the outline asks.** The query comes off her file on the fourth of January and she prices herself from that morning (Ch 51, 52); she says *do not make me useful* (Ch 64); she asks for six marks in daylight and does not ask twice (Ch 64); she is refused four marks **in writing** by a shop at Ashtide for an opinion she gave in a market and says the forty is not one mark smaller for it (Ch 89); and in the hearing room of the first of May, **before anybody asks her anything, for the fourth time in five months**, she says the writ cannot reach her and she is not of it, and that she has a paper of her own going up the coast on her own account with three clauses about a false seal and a boat and three questions about a chart, and that she is not an assistant and is not going to be one this afternoon (Ch 97). **He has asked her for nothing since the twenty-seventh of January and came within four inches of asking four times and did not** (Ch 90, 98). **Her break is Volume 4 and it is not near, and nothing in this volume is a step toward it that she has not chosen.**

**Calder and Kest.** The outline asks that the reeve's own spending be spendable against him and that the volume resolve it with a reeve who is not a hero and who chose to tell the truth only after understanding what silence had cost. **Paid, in a different mechanism than the outline implies, and the mechanism is better.** Kest does not spend his spending against himself; **he enters it against himself six times in his own hand**, on a permit of the eighth of January, a reason of the twelfth, a claim window of the seventeenth, a line under a reason on the nineteenth, a claim window of the seventh of April, and a reason of the twenty-seventh of April, each time stating that this office has no power to lift the notice of the thirty-first and is not lifting it and has never lifted it, and each time naming the six earlier entries so that a man in Veyr in four years will find them in one ledger. **The sixth was asked for again by a party, which is the second time, and the cost of it is his and always was and is never free** (Ch 94). He pays in a mooring and in a tide-gang man's wages and in the mole steps that will not be cleared in June, and he says each price out loud before granting the thing. **He is not a friend. He is a man whose promises have dates on them and the last date has passed** (Ch 80).

**Calder and Bevin.** The outline asks that the boy move from a boy who is owed forty marks to a boy whose claim is being paid out of somebody else's pocket in part, and who still cannot close his hand. **Paid, and the boy's own decision is the beat.** Sixteen marks of wage go out of four hands in a market on Saturday the twenty-ninth of April, **not one mark of it out of the yard's own work**, and he takes the ninth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth weeks and **leaves the fifth of March in the column, which is the oldest line in it, because he is not going to take a man backwards in his own book** (Ch 96). The forty marks of claim stand whole, unpaid, unasked to be reduced. His hand has been going since the fourth of April, the yard says so out loud in a room in front of a clerk of the circuit office on the first of May, **he is to be off the stone on the fifteenth of May whatever his own arithmetic says**, and the bonesetter's book still says a boat hand in June and June is a sentence in a book and not a promise and not a lie. He has entered a second omission of his own — that he has been to the fishery twice since the twenty-first of March and had his hand open both times and has not put that down — and called it an omission and entered both in the same breath, and **nobody in that crew asks him one word about it, then or afterwards** (Ch 92).

**Calder and Alden.** The outline says they do not move, that a man at a far bench is not asked for anything, and that Calder does not go. **PAID, exactly, and the volume closes on it.** Alden Marr is at the far bench in six of the ten chapters of the last batch, is asked for nothing, and **the bench moves under the drip on the twenty-sixth of April and moves itself, and the two of them are in one room for nine hours in a gale and do not speak, and Calder Marr does not go over to him** (Ch 93, restated in Ch 98 and Ch 100). No fragment, no report talk, no second story, no confession.

**Nessa Pike**, whom the outline does not list among the starting relationships, is the volume's most consistent character and gets the only sentence the batch reviews could not improve: *the yard that owes a boy four marks a week and a bellwright eight marks will not put a man off a boat in its shed* (Ch 88). **She is right, she wins, and it costs somebody.**

### 12. The volume climax — **RULED: discharged in a different form, and the volume's form is the better one**

`outline/volume-02.md`: *Calder abandons a valuable official route to rescue a trapped fishing crew during a glass storm.
