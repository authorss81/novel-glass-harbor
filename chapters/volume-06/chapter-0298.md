# Chapter 298

*Wednesday the Twelfth of May, a Lead and a Slate at Seventy Fathoms With No Name on the Slate, Eleven Stamped Links With a Date on Each of Them, and Nine Lines He Cannot Say Any More*

Wednesday the twelfth of May, off the south end of that mole, and on the crown of that mole in the afternoon.

**The wall did not come down on the said Wednesday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The six conditions were read at the boat at about nine in the morning in order and the bar was named and the tide was half past the ebb, and a lead of about fourteen pounds marked at every fathom went over the side on a warp with a hand on the warp and a pot under the lead and a slate tied to the line above it.

**Nobody signs anything in this yard and nobody has signed anything in it since the third of April past, and the slate went down blank except for the rule, and it came up with a figure and a day and a count on it and nothing else, and the reason against the line of a name at the top of it is the standing one and it is not improved on.**

The entry was made in the ship's own left hand off the slate in the boat and read out to the two of them in the boat, and it is this, and it is the whole of it: **THE PLATE IS IN THE REBATE. THE BED IS EMPTY. THE ROUND IS OUT. THERE ARE TWO HUNDRED AND NINE MARKS IN THE SILL AND A DAY IN THE SILL, BEING THE ELEVENTH OF MAY, AND THE DAY IS THE SAME DAY IT WAS ON THE THURSDAY PRECEDING AND HAS NOT BEEN CUT AGAIN.**

"**That is checkable by anybody with a lead and fourteen pounds,**" said Fisk. "**It took about nine minutes and it is the first time in about four hundred years that anybody has written that sill down and put it in a book he could lose.**"

"**Nobody has lost it,**" said the ship's hand.

"**That is what I said.**"

---

Then he went up the steps at about eleven and sat on the bench inside the door of that shed for a while, and then at about one he went out and up the crown and took the tarpaulin off the crate on the mole steps.

There was nobody on the crown of that mole. The two objects of nobody's were nine feet off under their two tarpaulins with a corner off one of them since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and the round of glass was in the crate with its chain coiled over the collar and the piece of worked glass bedded in it and its own face upward in the daylight.

He read the dates on the eleven links. It took him about half an hour and he did it the way a man does a thing he intends to be sure of, one at a time, out loud, twice, and the last link is thicker than the other ten and has something struck on the face of it under the shadow of the collar.

**And he did not scratch at it. He took a flat piece of glass out of the crate and held it to the light and read it that way, and it says, in one line: *Sealed and entered at the Morrow circuit office by order of Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner.***

There is a date struck on the outside of the collar itself, and the collar is this office's own work, and the strike on the collar is later than the strike on that link, and the difference between the two is a difference in two made things, and it says nothing whatever about which of them is right, or why, or by whom.

**Nobody is in a room about it. There is no such person in this harbor, and none of them has been sent for, and none of them has been asked anything, and the name on that line has not been spoken to in that shed this afternoon and is not going to be spoken to in this harbor by anybody but the ship's hand, and she will say it once, out loud, in that shed, when she reads a column back, and that is the one time and it is not a conversation.**

He read it and he read it again and he put the piece of glass down and put the tarpaulin back and came down off the crown and went and sat at the bench outside the shed.

---

And then Nessa Pike came out at about half past two and asked him a question she had asked him in one form or another about nine times in three weeks, and got an answer she had not had before.

"**Say the third line.**"

"**Which one.**"

"**The order. The nine lines. The third one.**"

He waited about four seconds, and then he said: "**I do not know.**"

"**Say the first one.**"

"**I do not know.**"

"**Say the ninth.**"

"**I do not know.**" He put his hands on his knees. "**I read them under a lamp at ten at night on the twenty-ninth of April with you in that room, twice, and then a third time with my finger on the third line, and you stopped me on the third line. And I have had them since the twenty-ninth of April and there is no stage or no fall or no weather since then. And I cannot say one of the nine.**"

She went and got the sheet out of the drawer, and the middle fold of it is still soft and still parting at one corner, and she put it on the bench face up and she did not say anything at all, and she read the nine lines out in that shed in the flat voice she uses for a column.

"**One. The fleet is ordered down. Two. The fleet is the transparent fleet and every vessel of it lies in the water of this city. Three. The order is of the fleet and not of a master of it. Four. The masters are not to asked which of them made it. Five. The order is carried by the masters and not by any office. Six. No copy of this order is to be made. Seven. A copy of this order is not this order. Eight. A man holding a copy of this order has not got an order. Nine. And it is to be carried out in one tide.**"

"**That is the fourth one wrong,**" he said. "**It is *are not to be asked*.**"

"**I know it is, and I read it wrong, and you have got it now, and you had not got it a minute ago.**" She put her hand flat on the sheet. "**Calder. Listen to me and then say one sentence and then I will never raise it again in this shed as long as I am in it. You can learn those nine lines again in about ninety seconds. You have just done it. And you will be able to say them tomorrow, and next week, and in four years, and every day of your life, because they are on a sheet in a drawer in a shed on a mole and anybody can come up this hill and ask to see it. What you have lost is the other thing. The other thing does not have a copy and there is no drawer for it and nobody is ever going to be able to check that you had it.**"

He did not answer that for a while.

"**Then it has gone.**"

"**What has gone is not the order,**" said Nessa Pike. "**What has gone is you carrying it about in your own head for a fortnight, and this yard has been standing in front of that wall for six movements saying that a record a man can look at is worth something and a record a man cannot look at is worth nothing, and it turns out that is not a rule about records. It is a rule about us, and it was never a rule that worked in our favor once.**"

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a thing relearned in ninety seconds off a sheet in a drawer is not a thing remembered; that a man who has lost a thing out of his own head has not lost the record of it and has not got to be told he has; that a difference between the strike on a link and the strike on a collar is not a finding and is not a reason and is not a motive; and that a name on a seal line is not a man and is not in this harbor.*

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twelfth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a lead of about fourteen pounds marked at every fathom was put over the side off the south end of that mole at about nine in the morning of the said day with a hand on a warp and a pot under it and a slate tied to the line above it, and that the entry made from that slate in the said boat is that a plate is in the rebate in a sill at about seventy fathoms and that the bed in the said sill is empty and that the round is out, and that there are two hundred and nine marks in the said sill and a day in the said sill being the eleventh of May, and that the said day is the same day it was on the Thursday preceding and has not been cut again; and that the said slate had no name on it and the reason against the line of a name is the standing reason of the third day of April past and is not improved on.*

*And that on the said round there is a chain of eleven links about a finger long, every link stamped and a date stamped on every link, and that the last link is thicker than the other ten; and that on the face of the said last link, under the shadow of the collar, there is a seal line, and the said seal line reads *Sealed and entered at the Morrow circuit office by order of Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*; and that a date is struck on the outside of the collar itself and that the said date is later than the strike on the said last link, and that the said collar is the work of the same office that is named in the said seal line, and that the difference between the two is a difference in two made things and says nothing whatever about which of them is right, or why, or by whom.*

*And that the holder of that writ read the said seal line on the said afternoon on the crown of that mole, alone, and that he scratched at nothing and nobody in that harbor was asked anything about it and nobody was sent for.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed at about half past two in the afternoon of the said day that he could not say one of the nine lines of the copy of the copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city, and that the said lines were read by him in that shed under a lamp on the twenty-ninth day of April past and had not been read out loud by him since; and that the said sheet is in the drawer in that shed and was read out to him on the said day in about ninety seconds and that the said ninety seconds is not the loss and is not entered as the loss.*

*And that the said loss is entered here without a figure, and there is no figure for it and there is not going to be one; and that the reason against the line of putting a name to it is that a name given to a thing once is a name somebody will use the next time it happens, and that the reason against the line of calling it the thing it is not is that the thing it is not has a reason against its own line and this one has not got one and is not going to be given it.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and there are four hundred and twenty-nine pence in the bag, which is two marks, nine shillings and one penny. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-five days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and first night, and neither has moved, and a man of thirty-two sat on a bench outside a shed at half past two in the afternoon and could not say one of nine lines that he had carried in his own head for a fortnight, and the lines are in a drawer in that shed, and he is not in a drawer, and there is no figure in this book for the difference.**
