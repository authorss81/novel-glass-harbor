# Chapter 280

*Eleven Entries and Forty-Eight Lines and the Whole of What It Cost, Read Back From a Leaf With Four Columns*

Saturday the twenty-fourth of April, in the shed on the mole, and on the bench outside it.

**The wall did not come down on the said Saturday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it, and the figure in the bag is the figure it has been since Wednesday night and that is the third night running in which nothing has moved in it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Saturday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour the shed does things, and the two of them and a woman of no office who had been in that shed for six days and had not been asked one question she had not answered, and there was no boy at the door and nobody sent for one.

Nessa Pike read it in her own left hand and read all four columns, including the empty ones, and she read the reasons against the lines in the second column in the same voice as everything else.

**The first column of that leaf has eleven entries in it, and there were three of them on Wednesday the fourteenth of April past and there are eight since.**

The three are the mark and the cut and the descent, and they stand where they stood.

The eight are these, and each of them is a day a man did something on.

*That four lines went into the ordinary post at a box on the corner of that mole on the fifteenth at a penny, addressed to a woman of no office of this harbor, unsigned, and that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said morning that all three of the things he had said on the Wednesday before were off.*

*That a woman of no office, thirty, of this harbor, came up that hill on the sixteenth with a form in her coat, and that on the eighteenth that form went into the ordinary post at a penny at the one address in that yard's book which is not that yard's, in a town two days down that coast.*

*That the mark was gone and looked at on the seventeenth, being thirty-six days on it, and was fouled about a foot and three-quarters on the weather side and about two inches on the crown, the shackle was found with a thumb and is there, and there is a flat on the crown of it that this yard does not fit and a line upon it that is not this yard's line.*

*That the four lines made on the sixth of March past were read out in full in that shed on the seventeenth, all four of them, and were not improved on, and that a woman of no office said out loud that there is not one address in those four lines and that every one of them is about a record.*

*That nine feet of chain and a bar of square iron were carried along the front street of that harbor on the twentieth and offered at three doors and refused at three, that a shilling was offered for the chain and refused, and that a man of about fifty-five of that harbor said out loud on his own step that the next man who comes down that street with a thing off a wreck will be able to say that this yard asks.*

*That the outer marks and the bar off them were looked at from a boat on the twenty-first, a mile and a quarter off the head of that mole, and that nobody and nothing of that yard was inside the said bar, and that the said bar was not narrowed by anything.*

*That a woman of no office said a thing out loud in the fish market of that harbor on the twenty-second in about forty people's hearing, and that no person of that yard was in that market and nobody was sent, and that what she said was true and that nobody in that market was able to tell whether it was true or not.*

*That a letter of one page came up that hill on the twenty-third in a young hand signed with two names, and that it said a sheet was on a step in a town two days down that coast on the twentieth and was read out to a man who cannot read on the twenty-second, and that the three dates were entered as three dates and were not made into one.*

---

**The second column of that leaf has forty-eight lines in it.** Seven of them were in it on Wednesday the fourteenth. Thirty-two of them went in between the fifteenth and the twenty-third. Nine of them went in this afternoon.

The ship's hand did not read those forty-eight out and did not read them out twice. She said the one thing that had gone wrong in them, because a book with forty-eight lines in one of its columns is a book that is telling whoever opens it something, and the something is that this yard has got very good at writing down the things it will not do.

"**The not-writing-back. The not-asking. The not-going. The not-sending. The not-paying. That is five of them in ten days and they are all true and every one of them was a decision and not a habit, and in about four years a man in a city is going to read this book and say: that yard never did anything, and he will be right, and it will be the wrong shape of right, and there is no way to write that down in a book that keeps four columns and a reason against every line.**"

"**Then we shall be a book of refusals,**" said the woman of no office.

"We are a book of refusals with the reasons on," said Calder Marr. "That is not nothing. It is what we have got."

---

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it, and the four lines above his named line are four and are nameless and are not struck out and they are in about sixty boats on that coast, and he was not on the stone on any of the ten days and he came down on none of them and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered.**

**And the fourth column has eleven lines in it.** Six of them were in it on Wednesday the fourteenth, being the lines of the fourteenth of May and of the fifth, the seventh, the eleventh, the thirteenth and the fourteenth of April. Five went in this week, being the lines of the fifteenth, the eighteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-second and this morning, and the last of them is the last thing in that book.

The first four of the five were read out and neither of the other two improved on one of them and neither of them will. The fifth is this, and it was read out in her own left hand, and it is the reason this leaf exists:

*What it is not: — that a word said in a market on the twenty-second of April past is not a record and is not a page and is not a drawer, and cannot be put in any column of this book, because no man in this book can go and check it; and that the four lines four people wrote on the sixth of March past are not a house and are not a wall and are not a bar, and are a record; and that a house is a place a person cannot refuse anybody anything about, and that a drawer is a house, and that this yard has been standing behind a house since the sixth of March past and had it the wrong way round.*

---

And then Calder Marr said the turn, and he said it once, and it is in the first column in those words and nothing was added to it afterwards.

"**A page in a drawer in a house is custody that an office can reach. We built that on the sixth of March and we were right about it and we have had it the wrong way round ever since, because we thought a house was a place a thing was safe in. A house is a place a person cannot refuse anybody anything about, and that is not safety, that is a door with no handle on the inside, and a drawer is a house, and a yard is a house, and this shed is a house, and I have known that since the seventeenth day of April past and I have not got a single thing in this book that puts a handle on the inside of any of them. The four people four days down that coast wrote four lines in March about a record and a man in this yard spent forty-two days leaning on them as if they were about a house, and a record has no address and a house has one, and that is the whole difference and it took a woman of no office two days to say it out loud. And the price of it is now a man of thirty who cannot read, two days down that coast, who lost a day of three marks off a step on a Wednesday, and a woman of about fifty-eight in this harbor who has a will and a marriage lines and a paper with a seal on it in a drawer under her bed and has not slept, and a girl of nineteen who read a page out in a kitchen in the autumn and a form on her father's step in April, and both of those are ours, and neither of them is money, and we are not going to see either of them again unless we write, and we are not going to write.**"

"Monday," said Nessa Pike.

"**On Monday the wall comes down bare for the fifty-first time and the cistern is charged for the forty-first time since the third of August past and the wage falls due for the twenty-eighth week and the column does not move, and this yard has nineteen marks, four shillings and ninepence and a chain on a nail it cannot sell and a bar in a drawer it cannot give away and a mole with a foot at forty fathoms that we cannot look at twice in one day, and no instrument and no money and nobody to ask.** And a woman in this harbor has told forty people in a market that a keeper is a function, and about sixty boats on this coast have a sheet in them that says where every other copy is, and in about two months those two things are going to meet in a place where neither of them is us. That is the only thing I have got to say about the future and I have said it."

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-fourth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said Saturday by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a woman of no office of this harbor, and that the said leaf holds eleven entries in its first column, being three on the fourteenth day of April past and eight since, and forty-eight lines in its second, being seven on the fourteenth and thirty-two from the fifteenth to the twenty-third and nine this afternoon, and none in its third, and eleven in its fourth, being the lines of the fourteenth of May and of the fifth, seventh, eleventh, thirteenth and fourteenth of April past and of the fifteenth, eighteenth, twenty-first, twenty-second and twenty-fourth of April past.*

*And that the four lines above the said boy's named line are four and are nameless and are not struck out, and that the said boy was not on the stone on any of the ten days and came down on none of them and was not put on anything and was not asked one word about the omissions he has entered.*

*And that the said leaf does not go on any wall in that harbor and never will, and that nothing went up on any wall in that harbor in these ten days and nothing came off, and that the two naval notices are not on that wall and were not taken down by that party, and that the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it.*

*And that the holder of that writ said in that shed at about half past four in the afternoon on the said Saturday, once, that a page in a drawer in a house is custody that an office can reach, and that this yard built that on the sixth of March past and had it the wrong way round, and that a house is a place a person cannot refuse anybody anything about and that a drawer is a house and that a yard is a house and that this shed is a house; and that the four people four days down that coast wrote four lines in March about a record and that this yard leaned on them as if they were about a house for forty-two days; and that the price of that is a man of about thirty who cannot read two days down that coast who lost a day of three marks, and a woman of about fifty-eight in that harbor with a drawer under her bed, and a girl of nineteen who read a page out in a kitchen in the autumn; and that the said sentence was entered without addition.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on any of these ten days, and that it may not be told in a shed, on a mole, in a market or in about forty people's hearing, and that it is owed to a room with two people in it at a price, and that it buys nothing and restores nothing, and that a woman of no office was in that shed on six of these ten days and was not told and did not ask.*

*And that five of the forty-eight lines in the second column of the said leaf are refusals, being the not-writing-back, the not-asking, the not-going, the not-sending and the not-paying, and that in about four years a man in a city reading that book will say that that yard never did anything, and that it will be the wrong shape of right, and that there is no column in that book for the saying of it.*

*And that the word keeper went into about forty mouths in that harbor on the twenty-second of April past and that no person of that yard was in that market and nobody was sent, and that the said word cannot be entered in any column of that book and is not, and that a woman of about fifty-eight of that harbor has a sheet with a seal on it in a drawer with her will and her marriage lines and is not to be written to by this yard, and the reason against that line is that this yard has made itself one address in this country and is not going to make a second.*

*And that a man of about thirty who cannot read in a town two days down that coast has a sheet on his table and no one to ask and lost a day of three marks, and that the three marks were not sent to him and are not going to be, and that the reason against that line is that the day this yard pays that man three marks he is a man this yard has paid, and that this yard put a page in his daughter's mouth in the autumn without asking him and has an arrangement with nothing.*

*And that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay was not asked anything in these ten days and asked nothing, and that the third lay is a berth short of two and has been since the autumn, and that a Crown chart of the western approaches is in a bag in a boat out of Ashtide belonging to him and this yard has not asked for it.*

*And that the four people of that coast, four days down it, who go into deep water without a book, were not asked, and that the reason against that line is the reason of the second day of April last about a trade and a price, and that the fact that this yard now has a method for reading a made floor at forty fathoms is not a reason to ask them and was not used as one, and that the limit four of them wrote and signed stands and the holder did not ask to have it lifted once in these ten days.*

*And that Tobias Wren was not on a page in these ten days and was not sent for and nothing was asked of him, and that nothing in these ten days made him a teacher and nothing made him a door, and that the reason against the line of the not-sending is the reason of the second day of April last.*

*And that no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days and no return of any kind was made out of any of them, and that four returns stand and there is no fifth, and that no sitting was held in that hearing room on any day of them.*

*And that the condition is charged as a body and not as a name, and one day of the ten was lost to it, being the twentieth, and the day carries no figure because there is no figure for a day, and the reason against the line is the reason that has been against that line on every lost day since the sixth day of October past and has no name in it and nobody is going to put one in it.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on any of the ten days ended on the said Saturday, and the whole of what has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, being the eleven marks of the sixth of April last, and there is nothing else and there is not going to be. The whole of what went out of that yard in those ten days is eight hundred and thirty-four pence, being five marks, two shillings and tenpence, and it is four things and two of them are a penny: a penny at a box on the corner of that mole on the fifteenth, four hundred and eighty pence for a day at the face of the second seam on the seventeenth on the hirers' own account with nobody in the water, a penny at the same box on the eighteenth, and three hundred and fifty-two pence on the twenty-first for the boat of the man of the second lay for a day at a mark and eightpence and a man of twenty-six hired by the day at a mark and two shillings, the price named on the Tuesday evening and not on the Wednesday morning. The whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is sixty-five marks, one shilling and eightpence in twenty-one transactions at seven counters, of which two are that penny at a box on the corner of that mole. Nineteen marks, four shillings and ninepence is in the bag, which is three thousand and ninety-seven pence, and it was twenty-four marks, seven shillings and sevenpence, being three thousand nine hundred and thirty-one pence, on the morning of the fifteenth, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks does not exist and did not exist on any of these ten days and will not come back while that bag is above six hundred and forty pence, and nobody in that shed said one word about it at any hour of any of them.**

**Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past, unmoved, and he is in no book and nobody was sent for him and no letter has gone to him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these ten days and no digit of anything he said is on a page of that book and the chest was not opened and the lead plug is in it with a number in no book in that harbor. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure, and that is in no sentence with the bed at Ashtide and in no sentence with a certificate. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, standing since the fifteenth of October past, neither reduced and neither paid, and a woman of no office stood in that shed on six of these ten days and nothing was asked of her for anything and her forty marks were not mentioned once. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand, the third column is unfilled and unappointed and his hand is at the head of it, and he was not on the stone on any of the ten days.**

**The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, and twenty-seven weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against them; the only wage Tuesday in these ten days was the twentieth and the column did not move on it, and the next wage Tuesday is the twenty-seventh of April next and the column will not move on that either. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one with its three counts of weeks at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven unmoved by arithmetic, and it has not been said out loud a fifth time and this yard is not going to say it. The slate wall came down bare once in these ten days, being the fiftieth Monday since the first of May, on the nineteenth, the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page. The cistern was charged at no figure on three rota days, being the fifteenth, the nineteenth and the twenty-second, and this party walked none of them, being the thirty-eighth, thirty-ninth and fortieth of the charges entered walked on none of since the third of August past, and no figure was entered against a day that is not a Monday or a Thursday. The far bench was occupied on three of these ten days, being the sixteenth, the twentieth and the twenty-fourth, and was not occupied at any hour on the other seven, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him on any of the ten. The bench under that pump is bare for the eighty-fourth night. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and seventeen days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved, and that party did not go near them on any of the ten days. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron two feet long in the drawer, neither measured nor cleaned since the ninth of April, and neither sold nor given away, and nobody has asked whose either is.**

**And the plate in eleven feet in the mole's own foundation stone was not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about on any of these ten days, and the note against it stands open for a third volume. The case of five bells is not raised and not asked about and the limit four people of that coast wrote and signed stands. The delayed note is at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and a hand's width of a second, and nothing was measured and no occasion was entered. The lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second of July are where they were. A chain of custody is a jurisdiction and a practice and is not a registry and is not a court and is not a harbor authority, and it was made on the sixth of March past and is not aged by this or any other ten days, and this yard did not look in one of the four drawers it cannot look in and wrote to nobody.**

**And a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything in these ten days and asked nothing, and a brother who did not go over to him on any of them, and no fragment and no report talk and no second story, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift. And a working harbor of that coast is poorer on the twenty-fourth day of April than it was on the twenty-third day of February: one herring berth of two out, the north berth out, the mole-head light not on because a light costs oil, the fire party's hose dead until the spring, the tide gang four men short, the hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November past, a boat of about forty tons a hand short since the middle of July past, a harbor authority's season ended on the thirty-first of March with nothing issued, a man of twenty-eight in a bed at Ashtide for five weeks because a net came up on him, a man of fifty-one who has lost about a half of a season's gear and whose ground is still shut, five marks of compressor's gland unpaid and useless and no hose in that harbor at all, nineteen winters in a list being made up in a city, a form on a step two days down that coast, a drawer under a bed in this harbor with a seal on a paper in it, nineteen marks, four shillings and ninepence in a bag on a mole, and the answer four hundred miles west under seventy fathoms, and nobody in this country can get there.**
