# Chapter 283

*The Twenty-Seventh Week With No Envelope, a Penny Off a Counter, and Fourteen Marks Three Shillings and Fourpence Named in a Shed Before Anybody Agreed to It*

Tuesday the twenty-seventh of April, in a room off the fish market, and in the shed on the mole.

**The wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it. The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday. A wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, no envelope was made, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and twenty-seven weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, the twenty-seventh of them being this day. Nothing came into that yard on it and one penny went out of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Tuesday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

He went at eight on the Tuesday, and the market was not on the boards, and there was nobody in the room but her.

She reached up without turning round and brought the sheet down off the top shelf at the back and put it on the counter face down, and he put a penny on the counter, and she put the penny in the box and did not look up, exactly as she had said she would.

Then she said, "**Turn it over,**" and he turned it over, and she did not look up, and that was the whole of the transaction and it took about eleven seconds.

**It is one sheet, about sixteen inches by eleven, of a heavy rag paper gone the color of a wet pipe, folded twice across and once down, with the fold across the middle gone soft and beginning to part at one corner. It is a copy. It is in a hand that is not hers, and the hand is nine years old, and it is copied entire on both sides and the copyist has ruled no lines and made no blot and has not put a heading on it that is not on the original, and at the head of the face there is a name of a chamber.**

"That is a pound and a bit off you," said Pernel Saye.

"**It is a pound and a bit off whoever bought the house,**" he said, and put the sheet in the inside of his coat, and went up the hill at a quarter past eight with it flat against his chest the whole way up because it is the only piece of paper in this coast that nobody in this country knows the effect of and he was not going to lose it on a hill.

---

**And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in the ship's left hand, the thirteenth line:**

*What it is not: — that a penny at a counter off the fish market is not a purchase of a record and is not a hire of anybody and is not an instrument, and that a sheet taken off a shelf is not a thing brought up out of seventy fathoms, and that a copy is not the thing it is a copy of; and that a price named in a shed at the bench before anybody has agreed to it is not a promise and is not a paper, and that this yard's own book is not the harbor authority's book and cannot stand in for it and is not the same thing.*

---

The rest of the day was the price.

Garrow Fisk had come back up that coast road on the Thursday before last and had been in and out of that shed all week doing nothing that gets into a book, and he came in at about ten and stood at the end of the bench with his cap in his hand and let the other two get to it.

"**The same set,**" said Calder Marr. "**Off the same book, out of the same pressure house at Ternwatch, for one day, and I am not going to a different man for it because a different man would cost the same and would cost us the pencil mark.**"

"You have not got a pot," said Fisk.

"I have not got a pot, a housing, a pipe, a compressor, three gauges, a man off a Crown house book, a hand on the hose, or a boat that is this yard's, and I have not had any of them since the tenth of April and I am not going to pretend to have had them." He put his finger on the bench. "**The three figures are the same three figures as the fifth of April, in the same order, and this yard is not going to improve on them. Nine marks a day for the compressor, the housing, the pipe, the gauges and one man off that book, being one thousand four hundred and forty pence. Two marks and eightpence a day for your own time, being three hundred and twenty-eight. A mark and two shillings a day for a hand on the hose, being a hundred and eighty-four. That is the day at twelve marks, two shillings and eightpence and it is what it was the last time and it has not moved.**"

"And a second man off that book," said Fisk.

"**And a second man off that book, at two marks and eightpence a day, being three hundred and twenty-eight, and that is the dearer and I want it written down that it is dearer and why before anybody agrees to a single thing.**"

---

He said the why standing up, and it took him a while and he did not have it ready and Fisk let him get there.

"**The first descent I did the reading. I came up out of forty fathoms with a figure in my hand that I could not get out of and I had it twice and I could not say what it was, and nobody in that boat asked me what I meant, and I have thought about that for three weeks and I have not got a better answer than that I was no use to anybody on the bottom. The set will not get cheaper because the man is a stranger, we have been told that by a coach that took fourteen hours, and the book sends the same man every time and the same man is the second one on it. And I am not sending one man down to hold a pot on a floor of cut stone at forty fathoms with a figure in it and a lead weight on a herringman's net, because I cannot hold a slate and read a figure at the same time and I am not going to find out on the bottom which of the two I lose.**"

"That is the whole of the reason?"

"**That is the whole of the reason and I have said it out loud before anybody agreed to it, which is the only rule there is left.**"

"**Then it is fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence,**" said Fisk, "**and I will have it out of Ternwatch by six in the morning and the book will send the second man and I have not got a word to say about that and neither have you.**"

"You have got one word," said Calder Marr. "**Say it now and not on the bottom.**"

"**I have got one word and it is that a party that cannot be hired is a party that has one thing left it can sell, which is a day of itself, and that the last time you sold one you were paid in somebody's gear and not in money and did not know it at the time.**"

"**That is fair,**" said Calder Marr, "**and put it in the second column and not the first, and do not improve on it if you ever say it again.**"

---

*Nessa Pike wrote the whole of it in her own left hand while he said it, and then read it back, and then she said the one sentence that is on the page for a Tuesday and is not new.*

"**And the six conditions are read aloud at the boat in the morning, in order, and there is no first entry, and there is no harbor authority's book to go in, and that office's season ended on the thirty-first of March past with nothing issued and the clerk keeps his own page, and this yard's own book is not that book and is not the same thing and cannot be the same thing, and I am not going to hear either of us say it in the other order.**"

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about eight in the morning the holder of that writ went into a room off the fish market and a copyist of this harbor put a penny in the box and did not look up, and that the said sheet is one sheet about sixteen inches by eleven of a heavy rag paper gone brown, folded twice across and once down with the middle fold soft and parting at one corner, and that it is a copy made nine years ago in a hand that is not the said woman's hand and is copied entire on both sides with no lines ruled and no blot, and that at the head of the face there is a name of a chamber, and that one penny went out of that yard's own book and that is the whole of what went out of it on the said day.*

*And that the said day was a wage Tuesday and a wage of four marks was not paid on it and no envelope was made and the column did not move and the envelopes did not move, and that twenty-seven weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them.*

*And that between about ten in the morning and about two in the afternoon the price of a descent to be made on the Wednesday following was named out loud in that shed at that bench by the holder of that writ before anybody had agreed to it, being nine marks a day for the compressor, the housing, the pipe, the gauges and one man off a Crown house book, two marks and eightpence a day for the ship's hand's man, and a mark and two shillings a day for a hand on the hose, and a second man off the said book at two marks and eightpence a day; and that the whole of the said day is fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence, being two thousand two hundred and eighty pence, and that no part of it was agreed to until the whole of it had been said.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said day from about ten until about four and was not approached, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the said descent is dearer than the descent of the eighth, ninth and tenth of April past is the second man off that book, and that the said holder of that writ said out loud that he could not hold a slate and read a figure at the same time, and that the reason against the line of the holding of it himself has been against that line since the twelfth day of April past and has no name in it and nobody is going to put one in it, and that the said day of the twentieth of April past is a day lost to the same thing and carries no figure because there is no figure for a day.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on the said day, and that a woman of no office was not in that shed on the said day and was not sent for and was not asked for anything, and that her forty marks first after food stand and were not mentioned once.*

*And that the four people of that coast were not asked on the said day and are not going to be asked about the said sheet, and that the reason against that line is the reason of the second day of April last about a trade and a price, and that the fact that a copy of a municipal sheet of the drowned city has come into this harbor on a Tuesday for a penny is not a reason to ask anybody anything.*

---

**One penny went out of this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing came into it, and nineteen marks, four shillings and eightpence is in the bag, which is three thousand and ninety-six pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is sixty-five marks, one shilling and ninepence in twenty-two transactions at eight counters, of which one is a penny at a counter in a room off the fish market.**
