# Chapter 259

*A Fireman of a Pressure House, and the Condition That Nothing Goes Under That Has Not Been Cut*

Thursday the first and Friday the second of April, in the shed on the mole, and at the head of that mole.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the thirty-fourth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and the third lay filled on the said Thursday at about seven in the morning and there is one herring berth short of two and there has been since the autumn.**

**And no day of work was booked in this yard on either of the said two days, and the holder of that writ lost a day to the body on the said Thursday, and that day is in the second column with a reason against the line and no name in it, and a day lost is a day and carries no figure, because there is no figure for a day.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him on either of the two days, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything on either of them and asked nothing on either of them.**

**And a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch came up that hill on the said Friday with a copy of that yard's printed sheet of thirty-eight lines in his coat, and two marks and eightpence went out of that yard's own book on the said Friday at about six in the evening, and a letter came up that hill in the ordinary post on the said Friday from a person of no office and was read on the said Saturday, and nothing was asked of anybody and nothing was answered.**

---

He had a fireman's burn across the back of the right hand that had been white for years and was not shiny, which is the difference between a new burn and an old one, and he had a coasting boat's hands on top of it, and he came up the mole steps at about half past two on the Friday afternoon with a folded paper in his coat and did not ask whether anybody was in.

**"Garrow Fisk," he said, to nobody. "Pressure house, Ternwatch. I am not here about a job and I am not here to see the yard. I am here because I have got a sheet of thirty-eight lines in my coat and I want somebody to tell me whether the thirty-eighth line is true."**

"That is not our sheet," said Nessa, from the bench.

"It is folded at the thirty-eighth line, which is the only line on it that says where to find the rest, and I have had it in that coat since the eleventh of March." He took it out and put it on the bench, folded, and did not unfold it. "My brother sails in one of about sixty boats. He brought it home and he could not read half of it and he read the thirty-eighth line twice and he said, *that is the yard, that is where it came from.* And I have been up this coast for a fortnight on my own account and I stopped on this mole because it is on the paper."

**"That line is true," said Calder Marr. "It has been true since the day we printed it and we have not taken it back and we are not going to."**

"Then I have had my morning and I will go down to the packet." He put his hand flat on the folded sheet and did not take it away. "And I am going to say three things, and I am not asking you for one of them, and I would rather say them in here than on your mole where the fish are in."

He said the first one in about a sentence and the second one in about three.

"**Air.** I can hire you a compressor and a housing and a man who knows how to run them, off a Crown house book at a Crown house rate, and I can have the Crown house rate said out loud in front of anybody who asks, and it is not a small figure and I am not going to pretend to you in a shed that it is a small figure, and the figure is the same figure a Crown hull pays and you are not a Crown hull and I am not going to pretend you are one.

"**A condition.** I will not go under anything that has not been cut. Not a plate, not a frame, not a hull, not a bell, not a door. I will go under a thing that has been cut and I will not go under a thing that has not, and that is not caution and it is not a rule in a book, it is what happened to a man I worked beside for nine years at a Crown yard on the western shelf, and he is alive and he does not go down at all now, and if you want the rest of that you can have it and if you do not want it then the condition stands without the rest of it.

"And the third thing is the price of my own time, and it is two marks and eightpence a day, and my travel is my own rate and I will tell you what it is when I know how many days it is, and I am telling you the day now, before you ask me anything, because I have been on this coast for a fortnight on my own account and I have not been paid for one hour of it and I am not going to be a man who asks afterwards."

**Nessa wrote it all down and read it back to him, and he corrected two words in it, and she made the corrections and read it back a second time, and he said that was right and that nobody had ever done that for him before.**

"And there is a fourth thing and it is not a condition, it is a question, and you can say no to it." He looked at the two of them. "**Who cuts?** Because that is the whole of it. That is the whole of everything I have just said. The air I can get and the housing I can get and a man who can run them I can get, and if there is nothing to cut then I have brought a pressure house up this coast for nothing, and I have not got a boat, and I am not going to pretend to be a boat."

**Nobody in that shed said anything.**

The man of the second lay was not there. The shipwright looked at the bench. Calder Marr looked at the wall of the shed, which is plaster and has been since before he was born.

**"Then we have not got a cutter," said Calder Marr.**

"That is what I am asking you about."

"We have not got a cutter."

**"Right," said Garrow Fisk, and picked up his folded sheet, and did not put it in his coat. "I will sit on that step and I will not bother you, and I am not going to say anything else about it in a shed, because I have been in four sheds on this coast this month and in three of them somebody has told me a thing that is true and nearly all of them have told me in a way that was for them. I will sit on the step.**"

He sat on the step for about an hour and a half and he did not come in, and Nessa brought him out a cup of water at about four o'clock and no more than that, and he drank the water and looked at the water off the mole for the rest of the hour.

---

He went down at about six and two marks and eightpence went after him to a box on the corner, and in the last of the light Calder Marr stood in the shed door and looked at the step.

"You are not going to ask him whether we could hire a cutter," said Nessa.

"No."

"You are not going to ask him what he would do if we found one in a month instead of a day."

"No, and I am not going to ask him to wait, because he has said he is not going to be a man who asks afterwards, and a man who is not going to ask afterwards will not wait and I would be insulting him."

"That," said Nessa, "is the third time in a week you have let a price stand instead of a favour, and I am not going to say anything about it, and it is going in the second column and not the first, because a man being let alone is not a thing a man on a mole can check."

**"Write this down and read it back to me," he said. "And then read this after it: the man of about thirty-one of the third lay lay alongside in that harbor from the Friday to the Sunday of last week with a chart in a bag, and on the Sunday there was no berth for him in the third lay because he was on that mole being answered, and the berth went to a boat out of the north basin, and nobody in this shed was asked about it and nobody in this shed has asked.**"

---

*First column, entered on Friday the second day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on the said Thursday the cistern was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and that party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the thirty-fourth of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past, and that the third lay filled on the said Thursday at about seven in the morning and that there is one herring berth short of two and has been since the autumn.*

*And that a man of about thirty-four, who gave his name as Garrow Fisk and who is of a pressure house at Ternwatch, came up that hill on the said Friday at about half past two in the afternoon with a copy of that yard's printed sheet of thirty-eight lines folded at the thirty-eighth line in his coat, and said that he was not there about a job and was not there to see the yard, and that he wanted somebody to tell him whether the thirty-eighth line was true, and that the holder of that writ said that it was true, that it had been true since the day it was printed, and that it had not been taken back and was not going to be.*

*And that the said man said three things and asked for none of them, being that he can hire a compressor and a housing and a man who knows how to run them off a Crown house book at a Crown house rate, and that the figure is the same figure a Crown hull pays and he would not pretend in a shed that it was a small figure and would not pretend that this party was a Crown hull; and that he will not go under anything that has not been cut, being a plate, a frame, a hull, a bell or a door, and that that is not a rule in a book and is what happened to a man he worked beside for nine years at a Crown yard on the western shelf, who is alive and does not go down at all now, and that the rest of it can be had or not had and the condition stands without it; and that the price of his own time is two marks and eightpence a day and his travel is his own rate which he will tell when he knows how many days it is, and that he named that before he was asked because he had been on this coast a fortnight on his own account and had not been paid for one hour of it and was not going to be a man who asks afterwards.*

*And that the whole of it was written down in that yard's own book and read back to him and that he corrected two words and that it was read back a second time and that he said that was right and that nobody had ever done that for him before.*

*And that the said man then asked one question, being who cuts, and that the holder of that writ said that this party has not got a cutter, and that the said man said that that was what he was asking about, and that nobody in that shed said anything else about it in a room, and that the said man then sat on the top step of that mole for about an hour and a half and did not come in, and was brought out a cup of water at about four o'clock and no more than that.*

*And that two marks and eightpence went out of that yard's own book at a box on the corner of that mole at about six in the evening on the said Friday, and that the price of that man's travel has not been given and is not yet owed and is not to be treated as a debt until it is named by the man who owes it.*

*And that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay lay alongside in that harbor from the Friday to the Sunday of the week last with a chart in a bag, and that on the Sunday there was no berth for him in the third lay because he was at the head of that dock being answered by a party of this yard, and that the berth went to a boat out of the north basin, and that nobody in that shed was asked about it and nobody in that shed has asked, and that the holder of that writ asked for that to be written down and read back to him.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ lost a day to the body on the said Thursday, and that the day carries no figure, because there is no figure for a day, and that the reason against the line is the reason that has been against that line every day since the sixth of October past and has no name in it and is not going to have one.*

*And that nobody in that shed named a man of about sixty-one at the far end of it, and that the not-naming is entered here and is not improved on, and that the reason it is not improved on is that the question of who cuts in this harbor is the question of a trade and not the question of a family, and that a man asked on a mole whether he cuts answers it with a price, and that a price answered to a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house is a thing this yard would then have to pay, and that it has not got, and that it is not going to ask a man of sixty-one what he will take.*

*And that a man being let alone is not a thing a man on a mole can check, and that it went in this column and not the first, and that the holder of that writ has let three prices stand in a week and has asked for no favour, and that this is not entered in the first column because the first column is a record of what a man did and not a record of what a man was.*


**Two marks and eightpence went out of this yard on the said Friday and nothing came into it on either of the two days and nothing went out of it on the said Thursday, and sixty-five marks and fourpence is in the bag, which is ten thousand four hundred and four pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is nine thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the eighteen days ended on the said Friday is nothing, and the reason is the form of the fourth of March and not a mood. The whole of what went out of that yard in the two days ended on the said Friday is two marks and eightpence, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is eight marks, six shillings and one penny in ten transactions at five counters, of which three are the boat of the man of the second lay at one mark and eightpence a day with a penny of line, and one is two marks and eightpence to a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and he is not in that book and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure, and that is in no sentence with the forty marks below. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, and a letter came up that hill in the ordinary post on the said Friday from that person of no office, and nothing was asked of her, and forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots stand, the third column is not filled, nobody is appointed to it, and the boy's hand is at the head of it. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-three weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and neither of these two days was a wage Tuesday and the column did not move, and the last wage Tuesday was the thirtieth of March and the next is the sixth of April. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it will not be said a fifth time. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of that yard has been inside its first clause on either of these two days, and no return of any kind was made out of either of them, and the season of that harbor authority ended on the thirty-first of March and no instrument has been issued out of that office since. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, was not raised and was not asked about in these two days, and the limit four people of that coast wrote and signed stands. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered and nothing was measured. The two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and ninety-five days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, and neither has moved, and that party did not go near them on either of these two days. The bench under that pump is bare for a sixty-second night, counting from the Sunday of the thirty-first of January instant, and it is the only bare thing in that shed.
