# Chapter 271

*The Thirty-Eighth Charge, Four Lines to a Woman Who Wrote First, and a Penny at the Box on the Corner*

Thursday the fifteenth of April, in the shed on the mole, and at the post on the corner.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the thirty-eighth of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Thursday, and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it, and the figure in the bag is the figure it was on Saturday night, and that is the fifth night running in which nothing has moved in it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Thursday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

He broke it before nine, and he did it out loud, and he did it to the two of them and not to the book.

"I said last night in this shed that I was not going to ask her and not going to write to her and not going to send anybody. **All three of those are off, and I am putting the date on them, which is the fifteenth of April, and I am not going to stand here and tell you the reason twice.**"

"Give it once," said Nessa Pike, "and then sit down, because you have not eaten and you are going to be writing all morning."

"**The reason is that a form exists.** A woman of no office wrote one page up this hill on the second of April past and said there is a form and that a person who keeps unsettled material in a house is a keeper of it under that form, and that she has never seen it served and cannot say what it does when it is, and we have read it once and put it in a book and written nothing back. That is eleven days. Eleven days is not a long time and it is the longest that yard has sat on anything since the sixth of March past. **And a yard that does not answer a letter is not a yard that is careful. It is a yard that has made itself an address that answers nothing, and an address that answers nothing is an address, and I wrote that down last night at about two and I have not been able to get round it since.**"

"Write it down again," said Nessa Pike, "and put the reason against the line, and not in the first column, because a thing that was not done is not a thing that was done."

He wrote it down. It is in the second column and it is four lines and she read it back and it stands.

---

Then he wrote to her, and it took him until about eleven, and the four lines are the four lines and there is no fifth line on it.

He did not sign it, and the reason is the reason of the third of April past and is not improved on, being that a name on a letter is a letter a clerk can produce in a room and say of.

**The first line asked her to come to this harbor.** Not this yard — he got that far and stopped and started again, and it is written the way he got it, not the way he would have got it.

**The second line said why, and the reason was not for your sake.** *You told me on the second of April past that an office you worked in for nine years has a form for material that office calls unsettled, and that you have not seen it served on anybody. I am not asking you for that form and I am not asking you for anything. I am asking you to come and let a person ask you a question in a room, and the question is what the form does, and I have asked it of myself for eleven days and I have got a book with two columns in it and no answer.*

**The third line was the one that took him an hour and it is the reason the whole of the week turned on.**

"*And I want to say a thing on the paper and not in a room, because a thing on a paper can be argued with for two days before anybody acts on it. On the fourteenth of April past I said in this shed that a form is served by being left at an address on a day, and that the price of it is a person's afternoon and their fear, and that neither of those is ours, and that we are not going to be the reason a person in a town two days down that coast is frightened in her own kitchen. I have had a night to think about whether I meant it. I did, and I was wrong, and the wrong is this: a form does not need a yard to be the reason. It needs an address, and it will find one, and the only reason a form has not been at the door of somebody on this coast in the last eleven days is that nobody on this coast has held one. That is not a protection. That is a fortnight of luck.*"

**And the fourth line was the ask, and it was five words, and she told him afterwards that she read that line eleven times and the three above it not once.**

"I am asking you. Not the yard."

---

It went to the box on the corner of that mole at about half past two in the afternoon and it cost a penny, and that penny is the whole of what that yard spent on the said Thursday, and there is no figure against any other hour of it, because nothing else was done and nothing else could be.

He came back up the mole at about three and put the receipt stub on the bench, which is what he does with every stub, and Nessa Pike turned it over and wrote the date on the back of it and filed it with the third of April's, which is the only filing in that shed that is anybody else's business.

"**Twenty-two days,**" she said.

"Since the second."

"Since the second. And she will not come for a form, Calder, she will come because you asked her, and if you had put a form in that letter you would have got neither."

"I know what I put in it," he said.

"You know what you put in it. You put a man in it. There is a difference and I have never found the word for it and I have stopped looking."

---

And the day itself was not booked, and there is no paper in that drawer to book it against, and that is the reason against the line and it is the same reason as it was on the eighth day of April last and it has not been improved on.

He swept the floor because the floor wants sweeping on a wet day, and the two of them took the drum out of the shed and beat it out on the mole and put the drum back, and that took about an hour and a half and there is no figure for it and there is not going to be one, because a man sweeping a floor is not a man working and the book has a column for the second and a column for the first and neither of them is the right one for an hour and a half with a stick in a drum.

"You are going to write something in the book about today," said Nessa Pike, "or somebody will come along in nine years and find a Thursday with nothing in it at all."

"There is a penny in it," he said, "and eleven days in it, and a bench with a form in it in nine years if we do not do something about that. That will do. **A book with a day in it that cost a penny is a better day than a book with a day in it that cost nothing, and I would rather have a day with a penny in it than a day with nothing in it and nothing to say.**"

---

The far bench was occupied from ten until about four, and the holder of that writ was in that shed from about eight and did not go over to him, and the two of them were in one shed for six hours and did not say one word to each other and that is entered.

And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that afternoon, in the ship's left hand, the seventh line:

*What it is not: — that the four lines written this day to a woman of no office are not a return, and are not a claim, and are not a fifth column, and do not go on any wall in this harbor; and that a yard which has been an address since the third of April past and has never once answered a letter is not a careful yard.*

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the fifteenth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the holder of that writ wrote four lines to a woman of no office at the bench on the said morning, that the four lines asked her to come to that harbor and said why, and that the holder of that writ did not sign them, and that the reason against that line is the reason of the third of April past about a name on a letter and it is not improved on; and that the said four lines went into the ordinary post at a box on the corner of that mole at about half past two in the afternoon and cost a penny; and that one penny went out of that yard's own book on the said afternoon and that is the whole of what went out of it.*

*And that the cistern was charged at no figure on the said morning at about ten and that party walked none of it, being the thirty-eighth of the charges entered walked on none of since the third day of August past, and that the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Thursday.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten until about four and was not approached, and that a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that the holder of that writ and the said man were in one shed for six hours and said nothing to one another.*

*And that one line was entered in the fourth column of the said leaf, being the seventh line in that column, being the line of the fifteenth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that on the fourteenth day of April past the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed that he would not ask a woman of no office and would not write to her and would not send anybody, and that on the fifteenth he did all three, and that the said three are entered here and not in the first column because a thing that was not done on the fourteenth is not a thing that was done, and that the reason against the line of the asking is that a yard which has been an address since the third of April past and has never answered a letter is an address that answers nothing; and that the reason is not improved on and is not going to be.*

*And that the fourth line of the said letter is a man and not a yard, and that the difference between a man and a yard is a thing the woman of no office raised in her letter of the second of April and said she had thought about for three days and had not got round to, and that it is still not got round to, and that nobody in that shed has improved on it, and that the said fourth line is entered in the first column in his words and the difference is not.*

*And that eleven days passed between that woman's letter and this one, and that no figure is entered against the eleven, and that the reason against that line is that eleven days is a fact about two letters and not about a man, and that a yard is a poor judge of how long a silence is.*

---

**One penny went out of this yard on the said Thursday and nothing came into it, and twenty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence is in the bag, which is three thousand nine hundred and thirty pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, being the eleven marks of the sixth of April last, and there is nothing else. The whole of what went out of that yard since the sixth of December past is fifty-nine marks, twelve shillings and threepence in eighteen transactions at seven counters, and one of those is a penny at a box on the corner of that mole, and it is the first of two that are going to that box inside four days.**
