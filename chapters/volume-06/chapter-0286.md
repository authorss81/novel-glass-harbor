# Chapter 286

*The Thirtieth of April, a Far Bench Occupied From Ten Until Four, and a Disagreement About Which Column a Penny Belongs In*

Friday the thirtieth of April, in the shed on the mole.

**The wall did not come down on the said Friday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Friday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Friday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

He wanted it in the first column by eight in the morning and she had the pen out of her apron before he had got the second sentence done.

"**No.**"

"It is a fact."

"**It is a fact about a sheet.**"

"It is a fact that a fleet was ordered down."

"**It is a fact that a sheet of paper says in nine lines that a fleet is ordered down, and this yard bought the sheet off a shelf for a penny off a woman who has never turned it over, and the woman who made the copy did not read what she was copying, and the man who gave her the original bought a house.** That is four people who have not looked at the thing. Put that in the first column and any man in this harbor can come up this hill and ask me where I got it and I have to say *off a shelf.*"

"**Then a man in this harbor comes up this hill and asks me what the sheet says and I have to say I read it off a copy, and that is the answer, and it is not a bad one.**"

"**It is a good one and it is the second column.**"

---

They went at it for about an hour and neither of them raised her voice once, which is not the same as not raising it.

"**Then say the whole of it out loud,**" said Nessa Pike, "**because you have been circling it since Tuesday. You want it in the first column so that in about four years there is one thing in that book that is not a refusal.**"

"Yes."

"**And I said on Saturday last at the bench in front of that woman that a man in four years will read that book and say that this yard never did anything, and that it will be the wrong shape of right, and that there is no column in that book for the saying of it. You said that is what we have got. So you have had three days with it and you have come back with a scheme, and the scheme is to put a penny's worth of a copy of a copy of a sheet off a drowned city into the column for things a man on a mole can go and look at.**"

"**Say the last part again.**"

"**A man on a mole cannot go and look at it. He can go and look at you.**"

---

Mara Venn came in at about eleven and had been in the shed at six that morning and had gone down the hill and come back, and she stood at the end of the bench and listened to about a minute and a half of it and then said:

"**Both of you are arguing about the wrong thing and you have been since eight o'clock. Neither of you is arguing about what goes in the book. You are arguing about what this yard is.**"

"Say it in one sentence," said Calder Marr, "**because I have not got the day.**"

"**The first column is what a man can go and look at. Not what is true, not what matters, what a man can go and look at. That is the whole rule and it is the best rule either of you has ever had, and you have both decided this morning that it is too small a rule for a big thing. It is not. It is exactly the size of the truth, and the big thing is on a shelf in a room off a fish market and there is no man alive who can go and look at it. So it goes in the second column with a reason against the line, and there are eighty lines in that column now and a reason against every one, and that is not a failure of the column, that is what the column is.**"

"And the first column stands at twenty-two entries," said Nessa Pike, "**and every one of them is a day somebody did something, and there is not one of them that a man could not go and check with his own eyes, and that is not an accident and it is not going to become one on a Friday.**"

---

And then Calder Marr said the thing that made the day, and he said it standing at the bench with the sheet still on it under the lamp that was not lit.

"**All right. Here is what I have got and I am going to say it once. For a long time this yard has stood in front of that wall and said to about forty people that the offices of this country profit from not knowing things. I have believed it. I still believe most of it. And I have got a sheet in this shed that says a fleet was ordered down by more than one community, together, and argued about afterwards, and put the argument on the paper and left it there.**"

"That is what it says," said Nessa Pike.

"**So the thing the Crown says it is guarding against is not a story. It is not a shape somebody's grandmother saw. It happened, to real people, in real water, and it was not one man's vanity, and that is a real thing and it really happened and the parties in it were real and they did not all agree.**"

"Yes."

"**And it does not make anybody safe. It is Friday. I have a bag with five marks in it and a wage column with a hundred and four marks on it and twenty-seven weeks of no envelope and a form in a post bag two days down that coast. Knowing that an office has been right to be careful for three hundred years does not put one penny in that bag and does not put a handle on the inside of one drawer in this shed. If anything it is worse, because now I know that the drawer matters.**"

Nessa Pike wrote for about a minute and then she put the pen down, which she does not do in the middle of a morning either.

"**And the other half,**" she said, "**which you are not going to say and which I am going to say and which is that finding the reason does not make the office right. There is a thing this yard has been saying at that wall since the beginning of March, and the thing is that this office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities. That is a figure and it is a true figure and it is in that book. And I am not withdrawing it and I am not softening it because a sheet has come down a hill. A party that says *false* about a record that is incomplete has stopped being the party that says what it is not, and this sheet is incomplete. It is nine lines with no reason in them and two marks that are not the same mark and a ninth they will not talk about and a month and a year gone off at a fold.**"

"That is the whole of my objection," said Calder Marr.

"**Then the motion is that the whole of the sheet goes in the second column with a reason against every line of it, and that no part of it goes on that wall, and that it is not a return and is not a finding and is not a fifth column, and that I will write all of that down this afternoon and read it back tonight.**"

"Agreed."

"**Second column again.**"

---

And then the last of it, and it was the only thing said that day that neither of them wanted to say.

"**We are not going to know who wanted it,**" said Calder Marr.

"No."

"**Nine lines and not one of them names a person, and two marks and not one of them is a face, and the ninth they have told us in their own hand not to ask about. In about four years somebody is going to stand in a room in a city and ask who ordered a fleet down, and this yard is going to have one sheet and a penny's worth of paper and a woman of fifty-eight who has never looked at it.**"

"**And we are not going to find out,**" said Mara Venn. "**Not this year and not by writing to a town, and not by asking four people four days down this coast who go into deep water without a book. I know exactly what that would be worth and I know exactly what it would cost and I am not going to let this yard pay it out of a piece of paper.**"

"And the not-finding is entered," said Nessa Pike, "**with a reason against the line and no name in it, and a second-column fact with no name in it is not a document, and it does not go in the first column, and it does not go on that wall, and nobody is going to be able to go and look at it in about four years and that is what it costs and it is not nothing.**"

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in the ship's left hand, the fifteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that an order made by more than one community and argued about after it was made is not a monster and is not a lie and is not a weapon, and is not a finding, and is not the end of the question; and that knowing why an office has kept itself dark for three hundred years is not the same thing as being safe, and does not put a handle on the inside of a drawer; and that a reason found is not a warrant.*

---

*First column, entered on Friday the thirtieth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said afternoon that the sheet says a fleet was ordered down by more than one community and that the communities did not all agree, and that the said holder said in his own words that the thing the offices of this country say they are guarding against is not a story and happened to real people in real water, and that the said holder said in his own words that it does not make anybody safe and that he has five marks in a bag and twenty-seven weeks of no envelope and a form in a post bag.*

*And that the ship's hand said out loud in that shed on the said afternoon that a party that says false about a record that is incomplete has stopped being the party that says what it is not, and that the said record is incomplete, being nine lines with no reason in them and two marks that are not the same mark and a ninth that the sheet says is not to be asked about and a month and a year gone off at a fold; and that the said figure of three hundred years was not withdrawn and not softened and stands where it has stood.*

*And that the whole of the said sheet was entered in the second column of the said leaf with a reason against every line of it, and that no part of it went on any wall in that harbor, and that it is not a return and is not a finding of worked glass and is not a fifth column, and that four returns stand and there is no fifth.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this yard is not going to find out who ordered the said fleet down, and that the reason against the line of the finding is that a sheet of nine lines with no reason in it does not name anybody, and that the reason against the line of the asking is the reason of the second day of April last about a trade and a price, and that the four people of that coast, four days down it, are not to be asked about it, and that a woman of no office said out loud in that shed on the said day that she knew what it would be worth and what it would cost and was not going to let this yard pay it out of a piece of paper.*

*And that a second-column fact with no name in it is not a document and is not put in the first column and does not go on any wall, and that in about four years nobody will be able to go and look at the said line, and that the reason against the line of the not-looking is the same reason and is not improved on.*

*And that a figure in a stone and two marks on a copy are both differences and neither of them is a truth, and that the said figure was cut in two hands at two times and the said marks are two sizes of the same mark, and that the two of them stand in the second column together and are not the same size of fact and are not to be read as if they were.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on the said day, and that Tobias Wren was not sent for and nothing was asked of him on the said day, and that the far bench was occupied from ten until about four and was not approached.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and five marks, one shilling and fourpence is in the bag, which is eight hundred and sixteen pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is seventy-nine marks, five shillings and one penny in twenty-three transactions at eight counters.**
