# Chapter 299

*Thursday the Thirteenth of May, the Forty-Sixth Charge Walked on None, Two Marks on the Bottom Four Hundred Yards Apart, a Name Said Out Loud Once, and Three People Saying Three Things That Are All True*

Thursday the thirteenth of May, in the shed on the mole, and on the water inside the harbor line in the morning.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-sixth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and one mark and eightpence went out of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Thursday, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

A boat of about forty tons was hired at a mark and eightpence for the day with a man in her, and one mark and eightpence went out of that yard's own book at about half past four in the afternoon at a box on the corner of that mole, and the hire was on a private person's account and for a private person's purpose and was not salvage and was not a claim and was not work under any instrument.

Nobody from that yard went out in her but three, and the man of fifty-one of the second lay was not in her and was not asked and was not sent for, and the reason against the line of the asking is the standing reason and it was given out loud on the steps of that mole on the first of May past and it has not been improved on since, and his ground is not in it.

They were inside the harbor line the whole morning and at no point in the day were they inside the bar of four hundred yards off the outer marks, and the bar was not narrowed by anything and was not asked to be.

---

**The two marks are four hundred yards apart and they are both on the bottom of this harbor's own ground.**

The first is this yard's own, set on the face of the second seam on the twelfth of March past, fouled on the weather side with green.

**On the twelfth of March it was fouled about four inches. On the thirteenth of May it is fouled about a foot and a half.** That is a figure a man can go and look at and count with a rule against a boat's thwart, and it is in the first column for that reason and for no other reason, and nobody in that boat said one word about why a mark fouls faster in some months than in others and nobody is going to.

**And the second mark carries a line on it and a shackle on it, and the shackle is of a make that nobody in this harbor makes.**

**The season of that mark is nine marks and this yard is not going to pay nine marks and is not going to ask anybody for nine marks and there is nobody in this country to ask, and that has not moved since the twelfth of March past and is not moving today.**

Tobias Wren was in that boat. He was not asked to go and he went, and he stood on the thwart with his left hand on the ring of a painter and his right arm in a sling against his chest, and he looked at the second mark for about four minutes from about ten feet off the bottom.

"**I have seen that make,**" he said, "**and I have not seen one in this harbor in twenty years, and there are two things I could say about it and I am not going to say either of them, because I do not know which of the two it is and a man of sixty-one does not get to be right about a thing he has only seen once.**"

"**That is the right answer,**" said Garrow Fisk, "**and it is the first time in about four hundred years anybody has said it and it is not going in a column.**"

"**It is going in the second column with the reason,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and the reason is that a man who has seen a make once is not a man who can be asked what it means and a yard that asked him would be a yard asking for its own comfort.**"

And then the thing that made the day, and it was made at about eleven o'clock in the morning with the boat lying still and about four people in her.

"**Somebody read the name out,**" said Nessa Pike, "**on the fourteenth of March past, in a shed, in front of two people, and the reason the name went in at the head of the third column is that a column with a hand at the head of it and no name is a column any clerk with a pen can fill, and that reason is as good today as it was in March and there is nobody here who can improve on it.**"

"**Then read it,**" said the holder of that writ.

"**No.**"

"**Read it. It is a name in your own book and I have never once asked you for anything out of that book in a year and a half.**"

"**I am not refusing it, I am doing it, and they are not the same thing and you know they are not the same thing.**" She turned the leaf round to the head of the third column and she did not touch the boy's hand at the head of it and she did not scratch it out and nobody in that boat was asked anything about the omissions he has entered and no name of his is in any column of that book and none has ever been. "**The name at the head of the third column is Mara Venn. It is not that boy's and it is not yours and it has not been said out loud in this boat or that shed before today, and it is said out loud once, and that is the once.**"

"**Say it again,**" said Mara Venn.

"**No. Once.**"

---

Then the three of them, on the mole, at about half past three in the afternoon, and the three were about the sill and about the round and about the room, and the three are about three different things and all three of them are true and none of them has been improved on since.

**The first was about the sill, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**Two hundred and nine marks in a row and a day at the end of the row, and a day that is the eleventh of May and was the eleventh of May when we read it on Thursday and had not been cut again. That is what the sill is and it is all the sill is.** A chamber was held, and the holding had a term on it, and the term was written where a man could read it, and no man read it. **I am not going to stand on these steps and tell forty people that a machine with a date on it is a motive, and I am not going to tell nine people either, and if you write *because* anywhere near any of it in that book I will take the pen off you.**"

**The second was about the round, and the woman of no office said it.**

"**I filled in forms for nine years for an office that had one sentence in it about keeping things, and I have never once been shown where that sentence came from. And I have now seen the other end of it, at about seventy fathoms, and the other end is a bed cut in a sill and a screw that goes in two turns and stops.**" She was not looking at either of them. "**A machine that holds a room open for a term, so that somebody has to go and look at it, is the most sensible thing I have ever heard of and whoever cut it deserves to be believed. And they were right. And a reason is not a warrant. And this yard does not get to be the one that comes out of ten days of work at seventy fathoms and says that office was right, because we have not read a word of what was in that room, and if we say false about it we have stopped being the party that says what it is not.**"

"And the two figures have not been swapped," said Calder Marr. "**Nine communities and six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and the two have not been in one another's sentence since the middle of March and they are not going in one tonight.**"

**The third was about the room, and he said it.**

"**Here is the whole of the answer and it is about half of one and the half that is missing is a name. Nine communities put what they had voted into one room, and the votes could not all be true at the same time, and they were held in there together, and a room in which nine sets of incompatible truth are held together is not an archive. It is a hazard. That is not a metaphor and I am not going to use it as one. A thing in that room that had come loose would have come loose into the water, and the water off this mole is where a man of twenty-eight was carried up about eleven feet and put into the edge of a course of squared stone on the ninth of April past, and that is not a symbol, it is a thing that happened to a man who is in a bed at Ashtide until the fifth of June.**"

"**And the proof?**" said the man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch, who was on the steps and had said nothing for an hour.

"**The proof is physical and it is labor-based and about half of it is unreadable. Two hundred and nine marks and a day, cut in a sill, and a round of glass with a collar and a chain on it, and a plate of glass that stood in a bed with nothing behind it and went down into a rebate at about a quarter past two on the eleventh in front of five men and a lead. A man can go and look at every word of that with a lead and fourteen pounds. And the other half is behind a plate of glass that is in a rebate at seventy fathoms, and it is going to stay there, and no chapter of this yard's life is going to say what is behind it.**"

"**Then you have not proved it,**" said the man of about thirty-four.

"**We have proved that the thing the offices of this country say they are guarding against is a room,**" said Calder Marr. "**We have not proved what was in it. And I am not going to say we proved a thing we did not prove, and I am not going to say we proved nothing, and there is a difference between those two sentences and this yard is the only party in this country that knows what it is.**"

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that a shackle of a make nobody in this harbor makes is not a name and is not a maker and is not going to be asked about by anybody in this yard; that two marks fouled at four inches and a foot and a half are not a season and are not a bill and the season of nine marks is not going to be paid; and that three people saying three true things about three different objects is not a finding and is not a resolution and is not the end of the question; and that a name said out loud once in a boat is not an appointment and nobody is appointed to anything.*

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the thirteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a boat of about forty tons was hired at one mark and eightpence for the day with a man in her, and that the hire was on a private person's account and for his own purpose and was not salvage and was not a claim and was not work under any instrument, and that one mark and eightpence went out of that yard's own book at about half past four in the afternoon at a box on the corner of that mole; and that the party was inside the harbor line the whole morning and was not inside the bar of four hundred yards off the outer marks at any hour of the said day and the said bar was not narrowed and was not asked to be.*

*And that two marks on the bottom of that harbor's own ground are four hundred yards apart; that the first of them is the mark of this yard set on the face of the second seam on the twelfth day of March past, fouled on the weather side about four inches on that day and about a foot and a half on the said day, the two figures being entered with the day and the month against each of them; and that the second of the said marks carries a line and a shackle of a make that nobody in this harbor makes; and that the season of the said mark is nine marks and is not paid and is not going to be paid and nobody is going to be asked for it.*

*And that a man of sixty-one went in the said boat unasked and stood on the thwart and looked at the said second mark from about ten feet off the bottom for about four minutes, and that the hand of that man is the right hand and is bandaged, and that no name of his is entered in that book as anything.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch stood on the mole steps on the said afternoon and asked whether the proof had been made, and that the holder of that writ answered him out loud, and that neither of them said the word *because* once.*

*And that a name at the head of the third column was said out loud once in that boat at about eleven o'clock in the morning of the said day, being Mara Venn, and that it is not the boy's and is not the holder of that writ's, and that it had not been said out loud before on any day of this yard's life, and that it is said once and is not to be said again; and that the reason against the line of the entering of any name in that column is the reason of the fourteenth day of March past about a column with a hand at the head of it and no name, and that the said column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it and the boy's hand is at the head of it and has not been scratched out.*

*And that the question asked out loud on the mole steps on the third day of May past, being who was the first one to use a mark on a paper to keep a thing in an emergency instead of holding it, has not been answered by anybody in ten days, and is not going to be answered by anybody in this yard, and goes in no column of that book and on no wall in that harbor and carries no figure.*

---

**One mark and eightpence went out of this yard on the said Thursday and nothing came in, and there are two hundred and sixty-one pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fivepence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and seventy-nine pence, being two marks, four shillings and eleven pence, and that figure is not going to be paid. The cistern was charged for the forty-sixth time this party has walked none of. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-six days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and second night, and neither of them has moved, and a name was said out loud once in a boat and it is in no column of that book.**
