# Chapter 258

*The Twenty-Third Week, and a Season That Ends Tomorrow With Nothing Issued*

Tuesday the thirtieth and Wednesday the thirty-first of March, in the shed on the mole, and in the reeve's office on the west basin.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and the number of weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them is TWENTY-THREE and is the twenty-third and last of that list, and the wage column did not move and the twenty envelopes did not move, and the reason is the reason entered on the twenty-ninth of December past and is not improved on. And the said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday and did not move the figure, and the cistern was not charged on either day, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on either day, and the last wipe was the forty-seventh on the twenty-ninth.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him on either of the two days, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything on either of them and asked nothing on either of them.**

**And no day of work was booked in this yard on either of the two days, and no instrument of any kind was issued to anybody out of the harbor authority's office on the said Wednesday or on any day after it, and the said season of that office ended on the said Wednesday at the end of the working day, and the reason against the line of the said Tuesday is the form of the fourth of that month.**

---

The office was a barge with a wheelhouse and a stove and a counter, and it was at the west basin in a berth of its own that the authority paid for and had not paid for since the autumn, and Ors Kest was standing up behind the counter when Calder Marr came in at about ten on the Wednesday, which was a thing he did, because a man who sits down when a party comes in has decided something in advance.

"I know why you are here," he said. "Say it quickly, because the office closes at four and the office closes at four whatever is in it."

"I want to know whether there is anything to be had out of this office after today."

**"There is not," said Kest, "and I will tell you the three things and then you can go and stand on my counter, because you have not asked me for one of them and I am going to give you all three, and a man who is given three things has not been refused three things, he has been told three things, and I would like that distinction in whatever book you keep."**

He held up a finger.

"One. This office issues no instrument after today. Not a claim window, not a work permit, not a one-season anything. The season ended this morning at the last sitting and the ledger is spent, and a municipal office which issues paper it has no money to weigh and no clerk to receipt is a thing that gets read back in a city in four years as an office that issued paper it could not account for. I have spent the ledger and I am not going to start again on the first of April with a sheet and a man and no money in the drawer, because I did that in the spring of a year I am not going to name and I am not going to do it twice."

He put the finger down and put up the same one again.

"Two. This office is not the office that can give that charter back, and never was, and you have heard me say it twice and there is a clerk in a city who can read it in a form, and I will say it a third time because it is the true sentence and a true sentence does not wear out."

"And the third."

**"The third is the only one that is any use to anybody and I have been sitting on it since the fourth of March and waiting to see whether you would come and get it, and you have come and got it, so here it is.** This office can seat two people and take a finding as it is given, without prejudice to any claim, and it is an ordinary power and it does not need a charter and it does not need a writ and it does not need a season. Two people. One of them from each side. A finding as it is given, and no cross-examination, and no finding that anybody is right. I can put a clerk in that room at eight on a Monday and take what two people say about a mark and write it down and the writing is worth exactly what two people said and not one penny more."

"Then take it," said Calder Marr.

**"On a Monday," said Kest.**

"Yes."

"The hearing room is over the tide-gang store and the tide gang stores its warp and its spare pins and its whole season's gear in that room on a Monday morning because there is nowhere else to put it, and if I sit at eight on a Monday then a tide gang of four men has nowhere to put its season on the one morning of the week it has got."

He said that without any weight on it at all, which was worse than if he had put weight on it.

"What does that cost you," said Calder Marr.

"Two of my four clerks and me, one afternoon a week, in a room I have to be warm for." Kest's mouth did not move. "That is what it costs me. Not a third party, and not a man of your party. Me. And I will do it on a Monday if you turn up with somebody on the other side of your mark, and I will do it without a season and without a charter and I will write down that I did it without either, in my own book, with the date on it, and the cost of it goes against the harbor authority and not against anybody in your shed, because I am the harbor authority and there is nobody else it can go against."

**"Who is the other side," said Calder Marr.**

"That," said the reeve, "is the whole of what is left in that office, and I have been waiting nine days to be asked it, and the answer is that the other side of your mark is a man of about fifty of the second lay who can say a thing or two about fouling and a smith in Ternwatch with a flat on the crown of a shackle who has never heard of you and would not come, and neither of those is an interested party, and if I seat a man of fifty of the second lay as a party I am inventing a proceeding to get at a name, and I do not invent proceedings. That is what this office has got left and it is not enough to seat anybody, and you can see the whole of it from where you are standing."

He put both hands flat on the counter.

"**And I am not a friend of your party, and this is not a rescue, and I have said that in the open twice in this harbor and I am saying it now because in about a month there will be a man in a market telling a story about a reeve who gave a poor yard a room, and I want the first half of that story to be harder to tell than the second half.**"

---

Calder Marr walked back up the mole and stood in the shed door and said the whole of it, in order, to the woman at the bench, and she wrote three lines of it down and then stopped and made him read the three lines back.

"He has one move left," she said.

"He has one move left and he has told us what it costs and what it is worth."

"Which is nothing." She put the nib down. "That's the fourth time an office has said a thing to us that was true and useless, and this is the first time the man saying it told us the price of the uselessness out loud before he said it, and I have written that down, and I am not going to make anything of it, because making something of it is how a man ends up owing a reeve a favour and calling it a partnership."

"He didn't want anything. He wanted us to see him not wanting something."

"He wanted us to see him not wanting something," said Nessa Pike, "and there is no column for that, and if there were it would be the fourth one."

They did not write to the tide gang, and they did not write to a man of fifty of the second lay, and on the Wednesday afternoon the last working day of that harbor authority's season, the counter in that office was shut at four and the stove went out at half past four, and the next instrument any office in that harbor issued, if one ever did, was not going to be a thing that could be used to go and look at anything.

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the thirty-first day of March next at about half past six in the evening in this shed: that on the said Tuesday, being a wage Tuesday, the twenty-third week of the list of weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them fell due, and that the wage column did not move and the twenty envelopes did not move, and that the reason is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December past and is not improved on, and that the said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday and did not move the figure either.*

*And that the holder of that writ went to the harbor authority's office on the west basin on the said Wednesday at about ten and asked whether there was anything to be had out of that office after that day, and that the reeve gave three things and said before giving them that a man who is given three things has not been refused three things.*

*And that the said first thing is that that office issues no instrument after that day, being no claim window and no work permit and no one-season anything, the season having ended at the last sitting and the ledger being spent, and that the reeve said that a municipal office which issues paper it has no money to weigh and no clerk to receipt is an office that will be read back in a city in four years as an office that issued paper it could not account for, and that he had spent the ledger and would not begin again on the first of April with a sheet and a man and no money in the drawer.*

*And that the said second thing is that that office is not the office that can give the charter back and never was, and that the reeve said he had said it twice and that a clerk in a city can read it in a form, and that he said it a third time because a true sentence does not wear out.*

*And that the said third thing is that that office can seat two people and take a finding as it is given without prejudice to any claim, by an ordinary power that needs no charter and no writ and no season, and that the reeve said he had been holding it since the fourth of that month and waiting to see whether this party would come and get it; and that the cost of it is two clerks and the reeve, one afternoon a week, in a room he has to be warm for, and that he said the cost of it goes against the harbor authority and not against anybody in that shed, because he is the harbor authority and there is nobody else it can go against.*

*And that the said finding would be taken at eight on a Monday in the hearing room over the tide-gang store, and that the tide gang of four men stores its warp and its spare pins and its whole season's gear in that room on a Monday morning because there is nowhere else to put it, and that the reeve said that to say without weight on it.*

*And that the holder of that writ asked who the other side is, and that the reeve said that the other side of that mark is a man of about fifty of the second lay who can say a thing or two about fouling and a smith in Ternwatch with a flat on the crown of a shackle who has never heard of this party and would not come, and that neither of those is an interested party, and that seating a man of fifty of the second lay as a party would be inventing a proceeding to get at a name, and that the reeve said he does not invent proceedings, and that the whole of what is left in that office is that and it is not enough to seat anybody.*

*And that the reeve said out loud in his own office that he is not a friend of that party and that this is not a rescue, and that he said it because in about a month there will be a man in a market telling a story about a reeve who gave a poor yard a room.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past six in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that nothing was written to the tide gang and nothing was written to the man of the second lay, and that the three lines the ship's hand wrote of that office were read back by the holder of that writ before the day was out.*

*And that a man of about fifty of the second lay has a boat he would come in and a fortnight he would give and a trade he is good at, and that this yard did not ask him for either, and that the reason is not a reason and is a person of no office, and that it is the same refusal that was made on this mole on the eleventh of March in about forty people's hearing and it is not improved on.*

*And that the said woman said out loud in that shed that making something of a reeve's not wanting something is how a man ends up owing a reeve a favour and calling it a partnership, and that there is no column in that book for a reeve not wanting something, and that if there were it would be the fourth one.*

**Nothing came into this yard on either of the said two days and nothing went out of it on either of them, and sixty-seven marks and one shilling is in the bag, which is ten thousand seven hundred and thirty-two pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is ten thousand and ninety-two pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the sixteen days ended on the said Wednesday is nothing, and the reason is the form of the fourth of March and not a mood. The whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is six marks, five shillings and five pence in nine transactions at five counters, and nothing has come into that bag since the fourteenth of March past. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and he is not in that book and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days and no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is on a page of that book. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, and that yard has not asked her for anything since the twenty-sixth of December past. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots stand, the third column is not filled, nobody is appointed to it, and the boy's hand is at the head of it. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-three weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and the figure of twenty-three is the whole of the list of that year and is entered in that book in the same breath and not one after the other, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, the second and the ninth of March instant, and the sixteenth, the twenty-third and the thirtieth of March instant. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it was said out loud for the fourth time in seven months on the fourth of that month and will not be said a fifth time. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of that yard has been inside its first clause on either of these two days, and no return of any kind was made out of either of them, and the season of the harbor authority ended on the said Wednesday and no instrument was issued out of that office on it or after it. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter out of a shed, and that office is not this one and has no power over it. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, was not raised and was not asked about in these two days, and the limit four people of that coast wrote and signed stands. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered and nothing was measured. The two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and ninety-three days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for a sixtieth night, counting from the Sunday of the thirty-first of January instant, and it is the only bare thing in that shed. A working harbor of that coast is poorer on the thirty-first day of March next than it was on the twenty-third day of February past, and one herring berth of two is out and the third lay fills on the first of April and the north berth is out and the mole-head light is not on and the fire party's hose is dead until the spring and the tide gang is four men short and the hard at Ashtide is unanswered.**
