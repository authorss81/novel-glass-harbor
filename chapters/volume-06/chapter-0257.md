# Chapter 257

*The Question at the Mole Head, and Three Facts That Are Not an Answer*

Sunday the twenty-eighth and Monday the twenty-ninth of March, at the head of that dock, in the shed, and at the reeve's bench on the west basin.

**At eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was the forty-seventh Monday since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Monday morning by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the thirty-third of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and no day of work was booked in this yard on either of the said two days, and the reason against the line of the said Monday is that four men this yard does not know have been at that cistern since ten.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him on either of the two days, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything on either of them and asked nothing on either of them.**

**And the question this volume exists for was asked out loud on the said Sunday at the head of that dock, in about eleven people's hearing, by a man of no office, and was not answered, and the answer given was three facts and a refusal, and the yard said out loud before it gave them that none of them is an answer.**

---

The man was thirty-one and of the third lay and he had a coaster out of Ashtide with a second hand on her, and he had come up this coast on the Friday before, being the twenty-sixth of that month, with a chart in a bag, and he put the chart on the fish quay at about ten on the Sunday morning where anybody could get at it, which is a thing that costs a man his afternoon and more.

It was a Crown chart of the western approaches. It had a plate on it and a number and a printer's name in Veyr, and the plate said, in a legend about six lines long, that the ground marked as a wreck field was the remains of a fleet lost in a sea catastrophe, and that vessels navigating in the vicinity should keep clear of the shoals, and that the loss of the fleet was a matter of record and not of dispute.

"I have been three years on that water," he said. "I have started that coaster on it. My father took a bigger boat than that on it and so did his father. I am not asking you for a story. I am asking you whether a fleet was scuttled or whether it went down, because one of those two is on this chart in Veyr and the other one is in eleven people's heads on this mole, and I have a mate who thinks the other one, and I cannot settle it with him, and I have tried."

He said it at the head of the dock at about ten, in front of the market going up, and about eleven people heard it, and about four of them listened.

**Calder Marr had been in that harbor four days and had not stood at the head of that dock since the Saturday before he went up the coast road, and he went down and stood there, and he answered it, and he answered it badly, and he knew he was answering it badly while he was doing it, which is a thing that has happened to him four times in five volumes and never once at the top of a mole with people standing round.**

"I am going to give you three things," he said, "and I am going to tell you before I give them that not one of them is an answer, and I am not going to stand here and call a fact an answer, and if anybody writes down after this that a yard in Morrow Quay was asked and said three numbers, then the three numbers will be the answer, and they are not, and you will be able to check that they are not because I have said it out loud.

"The first. There is nobody in this harbor old enough. Everybody who was on that water is dead, and the record that would carry it is not a record anybody alive can hold in their hands, and I have been at that for a year and I have got four lines in a hand that is not mine that I did not ask for, and I could not pay for the cart.

"The second. The only thing that could answer you is at the bottom. Seventy fathoms, which is four hundred and twenty feet, and about four hundred miles west-southwest of the head of this mole, and there is a name for the place and I am not going to say the name in a market, because a name said in a market is a thing that gets repeated, and a name repeated is a name that is asked for by offices, and there is a man of sixty-one of this harbor who said that name once, years back, to four people in a shed, and he said that nobody on this coast goes there, and he has not been asked about it since and I am not going to ask him about it today.

"And the third. This party has no instrument of any kind. There is a paper that used to let us work inside this harbor line and it was taken off this mole on the fourth of this month in a hand, and the form it came in says nothing wrong in it, and the writ that was never the same thing stopped existing at ten minutes past midnight on the first of this month, and the licence that would take a man past the outer marks is shut to me on a form that is not a refusal and is not a penalty, and there is a notice on a door in this town with four clauses in it that I cannot lift, and there is a bar at four hundred yards off the outer marks that nobody has narrowed."

He stopped there. He had said three things and he had said that none of them was an answer, and he had not said why the second one had no name on it, and he was aware of both of those things while he was standing there.

The man of thirty-one looked at him for a while.

"That is three numbers and a refusal," he said.

"Yes."

"That is a refusal and three numbers, and the second one has a hole in it, and I have a hole in mine, and I would rather have the hole than the sentence." He shouldered his bag. "I am not going to tell you that I am satisfied, because you have not told me anything and I did not expect you to, and I have got a chart in Veyr that says what it says and I cannot unbuy it with a morning at a mole. I am going to take that chart back to Ashtide and I am going to sail on it. And I would ask you one thing and I would rather you said no than said nothing."

"Ask it."

"You have printed a sheet of thirty-eight lines." The man did not raise his voice. "There is a copy of it in about sixty boats on this coast and the thirty-eighth line says where every other copy of it is, and the man who printed it says in that line that if a man's copy has changed he is to go and say so. **I am asking you whether the thirty-eighth line is going to be there in four years.** That is all. I am a coaster's mate with a chart in Veyr and a mate who thinks the other one, and I am not asking you for a fleet. I am asking you for a line."

**"It will be there in four years," said Calder Marr, "because it is nailed to a wall in a town four days up this coast under four nails with nothing over it, and nobody can take a sheet off a wall without somebody seeing, and if it goes they will see. And if it does not go, that is a thing we will not find out about for four years either, and I am not going to tell you a better answer than that, because I have not got one and every time I have given this yard a better answer than that it has been a worse answer six months later."**

The man of thirty-one nodded at that, which he had not expected to do, and went up the mole, and the market came down about the middle of the hour, and about eleven people had heard a question and four of them had listened, and the four lines said on the coast road were in a coat in a shed upstairs and were not on a wall in this harbor.

---

Nessa came in at about eleven and asked him what he had said, and he told her, and she wrote none of it down.

"You will write none of it down."

"None of it. He asked me a question in front of a market and I answered him in front of a market and both of those are gone by the middle of the hour. If I write it down it is a thing a yard said about itself in a book, and in four years a man in a city reads it and thinks a yard knows whether a fleet was scuttled."

"**You told him a chart in Veyr is wrong.**"

"I told him we have no instrument and no witness and a hole in the second thing, and I told him that none of it is an answer, in those words, out loud, in front of people. He will go back to Ashtide and sail on a chart that says what it says and he will say to anybody who asks him that he went to Morrow Quay and asked a yard, and he will say the yard told him the name of the place was in its mouth and would not say it." He put his hand flat on the table. "That is going to be in about nine other harbors by midsummer, Nessa. That is what a question at a mole head is for. You cannot un-say it and you should not want to, and every one of those nine places is going to send somebody up that coast road, and the four people who keep the book of ground are going to be asked nine times a year by strangers about a page that is theirs."

"Yes," said Nessa. "I have been thinking about that since Tuesday and I have not got a better version of it. That is what the thirty-eighth line buys. You put a line on a sheet that says where every other copy is, and eleven days later a man of thirty-one stands at a mole head asking a question he got from a sheet, and the answer he gets is a refusal, and the refusal is better than the sheet was, and neither of them is the fleet."

"No," he said. "Neither of them is the fleet."

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-ninth day of March next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind that harbor authority's reeve's bench came down and came down bare, and that that was the forty-seventh Monday since the first of May past, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book.*

*And that on the said Monday the cistern was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and that party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the thirty-third of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past.*

*And that on the said Sunday at about ten in the morning at the head of that dock, with the market going up and about eleven people standing, a man of about thirty-one of the third lay who has a coaster out of Ashtide with a second hand in her put a Crown chart of the western approaches on the fish quay where anybody could get at it, and said out loud that he was not asking for a story and was asking whether a fleet was scuttled or whether it went down, and gave the reason, being that he has been three years on that water and has started his coaster on it and has a mate who thinks the other of the two and cannot settle it with him.*

*And that the plate on the said chart says in a legend of about six lines that the ground marked as a wreck field is the remains of a fleet lost in a sea catastrophe and that vessels should keep clear of the shoals and that the loss of the fleet is a matter of record and not of dispute; and that the said man said that he was not going to let this yard unbuy him a chart in Veyr with a morning at a mole, and that he was going to sail on it.*

*And that the holder of that writ answered the said question in the said place in the said hearing, and gave three things, and said out loud before he gave them that not one of them was an answer and that he was not going to call a fact an answer; and that the three things were that there is nobody in this harbor old enough and that the record that would carry it is not a record anybody alive can hold; that the only thing that could answer it is at the bottom at seventy fathoms and about four hundred miles west-southwest of the head of that mole and that there is a name for the place and that he was not going to say the name in a market, and the reason against that line being that a name said in a market is a name that gets asked for by offices; and that this party holds no instrument of any kind, being the paper taken off that mole on the fourth of that month, the writ that ceased to exist at ten minutes past midnight on the first, the licence that is shut to him on a form which is not a refusal and not a penalty, a notice with four clauses on a door in that town that he cannot lift, and a bar at four hundred yards off the outer marks that nobody has narrowed.*

*And that the said man said that that was three numbers and a refusal and a hole in the second of them, and that the holder of that writ said yes, and that nobody said anything else about it.*

*And that the said man asked one question, being whether the thirty-eighth line of that yard's printed sheet will be there in four years, and that the holder of that writ answered that it will be there in four years because it is nailed to a wall in a town four days up that coast under four nails with nothing over it and nobody can take a sheet off a wall without somebody seeing, and that if it does not go nobody in that party will find out about that for four years either, and that he said out loud that he has not got a better answer than that.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that nothing of that answer was written down, and that the reason is that a thing said out loud at the head of a dock in the morning is gone by the middle of the hour, and that a thing written down in that yard's own book is a thing a yard said about itself, and that in about four years a man in a city would read it and think a yard knows whether a fleet was scuttled, and that it does not know.*

*And that a question asked at the head of a dock goes to about nine other harbors by midsummer off the back of the thirty-eighth line, and that the four people who keep the book of ground four days down that coast are going to be asked nine times a year by strangers about a page that is theirs, and that the said woman said that that is what the thirty-eighth line buys, and that the answer those nine places will get is a refusal, and that a refusal is better than a sheet and is not a fleet, and that neither of them is the fleet.*

*And that the name of the place at seventy fathoms is not on a page of that yard's book and was said once in a shed in the year before last by a man of sixty-one to four people and has not been said since and is not going to be said in a market, and that no office in Veyr is going to be given it, and that the holder of that writ put that refusal in a market in about eleven people's hearing in the order the second of three things rather than at the end of it.*


**Nothing came into this yard on either of the said two days and nothing went out of it on either of them, and sixty-seven marks and one shilling is in the bag, which is ten thousand seven hundred and thirty-two pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is ten thousand and ninety-two pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the fourteen days ended on the said Monday is nothing, and the reason is the form of the fourth of March and not a mood. The whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is six marks, five shillings and five pence in nine transactions at five counters, and nothing has come into that bag since the fourteenth of March past. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and he is not in that book and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, and that yard has not asked her for anything since the twenty-sixth of December past and did not ask her in these two days, although a man of about thirty-one of the third lay was in that harbor on the Friday and the Saturday and is not on that coast and is not a woman of no office and was not asked anything either. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots stand, the third column is not filled, nobody is appointed to it, and the boy's hand is at the head of it. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-two weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and neither of these two days was a wage Tuesday and the column did not move. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it was said out loud for the fourth time in seven months on the fourth of that month and will not be said a fifth time. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of that yard has been inside its first clause on either of these two days, and no return of any kind was made out of either of them. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter out of a shed. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, was not raised and was not asked about in these two days, and the limit four people of that coast wrote and signed stands. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered on either of these two days and nothing was measured and the delay has not moved and nobody in that harbor has ever said why. The two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and ninety-one days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, and neither has moved, and that party did not go near them on either of these two days. The bench under that pump is bare for a fifty-eighth night, counting from the Sunday of the thirty-first of January instant, and it is the only bare thing in that shed.
