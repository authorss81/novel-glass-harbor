# Chapter 294

*Saturday the Eighth of May, a Pane of Plate Glass on a Mole, a Mark and Four Shillings to a Bonesetter, and Until the Second of June*

Saturday the eighth of May, on the crown of that mole, in a room in this harbor, and in the shed on that mole in the evening.

**The wall did not come down on the said Saturday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and two hundred and eight pence went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The pane of plate was still on the mole stones where it had lain since about six the evening before, four feet by three, edge up, and the wind had got under it twice in the night and it had not gone anywhere, and the man of about fifty-five had come back for it at seven in the morning with a boy of about seventeen and a rope.

There is a thing about a pane of plate glass and it is not in any book: two men can lift four feet by three between them and they can carry it the length of a mole, and they cannot put it back into a frame in a wind, and everybody in that harbor knows it and nobody has ever written it down.

Tobias Wren was on the crown because he had been on the crown since six and because a man who has been on a mole for an hour will help with a thing on a mole.

He took the near end and the man of about fifty-five took the far end and the boy took the rope, and about two-thirds of the way along it the wind came round the head of the mole and took the pane and put it over on the crown of the mole and across the right hand of a man of sixty-one, edge first, once.

---

**The first part of it is the hand.**

The root of the thumb and the side of the little finger of the right hand, opened along two lines about two inches and a half long, and it is the right hand and not the left, and the hand is a hand of about forty and it is the hand that goes on a cold ring and takes a warp and holds a lead line and holds nothing else worth the name.

He did not say anything about it on the mole. He sat down on the crown of the mole about four feet away from where the pane was and put his hand in his coat, and when Garrow Fisk came up the steps at about half past seven and saw him sitting, Fisk did not ask him what was the matter, and Fisk went and got him water.

---

**The second part of it is the bill.**

The bonesetter is a man of about sixty in a room over a chandler's two streets off, and he has set about four hundred things in thirty-one years and he has never been asked by this yard for anything in his life, and he came down to the mole at about nine in the morning because he does not go up a hill for a man who will not come down.

He washed it with something out of a brown bottle and closed it with two stitches and a strip, and he took his own time and he did not talk while he did it.

"**That is the palm and not the back,**" he said at last. "**Two lines and they are clean and the one across the root of the thumb has gone into the muscle and the one along the little finger has not gone near the bone. I have felt for it and I know what I felt for.**"

"**Will it close.**"

"**It will close. That is not the question and you know it is not the question.**" He put the brown bottle away. "**The question is a hand of forty in a man of sixty-one and a hand of forty is not a young man's hand. This one will grip again by the middle of June. It will grip about two-thirds of what it gripped on Friday, and the two-thirds is a cold thing — a cold ring, a cold lead line, a wet warp in December. It will be nine parts in ten by Christmas and it will be nine parts in ten for the rest of what you have got, and you will be able to do most things and you will not be able to do the one thing, and you already know which one it is.**"

"**Say the figure and say it once.**"

"**A mark and four shillings, being two hundred and eight pence, and that is for the two stitches and the bottle and my time and it is not going up, and I am not going to be asked twice and I am not going to say no to you once.**" He looked at him. "**And I am not going to hear a word about who brought you.**"

Nessa Pike paid it out of that yard's own book at about twenty past ten in the morning at a counter over a chandler's, in the ship's own left hand, and two hundred and eight pence went out of that yard and nothing came into it.

---

**The third part of it is the absence, and it is a figure and not a hope, and he is the man who insisted on that.**

"**I was going out on the nineteenth of May,**" said Tobias Wren. "**There is a thing on the north bar that has been on since Tuesday and I have the gear for it and there is nobody else in this harbor with gear for it.**"

"**Then it stays.**"

"**Then it stays.**" He said it without any weight at all. "**And I will not be on the water until the second of June and that is a figure and not a hope and I have got it from the same man who has just told me about the cold, and I am not going to be argued out of it by a yard of two and a half.**"

"**I am not going to try,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No. You are not. And that is the second thing I have got from you this week and I have not thanked you for it and I am not going to.**"

---

And then the thing that had gone, which nobody in that shed had looked at until about four in the afternoon.

**His boat of about nineteen tons was lying alongside that mole on Thursday night and she had taken a stove on the Friday night in the weather, and nobody saw it happen and nobody has said what caused it and this yard is not going to.**

She was sitting on her marks in the morning tide with about nine inches of water in her and about two feet over her rail, and she is a boat he built himself in a shed at a town four days up that coast fifty years ago or near it, and the frames came out of her in eleven pieces on the Saturday afternoon and the eleven pieces are on that mole and they are not going back in.

There is no other one. There is no figure for it in this book and there never will be and there is nobody in this country this yard could ask for one.

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-first line:*

*What it is not: — that a hand of about forty in a man of sixty-one is not a young man\'s hand and does not close like one, and that a figure given by a bonesetter about a cold ring is not a hope and is not to be entered as a hope; and that a boat a man built himself is not a boat that can be hired at a mark and eightpence; and that a man who is not thanked has not been given something and is owed nothing for it.*

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the eighth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about half past seven in the morning of the said day, on the crown of that mole, a pane of plate glass about four feet by three, which had lain on those stones since about six in the evening preceding, went over across the right hand of a man of sixty-one, edge first, once; and that the said man is a retired salvage foreman, half deaf, who owns a boat that is not this yard\'s, and whose right hand is a hand of about forty and is the hand that goes on a cold ring and takes a warp and holds a lead line.*

*And that a bonesetter of about sixty of this harbor, who has set about four hundred things in thirty-one years and who has never been asked by this yard for anything in his life, came down to that mole at about nine in the morning of the said day, and that the said man of sixty-one has two stitches in the said right hand and the said bonesetter said out loud that the said hand will grip again by the middle of June and about two-thirds of what it gripped on the Friday, and about nine parts in ten by Christmas and about nine parts in ten for the rest of what the said man has got; and that two hundred and eight pence went out of this yard\'s own book at about twenty past ten in the morning of the said day at a counter over a chandler\'s, being a mark and four shillings, and that nothing came into this yard on the said day.*

*And that the said man of sixty-one said out loud on the said afternoon that he would not be on the water until the second day of June, and that he had a job on the nineteenth day of May on the north bar and the gear for it and would not be going, and that he said that figure was a figure and not a hope and that he would not be argued out of it.*

*And that a boat of about nineteen tons lying alongside that mole had taken a stove on the night of the Friday preceding in the weather, that nobody saw it and that nobody has said what caused it and that this yard is not going to; and that the said boat was built by her owner in a shed in a town four days up that coast fifty years ago or near it, and that eleven pieces of her frame came out of her on the afternoon of the said day and are on that mole and are not going back in.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said man of sixty-one was not thanked and said he was not going to be, and that the reason against the line of thanking him is that he said out loud on the Wednesday preceding that he did not want to be, and that the yard is not going to improve on a man telling it what he does not want.*

*And that this yard has a rule about a man it has paid and has not got a rule about a man it has hurt, and that the said holder of that writ said so out loud in that shed on the said afternoon and did not pretend there was a third thing about it.*

*And that the said pane of plate glass and the said boat and the said hand are three things and are not one thing, and that the two of them about the weather are not the weather\'s fault and the one of them about the hand is not a glass storm\'s fault either, and that no cause is entered against any of them.*

---

**Two hundred and eight pence went out of this yard on the said Saturday and nothing came in, and there is one thousand two hundred and fifty-seven pence in the bag, which is seven marks, eleven shillings and fivepence. A man of sixty-one has two stitches in a right hand of about forty and will not be on the water until the second day of June, and a boat of about nineteen tons is in eleven pieces on the crown of that mole, and the two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-one days up that crown about nine feet from a crate, and the bench under that pump is bare for the ninety-seventh night, and none of those three things carries a figure in this book except the one that carries a figure.**
