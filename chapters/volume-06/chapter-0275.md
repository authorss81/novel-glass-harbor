# Chapter 275

*The Fiftieth Time That Wall Came Down, and an Address a Mile and a Quarter Off the Head of a Mole*

Monday the nineteenth of April, in the shed on the mole, and on the crown of that mole.

**At eight in the morning the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was the fiftieth Monday since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Monday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the thirty-ninth of the charges entered walked on none of since the third of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil. The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

The wall is the fifty-first thing that happened that week and nobody in that harbor mentioned it to anybody, and the reason nobody mentioned it is in the second column and it is the reason of the fourth of March past and has not been improved on.

What the two of them did instead, between nine and about one, was wait, and there is no figure for waiting and there is not going to be one.

That is the honest name for it. A sheet of paper went into a box on a corner on Sunday afternoon at half past four and it is somewhere on a coast road now with a man in a cart in front of it, and the road is two days, and the post in that town comes up the hill and goes down the front street, and there is no way on this coast to know which day of that week it will arrive on, and that is not a thing that can be improved on by anybody sitting in a shed.

"**Say the arithmetic out loud,**" said Nessa Pike. "**A sheet is a sheet. A man can go and look at a sheet. That is the whole of what a first column is for and you have had it in your mouth for six years. This yard has a book with two columns and a third and a fourth, and the whole of the house it has been arguing is that a record a man cannot check is worth less than one he can. And this yard has put a sheet in a post office and cannot go and look at it.**"

"That is the first thing this yard has written that it cannot go and look at," said Calder Marr.

"**No it is not. It is the third. The five pages of the sixth of March are the first and the six lines in the fourth column are the second, and you did not write a word about either of them, and you have not said the sentence about either of them either.**"

"Then why am I saying it now."

"**Because this time it is going to arrive on somebody's step and not in somebody's drawer and because in about four years you are going to want to say that you did not know what was on it, and you did not know what was on it, and the four lines in that column say so already, and you did not have to say it out loud in a shed on a Monday and you have now, and that is the whole of what saying it out loud in a shed on a Monday does.**"

---

And then they went out to the outer end of the mole at about two o'clock, which is not work and is not a decision and is what a man does with an afternoon he has nothing for.

A man can stand on the crown of that mole for about a minute and a half and see the outer marks, and they are a mile and a quarter off the head, and from the outer end they are two points off the port bow with the sea breaking in between.

They stood there for about twenty minutes.

"**I have been on that mole every working day of my life,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have never once laid my eye on the outer marks from this ground, and there is a figure in a book in a town two days down this coast that says where they are, and a figure in a book is not a mark.**"

"**You have looked at them,**" said Nessa Pike, "from the top of the head of this dock, on the day in March when a man of thirty-one put a Crown chart on a fish quay in this harbor and got three numbers and a refusal."

"That was a mile and a quarter and I was looking through about nine people and a boat's side. And that is not the same thing as standing on the end of the ground you are working and putting your thumb on where the marks are, and the difference between those two is the difference between a thing a man said and a thing a man did, and this yard has one column for each of them and it has been writing in the wrong one for a fortnight."

"**Then go and do it,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I cannot.** The bar is four hundred yards off the outer marks and the bar has not been narrowed by anything and this party is not going to ask to have it narrowed, and if a boat of this yard goes to the outer marks it is in the water half a mile short of where the marks are and every man in this harbor will know it, and there is a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a Crown chart in a bag who will hear about it inside a week and come up this hill with it. And I am not going to stand on this mole in April and go out and look at a line I have told the world I am keeping to by not looking at it."

"**Then a man who is not this yard can look at it,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and a man of this yard can pay him, and there is no paper in that drawer to book it against and that is the same reason it was on the eighth day of April last and it has not been improved on.**"

---

They walked back in. And the decision they took on the way in was a small one and it is entered, and it is the second time in six days that this yard has refused to do a thing it could have done.

"Do not write to him," said Calder Marr.

"**No,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and put the reason against it, because you are going to think of it on Wednesday and you are going to want to.**"

"**We put a sheet in the post on Sunday at half past four and it is going to arrive at that address. If a letter goes behind it, then two things from this yard arrive in one week at one door, and the first of them was a letter and the second one is a form, and a man who cannot read is going to have two pieces of paper from a place he has never heard of and one of them is going to be about the other one. That is not a channel. That is a siege.**"

"And the reason against the line of the not-writing is entered and it is that a channel is a door and this yard said that out loud on the third of April past and it has had eleven days to think about what it meant."

---

*First column, entered on Monday the nineteenth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wall behind that harbor authority's reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning of the said Monday, being the fiftieth Monday since the first of May past, and that the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern was charged at no figure at about ten on the said morning and that party walked none of it, being the thirty-ninth of the charges entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that two people of that yard stood on the crown of that mole at about two in the afternoon of the said day for about twenty minutes and looked at the outer marks, being a mile and a quarter off the head of that mole, and that the said outer marks were seen and no figure was taken and nothing was entered against them, and that the reason against the line of that is that the bar of four hundred yards off the outer marks has not been narrowed by anything and this party did not ask to have it narrowed and did not ask for it in advance.*

*And that it was said out loud in that shed on the said afternoon that the said outer marks have not been laid from that ground before, and that it was said out loud in that shed that a boat of that yard may not go to the outer marks on the said day and did not go, and that no person and no boat and no lead of that yard was inside that bar on the said day or on any day.*

*And that it was decided in that shed on the said afternoon that no letter is to be written to the man of about thirty who cannot read in the town two days down that coast, and that no letter was written, and that the reason against the line of the not-writing is in the second column.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that two people sat in that shed between nine and one on the said morning doing nothing, and that the reason against that line is that a sheet of paper was in a mail bag on a coast road and that there is no way on this coast to know the day of a week it will arrive on, and that there is no figure for waiting and there is not going to be one.*

*And that the said shipwright said out loud in that shed that this yard has put three things into the world that it cannot go and look at, being the five pages of the sixth of March, the six lines of the fourth column, and the said sheet, and that the third of them is going to arrive on a step and not in a drawer, and that the reason against the line of that is that a record a man cannot check is worth less than a record he can and that this yard has said that in that shed and in that book and does not get to have it both ways.*

*And that the holder of that writ said in that shed that a boat of that yard may not go to the outer marks because the bar stands four hundred yards off them and the party has told this coast it is keeping to that bar by not going near it, and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay has a Crown chart in a bag and would hear of it inside a week, and that the reason against the line of that is that the bar has not been narrowed by anything and this party did not ask to have it narrowed and is not going to.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that the said line is entered here and is entered in the first column of the days on which the said bench was occupied, and that the said bench was occupied on the sixteenth of April past and was not occupied at any hour on any other day up to and including the said Monday, and that the count of the ten is not entered until the ten are done.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and twenty-one marks, seven shillings and fivepence is in the bag, which is three thousand four hundred and forty-nine pence, and that is the same figure it was on Sunday night. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is sixty-two marks, twelve shillings and fourpence in twenty transactions at seven counters. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-five weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and the next wage Tuesday is the twentieth of April next.**
