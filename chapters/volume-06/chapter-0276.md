# Chapter 276

*The Twenty-Sixth Week, a Morning Lost to the Body, and Three Doors on a Front Street*

Tuesday the twentieth of April, in the shed on the mole, and along the front street of that harbor.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and the wage was not paid, and the twenty-sixth week fell due with no envelope standing against it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks where it has stood since the fifth of March past and did not move a line, and the twenty envelopes are on that bench, and twenty-seven weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them.**

**And the wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, and no figure is entered against a day this party did not see, and nothing came into that yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten until about two and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Tuesday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

He lost the morning.

He was up at eight and he sat down at about half past nine to write Monday's figures out fair, and he got one line, and he put his hand flat on the bench and left it there and said, "I have got to go and stand at something for a bit," which is the only sentence anybody in that harbor has ever used for it and which he has not coined and has not been asked about.

The ship's hand brought him out a cup of water at about eleven and no more than that, and he stood at the bench outside the shed until about one, and then he was better in a way nobody could check, and at about two he went down the front street with nine feet of chain over his shoulder and a bar of square iron in his coat.

**The day carries no figure, because there is no figure for a day.** It is in the second column with a reason against the line and the reason has no name in it and nobody is going to put one in it, and the reason against the line is the reason that has been against that line on every day lost since the sixth of October past.

---

Three doors, and about nine people in the street, and nobody counted them twice.

**The first was a chandler with a shop on that front street, and a chandler's shop is a place where wire is bought every day of the week by people who know what wire is.**

The man looked at about a foot of it and put it down.

"That is not wire," he said. "**That is chain, and it is made chain, and it is nine feet of it, and I have had a thing like that out of a wreck once and I paid a man four shillings for it and I did not know what I had bought for another three weeks.**"

"You would not take it at four shillings now."

"I would not take it at nothing and I would not take it at four shillings, and there is a difference and I do not care which one you are trying to make. **I have a shop and a name and I am not putting a thing in my window that came off a wreck I was not there at.**"

---

**The second was a boat's owner of about fifty-five who keeps one boat and has been a berth short since the middle of July past and has not said so to this yard and did not say so now.**

He came out onto the step and did not ask what it was, which is a thing a man of about fifty-five does.

"**Whose is it,**" he said.

"It came up out of a cut off the south end on the ninth of April past and it was on the bottom at forty fathoms and it is not ours."

"Then it is not yours and you have been carrying it about the front of that harbor for a fortnight, and if you had asked me that in the first ten seconds I would have told you not to leave it on a wall for a cat to sit on." He looked at it and did not touch it. "**You are carrying a man's chain and you have told nine people on this street that you have, and I hope you go on saying so, because the next man who comes down here with a thing off a wreck is going to be able to say that this yard asks.**"

"That was not the idea."

"**No. And I have told you the next worst thing that happens to a thing, and a cat is not it.**"

---

**The third was a slop-chest and a counter in a shed off the back of that market, and the man in it is about sixty-one and he offered a shilling.**

He took about two feet of it in his hand and turned it and put it down again.

"**A shilling,**" he said, "**and I would take it and I would put it over that counter and I would be two shillings better off than I was on Monday, and I will not do it, and you are going to refuse it and I want to know that you are going to refuse it for the reason I think and not for a better one.**"

"I am not taking a shilling for it."

"**Say the reason.**"

"Because a shilling is money, and the day that thing is a transaction this yard is trading in a thing it does not own and cannot say whose it is and has not asked, and I would rather carry it about this harbor for the rest of my life than have one shilling of it in this book. **I would rather it sat on a nail in that shed and everybody knew what it was, because a nail is a thing that cannot be a sale.**"

The man of about sixty-one looked at him for a while and then gave him back the two feet of it.

"That is the reason," he said. "**I have been in this trade forty years and that is the first time a man has given me that one.** And I am not going to tell you what I would have said if you had said a better one, because you did not say one, and I have had a week in which to make up my mind about this yard and I have not made it up."

---

And on the way back up the mole he stopped at the head of that dock, which is where a man of about thirty-one of the third lay put a Crown chart on a fish quay on the twenty-eighth of March past and got three numbers and a refusal, and there was nobody on the quay at half past five on a Tuesday in April and there has not been a herring boat in that market for two days.

"He is not here," said Nessa Pike.

"**He is never here on a Tuesday, and he is not going to be, and we are not going to find out whether he is here.**"

---

That was the whole of the afternoon and there is no figure for it and there is not going to be one, because nothing was bought and nothing was sold and nothing came in and nothing went out.

What it cost is in the first column and it is not a money figure and there is no conversion for it: **three doors on a front street and about nine people now know that this yard has nine feet of chain and a bar of iron it is trying to get rid of, and one man of about fifty-five has said out loud that the next man down that street with a thing off a wreck will be able to say that this yard asks.**

Nessa Pike entered that in the first column because a man said it in the street with the door open, and entered it again in the second column with the reason against the line, and the reason against the line is that the first column is where a man goes and looks.

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twentieth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the twenty-sixth week fell due on the said Tuesday with no envelope standing against it, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that there are twenty envelopes on that bench.*

*And that nine feet of chain and a bar of square iron two feet long were carried by the holder of that writ along the front street of that harbor on the afternoon of the said day, from about two until about five, and were offered at three places, being a chandler's shop, a boat's owner of about fifty-five, and a slop-chest and a counter in a shed off the back of that market; and that all three were refused, and the reasons are given with the refusals in the second column, and that a shilling was offered for the said chain and was refused, and that the reason against the line of that refusal is given in the first column in the holder of that writ's words.*

*And that a man of about fifty-five of that harbor said out loud on his own step that the next man who comes down that street with a thing off a wreck will be able to say that this yard asks, and that the said sentence was entered without improvement, and that about nine people were in that street and the said man was one of them and none of them is named.*

*And that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ lost the morning of the said day to the body, and that the day carries no figure because there is no figure for a day, and that the reason against the line of that is the reason that has been against that line on every day lost since the sixth day of October past and it has no name in it and nobody in that shed is going to put one in it.*

*And that a chandler refused the said chain and said he would not take it at nothing and would not take it at four shillings and that there is a difference, and that the reason against the line of the asking is not entered because a chandler does not have to give a reason to a man on a street.*

*And that a boat's owner of about fifty-five of that harbor asked whose the said chain was, and was told that it came up out of a cut off the south end on the ninth of April past at forty fathoms and is not that yard's, and that the reason against the line of the asking is that the question cannot be answered and the answer that was given is true, and that a man asked a question he cannot have the answer to is a man who has been told a fact about a thing and not about a person, and that this yard is not going to write a name against a fact.*

*And that a man of about sixty-one behind a counter in that market offered a shilling for the said chain and it was refused, and that the refusal is entered in the first column, and that what the man of about sixty-one said about having had a week in which to make up his mind about that yard is in the second column and is not in the first, and that the reason against that line is that a man deciding what he thinks of a yard is not a thing a man on a mole can check.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and twenty-one marks, seven shillings and fivepence is in the bag, which is three thousand four hundred and forty-nine pence, and that is the same figure it was on Sunday night and it will be the same figure on Wednesday night. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and there is nothing else. The whole of what went out of that yard since the sixth of December past is sixty-two marks, twelve shillings and fourpence in twenty transactions at seven counters. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-seven weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and the next wage Tuesday is the twenty-seventh of April next, and the column will not move on that either.**
