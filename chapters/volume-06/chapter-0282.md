# Chapter 282

*The Fifty-First Time That Wall Came Down, the Forty-First Charge Walked on None, and a Woman of Fifty-Eight Who Does Not Read What She Copies*

Monday the twenty-sixth of April, in a room off the fish market, and in the shed on the mole.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-first time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-first of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past.**

**And the said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it, and the figure in the bag is the figure it has been since Wednesday night. And the far bench was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it.**

---

He went down the hill at a quarter to nine because the room is shut when the market is off the boards and open before it, and he went alone, and the reason he went alone is that the ship's hand writes in her own left hand and a reading of a sheet is her work and this was not a reading.

There were two other people in the room and neither of them looked up. A counter in a room off the fish market in this harbor is the one place on this coast where a man may stand with his hands empty and be in nobody's way.

"**Good morning,**" said Pernel Saye, without looking up, and then she did look up, because he had not sat down.

Pernel Saye is fifty-eight and has kept that counter for thirty-one years and has no office above it and is not of this party and is not on a ladder.

"**I want to ask you a question and it is about where sheets are and not what is on one,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then ask it,**" she said, "**and stand out of the light, because that lamp is nine years old and I have not the money to be short-sighted about.**"

He moved.

"**Where in this town would a copy of a thing out of the drowned city be,**" he said.

The pen stopped.

She did not put it down, which is what she did in the autumn when two strangers came up a hill with a question, and he noticed that she did not put it down and he did not think anything about it then and thought about it about four hours later.

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Pernel Saye.

"**That is the rest of it. I want to know where they are. I am not asking what is on one.**"

"**I have got one,**" she said.

The room went on being a room. A man at the far counter turned a page.

"**On the top shelf, at the back, on its face out because I put it that way and never moved it. I made it nine years ago for a man who was buying a house and wanted the oldest paper that came with it copied, and he paid, and he did not come back for it, and I have had it nine years.**"

"Have you read it."

"**No.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because I do not read what I copy.**" She went back to the copying and her voice came along with it. "**That is not modesty, it is the whole of my trade and I have been doing it since I was twenty-seven. When a thing is on my counter I make a copy of it and I do not find out what it is, because the day I find out what a thing is I am a person who knows what it says. And then I am a person who knows what it says, and there is a form in this country that a person who knows what a paper says is somebody's to ask. You cannot have a counter on a street and know what is on the shelf behind your own head. It is the one rule I have and I have never broken it and I have kept this counter thirty-one years on it.**"

"All right," said Calder Marr.

"**All right? You have been standing in that light for ten minutes asking me where a sheet is and you have not asked me a single other thing and I want you to hear me say that I noticed.**"

"**I noticed too.**"

"**Then here is the rest of it, and it is the only time in nine years I am going to say it to a man who came in on his own two feet. Take it off the shelf and do not come back and tell me what is on it. I have never turned it over and I am not going to start, and if you tell me, then I will have to think about it for the rest of my life, and I have a counter and a street and a name on a door.**"

"I will not tell you," he said.

"**Then come back on a Tuesday or a Wednesday when the market is not on the boards, and it will be a penny, and I will not ask you a question about it, and I will put the penny in the box and I will not look up.**"

---

He did not buy it.

He stood there for about another minute and a half, and he thought about the eleven marks, and about a bag with nineteen marks in it, and about a man of fifty-one whose net was still on a floor at forty fathoms, and about a sheet that had been on a shelf in this harbor for nine years and had cost a man in a town a shilling's worth of copying thirty-one years of a stranger's trade, and he worked out that the whole of the transaction was one penny, and he worked out that he was not going to do it on a Monday, and he went up the hill and did not do it.

That is in the second column and not the first, because a thing that was not done is not a thing that was done, and the reason against it is not money, which this yard has never once had enough of to be the reason for anything.

---

She was at the bench inside the door when he came in, writing the day's book in her own left hand at about twice the time it takes her.

"Well?"

"**There is a sheet in this town,**" he said, "**and it is a copy, and it is of something that came out of the drowned city, and it was made nine years ago and it is on a shelf in a room off the fish market and the woman who made it has not read it and is not going to.**"

"**And you did not ask her what is on it.**"

"**No.**"

"**Good,**" said Nessa Pike, and wrote a figure, and did not look up, and then said, "**And you did not buy it.**"

"No."

"**On what grounds.**"

"**Because it was Monday and because a person of no office told me on the yesterday at the bench that if I came up that hill with a story about a sheet in it she would stop coming up it, and I have not got anybody else on this coast who has read a method's words on a made thing in nine years, and I am not going to spend her on a Monday.**"

"**Write that down,**" she said, "**all three parts of it, and put the reason against the line and not the whole sentence in the first column, because a man not buying a sheet is not a thing a man did.**"

---

And then he said the other thing, and he said it to the bench and not to her, and it is the sentence this yard has been carrying since the eighth day of April past and has not improved on.

"**Say the rest of it, because I have to write the reason against this line and I have not got it and I have been not having it for three weeks.**"

"**The reason is that a set is a pressure house's set on a book,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and it goes where the book sends it. There is no book on that coast that sends a set four hundred miles west. There is no yard on that coast that rents one. There is no man on that coast with a reason deep enough and a book good enough to be allowed to ask, and there is nothing in this country a man can hire that will go and stand in a room seventy fathoms down and hold a lamp in it.**"

"And so we do not go."

"**We cannot go. Those are different words and I would like the second one kept,**" she said, "**because a party that says it cannot go has not tried and a party that says it will not go has given up, and we have not done either.**"

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-sixth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about a quarter to nine on the said morning the holder of that writ went down that hill alone into a room off the fish market to a counter that is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, and that a copyist of this harbor, being Pernel Saye, fifty-eight, who has kept that counter for thirty-one years, was behind it with two other people in the room; and that the holder of that writ asked her one question, and that the said question was where in this town a copy of a thing out of the drowned city would be, and that he said out loud at her counter that he was not asking what was on one.*

*And that the said woman said of her own account and unasked that she had one, on the top shelf at the back of the said room on its face out, that she had made it nine years ago for a man who was buying a house and wanted the oldest paper that came with it copied, and that the said man paid and did not come back for it, and that the said woman said that she does not read what she copies, and gave the reason, which is that the day a copyist finds out what a thing is she is a person who knows what it says, and that a person who knows what a paper says is somebody's to ask.*

*And that the said woman said that if the holder of that writ took the said sheet off the said shelf she was not to be told what was on it, and that the holder of that writ said that he would not tell her; and that the said woman said that the said sheet would be a penny on a Tuesday or a Wednesday when the market is not on the boards, and that she would not ask a question about it.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on the said morning for the fifty-first time since the first of May past and the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page, and that the cistern was charged at no figure on the said morning and that party walked none of it, being the forty-first of the charges entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ did not buy the said sheet on the said day, and that the reason against the line of the not-buying is not money and never has been in this yard, and that the reason is that the said day was a Monday and that a person of no office said on the day before at the bench in that shed that if he came up that hill with a story about a sheet in it she would stop coming up it.*

*And that the holder of that writ did not ask the said copyist one question about what was on the said sheet, and did not ask her twice, and did not ask her a third time, and the reason against the line of the not-asking is that a sheet a woman has not read is a sheet she has nothing to answer for, and that the holder of that writ is not going to be the man who makes her read one.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on the said day, and that a man of about thirty who cannot read in a town two days down that coast was not written to on the said day and a woman of about fifty-eight of that harbor was not written to on the said day, and that the reason against both of those lines is that this yard has made itself one address in this country on the third day of April past and is not going to make a second.*

*And that Tobias Wren was not sent for and nothing was asked of him on the said day, and that the reason against the line of the not-sending is the reason of the second day of April last and is not improved on.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and nineteen marks, four shillings and ninepence is in the bag, which is three thousand and ninety-seven pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is sixty-five marks, one shilling and eightpence in twenty-one transactions at seven counters.**
