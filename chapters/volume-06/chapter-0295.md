# Chapter 295

*Sunday the Ninth of May, Four Marks and a Shilling to a Glazier, One Man's Own Book Four Streets Off, and the Figure the Bag Is Short of the Wage of Four Marks Coming Back*

Sunday the ninth of May, in the shed on the mole, and in a room four streets off it.

**The wall did not come down on the said Sunday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and six hundred and sixty pence went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The glazier came at eight in the morning and did two lights and a skylight and charged four marks and a shilling, and Nessa Pike paid it at about eleven at his own counter in the ship's own left hand, and six hundred and sixty pence went out of that yard's own book on the ninth of May.

The shed has a door that is a door and a light in it, a light at the back, and a skylight about two feet by three in the slope over the bench. Those are the three this yard owns.

**The other glass in this harbor on the seventh of May was other people's, and the yard paid none of it and was asked for none of it and did not offer any of it.** The man of about fifty-five had the running glass out of his own boat put in on the Saturday evening by a man of about nineteen who is in the second lay and is not a man of that yard, out of his own pocket, being sixpence, and he put it in himself and would not be paid for it and would not be thanked for it and nobody in that shed has been told his name and it is not going in that book.

The eleven windows along that front are eleven households and four of those households are people this yard has a reason about, and one of the four is a woman of about fifty-eight with a drawer under her bed and a paper with a seal on it in it, and none of the four has been written to and none is going to be.

The lens of the mole-head light is the harbor's and there is nobody in this harbor who has bought a lens in nine years.

---

Then the other page, and it was the only thing done on the ninth of May that was not arithmetic.

Tobias Wren came up the steps at about one in the afternoon with a book under his arm, and he put it on the bench and did not open it, and there was a date on the outside of it in his own hand and a place where a boat's name would go if a boat had a name.

"**That is Friday,**" he said. "**From about six in the evening until about ten at night, and it is the same account as the one in that yard's book and it is the other half of it and it is not on top of yours and it is not going to be.**"

"**We did not ask you for that,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**No. I know you did not, and I want it in that book that I brought it up and that nobody asked me.**" He turned one page over with the fingers of his left hand. "**It is in my own book, in my own hand, and my book goes in the house of my boat, and my boat is in eleven pieces on that mole, and in about four years that page is the only record in this country of what that front looked like on the seventh of May, and it will be in a hand of one man and not a hand of three, and that is what I have.**"

"**Then it is in the second column,**" said Calder Marr, "**and the reason against the line of it being in the first is that a man can go and look at the glass and cannot go and look at a page in another man's book, and the two are the same weather and are not the same record.**"

"**And you are not going to be thanked,**" said Tobias Wren.

"**No.**"

"**Good.**"

"**One more thing and then I will go and sit down,**" he said. "**You will be asked, because the wage is not being paid and a wage not being paid is the sort of thing people ask about. I have been in this harbor sixty-one years and I have never once seen it not asked about. If somebody does ask you, and somebody will, then do not tell them it is a chamber and do not tell them it is a term and do not tell them a man of sixty-one said a word in a shed on a Wednesday. Tell them a yard of two and a half cannot afford its own plant. That is true and it is the whole of what they need and it is not a lie and you will not have to improve on it.**"

"**Write that down,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I have,**" said Tobias Wren, and put his hand flat on the book, "**and it is in my own book and not in yours, and I am not going to say it twice.**"

---

And then the ship's hand did the arithmetic, and she did it out loud, and she had not been able to do it since the seventh of April and everybody in that shed knew that.

"**Five marks, one shilling and fourpence on the fourth of May, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and I have been putting a figure against that gap in this book since the third week of March, and there has been nothing to put it against since the seventh of April.**"

She wrote it in the day's column and turned the book round.

"**There are five hundred and ninety-seven pence in that bag. The wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence. The figure this bag is short of the wage of four marks is forty-three pence, being three shillings and sevenpence.**"

"**Read it again,**" said the holder of that writ.

"**Forty-three pence, being three shillings and sevenpence.**"

"**That has got a subject,**" he said. "**It has had a subject three times in this yard's life, twice in this book and once before it, and the first of the two in this book was a mark and a bit and the second was a form off a mole and both of them got better. And it has come back and it is not going to be paid and nobody in that shed is to write one word under it that says anybody is going to pay it.**"

"**Second column,**" said Nessa Pike. "**And the reason against the line of it being in the first is that a man in four years can go and count what is in the bag and cannot go and count what is not in it, and that is what it is for and it is not a debt and it is not a bill and it is not a thing anybody is owed.**"

---

Nobody in that shed said one word about the weather, and nobody is going to.

The second column has two hands on it and two pages and they are a mile and a quarter apart, and the other one is in a book four streets off in a house that is the house of a boat in eleven pieces, and it is in the hand of a man of sixty-one who is not thanked and who brought it up himself on a Sunday afternoon and was not asked for it.

**And this yard is not going to ask anybody what they thought of the seventh of May, and not the man of about fifty-five, and not the boy of about seventeen, and not the four people four days down that coast, and not one of the about a hundred people who were out in the rain, and the reason against the line of all of those is the same reason and it is that a thing a man can be asked about has not happened, it has only been spoken about.**

The four people four days down that coast go into deep water without a book. They were not asked about the weather of the seventh, and they were not asked about a round of glass that came off a bed in a sill at seventy fathoms on that coast's own ground, and **the fact that this yard has now read the sill of a thing of that kind four hundred miles from where they keep their page is not a reason to ask them and is not going to be used as one**, and the reason against the line of asking is the reason of the second of April last about a trade and a price and it has not changed since the spring and it is not improved on today.

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the ninth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that two lights and a skylight were put into that shed on the said morning by a glazier of this harbor and six hundred and sixty pence went out of that yard's own book at about eleven in the morning, being four marks and a shilling; and that the said glass is the whole of the glass this yard owns in that harbor; and that the running glass of a boat of about forty tons belonging to a man of about fifty-five was put in by a man of about nineteen out of that boy's own pocket on the Saturday preceding and that the said man of about fifty-five paid for it himself and that this yard paid no part of it and was not asked for any part of it; and that the lens of the mole-head light came out on the seventh and is the harbor's and there is nobody in this harbor who has bought a lens in nine years and that light is not lit and is not going to be lit this month.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that an account of the weather of the seventh of May, in another hand and in another book, was brought up that mole and put on that bench at about one in the afternoon by the man who wrote it, unasked, and that the said book is his own and is kept in the house of his own boat and is not on top of this yard's page and is not going to be; and that the reason against the line of the said account being entered in the first column is that a man can go and look at the glass and cannot go and look at a page in another man's book.*

*And that the figure this yard's bag is short of the wage of four marks, which had had no subject since the seventh of April, came back on the said day at about two in the afternoon, being forty-three pence, being three shillings and sevenpence; and that the said figure is entered in the second column and not the first, and that the reason against the line of the first is that a man in four years can count what is in a bag and cannot count what is not in it; and that the said figure is not a debt and is not a bill and nobody in that shed is to write one word under it that says anybody is going to pay it.*

*And that this yard is not going to ask anybody what they thought of the weather of the seventh of May, and not the man of about fifty-five and not the boy of about seventeen and not the four people four days down that coast and not one of the about a hundred people who were out in the rain; and that the reason against the line of all of those is that a thing a man can be asked about has not happened and has only been spoken about.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and six hundred and sixty pence went out of it, and there are five hundred and ninety-seven pence in the bag, which is three marks, nine shillings and ninepence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is forty-three pence, and that figure came back on the said day after a month and two days and it is not going to be paid. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-two days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the ninety-eighth night, and the crate under a tarpaulin is eight feet from the two of them and neither has moved.**
