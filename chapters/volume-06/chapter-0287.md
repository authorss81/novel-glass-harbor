# Chapter 287

*Saturday the First of May, Six Fathoms of a Herringman's Net Told on the Mole Steps, and Six Doors and a Seventh One That Is a Drawer*

Saturday the first of May, the mole steps, and the shed on that mole.

**The wall did not come down on the said Saturday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

He told him on the steps at about half past ten in the morning with the market getting up behind him and about nine people within hearing, because there was no other place to say it and because a thing said on a mole steps in front of nine people is a fact and a thing said in a shed with the door shut is a story, and this yard has never once been able to make itself tell the difference.

Elias Voke is fifty-one and has nineteen winters in a list being made up in a city and is of the herring boat of the second lay.

"**I put a pot on your ground on Wednesday morning,**" said Calder Marr. "**Forty fathoms, off the south end, round the corner, six conditions read at the boat, price named the day before, nobody in the water but a hand off a book. The pot landed on the top course, where the bar of square iron came out of its socket on the ninth of April, and the lead weight came down on the belly of your net.**"

Voke did not move.

"**About six fathoms of it is flat on a course of cut stone under a weight now, and it is not cut and it is not mended, and it will not come up whole. That is what I came up the steps to say and I am not going to say it in a softer figure than that.**"

"Six," said Elias Voke.

"Six."

"**Then put six in your book and not about six. About is what a man writes when he wants to be argued with, and I have nineteen winters in a list and I have had enough of about.**"

"Six," said Calder Marr, "**and not about six, and I will read it back to you out of the first column whenever you want to hear it, and that is the only thing I have got for you this morning and it is not money.**"

"**I did not ask you for money and you are not going to give me any, and if you gave me any I would have to be a man you had paid, and I have got nineteen winters and a floor and I would rather be a man you have not paid.**"

"And the reason I did not ask you on the Tuesday is that reason and I am not going to improve on it or pretend it is a better one than it is. I have paid you once, in April, and you have not been thanked and have not wanted to be, and there is a standing in this yard's own book about what happens to a man after he has had money off us, and I have not got the right to make you one."

"**Then that is the reason and it is not a good one and it is not meant to be,**" said Elias Voke, "**and it is the same one you gave a man of thirty two days down this coast and I have heard about that too, because there are forty people in a market and nine on some steps, and you do not have a speech, you have a sentence, and the sentence is going round.**"

"Then it is going round," said Calder Marr, "**and I have not got a better one, and if I had one I would give it to you now and you would take it and it would be worse.**"

Voke looked at the water for a while, at the flat pale water inside a line of white water four hundred yards beyond two baulks of green timber a mile and a quarter off the head of that mole.

"**Twenty-nine of March,**" he said. "**I put a warp and a net and eleven cork floats into the water on the night of the twenty-ninth of March on my own ground, and I came up on the eighth day of April and asked what it would cost, and I named eleven marks before anybody had said a price. And I have not been thanked and I do not want to be, and that was to be a fixed arrangement, and I said it in this harbor in front of about eleven people.**"

"Yes."

"**And now there is a second thing on my ground that I did not ask for and did not say no to, and you have come up these steps and told me the figure before I asked you for it, and the figure is six and not about six.**" He shifted his weight. "**That is the arrangement. That is the whole of what I have got out of this yard and I have got it twice and I am not going to be thanked for it and you are not going to be.**"

He went down the steps. Nobody offered him anything and he asked for nothing and about nine people were standing on a mole at half past ten in the morning.

---

And then they went through the doors, because a sheet has to go somewhere and there are a fixed number of places.

The copy of a copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city is on a bench in that shed at about eleven o'clock on the first of May and it is going to have to live somewhere and the yard has been here before.

**It is not going in the tin. The tin holds six pieces of paper and the charter's place is empty, and the tin shuts, and this yard spent a Friday finding out what a drawer is and is not going to do two things in a week.**

**It is not going on a wall. A sheet on a wall in that harbor is a sheet that goes out of that harbor, and that yard's own sheet has gone out of it four times, and the thirty-eighth line of it is nailed up in a town four days up this coast where there has never been a paper on a wall, and that is a sheet about a place and it is not going to be helped by a second one.**

**It is not going in the post. A thing put in the post gets an address, and this yard made itself an address in the ordinary post on the third of April past for a good reason and has one form sitting on a step two days down that coast as the price of it, and it is not going to make a second address, and it is not going to send nine lines of an old order to a man of thirty who cannot read and who has a sheet of an office of its own on his table already.**

**It is not going to the four people four days down that coast. They wrote four lines in March about a record and signed them and they are not going to be asked about a record, and the fact that a copy of a municipal sheet has come into this harbor off a shelf for a penny is not a reason to ask them and is not going to be used as one.**

**It is not going back to the copyist. She made it and she did not read it and she asked for one thing and the one thing was that she never be told what is on it, and if it goes back on her shelf then she is a person in a house keeping a copy of an order of a drowned city, and she has a counter on a street and a name on a door.**

**And it is not going to be burnt. A person who burns a paper with unsettled material on it has handled it, and a person who has handled it has kept it, and a person who has kept it is the thing the paper is about, so the burning is the keeping and it is the same door. She said that out loud in this shed on the eighteenth of April past and nobody improved on it then and nobody is going to improve on it now.**

Six doors and one box on a corner, and the box was on the eighteenth of April past and it is not on again.

"So it is the drawer," said Calder Marr.

"**It is the drawer,**" said Nessa Pike, "**in this shed, in this harbor, in a yard that has been standing behind a house since the sixth of March past and had it the wrong way round for fifty-six days. And we said all of that out loud on the Saturday before last and meant it, and on the Tuesday we went down the hill and put a penny on a counter, and here we are.**"

"**Then the price of a penny is a drawer,**" he said, "**and the price of the drawer is that a woman of fifty-eight in this harbor has a paper with a seal on it in a drawer under her bed, and a man of thirty two days down this coast has one on his table that went into the ordinary post on the eighteenth of April past and has been on that step since the twentieth, and this yard has now got three of them, and there is no man in this country who could tell me which of the three is a keeper.**"

"**None of them,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Write that. That is the sentence. None of them is a keeper and all three of them are, and that is not a paradox and it is not a defect, and it is the first thing this yard has ever written down that is true about a person instead of true about a piece of paper.**"

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the first day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about half past ten in the morning of the said day the holder of that writ told Elias Voke, fifty-one, of the herring boat of the second lay, on the steps of that mole and in about nine people's hearing, that a pot had been landed on the said man's ground on the Wednesday preceding at about forty fathoms off the south end of that mole and that the lead weight of that pot had come down on the belly of his net, and that about six fathoms of the said net is now flat on a course of cut stone under a weight and is not cut and is not mended and will not come up whole.*

*And that the said man said out loud that the figure was six and not about six and that about is what a man writes when he wants to be argued with, and that the said figure of six is entered in that book and not about six, and that the said man said out loud that he had not asked for money and was not going to be given any, and that nothing was given to him and nothing went out of that yard on his account.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud on the said steps that the reason the said man was not asked on the Tuesday preceding is the standing reason of that yard about a man this yard has paid, and that the said reason was not improved on and was not made smaller, and that the said man said out loud that the same sentence has been going round forty people and nine steps and that that is the arrangement.*

*And that between about eleven in the morning and about two in the afternoon of the said day six places were ruled out for the copy of a copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city, being the tin, a wall, the ordinary post, the four people of that coast, the counter off the fish market, and a fire, and that the seventh place is a drawer in that shed, and that the copy is in that drawer on the said afternoon and that the said drawer shuts and that the said drawer is a house.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that no money of any kind went to the said man of fifty-one and none is going to, and that the reason against the line of the not-paying is the reason of the second day of April last and is the reason against the line of the three marks, and is not improved on and was not improved on in that shed on the said day.*

*And that the whole of what the said descent cost a third party is six fathoms of a herringman's gear and a man's afternoon on a mole at half past ten, and that neither of those is a figure this yard can enter in any column without the man it belongs to, and that no figure was entered for the said afternoon.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on the said day, and that a woman of about fifty-eight of that harbor was not written to on the said day and a man of about thirty who cannot read two days down that coast was not written to on the said day, and that the reason against both of those lines is that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second, and that the said copy is now the third such thing in this country that this yard knows about and cannot help.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and no money of any kind went to a man of fifty-one and none is going to. Six fathoms of another man's gear is flat on a course of cut stone at forty fathoms under a lead weight, and about nine people were standing on that mole at half past ten in the morning and not one of them is going to be asked what they made of it, and a copy of a copy of a municipal sheet of a drowned city went into a drawer in that shed at about eleven o'clock and is in it, and neither of those carries a figure and the one of them is never going to get one.**
