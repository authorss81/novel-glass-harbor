# Chapter 292

*Thursday the Sixth of May, the Forty-Fourth Charge Walked on None, Fourteen Marks Three Shillings and Fourpence Off a Book That Is Not This Yard's, and a Doorway at Seventy Fathoms*

Thursday the sixth of May, off the south end of that mole, and in the shed on that mole in the evening.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-fourth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it, and not one penny of the day's plant and air came out of it either, because it never went in.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

The set was on the mole at six in the morning, off a boat out of Ternwatch, and the hose was coiled before the light was properly up, and a man of about thirty-four of that pressure house came ashore with it and put the paper down on the bench in that shed in his own hand, which is a thing he has done every time and has never once been asked to do twice.

"**The book sends Searle,**" he said. "**He is the first name on it and he is the name the book has sent every time since the spring.**"

"**Then say the rest of it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Amos Searle, twenty-eight, of that house, is carried on that book as unfit for five weeks from the ninth day of April and as fit for light work after and as fit on the fifth day of June.**" The man put his thumb on the paper. "**The fifth of June is the twelfth day. He is not a man who goes down on the sixth of May and I will not send him and I would not send him if you asked me to.**"

"**Nobody is going to ask you,**" said Calder Marr. "**And the reason is written down and it is the twenty-eighth of April and the ninth of April, and this yard is not going to soften it by making it sound like care of a man. It is a book. It says he cannot and it is right and we are not going to argue with a book in front of the man who has to read it out.**"

"**Then the book sends nobody,**" said Fisk, "**and the day is off.**"

"**The book sends the second name,**" said the man of about thirty-four, "**at two marks and eightpence a day, the same as the first, and the same hour, and the paper says fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence and the paper is right and I have not come four hundred miles to argue about fourpence.**"

They read the six conditions out loud at the boat at about eight in the morning in order and it took under two minutes and nobody improved on it and nobody laughed, and the holder of that writ said the thing that has to be said at the boat, which is not a formality and has never been a formality.

"**The outer marks are a mile and a quarter off the head of that mole. The bar is four hundred yards off the outer marks and is about a mile and a half long on the north-west line. We are going off the south end and we are going round the corner from all of it, and nobody and nothing of this yard is inside that bar today, and that bar has not been narrowed by anything, and this party is not going to ask to have it narrowed, did not ask for it in advance, and is not asking for it in this boat.**"

"**Same as the seventh of April,**" said Fisk, and it was not a question.

"**Same as the seventh of April.**"

---

The pot went down at about twenty past eight on the flood and came up at about half past eleven, and it was in the water about three hours and ten minutes, which is longer than the last one and about half as long again as the first of them, and nobody said anything about the difference because a man in a boat does not say anything about the difference.

**The gauge is a pressure gauge and the figure on it is pounds and not feet. It read eighty-four pounds on the face of a hundred and fifty, as it read on the eighth, ninth and tenth of April past and as it read on the twenty-eighth, and the pencil mark on the face where the needle stood on the tenth of April past was not moved, and that mark is the only reason the two books are the same book.**

At about seventy fathoms the ground is not the mole's own floor. The courses with the figures in them stop at about forty and the trench runs on, and past the trench the rock is cut.

It is cut as a face, about thirty feet of it, and standing in the face is a doorway with two dressed jambs and one dressed stone across the top of it, and the jambs are about five feet high, and the floor inside the doorway is the floor of a room and not the floor of a rock, and the room is about nine feet across.

**The sill is a dressed stone about four feet long and the width of a hand, and in the sill there is a bed cut the whole length of it, and the bed is a groove about the width of two fingers and about two inches deep, and below the groove there is a second groove, deeper and wider, cut under the lip of the sill, and that one is not a bed. That one is a rebate.**

**And there is a sheet of glass standing in the bed.**

It is a plate about the width of the doorway and about a hand and a half high and the thickness of two fingers, and it is green at the edges and clear in the middle the way a plate is when it has been made in a furnace and not blown, and it stands in the bed and the sea presses on it from the front, and there is nothing behind it but the room.

**Fisk looked at the rebate for a long time and then wrote on the slate and showed it round the boat, and the slate said: THE BED IS EMPTY AND THE REBATE IS UNDER IT AND THE PLATE IS IN THE BED AND NOT IN THE REBATE. And under that it said: SOMEBODY HAS TAKEN THE ROUND OUT OF IT.**

Nobody put a hand in.

"**We are not going in,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I did not say that we were.**"

"**I know you did not. I am saying it out loud so that it is a record and not a story, and so that neither of us can say afterwards that we thought of it later.**" He put both hands flat on the thwart. "**There is a room behind that plate and we are not going to read it and we are not going to take one piece of it out, and I said that out loud on a mole at four o'clock yesterday before any of us had agreed to anything, and the price of it is on the same page as the price of the day.**"

"**Say it again.**"

"**No.**"

---

They counted the row of marks before they came up, and it took a long time and it was done twice.

They are cut in the sill on the inside face, to the left of the bed, one under another, and they are not figures and they are not letters, and they are cut in the same hand as the figures at the near end of every course on that mole's own floor forty fathoms up, and they are a row.

**There are two hundred and nine of them.** There is one more cut below that, deeper and not the same cut, and the ship's hand counted the whole row twice off the slate and said the same number both times and read it out, and the holder of that writ counted it a third time from the top down and got the same number and said nothing about having done it.

**And beside the row, cut in the sill, there is a day.** It is cut the way a man cuts a day he wants read by somebody who comes after him, in the figures of that hand and in the same stroke: the eleventh of May.

Fisk read it twice and then said the only thing he said about it.

"**Then the plate goes into the rebate on the eleventh, or the bed lets go, and either way that is a day and not an hour, and there is a man in this boat who can come and look at it from off the water in about the same water and count the marks himself.**"

"**Then it is in the first column,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it is in the first column because a man in a boat can go and look at it, and not because we know what it means.**"

---

They came up at about half past eleven and he was on the crown of that mole from about one in the afternoon until about five and did not come down and did not do anything, and there is no figure for an afternoon and there is no figure for a day, and the reason against the line of either of those is the reason that has been against that line since the sixth of October past and has no name in it and is not going to get one in this yard.

The entry of the day was made in the first column in his own right hand, slowly, in about twenty minutes, with the ship's hand reading the slate out to him twice and then putting the slate face down on the bench and not picking it up again.

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the sixth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the pot was put on the bottom at about twenty past eight in the morning on the flood off the south end of that mole and came up at about half past eleven, being about three hours and ten minutes in the water; that the gauge is a pressure gauge on a face of a hundred and fifty and read eighty-four pounds as it read on the eighth, ninth and tenth of April past and on the twenty-eighth, and that the pencil mark on that face was not moved; and that at about seventy fathoms off the said south end the ground is cut as a face of about thirty feet and that standing in that face is a doorway with two dressed jambs and one dressed stone across the top of it about five feet high, and a room behind it about nine feet across.*

*And that the sill is a dressed stone about four feet long and the width of a hand, and that there is a bed cut the whole length of the sill about two inches deep and the width of two fingers, and that below the lip of the sill there is a second groove, deeper and wider, which is a rebate and not a bed; and that a plate of glass about the width of the doorway and about a hand and a half high and the thickness of two fingers stands in the said bed and not in the said rebate, and that the sea presses on the said plate from the front and there is nothing behind it but the said room; and that the said bed is empty.*

*And that in the inner face of the said sill to the left of the said bed there is a row of two hundred and nine marks cut one under another in the same hand as the figures at the near end of every course on that mole's own floor, with one further cut below the row and deeper and not the same cut, and that the said row was counted twice by the ship's hand and a third time by the holder of that writ and all three counts were the same; and that beside the said row there is a day cut in the same hand and the same stroke, being the eleventh of May.*

*And that the man off the Crown house book named on that book as unfit for five weeks from the ninth day of April was not sent for and was not asked and was not in that boat, and that the reason against the line of asking was entered out loud at that bench before the said day and was the ninth and the twenty-eighth of April, and that the second name on the said book was sent at the same figure, being two marks and eightpence a day; and that no person of that yard was inside the bar of four hundred yards off the outer marks on the said day and the said bar was not narrowed by anything and was not asked to be.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the whole of what the day's plant and air came to, being fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence, went off a book at a pressure house at Ternwatch and not one penny of it went out of this yard's own book; and that the day was asked for by a man of sixty-one with a boat that is not this yard's; and that the reason against the line of this yard's paying for it is that it could not, and that the reason against the line of a man this yard is being paid by is that this yard has a rule about a man it has paid and has none about a man paying for it.*

*And that the afternoon of the said day was lost to the body and carries no figure, and that the reason against the line of the loss is the reason that has been against that line since the sixth day of October past and has no name in it.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that boat at about half past ten in the morning of the said day that neither he nor the man in the boat was going into the said room, and that the said decision was entered in the first column on the Wednesday preceding at about four o'clock in the afternoon before anybody had agreed to the day, with the whole price of it in the same entry; and that the reason against the line of the not-going is the reason of the sixth day of March past about a record a man cannot check.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there is one thousand four hundred and sixty-five pence in the bag, which is nine marks, two shillings and one penny. Fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence went off a book four hundred miles off in a house with a counter in it, and the whole of that day's labor came out of a man who is sixty-one and owns a boat, and the two objects of nobody's are three hundred and twenty-nine days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the ninety-fifth night, and neither of them has moved, and nobody went near either of them on the said day.**
