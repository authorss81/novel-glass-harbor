# Chapter 285

*The Forty-Second Charge Walked on None, and a Copy Read Under a Lamp by Two People and Not by the Third*

Thursday the twenty-ninth of April, in the shed on the mole.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-second of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

He asked her on the Wednesday evening, at the bench, and she said yes before he had got to the end of the sentence.

Then he told her the rest of it, and she put the pen down.

"**You are not having me in the room tomorrow night.**"

"No."

"**Say the reason and do not make it smaller than it is.**"

"**A room with a lamp in it over a sheet a man cannot check is the room your certificate was for,**" he said. "**The whole standing of a certificate in the reading of worked glass and its faults is that a person may say a thing under that method where a thing is on a table and everybody in the room may act on it, because a method is behind it and a method can be checked by anybody who holds the same method. I am not going to put a method's words on that sheet. You said the words in a market on the twenty-second in about forty people's hearing and it cost you the standing of a certificate in a market, and the certificate is not withdrawn and cannot be, and I stood in that shed on that day and watched a thing I have no power to give back get spent. I am not spending you twice for one night's work.**"

"**That is the reason and it is a good one and it is also half of it,**" said Mara Venn. "**The other half is that I am the only one of the three of us who could tell you whether it is true. You would take it as a stone. She would take it as a page. And if I say it, then in about four years a man in a city writes *she said so* on the back of a sheet, and a person who held a certificate and has not got one any more becomes the hand a thing rests on. So you are not leaving me out because I am not able. You are leaving me out because I am the one who could carry it.**"

"Yes."

"**Then I will be in this shed at six tomorrow morning and I will not come up at ten, and I will not come up at nine either, and you are not to leave me anything on the bench.**"

"There will be nothing on the bench," he said, "**because I am not going to write one word of it down until I have read it.**"

---

They lit the lamp at about ten and turned it round, and there were two of them in that shed and there was nobody else in it, and the market was off the boards and the mole was empty and the door was shut.

**Two people read it. That is the whole of the arithmetic of it: two, and one lamp, and one sheet of a copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city, and about three quarters of an hour.**

---

The copyist ruled no lines and made no blot and put nothing on it that was not on the original, and everything on the page is in her client's hand and not in hers, and that is a thing a man can go and look at on a counter off a fish market if he goes and looks.

**At the head of the face there is a name of a chamber. It goes in the first column because a name on a piece of paper is a thing a man can go and look at, and this yard is not sending for a book, and there is no gazetteer in that shed and no chart with it on and the sheet rolled behind the tide table does not have it, and that is entered as a name this yard has not been able to place and is not going to place this week.**

**Under the head there is a date. It is the eleventh day of a month. The month and the year are both on the line that the fold has parted at, and the fold has gone soft across the middle of the sheet and is coming apart at one corner, and the holder of that writ did not mend it and did not hold it to the lamp and did not guess.**

---

**The order is nine lines and it is in the copyist's client's hand and it is this:**

*One. The fleet is ordered down.*
*Two. The fleet is the transparent fleet and every vessel of it lies in the water of this city.*
*Three. The order is of the fleet and not of a master of it.*
*Four. The masters are not to be asked which of them made it.*
*Five. The order is carried by the masters and not by any office.*
*Six. No copy of this order is to be made.*
*Seven. A copy of this order is not this order.*
*Eight. A man holding a copy of this order has not got an order.*
*Nine. And it is to be carried out in one tide.*

Nessa Pike read it twice and then a third time with her finger on the third line and stopped there.

"**There is no reason in it,**" she said.

"There is no reason in it."

"**Nine lines and not one of them says why and not one of them says who.**"

"**No.**"

"**And that is not an omission in the copy. A copyist who copies nine lines out of nine does not leave out the tenth because it is the one that matters. It is there or it was never there.**"

---

**The foot of the sheet is where the day was.**

**There are nine marks on it. Six of them are the same mark: a circle with one stroke struck through it, struck hard and struck even, and all six of them about the same size and all six of them in the same hand. Two of them are the same mark and not that mark: the same circle with two strokes, struck smaller and off the center, and the two of them are not even with each other, one of them harder than the other and a third of a size out.**

**And the ninth is not a struck mark. It is nine short lines in a group, and they are cut and not struck, and they go about a third of the depth of the other eight, and they are at an angle to the sheet that none of the other eight is at.**

Under the nine, in the hand of the sheet and not the hand of the copy, there is one line:

*And the ninth is not one of ours and is not to be counted with the nine and is not to be asked about.*

---

They did not speak for a while. Outside, water moved against the mole steps the way it does.

"**Two marks,**" said Nessa Pike at last. "**Six and two. That is not nine communities and that is not one community. That is two, and they are on one sheet under one order, and they are struck with two different marks, and the sheet does not say the two of them agreed and does not say the two of them did not.**"

"It does not say anything about the two of them at all."

"**No. And the sheet's own line counts the nine and then puts the ninth outside the nine. So the sheet knows the ninth is not one of the marks that made it. That is on the sheet. That is not this yard's reading of it. That is the sheet saying it about itself in its own hand.**"

"Yes."

"And the six and the two do not agree with one another, and the yard is going to write that down, and the yard is going to write down that it is a yard's reading of two sizes of mark on a copy and not a finding, and that a mark is a mark and not a sentence and that a mark does not agree with a mark except by being the same mark and these two are not the same mark."

"**And the disagreement is the finding,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it is not a defect in the evidence. If those two marks were the same mark there would be nothing on that sheet but a decision, and a decision with one mark on it is a thing a man can repeat and this one they argued about after they made it and they put the argument on the paper and left it on the paper.**"

---

And then the ninth, and the two of them had it in front of them on the bench under the lamp and neither of them wanted to go near it.

"**We have seen that making before,**" said Nessa Pike, and she did not say where, and the holder of that writ did not ask her, because he had been in that room and she had not.

"It is a builder's mark. It is a mark of a pre-Accord construction. It is not a claim seal and it is not a mark of consent and no office in Veyr can say what it means, and that has been on the page since before either of us was in a boat."

"**And the sheet says it is not to be counted with the nine and is not to be asked about,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and this yard is not going to ask about it, and I am going to write that down tomorrow and I am going to write down that we did not ask, and neither of us is going to say one word about what it is for, in this shed or anywhere else, ever.**"

"Agreed."

"**Write *agreed* in the second column and not the first,**" she said, "**and do not improve on it.**"

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fourteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a copy of an order is not the order, and that a copyist who does not read what she copies is not a keeper of it and is not to be made one; and that two marks of two sizes on one sheet are not a finding and are not a discovery and are not a market; and that a mark the sheet itself says is not to be asked about is not going to be asked about by this yard, and that a thing which is not asked about is not a thing this yard knows.*

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of April next at about half past eleven in the evening in this shed: that at about ten in the evening of the said day the copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city, bought at a penny on the Tuesday, was read out in that shed under a lamp by the holder of that writ and by the ship's hand, and that there were two of them in that shed and no other person, and that a person of no office of this harbor was asked on the Wednesday evening and said that she would not come, and the reason against the line of the not-coming is the standing of a certificate of method in a room with a lamp in it, and that she said out loud that she was not being left out because she was not able.*

*And that at the head of the said copy there is a name of a chamber which this yard has not been able to place in any book in that shed or in the sheet behind the tide table, and that a name on a piece of paper is a thing a man can go and look at and it is entered, and that the month and the year of the date on the said copy are on the line at which the fold has parted and were not guessed at and the fold was not mended.*

*And that the order on the said copy is nine lines, and that the ninth of them is that it is to be carried out in one tide, and that the said nine lines say that the fleet is ordered down, that the order is of the fleet and not of a master of it, that the masters are not to be asked which of them made it, that the order is carried by the masters and not by any office, and that no copy of it is to be made and a copy of it is not it and a man holding a copy has not got an order.*

*And that at the foot of the said copy there are nine marks, of which six are a circle with one stroke struck through it and of the same size and in the same hand, and of which two are a circle with two strokes struck smaller and off the center and not even with each other, and of which the ninth is nine short lines cut and not struck and about a third of the depth of the other eight and at an angle to the sheet that the other eight are not at; and that under the said nine, in the hand of the sheet and not the hand of the copy, there is one line which says that the ninth is not one of ours and is not to be counted with the nine and is not to be asked about.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past eleven in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said order carries no reason in it and names nobody, and that the holder of that writ and the ship's hand each read the said nine lines three times and neither of them found a tenth, and that the reason against the line of the reading of the tenth is that a copyist who copies nine lines out of nine does not leave out the tenth.*

*And that two marks of two sizes on the foot of the said copy are a yard's reading of the copy and not a finding, and that the sheet's own line puts the ninth outside the nine and that the said line is on the sheet, and that the disagreement between the six and the two is the finding and is not a defect in the evidence, and that the sheet says nothing whatever about whether the two agreed and nothing whatever about why they did not.*

*And that the ninth of the nine marks on the said copy is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction and is not a claim seal and is not a mark of consent, and that no office in Veyr can say what it means, and that the holder of that writ and the ship's hand agreed out loud in that shed that neither of them was going to ask about it or to say one word about what it is for, and that the said agreement is entered here and not in the first column, because a thing that was not done is not a thing that was done, and the reason against the line of the asking is the line the sheet itself sets, and it is not improved on.*

*And that this yard has bought at a penny a copy of a copy and can check the paper, the fold, the hand, the two sizes of mark and the shape of the ninth, and can check nothing else whatever, and that the reason against the line of the buying is that a record a man can check is worth what the man who checks it is worth, and that this yard is a poor man with a compass.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on the said day, and that a person of no office was in that shed at six in the morning and was not in it at ten at night, and was not told and did not ask, and that this is the fifth day of ten on which that woman has been in that shed or on that mole and has not been asked for anything.*

---

**Nothing came into that yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it. The cistern was charged for the forty-second time and this party walked none of it. A lamp was lit in that shed at about ten and turned round and went out again and about three quarters of an hour passed with two people in the room, and a woman of no office was in that shed at six that morning and not at nine or at ten, and there is no figure for the hour either and there is not going to be one.**
