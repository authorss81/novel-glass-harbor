# Chapter 270

*The Whole of What It Cost, Read Back From a Leaf With Four Columns, and the Two Halves of One Sentence*

Wednesday the fourteenth of April, in the shed on the mole, and at the head of that mole.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday and the column did not move, and the wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and nothing came into that yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and the figure in the bag is the figure it has been since Saturday night and this is the fourth night in a row that nothing has moved in it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Wednesday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour the shed does things, and the two of them and the boy, who was not on the stone and came down and was not put on anything.

Nessa Pike read it in her own left hand and read all four columns, including the empty ones, and she read the reasons against the lines in the second column in the same voice as everything else, and it is the same voice because she has read them aloud in that shed for six years and has never once found a reason for a different one.

**The first column of that leaf has three entries in it and no more.** The mark set on the twelfth of March, fouled on the weather side, shackle off and on again, wire cut and mended on the water side, a line that is not that yard's and a flat on the crown of the bow. The cut made on the seventh of April, three turns on the water side, against that yard's own practice. And the descent: forty fathoms, the eighth, ninth and tenth of April, a floor of laid stone, a figure at the end of every course, a bar in a socket, nine feet of chain, a man of twenty-eight hurt, and a week's bed at Ashtide.

**The second column has the cut, the reason, the lost day, the not-asking, the not-writing, the not-asking a boy, and the sentence about a wrong party for a question.**

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the boy's hand is at the head of it, and the four lines above the named line are four and they are nameless and they are not struck out and they are in about sixty boats on that coast.**

**And the fourth column has four lines in it, being the lines of the fourteenth of May and of the fifth, the eleventh, the thirteenth and the fourteenth of April, and the last of them was written at about half past three that afternoon and is the last thing in that book.**

The first three of them were read out and neither the holder of that writ nor the shipwright improved on one of them and neither of them will. The fourth is this, and it was read out in her own left hand and it is the reason this book exists:

*What it is not: — that a sentence said in a shed is not an answer, and that the question is not answered, and that two hundred and forty feet and four hundred and twenty feet are not a fleet and are not a seabed, and that this yard is not going to call a made floor a fleet, and that the reason is that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay was answered on the twenty-eighth of March with a refusal and was right to take it as one, and that the answer to the question is four hundred miles west under seventy fathoms and that there is nobody on this coast to ask and that this yard is not going to ask a man of sixty-one what he will take.*

And that is the fourth column and it is a column a man cannot check, which this yard knows, and entered on the twenty-seventh of March, and has been paying for since.

---

Then Calder Marr said the thing that the ten days were for, and he said it in four sentences and it is in the first column in those four sentences and no more was added to it afterwards.

"**We went down forty fathoms off the south end of this quay and we found a floor that a man laid, and the floor has the crown of this mole on it, and the numbers cut at the end of its courses are a working mark and not a name. The question this yard has been asking for five volumes is whether the fleet was scuttled or whether it went down. There is no fleet on that floor and there is not going to be, and this yard is not going to tell anybody that there is, and a man of thirty-one of the third lay put a Crown chart on a fish quay in this harbor on the twenty-eighth of March and got a refusal and was right to take it as one. So the floor is not the fleet and the floor is not the answer. The floor is the method. The method is at seventy fathoms about four hundred miles west, and the order a made thing was done in is cut into the made thing, and a man can go and look at it. And this party is the wrong party for it, and that is the other half of the same sentence, and I am not going to stand in this shed and pretend the two halves are bad news, because they are one thing, and the thing is that the volume's question is answerable and the answering of it is four hundred miles west under seventy fathoms and everybody who can get there is somebody we are not going to ask.**"

"What do we do on Monday," said Nessa Pike.

"**We have a bag with twenty-four marks and seven shillings and sevenpence in it and twenty-five weeks of wage behind us and a chain on a nail and a bar of iron in a drawer and nothing booked, and the answer is on a floor four hundred miles west, and there is one form in this volume's whole life that has never been served on anybody and nobody in this country knows what it does.** And a form is served by being left, at an address, on a day, and a form costs the person it is left on their afternoon and their fear, and neither of those is ours, and we are not going to be the reason a person in a town two days down that coast is frightened in her own kitchen."

"You are not going to ask her."

"**I am not going to ask her, and I am not going to write to her, and I am not going to send anybody, and I am not going to have it in a book that we did not, and I am going to have it in a book that we did not, in the second column, where it can be argued with.**"

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fourteenth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon on the said Wednesday by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a boy of twenty-two who came down on his own account and was not put on anything; and that the said leaf holds three entries in its first column, eleven lines in its second, none in its third, and three in its fourth; and that the four lines above the said boy's named line are four and are nameless and are not struck out.*

*And that the said leaf does not go on any wall in that harbor and never will, and that nothing went up on any wall in that harbor in these ten days and nothing came off, and that the two naval notices are not on that wall and were not taken down by that party, and that the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it.*

*And that the holder of that writ said in that shed at about half past four in the afternoon on the said Wednesday, in four sentences, that the floor found at forty fathoms off the south end is not about the fleet and is not an answer, and that the finding is the method, and that the method is at seventy fathoms about four hundred miles west, and that this party is the wrong party for it; and that the said four sentences were entered without addition or improvement.*

*And that a compressor, a housing, a pipe, gauges and one man off a Crown house book were engaged for three days on the sixth day of April past at nine marks a day and were charged for three days, and that the three days at the base rate came to thirty-six marks and eight shillings, and that a second man off that book came on the third of those three days at a further two marks and eightpence, and that the three days together came to thirty-eight marks, eight shillings and eightpence, and that the travel of the man of about thirty-four came to six marks and two shillings, and that the whole of what that descent cost is entered in the three days and in nothing else and no part of it was paid out of anything but that yard's own book.*

*And that a man of twenty-eight of a pressure house at Ternwatch is in a bed at a hospital at Ashtide with a shoulder that will not go above his head and two ribs broken, and that a Crown house book carries him as unfit for five weeks from the ninth day of April and as fit for light work after and as fit on the fifth day of June, and that a week of that bed and the surgeon's fee and the boat to get him there were paid out of that yard's own book on the tenth day of April, and that the second week is not that yard's.*

*And that a man of fifty-one of the second lay paid eleven marks on the sixth day of April past and has lost about a half of a season's gear and has got back two hundred and eight fathoms out of four hundred and eleven, and that the ground is shut and the herring come off it at the end of the next month, and that he has said he will not pay the difference and will not be thanked, and that he was not thanked.*

*And that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay was not asked anything in these ten days and asked nothing, and that a man of about thirty of the second lay was not asked anything about the fleet in these ten days either, and that the third lay is a berth short of two of the two it is short of and has been since the autumn.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the four people of that coast, four days down it, who go into deep water without a book, were not asked, and that the reason against that line is the reason of the second day of April last about a trade and a price, and that it is not improved on, and that the reason it is not improved on is that a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything on any of these ten days and the holder of that writ did not go over to him on any of them and a man of sixty-one did not come to this shed and was not sent anything.*

*And that not writing to a woman of no office, and not going up that coast road to a doorstep, and not asking her for anything, are not things that were done and are not in the first column, and that this yard is an address as of the third day of April past in the ordinary post at a penny, and that an address is a thing that can be given to a man who is going somewhere, and that a form is served by being left and that the price of it is a person's afternoon and a person's fear and that this yard is not going to be the reason.*

*And that the condition is charged as a body and not as a name, and one day of the ten was lost to it, being the eleventh, and the day carries no figure because there is no figure for a day, and the reason against the line is the reason that has been against that line on every lost day since the sixth of October past and has no name in it and is not going to have one in this volume.*

*And that no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days and no return of any kind was made out of any of them, and that four returns stand and there is no fifth.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this volume and was not told in any of these ten days, and that it may not be told in a shed, on a mole, in a market or in about forty people's hearing, and that it is owed to a room with two people in it at a price, and that it buys nothing and restores nothing and is not due until a person has earned it.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard in the ten days ended on the said Wednesday and eleven marks came into it on the sixth of April, and that is the whole of it, and twenty-four marks, seven shillings and sevenpence is in the bag, which is three thousand nine hundred and thirty-one pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks does not exist, because there has not been such a figure since the seventh day of April, and nobody in that shed said one word about it at the time. The whole of what went out of that yard in the ten days ended on the said Wednesday is fifty-one marks and six shillings, being the set for three days at thirty-six marks and eight shillings and then a second man off that Crown house book at two marks and eightpence, the travel of the man of about thirty-four at six marks and two shillings, the boat of the man of the second lay for a day and four fathoms of line at one mark and eightpence and fourpence, the boat to a hospital at Ashtide with a man in her at one mark and eightpence, the man's fee at Ashtide at four marks, and a week's keep of a bed at seven shillings; and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is sixty marks and tenpence in seventeen transactions at six counters. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past, unmoved, and he is in no book and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure, and that is in no sentence with the bed at Ashtide. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these ten days and no digit of anything he said is on a page of that book and the chest was not opened and the lead plug is in it with a number in no book in that harbor. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, and a letter came up that hill on the second of April and nothing was asked of her and nothing was written back. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand, the third column is not filled, nobody is appointed to it, and the boy's hand is at the head of it, and he was not on the stone on any of these ten days. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest, there are twenty envelopes on that bench, and twenty-five weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and the wage was not paid on the sixth of April or on the thirteenth and the column did not move on either. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it will not be said a fifth time. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of that yard has been inside its first clause on any of these ten days, and the three days on which a person of that yard went into the water are entered in the first column with the reason against the line and the price named before it was agreed, and the reason against the line is that this is that yard's own book and is not that harbor authority's book and is not the same thing, and that there is no harbor authority's book to go in. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about on any of these ten days, and the note against it stands open, and the name of the place at seventy fathoms is not on a page of that book. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, was not raised and was not asked about in these ten days, and the limit four people of that coast wrote and signed stands and the holder did not ask to have it lifted once in twenty-one days. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered and nothing was measured and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved, and that party did not go near them on any of these ten days. The bench under that pump is bare for a seventy-fourth night, counting from the Sunday of the thirty-first of January instant, and it is the only bare thing in that shed. The slate wall came down bare twice in these ten days, being the forty-eighth and the forty-ninth Mondays since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it. The cistern was charged at no figure on three rota days and this party walked none of them, being the thirty-fifth, the thirty-sixth and the thirty-seventh of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil. The third lay filled on the first of April and is a berth short of two. The season of that harbor authority ended on the thirty-first of March past and no instrument has been issued out of that office since. A working harbor of that coast is poorer on the fourteenth day of April than it was on the twenty-third day of February past: one herring berth of two is out, the north berth is out, the mole-head light is not on because a light costs oil, the fire party's hose is dead until the spring, the tide gang is four men short, a boat of about forty tons is a hand short since the middle of July past, the hard at Ashtide is unanswered, nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and a reeve's office has shut for the season. And a yard on that mole has bought three days of a Crown house's air, and has found out that its own mole has a foot at forty fathoms, and has given a man of twenty-eight five weeks and a hospital, and has lost forty marks and six shillings on eleven, and has not paid a wage in twenty-five weeks, and has two objects of nobody's on its own crown and a chain on a nail and a bar of iron in a drawer, and nobody in this country to ask, and there is nobody on this coast to go to, and the answer is four hundred miles west.**
