# Chapter 274

*Five Doors, One of Which Is a Post Office, and a Penny at the Box on the Corner*

Sunday the eighteenth of April, in the shed on the mole, and at the post on the corner.

**The wall did not come down on the said Sunday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and one penny went out of it at about half past four in the afternoon, and no day of work was done in it and no day of work could be booked in it and both of those are the same fact.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

The sheet lay on the bench from about nine in the morning and it was not in the drawer and it was not in the tin and it was not on a wall and it was not burnt, and by about half past eleven the two of them and the woman of no office had said all of that out loud, and it went into the second column in that order, and the order matters and it is the order it happened in.

The drawer came first, because the drawer is where six pieces of paper live in that shed and the charter's place is empty, and Nessa Pike put the sheet in her hand and stopped with it halfway, and put it back down on the bench.

"**No,**" she said. "**Not in that drawer. There is a page in that drawer that five people in five towns agreed to keep and this yard is one of the five, and I am not putting an office's paper on top of it on a Sunday because on Monday it will be in the drawer and a drawer is a drawer and this yard has spent a day this week finding out what a drawer is.**"

"You are saying it is a keeper," said Mara Venn.

"I am saying a drawer is a drawer. **You are the one who has been saying the other thing since eight o'clock this morning and I would rather you said it than me, because you have a certificate for saying it and I have a book with four columns in it.**"

The tin was next. There is a tin in that shed and nothing has been in it since the twenty-eighth of April past, and the lid has not been oiled since the winter, and it shuts.

"**No,**" said Nessa Pike, without lifting it. "**Nothing has been in that tin since the twenty-eighth of April and there is a reason for that and the reason is that a tin shuts.**"

A wall was next and it took about nine seconds. The market wall in this harbor carries this yard's own fourth return under one sheet and is still the only paper on it that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and a sheet goes up on the outside of whatever is there, and the two naval notices are not on it and were not taken down by this yard and the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it.

"**No,**" said Calder Marr, "and I want it written down that the reason is not a reason about this yard's figures. **The reason is that a sheet on a wall in this harbor is a sheet that goes out of this harbor, and this yard has put four copies of its own sheet out of this harbor already and cannot undo one of them.**"

And a fire was the fourth, and nobody said a word for about twenty seconds, and then the woman of no office said it herself, which is in the first column in her words.

"**You may as well say it out loud,**" she said. "**You have all three of you been not saying it since Friday. I cannot burn it, and neither can you, and the reason is that a person who burns a paper with unsettled material on it has handled it, and a person who has handled it has kept it, and a person who has kept it is the thing the paper is about. You did not need me to tell you that. You worked it out between Tuesday and Friday and you have not written it down because writing it down makes it a rule and you do not want a rule in that book.**"

"No rule," said Nessa Pike. "**Four lines in the second column and no rule, and a rule in a book of mine is a thing I have to argue with for the rest of my life.**"

---

So there were five doors and four of them shut inside of two hours, and the fifth one is a box on the corner of a mole.

"It costs a penny," said Nessa Pike, "and the penny is nothing. I want the penny written down as nothing so that whoever reads this in nine years does not think the price was in the money."

"**The price is not in the money and the price is going to be in a town two days down that coast on somebody's step and it is an afternoon and it is a day of work and it is whatever a man of about thirty has left after that, and the form does not ask for any of it and does not need any of it and does not care, and that is what a form is. A form is a thing that costs a person nothing to obey and everything to refuse, and the whole of the expense of it falls on the person it is left on and not one penny of it comes back to anybody.**"

"**And we are going to do it,**" said Mara Venn.

"We are going to do it, and you are going to tell us the address, and the reason we are doing it is not a good reason and I want it written down in the second column in the words I say it in and not improved on afterwards."

---

There are three addresses in this yard's book for anybody who is not of this harbor and two of them were shut in about a minute.

**The first was this yard, and it was shut by the woman herself and not by either of them**, and she said that if the sheet is left at an address in this harbor it is left on a person, and that a person has to be a person and not a shed, and that if a form can be left on a shed then it can be left on a room and then on a county and then there is no end to it, and she said that in a voice that was not dramatic at all.

"**Put my name down as a place it could go,**" she said. "**Not because I would mind. Because I have been carrying it eleven weeks and I would be glad to be rid of it, and because I am the only person in this room who is not frightened of being a keeper, and a form that gets put on a person who is not frightened of it is a form that has been put in the wrong place, and I will not be the wrong place.**"

**The second was the boy, and it was shut in one sentence by Nessa Pike**, and the reason is entered.

"Nobody in this yard is put on a thing that has not been asked for, and he is twenty-two and he has been off the stone since the fifteenth of May past and he has forty marks standing whole and he is not on this."

"And the third," said Calder Marr, "**is the only one left, and it is in that drawer, and it is the address of a man of about thirty who cannot read, in the town this yard wrote to on the third of April past, who has a daughter of nineteen and a kitchen that a page of ours was read out in once in the autumn to about nine people and is not going to be read in again.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**So it is him,**" he said. "**Not because he is a keeper. He is a man with a house and a drawer in it and a page in the house that this yard put there without asking him and a form cannot tell the difference and neither can I, and the reason we are sending it to that address is not that he deserves it. The reason is that it is the only address in this book that is not ours.**"

He took the sheet up at about half past three in the afternoon and squared it on the bench with the heel of his hand the way she had squared it on Friday, which he did not decide to do and which she saw him do.

It went into the box on the corner of that mole at about half past four on the Sunday afternoon and it cost a penny.

---

And the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in the ship's left hand, the eighth line:

*What it is not: — that a sheet put in a post office is not a thing this yard has given away, and is not a thing this yard has kept, and is not a return, and does not go on any wall in this harbor; and that four doors shut on it inside two hours is not a decision and is a figure of what this yard has.*

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the eighteenth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said sheet was put in that drawer and taken out again on the said morning at about nine and not put back, and that it was not put in the tin in that shed, and that it was not put on any wall, and that it was not burnt, and that the said four were said out loud in that order and were entered in the second column in that order and the order is the order they happened in.*

*And that a woman of no office said out loud in that shed that a person who burns a paper with unsettled material on it has handled it and a person who has handled it has kept it, and that the said sentence was entered in the first column in her words and that neither of the other two said anything before she said it.*

*And that the holder of that writ ruled out three addresses in that shed on the said afternoon, being that shed itself, and the said woman of no office, and the boy of twenty-two; and that the said woman ruled out her own name and gave the reason in her own words; and that the said boy was ruled out in one sentence by the ship's hand and no name of his is in any column of that book and none has ever been.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said afternoon that the reason the said sheet is going to an address in a town two days down that coast is that it is the only address in that book which is not that yard's, and that the man at it is not being sent it because he is a keeper of it and that a form cannot tell the difference and neither can the holder of that writ; and that the said sentence was entered without improvement.*

*And that the said sheet went into the ordinary post at a box on the corner of that mole at about half past four in the afternoon on the said Sunday at a penny, being the one address in that book in a town two days down that coast, and that one penny went out of that yard's own book and that is the whole of what went out of it on the said day.*

*And that one line was entered in the fourth column of the said leaf, being the eighth line in that column, being the line of the eighteenth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the sheet was not put in the drawer, and the reason against that line is that a drawer is a drawer and that this yard spent the seventeenth day of April past finding out what a drawer is; and that it was not put in the tin and the reason is that a tin shuts; and that it was not put on a wall and the reason is that a sheet on a wall in that harbor is a sheet that goes out of that harbor; and that it was not burnt and the reason is the reason the woman of no office gave out loud and it is entered in the first column in her words and is not improved on; and that the said four are in this column and not the first because four doors shutting is not a thing a man did.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed that a form is a thing that costs a person nothing to obey and everything to refuse and that the whole of the expense of it falls on the person it is left on and not one penny of it comes back to anybody, and that the said sentence was entered without improvement, and that the reason against the line of putting it in the post is that four of the five doors in that yard were shut on the said day and the fifth one is a box on a corner, and that a yard which says it had a choice on a day and did not is a yard that is describing the inside of a building.*

*And that on the fourteenth day of April past the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed that this yard was not going to be the reason a person in a town two days down that coast is frightened in her own kitchen, and that on the eighteenth day of April past it put a sheet in the ordinary post at that address, and that the said undertaking is broken and is entered in the first column as broken and not as kept, and that nobody in that shed offered a reason that made it smaller, and that the reason is the one above and the reason is about a drawer and not about a person.*

---

**One penny went out of this yard on the said Sunday and nothing came into it, and twenty-one marks, seven shillings and fivepence is in the bag, which is three thousand four hundred and forty-nine pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and there is nothing else. The whole of what went out of that yard since the sixth of December past is sixty-two marks, twelve shillings and fourpence in twenty transactions at seven counters, of which two are a penny at that box.**
