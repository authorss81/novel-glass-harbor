# Chapter 290

*Tuesday the Fourth of May, the Twenty-Eighth Week With No Envelope, and Twenty-Two Entries and Eighty-Eight Lines Read Back From a Leaf With Four Columns*

Tuesday the fourth of May, in the shed on the mole, and on the bench outside it.

**The wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it. The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, no envelope was made, the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and twenty-eight weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Tuesday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour the shed does things, and there were three of them in that shed and nobody else and nobody sent for, and a woman of no office of this harbor, thirty, stood at the end of the bench and did not sit down because she had not been asked to.

**The first column of that leaf has twenty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were thirteen of them on Saturday the twenty-fourth of April past and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Sunday the twenty-fifth to Monday the third, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own entry makes twenty-three. The nine are a word in a shed, a counter where a woman does not read what she copies, a penny off that counter and a descent priced at that bench, the descent itself, a reading under a lamp, a disagreement about a column, six fathoms on some steps, a morning on the crown of that mole, and a question nobody answered.

**The second column of that leaf has eighty-eight lines in it.** Forty-eight of them were in it on the twenty-fourth of April past, thirty-one went in between the twenty-fifth and the third of May, and nine went in this afternoon.

The ship's hand did not read those eighty-eight out and did not read them out twice. She read the nine of this afternoon in her own left hand with the reasons against the lines in the same voice as everything else, and then she said the one thing that had gone wrong in the ten days, because she always says it.

"**Nine refusals in ten days,**" she said, "**and four things that cost money, and one of them cost a man of fifty-one six fathoms of his gear. And in about four years a man in a city will read that book and say this yard did nothing, and there is still no column in it for the saying of that, and I have stopped asking for one.**"

---

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it, and the four lines above his named line are four and are nameless and are not struck out and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and came down on none of them and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered.**

**And the fourth column has eighteen lines in it**, eleven of them from before the twenty-fourth of April and seven of them in the ten days, and the last of them went in this afternoon and it is the last thing in that book:

*What it is not: — that a sheet bought off a shelf for a penny is not the order and is not a reading of anything; that nine lines with no reason in them are not an answer; that two marks of two sizes are not a community and are not a name; and that a question asked on a mole head is not a thread and does not go in this book, and that this yard has put three things in houses in ten days and cannot help any of the three.*

---

And then the three of them said three things, and none was argued with afterwards and none has been improved on since, and they are about three different objects and all three of them are true.

**The first was about the words, and the holder of that writ said it.**

"**The order says the fleet was ordered down. It is nine lines and there is no tenth. The second says the order is of the fleet and not of a master of it and the fourth says the masters are not to be asked which of them made it, and so somebody went out of their way to write down that no man is to be able to say it was his. That is in it, on a sheet in a drawer in this shed, and it cost a penny. And I have not got one word of who wanted it and I am not going to have it. This is the answer to the thing this yard has carried since the middle of March, and it is about half of one, and the half that is missing is a name.**"

**The second was about the marks, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**Six and two and a ninth. Two communities and an argument, put on the paper and left there on purpose, and the sheet's own hand then says the ninth is not one of ours and is not to be counted with the nine. And here is the part I have not said out loud in ten days. If those two marks had been one mark there would be nothing on that sheet but a decision, and a decision with one mark on it is a thing a man can repeat, and this is not, because these people argued about it afterwards. So the marks are the finding and the marks are not a resolution, and a man who comes out of that room and says the fleet was ordered down and stops there has said a quarter of it in a key nobody in this country can check.**"

**The third was about the house, and the woman of no office said it.**

"**A drawer in a shed on this mole. Bought for a penny off a shelf, made nine years ago for a man who was buying a house, by a woman who has never turned it over and has asked never to be told what is on it and never will be. That is the third of them. The other two are a form on a step two days down this coast and a paper with a seal on it under a bed in this harbor. None of the three of us is a keeper and all three of us are, and that is not a puzzle, that is a chair, and the chair is in a house, and a house is a place a person cannot refuse anybody anything about. There is no handle on the inside of any of the three of them and I would not know what to do with one if there was.**"

---

And then the holder of that writ said the turn out loud, and it was the second time in ten days, and he gave the reason for that first, being that a thing said once to two people in a shed with the door shut is a story and the same thing said to three people in the same room is a record, and that there is nothing in the difference except the number of people in the room. It went into the first column in the words of the Friday and nothing has been added to it since.

"**Here it is, and I am saying it twice, and I have told you why before I started.** The thing this yard has been calling an office's fear was real. It happened to real people in real water, and it was not one man's vanity and it was not a lie and it was not a weapon, and it was ordered, by more than one of them, together, and they argued about it afterwards and left the argument on the paper. Knowing that does not make anybody safe. I have five marks in a bag and twenty-eight weeks of no envelope, and I know now that the drawer matters, and that is worse than not knowing. And the other half, which is the ship's and goes in the book under her hand: the opacity was expensive for a reason, the reason is on a sheet in a drawer in this shed, and a reason is not a warrant. This yard does not get to be the one that says that office was right. The sheet is incomplete, and a party that says false about an incomplete record has stopped being the party that says what it is not.**"

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the fourth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said Tuesday by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a person of no office of this harbor; and that the said leaf holds twenty-three entries in its first column, being thirteen on the twenty-fourth day of April past, nine from the twenty-fifth to the third day of May and one this evening, and eighty-eight lines in its second, being forty-eight on the twenty-fourth, thirty-one from the twenty-fifth to the third and nine this afternoon, and none in its third, and eighteen in its fourth, being eleven from before the twenty-fourth and seven in the ten days.*

*And that three people in that shed said three things about the said sheet on the said afternoon, the first about the words and the second about the marks and the third about the house, and that the three differ in what they are about and not in whether they are correct; and that the holder of that writ said in that shed at about a quarter to five that the thing this yard has been calling an office's fear was real and was ordered by more than one community and argued about afterwards with the argument left on the paper, and that knowing it does not make anybody safe, and that the opacity was expensive for a reason and a reason is not a warrant, and that this yard does not get to be the one that says that office was right; and that he had said the same thing in that shed on the Friday afternoon to two people and said out loud on the said Tuesday that he was saying it a second time, and that the reason against the line of the saying of it twice is that a thing said once to two people with a door shut is a story and the same thing said to three in the same room is a record; and that the said sentence was entered without addition.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that nine of the thirty-one lines entered in the second column between the twenty-fifth day of April past and the third day of May are refusals, being the not-buying, the not-asking, the not-counting, the not-writing, the not-going, the not-sending, the not-paying, the not-answering and the not-saying; and that four things were done in those ten days which cost money and one of them cost a man of fifty-one six fathoms of his gear, and that there is still no column in that book for the saying of any of it.*

*And that who was the first to use a mark on a paper to keep a thing in an emergency instead of holding it was asked out loud at a mole head on the third day of May in about nine people's hearing by a person of no office and was not answered by anybody, and that the holder of that writ said out loud that he did not know and that there is nobody in this country he could ask, and that the said question goes in no column of that book and on no wall in that harbor.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on any of the ten days, and that it may not be told in a shed, on a mole, in a market or in about forty people's hearing, and that it is owed to a room with two people in it at a price and it buys nothing and restores nothing; and that a woman of no office stood in that shed on five of the ten days and was not told and did not ask, and that her forty marks first after food were not mentioned once and stand.*

*And that no person of that yard was in a fish market on any of the ten days and nobody was sent to one; that a certificate of method in the reading of worked glass and its faults has had the standing of it spent in that market on the twenty-second day of April past and is not withdrawn and cannot be and is not going to be restored; that a man of about thirty who cannot read two days down that coast has a sheet on his table and three marks that are not going to be paid; that a woman of about fifty-eight of that harbor has a paper with a seal on it in a drawer with her will and has not been written to; and that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second.*

*And that the four people of that coast, four days down it, who go into deep water without a book, were not asked on any of the ten days and the complaint was not raised on any of them, and the reason against the line of all of it is the reason of the second day of April last about a trade and a price, and that the fact that a copy of a municipal order came out of a drowned city for a penny is not a reason to ask them and was not used as one, and that the limit four of them wrote and signed stands.*

*And that the condition is charged as a body and not as a name, and that two days of the ten were lost to it, being the twentieth day of April past and the second of May, and that neither carries a figure because there is no figure for a day, and that the reason against the line of both is the reason that has been against that line since the sixth day of October past and has no name in it.*

*And that the holder of that writ lost the second of those two days to a slate and to a compass on the same morning, and that the ship's hand entered the figure in the top course in her own left hand and he did not touch the slate and did not check the entry, and that the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it since the twelfth day of April past.*

*And that Tobias Wren was not sent for on any of the ten days and nothing was asked of him on any of them, and that nothing in the ten days made him a teacher and nothing made him a door; and that a man of sixty-one at a far bench was occupied on three of the ten days and was not asked for anything on any of them and asked nothing on any of them, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him on any of the ten; and that no office was in a room on any of the ten days and no clerk in any copying room explained what the said sheet does, and that Halven Rook is on a seal line at the foot of a form on a step two days down that coast and is in no room.*

*And that the whole of what the said sheet cost is a penny, a drawer in this shed, and the rest of a woman's life not knowing what is on it; that the reason against the not-telling her is a thing she asked for on a Monday morning in a room off a fish market in her own words; and that a sheet in a house is custody an office can reach, and that this yard built that on the sixth of March past and had it the wrong way round for fifty-nine days.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on any of the ten days ended on the said Tuesday, and nothing has come into it since the eleven marks of Elias Voke on the sixth of April last and nothing is going to. The whole of what went out of it in those ten days is two thousand two hundred and eighty-one pence, being fourteen marks, three shillings and fivepence, and it is two things: a penny at a counter off the fish market on the twenty-seventh, and two thousand two hundred and eighty pence on the twenty-eighth for one day of a set belonging to a pressure house at Ternwatch, the price named on the Tuesday evening before. Five marks, one shilling and fourpence is in the bag, and it was nineteen marks, four shillings and ninepence on the morning of the twenty-fifth, and that was itself five marks, two shillings and tenpence shorter than the bag held on the morning of the fifteenth of April past. The figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks does not exist and will not come back while that bag is above six hundred and forty pence, and nobody in that shed said one word about it at any hour of any of them.**

**What is owed stands where it has stood. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these ten days and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced and neither paid. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, nobody has asked whose either is, and this yard went back down to the socket the bar came out of on the twenty-eighth of April past and was not able to say one word about it.**

**Nothing moved. The wage column did not move on either of the two wage Tuesdays in these ten days, and the next is the eleventh of May and it will not move on that either. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts unmoved and was not said out loud a fifth time. The slate wall came down bare twice, the cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them, and no figure was entered against a day that is not a Monday or a Thursday. The far bench was occupied on three of the ten days and was not approached. The bench under that pump is bare for the ninety-third night, and the two objects of nobody's are three hundred and twenty-seven days up the crown of that mole and neither has moved.**

**Nothing else was touched. The plate in eleven feet was not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about on any of these ten days, and the note against it stands open for a third volume. The case of five bells is not raised and the limit four people of that coast wrote and signed stands. The delayed note is at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was measured. A chain of custody is a practice and not an office and was made on the sixth of March past and is not aged by this. No finding of worked glass was made in these ten days and four returns stand and there is no fifth, and no sitting was held in that hearing room on any day of them.**

**And a working harbor of that coast is poorer on the fourth day of May than it was on the twenty-third day of February: one herring berth of two out at that third lay, the north berth out, the mole-head light not on because a light costs oil, the fire party's hose dead until the spring, the tide gang four men short, the hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November past, a harbor authority's season ended on the thirty-first of March with nothing issued, a man of twenty-eight in a bed at Ashtide until the fifth of June, a man of fifty-one with about a half of a season's gear gone and six more fathoms of it pinned under a lead at forty fathoms, nineteen winters in a list being made up in a city, a form on a step two days down that coast, a drawer under a bed in this harbor, a third thing in a drawer in this shed, five marks and a shilling in a bag on a mole, and a sheet a woman of fifty-eight has never looked at and never will, and the answer is four hundred miles west under seventy fathoms, and nobody in this country can get there. On Monday the tenth of May the wall comes down bare for the fifty-third time, and the wage falls due on the eleventh for the twenty-ninth week and the column will not move on it. And about sixty boats on this coast have a sheet in them that says where every other copy is, and in about seven weeks those two things are going to meet somewhere that is not this yard, and this yard is not going to be the one that moves them along.**
