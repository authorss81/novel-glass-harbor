# Chapter 288

*Sunday the Second of May, Two Instruments and Two Hands, a Morning That Carries No Figure, and Nine Short Lines That This Yard Is Not Going to Count*

Sunday the second of May, the crown of that mole, and the shed on that mole.

**The wall did not come down on the said Sunday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

He was on the crown of that mole at about half past seven with a compass in his hand and he could not use it.

That is the whole of it. There is a box and a card and a needle and a bit of looking, and on the Wednesday he had handed a slate across a thwart, and on the Sunday morning he got up out of the place where he sleeps about half past five because he could not lie there, and came up the steps in the dark, and stood on the crown of that mole with the mole-head light not on because a light costs oil, and looked at two baulks of green timber a mile and a quarter off the head of it, and could not do it.

The bar was four hundred yards beyond them and about a mile and a half long, and the water on the far side of it was a shade darker and rougher and stood up in a low sea, and the water between it and the mole was flat and pale.

"**I have got to go and stand at something for a bit,**" he said, to nobody, at about ten past eight, and then went and stood at the thing.

There is no figure for a day and there is no figure for a morning, and the day of the twentieth of April past is in the second column of that book with a reason against the line and no name in it, and this morning goes in the same column for the same reason, and the reason is the reason of the sixth of October past and it is not improved on and nobody is going to improve on it.

He came down off the crown at about one and went and sat on the bench inside the door and did not write anything and did not say anything about it, and nobody asked him about it, and that is the whole of what is entered.

He had eaten nothing since the night before and she put a cup of water on the bench beside him at about one and a half and he drank the whole of it without noticing that he was doing it, and it was a quarter past one before he was able to hold a pen, and he wrote three lines in the day's book in his own right hand, slowly, and gave the pen back, and she read what he had written and did not change one word of it, and that is the whole of what is entered.

---

Nessa Pike had the slate on the bench when he came in and a stick of chalk beside it, and the entry she was writing was the figure.

"**Forty-second, top course, about a quarter of an inch deep where the other forty-one are about an inch, strokes laid cross-stroke first,**" she said. "**That is Fisk's hand from Wednesday and it is a hand that has been to that floor and has seen both,**" and she wrote it in her own left hand, which takes her about twice the time it takes her.

"You are not going to ask me to check it."

"**No.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because if I take that slate in my hand it will be a join in my hand, and then there will be two, and there is no third instrument in this shed for the second one to get out of the way of.**"

"Then who signs it."

"**Nobody signs anything in this yard, and you know that, and you have known it since the third of April past and the reason has not changed.**"

---

And then the ninth, and it is the only thing either of them wanted to talk about on that Sunday and neither of them had said a word about it since the Thursday.

The ninth mark on that copy is nine short lines in a group. It goes about a third of the depth of the other eight and it is cut and not struck and it is at an angle to the sheet that the other eight are not at.

**This yard has a book with a nine-line mark in it. It came up out of a wreck in the summer before last, it was carried a long way and it closed, and what it closed as is this: that it is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction, that it is not a claim seal, that it is not a mark of consent, and that no office in Veyr can say what it means. And nobody in this yard knows what it is for and nobody in this country is going to be told.**

The ninth mark on that copy has the same shape.

"**Well,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I have seen it,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I have seen it too. And I have got a piece of paper in the drawer under the table with nine short lines on it that are the same shape as nine short lines at the bottom of an order off a drowned city that was copied nine years ago off a shelf off a man who was buying a house, and the first of them is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction and the second one is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction, and both of them are the same shape, and neither of them is the other one.**"

"**Then say the sentence and do not improve on it.**"

"**A shape is not a thing. Two things of one shape are two things, and this yard has a piece of paper that proves it and a piece of paper that might be a different piece of paper, and there is no man in this country who could go and look at the second one, and there is nothing in this shed that would let me put the two of them side by side and say they are one. A man laid stone with a figure cut in the end of every course so that the man setting it would know whether he had laid a whole course or part of one. That is a mark, and it is a good mark, and it is the work of somebody who wanted the next man to know. I am not going to take that mark and put it on a sheet and say it was a claim on anything. It is not a claim on anything. It is nine lines.**"

"**Then we do not count it,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and we do not go to Ternwatch and ask a man in a pressure house whether his foreman cut cross-stroke first, and we do not write to a town four days up this coast where there is a drawer with a copy of a sheet in it, and we do not put the two of them in a line together and leave it there for a man in four years.**"

"No."

"**No. And that is a refusal, and refusals go in the second column, and I am going to write it as one and I am going to write the reason against it and the reason is that a shape is not a thing and that the reason two things of one shape are two things is that nobody can go and look at either of them.**"

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in the ship's left hand, the sixteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that nine short lines in a group at the foot of a copy of an order off a drowned city are not the nine-line mark this yard already has in a book, and are not to be counted with it, and are not to be put in a line with it; and that the same shape in two places is not the same thing in two places, and a shape is not a thing; and that a mark a sheet says is not to be asked about is not going to be asked about by anybody in this yard, and that the not-asking is a refusal and is entered as one.*

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the second day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the holder of that writ stood on the crown of that mole from about half past seven in the morning until about one in the afternoon with a compass in his hand and could not use it, and that he said out loud at about ten past eight in the morning the sentence that is in that harbor's whole history for that thing, being that he has got to go and stand at something for a bit, and that nobody asked him about it on the said day.*

*And that at about one in the afternoon the entry of the figure in the top course was written in the first column in the ship's left hand from the slate and the hand of the ship's hand's man, and that the holder of that writ did not touch the said slate and did not check the said entry, and that the reason against the line of his checking it is in the second column of the said leaf with a reason against the line and no name in it.*

*And that nine feet of chain lies on the floor in a heap about the width of a dinner plate on the said day and has not been measured or cleaned since the ninth day of April past, and that a bar of square iron two feet long with a cold eye in it is in the drawer of that shed, and that neither has been sold and neither has been given away and nobody has asked whose either is and that this yard was on the floor on the Wednesday preceding where the bar came out of a socket cut in the dressed face of a stone and was not able to say one word about whose the bar is.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the morning of the said day was lost to the body and carries no figure, and that the reason against the line of the loss is the reason that has been against that line since the sixth day of October past and has no name in it, and that the twentieth day of April past is the same kind of line and carries no figure either, and that there is no figure for a day and there is no figure for a morning.*

*And that the two of them refused out loud in that shed on the said day to count the ninth of the nine marks on the said copy with the nine-line mark already in that yard's own book, and to go to a pressure house at Ternwatch and ask a man there whether his foreman cut cross-stroke first, and to write to a town four days up this coast, and to put the two of them in one line together, and that the reason against each of those four lines is the same reason, which is that a shape is not a thing and that nobody in this country can go and look at either of them.*

*And that the concealment is owed to this yard and was not told on the said day, and that a person of no office was in that shed at six in the morning and again at about two in the afternoon, and was not told and did not ask, and that her forty marks first after food stand and were not mentioned once in the said ten days they have stood in that shed for.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and five marks, one shilling and fourpence is in the bag, which is eight hundred and sixteen pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is seventy-nine marks, five shillings and one penny in twenty-three transactions at eight counters.**
