# Chapter 293

*Friday the Seventh of May, a Far Bench Occupied From Ten Until Four, a Round of Glass Out of a Bed, and About Six Hours of Weather in That Harbor*

Friday the seventh of May, off the south end of that mole, and in the shed on that mole.

**The wall did not come down on the said Friday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Friday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it, and not one penny of the day's plant and air came out of it either, because it did not go in.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Friday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The reason for going out again was said out loud at the bench at eight in the morning, and it was said before anybody was asked to do anything, and it is the whole of it.

"**The plate stands in the bed. The bed is empty. On the eleventh the plate goes into the rebate, and when it goes it goes on that round, because the round is on the floor of that room and it is loose and a plate the width of a doorway does not come down on a stone floor and stay off it.**"

"**So it comes up,**" said Fisk.

"**So it comes up, or it stays down there and the plate grinds it into about nine pieces in a room at seventy fathoms and nobody in this country ever hears of it again, and that is the other end of the same decision.**" He turned round to the man of about thirty-four. "**Say the price of it out loud before I agree to it, because that is the rule of this yard and the rule is not going to stop being the rule because I am frightened of what I am about to do.**"

The man of about thirty-four read it off the paper in the voice he uses for a book.

"**The day is fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence and it is the same as yesterday and I have not come to argue about it. The round comes up and it goes on the crown of that mole under a tarpaulin and it does not come into that shed, because the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in this harbor and I have read that notice and you have not.**"

"**And that is not in a book of ours,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Write that down. Not in the first column. In the second column, with the reason, which is that the reason is a clause of a notice and the yard did not write it and cannot lift it.**"

"**Agreed.**"

---

They read the six conditions at the boat again in the same order and the bar was named again in the same words, and the pot went down at about nine and came up at about a quarter past eleven, and the gauge read eighty-four pounds and the pencil mark was not moved.

The man off the Crown house book went down, and the hand on the hose held him, and the ship's hand called the depth off the slate at every ten feet and the holder of that writ stood with his thumb on the frame of the boat and did not go in at all that day, and nobody has ever said out loud why and it is not going to be improved on.

**And this is the whole of what came up.**

A round of glass about the size of a saucer, in a collar of metal, with a wheel in the collar and a screw through the collar, and the screw is down about two turns and stopped. The round is dirty in a way that glass does not get dirty on a shore and it has the green of a thing that has been made in a furnace and not blown.

**And on the collar there is a chain.** It is not a chain of this yard and it is not a chain of iron. It is eleven links about a finger long, and every link is stamped, and there is a date stamped on every link, and the dates are not the same date and they do not all read the same way, and the last link is thicker than the others and has something on the face of it that is struck and not cut and is under the shadow of the collar, and nobody scratched at it to see.

**And under the round, on the floor of the room, there was a piece of worked glass about the size of a hand. It would not come away from the round. It is bedded in it, or the round is bedded in it, and two men in a boat at about half past eleven in the morning could not separate them and did not try hard, because a thing two men cannot separate is a thing that goes up the way it is.**

Nobody read the marks on it. There are marks on it and they are cut and not struck and this yard is not going to say what they are, and the reason against the line of saying is that a mark a man cannot read is a mark and not a sentence, and the reason against the line of reading is that there is a collar over it.

---

The crate went up onto the crown of that mole at about one and it is under a tarpaulin about nine feet from the two objects of nobody's and about four feet from where they have stood since the autumn, and it did not go into that shed and it is not going into that shed.

Then the weather came up.

It came out of the south-west at about four in the afternoon and it did not stop until about ten that night, and there is no account of it in any column of that yard's book and there never will be one.

**The skylight of that shed went at about half past four. The door light went at about five. A pane of plate about four feet by three came out of a shopfront on that front at about six and lay on the mole stones and stayed there. Two boats had their running glass taken out of them at about half past six and one of them is a boat of about forty tons lying inside the harbor line with a man of about fifty-five's own ground under her. Eleven windows along that front lost something between seven and eight. The lens of the mole-head light went at about a quarter to eight, and that light had not been lit since the fifteenth of March past because a light costs oil, and it is not lit now either, and the two things are not put in the same sentence by anybody.**

About a hundred people were out of that front in the rain. Nobody was hurt on the said Friday. Nobody has said out loud that the round of glass came up at about a quarter past eleven and the weather came up at about four, and the reason against the line of any character having said it is in the second column and it is the reason against the line of the weather itself.

---

*And in the second column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in a hand that is not the ship's, on a page that is not on top of the ship's page:*

*That a glass storm went through this harbor on the seventh day of May next from about four in the afternoon until about ten at night, and that the skylight of this shed, the door light of this shed, a pane of plate out of a shopfront on that front, the running glass of two boats inside the harbor line, eleven windows along that front and the lens of the mole-head light were broken or taken out on the said day, and that about a hundred people were out in the rain, and that nobody was hurt, and that this yard is not going to say what caused it in any volume and is not going to write a cause against this line, and that the reason against the line is that a yard which cannot check a thing does not get to name what made it.*

*That is the twenty-first of the two hands and the two pages, and the other hand is the ship's and the other page is in that book on the mole, and neither page is on top of the other, and the two of them are a mile and a quarter apart in a boat.*

---

*And in the fourth column of the said leaf, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twentieth line:*

*What it is not: — that a round of glass out of a bed in a sill is not a seal and is not a mark and is not a reading of anything; that a bed with nothing in it is not an entry and is not a keeper; that a chain of eleven links with a date on each of them is not a record of this harbor and is not of any date that anybody in this shed can check; and that weather is not a finding, and that this yard has put nothing in a column about the weather and is not going to.*

---

*First column, entered on Friday the seventh day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the pot was put on the bottom at about nine in the morning off the south end of that mole and came up at about a quarter past eleven; that the gauge read eighty-four pounds as it read on the sixth and the pencil mark was not moved; that a round of glass about the size of a saucer in a metal collar with a wheel in the collar and a screw through the collar was taken off the floor of the said room at about seventy fathoms, the screw being down about two turns and stopped; and that a piece of worked glass about the size of a hand was under the said round and would not come away from it and went up with it.*

*And that on the said round there is a chain of eleven links about a finger long, every link stamped and a date stamped on every link, the dates not being the same and not all reading the same way, and that the last link is thicker than the others and has a mark struck on the face of it under the shadow of the collar, and that nobody scratched at the said mark and nobody read it.*

*And that the price of the said day was named out loud at that bench at about eight in the morning before anybody had agreed to it, being fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence off a book at a pressure house at Ternwatch, and that the said round was put on the crown of that mole under a tarpaulin at about one in the afternoon and did not go into that shed; and that no person of that yard was inside the bar of four hundred yards off the outer marks on the said day and the said bar was not narrowed by anything and was not asked to be.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said round of glass is not in that shed and is not in any shed, and that the reason against the line of its being in one is the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October past, which keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in this harbor, and which this yard did not write and cannot lift.*

*And that a glass storm went through that harbor on the said day and no cause is named against it in this book or in any other, and that the reason against the line of naming one is that a yard which cannot check a thing does not get to name what made it; and that the same entry in a second hand and on a second page, in a boat a mile and a quarter off that mole, is the other half of the same account and is not on top of this page.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there is one thousand four hundred and sixty-five pence in the bag, which is nine marks, two shillings and one penny. Fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence went off a book four hundred miles off, and a round of glass is on the crown of that mole under a tarpaulin with a chain on it that has a date on every link and a mark on the last one that nobody has read, and the two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty days up that crown and the bench under that pump is bare for the ninety-sixth night, and the skylight of that shed is out and the door light is out and the lens of that light is out, and there is no figure in this yard's book for any of it and there never will be.**
