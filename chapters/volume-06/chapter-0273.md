# Chapter 273

*Four Lines, a Wetted Line Cut Away, and an Afternoon at the Face of the Second Seam*

Saturday the seventeenth of April, at the face of the second seam, and in the shed on the mole.

**The wall did not come down on the said Saturday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on the said Saturday and four hundred and eighty pence went out of it at about half past four in the afternoon.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

They went up to the face of the second seam on the top of the flood, which is a little after half past eight, and the weather was that flat sort of nothing that comes with a half moon and does nothing to anybody.

The mark was set on the twelfth of March past and there are thirty-six days on it, and that is a figure a man can get himself.

It is a foot and three-quarters of green on the weather side, going up to about two inches on the crown where the water only touches at the top of a tide, and the shackle is under all of it and you cannot see the shackle. Which is the point of the thing and is why it was set.

**You cannot see it and you can find it.** Nessa Pike put a thumb in and turned the shackle up and there it was, and the eye of it was clean and the crown of the bow was clean, and there is a quarter-inch flat on that crown and this yard does not fit it, and there is a line round the eye of it and it is four-strand and untarred and tied with a sheet bend, and it is not this yard's line.

"**Thirty-six days,**" said the man of the second lay, who had come up for nothing, as he comes up for nothing. "**And a foot and three-quarters on the weather side and two inches on the crown. It was four inches on the seventeenth of March and a foot on the twenty-sixth and nobody has had a leg to it since the fourth of April and it has come about three inches in thirteen days, and that is a green and not a season's, because a season's on this coast at this temperature comes in about nine weeks and the ninth of May is the fourteenth of May and there is a line in your fourth column about it.**"

"And the shackle is still there," said Calder Marr.

"The shackle is still there because a man in a boat can find it with his thumb. That is all a shackle is for. It is not a name."

---

Four hundred and eighty pence went out of that yard's own book at about half past four and it is not entered against any day in a column and the reason is the reason that has been against that line since the sixteenth of March past.

It is a day of work at the face of the second seam at four hundred and eighty pence, being the figure this yard has always charged a hirer for a day on that face, and the said day was on this yard's own account and has been charged to itself, and there is nobody in the water, and there is no paper in that drawer to book it against, and the party is one and a half, and the half is a woman with four stitches in her right hand who writes with the other one.

**A day of work was done in that yard on the said Saturday and nobody was hired for it and nobody is named against it, and the four hundred and eighty pence went out of that yard's own book on the day the work was done and not on a day it was agreed, and this yard has charged itself for that day at the hirer rate and there is no hirer, and that is the whole of what that figure is and it is not a figure against any day.** And there is no figure against the half.

---

Then the four lines, at about half past five, with the light going, in the ship's left hand, out of the book.

They are four lines on a sheet made on Saturday the sixth of March past, and the four hands are four days down that coast, and the four people are of no office, and none of them has been asked anything by this yard since the day they signed and this yard is not going to.

"Read them out," said the woman. "**All four of them, and do not do it nicely.**"

Nessa Pike read them out. They are short. They are the whole of a page's head and they say, in four hands and four people's English:

> *One. Nobody comes for this.*
> *Two. Nobody copies this, and nobody is asked to say no to copying it, because a no is a thing that can be asked about.*
> *Three. It does not move, and it does not go anywhere, and it does not go with anybody, and nobody is to be told where it is by us.*
> *Four. If anybody comes, they are told there is a drawer and the drawer is a drawer, and they are not told what is in it, and they are not told it is not in it either.*

Nobody said anything for about a minute. The compressor was not running and the mole was very quiet.

"**There is not one address in those four lines,**" said Mara Venn. "**Not one. And there is not the word house in them either, and I have been looking at them for three minutes and I have read them about nine times and I could not tell you this morning which of the two of those was the reason.**"

"They are four lines about a page," said Calder Marr.

"They are four lines about a **record**," she said. "**Every one of them. Read them again and count the words. A record is a thing somebody keeps on purpose and a person may be asked who has read it, and that is the whole trade, and that is why the second line is written the way it is written — a no can be asked about, so a no is not written, so nobody has to say one.** That is a record, and nobody but a records man in three hundred years thinks that way."

"And a house is not a record," said Calder Marr. "A house is a thing a person lives in. A person may be asked about a house and there is nothing in the asking."

"**A house is the only kind of thing in this country you cannot refuse anybody anything about, and the whole of that form at the head of it says a person who keeps unsettled material in a house, and it does not say a record, and it does not say a drawer, and it does not say a page.**"

"And the thing it is asking after is in a drawer in this case and in a house in that case," said Nessa Pike, "and the yard has been reading those four lines since the sixth of March past as a set of refusals."

"**They are a set of refusals and they are about a record, and this yard has spent forty-two days treating them as if they were about a house,**" said Mara Venn, "**and they are the best four lines anybody has ever written about anything and they will not do a thing about that form, because a form cannot be served on a record. A record has no door. A record has no street. You cannot write a record's address down because it does not have one and the only way to give it one is to write down where the drawer is, and if you write down where the drawer is then you have converted a record into a house and a house is a thing anybody can be served at.**"

---

And Calder Marr said the thing out loud then, and it is the whole of that Saturday and it is entered in the first column in his words.

"**Then we did it.** We did it on the third of April past, and we did it in a good cause, and we did it to a man who cannot read. A letter is a channel and a channel is a door and we said that out loud and we were pleased with ourselves. And a page in a drawer in a house is custody that an office can reach, and the only reason it is not custody the office can reach this afternoon is that a form in this country has never been in a post office."

"**Those four lines were right, and they were right about a record, and we have been leaning on them as if they were about a house for six weeks, and a house is the one thing on this coast that a form can be posted to.**"

Nobody improved on it.

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the seventeenth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man and a woman of this yard went up to the face of the second seam on the top of the flood on the said morning at a little after half past eight, and that thirty-six days are on that mark, being the twelfth of March past to the said day, and that the mark is fouled about a foot and three-quarters on the weather side and about two inches on the crown, and that the shackle was found with a thumb and is there, and that the eye of it is clean and the crown of it is clean and there is a flat of about a quarter of an inch across that crown which is a make this yard does not fit, and that there is a four-strand untarred line upon it tied with a sheet bend and the said line is not this yard's line; and that the said figures were read out loud at the mark and not improved on.*

*And that four hundred and eighty pence went out of that yard's own book on the said afternoon at about half past four, being a day at the face of the second seam on the hirers' own account with nobody in the water, and that the said day is not entered against a column, and that the reason against the line of that is that there is no paper in that drawer to book it against and the party is one and a half.*

*And that the four lines made on the sixth day of March past by four people of no office four days down that coast were read out in full at about half past five in the afternoon of the said day in that shed, all four of them, and were not improved on; and that a woman of no office of this harbor said out loud in that shed that there is not one address in those four lines and that every one of them is about a record and not about a house, and that the said four lines are a set of refusals and are not a set of refusals to being asked, and that the said sentence was entered without addition.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand refused to copy the said four lines into the fourth column of the said leaf, and that the reason against the line of the copying is that copying four lines written by four other people into that yard's own book makes that yard a second hand on a page it does not keep, and that the six of March past is the date and the five pages in five drawers in five towns in five hands is what would be added to, and that this yard is not adding a sixth page to anything; and that the said refusal is entered here and not in the first column because refusing is not a thing that was done.*

*And that a page in a drawer in a house is custody that an office can reach, and that the reason against the line of that is that the four lines of the sixth of March are about a record and are not about a house, and that this yard has been reading them as if they were about a house since the day they were written, and that the reason this yard is entered as having done that is that on the third day of April past this yard put a letter into the ordinary post in a town two days down that coast and said out loud in its own book that a letter is a channel and a channel is a door.*

*And that the said woman of no office said that a form cannot be served on a record and the reason is that a record has no address, and that a record is given an address by writing down where a drawer is, and that the reason against the line of that is that no office and no person in this harbor has ever done that and that is not a protection and is a fortnight of luck.*

---

**Four hundred and eighty pence went out of this yard on the said Saturday and nothing came into it, and twenty-one marks, seven shillings and sixpence is in the bag, which is three thousand four hundred and fifty pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is sixty-two marks, twelve shillings and threepence in nineteen transactions at seven counters, of which one is four hundred and eighty pence for a day at the face of the second seam on the hirers' own account with nobody in the water.**
