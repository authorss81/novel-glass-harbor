# Chapter 278

*The Fortieth Charge, a Question in a Fish Market, and a Woman of Fifty-Eight With a Drawer Under Her Bed*

Thursday the twenty-second of April, in the shed on the mole, and in the fish market on that quay.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fortieth of the charges entered walked on none of since the third of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday, and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

Nobody from that yard went to the market. That is entered and it is the reason the morning of the said day is short.

The woman of no office went on her own account, at about ten, on the strength of nothing, because she has been out of work since the fourth of January past and a market is the only place in this harbor where a person is not doing anybody anything by being in it.

And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, the tenth line:

*What it is not: — that a market is not a room and is not a counter and is not an office, and that forty people in a market is not forty witnesses and is not anybody's hearing, and that a certificate of method is not a thing that can be said out loud in a market and taken back into a room afterwards.*

---

The market was at its best by about half past ten. The boards went up on the second of April and have not come down since, and the third lay is a berth short of two and has been since the autumn, and a herring boat came in on the Tuesday with her hold two-thirds and unloaded it by about eleven and went off again.

The woman of about fifty-eight has the end stall in the second row and has sold fish off that quay for thirty-one years and has four boxes of the small sort and a knife and a pair of hands and a son in a city.

She did not know the woman of no office. She knew of her. That is a different thing in a market of that size and it made the morning worse and not better, and nobody in that market made it worse on purpose.

---

What she said was that she had a boy in a city, and that in the autumn he had brought a paper up and left it with her and gone, and that she had asked him what it was and he had said it was nothing, and that she had put it in the drawer under her bed with her will and her marriage lines, and that there was a seal on it, and that in February a man she sells to had asked her whether she had had any paper come from anybody.

"**No,**" she said. "**And he said he had heard there was a woman of no office in this harbor who has been in an office, and he said she would know what a paper was.**"

She said that part to about nine people and it got to about forty inside a minute, which is how a market works, and by the time the woman of no office came down that second row there were about forty people in it and every one of them turned round.

---

She did not ask whether anybody was coming. She asked the other thing.

"**Is a person allowed to burn a thing like that,**" she said. "**That is all. I have been thinking about it since February and I cannot ask my son because he is in a city and he would say nothing, and I cannot ask the man who asked me because he sells me, and you are the only one anybody has said would know.**"

And she answered in the middle of a fish market in about forty people's hearing, and she answered in the terms of a method, and the terms of a method are not anything else.

"**No,**" she said. "**You may not burn it. And that is not because a man in an office says so, and there is no man in an office who has ever said so to you and nobody is going to. It is because a person who burns a paper with unsettled material on it has handled it, and a person who has handled it has kept it, and a person who has kept it is the thing the paper is about. So the burning is the keeping. It is not a second door out of that house, it is the same door, and the only way to be on the outside of it is to be somewhere else, and there is nowhere else, because a house is a house and a drawer is a drawer and you have both of them and you are in one.**"

"And the man who asked me," said the woman of about fifty-eight.

"**Nobody is coming. Nobody knows you have it, and nobody is going to come, and the reason nobody is going to come is that nobody has written your name on anything, and the moment somebody writes your house on a piece of paper you are a keeper of a thing you have never seen, in a place where there is nothing to appeal to.** And I am going to say the next part out loud in this market because there is no room in this country where I am allowed to say it and because there is nobody who can take anything off me for it, and I have been of no office since the fourth of January past and that is the last of it I am going to be able to say anywhere."

She said it with her hands wet, because she had been doing that.

"**A keeper is not a person. A keeper is a function. And a function begins in a house and it does not end in one, and the whole of that form is the sentence that puts a function in a house and then calls the house a person.** Nine years I was in that office. Two of them I had the pen on that sheet. And I am not going to say any more of it than that, and I have said it in a fish market, and I have said it in the words of a method, and there is no glass in this market and no bench and no oath, and that is what I have spent."

---

**What it cost, in full, and it is a figure whether there is one for it or not:**

A certificate of method in the reading of worked glass and its faults, held by a woman of thirty of no office, issued in the ninth year of her time in that office and kept by her since, on which the standing is this and no more: **that a person may repeat what she has said under that method in a room where a thing is on a table, and every person in that room may act on it, because a method is behind it and a method can be checked by anybody who holds the same method.**

A method's words said in a market, about a thing that is not glass, in about forty people's hearing, with no bench and no counter and no oath, are a story told by a woman who used to be certified. That is all. **The certificate is not withdrawn, and no office exists in this country to withdraw it from her, because she is of no office and the office that keeps the copy is a building with a door on it and no person behind the counter, and the sheet she was issued is in a drawer in a town four days up this coast in somebody else's keeping and she has not asked for it and is not going to.**

The standing of it in that market is gone from that afternoon. It is not gone anywhere else, and it may be good again in a room, and she will not find out, because nobody in this country is going to sit in a room about a piece of glass and ask a woman of no office to swear to a method again.

And what she said was true. That is entered and it is not improved on, and nobody in that market was able to tell whether it was true or not and that is the whole of the price.

---

Nobody in that yard was in the market and nobody from that yard sent anybody, and that is in the second column, and the reason against the line of that is that a yard which puts a woman of no office in a market is a yard which has made her an instrument and she is not one and she has said so twice.

And Calder Marr, in that shed, at about half past eleven, with nothing to do and the compressor cold, said one thing out loud to an empty bench and it is entered because he said it in a shed with the door open and about four people were in the street.

"**That is a woman in a market telling a woman with a drawer that she is a keeper, and neither of them is a keeper, and the whole of what happened this week is that the word has left the sheet and gone into a market and it is in about forty mouths in a town where this yard has never had a single question put to a single person.**"

"Nessa," said the man of the second lay, from the ring of the compressor's flange, which he is not supposed to be on and which everybody lets him be on. "**You are going to want to write that down. Do not write down what the market said. Write down that you did not go.**"

"That," said Nessa Pike, "is the first sensible thing anybody has said in that shed this week."

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-second day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern was charged at no figure at about ten on the said morning and that party walked none of it, being the fortieth of the charges entered walked on none of since the third day of August past; and that the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it.*

*And that no person of that yard was in the fish market on the said day, and that no person of that yard sent anybody, and that a woman of no office of that harbor was in the said market at about half past ten in the morning of the said day on her own account, and that the said fact is in the first column and the reason against the line of it is in the second column.*

*And that a woman of about fifty-eight, who has sold fish off that quay for thirty-one years and has the end stall in the second row of that market, said out loud in about nine people's hearing and then in about forty, that a man who buys fish from her had told her in February that he had heard there was a woman of no office in that harbor who had been in an office, and that she had a paper in a drawer under her bed with her will and her marriage lines in it and that her son had brought it in the autumn and said it was nothing and gone to a city; and that the said woman asked the said woman of no office in about forty people's hearing whether a person was allowed to burn a thing like that, and that the said question was asked in the second row of that market and not in a room and is entered as a market.*

*And that the said woman of no office answered that question out loud in about forty people's hearing, and that what she said is entered in the second column in her words and is not improved on, and that it was said in the words of a method and that no glass and no bench and no oath were in that market.*

*And that one line was entered in the fourth column of the said leaf, being the tenth line in that column, being the line of the twenty-second day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that no person of that yard was in that market on the said day and nobody was sent, and that the reason against the line of that is that a yard which puts a woman of no office into a market has made her an instrument, and that she is not one and has said so twice.*

*And that a woman of about fifty-eight of that harbor has a sheet of paper in a drawer under her bed with her will and her marriage lines in it, that she has had it since the autumn, and that she does not know what it is, and that a man who buys fish from her asked her in February whether she had had any paper from anybody and told her of a woman of no office who would know what a paper was, and that the said sheet has a seal on it and that no office and no person has ever said one word to her about it and that nothing has been served on her and that she is afraid.*

*And that a certificate of method in the reading of worked glass and its faults, held by a woman of thirty of no office, was issued in the ninth year of her time in that office, and that the standing of such a certificate is that a person may repeat what she has said under that method in a room where a thing is on a table and every person in that room may act on it, and that the standing of the said certificate in that market was spent on the said day in about forty people's hearing by the use of a method's words about a thing that is not glass in a place where there is no method and no bench and no oath, and that the certificate is not withdrawn and cannot be withdrawn by any office in this country because she is of no office and the office that keeps the copy is a building with a door on it and no person behind the counter, and that the reason against the line of that is that a copy of a certificate in a drawer in a town four days up that coast is a record and is not a room and there is no person in this coast who could go and get it and this yard is not going to write to that town.*

*And that what the woman of no office said in that market was true, and that nobody in that market was able to tell whether it was true or not, and that both of those are entered here and the second of them is the price.*

*And that the price of the volume's own answer is now a woman of about fifty-eight in a town where this yard has never put a question to anybody, and that the said sentence was said out loud in that shed at about half past eleven on the said morning with the door open and about four people in the street, and that a man of about fifty from the second lay said out loud in answer to it that they were not going to write down what the market said and were going to write down that they did not go.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and nineteen marks, four shillings and ninepence is in the bag, which is three thousand and ninety-seven pence, and that is the same figure it was on Wednesday night. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and there is nothing else. The whole of what went out of that yard since the sixth of December past is sixty-five marks, one shilling and eightpence in twenty-one transactions at seven counters. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-seven weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them.**
