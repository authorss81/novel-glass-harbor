# Chapter 268

*The Forty-Ninth Wipe, a Bearing Taken With a Compass, and a Floor a Mile and a Half Off the South End*

Monday the twelfth of April, in the shed on the mole, on the crown of that mole, and at the top of that mole with a compass.

**At eight in the morning the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was the forty-ninth Monday since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Monday morning by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the thirty-seventh of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil. And nothing came into that yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about two and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Monday, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing.**

---

**A join cannot be read with a compass. That is the first thing about the day and it is the thing that made the day possible.**

He has not been able to read one since the seventeenth of September past, and everybody in that shed knows it in the way they know a man cannot get up a ladder, and nobody has ever said it out loud, and it is in the second column of that book with a reason against the line and no name in it, and nobody in that shed is going to put a name in it.

A compass is a different animal altogether. A compass is a needle and a card and a bit of looking, and a compass is not a join, and a man who cannot hold a slate can hold a compass, and holding the second one is the first thing he has been able to do in eleven days and it took him four minutes and it is in the first column as a thing that was done.

They went out on the crown of the mole at about half past ten with a compass, a hand bearing card, a line, and Nessa Pike with the slate that he could not hold, which she wrote in because her left hand is slower and steadier than his right and always has been.

They took the bearing of the crown of that mole off the outer end, and then they walked in along the south side to the head, and then they walked back out, and they took it four times, because a bearing is a cheap thing to take four of and an expensive thing to be wrong about.

Then they went in the boat to a mile and a half off the south end, which is round the corner from the bar off the outer marks and on the seaward side of it, and had been on all three days, and they lay a boat's length along the course of the stones on the floor, because the courses run in a straight line and a straight line has a bearing, and the pot had been on it and the stones were under about three feet of water in a swell.

"**North-west by west, and it does not move,**" said the man of the second lay, who has a compass of his own and a good one and who came out for nothing but the bearing. "About two hundred and seventy. And I have been working inside that line for nineteen years and I have never once been told about it and I am telling you now that I have never once been told about it, and that is not the same as not knowing it was there."

---

**And then, at about one o'clock, Calder Marr stood on the crown of that mole and put a finger on the chart and it was his own finger and it did not go to any part of the chart, because he did not have a chart of that ground and the Crown chart of the western approaches is in a bag in a boat out of Ashtide belonging to a man of about thirty-one who was answered at the head of a dock on the twenty-eighth of March and has not been asked anything since and is not going to be asked anything by this yard this season or any other.**

The finger went to the *plan of Morrow Quay's mole* on the sheet that is rolled behind the tide table, which is not a Crown document and is not anybody's document and is a sheet of paper that has been in that shed since before the first of the four of April.

It is a plan of the mole. It has the mole on it in a heavy line and the crown of it and the outer end of it and the north wall and the second seam and the first and the third, and it has a scale in feet at the bottom in a hand that stopped being the harbour's business about eighty years ago, and it has no soundings on it and no depths and nothing whatever about the ground off the mole.

**The bearing of the crown of that mole, taken at the outer end, is north-west by west, about two hundred and seventy.**

Nessa Pike wrote the two figures side by side on the slate and did not say anything for about ten seconds, which for her is a very long time.

"**It is the same line,**" she said at last. "It is the same line and it is a mile and a half out and it is forty fathoms down, and the crown of this mole is a line on this sheet, and the two of them are one line."

"That is what a compass says," said Calder Marr. "**And I am going to be very careful now, because this is exactly the moment in this yard's own book where a man says something that sounds like more than he has, and I have done it four times and been wrong four times.** So here is what it is and here is what it is not. It is not a discovery. It is two figures of a bearing, and one of them is off a sheet of paper in this shed and one of them is off a needle in a man's hand, and they are the same, and a man in four years can take that sheet out of this shed and lay a compass on it."

"And what is it then," said the man of the second lay.

"**It is a floor that somebody laid,**" said Calder Marr, "a mile and a half off the south end of this quay, at the bottom of a cut, in courses, with a figure cut at the end of every course so that a man setting stone could tell whether he had laid a whole course or part of one, and it is a line, and this mole is a line, and they are the same line. **Which means this mole has got a foot. And the foot of it is not rubble on a slope. It is a floor that a man laid, and a man cut numbers in it, and it is under forty fathoms of water, and there is not one person in this harbor who has ever known that, and I have walked on that mole every working day of my life and not once knew it had a foot.**"

---

They did not do anything else that day. That is in the first column and it is the most important line in it.

They did not go and look at anything. They did not go and find a man. They did not write to a town. They did not put a figure on a wall, and there is no wall in that harbor with a figure on it for anything, and there is not going to be.

The reason, which is in the second column, was given by Calder Marr at about half past four in the afternoon and is in these words:

"**We have found a thing and we have not got one penny and we are not going to sell it, because I stood on a mole in this harbor in March and asked eleven people a question and got a refusal, and a refusal is better than a sheet, and what I have in my hand now is worth less than the refusal, and if I put it on a wall then in about nine other harbors by midsummer there will be a story about a made floor forty fathoms down under this quay, and by Christmas somebody will be selling a chart of it out of a town, and a chart of a made floor is the second worst thing that can happen to a made floor.**"

"Who is the first worst," said Nessa Pike.

"**A man going down and being wrong about it,**" he said.

---

And the arithmetic of the deep water went into the book at about five o'clock on the Monday, and it is four lines and it is all of the rest of the day.

"That floor is at forty fathoms, being two hundred and forty feet, and the needle went to eighty-four on a face of a hundred and fifty, and eighty-four is not near the end of that face." Garrow Fisk said it, because it was his gauge and his trade and he would not have it said by anybody else. "**The place that question is about is at seventy fathoms, being four hundred and twenty feet. That is a hundred and eighty feet under where that floor is, and every part of that business is multiplied: the air, the housing, the pipe, the line, the pot, the man, the hours, the man to bring you up, the man to stand on the bottom, and a man who has to stand on the bottom for two hours at four hundred and twenty feet is a different animal from a man who stood on one for two hours at two hundred and forty.** And the third part is the third part and it is not a number, and the third part is: that set is a pressure house's set, and it is on a book, and it goes where the book sends it. There is no book in this country that sends it four hundred miles west. **And there is no yard on this coast that rents one. Not one. I have been in this trade nineteen years and there is not one, and I know that for the same reason every man in my trade knows it, which is that there has never once been a man on this coast with a reason deep enough and a book good enough to be allowed to ask.**"

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twelfth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at eight in the morning the wall behind that harbor authority's reeve's bench came down bare, being the forty-ninth Monday since the first of May past, and that the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern was charged at no figure at about ten on the said Monday and that party walked none of it, being the thirty-seventh of the charges entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that a bearing was taken four times on the said Monday off the outer end of the crown of that mole and four times along the south side of it and four times off the head of it, by the holder of that writ, with a compass and a hand bearing card, and that the figure is north-west by west, about two hundred and seventy, and that it did not move, and that the same figure was taken in a boat a mile and a half off the south end along the course of the stones on that floor, and that the said figure is the same figure; and that the said ground is on the seaward side of the bar of four hundred yards off the outer marks and not inside it, being off the south end and round the corner from the north-west line the outer marks are on, and that the said bar was not narrowed by anything and this party did not ask to have it narrowed.*

*And that the said figure was set against a plan of that mole which is a sheet rolled behind the tide table in that shed and is not a Crown document and is not anybody's document, and that the bearing of the crown of that mole on that sheet is north-west by west, about two hundred and seventy, and that the two figures are the same, and that a person in four years can take that sheet out of that shed and lay a compass on it.*

*And that it was entered at about one o'clock in the afternoon of the said Monday that a floor of cut stone in courses lies a mile and a half off the south end of that quay at forty fathoms, and that the courses run on a straight line of about north-west by west for a hundred and forty feet, and that the crown of that mole runs on the same line, and that the said entry was read out loud twice and was not improved on.*

*And that no work of any kind was done on that ground after the said bearing, and that nobody was sent for, and that no figure was put on a wall, and that no letter was written, and that the reason against the line of all four of those is that a refusal is better than a sheet.*

*And that the said arithmetic was entered at about five o'clock in the afternoon, being that the floor is at forty fathoms being two hundred and forty feet, and that the gauge read eighty-four pounds on a face of a hundred and fifty and eighty-four is not near the end of that face, and that the place in question is at seventy fathoms being four hundred and twenty feet, being a hundred and eighty feet under where that floor is, and that the air, the housing, the pipe, the line, the pot, the man, the hours, the man to bring him up and the man to stand on the bottom are all multiplied, and that the third part of it is not a number and is that the set is a pressure house's set on a book and goes where the book sends it, and that there is no book in that country which sends it four hundred miles west.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ could not hold a slate on the said Monday and could hold a compass, and that the reason against the line of that is the reason that has been against that line on every day since the seventeenth of September past, and it has no name in it and nobody in that shed is going to put one in it, and a compass is a needle and a card and a bit of looking and is not a join and the two are not the same instrument.*

*And that the man of the second lay said that he has worked inside that line for nineteen years and has never once been told about it, and that not knowing a thing was there and never being told about it are two different facts and that he is entered as having said the second and not the first, and that the reason against the line is that a man's not knowing is not a thing a man on a mole can check and a man's never having been told is.*

*And that the man of about thirty-four said out loud at about half past four that a chart of a made floor is the second worst thing that can happen to a made floor and that a man going down and being wrong about it is the first, and that neither of those is an answer to anything and that both of them are in this column because the reason a man gives for not doing a thing is the only part of it that cannot be checked later.*

*And that nobody was sent for, and that four people this yard has heard of on that coast go into deep water without a book, and that this yard is not going to ask any of them, and that the reason against that line is the reason given on the second day of April last and not improved on, and the reason is about the trade and not about a family: a man asked on a mole whether he cuts answers it with a price, and a price answered to a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house is a thing this yard would then have to pay, and it has not got, and it is not going to ask a man of sixty-one what he will take. And a man of sixty-one was at a far bench on the said Monday from ten until two and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and the not-asking was not this week and is not of this week and was entered in the second column of the second day of April last.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and twenty-four marks, seven shillings and sevenpence is in the bag, which is three thousand nine hundred and thirty-one pence, and that is the same figure it was on Saturday night and the same figure it will be on Tuesday night. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, being the eleven marks of the sixth of April, and the whole of what went out of that yard since the sixth of December past is fifty-nine marks, twelve shillings and twopence in seventeen transactions at six counters.**
