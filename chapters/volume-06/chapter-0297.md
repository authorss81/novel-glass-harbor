# Chapter 297

*Tuesday the Eleventh of May, the Twenty-Ninth Week With No Envelope, a Round of Glass Carried Down to a Boat and Not Put Back, and a Sheet of Glass at Seventy Fathoms Going Down Into a Rebate and Stopping There*

Tuesday the eleventh of May, off the south end of that mole, and in the shed on that mole in the evening.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and twenty-nine weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, the twenty-ninth of them being this day. Nothing came into that yard on it and one mark and eightpence went out of it.**

**The wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it. And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Tuesday, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The crate was under the tarpaulin on the crown of that mole from the seventh and it came off at about ten in the morning of the eleventh, and it went down the mole steps in a handcart with a man of about fifty-five and a boy of about nineteen who are not of that yard and who were not asked and were paid nothing and were told nothing.

**And the round of glass came out of the crate about two feet from the top of the mole steps at about eleven o'clock, and it stood on the stone of the steps, in the open air, in front of about nine people who were going to the hard and none of whom stopped.**

It is a round of glass about the size of a saucer in a collar of metal with a wheel in the collar and a screw through the collar, and there is a chain of eleven links on it with a date on every link, and there is a piece of worked glass the size of a hand bedded in it, and the whole of it weighs about nine pounds and a man of sixty-one with two stitches in his right hand carried it down three steps and then gave it to the holder of that writ and did not say anything about it.

The boat was at the foot of the steps at half past eleven. The six conditions had been read at the boat at about a quarter past eleven in order and the bar had been named in the same words it has been named since the seventh of April, and the day was a Tuesday and the tide was making.

---

"**Say it,**" said Nessa Pike, from the boat. "**Say the whole of it and say it now, and then it is a record and not a story, and I have written it once already and I am not going to write it twice.**"

Calder Marr was standing in about four inches of water on the mole steps with the round of glass in both hands.

"**On the Wednesday before last I said at that bench at four o'clock, before anybody had agreed to a day, that if there was a chamber and if it was open enough to be read, this yard would not read it and would not take one piece of it out, and I named the price of that out loud in the same breath, and the price was that the only proof this yard will ever have of anything about a drowned city will be in a thing that closed.**"

"**And on the Friday,**" said Fisk.

"**On the Friday I took that round out of a bed in a sill and the plate was standing in the bed with nothing behind it, and I named that at that bench at eight in the morning before anybody had agreed to any part of it, and I said the plate goes into the rebate on the eleventh and that either the round comes up or the plate grinds it into about nine pieces.**" He turned the round over once in his hands. "**So here it is, and here we are, and the day is today, and I have got it in my hands.**"

Mara Venn came down the steps behind him and stood on the stone above the water.

"**Then you are going to put it back,**" she said.

"**No.**"

"**Say why and do not make it smaller than it is.**"

"**Because putting it back holds the plate up in the bed, and holding the plate up in the bed opens that room, and we would go in there and we would read it, and we would have a proof. And the proof would be in a thing that closes, and it closes on the eleventh, and a proof in a thing that closes is a proof nobody can check, and this yard has said in six movements out loud that a record a man cannot check is worth less than a record a man can look at.**" He did not raise his voice and he was not looking at her. "**And if we put it back we will have nine communities' answers in a basket at seventy fathoms and no man in this country who could go and look at one of them, and in about four years a man in a city will write that a yard of two and a half made that claim, and he will be right to write it.**"

"**And the Crown's engineers built a thing that holds a room open,**" said Mara Venn, "**and it is in that frame, and it is in our hands, and we are not going to use it.**"

"**It is a term and not a lock,**" said Tobias Wren from the boat. "**Whoever cut it wanted a thing held and wanted it held for so long and no longer. I am not going to tell either of you what I think about that and I have not been asked and I am not going to be.**"

"**Then put it down,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and say the sentence, because I am going to write one line about this and I want it to be yours.**"

He put it down on the stone of the mole steps and stood with his hand flat on it for a moment and then took his hand off.

"**The chamber closes whether it is read or not, and we are letting it close.**"

"**And that is not a surrender,**" said Mara Venn, "**and it is not a proof, and nobody in this boat is going to stand here and say we were right about it. We do not know that we were right about it. We know what it costs and we know what it is worth and they are not the same figure and the second one is zero.**"

"**Write that,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Both halves. And write that it was decided on the Wednesday and carried out on the Tuesday and not in between, and that nobody in between was asked, because that is the only way anybody is ever going to be able to check it.**"

Nobody wrote it on the day. It went in at about half past nine that night in the ship's own left hand, in that shed, out loud, with all three of them standing at the bench while it went in.

---

They went out at about half past eleven and the pot was on the top course in the empty socket by about twenty past twelve and it stayed on the bottom for two hours and came up at about a quarter past two, and the pot was not landed on anything at all. It was put on the top course and lifted straight off again and came up.

Fisk read it off the slate and showed it round the boat and the slate said: **THE PLATE IS DOWN IN THE REBATE AND IT IS NOT COMING UP AND THE REBATE IS DEEPER THAN THE BED BY THE THICKNESS OF THE PLATE AND THE PLATE IS IN IT AND NOTHING IS HOLDING IT UP.**

"**That is the whole of the eleventh of May,**" he said, "**and it took about four minutes and nobody in that boat saw the beginning of it, and a man in a boat on Friday could see the end of it. That is what a figure cut in a sill is for.**"

"**Write both of those,**" said Calder Marr. "**Write that nobody saw the beginning of it. That is the whole of what has happened in about three hundred years on the coast of this country and the whole of what happened in five minutes in a room at seventy fathoms and they are the same sentence.**"

---

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-second line:*

*What it is not: — that a room that has been closed is not a room that has been proved and is not a room that has been lost; that letting a thing close is not a surrender and is not a proof and is not the yard being right; that a machine that holds a room open for a term is not a lock and is not a keeper and is not a promise; and that a decision taken on a Wednesday and carried out on a Tuesday and not in between is not a decision taken late.*

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the eleventh day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday and a wage of four marks was not paid on it and no envelope was made and the column did not move and the envelopes did not move, and that twenty-nine weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them; and that one mark and eightpence went out of that yard's own book at about ten in the morning of the said day for a boat and a man in her, and that nothing came into that yard on the said day.*

*And that a round of glass about the size of a saucer in a metal collar with a wheel in the collar and a screw through the collar, with a chain of eleven stamped links on it and a piece of worked glass bedded in it, was taken out of a crate on the crown of that mole at about ten in the morning of the said day and was carried down the mole steps and came off the cart about two feet from the top of the steps at about eleven o'clock in the morning in front of about nine people, and was not put into any shed at any hour of the said day; and that it was put down on the stone of the mole steps at about half past eleven in the morning of the said day and was not put back into the bed in the said sill, and that the said sill is at about seventy fathoms off the south end of that mole, and that the said round has not been in the said bed at any hour that anybody knows of.*

*And that the holder of that writ and a person of no office of this harbor said out loud on the stone of the mole steps, with the ship's hand asking for it from the boat, between about eleven and about half past eleven in the morning of the said day, that they were letting the chamber close, and that the said decision was taken on the fifth day of May at about four in the afternoon before anybody had agreed to the day, and was carried out on the said day, and that nobody in between was asked; and that nothing was written on the said day, and that the whole of it went in at about half past nine that night in that shed with all three of them standing at the bench, and that the whole of the price of it was in the first column of the fifth of May.*

*And that the pot was put on the top course of that mole's own floor at about twenty past twelve and was lifted straight off and came up at about a quarter past two, and that from the said boat at about seventy fathoms it was read that a plate of glass is down in the rebate in the said sill and is not coming up, and that the said rebate is deeper than the said bed by the thickness of the said plate, and that nothing is holding the said plate up.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the decision to let the said chamber close was not taken on the said day and was not taken on the Saturday or the Sunday or the Monday, and that it was taken in advance on the Wednesday preceding, and that the whole of its price was named out loud before the day and not after it.*

*And that nobody on that mole said out loud that the yard was right about it, and that the reason against the line of the saying is that the yard does not know that it was right about it and has a rule about what it does not know.*

*And that the concealment was not told on the said day and was told once and once only on the day preceding in a room with two people in it, and that it goes in no column of that book, and that the reason against the line of the entering of it is the reason of the first day of March past about a record a man cannot check.*

---

**One mark and eightpence went out of this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing came in, and there are four hundred and twenty-nine pence in the bag, which is two marks, nine shillings and one penny, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and eleven pence, being one mark, four shillings and threepence. Twenty-nine weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, and the wage falls due again on Tuesday the eighteenth of May for the thirtieth week and the column will not move on it. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-four days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundredth night, and a round of glass is lying on the stone of the mole steps in the open air under a tarpaulin that came off a cart, and about nine people walked past it all afternoon and none of them stopped, and there is not one piece of glass in this harbor that is lighter than it was on the fourth of May.**
