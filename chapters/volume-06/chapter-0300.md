# Chapter 300

*Friday the Fourteenth of May, a Leaf With Four Columns Read Back, Thirteen Things That Are the Answer, and a Working Harbor Poorer on the Last Page Than on the First*

Friday the fourteenth of May, in the shed on the mole, and on the mole steps in the afternoon.

**The wall did not come down on the said Friday, the cistern was not charged on it, and it was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and nobody else.

**The first column of that leaf has thirty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were twenty-three of them on the fourth of May and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Wednesday the fifth to Thursday the thirteenth, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own makes thirty-three. The nine are a day's work paid by a private person and a price named before anybody agreed to it, a working day at seventy fathoms, a round of glass out of a bed, a pane of plate glass on a mole, a glazier and a bag that came back short of a wage, a thing said twice in a room, a plate going down into a rebate, a lead and a slate, and two marks four hundred yards apart.

**The second column has a hundred and twelve lines in it at the hour of the read-back.** Eighty-eight of them were in it on the fourth of May and twenty-four went in between the fifth and the thirteenth, and three this afternoon.

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and the four lines above his named line are four, nameless and not struck out, and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered, and no name of his is in any column of that book.**

**And the fourth column has twenty-four lines in it at the read-back and twenty-five at the close**, the twenty-fifth going in this afternoon, and it is the last thing in that book.

---

And then the ship's hand read the thirteen, in the voice she reads a column in, and nobody stopped her and nobody improved on any of them afterwards, and they are the whole of what this yard has.

**One.** That a copy of a copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city, bought for a penny on the twenty-seventh of April, says in nine lines that the fleet was ordered down, names nobody, gives no reason and has no tenth line; and that the copy is in a drawer in this shed and it is a house.

**Two.** That the order is of the fleet and not of a master of it, that the masters are not to be asked which of them made it, and that the six marks and the two at the foot of it do not agree; and that the ninth is nine short lines the sheet itself says are not to be counted with the nine, and this yard refused out loud to count it with the nine-line mark in its own book; and that nothing has ever said what either is for.

**Three.** That the thing the offices of this country say they are guarding against was real, happened to real people in real water, and was ordered by more than one of them together and argued about afterwards with the argument left on the paper; that knowing it makes nobody safe; that the opacity was expensive for a reason and a reason is not a warrant; and that this yard does not get to say that office was right.

**Four.** That on the old evidence chain, being the eleven links on a round of glass off a bed in a sill at about seventy fathoms off the south end of that mole, there is a seal on the last link, thicker than the other ten and carrying a date, and the seal line on its face reads *Sealed and entered at the Morrow circuit office by order of Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*; and that a date struck on the collar is later than the strike on that link, the collar being the work of the office named on that seal line, and that the difference between the two is a difference in two made things and says nothing whatever about which is right, or why, or by whom. **And that no such person is in a room, on a mole, in a market or in this harbor, was sent for, or was asked anything; that the name on that line is a line and is not a man; and that it is in that yard's own book in the ship's own left hand and is not going on a wall in that harbor.**

**Five.** That a chamber exists at about seventy fathoms in the ground of this harbor, round the corner from the bar; that no person and no boat and no lead of this yard was inside that bar on any of the ten days; and that the bar has not been narrowed by anything.

**Six.** That a Crown Seal is a machine and not a person and not a mark on a paper, that it holds a thing open for a stated term by a screw that goes in about two turns and stops, and that whoever cut it wanted it held for so long and no longer and cut the figure on the sill for the next man; that this yard has proved the Crown's engineers built it for a real reason; and that that is the whole of the moral difficulty.

**Seven.** That the chamber was not entered, was not read, and had nothing taken out of it, and that this yard does not know and will not know what was in it; and that the price of that was named out loud on a Wednesday at four o'clock before anybody had agreed to anything, being that the only proof this yard will ever have of anything about a drowned city is in a thing that closed, and a proof in a thing that closed is one nobody can check.

**Eight.** That a forced consensus of incompatible testimonies is a physical hazard and is not a metaphor, and that a room in which nine communities' votes are held together is a room and not an archive; and that the man of twenty-eight carried up about eleven feet off this mole on the ninth of April is in a bed at Ashtide until the fifth of June, and is not a symbol.

**Nine.** That the proof this yard has is physical and labor-based and about half of it is unreadable, and that the unreadable half is behind a plate of glass that went down into a rebate at a quarter past two on the eleventh and is not coming up; and that a man in a boat on the twelfth read it off a slate with a lead and fourteen pounds, so that anybody may go and read it, and nobody is to be asked what is behind it.

**Ten.** That the holder of that writ has lost a portion of his own recall, permanently, that there is no figure for it, and that it goes in the second column with the twelfth of May against it and a reason against the line and no name in it; and that a thing relearned off a sheet in ninety seconds is not a thing remembered, and that the record outlives the man, and that this yard has been saying that for six movements without once being told it was a rule about records.

**Eleven.** That a glass storm went through this harbor on the seventh of May from about four in the afternoon until about ten at night, and that it is in that yard's second column in one hand and in another man's book, four streets off, on a page that is not on top of the first; that no cause is named for it in this book or in any other; and that about a hundred people were out in the rain, nobody was hurt, and this yard is not going to ask any of them.

**Twelve.** That a man of sixty-one is hurt in a right hand of about forty, that the bill was a mark and four shillings, that the absence is until the second of June, and that a boat of about nineteen tons he built himself is in eleven pieces on the crown of that mole; that he was not thanked and said he was not going to be; and that this yard has a rule about a man it has paid and none about a man it has hurt.

**Thirteen.** That a working harbor of that coast is poorer on the fourteenth day of May than it was on the twenty-third day of February: one herring berth of two out, the north berth out, the mole-head light not lit because a light costs oil and its lens out since the seventh, the fire party's hose dead until the spring, the tide gang four men short, the hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November, a harbor authority's season ended on the thirty-first of March with nothing issued, a man of twenty-eight in a bed at Ashtide until the fifth of June, a man of fifty-one with about a half of a season's gear gone and six more fathoms of it pinned under a lead at forty fathoms, nineteen winters in a list being made up in a city, a form on a step two days down that coast, a paper with a seal on it under a bed in this harbor, a boat in eleven pieces, a front out of glass, and two hundred and sixty-one pence in a drawer on a mole.

---

And then the round of glass, the last thing done that day, in about four minutes, and nobody made a speech about it.

"**I go up that coast on Monday the seventeenth,**" said Mara Venn, "**on the deck of a boat going up for coal, and I am going for a sheet of mine in a drawer in a town four days up that coast in somebody's keeping that I have not seen since the fourth of January. And I am going to carry a round of glass up there, in a drawer in my hand, and on the eighteenth of May it will be a thing in a house that this yard cannot reach and is not going to try to reach.**"

"**Why you.**"

"**Because you cannot keep it and I will not ask you to, and because a thing this yard has read and then put in a drawer in a shed is a house, and I said that out loud on your own steps in nine people's hearing and am not going to unsay it to be comfortable.**" Her hand was on the crate. "**And because nobody in this country has one and I am the only person I know who has ever held one, and that is not a reason to keep it. It is a reason to put it somewhere it is not a weapon in a shed.**"

"**It is not going into your forty marks,**" said the holder of that writ.

"**It is not going into anything,**" said Mara Venn. "**My forty marks stand first after food and they are not reduced and they are not paid, and nothing that has happened in ten days has changed that, and a man who has put a round of glass in a drawer in my hand has not bought a shilling of it.**"

Nobody thanked anybody, it went into the crate, the tarpaulin went over it, and it did not go into that shed and is not going into that shed.

---

*First column, entered on Friday the fourteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a person of no office; and that the said leaf holds thirty-three entries in its first column, being twenty-three on the fourth of May, nine from the fifth to the thirteenth and one this evening; a hundred and fifteen lines in its second, being eighty-eight, twenty-four and three; none in its third; and twenty-five in its fourth, being eighteen, six and one.*

*And that the thirteen things read out of that leaf at about four in the afternoon of the said day were entered in it without addition and that no figure was altered in the entering of any of them, and that the fourth and the thirteenth of them are the two the yard is held to; and that a round of glass with a chain of eleven stamped links on it was put into a crate on the mole steps at about half past four in the afternoon in the keeping of a person of no office of this harbor, who goes up that coast on the seventeenth, and that the crate did not go into that shed and is not going into any, and that nothing was given to her for it and her forty marks first after food were not reduced and were not paid.*

*And in the fourth column of the said leaf, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that thirteen things said out loud in a shed on a Friday afternoon are not a resolution and are not the end of a question; that a round of glass in a drawer in a town four days up that coast is not a record this yard can check; and that a hand going down with a slate and a lead and no name on it is not a signature and is not a witness.*

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a round of glass in a house four days up that coast on the eighteenth of May is not a record, a return or a finding; that the reason against the line of this yard's keeping it is that a drawer in a shed is a house and a yard is a house, and this yard said so out loud on the twenty-fourth of April and on the twenty-seventh put a penny on a counter; and that the reason against the not-keeping is that the yard has a rule about what it can check and this is not such a thing.*

*And that the question asked out loud on the mole steps on the third of May past, being who was the first one to use a mark on a paper to keep a thing in an emergency instead of holding it, has not been answered by anybody in eleven days and is not going to be answered in this yard, goes in no column of that book and on no wall in that harbor, and carries no figure; and that the concealment owed to this yard was told once and once only on the tenth of May in a room with two people and nobody else, and neither called it a reunion; and that the four people of that coast were not asked anything on any of the ten days, the complaint was not raised, their limit stands, and this yard having read the sill of a chamber of that kind at seventy fathoms in its own ground is not a reason to ask them and was not used as one.*

*And that no name of the boy of twenty-two is in any column of that book and none has ever been, and that the third column has not been filled on any of the ten days and nobody appointed to it; that the chandler's note is at thirty-one, not re-written, its three counts of weeks at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and was not said out loud a fifth time; that the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing measured; that the notice of the thirty-first of October stands with its four clauses and no tenth acknowledgement; that four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days; that the plate in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, its note open; that the case of five bells is not raised; the lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second of July are not touched; and that five pages are in five drawers in five towns, not looked in.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and sixty-one pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fivepence. The figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and seventy-nine pence, being two marks, four shillings and eleven pence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. What came in in the ten days ended on the said Friday is six hundred and forty-nine pence on a private person's account, and what went out is one thousand two hundred and four pence, being two hundred and eight to a bonesetter, six hundred and sixty to a glazier, and a mark and eightpence twice for a boat; and not one penny of the fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence a day of plant and air on the sixth and the seventh went out of this yard at all. What has gone out since the sixth of December past is thirteen thousand nine hundred and five pence, being eighty-six marks, twelve shillings and one penny, in twenty-seven transactions at ten counters. The wage column did not move on the one wage Tuesday in these ten days, twenty-nine weeks have fallen due with no envelope, and the next falls due on Tuesday the eighteenth for the thirtieth week. The wall came down bare once, the fifty-third, on Monday the tenth, the counter not restarting, the clerk keeping his own page, and no sitting was held in that room on any day of this yard's life. The cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them, the forty-fourth, the forty-fifth and the forty-sixth. The far bench was occupied on three of the ten days, the seventh, the eleventh and the thirteenth, and was not approached on any of them.**

**And here is the whole of what this yard is at the end of it. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and thirty-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and third night. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, the chest not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand, the third column unfilled and unappointed. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, and nobody has asked whose either is. Six pieces of paper in that drawer and the charter's place empty, and one of the six is a copy of a drowned city, and that is a house. A form on a step two days down that coast, a paper with a seal on it under a bed in this harbor, and a drawer in a shed on a mole: none of the three is a keeper and all three are, and none has a handle on the inside. Nothing in that shed was signed, nobody in that yard was thanked, nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and nobody in that country has been told one word of it by anybody who is not in it.**

**And on the fourteenth of May next there is a hand going down seventy fathoms with a slate and a lead on the line and no name on the slate, because nobody signs anything in this yard; a sheet of glass that has closed and cannot be opened at about seventy fathoms off the south end of that mole, and the round that came out of the bed beside it and went up that coast in a drawer in a town four days up it on the eighteenth of May in a hand that is not this yard's; a man of about sixty-one at a far bench, asked for nothing, asking nothing, with one shoe on the bench beside him that is not his and that nobody put there; two marks on the bottom four hundred yards apart, one fouled a foot and a half and one of a make nobody in that harbor makes; and the boy not on the stone, and a day lost to a thing that has no name in it, and a name said out loud once in ten days in a boat that is neither the boy's nor the holder's.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and nobody in that country has been told one word of it by anybody who is not in it.**

**And none of it is an ending, and none of it is a victory, and the truth about the oldest thing on that coast turned out to be in the wrong place and it was found by people who were not looking for it, and the answer is a decision that nine communities made together and argued about afterwards, and no office in that country has ever had to answer for it, and a harbor is poorer.**
