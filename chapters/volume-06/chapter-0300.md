# Chapter 300

*Friday the Fourteenth of May, a Leaf With Four Columns Read Back, Thirteen Things That Are the Answer, and a Working Harbor Poorer on the Last Page Than on the First*

Friday the fourteenth of May, in the shed on the mole, and on the mole steps in the afternoon.

**The wall did not come down on the said Friday and the cistern was not charged on it, and the said Friday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and nobody else and nobody was sent for.

**The first column of that leaf has thirty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were twenty-three of them on the fourth of May and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Wednesday the fifth to Thursday the thirteenth, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own entry makes thirty-three. The nine are a day's work paid by a private person and a price named before anybody agreed to it, a working day at seventy fathoms, a round of glass out of a bed, a pane of plate glass on a mole, a glazier and a bag that came back short of a wage, a thing said twice in a room, a sheet of glass going down into a rebate, a lead and a slate, and two marks four hundred yards apart.

**The second column has a hundred and twelve lines in it at the hour of the read-back.** Eighty-eight of them were in it on the fourth of May and twenty-four went in between the fifth and the thirteenth, and four this afternoon.

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name set beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder of that writ's, and the four lines above his named line are four and are nameless and are not struck out and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and came down on none of them and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered, and no name of his is in any column of that book and none has ever been.**

**And the fourth column has twenty-four lines in it at the read-back and twenty-five at the close**, and the twenty-fifth went in this afternoon and is the last thing in that book.

---

And then the ship's hand read the thirteen, and she read them the way she reads a column, and nobody stopped her and nobody improved on any of them afterwards, and they are the whole of what this yard has after ten days at seventy fathoms.

**One.** That a copy of a copy of a municipal sheet of a chamber of a drowned city, bought for a penny on the twenty-seventh of April, says in nine lines that the fleet was ordered down, and that there is no tenth line, and that the nine lines name nobody and give no reason whatever, and that the copy is in a drawer in this shed and it is a house.

**Two.** That the order is of the fleet and not of a master of it, and that the masters are not to be asked which of them made it, and that the six marks and the two at the foot of it do not agree, and that the ninth is nine short lines the sheet itself says are not to be counted with the nine, and that this yard refused out loud to count that ninth with the nine-line mark already in its own book, and that nothing in that yard's life has said what either of them is for.

**Three.** That the thing the offices of this country say they are guarding against was real and happened to real people in real water and was ordered by more than one of them together and argued about afterwards with the argument left on the paper, and that knowing it does not make anybody safe, and that the opacity was expensive for a reason, and a reason is not a warrant, and this yard does not get to be the one that says that office was right.

**Four.** That on the old evidence chain, being the eleven links on a round of glass off a bed in a sill at about seventy fathoms off the south end of that mole, there is a seal on the last link, which is thicker than the other ten and is stamped with a date, and the seal line on its face reads *Sealed and entered at the Morrow circuit office by order of Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*; and that a date struck on the collar of the said round is later than the strike on that link, and the collar is the work of the same office named on that seal line, and the difference between the two is a difference in two made things and says nothing whatever about which of them is right, or why, or by whom. **And that no such person is in a room, on a mole, in a market or in this harbor, was sent for, or was asked anything, and that the name on that line is a line and is not a man and nobody in that yard has ever spoken it.**

**Five.** That a chamber exists at about seventy fathoms in the ground of this harbor, round the corner from the bar, and that no person and no boat and no lead of this yard was inside that bar on any of the ten days, and that the bar has not been narrowed by anything.

**Six.** That a Crown Seal is a machine and not a person and not a mark on a paper, and that it holds a thing open for a stated term by a screw that goes in about two turns and stops, and that whoever cut it wanted the thing held for so long and no longer, and cut the figure on the sill where the next man would read it, and that this yard has proved the Crown's engineers built it for a reason that was a real reason, and that that is the whole of the moral difficulty.

**Seven.** That the chamber was not entered, was not read, and had nothing taken out of it, and that its contents are not known and will not be known by this yard, and that the price of that was named out loud on a Wednesday at four o'clock before anybody had agreed to anything, being that the only proof this yard will ever have of anything about a drowned city is in a thing that closed, and a proof in a thing that closed is a proof nobody can check, and that such a proof is worth less than a record a man can look at.

**Eight.** That a forced consensus of incompatible testimonies is a physical hazard and is not a metaphor, and that a room in which nine communities' votes are held together is a room and not an archive, and that the man of twenty-eight who was carried up about eleven feet and put into the edge of a course of squared stone off this mole on the ninth of April past is in a bed at Ashtide until the fifth of June and is not a symbol.

**Nine.** That the proof this yard has is physical and labor-based and about half of it is unreadable, and that the unreadable half is behind a plate of glass that went down into a rebate at about a quarter past two on the eleventh of May and is not coming up, and that a man in a boat on the twelfth read it off a slate with a lead and fourteen pounds and wrote it in the first column, and that anybody may go and read it and nobody is to be asked what is behind it.

**Ten.** That the holder of that writ has lost a portion of his own recall, permanently, and that there is no figure for it, and that it goes in the second column of that leaf with the twelfth day of May against it and a reason against the line and no name in it; and that the nine lines of the order are in a drawer in that shed and were read out to him in about ninety seconds on the said day, and that a thing relearned off a sheet in ninety seconds is not a thing remembered, and that the record outlives the man, and that this yard has been saying that for six movements and has not once been told it was a rule about records.

**Eleven.** That a glass storm went through this harbor on the seventh of May from about four in the afternoon until about ten at night, and that it is in that yard's second column in one hand and in another man's book, four streets off, on a page that is not on top of the first, and that no cause is named for it in this book or in any other and is not going to be, and that about a hundred people were out in the rain, nobody was hurt, and this yard is not going to ask any of them what they thought of it.

**Twelve.** That a man of sixty-one is hurt in a right hand of about forty, and the bill was a mark and four shillings, and the absence is until the second of June, and a boat of about nineteen tons that he built himself is in eleven pieces on the crown of that mole, and that he was not thanked and said he was not going to be, and that this yard has a rule about a man it has paid and has not got a rule about a man it has hurt.

**Thirteen.** That a working harbor of that coast is poorer on the fourteenth day of May than it was on the twenty-third day of February: one herring berth of two out, the north berth out, the mole-head light not lit because a light costs oil and its lens out since the seventh, the fire party's hose dead until the spring, the tide gang four men short, the hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November, a harbor authority's season ended on the thirty-first of March with nothing issued, a man of twenty-eight in a bed at Ashtide until the fifth of June, a man of fifty-one with about a half of a season's gear gone and six more fathoms of it pinned under a lead at forty fathoms, nineteen winters in a list being made up in a city, a form on a step two days down that coast, a paper with a seal on it under a bed in this harbor, a copy of a drowned city in a drawer in this shed and a round of glass out of a bed in a sill at seventy fathoms, a boat in eleven pieces, a front out of glass, and a yard with two hundred and sixty-one pence in a drawer on a mole.

---

And then the round of glass, and it was the last thing done that day and it took about four minutes and nobody made a speech about it.

"**I go up that coast on Monday the seventeenth,**" said Mara Venn, "**on the deck of a boat going up for coal, and I am going for a sheet of mine that is in a drawer in a town four days up that coast in somebody's keeping and that I have not seen since the fourth of January. And I am going to carry a round of glass up there, and it is going in a drawer in my hand, and on the eighteenth of May it will be a thing in a house that this yard cannot reach and is not going to try to reach.**"

"**Why you.**"

"**Because you cannot keep it and I will not ask you to, and because a thing this yard has read and then put in a drawer in a shed is a house, and I have said that out loud on your own steps in about nine people's hearing and I am not going to unsay it to be comfortable.**" She had her hand on the crate. "**And because nobody in this country has one and I am the only person I know who has ever held one, and that is not a reason to keep it. It is a reason to put it somewhere it is not a weapon in a shed.**"

"**It is not going into your forty marks,**" said the holder of that writ.

"**It is not going into anything,**" said Mara Venn. "**My forty marks stand first after food and they are not reduced and they are not paid, and nothing that has happened in ten days has changed that, and a man who has put a round of glass in a drawer in my hand has not bought a shilling of it and is not going to.**"

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said it was a good thing and it was put in the crate and the tarpaulin went over it, and it did not go into that shed and is not going into that shed.

---

*First column, entered on Friday the fourteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said Friday by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a person of no office of this harbor; and that the said leaf holds thirty-three entries in its first column, being twenty-three on the fourth day of May, nine from the fifth to the thirteenth and one this evening, and a hundred and sixteen lines in its second, being eighty-eight on the fourth, twenty-four from the fifth to the thirteenth and four this afternoon, and none in its third, and twenty-five in its fourth, being eighteen on the fourth of May and six from the fifth to the thirteenth and one this afternoon.*

*And that the thirteen things read out of that leaf at about four in the afternoon of the said day were entered in it without addition and that no figure was altered in the entering of any of them, and that the fourth of the said thirteen and the thirteenth of the said thirteen are the two the yard is held to; and that a round of glass in a metal collar with a chain of eleven stamped links on it was put into a crate on the mole steps of that mole at about half past four in the afternoon of the said day in the keeping of a person of no office of this harbor, who goes up that coast on the seventeenth day of May, and that the said crate did not go into that shed and is not going into any shed, and that the said person's forty marks first after food were not reduced and were not paid and nothing was given to her and nothing was asked of her.*

*And in the fourth column of the said leaf, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that thirteen things said out loud in a shed on a Friday afternoon are not a resolution and are not the end of a question; that a round of glass in a drawer in a town four days up that coast is not a record this yard can check and is not going to be one; and that a hand going down with a slate and a lead and no name on the slate is not a signature and is not a witness and is not going to be either.*

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a round of glass in a house four days up that coast on the eighteenth of May is not a record and is not a return and is not a finding, and that the reason against the line of this yard's keeping it is that a drawer in a shed is a house and a yard is a house and this yard said so out loud on the twenty-fourth day of April past and on the twenty-seventh it put a penny on a counter; and that the reason against the line of the not-keeping is that the yard has a rule about what it can check and this is not a thing it can check.*

*And that the question asked out loud on the mole steps on the third day of May past, being who was the first one to use a mark on a paper to keep a thing in an emergency instead of holding it, has not been answered by anybody in eleven days and is not going to be answered by anybody in this yard, and it goes in no column of that book and on no wall in that harbor and carries no figure; and that the concealment owed to this yard was told once and once only on the tenth day of May in a room with two people in it and nobody else, and neither of the two of them said that it was a reunion; and that the four people of that coast were not asked about anything on any of the ten days, the complaint was not raised, the limit four of them wrote and signed stands, and the fact that this yard has now read the sill of a chamber of that kind at seventy fathoms in its own ground is not a reason to ask them and was not used as one.*

*And that no name of the boy of twenty-two is in any column of that book and none has ever been, and that the third column has not been filled on any of the ten days and nobody has been appointed to it; and that the chandler's note is at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and it was not said out loud a fifth time; that the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing was measured; that the notice of the thirty-first of October stands with its four clauses and no tenth acknowledgement was entered; that four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days; that the plate in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open; that the case of five bells is not raised; the lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second of July are not touched; and that five pages are in five drawers in five towns and were not looked in and were not written to.*

*And that a working harbor of that coast is poorer on the said Friday than it was on the twenty-third day of February, and that the four returns are four and there is no fifth, and the five drawers are five drawers, and the plate in eleven feet is in eleven feet with its note open, and the forty marks stand twice, and the wage column stands, and a harbor is poorer on the last page of that yard's book than it was on the first.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and sixty-one pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fivepence. The figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and seventy-nine pence, being two marks, four shillings and eleven pence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it that says anybody is going to pay it. The whole of what came into that yard in the ten days ended on the said Friday is six hundred and forty-nine pence on a private person's account, and the whole of what went out of it is one thousand two hundred and four pence, being two hundred and eight to a bonesetter, six hundred and sixty to a glazier, and a mark and eightpence twice for a boat and a man in her; and not one penny of the fourteen marks, three shillings and fourpence a day of plant and air on the sixth and the seventh went out of this yard at all. The whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is thirteen thousand nine hundred and five pence, being eighty-six marks, twelve shillings and one penny, in twenty-seven transactions at ten counters. The wage column did not move on the one wage Tuesday in these ten days and twenty-nine weeks have fallen due with no envelope, and the next falls due on Tuesday the eighteenth for the thirtieth week and the column will not move on it. The slate wall came down bare once, the fifty-third, on Monday the tenth, the counter not restarting, the clerk keeping his own page, and no sitting was held in that room on any day of this yard's life. The cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them, being the forty-fourth, the forty-fifth and the forty-sixth. The far bench was occupied on three of the ten days, being the seventh, the eleventh and the thirteenth, and was not approached on any of them.**

**And here is the whole of what this yard is at the end of it. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and thirty-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and third night. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand, the third column unfilled and unappointed with the boy's hand at the head of it and a name set beside it that is not his. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, nobody has asked whose either is. Six pieces of paper in that drawer and the charter's place empty, and one of the six is a copy of a drowned city, and that is a house. A form on a step two days down that coast, a paper with a seal on it under a bed in this harbor, and a drawer in a shed on a mole: none of the three is a keeper and all three are, and there is no handle on the inside of any of them. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and nobody in that country has been told one word of it by anybody who is not in it.**

**And on the fourteenth day of May next there is a hand going down in a place seventy fathoms down with a slate and a lead on the line and no name on the slate, because nobody signs anything in this yard; a sheet of glass that has closed and cannot be opened at about seventy fathoms off the south end of that mole, and a round cut out of it lying in a drawer in a town four days up that coast in about four days' time in a hand that is not this yard's; a man of about sixty-one at a far bench, asked for nothing, asking nothing, with one shoe on the bench beside him that is not his and that nobody put there and that nobody is going to ask about; two marks on the bottom four hundred yards apart, one of them fouled a foot and a half and one of them of a make nobody in that harbor makes; and the boy not on the stone, and a day lost to a thing that has no name in it, and a name said out loud once in ten days in a boat that is neither the boy's nor the holder's.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and nobody in that country has been told one word of it by anybody who is not in it.**

**And none of it is an ending, and none of it is a victory, and the truth about the oldest thing on that coast turned out to be in the wrong place and it was found by people who were not looking for it, and the answer is a decision that nine communities made together and argued about afterwards, and no office in that country has ever had to answer for it, and a harbor is poorer.**
