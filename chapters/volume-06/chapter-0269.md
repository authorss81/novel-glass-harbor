# Chapter 269

*Twenty-Five Weeks on a Tuesday, a Man Who Is Not Coming Back, and the Sentence the Whole Thing Was For*

Tuesday the thirteenth of April, in the shed on the mole, and at the head of that mole.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and the wage was not paid, and the twenty-fifth week fell due with no envelope standing against it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks where it has stood since the fifth of March past and did not move a line, and the twenty envelopes are on the bench.**

**And the wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, and no figure is entered against a day this party did not see, and nothing came into that yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay was not asked anything in these ten days and asked nothing, and the third lay is a berth short of two and has been since the autumn.**

---

Elias Voke came up at about ten and sat on the ring of the compressor's flange, which he was not supposed to do and which everyone let him do, and he had a piece of paper in his hand and did not offer it.

"**Two hundred and eight fathoms,**" he said. "That is what came up. It was four hundred and eleven and I have had the figure off the head rope's markings and off the shot line's coil and I have had it two ways and it is two hundred and eight. And the eleven floats and the head rope entire, and the belly is down there, and the belly was the money. **So I have lost about a half of a season's gear and I have got back about two fifths of it, and the ground is shut, and the herring come off that ground at the end of next month.**"

"You knew that on the Thursday," said Calder Marr.

"I knew it on the Thursday, and I knew it on the Friday, and I knew it at ten o'clock this morning, and I have come up here to say it out loud to the two of you so that it is said out loud, because I have found that a thing I have thought about on my own for four days is about a third smaller than it was, and that is the only trade I have got left and I am not going to let it get away from me too."

"And the eleven marks," said Nessa Pike. "It is paid and it is entered and it is three weeks ago."

"Eleven marks is paid and I am not going to hear a word about it," said Voke. "**I will not pay you the difference. I want that said in the shed and not in a book, because a book is a thing you can get out. There is no version of the next six weeks in which I have that money, and if I had it I would not give it to you, because you are not the only people in this harbor with a boat and a bad season, and I have been on nineteen winters of a list and I have never once been the man who was carried, and I have not got the whole of a man to carry anybody.**"

"That is fair," said Calder Marr, "and it is in the second column with the date on it, and not in the first, because you have not given us anything on that account and we have not asked you for it."

"Do not thank me," said Elias Voke. "I have not thanked you and you have not thanked me and I would like that to be a fixed arrangement between us, and I will be down at the south end on Thursday with somebody else's ground, because a man does not stay shut."

---

And then the argument, which was in that shed from about eleven until half past two, and which is the argument this volume is for, and which nobody in that harbor heard except the two of them and the man who was not going to be thanked.

It was about the depth, and it was not about the fleet.

"**Say the thing properly,**" said Nessa Pike. "You have had it in your mouth since the Monday and you have not said it. I have watched you walk round it four times today. Say it and let it be in the room."

"There is no fleet on that floor," said Calder Marr.

"No."

"There is a floor. There is a foot under this mole at forty fathoms, laid in courses, numbered at the end of every course, and the numbering is a working mark and not a name, and there is a chain in it that was never cut and a bar in a socket, and there is nothing else on it and there is not going to be anything else on it, because a fleet is not four hundred and fifty years old and a fleet did not lay it."

"No," said Nessa Pike. "So it is not about the fleet, and you are not going to make it about the fleet, and I am not going to let you stand in that shed and make a made floor forty fathoms down under a mole mean a thing it does not mean, because on the twenty-eighth of March a man of thirty-one put a Crown chart on a fish quay and asked a plain question and got three numbers and a refusal, and it is on the second column of this book why we did not write it down, and the reason was that a thing written down in this yard's book is a thing this yard said about itself, and **in four years a man in a city reads it and thinks a yard knows something about a fleet.** And if we stand here in April and start handing that man a floor, then in four years he will say a yard told him the ground was made, and he will have been told that, and he will not know that what it was told was that a man had a magnetised needle and a sheet of paper behind the tide table."

"**Then what is the floor for,**" said Calder Marr.

**And this is where he said it, and he said it badly, and it took him two attempts, and it is in the first column in his words with both attempts left standing, because the second one is not better than the first one and both of them are true and a man in four years is entitled to see that it was not a prepared sentence.**

"The first time he said it: *the floor is a floor, and it is a floor at forty fathoms, and the ground the fleet is on is at seventy fathoms, and if the ground at seventy fathoms is a made thing then it is a made thing, and a made thing has a beginning and an end and an order it was done in, and the order a made thing was done in is in the thing, cut into it, and a man can go and look at it.*"

"The second time he said it: *we have found the method. That is all we have found. Somebody laid stone at forty fathoms off this quay and cut a number at the end of every course so the man setting it would know whether he had laid a whole course or part of one, and that is the method, and the method is four hundred miles west at seventy fathoms, and the method is readable by anybody with a pot and a reason to be in the water.*"

"And then he said the third thing, which is the whole of it, and he said it to the floor and not to her.

"**And this party is the wrong party for it.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about a minute. The compressor was not running and the mole was very quiet and the market was not up.

"Say the rest of that," said Nessa Pike.

"There is no Crown house book on this coast that sends a set four hundred miles west, and there is no yard that rents one, and there are four people on that coast who go into deep water without a book, and they are four days down it, and **we are not going to ask them, and the reason is in the second column of the second day of April and it is about a price and it has not been improved on and it is not going to be.**"

"**Then the volume's question is answerable,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and this party is the wrong party for it, and those are the two halves of one thing and they are the first true thing anybody has said in this shed since the fourth of March.** Write it down in that order and not the other way round, because the other way round it sounds like a man who has been beaten and it is not, and a man who has been beaten does not go and pay a Crown house rate for three days to find out that he is the wrong man."

"Write it in the second column," said Calder Marr. "Not the first. A man on a mole cannot check any of it."

"**No,**" said Nessa Pike, "**in the first. The two figures in it are figures. Two hundred and forty feet and four hundred and twenty feet, and one hundred and eighty feet between them, and a bearing of two hundred and seventy taken twice off two different instruments. A man on a mole can go and check the two feet figures in a chart and can take a bearing on the crown of this mole and get the same line I got. Put it in the first column, and put the rest of it in the second, and let a man have some of it.**"

---

And in the fourth column, that afternoon, in the ship's left hand, the third line:

*What it is not: — that this yard is not the party that can answer the question, and that the reason is not the law and is not a man, and that the reason is that there is one set on this coast that will take a pot to four hundred and twenty feet and it stands on a Crown house book, and that a man with no instrument is not a party to a Crown house book, and that neither of those two sentences is an answer and that the answer is four hundred miles west and is not in this harbor.*

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the thirteenth day of April next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of fifty-one of the second lay came up that mole on the said Tuesday at about ten in the morning and said that two hundred and eight fathoms of net came up out of four hundred and eleven and that the eleven cork floats and the head rope came up entire, and that the belly of that net is on the floor of the said cut, and that the ground is shut, and that the herring come off that ground at the end of the next month.*

*And that the said man said out loud in that shed that he will not pay the difference, and that there is no version of the next six weeks in which he has that money, and that if he had it he would not give it, because there are other people in that harbor with a boat and a bad season and he has been on nineteen winters of a list and has never once been the man who was carried; and that the holder of that writ said that that is fair and that it goes in the second column and not the first, because nothing has been given on that account and nothing has been asked for.*

*And that the said man said that he does not want to be thanked and has not been thanked and would like that to be a fixed arrangement, and that he will be down at the south end on the Thursday on somebody else's ground.*

*And that the twenty-fifth week fell due on the said Tuesday with no envelope standing against it, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that there are twenty envelopes on that bench.*

*And that the holder of that writ said in that shed at about half past eleven in the morning, and said it twice and left both attempts standing, that the floor at forty fathoms is not about the fleet, and that the finding is the method, being that somebody laid stone at forty fathoms off that quay and cut a figure at the end of every course so that the man setting it would know whether he had laid a whole course or part of one, and that the same method is at seventy fathoms about four hundred miles west, and that the order a made thing was done in is in the made thing and can be read by anybody with a pot and a reason to be in the water; and that he then said that this party is the wrong party for it.*

*And that the ship's hand said that a figure is a thing a man can check and that the two depths and the one bearing were entered in the first column and the rest of it in the second, and that a man on a mole can take a bearing on the crown of that mole and get the same figure she got.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of fifty-one saying out loud in a shed that he will not pay a difference and why is not a thing that can be checked, and that the reason given is in the first column because a man said it with his name on it in a shed with the door open, and that the not-asking of him for it is entered here and not in the first.*

*And that the said party is the wrong party for the question, and that the reason is that there is no book on that coast that sends a set four hundred miles west and there is no yard that rents one, and that the four people on that coast who go into deep water without a book were not asked, and that the reason against that line is the reason of the second day of April last about a price, and that it is not improved on and is not going to be.*

*And that a man's not being the party for a thing is not a defeat and is not a discovery, and that both of those words have been used in this book before about other things and that this one is neither of them, and that there is no word in this shed for what it is, and that the not having a word is entered here so that a later chapter does not invent one.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and twenty-four marks, seven shillings and sevenpence is in the bag, which is three thousand nine hundred and thirty-one pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and seventy-six pence, being the eleven marks of the said man of the second lay on the sixth of April, and there is nothing else. The whole of what went out of that yard since the sixth of December past is sixty marks and tenpence in seventeen transactions at six counters. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty-five weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and the next wage Tuesday is the twentieth, and the column will not move on that either.**
