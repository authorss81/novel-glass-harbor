# Chapter 289

*The Fifty-Second Time That Wall Came Down, the Forty-Third Charge Walked on None, and a Question Asked at a Mole Head and Not Answered*

Monday the third of May, the mole steps, and the shed on that mole.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-second time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-third of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third of August past.**

**And the said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, and nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it. And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Monday.**

---

They had the argument out on the mole steps at about half past ten in the morning with about nine people within hearing, and it was not a market and the market wall is four streets off and was not touched on the said day and has not been touched on any day of this book by this party.

The argument was about whether to say any of it out loud to about forty people, and it finished in about four minutes because Nessa Pike had it written out before Calder Marr got to the end of the first sentence.

"**No,**" she said, "**and the reason is standing about thirty feet from where we are standing and she is the reason.**"

"That was a method's words about a thing that is not glass."

"**It was a true thing said in a market in forty people's hearing by a woman who used to hold a certificate of method, and it cost her the standing of a certificate of method in a market, and it cannot be given back, because the office that keeps the copy is a building with a door on it and no person behind the counter, and the sheet she was issued is in a drawer in a town four days up this coast that this yard is not going to write to. That price has been paid once this month. I am not having it paid twice because the sheet is better.**"

"And if it is better," he said, "**then somebody is going to be told that it is better.**"

"**Then somebody is going to be told it by a person who can be asked about it, at a time of their choosing, in a room of their choosing, and this yard is not that person and is not going to arrange for it to be that person.**"

"That is the third refusal in four days."

"**It is the same refusal and it is not improved on and it is the best thing this yard owns, and the fourth of March past says this yard will not say the chandler's note a fifth time and this is that, in a different coat.**"

---

And then the thing that made the day came up the steps, and it came up them because she had thought about it for two days and had not found anywhere else to put it.

"**I want to ask a question and I want to ask it where nine people can hear it and not forty, and I am asking you, Calder, and I am not asking the yard.**"

"Go on."

"**Somebody, a long time before any of the nine of you were in a boat, made a mark on a piece of paper for a reason. And the reason was that in an emergency a thing had to be held by somebody that evening, and the mark said so, and everybody agreed, and it was the right thing to do and everybody in the room knew it was the right thing to do. And then the morning came.**" She was not looking at either of them. "**And the mark was still on it. And the emergency was over. And nobody ever took the mark off. And the mark says what the paper may be used for, and the paper is in a drawer, and the drawer is in a house, and the house has a door on it, and there is no office in this country that a house can appeal to and there is not one for a drawer either.**"

The water went on against the mole steps for a while.

"**So my question is not about a person,**" she said. "**There is no person in it and I want it written down that there is no person in it. My question is about a practice. It is a question anybody could have asked at any time in a hundred and fifty years. Who was the first one to use a mark like that to keep a thing instead of holding it?**"

---

Nobody answered her on the steps, and she did not ask twice, and about nine people were on a mole that Monday morning and every one of them heard it and not one of them can be asked what they thought of it.

"**I don't know,**" said Calder Marr.

"I know you don't."

"**And there is nobody in this country I could ask, and I am not going to write to a town to find out, and I am not going to ask the four people four days down that coast, and if you want the reason for that it is the reason of the second of April last and it is about a trade and a price and not about a family and it has not changed since the spring.**"

"**I know that,**" said Mara Venn. "**I asked it because I have had it in my head since the Thursday and because there is one more thing about it and I am going to say it once. I worked nine years in an office that had a form in it, and the form was not a seal and the form was not a man, and the form said a person who keeps unsettled material in a house is a keeper of it. And I have never once in nine years been shown where that sentence came from, and it is older than everybody in that building, and I have never once seen it written in a hand by somebody who was frightened of being asked.**"

A man went past them on the steps with a basket on his shoulder and did not stop, and about four of the nine went on down to the hard, and one of them stayed where he was for a while and then went too, and none of them said one word about it, and none of them will remember it, and that is not a thing to complain about.

She got up off the step.

"**That is not an answer. It is the shape of the hole and I am the only person I know who has stood in it.**"

---

And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh of March past, that evening, in the ship's left hand, the seventeenth line:

*What it is not: — that a question asked at a mole head in about nine people's hearing is not an answer and is not a finding and is not a return, and is not going in any column of that book, because a question a man in a city can read and repeat is a question that gets an office to ask it back; and that a copy of a copy of a municipal sheet is not a thing to be said out loud in about forty people, and that this yard has already watched one true thing cost a person the standing of a certificate in a market in this harbor in this month and is not going to pay that price a second time.*

---

*First column, entered on Monday the third day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about half past ten in the morning of the said day, on the steps of that mole and in about nine people's hearing, a person of no office of this harbor asked a question out loud and did not ask it twice, being who was the first to use a mark on a paper to keep a thing in an emergency instead of holding it; and that the said question was not answered by anybody on that mole on that morning, and that the holder of that writ said out loud that he did not know and that there is nobody in this country he could ask, and that the said question is not entered in any column of that yard's own book and no figure is entered against it.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on the said morning for the fifty-second time since the first of May past and the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page, and that the cistern was charged at no figure on the said morning and that party walked none of it, being the forty-third of the charges entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that on the said morning it was decided in that shed that no part of the copy of the copy of the said municipal sheet is to be said out loud in about forty people, and that the said decision was taken in the said shed before the question was asked on the said steps, and that the reason against the line of the saying is entered in the second column.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the whole of what the said sheet might be worth to about forty people in this harbor is not going to be said by this yard, and that the reason against the line of the saying is that a certificate of method in the reading of worked glass and its faults has already had the standing of it spent in that fish market in about forty people's hearing on the twenty-second day of April past, that the said certificate is not withdrawn and cannot be withdrawn because there is no office in this country to take it from her, and that a person of thirty of this harbor paid that on the said day and has not been asked for anything in this yard since.*

*And that who was the first to use a mark on a paper to keep a thing instead of holding it is not known to this yard and is not going to be found out by this yard, and that the reason against the line of the finding is the reason of the second day of April last about a trade and a price, and that no letter is to be written to any town on the coast and no man is to be sent to any town on the coast, and that the fact that this yard has bought a sheet that came off a drowned city for a penny is not a reason to ask anybody anything.*

*And that about sixty boats on this coast have a copy of that yard's printed sheet of thirty-eight lines and that the thirty-eighth of those lines is the only one this yard did not write for itself and it says where every other copy is, and that a woman in this harbor has said a thing out loud in a market that about forty people have heard and about nine people heard repeated on a mole on the said morning, and that the said two things are about two months from meeting in a place where neither of them is this yard, and that this yard is not going to be the one that moves them along and has entered that it is not going to.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and five marks, one shilling and fourpence is in the bag, which is eight hundred and sixteen pence. The whole of the money that has come into that yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand seven hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of it since the sixth of December past is seventy-nine marks, five shillings and one penny in twenty-three transactions at eight counters.**
