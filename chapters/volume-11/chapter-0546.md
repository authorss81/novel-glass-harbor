# Chapter 546

*Monday the Seventeenth Day of January, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand Because the Hose Is Dead Until the Spring and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, and the Figure of That Hundred Not Said Out Loud a Second Time This Winter, Three Days Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Two Thousand and Sixteen Pence, Being Twelve Marks and Eight Shillings, Going Onto the Sheet at About Ten in the Morning With the Seventeenth, the Eighteenth and the Nineteenth All on the Face of That One Line, Those Four Men Told Out Loud in About Fourteen People What the Three Days Are For and What They Are Not For and One of Them Asking What a Sill Is and Being Answered, a Man of About Forty-Seven Who Owns a Boat of About Twenty-Two Tons Saying Out Loud That He Took Three Men up That Coast at the Figure in Chalk and Wrote It in His Own Book and Is on No Sheet and Was Not Thanked, a Pump Hand of About Forty-Four Saying Out Loud That He Was in a Dinghy All Night and Is Not Going to Do It Again Until a Second Man Is Asked, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Seventy-Three Thousand One Hundred and Forty-Six Pence, Being Four Hundred and Fifty-Seven Marks, Two Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the seventeenth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shipyard on that quay with a roof on it that is nine days old and no sill under it.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred is not going to be said out loud a second time this winter. One line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings, with the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth of this month all on the face of it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at eighty-three thousand seven hundred and eighty-six pence out, being five hundred and twenty-three marks, eight shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is seventy-three thousand one hundred and forty-six pence short, being four hundred and fifty-seven marks, two shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fiftieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-six days. The hose that goes in at the top of that cistern has been dead since the spring and two men go in on their knees to the butt behind that market and neither of them is on that rota board and neither of them has ever been on it. Three marks to a man of that hard eleven miles up that coast came in on the entry and were ruled and not re-dated, and they have not been sent for and nobody has asked for them and they are not going to be asked for this winter. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office is neither reduced nor paid nor dated nor spoken of since the middle of September, and that person read five lines out loud in a room over that market on the Sunday and did not mention it and was not asked about it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-four days and he was at a fish stall at the end of that market for about two hours on the Sunday and said one word to a man he was working beside and has not said anything since.**

---

The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and seventeenth time at about eight in the morning, and the two men on it were there until about a quarter past nine, and neither of the two is on that rota board and neither of the two is paid out of the berth fund, and the name at the head of that board is still off it and is going to still be off it.

---

And at about ten in the morning, with the door of that shed open on the slip and about fourteen people in two hundred yards of it, the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and the four men of that yard stood in a row against the wall while he said it.

"Three days apiece in advance to the same four men. Two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence a man a day for three days at the figure in chalk. The seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth are all on the face of that line, being the days the work is to be done, and it goes into the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because this afternoon is the day I settle it, and **a man with a leaf and a pencil can take three days off that one line and find they are three consecutive days and not two of them, and that is the first time in ten days that a line of this sheet has carried three days.**"

"Those four men are on no sheet in any hand and they are not counted on it and their names are in the book the days are paid out of, and that has been the arrangement since the sixth of December and it is not going to change this month."

---

And then the person whose yard it is told those four men, out loud, in front of about fourteen people, what the three days are for and what they are not for, and she said it once, and one of the four men asked her a question and was answered.

She said it in about a hundred and fifty words and she did not say it twice.

"The roof went on in three days and that was the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, and this is a second three days and it is the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth. **This one is not a roof. This one is the north wall and the sills and the two doors and the glass, and there is not one sill under that roof and there has not been one since the eighth, and a man who is not told that is going to put his weight on a thing that is not there.** The figure for it is not on that sheet yet and it will be on it on Wednesday and it will not be the figure on it now, and any man in this yard who hears those two numbers and thinks they are the same number is welcome to come and look at the sheet on Wednesday at ten o'clock."

A man of about forty of that yard asked her what a sill was, in front of about fourteen people, and she answered him in about forty words and he did not ask a second question.

"A beam that goes along the bottom of a wall and takes the weight of what is above it. **You have not put one in and I have not got one in, and I have been telling the yard that since the ninth of this month.**"

Nobody thanked her for either of those two things and nobody thanked the four men for taking money in advance for three days of work they have not begun.

---

And a man of about forty-seven who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons stood at the end of that slip at about half past ten in the morning and said out loud, in front of about eleven people, that he took three men up that coast with him on Friday at the figure in chalk and paid them himself and wrote it in his own book, and that he is on no sheet and is not going to be, and he was not thanked for it.

"Twenty-eightth of December I was told to go and take three of the men who came up this road, and I did, and I paid them out of my own pocket and it is in my book on the page with my own fuel in it. **I am telling the room that I did it and not telling the room that it was a favour to anybody, and if that sheet ever wants a line for it, it can have one, and it will be mine and not that man's.**"

The man who keeps the leaf said that the sheet does not want a line for it and never will, in about thirty words.

"That sheet does not want a line for it and it never will. If it ever does it can ask me for one in a room with a door on it."

The man of about forty-seven said that was what he had wanted to hear and went off up the quay.

---

And a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors stood at the corner of that slip at about two in the afternoon and said out loud that he was in a dinghy all night on Friday and is not going to do it again until a second man is asked, and nobody argued with him about it and he was not thanked and he is a member of nothing.

He said it in about a hundred and twenty words and he said it to nobody in particular and about eleven people heard it.

"I stood off that ground for three hours and forty minutes with my own hand on a chain weight and I did not hear a thing out of the water that I could put a name to except one noise and that noise is a boat's chain on a stone and I know that noise from nineteen years of it. **I am not a good man to have on the end of a line and I will say that out loud now so that the next time somebody wants a man on the end of a line they have to ask for two, and the second one has to be asked for in the same breath and not after the fact.**"

The man who keeps the leaf said that was the correct order of things and did not say it twice, and did not set a figure against it, and the pump hand went off up the quay and has not been asked for a second time and has not asked for one.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and said that the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.

"Seventy-three thousand one hundred and forty-six pence short, being four hundred and fifty-seven marks, two shillings and twopence. It moved this morning by two thousand and sixteen pence, and that is three days apiece in advance to four men at the figure in chalk, and **that is the only kind of money this sheet has carried since the sixth of December that is not a bill for a thing somebody did, and it is the only kind that has to be paid before anybody has done anything, and both of those are the reason it is the line I would defend first if this yard were asked which line on the sheet is the one that matters.**"

"Eighty-three thousand seven hundred and eighty-six out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree. Four men have got money in their hands for three days they have not started, and a roof on that quay has no sill under it, and a man of about forty-four has said out loud in front of eleven people that he will not stand at the end of a line by himself again, and nobody thanked him for saying it, and neither of those two things is a cost and both of them are on the page."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Monday, and nobody was thanked for the three days in advance and nobody was thanked for the sill.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. THREE DAYS A PIECE IN ADVANCE TO THE SAME FOUR MEN WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT TWO THOUSAND AND SIXTEEN PENCE, BEING TWELVE MARKS AND EIGHT SHILLINGS, WITH THE SEVENTEENTH, THE EIGHTEENTH AND THE NINETEENTH ALL ON THE FACE OF THAT ONE LINE. THE PERSON WHOSE YARD IT IS TOLD THOSE FOUR MEN OUT LOUD THAT THE THREE DAYS ARE THE NORTH WALL AND THE SILLS AND THE TWO DOORS AND THE GLASS AND ARE NOT A ROOF, AND SAID THAT THE FIGURE FOR THEM IS NOT ON THAT SHEET YET AND WILL NOT BE THE FIGURE ON IT NOW. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SEVEN TOOK THREE MEN UP THAT COAST AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK AND WROTE IT IN HIS OWN BOOK AND IS ON NO SHEET. A PUMP HAND OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WILL NOT BE ASKED FOR ALONE AGAIN. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SEVENTY-THREE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SEVEN MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the seventeenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand because the hose is dead until the spring and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and that the figure of that hundred is not said out loud a second time this winter; and that three days apiece in advance to the same four men went onto that sheet at about ten in the morning at two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence a man a day for three days at the figure in chalk, with the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth of January all on the face of that one line and settled on the seventeenth; and that those four men are on no sheet in any hand and are not counted on it and their names are in the book the days are paid out of; and that the person whose yard it is told those four men out loud in front of about fourteen people what the three days are for and what they are not for, being that this one is the north wall and the sills and the two doors and the glass and not a roof, and that there is not one sill under that roof and there has not been one since the eighth of January, and that the figure for those three days is not on that sheet and will be on it on the nineteenth and will not be the figure on it on that morning, and that a man of about forty of that yard asked her what a sill was in front of about fourteen people and was answered in about forty words and did not ask a second question; and that a man of about forty-seven who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said out loud in front of about eleven people that he took three men up that coast on Friday at the figure in chalk, paid them out of his own pocket and wrote it in his own book on the page with his own fuel in it, and that he is on no sheet and is not going to be, and that the man who keeps the leaf said in about thirty words that that sheet does not want a line for it and never will, and that the man of about forty-seven said that was what he had wanted to hear; and that a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors said out loud in front of about eleven people that he stood off that ground for three hours and forty minutes with his own hand on a chain weight, that he is not a good man to have on the end of a line, and that the next time somebody wants a man on the end of a line two are to be asked for in the same breath and not after the fact, and that the man who keeps the leaf said that was the correct order of things and did not say it twice and set no figure against it, and that he has not been asked for a second time and has not asked for one; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at eighty-three thousand seven hundred and eighty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is seventy-three thousand one hundred and forty-six pence short, being four hundred and fifty-seven marks, two shillings and twopence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and seventy-nine, one thousand two hundred and sixty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and seventy-eight, one thousand two hundred and sixty-seven; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the three days in advance went on that sheet before anything has been done is his own, and is that the two lines under that roof on the sixth, the seventh and the eighth were settled on the Saturday and were four days behind the work by the time a stranger could look at them, and that a line which is always behind cannot be checked by anybody standing in front of it, and that paying a man first is the only way to make the day the work was done and the day the line is written be the same day more often than they are not; and that the reason the person whose yard it is said out loud that the figure for the three days is not on the sheet yet and will not be the figure on it now is hers, and is that four men are about to be handed money for three days on the strength of a number she has not written down, and that the only honest thing a person in that position can do is tell them the number is coming and tell them it is a different number, and that a man told that is not being done to; and that the reason the man of about forty-seven was not thanked for taking three men up that coast at his own figure is his own, and is that the day somebody in this harbor thanks him for it is the day a man with a boat of about twenty-two tons is a man who does things for this yard, and that a man with a boat of about twenty-two tons who does things for this yard is the beginning of a thing this harbor cannot undo and does not want; and that the reason the pump hand of about forty-four was not put on the sheet and not counted and not thanked for what he said is his own, and is that a man with nineteen years of compressors who has said out loud that he will not stand at the end of a line by himself has just made himself the only person in that harbor who has put a limit on his own usefulness, and that a person who does that is not owed a figure and is owed the thing nobody ever gives them, which is to be asked twice; and that the reason nothing in that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Monday is his own, and is that a woman of no office read five lines out in a room on Sunday and did not mention forty marks first after food and was not asked to, and that three marks to a man eleven miles up that coast came in on the entry and were ruled and not re-dated and have not been sent for, and that neither of those two figures moved today and neither of them is going to move this winter, and that the man who keeps the leaf is the only person in that harbor who writes figures down for a living and has not written a figure against either of those in eleven weeks.*
