# Chapter 525

*Monday the Twenty-Seventh Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Eleventh Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still Off It, Line, Glass, Tin and Gaskets for a Second Pressure Case Going Onto the Sheet at One Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty Pence, Being Eight Marks, With the Twenty-Seventh of December Against It, and a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Whole of That Working Party's Own Book on the Day of the Three Written at the Bench Stone at About Half Past Eleven in the Morning in Eleven Words, Being That the Party Is Not Going to Be on That Water at All on the Day of the Three, Said Out Loud by a Man of About Thirty-Two and Not Argued With and Not Thanked, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Five Thousand Four Hundred and Forty-Two Pence, Being Two Hundred and Eighty-Four Marks and Twopence, the Four Heads Not Changed, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the twenty-seventh day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and eleventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Line, glass, tin and gaskets for a second pressure case went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand two hundred and eighty pence, being eight marks, with the twenty-seventh of December against it, and a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-six thousand and eighty-two pence out, being three hundred and fifty marks, six shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-five thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being two hundred and eighty-four marks and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventeenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-five days. A case stands on a long table in the room over that market with its lock shut and the key in the keeping of the woman who wrote the minute, and nobody in that harbor has been shown what is in it and nobody has asked. There is a hundred and forty yards of boards between that shed and that room and about eleven people in that room cannot read a page. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-three days and on that Monday he went out in a boat with two men and heard two things on a gunwale that he did not hear on the Saturday and has not said what they were.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by two men who did not stop walking, and the water went into it out of a hose that is dead until the spring and had to be carried from the butt behind that market in eleven trips, and neither of those two men is at the head of that rota board and the name that is at the head of that rota board is still off it.**

The man who keeps the leaf was in that shed at eight with the door shut and did not go out and watch it, and said the same words to the slate that he has said to the slate for a hundred and ten of them.

"The hundred and eleventh. Same words as the hundred and tenth. **I am not going to improve on it because there is a page with four names on it went up on the boards of that market yesterday and a harbor that has agreed to something loud does not start writing good sentences about cisterns.**"

---

**At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put two lines onto that sheet and said the whole of both out loud before he wrote a figure against either, and nobody argued with him about any part of them.**

"Line, glass, tin and gaskets for a second pressure case. One thousand two hundred and eighty pence, being eight marks. With the twenty-seventh of December against it, being the day the work was done, and it goes into the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. Thirty feet of two-inch stuff, a pane about the size of two hands laid flat, a ring of tin and four gaskets. **A pane that a glove goes over every time a man gets into it stops being a thing you can see through, and a scratched pane at four fathoms gets worse and not better**, and the first one is scratched."

"And a day apiece in advance to the same four men, being four men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence. Their names are in the book the days are paid out of and not on this sheet."

The four men took their day in advance at the bench stone at about half past ten. One of them asked whether that pressure case was for going out to the buoys, and the man who keeps the leaf said that a pressure case is for a man going into water and that whether that man is going into water to a buoy of that port or to a stone on this quay is not a line on this sheet and is not going to be.

---

**And at about half past eleven in the morning the whole of that working party's own book on the day of the three was written at that bench stone, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote it and a man of about thirty-two said it out loud, and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him.**

The three men who had been asked to hold one channel each of that stretch of about four fathoms off that third lay had said they would be asked on Wednesday, and the whole of that working party's own book on the day of the three was eleven words long and the man of about thirty-two said them out loud at the bench stone with about nine men on that slip.

**"We are not going to be on that water at all on the day of the three."**

Somebody at the end of that slip asked him what he meant by that, since a party of nine with two pressure cases was not a thing that stayed off a quay, and he said that he meant that the day of the three was not this yard's day and was not this yard's water and was not this yard's mark, and that the mark on that water belongs to four men of this harbor who took a channel each on the twenty-first of December and held the line between them and struck a bell once, and that if the day of the three is going to be done then it will be done by three men asked in the same words at the same rate and this yard will carry a slate and pull a cutoff and hold a boat, and that is the whole of what this yard is on that water for.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that that sounded like a party giving up the only water it had ever been any good at, and the man of about thirty-two said that it was not giving anything up, it was that this party had put a mark in four fathoms on the twenty-first of December with two ends of a line and not three, and had crossed on four boats and not five, and that a thing that stops when one of three stops is not a thing anybody gets to keep, and that four local men in this harbor know that better than he does because they were two of the three.

Nobody argued with him. Nobody thanked him. **He is not on the fitting-out sheet and is on no line of it and is not paid on that line and is not a name on the buoys list on the boards of that market, and the man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down read that list yesterday and said nothing about it and was not asked.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight who works that compressor asked one question at about one o'clock in the afternoon, and got an answer, and was not thanked for asking it and was not paid for asking it and is on no line of any sheet in that harbor.**

He asked whether three men in about four fathoms with a line between them was the same as three men in about four fathoms with a line between them and somebody going to a float on it afterward, and the man of about thirty-two said no, and that the difference was a person in the water and that this party was not going to be one of the three on that water on that day and had said so out loud in front of nine men at about half past eleven that morning, and that the man of about thirty-eight was the only person in that shed who had understood that on the first time it was said and that was not a thing anybody was going to make anything of.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Forty-five thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being two hundred and eighty-four marks and twopence. It moved this morning by one thousand nine hundred and fifty-two pence and that is two lines and both of them are on the sheet with the twenty-seventh of December against them, and the day those two lines were written is the day the work was done, which is today."

A woman at the end of that group said the word party and the man who keeps the leaf said the word party back, once, and then said the rest of what he had to say about it, and what he said was not about the word.

"Eleven words at about half past eleven this morning cost this yard nothing and cost this yard everything, and neither of those is a figure on this sheet. **A yard is not a man and a man is not a crew and this sheet carries the cost of a yard and does not carry a claim on anybody's water.** The wage column four hundred yards away is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it has not moved since the seventh of October and tomorrow is a Tuesday and it will not move tomorrow either, and the lamp oil is at four hundred and fifty pence and there is no flour in that market and the sixty-second week stands one day off being fallen due."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for anything on that Monday.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH TIME AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A SECOND PRESSURE CASE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE, BEING EIGHT MARKS, AND FOUR MEN TOOK A DAY APIECE IN ADVANCE AT THE BENCH STONE. ELEVEN WORDS WERE SAID OUT LOUD AT THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE MEN, BEING THAT THE PARTY IS NOT GOING TO BE ON THAT WATER AT ALL ON THE DAY OF THE THREE, AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH THEM AND NOBODY THANKED HIM. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-ONE OUT OF A COAST TOWN FOUR DAYS DOWN READ THE BUOYS LIST ON THE BOARDS YESTERDAY AND SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT AND WAS NOT ASKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-FIVE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-seventh day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and eleventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and the figure of that hundred was not said out loud on it; and that line, glass, tin and gaskets for a second pressure case went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand two hundred and eighty pence, being eight marks, with the twenty-seventh of December against it being the day the work was done, and that a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of both lines out loud before he wrote a figure against either and that both of those lines are in the total at the foot of that sheet as of about four in the afternoon of that day, the day they were written being the day the work was done; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-six thousand and eighty-two pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-five thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being two hundred and eighty-four marks and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by one thousand nine hundred and fifty-two pence on that day; and that the whole of that working party's own book on the day of the three was written at that bench stone at about half past eleven in the morning in eleven words, being that the party is not going to be on that water at all on the day of the three, and that it was said out loud by a man of about thirty-two in front of about nine men and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him, and that no man of that party is to be one of the three on that water on that day; and that a man of about thirty-four of a fisher commonwealth at a port four days down that coast read the buoys list on the boards of that market on the Sunday and said nothing about it and was not asked; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-eight, one thousand and one hundred and sixty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-seven, one thousand and one hundred and sixty-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason no man out of a port four days down that coast was asked to look at a buoys list on the boards of that market is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a man who is off a coast road with a condition to guard is not a reader and a page he has to check is a page that can be checked by anybody in that market, and that the man of about fifty-one of the coast town four days down read it and said nothing and is not a reader either and has a page of six names of his own that he has said nothing about for a hundred and sixty-five days, and there is no room in this harbor for one man to check another man's list; and that the reason a second pressure case went on the sheet on a Monday when this party has said out loud it is not going to be in that water is his own, and is that a case is glass and a pane does not know whose water it is going to look at, and that this yard is a yard and sells its hours to whoever is buying on the day, and that a man who stops building because he does not know who the buyer is has stopped being a yard and started being a position; and that the reason those eleven words went into the second column of that leaf and not one word of them went into the first is his own, and is that a stranger with a pencil can count a day and cannot count what a party has agreed not to do on a Wednesday, and that eleven words is the whole of it and there is no figure on it and no name against it; and that the reason not one item on that harbor's standing list was paid, advanced, knocked down, carried forward or shut on that Monday is this yard's own mouth, and is that the wage column has not moved since the seventh of October and the mole head has been dark since the seventh of May and the tide gang is four men short and none of those four is a man this yard could pay, and the hundred and twelfth of those cisterns is on Thursday, and there is no flour in that market, and this yard is going down a coast road on Sunday.**
