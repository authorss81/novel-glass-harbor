# Chapter 544

*Saturday the Fifteenth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Two Boats Lying on the Ground off the Outer Water Four Days Down That Coast With a Hole Under the Forefoot of Each of Them and the Gear of Each of Them in the Bottom and Nobody Standing On Either of Them at About Six in the Morning, Three Bells Not Rung Again, Nine Boats Counted Inside That Line on the Ebb and Seven Counted Back, Four Marks in That Water and Three of Them Answered For on a Page on Four Tacks on the Boards of a Market Four Hundred Yards From a Bench Stone at Morrow Quay, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking Out Loud in Front of About Fourteen People Why Two Boats Are Going on a Sheet That Did Not Put Them on the Ground and Being Answered in About Nine Words, the Two Owners of Those Boats Not Asked for a Penny and Not Thanked, a Second Week of a Room Over a Store at Ternwatch Going Onto the Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, With the Twelfth of January Against It and Settled on the Fifteenth, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Back on That Quay at Morrow Quay at About Half Past Four, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Seventy-One Thousand One Hundred and Thirty Pence, Being Four Hundred and Forty-Four Marks, Seven Shillings and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the fifteenth day of January, at a port four days down that coast, and in that harbor at Morrow Quay in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the twelfth of January against it and settled on the fifteenth, and it is the second time that sheet has carried a figure off this coast. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at eighty-one thousand seven hundred and seventy pence out, being five hundred and eleven marks and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is seventy-one thousand one hundred and thirty pence short, being four hundred and forty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-four days. A lighter of about eleven tons lies at a slip on the north side of that harbor at any state of the weather and has lain there since the spring, and her master has said out loud twice that he would rather have a berth on the third lay, and there is one berth short on the third lay, and the column on the west basin is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and has not been in that harbor's own book for two months. A register copy of what that harbor authority has been given is kept in a drawer in an office on the west basin and it is a copy and not the thing, and the four heads on the wall of the room over that market are not that copy and are not on that copy. The figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall has not moved since the first of December, and there are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning, and there were nine on Thursday. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-two days and he was in that hut again from about eight in the evening until about three in the morning on Friday night and has not been up that road to look at the pump and has not been asked to.**

---

**At about six o'clock in the morning the two boats were found, and they were found by a boat that had gone out at first light to look for them, and there was nobody standing on either of them and there had been nobody standing on either of them since about half past four in the morning.**

Each of them had a hole under the forefoot about the size of a man's two hands together, and the gear of each of them was in the bottom of her and there was no gear on either deck, and the water in each of them was about two feet down and neither of them was breaking up. One of them was a boat of about fourteen tons and one was a boat of about nine tons, and they were lying about forty yards apart with their bows pointing the same way, and the piece of lead in a hole in a stone that put them there was about nine feet off the port bow of the boat of about nine tons and was not on any page.

**Their crews were on that quay at about six in the morning. They had been in the other boats. Nobody was hurt, no body is named, no figure of any person is printed here, and nobody has been made a casualty of anything that happened on that water, and a lost boat is a boat and is not a lost man and is not written down as one.**

---

**And at about half past eight in the morning a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood on that quay in front of about fourteen people and asked out loud why two boats are going on a sheet that did not put them on the ground, and he was answered in about nine words, and he did not say it was enough and he did not say it was not enough.**

"Because **I** put them there and not them."

He said that was not an answer and that a man who pays for a thing he did not break is a man buying his way past something, and the man who keeps the leaf said one more thing and only that.

"The page of hours had three windows on it and no name at the top of it and the fourth mark was on neither. **I have got a book with a pencil's worth of days in it and that book is the only thing between this quay and a man deciding for himself what the night was. If those two boats are not on it then that book is a book of hours and nothing else, and in a month somebody will put a name at the top of it and there will be nothing in the book to stop him.**"

Nobody thanked him for either of those two things and nobody argued with him about the rest of it and he went off up the quay and has not asked it again.

---

**And the two owners of those two boats were found on that quay on that Saturday morning, and neither of them was asked for a penny and neither of them was thanked and neither of them was asked what he wanted done about it.**

One of them is a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about fourteen tons and the other is a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay and owns the boat of about nine tons himself. The man of about thirty-one said one sentence out loud to a man of about twenty-eight who was standing next to him and it was this, and it went into the second column in his own words and not into the first.

"I have got a boat that floats and I have got a hole in the ground with a boat in it and both of those were mine on Thursday, and I have not got one penny the worse off and I have not been asked for one, and I do not know yet which of those two I am going to be gladder about in March."

The man of about fifty said nothing at all about it and went and looked at the boat of about fourteen tons for about a quarter of an hour and then went and found a man who could get a warp onto her, and he paid that man out of his own pocket, and that figure is on no sheet in any hand and is not going to be.

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was the named tender on the surface at the third point on Friday night in a boat of her own and was not in that water at any hour, and she came into that port on the Saturday morning at about seven, and she said one thing out loud on that quay, and it was about eleven words, and nobody asked her a second question about it.**

"Three of them are mine and one of them is not, and I did not say a word about the one that is not, and I am not going to now."

Her condition is still on the face of a record four hundred yards from that bench stone at Morrow Quay, with four lines and an hour under each, and it has not been settled and it is not agreeable, and nothing that happened off that quay on the night of the fourteenth has settled it and nobody in that port has asked her about it.

---

**And at about ten o'clock in the morning at Morrow Quay, four hundred yards and four days' sail from either of those two boats, the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and about nine people were on that slip.**

"A second week of a room over a store at Ternwatch. Six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, being eight shillings a day for seven days, which is ninety-six pence a day and seven times ninety-six is six hundred and seventy-two. **The woman who keeps that store quoted me the same figure she quoted on the sixth of this month and I did not ask her to come down and she did not offer, and the twelfth of January is against that line because that is the day the room was taken, and it goes into the total at the foot of this sheet this afternoon because this afternoon is the day I am settling it, and that is the rule and the two days are both on the face of the line.**"

"**A man of about twenty-nine has been in a hut on a shoulder above a street of about forty houses for a second week and he has heard three bells he cannot name and a pump he has never walked up to look at and his own breathing, and none of those three is on this sheet and none of them is going to be, and I am not setting a figure against any of them and there is not going to be one.**"

He put the pen down flat and said nothing else about it.

---

**And the man who keeps the leaf came back onto that quay at Morrow Quay at about half past four in the afternoon, four days' sail and a night's sleep, and the day had gone in and out of that yard without him, and at about half past five in the afternoon he said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about nine people were on it and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Seventy-one thousand one hundred and thirty pence short, being four hundred and forty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence. It moved this morning by six hundred and seventy-two pence and that is a room with a bed in it on a coast four days down that coast, and it is the second figure this sheet has carried from off this coast, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take both of them off it."

"And **two boats are on the ground four days down that coast and neither of them is on this sheet yet, and there is a figure for the first of them going on on Tuesday and a figure for the second of them going on on Thursday, and between them they are seventeen thousand two hundred and eighty-eight pence, being one hundred and eight marks and eightpence, and that is a bill and not a wound and it is two boats and not two men, and I have not got a figure for the four men who went into that water because the four men who went into that water are paid at the figure in chalk out of a fund that pays a day for a day and their names are in a book and not on this.**"

"Nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon, and the figure in chalk has not moved since the first of December, and the lamp oil board is at four hundred and fifty pence, and there is still no flour in that market, and a man came back onto this quay at half past four this afternoon after four days and did not sleep in a bed on the Thursday and has not been thanked for it and was not going to be."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Saturday, and nobody was thanked for the two boats and nobody was thanked for the two owners of them and nobody was thanked for the eleven words.

---

**TWO BOATS WERE FOUND ON THE MORNING OF THE FIFTEENTH ON THE GROUND OFF THE OUTER WATER FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST WITH A HOLE UNDER THE FOREFOOT OF EACH AND THE GEAR OF EACH IN THE BOTTOM AND NOBODY STANDING ON EITHER. BOTH CREWS WERE ASHORE BY SIX AND NOT ONE MAN OF THEM WAS HURT AND NO BODY IS NAMED AND NO FIGURE OF ANY PERSON IS PRINTED. THREE BELLS WERE RUNG ONCE EACH ON THREE MARKS THAT ARE ANSWERED FOR ON A PAGE ON FOUR TACKS ON THE BOARDS OF A MARKET FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT BENCH STONE, AND A FOURTH MARK IN THAT WATER IS A PIECE OF LEAD IN A HOLE IN A STONE AND IS ON NO PAGE. NINE BOATS WENT INSIDE THAT LINE ON THE EBB AND SEVEN CAME BACK. A SECOND WEEK OF A ROOM OVER A STORE AT TERNWATCH WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE WITH THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AGAINST IT AND SETTLED ON THE FIFTEENTH. THE FIGURE IN CHALK HAS NOT MOVED SINCE THE FIRST OF DECEMBER AND THERE ARE NINE HULLS OFF THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SEVENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the fifteenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed at Morrow Quay: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that two boats lying on the ground off the outer water four days down that coast were found at about six in the morning by a boat that had gone out at first light, that they were about forty yards apart with their bows pointing the same way, that each of them had a hole under the forefoot, that the gear of each of them was in the bottom of her and there was no gear on either deck, and that there was nobody standing on either of them and there had been nobody standing on either of them since about half past four in the morning, and that both of their crews were on that quay at about six in the morning and not one of them was hurt and no body is named and no figure of any person is printed and nobody was made a casualty; and that there were four marks in that water and three of them are answered for on a page on four tacks on the boards of a market four hundred yards from that bench stone and the fourth is not on it and is a piece of lead in a hole in a stone under about four fathoms; and that nine boats went inside that line on the ebb of the fourteenth and seven were back inside it before six in the morning, and that the three bells were rung once each and were not rung again; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked out loud in front of about fourteen people why two boats are going on a sheet that did not put them on the ground and was answered in about nine words, being that the man who keeps the leaf put them there, and said once more that a book with a pencil's worth of days in it is the only thing between that quay and a man putting a name at the top of it, and was not thanked and did not ask it again; and that the two owners of those two boats were found on that quay and neither of them was asked for a penny and neither of them was thanked and neither of them was asked what he wanted done about it, and that one of the two owners paid a man out of his own pocket to get a warp onto a boat of about fourteen tons and that figure is on no sheet in any hand; and that a woman of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was the named tender on the surface at the third point in a boat of her own and came into that port at about seven in the morning and said out loud in about eleven words that three of the four marks in that water are hers and one of them is not and that she did not say a word about the one that is not and is not going to; and that a second week of a room over a store at Ternwatch went onto that sheet at about ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, being eight shillings a day for seven days, which is ninety-six pence a day and seven times ninety-six is six hundred and seventy-two, quoted at the same figure as the first week by the same woman and not argued with, and eight shillings a day for seven days is six hundred and seventy-two pence, with the twelfth of January against it being the day the room was taken and settled on the fifteenth; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at eighty-one thousand seven hundred and seventy pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is seventy-one thousand one hundred and thirty pence short, being four hundred and forty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence; and that the man who keeps the leaf came back onto that quay at about half past four in the afternoon after four days; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and seventy-seven, one thousand two hundred and fifty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and seventy-six, one thousand two hundred and fifty-seven; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed at Morrow Quay, and no name is against it: that the reason two boats are going on that sheet is his own and has not changed since he said it out loud on a quay at about half past eight in the morning, and is that a sheet which carries the days a party spent and not the ground those days put something on is a sheet that can be audited by the party alone, and that the whole of the use of a pencil in this world is that a stranger can take the figure off and the stranger does not have to like anybody; and that the reason the four men who went into that water are not on that sheet is his own, and is that a day asked for by name is paid at the figure in chalk out of a fund that pays a day for a day and their names are in a book, and that a line on that sheet for each of them would be a second payment of a day that has already been paid and a sheet that pays a man twice is a sheet that has stopped being a record and become a receipt; and that the reason the man of about thirty-one said one sentence and went no further is his own, and is that a man who has lost a boat and has not been asked for a penny and does not yet know which of the two facts he is gladder about has said the whole of it and anything more would be a man performing being owed something; and that the reason the second week of that room went on at the same figure and was not argued about is his own, and is that the woman who keeps that store did not come down and he did not ask, and that a person who is not asked to come down has been given the only thing in this coast that a person can be given and keep, and that he is not going to be the man who makes her quote it twice; and that the reason nothing standing in that harbor was touched on that Saturday is the person whose yard it is, and is that a roof went on that yard on the sixth, the seventh and the eighth of this month and there is not one sill under it and she has said so out loud twice and the sill is not on this sheet, and that a roof with no sill is a roof that a man is waiting under and that what goes into that shed next is a second figure and not the first one, and that the six beams of oak, the eleven fathoms of three-quarter and the fourteen panes are none of them on a line that is written yet, and that she has known that since the ninth and has not asked anybody for a penny of it.*
