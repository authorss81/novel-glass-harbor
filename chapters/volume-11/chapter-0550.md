# Chapter 550

*Friday the Twenty-First Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Leaf Read Back in Full at About Ten Past Five in the Evening on the Flat of That Bench Stone in the Open, All Four Columns Including the Empty One, Read Twice Because About Four Men of That Party Cannot Read, With Both Definitions in the Same Mouth and the Same Words as Every Other Read-Back and With Its Own Derivation Printed on the Face of the Reading, the First Column at Two Hundred and Eighty-Two at the Hour of That Reading and Two Hundred and Eighty-Three at the Close of It, the Second at One Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Seven at the Hour and One Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Nine at the Close, the Third None With a Boy of Twenty-Two's Hand at the Head of It and His Surname on No Page of That Leaf, the Fourth at One Hundred and Twenty-Five With No Hundred and Twenty-Sixth Line in It and Neither Closed Nor Struck, the Sheet That Carries the Money Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, a Question Asked Once at About a Quarter Past Ten at Night in That Yard in the Open in a Working Woman's Mouth, Answered by Nobody and Entered in a Minute as Asked, the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed and No Fifth Head Added, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the twenty-first day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety days. The proof is in a locked case on a long table in the room over that fish market, in a lining of oilcloth, and it was not read out on any of the ten days of this month and nobody in that harbor has got an answer out of it and nobody is going to get one. The four heads on the wall of that room are the four heads, and no fifth head went on that wall at any hour of this month, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and was not read out in it. The board at the head of that mole still carries two papers with a seal on them and no figure on either, and one of those two has been on that board since the third of December and was not opened then and has not been opened since, and the light that is meant to stand over that board is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-eight days and he has not been to the road with the pump on it and nobody has sent for him and he has not asked anybody for anything.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about nine people were on it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Thursday morning. One hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree. **The figure in chalk has not moved since the first of December, the lamp oil board is at four hundred and fifty pence, the mole head is dark, there is no flour in that market, and the sixty-fifth week of that harbor stands fallen due at the close of the eighteenth of this month and nobody has been paid a penny out of that chest.**"

---

**And at about ten past five in the evening the man who keeps the leaf read that leaf back in full on the flat of that bench stone, in the open, in his own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, and he read it twice because about four men of that party cannot read it, and both definitions came out of the same mouth in the same words as on every other read-back of that leaf, and the whole of his own arithmetic was said out loud and printed on the face of the reading before a single figure in it was believed.**

He said the definitions first, and they are the definitions.

1. *The first column takes one entry a day, whatever else that day prints, and the entry against the day itself goes in after that day's reading and not before it, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that column by counting days and cannot be stopped from doing it.*
2. *The second column takes five lines a day, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, and that column is the ledger's own and is not a count of anything that anybody can check from a mole.*

Then he said the derivation of each of the four figures out loud, and then he gave the four figures, and about nine people were on that slip and about four of them cannot read what is in his hand.

"The first column. **Two hundred and eighty have gone in on the fifty days from Friday the third day of December to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them has not got an entry against it, which is the first time in fifty-one days that those two things have been the same. And the count at the hour of this reading is two hundred and eighty-two, because the entry against this day goes in after this reading and not before it, and two hundred and eighty-two plus this one is two hundred and eighty-three, which is the count at the close of it.**"

"The second column. **Five have gone in on each of those same fifty days, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the two hundred and fifty that have gone in, two hundred and forty-five went in on the forty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and two hundred and fifty less two is two hundred and forty-eight, and one thousand and thirty-nine and two hundred and forty-eight is one thousand two hundred and eighty-seven at the hour of this reading, and one thousand and thirty-nine and two hundred and fifty is one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine at the close of it.**"

"The third column. **None. Nobody has been appointed to it, nobody has offered for it, a boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of it, his surname is on no page of that leaf, and he is not on the water.**"

"The fourth column. **One hundred and twenty-five. It has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, it is neither closed nor struck, and the reason it took no line was entered once, in the second column, at about ten minutes past nine in the evening of a day in the summer, and it is not entered again here and it is not going to be.**"

He read all of it a second time, at about a quarter to six, in the same words and in the same order, and about four men of that party heard both of the readings and could not read either of them, and nobody stopped him at a word, and a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay stood at the end of the slip through both of them and said nothing at all and went home.

**The four columns are at two hundred and eighty-three, one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the close of this day, and at two hundred and eighty-two, one thousand two hundred and eighty-seven, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the hour of the reading.**

---

**And at about a quarter past ten at night, with the door of that shed still open on the slip, the person whose yard it is asked one question out loud, once, in that yard, in the open, in front of about nine people of whom about four cannot read what is on the bench stone.**

She had a lamp in her hand and a plane in the other and she put the plane down on the bench stone before she said it, and she said it once and she did not say it twice and nobody in that yard answered it.

"**What are people going to do when one voice says that it is the only safe way to remember.**"

Nobody answered it. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands opened his mouth and shut it again. The man who keeps the leaf did not answer it and said afterwards, out loud and once, in about eleven words, that he was not going to answer it and that there was nothing in that yard that could.

"It is not mine to answer and it is not yours to answer. It is a question and it is on the page and it is not answered."

**It was entered in a minute as asked, at about a quarter past ten at night on the twenty-first day of January, by the man who keeps the leaf, in the second column of that leaf and in the minute he keeps under that bench stone, and neither of those two places carries anybody's name against it. It was not asked again on that night and it has not been asked since, and nobody in that harbor has been asked it and nobody in that harbor has answered it.**

---

**THE LEAF WAS READ BACK IN FULL AT ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE IN THE EVENING ON THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE IN THE OPEN, ALL FOUR COLUMNS INCLUDING THE EMPTY ONE, TWICE, WITH BOTH DEFINITIONS IN THE SAME MOUTH AND THE SAME WORDS AS EVERY OTHER READ-BACK, AND WITH ITS OWN DERIVATION PRINTED ON THE FACE OF THE READING, AND THE FOUR COLUMNS ARE AT TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE, ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINE, NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY AND AT TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-TWO, ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SEVEN, NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE AT THE HOUR OF THAT READING. THE THIRD COLUMN IS EMPTY AND UNAPPOINTED WITH A BOY OF TWENTY-TWO'S HAND AT THE HEAD OF IT AND HIS SURNAME ON NO PAGE OF THAT LEAF. THE FOURTH COLUMN STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE AND TOOK NO LINE ON ANY DAY OF THIS MONTH AND IS NEITHER CLOSED NOR STRUCK. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. A QUESTION WAS ASKED ONCE IN THAT YARD IN THE OPEN AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST TEN AT NIGHT AND WAS ANSWERED BY NOBODY AND WAS ENTERED IN A MINUTE AS ASKED. THE LAMPS IN THAT HARBOR ARE LIT ON THE QUAY AND NOT AT THE HEAD OF THE MOLE, AND A BELL WENT AT THAT PORT FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN ON THE NIGHT OF THE FOURTEENTH AND DID NOT GO AGAIN, AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK HAS NOT MOVED SINCE THE FIRST OF DECEMBER.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-first day of January at about half past ten at night in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence; and that the sixty-fifth wage Tuesday of that harbor was the eighteenth of this month and the wage column did not move on it, that a further week stands fallen due at the close of it, and that nobody was paid a penny out of the chest with twenty envelopes in it; and that that leaf was read back in full at about ten past five in the evening on the flat of that bench stone in the open by the man who keeps it in his own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, and that it was read twice because about four men of that party cannot read it, and that both definitions of the four figures were stated on the face of that reading in the same words as on every other read-back of that leaf, being that the first column takes one entry a day and the entry against the day itself goes in after that day's reading, and that the second column takes five lines a day of which three are before the hour of that day's reading and two after it and is not checkable from a mole; and that the derivation of the first column was said out loud, being that two hundred and eighty have gone in on the fifty days from Friday the third day of December to this day, one against each of them, with no day in two of them without an entry against it, and that the count at the hour of that reading is two hundred and eighty-two because the entry against this day goes in after that reading, and that two hundred and eighty-two plus one is two hundred and eighty-three; and that the derivation of the second column was said out loud, being that five have gone in on each of those same fifty days, that two hundred and forty-five of the two hundred and fifty went in on the forty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and that two hundred and fifty less two is two hundred and forty-eight, and that one thousand and thirty-nine and two hundred and forty-eight is one thousand two hundred and eighty-seven at the hour of that reading and one thousand and thirty-nine and two hundred and fifty is one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine at the close of it; and that the derivation of the third column was said out loud and is that nobody has been appointed to it, that nobody has offered for it, that a boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of it and his surname is on no page of that leaf and he is not on the water; and that the derivation of the fourth column was said out loud and is one hundred and twenty-five, that it has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, that it is neither closed nor struck, and that the reason it took no line was entered once in the second column at about ten minutes past nine in the evening of a day in the summer and is not entered again; and that the four columns are at two hundred and eighty-three, one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the close of that day and at two hundred and eighty-two, one thousand two hundred and eighty-seven, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the hour of the reading; and that a question was asked once in that yard in the open at about a quarter past ten at night in the mouth of the person whose yard it is, and that it is entered in a minute as asked with no name against it, that nobody answered it, that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that it was not his to answer and was not anybody's in that yard to answer, and that it has not been asked again.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past ten at night in that shed, and no name is against it: that the question that was asked in that yard at about a quarter past ten at night is this, and it is entered here as asked and it is not answered here and it is not answered anywhere in that harbor: what are people going to do when one voice says that it is the only safe way to remember. It was asked once, in the open, in about nine people of whom about four cannot read what is on that bench stone, by a person who prices things in hours and material and who put a plane down on the stone before she said it, and she said it once and did not say it twice, and a man of about thirty-three who hires hands opened his mouth and shut it again, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that it was not his to answer and was not anybody's in that yard to answer and that it is a question and it is on the page and it is not answered. And the reason the answer to it was not given is his own, and is that this yard has never once in fifty-one weeks had the standing to speak for a person and the day it did would be the day the first column and the second column and the third column and the fourth column were all the same page, and that the third column is empty because the only honest place for that question is the third column and nobody has ever been appointed to it; and that the reason it was entered in a minute as asked with no name against it is his own, and is that a question with a name against it gets answered by the man whose name it is within a day, and a question with an hour against it and no name is still an open thing in three months, and that a coast that has four marks on it and a page of three bells is not going to know what to do with a single voice until somebody has had three months with the question sitting in front of them not answered; and that the reason that leaf was read back twice at ten past five and again at a quarter to six with about four men of that party unable to read either reading is his own and is fifty-one weeks old, and is that a figure said once to a room of twenty where eleven cannot read is a figure nine of them heard, and that the only correction for that in this world is a second time in the same words with the same mouth and the arithmetic printed on the face of it so that a man who cannot read can check the mouth against itself; and that the reason no debt in that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Friday is his own, and is that on the twentieth of this month a man of about fifty said eleven words in a room about twenty people and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him, and that those eleven words and this question are in the same column of the same book four hundred yards from a lamp oil board at four hundred and fifty pence, and that neither of them is going to be answered this winter and the board is not going to come down, and that a man who keeps figures in a yard where nothing gets paid is a man who has been made the only kind of head that can be made out of a piece of paper, and that is not a promotion and it is not going to be thanked and there is nobody to thank.*
