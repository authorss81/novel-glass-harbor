# Chapter 536

*Friday the Seventh Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Listening Station at Ternwatch Entered for the First Time in About Two Hundred and Thirty Years of That Harbor Being Written Down Anywhere, a Hut of Tarred Board on a Low Shoulder Above the Water With a Bell Under a Board on Four Sticks and No Board on Any Post, a Man of About Twenty-Nine at That Station for Five Days and a Man of About Fifty-Two Who Ran It for Nineteen Years and Is Not Running It Now and Says He Is Not Going to Be the Second Man in It, a Week's Hire of a Room Over a Store at That Coast Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, Quoted at Five Shillings and Fourpence a Day by the Woman Who Keeps That Store and Agreed in a Cart on That Coast Road on the Fourth and Settled on the Seventh With the Seventh of January Against It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Fifty Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty Pence, Being Three Hundred and Fifteen Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the seventh day of January, at a listening station on a coast that is being closed, and in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. A week's hire of a room over a store at Ternwatch went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the seventh of January against it, being the day the work was done, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at sixty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-six pence out, being three hundred and ninety-four marks, twelve shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is fifty-two thousand five hundred and forty-six pence short, being three hundred and twenty-eight marks, five shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fortieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirteenth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-six days. The ninth hull of that line about two sea miles off the head of that mole was counted once at about two o'clock in the afternoon on that Friday by a man on that mole head with a glass and he told one other person and that person has told nobody. A hatch cover off a store on the second lay has been off since the twenty-ninth of December and two men in that harbor have a bet about it that neither of them has said out loud. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-four days and he has been five days at that listening station and nobody in either harbor has said a word about it to him, and he is not asked to explain it and has not explained it.**

---

**The hire of that room went onto the sheet with the seventh of January against it and was settled the seventh, and neither of those two days was decided by a man in that yard, and the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it.**

"A week of a room over a store at Ternwatch. Six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, being five shillings and fourpence a day for seven days. The woman who keeps that store quoted me that figure on Tuesday in a cart on the coast road and I did not argue with her and she did not come down off her own price, and it went up that road on a slip in an envelope on Wednesday and it goes on this sheet today and the seventh of January is against it because that is the day it was done, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon."

"And nobody in this harbor has been asked for that figure and nobody in that coast has been asked for it. **A room is not a favour and a man who sleeps in it for a week is not a man this harbor has taken in.**"

He put the pen down flat and said nothing else about it.

---

**And the room is one room over a store on a street of about forty houses where nobody has been out on the water since the autumn, and a man of about twenty-nine has been in it five nights, and the listening station is about three quarters of a mile up a shoulder above that street, and that is the whole of what Ternwatch is on these pages.**

The station is a hut of tarred board about nine feet by eleven with a chimney out of it and a bench inside and a bell under a board on four sticks on the shoulder above it, and there is no post anywhere near that bell and nothing about it that anybody in that coast has changed. It has stood on that shoulder for nineteen years and the man of about fifty-two who ran it, sitting in it from dusk until about three in the morning and a man beside him who wrote down what he said he could hear, has not sat in it since the autumn, because the board on the post was taken down in the autumn and has not gone back up and he is not the man who put it back.

He came up the shoulder on the Thursday, on his own, at about four in the afternoon, and he stood in the doorway of that hut with his hands behind his back and he looked at the bell and he did not go in and he said about forty words.

"I took nineteen years in that hut and I had a man beside me the whole of it and that is the only reason it was worth anything, and there is one man in there now and that is not a listening station. It is a man sitting in a box. **You have got no book and no second pair of ears and nobody to say no to you at three in the morning, and if you write down what you think you heard you will be two men and one of them is not there.** I am not going to be the second man in it and I am not going to sit on that shoulder in the dark pretending, and you are welcome to sit in it, and I would not do what you are doing for nothing and you have not offered me nothing."

Nobody argued with him and he went down the shoulder and he has not been asked to come back and he has not been thanked.

---

**And the man of about twenty-nine sat in that hut from about eight in the evening until about three in the morning on the Thursday and on the Friday, and he wrote down what he could hear on the back of a sheet of paper out of a book, and he gave his account of it to the man who keeps the leaf at about half past ten on the Friday morning in a room over a store in about sixty words, and nobody in either harbor said one word about it that made him anything.**

"Three things. The pump at the third house up that road goes at about eleven and stops at about twelve and I can hear it start and I can hear it stop and I have never been up that road to look at it. There is a bell about two o'clock, out over the water to the south, and it is a bell and not a gun and I know the difference and I have heard it every night since Tuesday and I cannot tell you whose it is and I am not going to walk out to find out. And the third thing is me, and it is that I can hear myself breathing on this side more clearly than I can hear you standing in a doorway, and I do not know what that is and I have not written it down."

The man who keeps the leaf asked him one question, which was whether he could tell the difference between the pump and the water in a pipe, and he said no, in three words, and that was the end of it.

"That is the correct answer and there is a book on a bench stone in my yard that has three men in it who say where their knowledge stops, and I am not going to ask a fourth. **A station with one man in it is not a station and a man who says he cannot tell is the only thing in it that is worth the week.** Write the three down and put the hours on them and do not put your name at the head of it, because a page with one man's name on it is a page a man argues with and a page with an hour on it is a page a stranger can take a figure off."

Nobody thanked him, no figure was set against him, he was not made a member of anything, and on the Friday morning he went down the shoulder at about eleven and came back up it at about seven in the evening and did the same thing again.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door open on the slip, and said the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Fifty-two thousand five hundred and forty-six pence short, being three hundred and twenty-eight marks, five shillings and sixpence. It moved this morning by six hundred and seventy-two pence and that is a room with a bed in it on a coast four days down that coast, and it is the first thing this sheet has carried this month that has not been in this harbor. Sixty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-six out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And **a man is sitting five nights in a hut on that coast hearing three things and one of the three is himself, and there is not one person in this harbor who has any more food this week than they had last week, and the lamp oil is at four hundred and fifty pence and the ninth hull of that line is still off the head of that mole and the mole head is still dark.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Friday.

---

**A WEEK OF A ROOM OVER A STORE AT TERNWATCH WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE, BEING FOUR MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, QUOTED AT FIVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE A DAY BY THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THAT STORE AND AGREED IN A CART ON THAT COAST ROAD ON THE FOURTH, AND THE SEVENTH OF JANUARY IS AGAINST IT AND IT IS IN THE TOTAL THIS AFTERNOON. A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE HAS SAT IN THAT STATION FROM DUSK UNTIL ABOUT THREE IN THE MORNING ON TWO NIGHTS AND HEARD THREE THINGS AND WROTE DOWN THREE THINGS AND SAID HE COULD NOT TELL A PUMP FROM THE WATER IN A PIPE AND WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION AND NOT ASKED A SECOND. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO WHO RAN THAT STATION FOR NINETEEN YEARS CAME UP THAT SHOULDER ONCE, STOOD IN THE DOORWAY, SAID THAT A STATION WITH ONE MAN IN IT IS A MAN SITTING IN A BOX, AND WENT DOWN AGAIN AND WAS NOT THANKED AND HAS NOT BEEN ASKED TO COME BACK. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FIFTY-TWO THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT MARKS, FIVE SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the seventh day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that a week's hire of a room over a store at Ternwatch went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, being five shillings and fourpence a day for seven days, with the seventh of January against it being the day the work was done and being in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon, and that the figure was quoted by the woman who keeps that store in a cart on that coast road on Tuesday and was not argued with by this yard and that she did not come down off her own price, and that the slip went up that road in an envelope on the Wednesday and was settled on the Friday; and that the station at that coast is a hut of tarred board about nine feet by eleven with a bell under a board on four sticks on a shoulder about three quarters of a mile above the street, and that it has stood there nineteen years and that the board on the post was taken down in the autumn and has not gone back up; and that a man of about fifty-two who ran that station for nineteen years with a second man beside him came up that shoulder on Thursday at about four in the afternoon, stood in the doorway, said that a station with one man in it is a man sitting in a box and that a man writing down what he thinks he heard is two men and one of them is not there, and that he is not going to be the second man in it and is not going to sit on that shoulder in the dark pretending, and went down the shoulder and was not argued with, was not asked to come back and was not thanked; and that a man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat sat in that hut from about eight in the evening until about three in the morning on the Thursday and on the Friday and gave his account of it in about sixty words at about half past ten on the Friday morning, being a pump at the third house up that road that goes at about eleven and stops at about twelve, a bell about two o'clock out over the water to the south whose owner he cannot name and is not going to walk out to find, and himself, and that he can hear himself breathing on that side more clearly than he can hear a man standing in a doorway and that he does not know what that is and has not written it down, and that he was asked one question, being whether he could tell a pump from the water in a pipe, and answered no in three words, and was not asked a second question and was not thanked and no figure was set against him; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at sixty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is fifty-two thousand five hundred and forty-six pence short, being three hundred and twenty-eight marks, five shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it moved by six hundred and seventy-two pence on that day; and that the ninth hull of that line about two sea miles off the head of that mole was counted once at about two o'clock in the afternoon by a man on that mole head with a glass and told one other person and that person has told nobody; and that the fourth column of that leaf took no line on that day and is neither closed nor struck.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that room was put on that sheet on the Friday and not on the Tuesday when the figure was agreed is his own, and is that the day a man agrees a figure on a cart and the day he writes it in a book are two different days and a sheet which mixes them is a sheet a stranger cannot check, and that the slip went up the road in an envelope and came down it in another and that is the whole of the correspondence; and that the reason that man of about fifty-two was not asked to go back up that shoulder is the reader's own and is not a slight, and is that a man who says no to a thing he did for nineteen years has just told this yard the edge of what he will still stand behind, and that asking him again would be asking him to draw it wider for the convenience of a week, and this yard does not do that to a man about fifty-two at the end of a day that already went wrong on Wednesday; and that the reason the man of about twenty-nine was not asked which of his three things he thought was the important one is his own, and is that a man being asked to rank his own ears is being asked to be a bigger thing than he is, and that the only thing this yard wanted off him was an hour against each line and no name at the head of it; and that the reason nothing owed to anybody in that harbor was moved on that Friday is his own, and is that a bellwright nobody has asked for, a man who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names, a week of a room in a rent book, and a lamp oil figure chalked on a board at an end stall are all exactly where they stood at ten o'clock in the morning, and that a room four days down that coast is the first thing this yard has spent money on that is not in this harbor since the third of December, and that spending money in this harbor is the only thing this yard has ever done and it has just stopped doing it for four days; and that the reason the settlement of that commonwealth is not touched by anything said at that station is his own, and is that a man sitting in a box at three in the morning can hear three things and none of them is a condition, and that the condition came up that coast road in a cart in December, out of a port four days down this one and is on four lines in a record four hundred yards from this bench stone with an hour under each, and that nothing that happened on that coast this week touches it and nothing on that coast this week was allowed to.
