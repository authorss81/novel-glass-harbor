# Chapter 534

*Wednesday the Fifth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Shipyard on That Quay Destroyed Between About Ten and About Eleven in the Morning in About an Hour and Nobody in It, a Woman of About Fifty-Two Who Keeps a Fish Stall at the Head of That Quay the First to See It and Saying the Shape of It and Not Saying What She Thought It Was, Two Hundred Yards of It Standing Open to the Sky at About Eleven in the Morning With a Roof and a Bench and a Stock of Sawn Fir and a Set of Hours in It and Nothing Else, No Body Named and No Casualty Figure Printed and Nobody Named a Casualty and Nobody Asked, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking Twice Who Did It and Not Being Answered Either Time and Not Asking a Third Time, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud Once That He Is Not Going to Look and That a Day Asked for by Name Is Paid at Four in the Afternoon at the Figure in Chalk Whatever Is Standing on That Quay, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Forty-Nine Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Three Hundred and Eleven Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the fifth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in about two hundred yards of a shipyard on that quay.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at sixty thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out, being three hundred and seventy-eight marks, one shilling and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-nine thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being three hundred and eleven marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eleventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-four days. There are eleven pieces of a boat on the crown of that mole and they have been there since the eleventh, and a man of about sixty-one who repairs boats came down that mole at about eleven, looked at what was burning for about a minute, went up it again and said nothing to anybody, and nobody thanked him and nobody sent for him. A slip on the north side of that harbor is under water at the top of the flood and dry at half ebb and has been both since the autumn and nothing about it has changed. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-two days and nobody in this harbor knows which side of that yard he was on this morning because nobody in that yard knows he exists.**

---

**The first person in that harbor to see it was a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a fish stall at the head of that quay, and she saw it from her own step at about ten in the morning, and what she said about it when somebody asked her was the shape of it and not what she thought it was.**

"Smoke coming off the west end of that roof at about ten and not off the east end, and the east end is the end the flue is in and the west end is the end the bench is in, and I have been selling fish off that step for thirty-one years and I know which end of a thing that yard burns from. It went along the west wall toward the door. It did not go out through the roof. I want that in whatever anybody is going to write, because every one of you are going to come down here in a week and tell me it went up through the rafters and it did not, and I watched it from eight feet away with a tray of fish in my hand."

She put the tray down and then picked it up again and went back to selling fish, and she was not asked a second question and she was not thanked.

---

**And at about eleven in the morning there were about two hundred yards of it standing open to the sky on that quay, and nobody was in it at any hour of that morning, and about twenty people were standing on the quay outside it at a distance that the slater of about forty afterwards said was about the right distance and did not say why.**

It went in about an hour and the smoke had stopped coming off it by about half past eleven and what was left standing was the wall the flue runs in at the east end, the door frame, and about nine feet of the north wall, and the iron in the bench frames on the floor of it, which had not been in the fire because the fire had gone along the west wall and had not been under them long enough.

Nessa Pike, of about twenty-seven, came out of the gap at about eleven and stood about thirty feet off her own yard with her hands in her pockets, and she gave an account of what she knew in about forty words, and she gave it in that order and said that she was giving it in that order on purpose.

"A roof, a bench, a stock of sawn fir and a set of hours, and that is the whole of it and that is the order I am saying them in. **There was a boat in that building on Monday morning and there is not one in it now, because I hired a cart off the west basin on Saturday for it and paid for it out of my own pocket and it is not on that sheet and it is not going to be, and that boat is at the north quay with her bilge blocks under her and she is the only thing that was ever in that yard that somebody can point at.** What else was in it was a glue pot and a set of saws and eleven years of shavings and the two benches that were in that shed when I took it, and the two benches are ash and ash goes."

Nobody in that harbor has named anybody a casualty, no figure of any kind has been set against a person on that quay that morning, and nobody was hurt, and nobody has written a single number against a man who was not there.

---

**A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked who did it at about a quarter past eleven, and again at about half past eleven, and nobody in that yard or on that quay answered him either time, and he was not asked a third question by anybody and he did not ask one.**

"What I want to know is whether that was the line."

Nobody answered him and the answer he was given, which he gave himself, about twenty minutes later, out loud, to about four people, was this.

"Two hulls came in past the west quay on the middle of the flood on Monday night and went out again on the top of it and I was awake and I heard them and I have not written it down because nobody asked me and I am not going to be the one that starts a list of things that go past in the night. **That is all I have got and I have not got a name and I am not going to get one by asking a man at that stall with a tray of fish in her hand.**"

---

**And at about a quarter to twelve in the morning the man who keeps the leaf said one thing out loud on that quay, and said it once, and did not say it again that week.**

"I am not going to walk up that coast road and ask anybody anything, and I am not going to walk down it either, and I want it written down that nobody in this yard is going to do that today. Not because a yard is a small thing. **Because I have four men on a wage and a bench to make and a sheet that anybody with a leaf and a pencil can check, and a man who goes looking is a man who is not making the bench, and the bench is a thing four people are going to be under in eight days and a question is going to be under nobody's feet at all.** That slate with fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence on it is still on that bench stone and I have not burned it and I am not burning it, and the day I burn it is the day this harbor stops being able to read this thing."

Nobody in that yard agreed with him and nobody disagreed with him and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him who he thought it was.

---

**The four men were paid at about four in the afternoon at the figure in chalk, one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece for a day asked for by name, with the water on that quay as flat as it had been on Tuesday, and they were paid before anybody had finished looking at what was in it.**

A man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said out loud in front of about nine people that he did not think it was right to pay four men for a day when there was nothing to mend, and the man who keeps the leaf said no in about five words and then said the rest of it once.

"**a day's work is a day that a man worked.** There is a figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall and that figure has not moved since the first of December and it is not going to move on a Wednesday because a building is standing open to the sky. You were all on that slip this morning at ten and I do not care what was standing at the end of it. Sixty-eight pounds of nothing and four men, and the four men are the reason this sheet is checkable."

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him for the rest of it.

---

**AND AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID THE SHORTFALL OUT LOUD AT THAT BENCH STONE AND IT DID NOT MOVE AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

"Forty-nine thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being three hundred and eleven marks, eight shillings and twopence. **Unmoved since ten o'clock on Monday morning, and it is going to be unmoved at half past five this afternoon, and I know what is two hundred yards from that bench stone and I have not put a penny of it there, because the day the work is done is not today.** A roof and a bench and a stock of sawn fir and a set of hours. That is the whole of what that is and that is the order it is in, and a man who wants to write it as anything else is writing about something that is not on this quay."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Wednesday, and nobody was thanked for the yard and nobody was thanked for the four men being paid.

---

**A SHIPYARD ON THAT QUAY WENT UP IN ABOUT AN HOUR BETWEEN ABOUT TEN AND ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND NOBODY WAS IN IT AT ANY HOUR OF THAT MORNING AND NOBODY WAS HURT AND NOBODY HAS NAMED ANYBODY A CASUALTY. A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO WHO KEEPS A FISH STALL AT THE HEAD OF THAT QUAY SAW IT FROM HER OWN STEP AT ABOUT TEN AND SAID THAT IT WENT ALONG THE WEST WALL TOWARD THE DOOR AND DID NOT GO OUT THROUGH THE ROOF, AND WAS NOT ASKED A SECOND QUESTION AND WAS NOT THANKED. THE BOAT THAT WAS IN THAT BUILDING ON MONDAY MORNING WAS NOT IN IT ON WEDNESDAY BECAUSE IT WENT OUT ON SATURDAY ON A CART HIRED OFF THE WEST BASIN AND PAID FOR OUT OF THE OWNER'S OWN POCKET AND IT IS AT THE NORTH QUAY WITH HER BILGE BLOCKS UNDER HER. THE FOUR MEN OF THAT YARD WERE PAID AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK. THE FIGURE IN CHALK DID NOT MOVE. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-NINE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN MARKS, EIGHT SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fifth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that a shipyard on that quay was destroyed between about ten and about half past eleven in the morning in about an hour, and that nobody was in it at any hour of that morning and nobody was hurt and no body has been named and no figure of any kind has been set against a person, and that nobody has been named a casualty; and that a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a fish stall at the head of that quay saw it from her own step at about ten in the morning and said that the smoke came off the west end and not off the east end and that the east end is the end the flue is in and that it went along the west wall toward the door and did not go out through the roof, and that she was not asked a second question and was not thanked; and that about twenty people stood on that quay at about eleven and that about two hundred yards of it stood open to the sky, and that what was left standing was the wall the flue runs in at the east end, the door frame and about nine feet of the north wall, and that the iron in the bench frames on that floor did not go; and that the person whose yard it is said out loud that the whole of it is a roof, a bench, a stock of sawn fir and a set of hours and that she was giving that account in that order on purpose, and that a boat was in that building on Monday morning and was not in it on Wednesday because it went out on Saturday on a cart hired off the west basin and paid for out of her own pocket and is not on that sheet and is not going to be, and that it is at the north quay with her bilge blocks under her, and that the two benches that were in that shed when she took it are ash and ash goes; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked who did it at about a quarter past eleven and again at about half past eleven and was not answered either time and did not ask a third time, and that nobody in that yard and nobody on that quay answered him and that no name of any man has been said out loud on that quay that day; and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once on that quay that he was not going to look for anybody, and gave the reason that he has four men on a wage and a bench to make and a sheet anybody with a leaf and a pencil can check, and that the slate carrying fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence is still on that bench stone and is not going to be burned until the work is done or not at all; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at sixty thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-nine thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being three hundred and eleven marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Tuesday; and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said out loud in front of about nine people that it was not right to pay four men for a day when there was nothing to mend, and was told that a day's work is a day that a man worked and that the figure in chalk had not moved since the first of December and was not going to move because a building is standing open to the sky; and that the four men of that yard were paid at about four in the afternoon at the figure in chalk and were paid before anybody had finished looking at what was in it; and that there are eleven pieces of a boat on the crown of that mole and a man of about sixty-one who repairs boats came down that mole at about eleven and looked at what was burning for about a minute and went up it again and said nothing and was not thanked.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that no name of any man was said out loud on that quay all day is the man who keeps the leaf's own and is not a law, and is that a name said once in a market about a thing that has happened at ten in the morning is a name said to about two hundred people by the middle of the afternoon, and that this harbor has four crews that wrote no name in December and nobody has asked them since, and that a place that starts asking is a place that starts keeping; and that the reason he did not go and look is his own and was not argued with by anybody, and is that a yard has one thing it can hand a stranger and it is a figure with a day against it, and that a man who spends a morning walking a coast road has nothing to hand anybody at the end of it and a man who spends a morning on a bench has a bench; and that the reason that figure of four hundred and fifty pence is not on that quay this morning is hers, and is that a lamp oil figure is what a yard is short of and not what it has lost, and that the two got confused for about ten minutes at the front of that market by a woman of about fifty-two and that nobody corrected her in front of the fish; and that the reason four men were paid for that Wednesday is the reader's own and is that a man who takes a wage on a morning is owed that morning whether or not there is anything to mend, and that a yard that stops paying on a bad day is a yard that starts asking a man to carry the bad day himself, and that this one has never asked a man to carry a day and is not going to; and that the reason the cost of that building was given in the order a roof, a bench, a stock of sawn fir and a set of hours is her own, and is that the other order is a shed, and a shed is a thing with a door, and a door on a Wednesday morning at about eleven is a thing that about twenty people in this harbor will walk into today and that every one of them will think of differently and that no page in this yard is answerable for what twenty people think in a yard; and that the reason not one item in that harbor was paid forward, cut back, carried, bettered or shut on that Wednesday is his own, and is that a fire is not an argument, and that the only number that moved in this harbor between ten o'clock in the morning and four o'clock in the afternoon was one hundred and sixty-eight pence going out of a shed for four men, and that the figure in chalk does not care what a shed looks like on the morning it is paid, and that this harbor has had nineteen mornings like that one since the eighteenth of November and has paid all nineteen.
