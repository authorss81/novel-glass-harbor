# Chapter 543

*Friday the Fourteenth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Three Crews Leaving a Quay Four Days Down That Coast at About Nine and Ten in the Evening in Three Boats and Not One of Them in the Same Boat as Anybody From Another of the Three, Three Bells on Three Marks and Three Written Windows and No Man at the Top of Any of Them, the First Bell Going at About Half Past Eleven and the First Man Over the Rail at About Half Past Eleven and the Last Helmet off at About One o'Clock, the Second Bell Going at About Ten Past One Which Is Inside a Quarter of an Hour of One o'Clock and the Last Helmet off at About Half Past Two, the Third Bell Going at About Twenty to Three and the Last Helmet off at About Five to Four, the Cutoff Pulled Twice Without Anybody Asking and an Ascent Asked for and Not Argued With, the Man of About Thirty-Two Third Down at the First Point and Not the Head of It, a Woman of About Thirty-Four of That Commonwealth the Named Tender on the Surface at the Third Point and Not in the Water at Any Hour, Nine Boats Inside That Line on That Ebb and Seven of Them Back Before Six in the Morning and Two of Them Found on the Ground off the Outer Water on the Morning of the Fifteenth, Both Holed Under the Forefoot and Both Empty and Both Crews Ashore by About Six and Not One of Them Hurt, a Fourth Mark in That Water That Is Not One of the Five on the Page and Is a Piece of Lead in a Hole in a Stone, Nothing Going Onto the Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going Into It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Seventy Thousand Four Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Four Hundred and Forty Marks, Four Shillings and Tenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the fourteenth day of January, at a port four days down that coast, in a market with a floor to it, on a quay with eleven boats lying against it, and in about four fathoms of water off three separate points that are about a mile apart.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on the cistern of that market on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at Morrow Quay at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and the man who keeps the leaf was four days down that coast at both of those hours and did not see either of them, and the day is a day and the figure in chalk has not moved since the first of December. There are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at eighty-one thousand and ninety-eight pence out, being five hundred and six marks, eleven shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is seventy thousand four hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being four hundred and forty marks, four shillings and tenpence. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that port could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twentieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-three days. There were eleven boats lying against that quay on the Friday evening and eleven were lying against it on the Saturday morning and two of the eleven came back in on the Saturday afternoon with their gear in the bottom and no gear on the deck. A store on the second lay of that port has had its shutter up since the middle of December and a woman of about forty-four who keeps it has told two people that she is not going to take it down until there is something to sell, and the two people are her own hands. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-one days and he was in a hut of tarred board on a shoulder above a street of about forty houses from about eight in the evening until about three in the morning on that Friday, and nobody in three harbors has said a word to him about it.**

---

**The three crews went off that quay at about nine o'clock in the evening and at about ten, in three boats, and not one man of the first of them was in the same boat as a man of the second or of the third, and no man of the second was in the same boat as a man of the third, and the three boats left from three different parts of that quay and not one of them left from the same slip.**

There were eight men in the water that night and three named tenders on the surface. Ivo Sark, of about twenty-four, one of a master's two men, was in a dinghy about nine feet off the first point with his own hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave, and he was not in that water at any hour. A pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors, who is in nobody's crew and is a member of nothing, was in a dinghy about nine feet off the second point with his own hand on a chain weight, and he was not in that water at any hour. A woman of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was in a boat of her own off the third point and was not in that water at any hour and her condition is still on the face of a record four hundred yards from that bench stone at Morrow Quay and is still not settled and is still not agreeable.

Every one of the eight had a reason written on a slate in his own words with the hour against it and a figure of twenty-five minutes, and every one of the eight had a separate thirty feet of three-inch on its own weight, and the only line in that water that was anybody's route was the line of the mark and the ground tackle to it, and that was the line of the crew of that port and not of anybody in those three boats. The man who keeps the leaf was not the tender at any of the three and said out loud at about nine o'clock in the evening, in front of about eleven people on that quay, that he was not.

**There was one man in the whole of that night who was in a position to tell any of the other seven what to do, and he did not use it, and there is no page anywhere with his name at the top of it.**

---

**The bell on the first mark was rung at about half past eleven by the crew that answers for that mark, and the first man went over the rail at about half past eleven, and the bell was not rung again.**

The first man was the man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay and whose name is at the top of the first of the three windows. He was over the rail at about half past eleven and on the bottom at about thirty-five minutes past eleven and came up without asking for anything, and his helmet came off at about twenty-five minutes past twelve, being four minutes going down and nineteen on that floor and two coming up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five.

The second was a man of about forty-one off the west quay of that port. He was over the rail at about one o'clock in the morning and on the bottom at about five minutes past one, and **at about twenty-four minutes past one the tender called for the cutoff because that man had said nothing for two minutes, and the cutoff was pulled without anybody asking anybody's leave, and the word came about four seconds after it was called for and was not asked for twice, and his helmet came off at about twenty-five minutes past one, being five minutes down and eighteen on that floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five.** He was not hurt and nobody has named him a casualty and no figure is set against him and he was not thanked.

The third was the man of about thirty-two. He was over the rail at about twenty-five minutes to one and on that floor at about twenty minutes to one and his helmet came off at about one o'clock, being four minutes down and nineteen on the floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five. He did not ask to come up and the cutoff was not pulled on him and he said nothing about it afterwards to anybody in any boat.

**Nobody went into that water twice that night, no two men were in it at once at any of the three, nobody went in at the top of that flood, and the top of that flood was about eleven o'clock and the first man went over the rail about half an hour after it.**

---

**A boat of about eleven tons went inside that line at about ten to two in the morning, with victual in her and six men in her, and she was inside the line in about twenty minutes and out of it again before three.**

---

**The bell on the second mark was rung at about ten past one in the morning, which is inside a quarter of an hour of one o'clock, which is the hour the first window closed, and that is the condition a pump hand of about forty-four asked out loud for on the Thursday afternoon and was answered, and it was the first thing in eight days that anybody in that port had asked for and had been given in writing before it happened.**

The first man at the second point was over the rail at about twenty past one and on the bottom at about twenty-five past one and his helmet came off at about twenty to two, being four minutes down and nineteen on that floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five.

The second was over the rail at about five past two and on that floor at about ten past two, **and at about twenty-five minutes to three he asked out loud for his ascent to be called, and the tender called it, and nobody argued with him about it, and his helmet came off at about half past two, being five minutes down and nineteen on that floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five.** He came up and asked in the boat for a cup of water and for nothing else, and he was given a cup of water, and no figure is set against him and he was not thanked.

---

**The bell on the third mark was rung at about twenty to three in the morning and the last helmet off that night came off at about five to four, and the top of the low water was about five o'clock and nobody was in that water at any hour near it.**

The first was over at about twenty to three and on the bottom at about a quarter to three and his helmet came off at about five past three, being five minutes down and eighteen on that floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five. The second was over at about ten past three and on that floor at about a quarter past three, and **at about twenty-four minutes past three the tender called for the cutoff because that man had said nothing for two minutes, and the cutoff was pulled without anybody asking, and the word came about four seconds after it was called for, and his helmet came off at about thirty-five minutes past three, being four minutes down and nineteen on that floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five.**

The third was over the rail at about twenty to four and on that floor at about a quarter to four and his helmet came off at about five to four, being four minutes down and nineteen on that floor and two up, which is twenty-five of his twenty-five. He was a man of about nineteen of the second lay and he was paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day in advance on the day before, out of a berth fund that pays a day for a day, and his name is in a book at Morrow Quay and is not on any sheet in any hand, and nobody in that port knew what he had been paid and he did not say.

---

**Nine boats went inside that line on that ebb and seven of them were back inside the line before six o'clock in the morning, and the two that were not back were found on the morning of the fifteenth lying on the ground off the outer water, both holed under the forefoot, and both of them empty.**

Their crews were in the other boats and were on that quay at about six in the morning, and not one of them was hurt, and no body is named, and no figure of any person is printed here, and nobody has been made a casualty of anything. **A lost boat is a boat.** One of the two was a boat of about fourteen tons and one was a boat of about nine tons, and both were of that port and both had victual in them, and both had a man in them on the Friday evening, and both of those men were on that quay at six in the morning and neither of them said anything about it to anybody who wrote anything down.

There was a fourth mark in that water. It is not one of the five on the page, and it was not on the page of hours, and it is a piece of lead in a hole in a stone under about four fathoms, and it was put in by somebody, and nobody at that port has a name for whoever put it in, and nobody asked for one at six o'clock in the morning and nobody has asked since. Both of those boats were over it at some time between four and six and neither of them saw it, and one of the two men in the boat of about nine tons has said since that he was looking at a bell and not at the water, and that is a thing a man says about himself and not about anybody else.

---

**A man of about twenty-nine sat in a hut of tarred board on a shoulder above a street of about forty houses from about eight in the evening until about three in the morning on that Friday, and he wrote down what he could hear on the back of a sheet out of a book, and he gave his account of it to nobody on that night and he gave it to a man of about thirty-three who hires hands at that port at about ten o'clock on the Saturday morning in about forty words, and that man wrote it down and did not read it out.**

"Three bells. One at about half past eleven, one at about ten past one, one at about twenty to three, and I can tell the gaps and I cannot tell the distance and I could not tell you whose they were if you had them in a list in front of me. There is a pump at the third house up that road that goes at about eleven and I heard it go and I heard it stop and I have still never been up that road. And the third thing is me, and it is the same as it was on Tuesday, and I have not written it down again."

**Nobody in that port said one word about any of that which made him anything. No figure was set against it, in that port or on any sheet, and there is not going to be one. He is not a member of anything, he did not ask anybody for anything, and the man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud and once that he was going to write the three hours down and was not going to write the rest of it, and wrote the three hours.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon, with the door of a room over the floor of that market open, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud in that room, and about fourteen people were in it and about five of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and nobody in that port was asked for the rest of it.**

"Seventy thousand four hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being four hundred and forty marks, four shillings and tenpence. It did not move today. **Four thousand six hundred and eight pence went out of that sheet yesterday to arrange one night, and not one penny of it has been paid, and the two boats on the bottom of that water this morning are not on that sheet either, and they will be, and I will say out loud why when I write them on.**"

"And nine boats went inside that line last night on an ebb and seven of them came back, and this yard had nothing to do with any of the nine and no name of this yard is at the top of any of the three pages and nobody in that port was asked for a penny of anything, and I have been four days down this coast for two of them and I did not sleep in a bed on the Wednesday and I am not going to be thanked for that and it was not done for that."

Nobody in that port was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that port to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Friday, and nobody was thanked for the eight men and the three tenders and the two cutoffs and the nine boats and the two boats that came back in with their gear in the bottom.

---

**THREE CREWS WENT OUT OF THAT PORT IN THREE BOATS AND NOT ONE MAN OF ANY OF THEM WAS IN THE SAME BOAT AS A MAN OF ANOTHER OF THEM, AND EIGHT MEN WENT INTO ABOUT FOUR FATHOMS AT THREE POINTS THAT ARE ABOUT A MILE APART ON THE NIGHT OF THE FOURTEENTH, ONE AT A TIME, AND NOBODY WENT IN TWICE AND NOBODY WENT IN AT THE TOP OF THE FLOOD. THE WHOLE OF THE COORDINATION WAS THREE WRITTEN WINDOWS AND A BELL RUNG AT AN HOUR ON EACH OF THREE MARKS BY THE CREW THAT ANSWERS FOR THAT MARK, AND THERE WAS NO NAME AT THE TOP OF ANY OF THE THREE. THE CUTOFF WAS PULLED TWICE WITHOUT ANYBODY ASKING AND AN ASCENT WAS ASKED FOR AND WAS NOT ARGUED WITH. NINE BOATS WENT INSIDE THAT LINE AND SEVEN CAME BACK AND TWO DID NOT, AND BOTH OF THOSE TWO WERE FOUND ON THE GROUND OFF THE OUTER WATER ON THE MORNING OF THE FIFTEENTH, AND BOTH CREWS WERE ASHORE BY SIX AND NOT ONE MAN OF THEM WAS HURT. NOTHING WENT ONTO THAT SHEET IN ANY HAND AND NOTHING WENT INTO IT. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL AT MORROW QUAY DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT ON ACCOUNT OF A NIGHT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SEVENTY THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY MARKS, FOUR SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the fourteenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in a room over the floor of a market four days down that coast: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe and that no charge was placed on the cistern of that market on it; and that three crews went off that quay at about nine in the evening and about ten in three boats, and that not one man of the first of them was in the same boat as a man of the second or of the third and the three boats left from three different parts of that quay; and that eight men went into about four fathoms at three points about a mile apart between about half past eleven at night and about five to four in the morning, one at a time, and that the first point was worked between about half past eleven and about one o'clock by three men, being about half past eleven to about twenty-five minutes past twelve, about one o'clock to about twenty-five minutes past one and about twenty-five minutes to one to about one o'clock; and that the second point was worked between about twenty past one and about half past two by two men, being about twenty past one to about twenty to two and about five past two to about half past two; and that the third point was worked between about twenty to three and about five to four by three men, being about twenty to three to about five past three, about ten past three to about thirty-five minutes past three and about twenty to four to about five to four; and that each of the eight was four or five minutes going down and eighteen or nineteen on that floor and two coming up and twenty-five of his twenty-five, and that the top of that flood was about eleven o'clock and the top of the low water about five o'clock and nobody was in that water at either; and that the cutoff was called for and pulled without anybody asking at about twenty-four minutes past one at the first point and at about twenty-four minutes past three at the third point, and that the word came about four seconds after it was called for in each case, and that one man at the second point asked out loud for his ascent at about twenty-five minutes to three and it was called and nobody argued with him about it; and that Ivo Sark, of about twenty-four, was the named tender on the surface at the first point in a dinghy and not in that water at any hour, that a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors was the named tender on the surface at the second point and is in nobody's crew, and that a woman of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was the named tender on the surface at the third point in a boat of her own and was not in that water at any hour, and that the man who keeps the leaf was not the tender at any of the three and said so out loud in front of about eleven people on that quay; and that the bell on the first mark was rung at about half past eleven and was not rung again, that the bell on the second mark was rung at about ten past one which is inside a quarter of an hour of one o'clock, and that the bell on the third mark was rung at about twenty to three; and that a boat of about eleven tons went inside that line at about ten to two in the morning with victual in her and six men in her and was out of it again before three; and that nine boats went inside that line on that ebb and seven of them were back inside the line before six in the morning, and that two of them were found on the morning of the fifteenth lying on the ground off the outer water, both holed under the forefoot and both empty, and that both of their crews were on that quay at about six in the morning and not one of them was hurt and no body is named and no figure of any person is printed and nobody was made a casualty; and that there was a fourth mark in that water which is not one of the five on the page and is a piece of lead in a hole in a stone under about four fathoms, and that nobody at that port has a name for whoever put it in and nobody asked for one; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing went into it and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning at Morrow Quay and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon and the man who keeps the leaf was four days down that coast at both of those hours; and that that sheet stood at eighty-one thousand and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is seventy thousand four hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being four hundred and forty marks, four shillings and tenpence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and seventy-six, one thousand two hundred and fifty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and seventy-five, one thousand two hundred and fifty-two; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in a room over the floor of a market four days down that coast, and no name is against it: that the reason he did not use the one thing he had on that night is his own and he has not written a better reason than that, and is that a man who is in one boat and is told what is happening in another boat will be a man in two boats by midnight whatever he says in the dark, and that the only way eight men who have never worked together stay eight men who have never worked together is that they stay in three places and get told nothing; and that the reason the two boats are not on that sheet tonight is his own, and is that a sheet which carries what went out and does not carry what that going out put on the ground is a sheet with a hole in it the shape of a Tuesday, and that he would rather be short by two bills this month than be short by nothing and be a man with a hole in his own book; and that the reason the bell at the second mark was rung at ten past one and not at half past twelve is the pump hand's, and is that he asked on a Thursday afternoon in front of eleven people for a thing that could be said out loud in a room and got it, and that a man who asks for a quarter of an hour and is given a quarter of an hour has been paid in a currency that does not exist anywhere else on that coast and that is the whole of the thanks he is getting; and that the reason the man of about twenty-nine's account of that night is three bells and a pump and himself and nothing else is a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, and is that the third of those three is not his to write down and not anybody's, and that a page with a man's own breathing on it in his own handwriting is a page that will be argued with in ten years by people who were not there; and that the reason nothing standing in that harbor or in that port was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Friday is his own, and is that a line of nine hulls outside a mole at Morrow Quay is still a line of nine hulls outside that mole this afternoon, and that the only thing that moved anywhere on that coast in eight hours was two boats onto a stone, and that both of those are boats.*
