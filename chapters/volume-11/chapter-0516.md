# Chapter 516

*Saturday the Eighteenth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Two Days of That Second Pair of Hands Written Into the Total at the Foot of the Sheet at About Ten in the Morning, Being Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Two Marks, One Shilling and Fourpence, With the Sixteenth of December Against Them and the Eighteenth on the Face of the Line as the Day the Total Moved, a Boat of About Fifteen Tons out of the Reed Channels Coming Over the Bar at the Back of That Mole on the Top of the Flood at About Half Past Two in the Afternoon and Not Being Stopped, About a Dozen People in That Harbor Standing and Looking at Her Coming Through, and Nobody in That Harbor Having a Figure of How Many Boats Have Come Through in Ten Days and Nobody Having Asked for One, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Thirty-Five Thousand Three Hundred and Forty-Six Pence, Being Two Hundred and Twenty Marks, Twelve Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Nobody Thanked*

Saturday the eighteenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. The two days of that second pair of hands were written into the total at the foot of the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning, being three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixteenth of December against them, being the day those two men were asked for and the day they began, and the eighteenth of December is on the face of that line as the day the total at the foot of that sheet moved, and those two days are two different days and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take either of them off it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-five thousand nine hundred and eighty-six pence out, being two hundred and eighty-seven marks, five shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-five thousand three hundred and forty-six pence short, being two hundred and twenty marks, twelve shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fourteen days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixteen; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twentieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-six days. The fire party's hose in that harbor is dead and will be dead until the spring, and the butt behind that market is what the cistern is filled out of. A float of tarred fir about five feet across is lying in about four fathoms off the head of that third lay with a cast bell under a board four sticks high and forty fathoms of two-inch stuff running down to a stone with a ring in it, and it is going over at about a foot an hour on the flood and the bell is not struck at any hour of any day. A crate came up in the middle of November with the batten back across its lid and it is in the forepeak of a boat lying off that third lay, and nobody in that harbor has been shown what is in it and nothing has been taken out of it since. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and nobody in that harbor has said anything to him about it this week.**

---

**At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf wrote the two days into the total at the foot of that sheet, and said the figure out loud and the day it moved and the day it is for, and he did not say the rule again, because he said on Thursday that he was not going to say it a third time this month.**

"Three hundred and thirty-six pence into the total at the foot of that sheet, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixteenth of December against the line, being the day they were asked for and the day they began, and today is the day the total moved. Those two days are in because the two men worked them and they have been paid for them at four o'clock yesterday, and the sixteenth is on the face of that line and the eighteenth is on the face of that line, and that is the whole of the difference between those two days and it is there on purpose."

Somebody at the end of that group said that a sheet with two days on a line was a sheet a stranger would have to think about, and the man who keeps the leaf said that a stranger with a pencil could take the sixteenth off it and the eighteenth off it and the figure off it in about four seconds and that thinking about it was the stranger's work and not his.

The two men took their day at the third lay at half past three on the Thursday and again on the Friday, and they were not thanked for it and they are not on that sheet and their names are in the book the days are paid out of and not anywhere else.

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon, on the top of the flood, a boat of about fifteen tons out of the reed channels came over the bar at the back of that mole and in, and nobody stopped her.**

She was a lighter with about forty tons of reed mats and two hundred bundles of withy on her deck and a woman of about fifty at the tiller who has made that passage for about thirty years and a man of about thirty-five and a boy poling at her head, and the top of that flood was at about twenty past two and she was over the bar at about half past two with about four foot of water on it, and the water inside that bar is a mile and a half long and is deep enough to take anything that floats in it.

**About a dozen people in that harbor stood and looked at her coming through, and they stood on the third lay and on the top of that mole and on the boards at the back of that market, and none of them said anything while she was coming.**

The nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole lay where they had lain since the third of December and did not turn a board while a boat of about fifteen tons came in over a bar with four foot on it at the top of the flood.

The woman at the end stall with the slate said one thing to the man beside her, and the man beside her repeated it to the woman on his other side, and by about ten past three about nine people in that market were saying it.

"She comes in on the top of the flood like she has done every year of my life."

**And a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said what he said about it, and what he said was about the water and not about anything else, and it is the whole of what was said in that harbor that afternoon.**

"There is four foot on that bar at the top of the flood and there is a mile and a half of water behind it. If you want to come in from the south you come in on the top of the flood and if you want to come in from the west you come in on the ebb because that bar fills with the tide on the west side and it blows a standing sea over it in anything from the south-west."

Somebody asked him how many of them had come through in the last ten days, and he said he had not counted, and a man of about thirty-three said that nobody in that market had counted, and a woman of about thirty-one who crews nothing and sells nothing said that if a man asked her she would have said she had been here for four years and had stopped counting things in her first winter, and nobody in that market asked her again and nobody went and got a figure.

**Nobody in that harbor has a figure of how many other boats have come through in ten days, and nobody has asked for one, and there is no count of them in that market, in that yard, or on any page in that town.**

Nobody of this party went aboard her and nobody of this party sent anything on her and she lay at the south quay from about three o'clock with her mats stacked and the withy bundles under a tarpaulin, and she sailed out again on the ebb at about five o'clock with the tarpaulin wet and nobody in that harbor having spoken to her master.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone, and the day was a Saturday and the water was as flat as it has been all week and the man who keeps the leaf's wage is four marks and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag.**

"Thirty-five thousand three hundred and forty-six pence short, being two hundred and twenty marks, twelve shillings and twopence. It moved this morning by three hundred and thirty-six pence and that is two men for two days and it is the last line in that book that is a day of hands on that lay. There is no line on that sheet for a boat of about fifteen tons coming in over a bar at the back of that mole and there is not going to be one, and I want it understood in that market that that is not because it was cheap and it is not because it was dear."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for anything on that day.

---

**A BOAT OF ABOUT FIFTEEN TONS CAME IN OVER THE BAR AT THE BACK OF THAT MOLE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ON THE TOP OF THE FLOOD WITH FORTY TONS OF REED MATS AND TWO HUNDRED BUNDLES OF WITHY ON HER DECK AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY AT THE TILLER, AND SHE LAY AT THE SOUTH QUAY UNTIL FIVE O'CLOCK AND WENT OUT ON THE EBB. ABOUT A DOZEN PEOPLE STOOD AND LOOKED AT HER COMING THROUGH AND NONE OF THEM SAID ANYTHING WHILE SHE WAS COMING. NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS A FIGURE OF HOW MANY BOATS HAVE COME THROUGH IN TEN DAYS AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR ONE. A CRATE WITH THE BATTEN BACK ACROSS ITS LID IS IN A FOREPEAK OFF THAT THIRD LAY AND NOTHING HAS COME OUT OF IT SINCE THE MIDDLE OF NOVEMBER. A FIRE PARTY'S HOSE IS DEAD. A LINE WENT INTO THE TOTAL AT THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET THIS MORNING AND THE SIXTEENTH OF DECEMBER IS ON THE FACE OF IT AND SO IS THE EIGHTEENTH. THAT SHEET IS THIRTY-FIVE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the eighteenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the two days of that second pair of hands were written into the total at the foot of the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning, being three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixteenth of December against the line and the eighteenth of December on the face of that line as the day the total moved, and that those two days are two different days and both are on the face of the line; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-five thousand nine hundred and eighty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-five thousand three hundred and forty-six pence short, being two hundred and twenty marks, twelve shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by three hundred and thirty-six pence on that day; and that a boat of about fifteen tons out of the reed channels came over the bar at the back of that mole on the top of the flood at about half past two in the afternoon and was not stopped by anybody in that harbor, and that about a dozen people stood and looked at her coming through and none of them said anything while she was coming, and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said that there is four foot on that bar at the top of the flood and a mile and a half of water behind it and that what she does is a thing about water; and that nobody in that harbor has a figure of how many other boats have come through in ten days and nobody has asked for one and no such count is kept in that market, in that yard or on any page in that town; and that nobody of this party went aboard her and nothing of this yard went on her; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and forty-nine, one thousand and one hundred and nineteen, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and forty-eight, one thousand and one hundred and seventeen.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason no figure of how many boats came through in ten days was taken and no count was begun is this yard's own mouth, and is that a number of that kind is the first figure on a page that turns into a line of boats, and that a harbor which has counted its own comings and goings this winter is a harbor that can be shown a number and asked to explain it; and that the reason no line went onto that sheet for that boat is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a page which carries what other people have managed to get in is a page about the weather and not about this yard, and that a woman of about fifty at a tiller has been doing that passage for thirty years and has never been a line in anybody's book and is not going to be one this week; and that the reason the sixteenth and the eighteenth are both on the face of that line is his own, and is that the day a thing was done and the day it was paid for are two facts and a page that keeps one of them has lost the other, and that the two men whose days those are could be asked about either of them; and that the reason a Saturday was not treated as a day off is this yard's own mouth, and is that a day asked for by name is paid on a Saturday and a day nobody asked for is not worked, and that this harbor has four men whose days are asked for by name and that is the whole of the working week in that shed; and that the reason this yard moved no standing figure in that harbor on that Saturday, and struck none of them and closed none of them, is this yard's own mouth, and is that a boat getting in over a bar is a fact about that bar and not about any account in that town, and that this yard put every standing figure in that harbor back where it stood that day and did not shorten one for a kind hour and a half.*
