# Chapter 547

*Tuesday the Eighteenth Day of January, the Sixty-Fifth Wage Tuesday, the Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Read Out on the Step at the Back of That Market at Ten o'Clock in the Morning in Front of About Forty People of Whom About Twenty Cannot Read the Column on the Lid and Not Moving, Nobody Paid a Penny Out of That Chest, a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Being the Sixty-Fifth, a Boat of About Nine Tons Found on the Ground off the Outer Water Four Days Down That Coast on the Morning of the Fifteenth Priced in Hours and Material at About Ten o'Clock on the Said Tuesday at Seven Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty-Two Pence, Being Forty-Eight Marks and Six Shillings, With the Fourteenth Against It and Settled on the Eighteenth, That Price Being Five Figures and a Sum and a Stranger With a Leaf and a Pencil Able to Take the Day Off It and Find the Fifteenth, the Man of About Thirty-One Who Owns That Boat Asking Out Loud in Front of About Nine People When He Is Going to Be Paid and Being Told That the Sheet Is a Record and Not a Purse, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Eighty Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Eight Pence, Being Five Hundred and Five Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, the Lamp Oil Board Still at Four Hundred and Fifty Pence With the First of June Above It, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the eighteenth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shipyard on that quay with a wall going up on it that has not had a door in it since the fifth of this month.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday of the shed and was the sixty-fifth wage Tuesday of that harbor. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the sixty-fifth. One line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at seven thousand seven hundred and fifty-two pence, being forty-eight marks and six shillings, with the fourteenth of January against it and settled on the eighteenth. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at ninety-one thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out, being five hundred and seventy-two marks, one shilling and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is eighty thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being five hundred and five marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-seven days. The lamp oil board at the back of that end stall is at four hundred and fifty pence with the first of June written above it, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence has not come down that board since the twenty-ninth day of August and has not been argued with since the sixth of December. A man of about forty-six who sells ice out of a barrow has asked that clerk on the west basin out loud in front of about forty people, on the fourth of this month, whether the column had moved since November, and was told that it had not, and said November twice in the same tone, and went back to his barrow. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-five days and he was on the west quay for about three hours on the Monday with two men and a handline and came in with about thirty fish in it, and nobody in that harbor has said a word to him about it and he did not say a word to anybody.**

---

**At about ten o'clock in the morning a clerk of that harbor authority read that column out on the step at the back of that market and put his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading, and about forty people were on the boards in front of that step and about twenty of them cannot read the column on the lid, and the column did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four and nobody asked to have it opened, and the sixty-fifth week stands fallen due at the close of that day.**

---

**And at about ten o'clock in the morning, at the same hour and about four hundred yards away, the person whose yard it is priced a boat, and she said every figure out loud as she wrote it, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it, and the whole of the price was said before a figure went against that line.**

"A boat of about nine tons, found on the ground off the outer water on the morning of the fifteenth with a hole under her forefoot. **This is what it costs to have her again, and it is a bill and it is not a wound, and there are no men in it.**"

1. *A new hull. Twenty fathoms of two-inch at two hundred and twenty pence a fathom. Four thousand four hundred pence, being twenty-seven marks, six shillings and eightpence.*
2. *A mast and a boom. Four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks exactly.*
3. *A warp and about thirty fathoms of line. One thousand one hundred and twenty pence, being seven marks exactly.*
4. *Paint and oil out of the store at the back of that yard. Two hundred and forty pence, being one mark, six shillings and eightpence.*
5. *Nine days of my own hours on that hull at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day. One thousand five hundred and twelve pence, being nine marks and six shillings.*

"That is seven thousand seven hundred and fifty-two pence, being forty-eight marks and six shillings, and it is the sum of those five and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can add them and get the same figure. **The fourteenth of January is against that line, being the day the boat was lost, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon, and the two days are both on the face of the line, being the day it happened and the day it was written down, and a man can take either of them off it.**"

She said one more thing about it, once, and did not say it again.

"And that is not the figure for the other one. **There is a boat of about fourteen tons on that ground as well and she is bigger and she is not priced yet, and she will be priced on Thursday and she will be a different number, and I am not going to let anybody in this yard put a figure on Thursday by adding to this one.**"

---

**And the man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay and owns the boat of about nine tons himself stood at the end of that slip at about half past ten in the morning and asked out loud, in front of about nine people, when he is going to be paid, and he was answered in about thirty words and did not ask it a second time.**

He said that he had not asked anybody for anything and that he was not asking anybody for anything and that he wanted to know which of two things was true, being that a boat gets built or that a figure gets written down.

"Neither today. **That sheet is a record and not a purse, and a record is worth something and a purse is worth something else, and the thing that is worth something to you about a record is that it is portable: any man with a leaf and a pencil can take seven thousand seven hundred and fifty-two off that slip and carry it up this coast to a mill, a chandler, a yard, or a man with a boat, and every one of those five will know what it is for and none of them needs to know a thing about you.** I have not got that money. I am short by eighty thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence and I have not asked this harbor for a penny of it and I am not going to, and that figure is on that sheet in my own mouth this afternoon and you can take it with you."

He said that was fair and that he had not expected the money and that he had wanted to know which of the two things was true, and that if it was a figure then he could work with a figure, and he went off up the quay and was not thanked and was not asked for anything.

---

**And a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked out loud in that yard at about one o'clock in the afternoon whether those two bills were the price of the night, and he was answered in about eleven words, and he did not ask it again.**

"Two of the prices. **Not all of them, and the ones that are not on that sheet are not on it because nobody in this yard has a figure for them and I have not gone looking for one, and that is the correct way round and not the lazy way round.**"

He said that was what he thought, and that a sheet is only as good as the day somebody asks what is not on it, and he went off up the quay, and nobody thanked him for that either.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and said that the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Eighty thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being five hundred and five marks, eight shillings and twopence. It moved this morning by seven thousand seven hundred and fifty-two pence, and that is a hull, a mast, a warp, paint and nine days of one woman's hours, and not one of those five is a thing that happened to a person. Ninety-one thousand five hundred and thirty-eight out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And the sixty-fifth wage Tuesday went past in that harbor this morning with a column at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, twenty envelopes in a chest behind a shut door, and a week standing fallen due at the close of today. **There is a man of about thirty-one walking up that quay this afternoon with a figure in a book and no money in his hand, and both of those are true, and the lamp oil board is at four hundred and fifty pence, and there is still no flour in that market, and the two boats on the ground four days down that coast are one of them priced and one of them not, and this yard is the only place in the world that knows the price of the first one and that is not going to be said in a room.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Tuesday, and nobody was thanked for the sixty-fifth wage Tuesday and nobody was thanked for the price of a boat and nobody was thanked for the question about the price of a night.

---

**THE COLUMN WAS READ OUT ON THAT STEP AT TEN O'CLOCK AND DID NOT MOVE AND NOBODY WAS PAID A PENNY OUT OF THAT CHEST AND THE SIXTY-FIFTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A BOAT OF ABOUT NINE TONS FOUND ON THE GROUND OFF THE OUTER WATER FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST WAS PRICED IN HOURS AND MATERIAL AT ABOUT TEN O'CLOCK IN THE MORNING AT SEVEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FIFTY-TWO PENCE, BEING FORTY-EIGHT MARKS AND SIX SHILLINGS, AS FIVE FIGURES AND A SUM, WITH THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY AGAINST IT AND SETTLED ON THE EIGHTEENTH. THE MAN WHO OWNS THAT BOAT ASKED WHEN HE IS GOING TO BE PAID AND WAS TOLD THAT THAT SHEET IS A RECORD AND NOT A PURSE AND THAT A FIGURE OFF IT IS PORTABLE. THE PERSON WHOSE YARD IT IS SAID OUT LOUD THAT THE BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS WILL BE PRICED ON THURSDAY AND WILL NOT BE THIS NUMBER PLUS SOMETHING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS EIGHTY THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FIVE HUNDRED AND FIVE MARKS, EIGHT SHILLINGS AND TWO PENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the eighteenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that it was the sixty-fifth wage Tuesday of that harbor, and that the wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and that nobody was paid a penny out of that chest and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of that day, being the sixty-fifth, and that the column was read out on the step at the back of that market at about ten o'clock in the morning by a clerk of that harbor authority with his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading in front of about forty people of whom about twenty cannot read the column on the lid, and that the chest stood shut behind that door from ten until four and nobody asked to have it opened; and that a boat of about nine tons, found on the ground off the outer water on the morning of the fifteenth with a hole under her forefoot and her gear in the bottom of her and nobody standing on her, was priced at about ten o'clock in the morning at seven thousand seven hundred and fifty-two pence, being forty-eight marks and six shillings, as a new hull of twenty fathoms of two-inch at two hundred and twenty pence a fathom at four thousand four hundred, being twenty-seven marks, six shillings and eightpence; a mast and a boom at four hundred and eighty, being three marks; a warp and about thirty fathoms of line at one thousand one hundred and twenty, being seven marks; paint and oil at two hundred and forty, being one mark, six shillings and eightpence; and nine days of one woman's hours at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at one thousand five hundred and twelve, being nine marks and six shillings; and that the whole of that price was said out loud before a figure was written against the line, and that the fourteenth of January is against that line being the day the boat was lost and that it is in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon being the day it was settled, and that both of those days are on the face of it; and that the person whose yard it is said out loud that the boat of about fourteen tons will be priced on Thursday and will not be this number with something added to it; and that the man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay and owns the boat of about nine tons asked out loud in front of about nine people when he is going to be paid and was answered in about thirty words, being that that sheet is a record and not a purse, that a figure off it is portable and can be carried to a mill, a chandler, a yard or a man with a boat, that the money is not in that yard, that it is short by eighty thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence, and that nobody in that harbor has been asked for a penny of it and is not going to be, and that he said that was fair and that he had wanted to know which of the two things was true and that if it is a figure then he can work with a figure; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked out loud in that yard at about one in the afternoon whether those two bills were the price of the night and was answered in about eleven words that they are two of the prices and not all of them; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at ninety-one thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out, being five hundred and seventy-two marks, one shilling and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is eighty thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being five hundred and five marks, eight shillings and twopence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and eighty, one thousand two hundred and seventy-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and seventy-nine, one thousand two hundred and seventy-two; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that bill went on the sheet on a Tuesday and not on the morning of the fifteenth is his own, and is that a line with one day on it and that day the day it was written down is a line a stranger cannot use, and that a man whose boat is on the ground four days down that coast had to have two dates in front of him before he was owed anything, being the day it stopped being his and the day the number was written; and that the reason the person whose yard it is would not let the second boat be priced by adding to the first is hers, and is that a price made by addition is a price made by somebody who is not going to stand in the water when the ninth fathom comes out wrong, and that a hull of about fourteen tons is a different length of fir and a different week of hours and that the difference between them is the whole of what a person is being paid to know; and that the reason the man of about thirty-one was answered about the money with the words record and not purse is his own, and is that the two things are in an argument with each other across every harbor on that coast and that the only reason the paper side of it has ever won an argument is that it can be carried, and that a man with a figure in a book can go to five trades in one morning and a man with an apology in his hand can go to none of them; and that the reason the wage column was read out and did not move is that harbor's own and not this yard's, and is that a column at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest is a statement about what that harbor owes and not about what it has, and that the sixty-fifth week standing fallen due at the close of this day is a figure and not an event, and that a line of nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole is not going to move it in any month of this winter; and that the reason nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Tuesday is his own, and is that a boat got priced today and a column got read out and neither of those two things has any authority over the four hundred and fifty pence on a board at the back of an end stall, and that the figure on that board is the oldest number in that harbor and is the one he would defend least and has defended least.*
