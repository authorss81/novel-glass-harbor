# Chapter 504

*Monday the Sixth Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Fifth Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still Off It, the Figure of That Hundred Said Out Loud Once on the Eighteenth of November and Not Said Out Loud a Second Time, a Second Pair of Hands for Two Days Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Twenty Past Nine in the Morning at Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Two Marks, One Shilling and Fourpence, With the Sixth of December Against It and Not Written Into That Sheet Until the Two Days Are Worked, a Man of About Fifty-Eight of the Second Lay Asking Out Loud in Front of Nine People What a Man Is Owed When the Boats Cannot Go Out at All, and Being Given the Same Answer in the Same Words as on the Friday and One More Sentence This Time, That Sentence Written Out in Full in the Yard's Own Hand on the Face of That Sheet and Not Entered in Either Column, and His Not Arguing With It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Out, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Twenty-Seven Thousand One Hundred and Ninety Pence, Being One Hundred and Sixty-Nine Marks, Twelve Shillings and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Monday the sixth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fifth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on that deck on the eighteenth of November and is not said out loud a second time. A second pair of hands for two days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about twenty past nine in the morning at three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-seven thousand eight hundred and thirty pence out, being two hundred and thirty-six marks, five shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-seven thousand one hundred and ninety pence short, being one hundred and sixty-nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that figure has not moved since the Saturday. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninety-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirteenth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty-four days. The tide gang of that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the spring. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday and is walked on at every charge. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head, and nobody in that harbor has said anything to him about it and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by two men who did not stop walking, and the water went into it out of a hose that is dead until the spring and had to be carried from the butt behind that market in eleven trips, and neither of those two men is on the top of that rota board and the name that is on the top of that rota board is still off it.**

The man who keeps the leaf was in that shed at eight and had the door shut and did not go and watch it, as he does not go and watch any of the hundred of them.

"The hundred and fifth," he said, to the slate. "And the same words as the hundred and fourth, and the hundred and fourth was the same words as the hundred and third, and I am not going to improve on it because a man came up on Saturday with a list in his hand and read it out in a room four hundred yards from this shed."

Nobody said the figure of the hundred out loud on that Monday. It was said out loud once, on the eighteenth of November, on an after deck, by a man who was asked for it once.

---

**At about twenty past nine in the morning the man who keeps the leaf wrote one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it before he wrote a figure against it, and then he said why the total at the foot of it had not moved, which is a thing he has never had to say before in four years of keeping it.**

"A second pair of hands for two days, being two men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece for two days, being three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixth of December against it, being the day they were asked for and the day they begin."

"And the total at the foot of that sheet."

"The total at the foot of that sheet has not moved and I will tell you why, because in four years it has never had to be told. **A line goes into that sheet with the day the work was done against it, and it is written into that sheet on the day it is written.** Those two men are hired for two days and they have not worked a day yet, and the sixth of December is on the line, and the line goes into the sheet when the two days are in and not before. A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take the day off that line and the day it was written on off that line, and they are two different days, and that is not a trick. It is the only way a sheet of this kind is worth anything to anybody who is not standing in this shed."

Nobody argued with him about it. Two men were hired for two days and they came at about half past nine and they were put on the third lay with a caulking iron and a bucket of oakum and a bench that has not been level since August.

---

**And at about half past eleven in the morning, with about nine people in that shed and the door propped, a man of about fifty-eight of the second lay asked the question he came up that slip to ask, and he asked it out loud, and the man who keeps the leaf gave him the same answer in the same words he gave on the Friday, and then gave him one more sentence.**

He had a boat of about nine tons on the second lay and two men on her and he was fifty-eight or thereabouts and he had been in that water since he was eleven.

"I am not asking about the figure," he said. "I have got the figure. I want to know what a man is owed when the boats cannot go out at all, and there is no answer to that in a berth fund or in a slate or in whatever it is you people keep, because a man cannot be worked at a thing that cannot be done and the man who says otherwise is paying him for sitting down."

Nine people were in that shed and all nine of them heard it.

"The figure in chalk is the figure in chalk," the man who keeps the leaf said, "and it has not moved and it is not going to move this week whatever is standing off that mole, and a man who is not on the water is not a man who has not been worked. That is the same answer I gave four men on Friday morning and it is the same answer I am going to give you and it is not going to change because you have asked it in front of nine people instead of four."

**And then the man who keeps the leaf turned the slate round on the bench stone and said the rest of it, and it is not the same answer.**

"**And if he is not asked for by name tomorrow he is not worked tomorrow, and that is a thing that harbor does and not this yard, and I have not got that to give anybody and I have not got it from anybody either.**"

**And then he wrote the whole of that answer out, both sentences of it and the third, in his own hand, on the face of the sheet that carries the money, under that morning's two lines, and the hour of about half past eleven against it, and it is not in the first column of that leaf and it is not in the second column of that leaf, and it is on the face of that sheet where a stranger can put a finger on it.**

The man of about fifty-eight read it standing up. He put his thumb under the last sentence and read that one twice and put his hand down and did not argue with it.

"That is the first time anybody in this harbor has written down a thing I can come back and look at," he said. "I am not thanking you for it and I am not going to say you are right about tomorrow. I am going to send my two men down to the second lay at seven in the morning and I am going to ask for them by name tomorrow, and if a man from this office comes up and asks me for a week instead of a day, that is a different morning and I will be standing in it."

He went down the slip. Nobody thanked him and nobody thanked the man who keeps the leaf and no figure is set against any part of that morning in any book in that harbor.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, and it was the same figure it had been at four o'clock on the Saturday, and he said both of those things.**

"Twenty-seven thousand one hundred and ninety pence short, being one hundred and sixty-nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since Saturday morning, and I have said why it is unmoved, and there is a line on that sheet with the sixth of December against it that is not in the total at the foot of it, and both of those facts are true and both of them are on that sheet."

Nobody was asked for the rest of it.

---

**A CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND FIFTH TIME AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. TWO MEN ARE ON THAT THIRD LAY WITH AN OAKUM IRON AND A BUCKET AND A BENCH THAT HAS NOT BEEN LEVEL SINCE AUGUST. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT READ AN ANSWER OFF THE FACE OF A SHEET WITH HIS THUMB UNDER THE LAST LINE AND READ THAT ONE TWICE, AND HE SENT HIS TWO MEN DOWN AT SEVEN IN THE MORNING AND HE DID NOT THANK ANYBODY. THE FIGURE IN THE CHALK IS ON A SLATE AT AN END STALL AND HAS NOT MOVED. THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT OF THE WAGE OF FOUR MARKS AND THE BAG IS ON THE BENCH STONE UNDER IT. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-SEVEN THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NOBODY HAS BEEN ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the sixth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fifth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it, and that the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on the eighteenth of November and was not said out loud a second time; and that a second pair of hands for two days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about twenty past nine in the morning at three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixth of December against it, and that a line goes into that sheet with the day the work was done against it and is written into that sheet on the day it is written, and that the two days had not been worked and the line was therefore not in the total at the foot of that sheet at the close of that day; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-seven thousand eight hundred and thirty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-seven thousand one hundred and ninety pence short, being one hundred and sixty-nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Saturday; and that a man of about fifty-eight of the second lay asked out loud in front of nine people in that shed at about half past eleven in the morning what a man is owed when the boats cannot go out at all, and was given the answer in the figure in chalk and in the words it was given on the Friday and one more sentence, and that the whole of that answer was written out in the yard's own hand on the face of that sheet under the morning's lines with the hour against it and not in either column of that leaf, and that he read it standing up, read the last sentence twice, did not argue with it, said he was not thanking anybody for it, and said he would ask for his two men by name on the Tuesday.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the two days of that pair of hands went on that sheet with the sixth against it and not into the total at the foot of it is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a sheet whose total moves before the work is done is a sheet that is counting on a promise, and that a total a stranger can check is worth nine day-counts in a shed; and that the reason the answer to a man of about fifty-eight was written out on the face of that sheet and not entered in a column is the yard's own mouth, and is that a column of that leaf is a thing this yard keeps about itself and that a thing said out loud in a shed to a man with nine tons of boat behind him is not about this yard and does not belong in this yard's own record of itself, and that a sheet a stranger can put a finger on is worth nine times a line in a book four hundred yards away; and that the reason nobody in that shed said the figure of the hundred out loud on that Monday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a figure that has been said once in a room and is said again in a room becomes a thing people begin checking against each other, and that the cistern is charged on a rota and is walked on at every charge and neither of those has ever in this harbor been in want of a number said twice; and that the reason no figure standing in that harbor was touched on that Monday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a day asked for by name comes out of the chalk and a standing debt does not come out of the chalk whatever a man says in a shed, and that this yard has a hundred and five cistern charges on a rota and not one settled account, and that a rota is not an account and does not become one because there is a line of boats off the mole;  and that the reason the man who was asked that question was not argued with by anybody is the yard's own mouth, and is that he came up that slip with a want and got the whole of the answer in front of nine people and did not get a speech about the line of boats, and that a man who has been given the whole of an answer and has said he will come back is worth more in a harbor than a man who has been given a speech and has said nothing.*
