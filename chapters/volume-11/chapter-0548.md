# Chapter 548

*Wednesday the Nineteenth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Last of Three Days on a Wall Going Up on a Shipyard on That Quay, the Seventeenth, the Eighteenth and the Nineteenth, with the First of the Three the Same Day the Four Men Were Paid a Day Apiece in Advance, Six of the Oak Sill Beams Going In Under a Roof That Had None in It Since the Eighth of This Month at About Ten o'Clock in the Morning, the North Wall and the Dressing to It and the Two Doors With Hangings and a Bar and Fourteen Panes of Glass and Three Days of One Woman's Hours Priced at About Ten in the Morning at Five Thousand Five Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Thirty-Four Marks and Eleven Shillings, the Whole of It Said Out Loud Before a Figure Went Against the Line, Which Is a Second Payment and Not the First One and Not Fifteen Thousand Three Hundred and Twenty Pence, a Man of About Fifty Asking Out Loud in Front of About Eleven People Why There Is a Wall Going Up While His Boat Is on the Ground Four Days Down That Coast, and a Room over That Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Eighty-Six Thousand Four Hundred and Seventy Pence, Being Five Hundred and Forty Marks, Five Shillings and Tenpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the nineteenth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shipyard on that quay with a roof on it that is eleven days old and a wall going up on the north side of it.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at five thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence, being thirty-four marks and eleven shillings, with the nineteenth of January against it and settled on the nineteenth. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at ninety-seven thousand one hundred and ten pence out, being six hundred and six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is eighty-six thousand four hundred and seventy pence short, being five hundred and forty marks, five shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-six days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-eight; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-six days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-second night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-fifth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fortieth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-seventh; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-eight days. There has been no barley in that market since the middle of September and there is none this morning, and a man of about thirty-three who hires hands was on that slip at about ten on the third of this month and asked for a week and was not given one and has not asked since. The boat that was in that yard on the Monday morning of the fifth and was not in it on the Wednesday went out on a cart hired off the west basin and paid for out of her owner's own pocket and is at the north quay this evening with her bilge blocks under her, and she has not been moved since and she is on no sheet in any hand. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-six days and he was in that shed for about two hours on the Wednesday morning and sat on that bench stone and said nothing and was said nothing to.**

---

At about ten o'clock in the morning the sixth of those six sill beams went in under that roof, and there had not been one in that building since the eighth of this month, and the person whose yard it is priced the whole of the three days out loud in about eleven people before a figure went against any of it, and a slater of about forty was not asked for a figure and gave none.

1. *The north wall. Eleven fathoms of three-quarter stuff at one hundred and forty pence a fathom. One thousand five hundred and forty pence, being nine marks, eight shillings and fourpence.*
2. *Six sill beams of oak under that roof, six fathoms at two hundred and forty pence a fathom. One thousand four hundred and forty pence, being nine marks exactly.*
3. *The boarding dressed to that wall, nine fathoms at one hundred and sixty pence a fathom. One thousand four hundred and forty pence, being nine marks exactly.*
4. *Two doors with their hangings and a bar. Four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks exactly.*
5. *Fourteen panes of glass and the putty to bed them. One hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eight pence.*
6. *Three days of my own hours on that wall at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day. Five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings.*

"That is five thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence, being thirty-four marks and eleven shillings, and it is the sum of those six, and **a man with a leaf and a pencil can take three days off that one line and find they are the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth of this month, and two of those three are days this line was not written on.**"

She said the next of it once and did not say it again.

"That is a second payment and not the first one. The first one was a roof at fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence over three days, and it is in a drawer under this bench stone with the figure on a slate in it, and **that drawer has never been locked in this yard and the figure in it and the figure on that sheet are the same figure and they are not this one and this one is not that one plus something.** The first one bought a roof. This one buys a wall and six beams and two doors and glass, and without the six beams a man puts his weight on nothing, and I have been saying so in this yard since the ninth of this month and nobody has given me a figure for it until today."

---

And a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons stood at the corner of that slip at about half past ten in the morning and asked out loud, in front of about eleven people, why there is a wall going up while his boat is on the ground four days down that coast, and he was answered in about a hundred and sixty words and did not ask it again, and he was not thanked for asking it.

"I will tell you the whole of it and then you can do what you like with it. **There was no roof on this building on the fifth, the sixth and the seventh of this month, and a compressor has to run in a building and a compressor has to have a fire under it, and eight bottles of air were charged in that room on the Thursday before Friday night, and those eight bottles are the reason the outer point could be worked at all.** The choice was made on the eighth of January by a man who was not going to walk up a coast road and ask anybody anything, and the cost of that choice was three days and a roof, and the cost of what the roof then let happen is three days and a wall. That is the second payment and I have got it in front of you and it is a different number from the first one."

He said that was not an answer to his question and it was the closest thing to one anybody in that yard was going to get, and he said he would think about the boat of about fourteen tons, and he went off up the quay.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and the thing on the wall of that room was the four heads and the four standing lines under them and the eleven names at the foot of the sheet, and it had not changed, and no fifth head went on it on account of a wall, a bill, a night or two boats.

The man who keeps the leaf stood where the long table is and said out loud, for the people who could not read what was on the wall, that the four heads on that wall are the four heads on that wall and that no fifth had gone on it that week on account of anything; that the sheet that carries the money is not in that room and is not on any wall in it and is not going to be read out in that room this winter; that a buoys list with four names on it went up on the boards of the floor of that market on the twenty-sixth of December, that it went down that coast in a cart on Monday the ninth of December past and came back up that road at about half past eleven in the morning on Wednesday the twelfth of this month with three windows on the back of it, and that that list is a different thing and is not this room; and that two boats were on the ground four days down that coast and that one of them had been priced on Tuesday and the other one had not, and that a price is a number off a piece of paper and not a story, and that anybody in that room who wants the number can come to a bench stone four hundred yards from here at about ten o'clock in the morning any day and read it off the paper himself, and that about eleven people in that room cannot do that and that is the fault of the harbor and not of the paper.

Nobody in that room asked him for anything, nobody asked for either of those two things to be put on a wall, and nobody was thanked.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and said that the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.

"Eighty-six thousand four hundred and seventy pence short, being five hundred and forty marks, five shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by five thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence, and that is a wall, six sill beams, the dressing to it, two doors, fourteen panes of glass, and three days of one woman's hours, and **the six sill beams are the whole of what that figure is for and a man could not have got them out of the number fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty by dividing it, and a stranger who wants to know what the two payments have in common can have the answer in one sentence: the first one is what it cost to be able to charge air, and this one is what it costs to stand under the roof that was charged in.** Ninety-seven thousand one hundred and ten out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And a man of about fifty-three asked me in front of eleven people this morning why there is a wall going up while his boat is on the ground four days down that coast, and I told him, and he is not satisfied, and he is not owed a second answer, and neither of us has been thanked for anything today."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Wednesday, and nobody was thanked for the six beams and nobody was thanked for the second payment and nobody was thanked for the two hundred yards of wall.

---

**SIX SILL BEAMS OF OAK WENT IN UNDER A ROOF ON THAT QUAY AT ABOUT TEN O'CLOCK IN THE MORNING AND THERE HAD NOT BEEN ONE IN THAT BUILDING SINCE THE EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH. THE NORTH WALL AND THE DRESSING TO IT AND TWO DOORS WITH HANGINGS AND A BAR AND FOURTEEN PANES OF GLASS AND THREE DAYS OF ONE WOMAN'S HOURS WERE PRICED OUT LOUD AT FIVE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE, BEING THIRTY-FOUR MARKS AND ELEVEN SHILLINGS, AS SIX FIGURES AND A SUM, WITH THE NINETEENTH OF JANUARY AGAINST IT AND SETTLED ON THE NINETEENTH, AND THAT FIGURE IS A SECOND PAYMENT AND IS NOT FIFTEEN THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE AND IS NOT THAT FIGURE PLUS ANYTHING. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY ASKED OUT LOUD WHY THERE IS A WALL GOING UP WHILE HIS BOAT IS ON THE GROUND FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST AND WAS TOLD THAT A COMPRESSOR HAS TO RUN IN A BUILDING AND THAT EIGHT BOTTLES OF AIR WERE CHARGED IN THAT ROOM ON THE THURSDAY BEFORE FRIDAY NIGHT AND THAT THOSE EIGHT BOTTLES ARE THE REASON THE OUTER POINT COULD BE WORKED AT ALL. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS EIGHTY-SIX THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY MARKS, FIVE SHILLINGS AND TEN PENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the nineteenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that a line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at five thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence, being thirty-four marks and eleven shillings, as the north wall at eleven fathoms of three-quarter stuff at one hundred and forty pence a fathom, being one thousand five hundred and forty, being nine marks, eight shillings and fourpence; six sill beams of oak at six fathoms at two hundred and forty pence a fathom, being one thousand four hundred and forty, being nine marks; the boarding dressed to that wall at nine fathoms at one hundred and sixty pence a fathom, being one thousand four hundred and forty, being nine marks; two doors with their hangings and a bar at four hundred and eighty, being three marks; fourteen panes of glass and the putty to bed them at one hundred and sixty-eight, being one mark and eight pence; and three days of one woman's hours on that wall at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, being five hundred and four, being three marks and two shillings; and that the whole of those six was said out loud before a figure was written against that line, and that the nineteenth of January is against that line and that it is in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon being both the day the work was done and the day it was settled, and that a man with a leaf and a pencil can take three days off that one line and find they are the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth of this month and that two of those three are days the line was not written on; and that that figure is a second payment and not the first one, and that the first one was fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence for a roof over the sixth, the seventh and the eighth of this month and is on a slate in an unlocked drawer under that bench stone, and that the two figures are not the same figure and the second is not the first plus anything; and that a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons asked out loud in front of about eleven people at about half past ten in the morning why there is a wall going up while his boat is on the ground four days down that coast, and was answered in about a hundred and sixty words, being that there was no roof on that building on the fifth, the sixth and the seventh of this month, that a compressor has to run in a building with a fire under it, that eight bottles of air were charged in that room on the Thursday before Friday night, and that those eight bottles are the reason the outer point could be worked at all, and that he said it was not an answer to his question and it was the closest thing to one anybody in that yard was going to get; and that a room over the floor of that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read, and that the four heads on the wall of that room did not change and no fifth head went on it on account of a wall, a bill, a night or two boats, and that the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and is not in it and was not read out in it, and that the two sheets were named separately and out loud in that room; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at ninety-seven thousand one hundred and ten pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is eighty-six thousand four hundred and seventy pence short, being five hundred and forty marks, five shillings and tenpence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and eighty-one, one thousand two hundred and seventy-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and eighty, one thousand two hundred and seventy-seven; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the two figures are not to be added together is his own and was said out loud in that yard and not said again, and is that the first figure is the cost of a decision and the second is the cost of the thing the decision turned out to be for, and that a man who can add them together is a man who thinks the first one was a mistake, and that nobody in that yard has said it was a mistake and that if a number on that sheet can be added to another number on that sheet then the sheet has stopped being a record of days and has become a column in somebody's argument; and that the reason a man of about fifty-three was answered about the compressor is his own, and is that the connection between a wall in Morrow Quay and a mark four days down that coast is not a matter of opinion and is a matter of eight bottles charged in a building with a roof on it, and that if he is not satisfied with that answer then the not-satisfaction is his and correct and does not need to be repaired by a second answer from a man who writes figures down for a living; and that the reason the four heads on that wall were named out loud in that room on that afternoon is his own, and is that a wall in a yard four hundred yards from a room is a thing that about eleven people in that room will be told about by somebody else by Tuesday, and that the only version of that anybody in that room will hear is the one somebody says out loud in the room where they can hear it, and that this is not a matter of the four heads and does not add to them and is not on that wall; and that the reason not one sum standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Wednesday is his own, and is that six beams of oak went into a building on that quay today at a figure printed on a slip in the open, and that is a thing this yard did with its own money about its own building, and that it has moved the number on that sheet by five thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence and has moved the four hundred and fifty pence on the board at the back of that end stall by nothing at all, and that there is no connection between those two numbers and there is not going to be one this winter.*
