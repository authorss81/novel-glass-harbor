# Chapter 526

*Tuesday the Twenty-Eighth Day of December, the Sixty-Second Wage Tuesday, the Wage Column in That Harbor Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest and Not Moving, Read Out on the Step at the Back of That Market at Ten o'Clock in the Morning in Front of About Forty People of Whom About Twenty Cannot Read the Column on the Lid, Nobody Paid a Penny Out of That Chest, a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, Being the Sixty-Second, Three Days of Air Going Onto the Sheet at Nine Hundred and Sixty Pence, Being Six Marks, With the Twenty-Eighth of December Against It, the Whole of That Line Said Out Loud Before a Figure Was Written Against It and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying Out Loud That That Line Is Not for the Three and the Three Are Not Going on It, a Twelfth Week of That Winter Somewhere in That Column and Nobody in That Room Saying a Word About It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Six Thousand Four Hundred and Two Pence, Being Two Hundred and Ninety Marks and Twopence, the Four Heads Not Changed, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the twenty-eighth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room at the back of that fish market.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and it was the sixty-second wage Tuesday. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the sixty-second. Three days of air went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks, with the twenty-eighth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-seven thousand and forty-two pence out, being three hundred and fifty-six marks, six shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-six thousand four hundred and two pence short, being two hundred and ninety marks and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirtieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighteenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-six days. A chest with twenty envelopes in it stood shut in a room at the back of that market all day and was opened at ten o'clock and shut again at ten o'clock and was not opened between and its key is in a pocket and the key has been in that pocket since the twenty-fourth of November. The nineteen winters of that harbor are on a page in an office on the west basin and are disputed and unpaid and the twelfth one of this winter is in a column four hundred yards from where the column is read out. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-four days and he was at the head of that third lay for about an hour on that Tuesday for a thing that was not diving and nobody in that harbor has said a word to him about it.**

---

**At about ten o'clock in the morning a clerk of the harbor authority read that column out on the step at the back of that market, and about forty people were on the boards in front of that step and about twenty of them cannot read the column on the lid, and he put his own name at the foot of the reading and the hour, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut at ten o'clock and was shut at four o'clock and the sixty-second week stands fallen due at the close of that day.**

The column did not move. It has not moved since the seventh of October, when it was at twenty-five lines and one hundred marks, and the twenty-sixth line and the last four marks went in on the twenty-fourth of November and nothing has gone on it since.

A man at the back of that step said out loud, in front of about forty people, that there was a twelth week of this winter in there somewhere, and somebody said the twelth, and he said the twelfth, and a woman at the front of that step said that the twelfth of what, and nobody answered her, and the clerk went on reading the next line and nobody stopped him and the twelfth week of that winter went back into the column without a word.

---

**And at about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Three days of air. Nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks. Off a boat alongside the west quay that carries her own bottles, with the twenty-eighth of December against it, being the day the work was done, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. **That line is not for the three, and the three are not going on it.**"

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said, out loud, that everything on that sheet this month was for the three, and the man who keeps the leaf said no, in about four words, and then said the rest of it and was not thanked for any of it.

"Three days of air is what a compressor burns in three days with a man in the water part of the time and it is bought now because air is bought in advance or not at all, and it is a line for the pump and not a line for anybody. **The three men are going to be asked on Wednesday what they will do and they will be asked it in the same words at the same rate and if any air goes down on Thursday it will be on a line with Thursday's date on it and Thursday's date on it will be air bought on Wednesday for a day nobody had told me about.** If a man goes in on Thursday and the air is short then the air was bought short on Wednesday and that is a figure that stands in the book at the time it was agreed and not at the time it went wrong."

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and the four heads on that wall and the four standing lines under them and the eleven names at the foot of the sheet were on that wall and had not changed and no fifth head went on it.**

Nothing was read out in that room on that Tuesday. A man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons stood at the back of it for about half an hour and said nothing, and a ropemaker of about sixty-one, who is not in a room of her own on a Saturday and has not had one for eleven years, came up those stairs on a Tuesday for the first time in a month and stood by the door and said nothing and left before three o'clock and put nothing in.

The man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was not in that room on that Tuesday and nobody sent for him and nobody has asked whether he will be in it on Wednesday, and the four lines that went onto the face of the record that room keeps on the twenty-fifth are in that record and the hour is under each of them and nobody read them out a second time and nobody asked for them to be.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone, and said that the day had moved it, and said the thing about the wage column once, out loud, and did not say it again that month.**

"Forty-six thousand four hundred and two pence short, being two hundred and ninety marks and twopence. It moved this morning by nine hundred and sixty pence and that is three days of air and it is on the sheet with the twenty-eighth of December against it and both those days are today. Fifty-seven thousand and forty-two out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And this afternoon the sixty-second wage Tuesday went past in this harbor the way the last four have gone past. A column at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, twenty envelopes in a chest, and a week standing fallen due at the close of today, and the week before that one fallen due since the twenty-first, and the one before that since the fourteenth, and there is a twelfth week of this winter in there somewhere and a woman at the front of that step asked which twelfth and nobody answered her. **A wage Tuesday is a harbor finding out what it owes and the finding out is not a payment, and this yard writes the finding out at half past five in the yard's own mouth because this yard is answerable for what it spends and is not answerable for what a council four hundred yards from here has not voted, and it is going to go on being not answerable for that until somebody in that room asks this yard a question about it.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Tuesday, including the man who keeps the leaf and including the clerk who read the column out on the step and put his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading.

---

**A CLERK OF THAT HARBOR AUTHORITY READ THE COLUMN OUT ON THE STEP AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT TEN O'CLOCK IN THE MORNING IN FRONT OF ABOUT FORTY PEOPLE AND ABOUT TWENTY OF THEM CANNOT READ THE COLUMN ON THE LID AND THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE AND NOBODY WAS PAID A PENNY OUT OF THE CHEST AND THE SIXTY-SECOND WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE. A TWELFTH WEEK OF THAT WINTER IS IN THAT COLUMN AND A WOMAN AT THE FRONT OF THAT STEP ASKED WHICH TWELFTH AND NOBODY ANSWERED HER. NINE HUNDRED AND SIXTY PENCE WENT ON THAT SHEET FOR THREE DAYS OF AIR AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID OUT LOUD THAT THAT LINE IS NOT FOR THE THREE AND THE THREE ARE NOT GOING ON IT. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-SIX THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND TWO PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-eighth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that it was the sixty-second wage Tuesday, and that the wage column in that harbor stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move and that nobody was paid a penny out of that chest and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of that day, being the sixty-second, and that the column was read out on the step at the back of that market at about ten o'clock in the morning by a clerk of that harbor authority with his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading in front of about forty people of whom about twenty cannot read the column on the lid; and that three days of air went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks, with the twenty-eighth of December against it being the day the work was done, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it and said out loud that that line is not for the three and that the three are not going on it, and that that line is for a compressor and not for a person; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-seven thousand and forty-two pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-six thousand four hundred and two pence short, being two hundred and ninety marks and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by nine hundred and sixty pence on that day; and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read what is put in front of them, and that nothing was read out in that room on that day and the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it and no fifth head is going to go on it; and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons stood at the back of that room for about half an hour and said nothing, and a ropemaker of about sixty-one came up those stairs on that Tuesday for the first time in a month and put nothing in; and that the man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was not in that room and nobody sent for him; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-nine, one thousand and one hundred and sixty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-eight, one thousand and one hundred and sixty-seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason air was bought on a Tuesday for three days and put on that sheet as a line of its own and not as a share of a day is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a line this yard cannot say the purpose of out loud is a line it should not have opened, and that a compressor is a compressor on the twenty-eighth and on the thirtieth and that the men who will be asked tomorrow are three men and are not a pump; and that the reason nobody from a port four days down that coast was in that room on that Tuesday is the reader's own, and is that a man who came four days up a coast road to put one condition on a harbor has not come to sit in a room twice a week while a harbor works out what to do about it, and that the four lines that went onto the record on the twenty-fifth are in that record with an hour under each and a page that is not read out again is a page that is not being argued with; and that the reason the twelfth week of that winter went back into that column without a word is his own, and is that a woman at the front of a step asked which twelfth, and that this yard does not know, and that the two of those are different things and that the day a harbor can answer the second from the first is the day that column stops being a column and becomes a ledger, and a ledger is a thing a person is on; and that the reason this yard touched no old account in either direction on that Tuesday is his own, and is that a wage Tuesday finds out what a harbor owes and the finding out is not a payment, and that this yard has now said that out loud twice this month in two different places and is not going to say it a third time, and that four hundred and fifty pence is on a board at an end stall, and the mole head is dark, and the miller is taking his flour in at the lower door, and the two wagons go down a coast road on the Sunday.**
