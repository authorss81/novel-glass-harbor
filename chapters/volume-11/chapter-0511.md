# Chapter 511

*Monday the Thirteenth Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Seventh Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still Off It, the Figure of That Hundred Said Out Loud Once on the Eighteenth of November and Not Said Out Loud a Second Time, Bread and Salt Beef for Nine Men for Ten Days Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Ten in the Morning at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty Pence, Being Twelve Marks, at the Figure the Increase of the Fourth Day Set and Not at the Figure It Stood At Before That, a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Hard at Ashtide Answered Out Loud in a Room Over a Fish Market by a Working Person of That Port of About Fifty on That Person's Own Terms With a Condition Attached to the Answer and the Three Marks That Stand Against That Entry Not Going and the Entry Not Settled and No Figure Set Against Any Person in That Room, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in the Afternoon at Thirty-One Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy Pence, Being One Hundred and Ninety-Nine Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, and Not Answered by Anybody, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the thirteenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and seventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on the eighteenth of November and is not said out loud a second time. Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand nine hundred and twenty pence, being twelve marks, being the figure the increase of the fourth of December set and not the figure that line stood at before it, and a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-two thousand six hundred and ten pence out, being two hundred and sixty-six marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-one thousand nine hundred and seventy pence short, being one hundred and ninety-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eleven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifteenth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twentieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-one days. The tide gang of that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the spring. A case stands on a long table in the room over that market with its lock shut and the key in the keeping of the woman who wrote the minute, and nobody in that harbor has been shown what is in it. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay has a hole in the middle of a Tuesday and has it still, and no figure is set against it anywhere and nobody has asked him about it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against that anywhere and he has said nothing about it this week and nobody has asked him.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by two men who did not stop walking, and the water went into it out of a hose that is dead until the spring and had to be carried from the butt behind that market in eleven trips, and neither of those two men is on the top of that rota board and the name that is on the top of that rota board is still off it.**

The man who keeps the leaf was in that shed at eight and had the door shut and did not go and watch it, as he does not go and watch any of the hundred and seven of them.

"The hundred and seventh," he said, to the slate. "And the same words as the hundred and sixth, and the hundred and sixth was the same words as the hundred and fifth, and I am not going to improve on it because a man came down that quay on Wednesday with a page in his hand."

Nobody said the figure of the hundred out loud on that Monday. It was said out loud once, on the eighteenth of November, on an after deck, by a man who was asked for it once.

---

**At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put two lines onto that sheet and said the whole of both out loud before he wrote a figure against either, and nobody argued with him about any part of them.**

"Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days. One thousand nine hundred and twenty pence, being twelve marks. That is not the figure I have been paying at since the spring and you know the figure I have been paying at, and it is not the figure I paid on the third of December either. On the third it was sixteen hundred for the same nine men and the same ten days, and when the same victual came ashore a second time on the fourth it was three hundred and twenty more than that and it went on as a line of its own because I am not going to carry an increase inside a bread line, and that increase is the whole of what a line of boats does to a price and it is worth a line anybody can find with a finger."

"And then four days apiece in advance to the same four men, being four men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence. They are the same four men and their names are in the book the days are paid out of and not on this sheet."

He put the pen down flat and did not say anything else about either line, and the four men took their day in advance at the bench stone at about half past ten and one of them asked whether the bread figure would be the same on the seventeenth and the man who keeps the leaf said it would not be the same on the seventeenth and he was not going to say what it would be.

---

**And at about twenty past one in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them.**

The thing on the wall of that room on that afternoon was the four heads and the four standing lines under them and the eleven names at the foot of it, and it had not changed and no fifth head was going on it, and nobody in that room was asked whether they agreed to anything and nobody in that room was asked to sign anything. A man at the back of that room said the question he had come up those stairs with, which was that the hard at that port had been unanswered since the ninth of November past and that the three marks standing against that entry were on an anchor date that came in on the entry and were not going to be re-dated, and that it was the thirty-fourth day of that, being under five weeks, and somebody in that harbor ought to know whether there was anybody left at the other end of it who could answer it.

**And a man of about fifty who fits boat bottoms said, out loud, from about the middle of that room, that he was from that port, and that he had been in this harbor nine weeks, and that he had not said so because it had not been anybody's business.**

He had a chest of tools against the wall by the door and a bed above a chandler's on the second lay and he did not own a boat in this harbor and he is not the man of about fifty who owns the boat of about twenty-two tons. Twenty people in that room heard all of what follows and about eleven of them could not read a word of it.

"You have been asking the wrong room," he said. "That wall does not answer for that port and neither does anybody standing in front of it. I have not come up here to give this harbor an answer and I am not going to stand here and be agreed with. Here is the answer, and it is mine, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be asked to say it again."

Nobody said anything.

"The boats at that hard will take victual off the outside at the top of the flood and carry it up to the cannery steps. They will take coal the same way. They will not take anything else off that hard, and there is nothing else of yours coming, and the first of anything else of yours that comes up that hard will be put back on the water where it was found, and it will not be opened by anybody at that port. And not one man of yours comes past the fish quay at that port. If one comes past the fish quay I will tell him to go back and I will not be sorry about it, and I will tell the hard that he was told."

**And then he said the part of it that was about the entry, and nobody interrupted him and nobody agreed with him.**

"And the three marks on that entry are not yours. They are not mine either. They are on the ninth of November and they have been on the ninth of November since the ninth of November and nothing that has happened in this harbor since then puts a day on them, and I am not here to move them and I am not here to pay them, and I would not pay them if I had it. That entry is open and it is going to be open when the ice goes and I have told you what is at the other end of it and that is all I have come up here for."

Somebody at the front of that room said, out loud, that the party was not in a position to accept anything on anybody's behalf, and that the four heads on that wall bind nobody who has not signed them and cannot send anybody anywhere.

"I know what that wall is," the man of about fifty said. "I am not offering it to them. I am telling a room what is at the end of a hard and I am telling it on my own hook, and if any of you want to carry it up the coast you may, and I will not be in the room when you argue about it."

**Nobody thanked him. Nobody put a figure against him and nobody put a figure against that party on the strength of what he said, and he was not thanked by the man who keeps the leaf in that shed that evening and he has not been thanked since, and the three marks have not gone and the entry is not settled.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door open on the slip, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Thirty-one thousand nine hundred and seventy pence short, being one hundred and ninety-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by two thousand five hundred and ninety-two pence and that is two lines and both of them are on the sheet with the thirteenth of December against them, and the day those two lines are written is the day they were written, which is today, and that is the same day this time and I am not going to get used to that."

Somebody at the back of that group said the word hard and the man who keeps the leaf said the word hard back, once, and then said the rest of what he had to say about it, and what he said was not about the hard.

"Two thousand five hundred and ninety-two pence, being sixteen marks, two shillings and eightpence, out of a line of this week's work and into a line of this week's work, and that is all the movement on that sheet this month. The three marks at that other port were on an entry with a day of its own and a man in a room four hundred yards from here has said out loud what is at the other end of it, and neither of those two things is one penny. **A thing somebody says out loud in a room is not money and does not go on a sheet and does not move a figure, and this yard did not move one this afternoon and has not moved one this month.**"

Nobody thanked anybody on that day. Nobody asked the man of about fifty for anything else and he was not asked for it.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND SEVENTH TIME AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. TWO MEN CARRY WATER IN FROM A BUTT BEHIND THAT MARKET IN ELEVEN TRIPS BECAUSE THE HOSE IS DEAD AND WILL BE DEAD UNTIL THE SPRING. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WHO FITS BOAT BOTTOMS HAS A CHEST OF TOOLS AGAINST THE WALL BY THE DOOR IN A ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT SHED AND HE HAS A BED ABOVE A CHANDLER'S ON THE SECOND LAY. HE HAS SAID ONCE WHAT IS AT THE END OF THE HARD AT THAT PORT AND HE HAS SAID IT ON HIS OWN HOOK AND HE HAS NOT ASKED FOR ANYTHING AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM FOR ANYTHING. THE THREE MARKS ON THAT ENTRY ARE ON THE NINTH OF NOVEMBER AND THEY ARE STILL ON THE NINTH OF NOVEMBER. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-ONE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS BEEN ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE. THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN A BAG UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY.**

*First column, entered on Monday the thirteenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and seventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it, and that the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on the eighteenth of November and was not said out loud a second time; and that bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand nine hundred and twenty pence, being twelve marks, at the figure the increase of the fourth of December set, and that a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of both lines out loud before he wrote a figure against either and that he put the increase of the fourth of December on as a separate line and did not carry it inside the bread line; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-two thousand six hundred and ten pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-one thousand nine hundred and seventy pence short, being one hundred and ninety-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved by two thousand five hundred and ninety-two pence on that day; and that a room over that market was let out at about twenty past one in the afternoon and about twenty people went up and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and that the four heads on that wall have not changed and no fifth head was added to it, and that a man of about fifty who fits boat bottoms and who is from that port and who has been in this harbor nine weeks said out loud from the middle of that room that he was from that port, and gave the answer that has been standing at that hard since the ninth of November past on his own hook and with a condition attached to it, being what may be landed at that hard and how far up it a man of this harbor may go, and that nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him and nobody thanked him and no figure was set against him or against that party; and that the three marks that stand against that entry are on an anchor date that came in on the entry and have not been re-dated and did not go on that day, and that the entry is not settled; and that the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line and the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and forty-four, one thousand and ninety-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and forty-three, one thousand and ninety-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the hundred and seventh charge was written down in the same words as the hundred and sixth is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a figure of a cistern has been asked for once in four months and asking for it twice would make it a thing a room starts checking against itself; and that the reason the victual went on that sheet at the figure the fourth of December set is his own, and is that a yard which buys at the figure it used to buy at while somebody in the market is charging the figure it is charging now is a yard that has stopped being a buyer and become a customer, and that the two thousand five hundred and ninety-two pence that went on that sheet this morning is the whole of what the price of that article has done to this yard this month; and that the reason a man from another port was allowed to answer a hard in a room over this market without anybody in that room answering for anybody is this yard's own mouth, and is that a page on a wall binds nobody who has not signed it and cannot carry another port's business, and that the only thing in that room that could have stopped him was an argument, and that a man who has come nine weeks down a coast road to say a thing once should not be made to say it twice to a room that cannot agree with him; and that the reason no figure was set against the party for what was said in that room is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a claim is not a debt and a word is not a figure, and that the day a harbor begins setting a number against somebody for having spoken in a room is the day that room stops being a place a person can speak in; and that the reason not one figure standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, knocked down, written off or shut on that Monday is this yard's own mouth, and is that a hard at another port has been open since the ninth of November and three marks have been standing on that entry since the same morning, and that a man saying in a room that the boats at that hard will take victual and nothing else is not a payment of three marks and is not a settlement of an entry and does not shorten a day for anybody in this harbor, and that this yard moved no old figure at all on that Monday.*
