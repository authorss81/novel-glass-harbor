# Chapter 513

*Wednesday the Fifteenth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a List That Came Ashore off the Supply Boat of the Fourteenth Read Out Word for Word With the Reader's Own Name and the Hour Under It and Not Stopped at Any Word, the Figure That Supply Is Asking Read Out in That Room and a Working Man of About Forty-One Asking Out Loud and Once Only What It Is For, and Being Answered Out of the Figure and Not Out of a Man, the Figure Being Four Hundred and Eighty Pence a Month, Being Three Marks, and the Figure Not Saying What It Is For, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Thirty-Four Thousand and Fifty Pence, Being Two Hundred and Twelve Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, Unmoved Since the Tuesday Before, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Nobody Thanked*

Wednesday the fifteenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-four thousand six hundred and ninety pence out, being two hundred and seventy-nine marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-four thousand and fifty pence short, being two hundred and twelve marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure has not moved since Tuesday afternoon. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eleven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirteen; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventeenth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninetieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-three days. The north berth of that harbor is out and has been out since the autumn. There is no physic at fourteen pence in that harbor, being one shilling and twopence, and there is none of it. A man of about thirty-two has a tremor in both hands on a cold morning and an afternoon of his own he cannot put in order, and no figure is set against either anywhere and nobody in that harbor has asked him about either. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it is not made into anything by anybody in that harbor, and he has not said a word about it this week.**

---

**A list came ashore off that supply boat on the Tuesday and went round that market on the Wednesday in the hand of a man who is not in any office in that town, and it is a list of eleven things with a figure against each of them and a name at the foot of it.**

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands at that market for whoever will pay him read it out in the room over that market at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and about twenty people went up those stairs and about eleven of them cannot read what was on the list. He read it word for word with his own name at the foot of it and the hour under his name, being two o'clock, and nobody in that room stopped him at any word, and that is the rule of that room and it is on the face of the paper and was written before the reading and not after it.

Nine of the eleven figures on that list are for victual and for coal and for oil and for hauling and for a day's hire of a lighter, and two of them are for a boat.

**And the man who is asked about his own work is the tenth, and it is the first of the two, being four hundred and eighty pence a month, being three marks, for one boat of about fourteen tons or thereabouts to lie under that line at the top of the flood on the nights of the new moon and take victual off a supply boat on the outside and bring it in between the nine hulls.**

---

**And at about ten past two in the afternoon a working man of about forty-one stood up about nine feet from the reader and asked one question out loud and asked it once.**

He ran a lighter of about eleven tons on the west quay with two men on her and he had that morning been told that the hauling of the week was given to two boats out of the west quay and not to his, and he had not made anything of it at the time, and he had come up those stairs with the list in his head and not on a paper.

"What is that for," he said.

About twenty people in that room heard it and about eleven of them could not read the list and heard all of it.

The reader put his finger on the line and read it again in the same words and then answered it, and what he answered came out of the figure and not out of a man.

"That is three marks a month. I can tell you what is written on that line and I cannot tell you what it is for, and I am not able to do better than that in this room, and here is why. **There is no line on that paper with a reason at the head of it. It has a figure and it has a date and it has a place and it has a name at the foot of it, and that name is a man, and that man is not in this room and he is not coming to this room.** So the figure is the figure and the figure does not say what it is for, and if anybody in this room tells you what it is for, they are telling you what they think it is for and not what anybody put on that paper."

Somebody at the back said that a man could read a thing and know what it was for without anything on it saying so.

"A man can. I am telling you what I can be answerable for in a room where twenty people are standing and about eleven of them cannot read what is in front of them, and it is not what a man with a boat and two men on her wants to hear. If I told you what I think that line is for you would all go away and do what I think it is for, and in a month one of you would come up here and say *he told us it was for that*, and I would be standing where I am standing with a thing I said that was not on the paper."

Nobody argued with him. Nobody asked him a second question and nobody thanked him for reading it out, and the man of about forty-one sat down and did not say anything else in that room, and he has not been asked since and he is not going to be.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone and said where it was standing, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Thirty-four thousand and fifty pence short, being two hundred and twelve marks, ten shillings and tenpence. Unmoved since two o'clock on Tuesday afternoon, and it is unmoved because nothing went out and nothing came in and nobody was asked for anything, and that is a whole day of this month's work written on a sheet in about nine seconds. If a yard is short thirty-four thousand pence and a Tuesday passes and nobody asks anybody for a penny, that is not a good sign about the harbor and it is not a bad one either. It is what this sheet is."

The woman of about twenty-seven of that yard was on the step of the shed with a bucket in her hand and she said that a page that did not move was still a page that had been kept, and the man who keeps the leaf said that was the first sensible thing anybody had said about the sheet this month and she was not thanked for it and neither of them said it a second time.

**Nobody thanked anybody on that day. The four heads on that wall in the room over that market have not changed and no fifth head was added to it on account of a list that came ashore off a boat, and the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on any wall in it and was not read out in it.**

---

**A LIST OF ELEVEN THINGS CAME ASHORE OFF A BOAT ON THE TUESDAY AND WAS READ OUT ONCE IN THAT ROOM ON THE WEDNESDAY WITH THE READER'S OWN NAME AND THE HOUR UNDER IT AND WAS NOT STOPPED AT ANY WORD. FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE A MONTH IS WRITTEN ON THE TENTH LINE OF IT AND THERE IS NO LINE ON THAT PAPER WITH A REASON AT THE HEAD OF IT. THERE IS NO PHYSIC AT FOURTEEN PENCE IN THAT HARBOR. THE NORTH BERTH IS OUT. A LIGHTER OF ABOUT ELEVEN TONS LIES AT THE WEST QUAY WITH HER OWNER'S PAINTER HALF IN AND TWO MEN ON HER WHO DID NOT GET THE HAULING OF THE WEEK. NOTHING WENT OUT ON THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY ON THAT DAY AND NOTHING WENT INTO IT AND IT IS THIRTY-FOUR THOUSAND AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND IT HAS BEEN SINCE THE TUESDAY.**

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fifteenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that a list of eleven things with a figure against each of them and a name at the foot of it came ashore off a supply boat on the fourteenth and went round that market on the fifteenth, and that it was read out word for word in the room over that market at about two o'clock in the afternoon by the man of about thirty-three who hires hands there, with his own name and the hour under it, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it, and that nobody in that room stopped him at any word; and that the tenth line of it is four hundred and eighty pence a month, being three marks, for a boat of about fourteen tons to lie under that line at the top of the flood on the nights of the new moon and bring victual in between the nine hulls, and that a working man of about forty-one who runs a lighter of about eleven tons on the west quay with two men asked out loud and once only what it was for, and was answered out of the figure and not out of a man, the answer being that there is no line on that paper with a reason at the head of it and that the name at the foot of it is a man who is not in that room; and that nobody argued with him and nobody asked him a second question and nobody thanked him for reading it out; and that nothing went onto the sheet that carries the money in any hand in any line on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-four thousand six hundred and ninety pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-four thousand and fifty pence short, being two hundred and twelve marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Tuesday; and that the four heads on that wall have not changed and no fifth head was added to it and that the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on any wall in it; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and forty-six, one thousand and one hundred and four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and forty-five, one thousand and one hundred and two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a list of eleven figures was read out in that room on a Wednesday and not argued about in it is the reader's own, and is that about eleven people in that room cannot read it and that a thing read out is the only form of a thing those eleven can be in the same room with, and that the room did its job on that afternoon by not doing anything else; and that the reason the man of about forty-one was answered out of the figure and not out of a man is the reader's own, and is that the moment a man in that room says what he thinks a line is for, eleven people who cannot read the line will go and act on his thinking, and that a room that lets a man do that once has stopped being a room where things are read out and has become a room where a man is agreed with; and that the reason no reason was invented for that figure by anybody in that harbor is this yard's own mouth, and is that four hundred and eighty pence a month is a figure and a figure is checkable, and that a reason invented for it would be a thing that could be checked against nothing and would be worth less in a month than the figure is now; and that the reason a day on which nothing moved on that sheet is still a day in that yard's book is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that this sheet has a figure for a day that is a straight line and a figure for a day that is not, and that a book that only has the crooked days in it is a book that makes the harbor look worse on the days it was better; and that the reason no old figure in that harbor changed on that Wednesday, in either direction, is this yard's own mouth, and is that a list came ashore and a room heard it and neither of those two things has ever in the history of harbors shortened a single figure that was standing, and that this yard left every old figure in that harbor exactly where it was on that day and would not have touched one to make that room's afternoon go better.*
