# Chapter 505

*Tuesday the Seventh Day of December, the Fifty-Ninth Wage Tuesday, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wipe, the Wage Column in That Harbor Read Out at Ten in the Morning on a Step at the Back of That Market at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest and Not Moving, and a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at Its Close, Three Days of Air Hired off That Same Supply Boat Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten Past Eleven in the Morning at Five Hundred and Forty Pence, Being Three Marks and Five Shillings, With the Fifth of December Against It, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying the Whole of That Line Out Loud Before He Wrote a Figure Against It and Stating That Every Descent From That Day Is on That Line and on Nothing Else, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Twenty-Seven Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty Pence, Being One Hundred and Seventy-Three Marks, Four Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Tuesday the seventh day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Tuesday was the fifty-ninth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Three days of air hired off that same supply boat went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten past eleven in the morning at five hundred and forty pence, being three marks and five shillings, with the fifth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-eight thousand three hundred and seventy pence out, being two hundred and thirty-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-seven thousand seven hundred and thirty pence short, being one hundred and seventy-three marks, four shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninety-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fourteenth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty-five days. The four heads on the wall in the room over that fish market have not changed and no fifth head is going on that wall on account of a line of boats or a notice or a figure. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered and nothing has come in on the entry to move it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head, and he has not said anything about it and nobody in that harbor has asked him and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

**At ten in the morning a clerk of the harbor authority carried the chest out onto the step at the back of that market, and about forty people were standing in that market and every one of them heard it, and about twenty of them cannot read the column that is on the lid.**

"Twenty-six lines," he said. "One hundred and four marks. Twenty envelopes in the chest. I am reading you the column and I am not paying anybody, and I have been reading you this column every second Tuesday since the twelfth day of October and it has not moved once in all that time and it is not going to move this morning because I have read it out."

A woman at the front said the thing that is said.

"Where is it."

"It is not here," the clerk said. "It is on a sheet in an office on the west basin and I have brought the chest and not the sheet, and there is a difference and the difference is that anybody in this market can come and count the envelopes in this chest and nobody in this market can check the sheet in the office without a permit, and I have not got a permit and I am not going to ask for one this morning."

He put the lid back on the chest and the two men who carried it out carried it in again, and nobody followed them, and nobody in that market was paid a penny out of that chest on that Tuesday, and that has been the case on every second Tuesday since the twelfth day of October.

**And at about ten minutes past ten the clerk said the rest of it, out loud, on that step, in the form the harbor authority has been using since the twelfth of October, and it is a form and it is not a sentence anybody wrote this morning.**

"A wage Tuesday is the day a harbor finds out what it owes. Fifty-eight weeks were standing fallen due at the close of the last day of November. **A further week stands fallen due at the close of this day, and that is fifty-nine.** The finding out is not a payment and nobody should go home and tell anybody it was one, and the form says what it says because the form is what this authority has said on every one of these days and I am not going to say a better one on a Tuesday in December."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands at that market put his hand up and asked whether the further week was a further week in the chest or a further week on the sheet.

"It is a further week on the sheet," the clerk said. "There are twenty envelopes in that chest and there have been twenty envelopes in that chest since the twelfth of October and one of them has been opened in that time and I did not open it and it was not opened by me."

Nobody argued with him about the form. About nine people went up out of that market and up the four hundred yards to the room over it and nobody in that market said what they were going to say about the fifty-ninth week until they were standing on the steps.

---

**At about ten past eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and nobody argued with him about any part of it.**

"Three days of air, hired off that same supply boat, being five hundred and forty pence, being three marks and five shillings, and the fifth of December against it, being the day I went down to that quay and agreed it and could not pay for it because she was not alongside and her master's man said she would be on the Friday."

"That is three days of a thing you have not got."

"That is three days of a thing I have hired and have not used and have not paid for until the day it is used, and it goes on this sheet with the Sunday against it and it goes in the total at the foot of this sheet on the day it is worked, which is this morning, and the total you are going to hear this afternoon has moved by five hundred and forty pence and not by three hundred and thirty-six."

He wrote the fifth of December against it and put the pen down flat and said the rest of it, once, and nobody wrote it down and nobody asked him a second question about it.

"**Every descent from that day is on that line and on nothing else.** If a man wants to go down on that air, he goes down on that line or he does not go down, and that goes in the second column and not in the first, and there is no other air in this harbor I can open a line for and there is not going to be one this week."

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, and he said the movement of the day out loud with it.**

"Twenty-seven thousand seven hundred and thirty pence short, being one hundred and seventy-three marks, four shillings and twopence. It has moved by five hundred and forty pence since yesterday afternoon and the whole of that movement is one line about air hired on Sunday. I have not put a penny into this sheet in four days and I am not going to, and the deficit comes out of this yard's own book, and nobody in this harbor has been asked for the rest of it."

---

**A CLERK OF THAT HARBOR AUTHORITY CARRIED A CHEST OUT ONTO A STEP AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND READ A COLUMN ALOUD TO ABOUT FORTY PEOPLE AND TWENTY OF THEM COULD NOT READ THE COLUMN ON THE LID, AND HE READ IT OUT AGAIN IN THE FORM THAT AUTHORITY HAS USED SINCE THE TWELFTH OF OCTOBER AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS PAID A PENNY OUT OF THAT CHEST. FIFTY-NINE WEEKS STAND FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY AND THE FINDING OUT WAS NOT A PAYMENT. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT STEP HAVE NOT CHANGED. THREE DAYS OF AN AIR LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT TEN PAST ELEVEN WITH THE FIFTH OF DECEMBER AGAINST IT AND EVERY DESCENT FROM THAT DAY IS ON THAT LINE AND ON NOTHING ELSE. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-SEVEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NOBODY HAS BEEN ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the seventh day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the fifty-ninth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wage column in that harbor stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest at ten in the morning and at half past ten and did not move, and that fifty-eight weeks stood fallen due at the close of the day before and a further week stands fallen due at the close of this day, and that fifty-nine weeks stand fallen due, and that a wage Tuesday is the day a harbor finds out what it owes and the finding out is not a payment, and that no money was paid out of that chest on that day and no money has been paid out of it since the twelfth day of October; and that three days of air hired off that same supply boat went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten past eleven in the morning at five hundred and forty pence, being three marks and five shillings, with the fifth of December against it, being the day it was agreed and could not be paid for, and that it was written into the total at the foot of that sheet on the day it was worked; and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it and said that every descent from that day is on that line and on nothing else; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-eight thousand three hundred and seventy pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-seven thousand seven hundred and thirty pence short, being one hundred and seventy-three marks, four shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by five hundred and forty pence on that day and nothing came in against it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the air line carries the fifth of December against it and not the seventh is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that the day a thing is agreed is the day a man remembers being asked for it, and that dating that line by the morning a man wrote it would say that this yard had arranged three days of air on a Tuesday when it stood on a quay on a Sunday with a man's man telling it she would be alongside on the Friday; and that the reason the clerk said the same form out loud that he has said since the twelfth of October is the harbor authority's own, and is that an authority which improves its form on the fifty-ninth Tuesday has admitted that the first fifty-eight were the wrong words, and that a form nobody is allowed to improve is a form about eleven people in a market can rely on being the same next Tuesday; and that the reason the further week was entered as a further week and not as a sum is the clerk's own, and is that a sum in a market on a Tuesday becomes a price in a market by the following Thursday, and that the wages in this harbor are a column and a chest and a number of weeks and the three of those are not the same thing and only one of them is money; and that the reason not one old figure in that harbor moved on that Tuesday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a wage Tuesday is a day a harbor looks at its own column and not a day it settles, and that the column was carried out onto a step in front of about forty people and put back in again without a penny of it moving, and that a harbor which settles anything on a Tuesday has stopped being a harbor that counts and has started being a harbor that owes;  and that the reason a day asked for by name was paid at four o'clock on a day when the column forty hundred yards away did not move is the yard's own mouth, and is that the two are kept in different books on purpose, and that a harbor which confuses a day's work with a week's wage is a harbor that will stop paying days and never notice it has done it.*
