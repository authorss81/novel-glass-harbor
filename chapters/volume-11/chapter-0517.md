# Chapter 517

*Sunday the Nineteenth Day of December, a Wipe, the Eighty-Fifth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, Five People Walking Past It and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon and the Reason for That Entered in the Second Column in New Words and Said Out Loud Once, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, Six Sentences Written at the Head of a Page Before the Reading and Read Out Word for Word With the Reader's Own Name and the Hour Under It, Being the Condition a Working Person of That Port of About Fifty Attached to the Answer He Gave on the Thirteenth, and Nobody in That Room Asked Whether They Agreed to It and Nobody Asked to Sign Anything and the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed and No Fifth Head Going On It, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Thirty-Five Thousand Three Hundred and Forty-Six Pence, Being Two Hundred and Twenty Marks, Twelve Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Sunday the nineteenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-five thousand nine hundred and eighty-six pence out, being two hundred and eighty-seven marks, five shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-five thousand three hundred and forty-six pence short, being two hundred and twenty marks, twelve shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifteen days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventeen; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-seven days. One damp brown page lies flat on a board in that room over that market under a weight, cut by nobody, and it is on no sheet in that boat and on no sheet in that harbor, and only one person in that harbor knows where it is and she has said so out loud once. The three marks that stand against the hard at that port are on an anchor date that came in on the entry and have not been re-dated and have not gone, and that entry has not been settled by anybody on that Sunday. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it is eleven days now and he has not said a word about it and nobody in that harbor has asked him.**

---

**The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-fifth time it has come down bare, and it went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and five people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.**

**And at about ten in the morning, with the door of that shed open on the slip, the man who keeps the leaf said out loud, once, why a day of the berth fund was paid on a Sunday, and about four men who were on that slip at the time heard it and it is not the reason the last Sunday of that week carried and it is not the reason the Sunday before that carried.**

"Because a fund that pays a day for a day does not know what day it is, and I am not going to stand at this bench stone on a Sunday and explain to four men why the fund paid them. **A fund is a thing that has to be dull, and the day a fund has to be explained to anybody is the day somebody in an office has to be asked whether it can be dull on a Tuesday instead.** It paid them at ten and it will pay them at ten next Sunday and the Sunday after that, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it this month and I have now said it once."

Nobody asked him about it again that day and nobody has asked him about it since.

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay and has done since he was nine, read out six sentences that were written at the head of a page before anybody in that room knew they were going to be read.**

They are the condition that a working person of that port of about fifty attached to the answer he gave in that room on the thirteenth, and they are in the words he said them in, and the rule of that room is on the face of the page above them, being that the reader's name goes under what he reads and the hour goes under his name and that anybody in that room may stop him at any word and put their own words against him and that what goes into the book of that room is what is on the paper and not what he makes of it going to it.

1. *The boats at that hard will take victual off the outside at the top of the flood and carry it up to the cannery steps.*
2. *They will take coal the same way and they will take nothing else off that hard.*
3. *The first of anything else belonging to this harbor that comes up that hard will be put back on the water where it was found and it will not be opened by anybody at that port.*
4. *Not one man of this harbor comes past the fish quay at that port, and if one comes past the fish quay he will be told to go back and he will not be sorry about it.*
5. *The three marks on that entry are not this harbor's and are not that port's and are on the ninth of November and are going to be on the ninth of November.*
6. *Nobody has agreed to any of this in that harbor and nobody is asked to, and this is a condition and it is not an offer and it does not bind anybody who has not said a word about it.*

**Hobb Lee read those six sentences once, word for word, with his own name and the hour of two o'clock under them, and about eleven people in that room could not read them and heard all of them.**

A man at the back of that room said, out loud, that nobody had agreed to that, and Hobb Lee said that the sixth sentence said so in the plainest words on the page and that he had read it last so that it would be the last thing about eleven people had in their ears on the way out.

"Then what is it for," the man said.

"It is a condition. It is what a man said he would do and what he would not do, and it is on a page because he said it once out loud in this room six days ago and I am not going to have it said a second time in a different shape. **A condition is not an offer. Nobody in this room can accept it, because a condition is not a thing that anybody here can do anything with.**"

Nobody in that room was asked whether they agreed to it. Nobody in that room was asked to sign anything, and nobody was handed anything to sign, and there is no fifth head on that wall and there is not going to be one, and the four heads that are on that wall are the four that were on that wall, and the names at the foot of that sheet are the names that were at the foot of it.

The man of about fifty who fits boat bottoms was not in that room on that Sunday and nobody sent for him and nobody has asked him anything since, and he has not been thanked and he was not thanked on the day he said it either.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, and said that the day had not touched it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Thirty-five thousand three hundred and forty-six pence short, being two hundred and twenty marks, twelve shillings and twopence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Saturday morning and not going to move tomorrow either, because tomorrow is Monday and Monday is a rota day and a rota day is a cistern and a cistern is not a cost. **A rota is not a line on that sheet. It never has been and it is not going to be, and if a hundred and eight of them are ever going to be anybody's, then they are going to be somebody else's.**"

Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody thanked anybody on that Sunday, and the wall went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the eighty-sixth is on the twenty-sixth.

---

**A WALL CAME DOWN BARE BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH FOR THE EIGHTY-FIFTH TIME AND WENT UP UNREPAIRED AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND FIVE PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. A DAY OF THE BERTH FUND PAID AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND A DAY PAID AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK DID NOT MOVE AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IS IN THE BAG UNDER IT. SIX SENTENCES WERE READ OUT ONCE IN A ROOM OVER THAT MARKET ON SUNDAY AFTERNOON WITH THE READER'S OWN NAME AND THE HOUR UNDER THEM AND THE SIXTH OF THEM SAYS THAT NOBODY HAS AGREED TO ANY OF IT AND NOBODY IS ASKED TO. THERE IS A DAMP BROWN PAGE FLAT UNDER A WEIGHT ON A BOARD IN THAT ROOM AND IT IS ON NO SHEET IN THAT HARBOR. THREE MARKS ARE ON AN ENTRY AT THAT OTHER PORT WITH THE NINTH OF NOVEMBER AGAINST THEM AND THEY ARE STILL ON THE NINTH OF NOVEMBER. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-FIVE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the nineteenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-fifth time at about half past ten in the morning and was walked on by none of it and went up unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and that five people of that harbor walked past it and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-five thousand nine hundred and eighty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-five thousand three hundred and forty-six pence short, being two hundred and twenty marks, twelve shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Saturday; and that a day of the berth fund was paid on that Sunday and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once and in new words why, being that a fund that pays a day for a day does not know what day it is; and that six sentences carrying the condition that a working person of that port of about fifty attached to the answer he gave in that room on the thirteenth were written at the head of a page before the reading and were read out word for word once in the room over that market at about two o'clock in the afternoon by Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, with his own name and the hour under them, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read them and heard all of them; and that nobody in that room was asked whether they agreed to it and nobody was asked to sign anything and nothing was handed to anybody to sign, and that the fourth of those six sentences says that not one man of this harbor comes past the fish quay at that port and that the sixth says that nobody has agreed to any of it and that a condition is not an offer; and that the four heads on that wall have not changed and no fifth head was added to it and the names at the foot of that sheet are the names that were at the foot of it; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty, one thousand and one hundred and twenty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and forty-nine, one thousand and one hundred and twenty-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a day of that fund was paid on the eighty-fifth wipe and the reason is not the reason the last wipe carried is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a fund which pays a day for a day is a fund that is allowed to be dull, and that a fund anybody has to be given a reason for is a fund that is about to be given conditions, and that this one has been left alone for eleven weeks on purpose and is going to be left alone until somebody in an office wants it explained; and that the reason six sentences and not nine went onto that page is the reader's own, and is that a man of about fifty said six things in that room on the thirteenth and that six things are what he said, and that the page was written in his order and not in a house order and that the sixth of them is his last one because it is the one that keeps the other five from being an offer; and that the reason nobody in that room was asked to agree to it and nobody was asked to sign anything is this yard's own mouth, and is that a condition that has been agreed to by a room of eleven people who cannot read it is not a condition, and that a page on a wall with four heads on it cannot carry a fifth head and is not going to be given one, and that the four heads on that wall do not represent one thing today and did not represent one thing last week; and that the reason the man of about fifty was not sent for and was not asked about again is this yard's own mouth, and is that a man who has said a thing once for a room does not owe that room a second performance, and that a person who is fetched back to say it again is a person who has been turned into a reading; and that the reason this yard struck nothing and reduced nothing and began nothing on that Sunday and opened no old account at all is this yard's own mouth, and is that eighty-five wipes is a number about a wall and not about a harbor, and that the wall went up on the eighty-fifth at the angle it has been at since the first and no bucket went under it on the eighty-fifth either, and that this yard struck nothing and reduced nothing and did not open a single one of those accounts on the one day of that week when the whole of that town was standing about its doors doing nothing.*
