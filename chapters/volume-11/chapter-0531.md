# Chapter 531

*Sunday the Second Day of January, a Wipe, the Eighty-Seventh Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, Five People Walking Past It and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, the Party on That Coast Road at About Eight in the Morning About Nine Miles Out and the Two Wagons Turned About at About Ten and That Quay at About Half Past Four in the Afternoon, Bread and Salt for Nine Men for Three Days Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at Seven Hundred and Twenty Pence, Being Four Marks, Six Shillings and Eightpence, and One Day of Air at a Cannery on That Coast Road at Three Hundred and Twenty Pence, Being Two Marks Exactly, Both With the Second of January Against Them and Both Said Out Loud Before a Figure Was Written Against Either, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon and the Reason for That Entered in the Second Column in New Words and Said Out Loud Once, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Eight Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Eight Pence, Being Three Hundred and Five Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the second day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and nine miles out on a coast road with two wagons on it.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. The cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it and is not charged on any Sunday and has not been since it was first set there. Bread and salt for nine men for three days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, with the second of January against it, being the day the work was done, and one day of air at a cannery on that coast road went onto the same sheet at about the same hour at three hundred and twenty pence, being two marks exactly, with the second of January against it, being that day, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-nine thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out, being three hundred and seventy-two marks, one shilling and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-eight thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being three hundred and five marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fortieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-one days. A handcart hired off the west basin came back up that coast road at about four in the afternoon with a boat standing upright on it in a cradle of loose shingles, and that boat went into the shipyard on that quay at about six in the evening and two men slept in that yard that night who had not slept in it before. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and the hundred and thirteenth of those is tomorrow. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-nine days and nobody in this harbor has said one word to him about it since the twenty-ninth of December.**

---

**At about eight in the morning, about nine miles out on that coast road, the two wagons and the handcart were standing at a gate where the road crosses a burn, and the party was standing about them, and none of the nine had been asked to be there and all of them had come.**

The man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons was the one who wanted to go on. He wanted to go on because he had been to that end of the road twice in his life and both times he had been paid for the going, and he said so in front of the wagons.

"We have a boat of victual on that cart and a boat of coal under a tarpaulin and nine of us standing in a burn at eight in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four of that port is not here, and I want to know what we are doing standing in a burn."

The man of about thirty-two answered him in about thirty words and said the rest of it once and did not say it again that month.

"There is nothing at the end of that road today that wants nine men. That commonwealth's people are on the water and the victual is not wanted until the water turns and the man who came up it with a chest of tools is not standing at that gate and he is not going to be, and **a party that is not on the water is a party standing about being paid a day a day, and I would rather turn two wagons about on a Sunday than pay nine men a Sunday for standing in a field.** We are going back up that road and we are going to be on that quay before the light goes."

Nobody argued with him about it and nobody thanked him and the wagons were turned about at about ten in the morning.

---

**And at about half past ten in the morning, four hundred yards and nine miles back from where that was said, the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare for the eighty-seventh time, and it went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and five people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.**

---

**At about ten in the morning the two lines went onto that sheet with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Bread and salt for nine men for three days. Seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence. That is two hundred and forty pence a day for nine men and two hundred and forty pence a day is the figure a boat alongside that west quay is selling it at and not the figure this yard has paid it at, and I am paying the boat and not the chalk, and the second of January is against it because that is the day it was done and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon."

"And one day of air. Three hundred and twenty pence, being two marks exactly, for a compressor run in a yard at a cannery on that road for a day while nine men stood about in it, and the second of January is against it and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon."

He put the pen down flat and did not say one word more about either of them, and he said out loud, once, why a day of that berth fund was paid on a Sunday, and it is not the reason either of the last two Sundays carried.

"Because a man of about fifty came into that shed this morning and asked me what it costs to keep nine men standing about on a coast road for a day and I told him what it costs and it is not nothing, and it is not less on a Sunday than on a Wednesday. **That fund is the only thing in this harbor that pays a man for a Sunday and it is going to go on paying him for a Sunday after nobody in this harbor can light a lamp in a house, and the day somebody argues with it about that is the day somebody starts arguing with it about the figure in chalk instead.**"

Nobody asked him about it again that day and nobody has asked him about it since.

---

**And at about half past four in the afternoon two wagons and a handcart came up that quay, and the gear went under the tarpaulin by the pump, and at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door open on the slip, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Forty-eight thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being three hundred and five marks, eight shillings and twopence. That is up by a thousand and forty pence since Saturday morning and a thousand and forty pence is three days of victual for nine men at somebody else's price and one day of air for a compressor, and that is what a day on a coast road costs and it is the whole of what it cost. Fifty-nine thousand five hundred and thirty-eight out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And this yard has spent this month going out of that harbor and has spent this morning coming back into it, and **not one thing standing in this harbor is one penny the better for either of those two facts, and the four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board at that end stall and there is still no flour in this market and the mole head is still dark.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Sunday.

---

**THE WALL BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE EIGHTY-SEVENTH TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT UP AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND FIVE PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. TWO WAGONS AND A HANDCART STOOD AT A GATE ON THAT COAST ROAD AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND WERE TURNED ABOUT AT ABOUT TEN AND WERE ON THAT QUAY AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE WENT ON THAT SHEET FOR THREE DAYS OF VICTUAL FOR NINE MEN AND THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE WENT ON IT FOR ONE DAY OF AIR, AND THE SECOND OF JANUARY IS AGAINST BOTH OF THEM AND BOTH OF THEM ARE IN THE TOTAL AT THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET THIS AFTERNOON. A HANDCART CAME UP THAT ROAD WITH A BOAT UPRIGHT ON IT AND THAT BOAT WENT INTO THAT SHIPYARD AT ABOUT SIX IN THE EVENING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-EIGHT THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND FIVE MARKS, EIGHT SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the second day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-seventh time at about half past ten in the morning and was walked on by none of it and went up unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and that five people of that harbor walked past it and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that two wagons and a handcart with that yard's gear in them stood at a gate about nine miles out on that coast road at about eight in the morning, that the party was nine people and none of them had been asked to be there and all of them had come, and that the wagons were turned about at about ten in the morning and were on that quay at about half past four in the afternoon; and that bread and salt for nine men for three days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, with the second of January against it being the day the work was done and being in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon, and that one day of air at a cannery on that coast road went onto the same sheet at about the same hour at three hundred and twenty pence, being two marks exactly, with the second of January against it and being in the total that afternoon, and that the whole of both lines was said out loud before a figure was written against either of them, and that the figure of two hundred and forty pence a day for nine men is the figure a boat alongside the west quay is selling victual at and is not the figure the board at that end stall carried and is not on this yard's sheet; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-nine thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-eight thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being three hundred and five marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by a thousand and forty pence on that day; and that a day of the berth fund was paid on that Sunday and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once and in words that are neither of the last two, why, being that that fund is the only thing in that harbor that pays a man for a Sunday and is going to go on paying him for a Sunday after nobody in that harbor can light a lamp in a house; and that a handcart hired off the west basin came back up that coast road at about four in the afternoon with a boat upright on it in a cradle of loose shingles and that boat went into the shipyard on that quay at about six in the evening and two men slept in that yard that night; and that the cistern at the back of that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and the hundred and thirteenth of those is tomorrow; and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and is going to still be off it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason those two wagons were turned about on a Sunday morning instead of being pushed on to the end of that road is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a road takes four days each way to a port four days down that coast and that nine men standing at a gate is nine days of a wage against a gate, and that the difference between a day of work and a day of standing is on the book and is visible and that is the whole of why it was turned about; and that the reason a day of air was put on that sheet at three hundred and twenty pence when nobody went into any water that day is his own, and is that a compressor burns air whether or not there is a man in the water, and that a line for a compressor is a line for a compressor and the day a line is not for a person is the day a stranger with a pencil can tell which is which; and that the reason that berth fund paid on that Sunday is the reader's own and is not the reason it paid on the twenty-sixth of December or the nineteenth, and is that once a fund is asked to account for itself to a market it has stopped being a fund and become an office with a slate in it, and that this yard has said nothing about that book for sixteen weeks, and that this yard has said nothing to anybody about that book for sixteen weeks and has no intention of starting on the one Sunday when nine men have walked back up a coast road for nothing; and that the reason not one item owing to a person in that harbor was touched on that day is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that the fortnight began with a shipyard two hundred yards from that bench stone standing with a roof on it and ends with a coast road and a bill, and that between the two of those there is not one page in this harbor that a man could read and see that anything has been paid forward, and that the wall in that room carries what it carried in December and the boards in that market carry what they carried in December and neither of the two is a fifth thing; and that the reason that a boat went into that yard at six in the evening rather than on the Monday is his own, and is that a boat that is standing in a yard on a Monday is a boat that cannot be got out of that yard at all, and that this is a thing a man does when he has a yard and not a thing anybody has said out loud, and that it is now on this page and nobody has been thanked for it.
