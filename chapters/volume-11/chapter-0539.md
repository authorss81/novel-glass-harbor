# Chapter 539

*Monday the Tenth Day of January, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Fifteenth Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking at About Eleven in the Morning for Two More Days in Advance to the Same Four Men Because There Are Three Instead of Four and Being Refused Out Loud in About Twenty Words, the Work Going On at About Three and a Bench Going In on the Monday and a Stack of Sixteen Fathoms of Two-Inch Fir Standing Against the East Wall of That Yard With About a Yard of It Under a Tarpaulin, a Man of About Twenty-Nine Coming Back From Five Days on That Coast With a Crate of Salt Cod in a Cart and Asking at About One in the Afternoon for a Day Asked for by Name and Being Given One at the Figure in Chalk and Sitting in That Shed From One Until Four and Saying Nothing and Being Said Nothing To, Nothing Going Out and Nothing Going In and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Sixty-Five Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Four Hundred and Eleven Marks, Seven Shillings and Sixpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the tenth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in that shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fifteenth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out, being four hundred and seventy-eight marks and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixteenth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-ninth days. A ropewalk of that harbor is worked by one woman of about sixty-one and she was on a quay about a mile from that shed on that Monday morning and nobody sent for her and she has not been in a room of her own for eleven years. The nineteen winters of that harbor are on a page in an office on the west basin and a man of about fifty who fits boat bottoms read that page this month and has not said a word to anybody about it and nobody has asked him. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-seven days and he came back up that coast road on the Saturday morning and has been in this harbor since and nobody in this harbor has asked him about any of it.**

---

**That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning by two men who carried the water to it and did not stop while they carried it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and the next of those is Thursday.**

---

**And at about eleven in the morning a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked, in front of about nine people on that slip, for two more days in advance to the same four men, and he gave his reason in about ten words and the reason was good, and he was refused out loud in about twenty words, and he did not argue with it and he did not take it back and nobody thanked him for asking.**

"There are three instead of four in that yard and the bench is going in today and it will go crooked if I stand and watch it instead of holding it. Two days apiece to two men is six hundred and seventy-two pence and I am not asking this harbor for it, I am asking you, because you are the only person on this quay with a book."

The man who keeps the leaf said no in about twenty words and said the rest of it once.

"No, and I am not going to do it in a smaller figure either. **That sheet is a thing a stranger can check because everything on it was decided before anybody was tired, and adding men to a bill because the work is going badly is how a bill stops being a bill.** You have said the reason out loud in front of nine people and the reason is a good one and it does not matter what the reason is, because the day I put a line on for a man because there were not enough men is the day there are never again enough men. Go and find him a day at the figure in chalk out of your own book and pay him yourself and come back here and put your hand to the bench, which is free."

The man of about thirty-three said that was not a good offer and he would take it anyway, and he went and found him.

---

**And at about one in the afternoon a man of about twenty-nine asked for a day asked for by name, and he gave his name and he was given one at the figure in chalk, and there was nothing else said about him on that slip for three hours.**

He had been in that harbor since the Saturday morning with a crate of salt cod in a cart and a week of a room behind him four days down that coast. He asked for a day, and got one, at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and he was paid at four in the afternoon with the other three, and he sat on a bench in that shed from about one until about four with his forearms on his knees and he did not say anything, and nobody in that shed said anything to him, and nobody asked him about the crate or the room or the hut on the shoulder, and nobody asked him about his left side, and he was not thanked.

At about half past three a man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay came in, looked at him, looked at the bench, and said one sentence to him that was about the bench.

"Mind that end, it has a nail in it."

He said all right.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door open on the slip, and said that the day had not touched it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Saturday morning, and it is going to be unmoved tomorrow, and tomorrow is a wage Tuesday and it is not going to move then either. **Six hundred and seventy-two pence is what it would have cost me to hire two more men for two days and it is the smallest figure anybody in this harbor has put in front of me this month that I have said no to, and I said no to it because the day I start paying for the trouble is the day the sheet stops being a thing anybody can take a pencil to.**"

"And this afternoon a man sat on that bench from one until four and said nothing, and he is paid at the figure in chalk the same as the three men he sat with, and there is no figure in this yard or on that sheet or in this harbor that is set against one ear of that man's head, and there is not going to be one."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Monday, including the man of about thirty-three who asked and was refused and went and found a man anyway.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND FIFTEENTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE ASKED FOR TWO MORE DAYS IN ADVANCE TO THE SAME FOUR MEN AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND WAS REFUSED OUT LOUD IN ABOUT TWENTY WORDS AND WENT AND FOUND A MAN HIMSELF OUT OF HIS OWN BOOK AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK AND CAME BACK AND PUT HIS HAND TO THE BENCH, WHICH IS FREE. A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE ASKED FOR A DAY ASKED FOR BY NAME AT ABOUT ONE IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS GIVEN ONE AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK AND WAS PAID AT FOUR WITH THE OTHER THREE AND SAID NOTHING FROM ONE UNTIL FOUR AND WAS SAID NOTHING TO AND WAS NOT THANKED AND NO FIGURE IN THAT HARBOR IS SET AGAINST HIM. THAT BENCH WENT INTO THAT YARD ON THAT MONDAY AND SIXTEEN FATHOMS OF TWO-INCH FIR ARE STANDING AGAINST ITS EAST WALL WITH ABOUT A YARD OF IT UNDER A TARPAULIN. NOTHING WENT OUT ON ANY LINE AND NOTHING WENT INTO IT AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR ANYTHING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SIXTY-FIVE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the tenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fifteenth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and the next of those is a Thursday; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked in front of about nine people on that slip at about eleven in the morning for two more days in advance to the same four men, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, and gave his reason in about ten words, being that there are three instead of four in that yard and the bench is going in today and will go crooked if a man stands and watches it instead of holding it, and that he said he was not asking this harbor for it but the man who keeps the leaf, and that he was refused out loud in about twenty words and did not argue with it and did not take it back and was not thanked for asking, and that the reason given was that everything on that sheet was decided before anybody was tired and that adding men to a bill because the work is going badly is how a bill stops being a bill, and that he was told to find a man at the figure in chalk out of his own book and pay him himself and come back and put his hand to the bench, which is free, and that he said that was not a good offer and he would take it anyway and went and found a man; and that a man of about twenty-nine asked for a day asked for by name at about one in the afternoon, gave his name, was given one at one hundred and sixty-eight pence and was paid at four in the afternoon with the other three, and that he sat on a bench in that shed from about one until about four and said nothing and nobody in that shed said anything to him, and that nobody asked him about a crate of salt cod in a store on the second lay or about a week of a room four days down that coast or about the hut on that shoulder, and that nobody asked him about his left side, and that no figure in that harbor or on that sheet is set against him and he was not thanked, and that the only words said to him by anybody in that shed between one and four were the words a man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay said about a nail in the end of that bench; and that a bench went into that yard on that Monday and sixteen fathoms of two-inch fir are standing against its east wall with about a yard of it under a tarpaulin; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Sunday; and that a ropewalk of that harbor is worked by one woman of about sixty-one and that she was on a quay about a mile from that shed that morning and nobody sent for her; and that a man of about fifty who fits boat bottoms read the page of nineteen winters in an office on the west basin this month and has said nothing to anybody about it and nobody has asked him.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that six hundred and seventy-two pence was refused is his own and is not the number, and is that the three days apiece to the same four men went onto that sheet on the Thursday because the Thursday is when the work began and not when it might need more men, and that a line that can be added to at two o'clock in the afternoon because a yard is short-handed is a line that can be added to at two o'clock in the afternoon for anything, and this sheet has been in a drawer since the sixteenth of October and has been added to seventeen times and he would like it to still be a thing that could be checked in a minute; and that the reason that man was given a day and nothing else is the reader's own and is not kindness and is not a reward, and is that a day asked for by name is exactly what it says on the slate at that end stall, and he asked for one and he got one and the figure is the figure in chalk because the figure in chalk does not have a clause in it about what a man can hear; and that the reason the reason that no figure is set against that man was put into that yard's own mouth at the close of a working day is his own, and is that a statement made once on a Monday in an empty shed in front of one man who says nothing is a statement that exists, and that this yard does not want anybody to be grateful to it for not putting a number on a person's head, because gratitude for restraint is still a debt and this yard has four men on a wage and no room for another one; and that the reason not one standing debt of that harbor was touched on that Monday is his own, and is that a ropewalk is still worked by one woman who is about a mile off, and that nineteen winters are still on a page in an office and have been read this month by a man who has not spoken, and that a rota board turned over an hour ago with the same name off it as it had on Thursday, and that a market which read a fire and then read a roof and then read a bench and started coming to one thing with which it could threaten an office is a market that has learned the wrong trade; and that the reason that room was shut on that Monday is his own, and is that there is one thing left in this week that wants a room and it is not yet a thing anybody can hear, and that he is not going to read a thing out twice in a month in a room where about eleven of the people cannot read it.
