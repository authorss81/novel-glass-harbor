# Chapter 529

*Friday the Thirty-First Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying the Whole of That Aloud at the Bench Stone and Saying That What the Three of Them Did on the Thirtieth Is Not on That Sheet and Never Is Going to Be and That the Air Is, Three Men Asked About the One Thing They Do Not Agree About, One at a Time and Separately and Not in Front of Each Other, Between About Ten o'Clock in the Morning and About One o'Clock in the Afternoon, Each Giving His Own Account of It in His Own Words, Three Accounts That Do Not Agree Entered in the Second Column in the Order They Were Said and Not Settled and Nobody Asked to Agree with Anybody and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Asked for His Opinion and Saying He Has Not Got One and Is Not Going to Get One, a Man of About Thirty-Two Not Certain Which of the Three Accounts Is His Own and Having Said So Once and Not Asked About It Since and Nobody in That Harbor Having Said a Word About It, the Aftertaste From the Thirtieth Not Lifted and Nobody Having Made It Lift and It Not a Condition to Be Fixed, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Forty-Seven Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Two Hundred and Ninety-Nine Marks, One Shilling and Sixpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the thirty-first day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-eight thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out, being three hundred and sixty-five marks, eight shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-nine marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-nine days. A mold of a trawlman's shot of about nineteen pounds to the mold is on a shelf in that market and it has not been used since the middle of September and nobody in that market has asked for it back. A ropewalk of that harbor is worked by one woman of about sixty-one and she was not in her room of her own on the Saturday and has not had one for eleven years and she is on a quay about a mile from that shed today and nobody sent for her. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-seven days and he told a man on a slip that morning that he could hear the compressor and nobody in that harbor has made anything of it and nobody has asked him to explain it.**

---

**At about half past eight in the morning the man who keeps the leaf wrote three questions at the head of a page on that bench stone, and they were about the one thing the three of them did not agree about on the thirtieth, and they were not altered after that hour by anybody, and each of the three men was asked them one at a time and separately and not in front of either of the other two.**

1. *Yesterday afternoon, off the head of that third lay, the three men said three things about that water and the three things fit together and are more than any one of the three had. One thing the three of them do not agree about, and this question is about that one thing and nothing else.*
2. *Whether that float was being held by the line to the setters mark, or was loose in that water and being carried about by it. That is the question and there is no other part of it.*
3. *A man asked will be asked it in his own words and will be answered in his own words and nobody will be asked to agree with anybody. Whatever a man says goes in the book on this bench stone with the hour against it, in the order it is said, and nobody is going to be asked to say it again and nobody is going to be asked whose it was.*

Nobody was asked his reason. Nobody was asked whether he was sure. Nobody was told which of the three things the other two had said, and each of the three was told before he was asked anything that nobody would be asked to agree with anybody and that nobody would be asked to say it a second time.

---

**The first was the man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay, and he was asked at about ten o'clock in the morning in that shed with the door shut, and he gave his own account of it in about thirty words and it was his own and it was not improved on by anybody.**

"The line held it. There is a setters mark in a stone under it and the line is made fast to that mark and the line was taut the whole time I was down there, and when the water went one way the float came back and when it came back the float went, and that is a thing held. **I do not say that a line does not stretch. I say that a thing that is loose goes where the water puts it and that thing did not go where the water put it.**"

**The second was the man of about thirty-three of the west quay, and he was asked at about half past eleven in the morning in that shed with the door shut, and he gave his own account of it in about thirty-five words and he was not told what the first man had said.**

"I will not agree with the other two and I have not been told what they said and I do not want to be told. That float was loose in my water. The line was there and the line was slack by the time I had been down five minutes and the float was where the water put it and the water went over that chain in a shape and the float went with the shape. **A line that is on a stone is a line that is on a stone. It is not a hand.**"

**The third was the man of about thirty-eight who works that compressor, and he was asked at about one o'clock in the afternoon in that shed with the door shut, and he gave his own account of it in about twenty words and he did not pretend to know more than he knew.**

"I was watching the trough. I was not watching the float and I have not got an account of the float and I am not going to make one up for that book on that bench stone. **Whoever of the two of you is right, the trough is there and it is a foot and a half wide and it goes north and I put my arm in it twice and it went in to the elbow, and that is mine and it is the only thing in that water I would put my name to.**"

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf asked a woman of thirty of no office, in that shed, in front of about nine people, whether three accounts that do not agree about one thing were a finding, and she answered in about forty words, and nobody thanked her for the answer and she was not thanked for anything on that Friday either.**

"A man who says he does not know is not half of a finding. He is a man who has said where his knowledge stops, and that is the only part of the three of them that anybody can lean on tomorrow. **The other two have each got a thing they are sure of and they are sure of different things, and a page that says so is a page that can be read out loud.**"

The man who keeps the leaf said he had not asked for an opinion, and she said he had asked her a question, and he said yes he had, and then said out loud that he had not got an opinion and was not going to get one and that if anybody in that harbor wanted his opinion on whether a float is held or loose he could go down to four fathoms with a slate and find out, and that the slate would be thirty feet of three-inch on its own weight and a figure of twenty-five minutes and a man on a compressor and a cutoff, and that is what the sheet is for.

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him again.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone, and said that the day had not touched it, and said the rest of it once, out loud, in his own mouth.**

"Forty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-nine marks, one shilling and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Thursday morning and not going to move on Saturday either, because Saturday is a Saturday and a Saturday is not a cost. **What the three of them did on the thirtieth is not on that sheet and is never going to be, and the air is, and the air is the only part of Thursday anybody can check with a pencil, and if that is a small thing to have bought with seven marks then it is a small thing and I am not going to pretend it is a bigger one.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Friday.

---

**And the whole of that Friday went in the second column of that leaf in the order it was said, and not one word of the three accounts went into the first column, and a man of about thirty-two was not certain which of the three accounts was his own and said so once at about ten to seven the evening before and has not been asked about it since and nobody in that harbor has said a word about it to him.**

The aftertaste of the thirtieth had not lifted. All three of those men still had it on the Friday, in the mouth, in three separate places, and one of them said out loud in that shed at about four o'clock in the afternoon that it was not getting better and was not getting worse and that he had stopped expecting it to. Nobody asked him what it was like and nobody asked him to describe it and nobody wrote a figure against it, and nobody in that harbor has made it lift and it is not a condition anybody in that harbor is going to fix.

---

**THREE QUESTIONS WERE ON THE HEAD OF A PAGE ON THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT HALF PAST EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND THREE MEN WERE ASKED THEM ONE AT A TIME AND SEPARATELY AND NOT IN FRONT OF EACH OTHER, AT ABOUT TEN O'CLOCK, ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN AND ABOUT ONE O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON, AND NOBODY WAS TOLD WHAT THE OTHER TWO HAD SAID AND NOBODY WAS ASKED HIS REASON AND NOBODY WAS ASKED WHETHER HE WAS SURE. THREE ACCOUNTS WERE GIVEN ABOUT ONE THING AND THEY DO NOT AGREE AND THEY WERE NOT SETTLED AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TO AGREE WITH ANYBODY. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAID OUT LOUD THAT A MAN WHO SAYS HE DOES NOT KNOW IS A MAN WHO HAS SAID WHERE HIS KNOWLEDGE STOPS. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID HE HAD NOT GOT AN OPINION AND WAS NOT GOING TO GET ONE. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO IS NOT CERTAIN WHICH OF THE THREE ACCOUNTS IS HIS OWN. THE AFTERTASTE FROM THE THIRTIETH HAS NOT LIFTED. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-SEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the thirty-first day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-eight thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-nine marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Thursday, and that nothing has been on that sheet since the twenty-eighth of December; and that three questions were written at the head of a page on that bench stone at about half past eight in the morning and were not altered after that hour, and that three men were asked them one at a time and separately and not in front of the other two, at about ten o'clock in the morning, about half past eleven in the morning and about one o'clock in the afternoon, and that each of them was told before he was asked anything that nobody would be asked to agree with anybody and that nobody would be asked to say it a second time, and that nobody was asked his reason and nobody was asked whether he was sure and nobody was told what either of the other two had said; and that the three accounts they gave are about whether that float was held by the line to the setters mark or was loose in that water, and that they do not agree, and that they were not settled and nobody was asked to agree with anybody, and that they went into the second column in the order they were said and not one word of them went into the first column, and that the man of about thirty-eight said that he was watching the trough and not the float and that he was not going to make an account up for that book; and that a woman of thirty of no office said out loud in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon that a man who says he does not know is a man who has said where his knowledge stops, and that the man who keeps the leaf was asked for his opinion and said he had not got one and was not going to get one; and that a man of about thirty-two said out loud at about ten to seven in the evening on the Thursday that he was not certain which of the three accounts was his own, and that nobody in that harbor has asked him about it since and nobody has said a word about it to him; and that the aftertaste from the thirtieth had not lifted on that Friday and nobody in that harbor has made it lift and it is not a condition to be fixed; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and sixty-two, one thousand and one hundred and eighty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and sixty-one, one thousand and one hundred and eighty-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason three accounts that do not agree were left not settled is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a harbor which settles that float is holding two men up and a third one out on the strength of a word, and that the one man who was honest enough to say where his knowledge stops has given that page the only joint it will ever have, and that a joint is worth more in a hard month than a finding; and that the reason the three questions were written at about half past eight in the morning and not in the afternoon is the reader's own, and is that a question written after a man has been asked it is a question that has been put to a man, and a question written before is a question that has been offered, and there is a difference and the difference is the whole of what a room is for; and that the reason nothing about the thirtieth went into the first column is his own, and is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can add two hundred and ninety-eight to two hundred and sixty-two and get five hundred and sixty and can go on checking the running of that sheet by itself as far back as the sixteenth of October, and that three men's mouths in a shed on a Thursday afternoon cannot be added to anything at all, and that the second column is the place for a thing that cannot be added to, and that this yard has two places on that leaf and has never once put the same fact in both; and that the reason the man of about thirty-two was not asked about his own uncertainty is this yard's own mouth, and is that a man who has said out loud in front of nine people that he does not know which of three accounts is his own has told that shed everything he owes it, and that asking him a second time would have turned a thing he said into a thing he owes; and that the reason not one item owing to a man in this harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, struck or shut on that Friday is his own, and is that a mold of nineteen pounds to the mold has been standing on a shelf since the middle of September and nobody in that market has asked for it back, and that the front of a board at that end stall is standing there this morning with no new figure on it, and a week of that winter is four days off being fallen due, and this yard's two wagons go down that coast road today, and there is a room over that market four hundred yards from this shed with about eleven people in it who cannot read what is put in front of them.**
