# Chapter 510

*Sunday the Twelfth Day of December, a Wipe, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench in That Harbor Coming Down Bare for the Eighty-Fourth Time at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Whole at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, a Berth Fund Day Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and the Same Figure Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon and the Reason for That Entered in the Second Column in New Words, a Leaf Read Back at About Ten Past Five in the Evening in the Open at the Head of That Third Lay in the Yard's Own Mouth, All Four Columns Including the Empty One, Read Twice Because About Four Men of That Party Cannot Read, Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading in the Same Words and in the Same Order as Every Other Reading of It and Its Own Derivation Printed on the Face of That Reading, Standing at Two Hundred and Forty-Two and One Thousand and Eighty-Seven at That Hour and at Two Hundred and Forty-Three and One Thousand and Eighty-Nine at the Close of That Day, the Sheet That Carries the Money Read Back After That in Full With the Day Against Every Line Carrying No Count of Persons, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Twenty-Nine Thousand Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Eighty-Three Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, and Not Answered by Anybody, and One Thing Said Out Loud Once at About Twenty to Six in the Evening and in Nobody's Mouth But the Man Who Keeps the Leaf's and Not Solved, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Sunday the twelfth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in the open at the head of that third lay.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty thousand and eighteen pence out, being two hundred and fifty marks, one shilling and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence short, being one hundred and eighty-three marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of those ten days came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ten; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fourteenth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and nineteenth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty days. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay has a hole in the middle of a Tuesday and has it still, and no figure is set against it anywhere and nobody has asked him about it and nobody has thanked him for anything. A man of about thirty-eight is keeping what he is keeping and has not been asked about it and has not said what it is. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against that anywhere and he has not said a word about it this week and nobody has asked him.**

---

**The wall came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-fourth time it has come down bare and gone up unrepaired, and it went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and five people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it, and the man who keeps the leaf was in the open at the head of that lay with a slate and a lamp and did not walk past it at all.**

---

**And at about ten past five in the evening, in the open, in that yard's own mouth, all four columns including the empty one, the leaf was read back, and it was read twice because about four men of that party cannot read, and both definitions came out of the same mouth in the same words and in the same order they came out of in the twenty-second of October and on the first, eleventh and twenty-second of November and on the second of December, and the reading prints its own arithmetic on its own face so that anybody with a pencil can take it and find that it agrees.**

"There are two hundred and forty-two entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and forty-three at the close of the day, and there were two hundred and thirty-three of them at the close of the second day of December past, and **ten have gone in on the ten days from Friday the third day of December to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them has not got an entry against it, which is the first time in fifty-one days that those two things have been the same.** A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has one thousand and eighty-seven lines in it at this hour and one thousand and eighty-nine at the close, and one thousand and thirty-nine of them were in it at the close of the second of December, and **five have gone in on each of those same ten days, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the fifty that have gone in, forty-five went in on the nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and fifty less two is forty-eight, and one thousand and thirty-nine and forty-eight is one thousand and eighty-seven.** That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and forty-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. **Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since, and that boy is not on the water and nobody has asked him why."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these ten days and it has taken no line in the ten days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

---

**And then the sheet that carries the money was read back after that, in full, with the day against every line, and it carries no count of persons, and the three who said no stand on it at the same size as the figures, and none of the three has been asked a second time in any of these ten days and nobody in that harbor has been asked for the rest of it.**

"Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Forty thousand and eighteen pence out, being two hundred and fifty marks, one shilling and twopence. **Twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence short, being one hundred and eighty-three marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against this yard's own book and is nobody's else.** Eight lines went out in these ten days and nothing went in on any of them. Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days, sixteen hundred, the third. A day apiece in advance to the same four men, six hundred and seventy-two, the third. The same victual bought a second time at a figure that had gone up by a fifth, three hundred and twenty, the fourth. Coal for the pump for ten days off that same supply boat, two thousand six hundred and eighty-eight, the fourth. Three days of air hired off that same supply boat, five hundred and forty, the fifth. A second pair of hands for two days, three hundred and thirty-six, the sixth. A buoy, a bell and a line, six hundred and forty, the seventh. A day apiece in advance to the same four men, six hundred and seventy-two, the ninth. That is seven thousand four hundred and sixty-eight pence, being forty-six marks and nine shillings, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And every line on it carries the day the work was done against it and the day it was written into it, and on four of the eight those two days are not the same day, and that is on the face of the sheet and not in a column."

"And on every one of those ten days one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and the whole of the ten days is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence out, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. **Not one penny of the whole of that came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**"

Nobody argued with him. Nobody was asked for the rest of it. About four men of that party stood in the open at the head of that lay and heard all of it twice and one of them asked him to say the second number again and he said it again.

---

**And at about twenty to six in the evening, with the lamp on the bench stone and the slip behind him and about nine people standing in the open at the head of that third lay, he said one thing out loud, once, and nobody in that harbor has repeated it since, and it is in nobody's mouth in this town but his own.**

"**A route held by nine people is a route that stops when one of the nine stops.**"

He said it and then he said the working of it, and the working of it is the whole of what he said and he said it the way a man reads a figure off a slate.

"There are seven names on that page in the room over the market and there are nine of us on this slip who answer for that mark, and the two of us who are not one of those seven names are the man of about thirty-four of the second lay and the man of about fifty who owns the boat of about twenty-two tons, and neither of them has been asked about anything else this week. And the four that did not write a name are not in the nine and are not counted with anybody, and nobody is going to ask any of the four about it again. And a day's work comes in at one hundred and sixty-eight pence whatever is on the water. And a day asked for by name is paid whether anybody is on the water or not. And a man can be asked for a date by somebody with an order and can say no and is not thanked and is not given a face for it. Those are three things and I have said all three of them out loud in this yard in the last ten days. And if the fourth of them is a man who stops going out, then the mark goes over to the weather in a fortnight and the weather is not going to answer for it."

Nobody answered that. A woman at the end of that slip said, out loud, that she had heard him, and he said that was all right, and she did not say it again either.

**It went into the second column of that leaf at about twenty to six in the evening on the twelfth of December with the hour against it and in nobody's mouth but the man who keeps the leaf's. It is not solved and nobody has solved it and nobody is going to solve it this month.**

---

**THE WALL CAME DOWN BARE BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH FOR THE EIGHTY-FOURTH TIME AND WENT UP WHOLE AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH. THE LEAF WAS READ BACK IN THE OPEN AT THE HEAD OF THAT THIRD LAY AT ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE IN THE EVENING, ALL FOUR COLUMNS INCLUDING THE EMPTY ONE, TWICE, AND THE READING PRINTED ITS OWN ARITHMETIC ON ITS OWN FACE AND A STRANGER WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL CAN TAKE IT AND FIND THAT IT AGREES. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY WAS READ BACK AFTER THAT IN FULL WITH THE DAY AGAINST EVERY LINE AND IT CARRIES NO COUNT OF PERSONS AND IT IS TWENTY-NINE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE. THE THIRD COLUMN IS EMPTY AND NOBODY HAS BEEN APPOINTED TO IT. THE FOURTH STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE AND HAS NO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIXTH LINE IN IT AND IS NOT CLOSED AND IS NOT STRUCK. THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN A BAG UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY. THERE ARE NINE HULLS LYING ABOUT TWO SEA MILES OFF THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twelfth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in the open at the head of that third lay: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-fourth time at about half past ten in the morning and was walked on by none of it and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and that five people of that harbor walked past it and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it on that Sunday, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty thousand and eighteen pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence short, being one hundred and eighty-three marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Saturday; and that the leaf was read back at about ten past five in the evening in the open in the yard's own mouth, all four columns including the empty one, and read twice because about four men of that party cannot read, and both definitions out of the same mouth in the same words and in the same order as on the twenty-second of October and the first, eleventh and twenty-second of November and the second of December, and that the reading prints its own arithmetic on its own face, being that ten have gone in on the ten days from the third of December to this day one against each of them with not one day in two of them without an entry against it, which is the first time in fifty-one days that those two things have been the same, and that five have gone in on each of those same ten days, three before the hour of that day's reading and two after it, so that of the fifty that have gone in, forty-five went in on the nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and fifty less two is forty-eight, and one thousand and thirty-nine and forty-eight is one thousand and eighty-seven; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor and he is not on the water; and that the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it and has taken no line in these ten days and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and has not been closed and has not been struck; and that the sheet that carries the money was read back after that in full with the day against every line and carries no count of persons and the three who said no stand on it at the same size as every figure on it and none of the three has been asked a second time in any of these ten days; and that at about twenty to six in the evening, in the open, in the yard's own mouth, and in nobody's mouth but the man who keeps the leaf's, it was said once that a route held by nine people is a route that stops when one of the nine stops, and that nobody answered it and it is not solved.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in the open at the head of that third lay, and no name is against it: that the reason a berth fund day was paid on the last Sunday of those ten days and the reason is not the reason the last Sunday carried is the yard's own mouth, and is that on the Sunday before this the yard said the fund does not care what is standing outside that mole, and that a fund which does not care is a fund nobody has to think about, and that a fund somebody has to think about is a fund that is about to be argued about, and this one has been left alone for ten weeks on purpose and it is going to be left alone for ten more whatever is on that water; and that the reason the first column carried an entry on every one of those ten days is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that the rule has always been one entry a day and that the only reason a day ever went without one in fifty-one days was that nobody was in that town on it, and that a day nobody is in is a day with a hole in it and a hole in a column is a thing a stranger cannot fill in afterwards; and that the reason four of the eight lines on that sheet carry a day against them that is not the day they were written in is his own, and is that a buoy goes into the water on a Wednesday and is paid for on a Saturday and that the day of the water is the day a stranger wants and not the day of the money, and that a page which carries only one of those days is a page that is wrong about three weeks in every year; and that the reason the third column is read back out loud on a Sunday when it is empty is this yard's own and it is not new, and is that an empty column that is read out loud is a column that exists, and that a column nobody reads out is a column that has quietly been closed without anybody saying so; and that the reason not one thing standing in that harbor was touched on that Sunday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a page read back out loud in the open on a Sunday is a page being counted and not a page being cleared, and that a column that is read out is a column that still stands where it stood, and that this yard struck nothing and reduced nothing and did not begin on the eighty-fourth;  and that the reason a route held by nine people being a route that stops when one of the nine stops went into this column at twenty to six on the twelfth of December and not into the first is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that it is the first thing this yard has written down in four months that nobody in this yard can do anything about at all, and that a thing a man can do something about belongs in the first column where a stranger can count it, and that this one is a shape and not an entry, and that a shape is what a second column is for.*
