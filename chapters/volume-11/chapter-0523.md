# Chapter 523

*Saturday the Twenty-Fifth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, Four Lines With His Own Name and an Hour Under Each Going Onto the Face of the Record That Room Keeps at About Half Past Two and Read Out Twice, Word for Word, With About Four People in That Room Who Cannot Read Them and About Eleven Who Heard Both Readings, Eleven Words Said Out Loud in That Room in Front of All of Them and Not a Speech and Not a Thank-You and Not a Restoration, Two Buoys, Two Bells and About Sixty Fathoms of Line Going Onto the Sheet at One Thousand One Hundred and Twenty Pence, Being Seven Marks, With the Twenty-Fifth of December Against It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Three Thousand Four Hundred and Ninety Pence, Being Two Hundred and Seventy-One Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the twenty-fifth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Two buoys, two bells and about sixty fathoms of line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand one hundred and twenty pence, being seven marks, with the twenty-fifth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-four thousand one hundred and thirty pence out, being three hundred and thirty-eight marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-three thousand four hundred and ninety pence short, being two hundred and seventy-one marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundredth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifteenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-three days. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and a miller who has shut his upper door for the winter takes his flour in at the lower one and has done since the middle of November. The north berth of that harbor has been out of use since the spring for want of a fender and the money to buy one. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay has a hole in the middle of a Tuesday and has it still, and nobody in that harbor has asked him about it since the spring and no figure is set against it anywhere. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-one days and he has not said a word about it to anybody and nobody has asked him.**

---

**At about ten in the morning two floats of tarred fir and two cast bells and about sixty fathoms of two-inch stuff went out of that yard on a barrow and along that quay to a boat, and about nine people in that shed worked on them and the man who keeps the leaf put one line on that sheet and said the whole of it out loud first.**

"Two buoys, two bells and about sixty fathoms of line. One thousand one hundred and twenty pence, being seven marks. With the twenty-fifth of December against it, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. **I am not going to say on this slip what those buoys are for, because what they are for is a thing a man of about thirty-four of a port four days down that coast put to this yard on Friday in a room and this yard has not answered it in a room yet, and a thing with a buoy in it does not go in a book before the words of it have been said where about eleven people can hear them.**"

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and the man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was standing at the side of that room with his hands behind him and had not been asked to say anything.**

The man of about thirty-two stood up in about the middle of that room and said, out loud, in front of all of them, that he was going to answer the condition, and that he was going to say it once, and that he was not going to be thanked for it, and that nobody was going to be asked whether they agreed with it.

Somebody at the back of that room said that a man who says in front of a room that he is not going to be thanked is saying it to be thanked. He said no to that in four words and did not say it again.

Then he said the eleven words, and they were these, and they were all of it.

**"We don't set the buoys, don't go out, and aren't told."**

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds, and then a woman at the back of it asked out loud what a person did on that coast, and he said that a person did whatever the crews of that port told him to do and that he would be one of the men carrying victual and coal for a port four days down that coast and that was the whole of it and he would take what was given and would not be one of the men who went out to a float.

He was not thanked and he was not restored by it and nothing standing in that harbor got a shilling cheaper and nobody in that room said one word about what he had given up, because the whole of what he had given up was a thing nobody in that room had ever held in their hands.

---

**At about half past two the man who keeps the leaf put four lines onto the face of the record that room keeps, in that man's own hand, each of them with his own name and an hour under it, and about four people in that room cannot read a line and about eleven heard every word of it.**

1. *That this party does not set a buoy on that coast, and does not hold one, and does not go out to one, at any hour, on any tide, in any weather.*
2. *That this party has given the naming, the keeping and the answering for of every mark on that coast to the crews of that port, and will not ask for any of it back.*
3. *That this party is not told what a route on that coast is doing, and has not asked to be told, and will not ask to be told this winter.*
4. *That this party is one body among the bodies on that coast and is not the body that decides, and a buoy is a thing on a quay and not a desk.*

**Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay and has done since he was nine, read those four lines out twice, word for word, at about half past two and again at about ten to three, with his own name and the hour of each of the two readings at the foot of them, and nobody in that room stopped him at any word in either reading.**

A man at the front of that room asked what the fourth line was for, and Hobb Lee said that it was in the words of the man who wrote it and that he was not going to improve on it, and that a man who has just said in a room that he is not the body that decides ought not to have a fourth line taken out of it by a reader with a better pen than he has.

The man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth read those four lines over the page himself and said that they were a fair copy of what he heard, and that he was not signing anything, and that the third of them was the one that would be true in a month and not on that Saturday.

Nobody asked him to sign anything and nobody was handed anything to sign and the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it.

---

**And the man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth was asked one question at the back of that room, and he answered it out loud, and the answer went into the book of that room, and nobody thanked him for the answer either.**

He was asked whether the crews of that port would come up that coast and put marks here.

"The crews of that port have got a coast of their own and a line of nine boats standing off somebody else's mole, and the last thing those crews want is a mark they have to answer for in a harbor they do not live in. **You will get the condition and you will not get a man.** That is the shape of it and I did not come up here to dress it up."

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about eleven people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Forty-three thousand four hundred and ninety pence short, being two hundred and seventy-one marks, ten shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by one thousand one hundred and twenty pence and that is two buoys and two bells and sixty fathoms of line, and those buoys are not ours, and they are not going to be ours, and I put the line on because they had to be built by somebody and this yard builds them."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, on that slip, that the party had given up the only thing it had ever had that anybody could see, and the man who keeps the leaf said that a man who gives up a thing everybody can see has not given up a thing, he has given up a claim, and that the four heads on that wall were claims and refusals and restricted records and a public correction, and that there were four of them and there were not going to be five, and that a page nobody can read is not a loss to a harbor and is a loss to a man.

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for anything on that Saturday, and the man of about thirty-four slept in that barn at the second lay for the third night running and was on that quay in the morning.

---

**TWO FLOATS OF TARRED FIR AND TWO CAST BELLS AND ABOUT SIXTY FATHOMS OF TWO-INCH STUFF WENT OUT OF THAT YARD ON A BARROW AND ALONG THAT QUAY TO A BOAT. FOUR LINES WENT ONTO THE FACE OF THE RECORD THAT ROOM KEEPS IN ONE MAN'S OWN HAND WITH HIS OWN NAME AND AN HOUR UNDER EACH OF THEM AND WERE READ OUT TWICE, AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO AND AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO THREE, AND NOBODY STOPPED THE READER AT ANY WORD IN EITHER. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. NOBODY WAS ASKED TO SIGN ANYTHING AND NOBODY WAS HANDED ANYTHING TO SIGN. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THAT COMMONWEALTH SAID THAT THE CREWS OF THAT PORT WILL GIVE THE CONDITION AND WILL NOT GIVE A MAN. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-THREE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-fifth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that two buoys, two bells and about sixty fathoms of line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand one hundred and twenty pence, being seven marks, with the twenty-fifth of December against it being the day the work was done, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it and said out loud that he was not putting what those buoys were for into any book until the words of it had been said in a room; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-four thousand one hundred and thirty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-three thousand four hundred and ninety pence short, being two hundred and seventy-one marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved by one thousand one hundred and twenty pence on that day; and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon and about twenty people went up those stairs and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and that a man of about thirty-four of a fisher commonwealth at a port four days down that coast was in that room and was not asked to say anything, and that a man of about thirty-two answered the condition put to this yard on the twenty-fourth out loud in that room in eleven words, being that this party does not set the buoys and does not go out to them and is not told, and that nobody in that room was asked whether they agreed with it and he was not thanked and was not restored by it; and that four lines went onto the face of the record that room keeps at about half past two in that man's own hand with his own name and an hour under each, being that this party does not set a buoy on that coast and does not go out to one, that it has given the naming and the keeping and the answering for of every mark there to the crews of that port, that it is not told what a route on that coast is doing and will not ask to be told, and that this party is one body among the bodies on that coast and is not the body that decides, and that those four lines were read out twice at about half past two and again at about ten to three by the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay with his own name and the hour of each reading at the foot of them, and that nobody stopped him at any word in either reading; and that the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it and nobody was asked to sign anything; and that a man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth said out loud that the crews of that port will give the condition and will not give a man, and that that answer went into the book of that room; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-six, one thousand and one hundred and fifty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-five, one thousand and one hundred and fifty-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason four lines went onto the face of that record and not one is the reader's own, and is that a page which is read out loud in a room has to be a page that can be read out loud, and that four lines is what a man can be held to in a room and nine lines is a speech, and that the house rule on that room is that anybody may stop him at any word and that a rule nobody can use is not a rule; and that the reason the fourth line was written in the words he used and was not tidied by the reader is this yard's own mouth, and is that a man who has said in a room that he is not the body that decides has been handed the most dangerous sentence of the four and the only honest thing to do with it is to leave it exactly as it came out of him; and that the reason those two buoys went onto that sheet before the condition was answered is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a yard is allowed to buy a thing on a Friday and answer a condition on a Saturday, and that what may not happen is a buoys list going up on a wall before the words have been said where eleven people who cannot read them have heard them, and that a list is a thing people can be put on and words are a thing people can be held to and only one of the two is a record; and that the reason not one thing a man in this harbor is owed was settled or bettered on that Saturday is his own, and is that a harbor which has been told out loud that a party is not the body that decides has not thereby got a mark cheaper, and that the woman with that end stall and that slate has four hundred and fifty pence on the front of her board and the figure of the first of June visible above it and both dates on the board, and that there is still no flour in that market, and that the third lay is still a berth short, and that a week of that winter falls due on the morrow.**
