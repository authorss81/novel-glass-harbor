# Chapter 521

*Thursday the Twenty-Third Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Tenth Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It and the Figure of That Hundred Not Said Out Loud a Second Time, Bread and Salt Beef for Nine Men for Ten Days Going Onto the Sheet at Two Thousand Four Hundred Pence, Being Fifteen Marks, at the Figure a Supply Boat Alongside the West Quay Is Selling It at and Eight Hundred Pence More Than the Third of December, a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, Two Wagons and a Handcart Going Down the Coast Road With That Yard's Gear in Them and Back Up It Empty Before Dark and Not to Sea, the Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Not in That Cart and Not Sent For and Nobody in That Harbor Having Asked Whether He Will Be or Will Not Be, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in the Afternoon at Thirty-Nine Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy Pence, Being Two Hundred and Forty-Nine Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the twenty-third day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and tenth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on the eighteenth of November past and is not said out loud a second time. Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at two thousand four hundred pence, being fifteen marks, being the figure a supply boat alongside the west quay is selling that article at, and a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty thousand six hundred and ten pence out, being three hundred and sixteen marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-nine thousand nine hundred and seventy pence short, being two hundred and forty-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and nineteen days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirteenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirtieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-one days. There is one berth short on the third lay and has been since the middle of November, and a master who wants it has a name against it in a book in an office on the west basin and no boat. There has been no physic in that harbor at fourteen pence a bottle since the ninth of November and none is expected. The figure of the first of June is still chalked on the back of the board at an end stall under the figure of the fourth of December, and it is not on any sheet in that yard, and the man who keeps the leaf has said out loud that it can be taken down by whoever sold it and by nobody else. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been nineteen days and it has not been made into anything.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by two men who did not stop walking, and the water went into it out of a hose that is dead until the spring and had to be carried from the butt behind that market in eleven trips, and neither of those two men is at the head of that rota board and the name that is at the head of that rota board is still off it.**

The man who keeps the leaf was in that shed at eight with the door shut and did not go out and watch it, as he does not go out and watch any of the hundred and ten of them.

"The hundred and tenth," he said, to the slate. "Same words as the hundred and ninth. The hundred and ninth was the same words as the hundred and eighth, and I am not going to improve on it because there are two wagons going down a coast road this morning with a handcart behind them."

Nobody said the figure of the hundred out loud on that Thursday. It was said out loud once, on the eighteenth of November, on an after deck, by a man who was asked for it once.

---

**At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put two lines onto that sheet and said the whole of both of them out loud before he wrote a figure against either, and nobody argued with him about any part of them.**

"Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days. Two thousand four hundred pence, being fifteen marks exactly. That is the figure a supply boat alongside the west quay is selling that article at this morning and not the figure I have been paying at since the spring. On the third of December the same nine men and the same ten days came to sixteen hundred pence, so this is eight hundred pence more than the third. The chalk on the step at that end stall said two thousand two hundred and forty on the fourteenth, which is six hundred and forty more than the third, and I am not paying the chalk. I am paying what the boat is selling, and the boat is selling it to men who can turn up in any harbor on this coast and I am not one of them."

He put the pen down flat and waited, and nobody in that shed said anything, and a man of about thirty-eight who works that compressor asked one question and got one answer.

"Will it be the same on the twenty-seventh."

"It will not be the same on the twenty-seventh and I am not going to say what it will be."

"And then a day apiece in advance to the same four men. Four men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence. Their names are in the book the days are paid out of and not on this sheet, and this sheet does not carry a count of persons and never has."

The four men took their day in advance at the bench stone at about half past ten. One of them is about thirty-eight and keeps to himself and was not asked about anything by anybody that morning.

---

**And at about half past ten the two wagons and the handcart went off that quay and down the coast road with that yard's gear in them, and they came back up it before dark the same evening empty, and the boat did not go to sea and is not going to sea for the length of this movement.**

The trench is two days' sail from that harbor and the boat is not pointed at it. The road that goes down that coast goes to a hard, and a cannery, and shore road the whole way, and a working place at the end of it where nobody in this harbor has ever been asked anything. What is in those two wagons is the gear for going down that coast and for staying out of another harbor's way, and the handcart is behind them because the second wagon has a wheel on it off a cart that has not been in use since the autumn and is not a good wheel. **That gear is lying at the end of that road under a tarpaulin tonight with a chain and a padlock on it and nobody standing over it, and the wagons are back up this end to fetch the rest of it, and the rest of it does not go down that road until the last day of this month.**

"Two days down that coast," the man of about thirty-two said, to the men on that slip, "and then a hard, and then people, and nobody up there has been asked for anything by anybody in this yard, including me, and I am not going to arrive up there with a list."

Somebody at the end of that slip asked him when the rest of them were going, and he said that the gear had gone and that the rest of them were going on the last day of the month and not before, and that he would be in this harbor on the Friday and the Saturday and would be in that room whenever it was let out, and that a condition is either answered in a room where about eleven people cannot read it or it is not answered, and that he was not going to answer one on a slip at half past eleven in the morning. The man who keeps the leaf said out loud, in that yard's own mouth, that the man of about thirty-two was right about the room and that this yard would be in that room on the Saturday with a pen and not with anything else.

---

**And the man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down is in this harbor and is not in that cart and was not sent for and nobody in this harbor has asked whether he will come or will not come, and he was on the quay at about eleven o'clock and saw the two of them already out on that road with the handcart behind them and said nothing about it to anybody.**

Nobody in that harbor has asked him. He has a room over a chandler's on the second lay and a bed in it and he has been in this harbor nine weeks and he has not been asked a question by anybody in this yard, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud on that slip that he was not going to be asked either.

"He is not on that sheet and he is not on a list and he is not a thing this yard is doing anything with, and I am not going to send for a man because it would be convenient to have him standing in a room when we get there. **If a man is useful he is useful on the road and if he is not useful on the road then he was not useful in the room either.**"

Nobody agreed with that and nobody argued with it, and the man of about fifty-one was not asked, and by the middle of the day he had a broom out and was sweeping out a hold on the second lay and being paid for it in the book the days are paid out of.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about eleven people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Thirty-nine thousand nine hundred and seventy pence short, being two hundred and forty-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by three thousand and seventy-two pence and that is two lines and both of them are on the sheet with the twenty-third of December against them, and the day those two lines were written is the day the work was done, which is today, and I am not going to get used to that. Fifty thousand six hundred and ten out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

A woman at the end of that group asked him out loud, in front of the rest of them, what the party was going down that coast for, and he said the party was going down that coast because a working man of about thirty-four of a fisher commonwealth at a port four days down that coast had put a condition to something on Sunday, and that what it was for was to be told out loud in a room, and that he was not going to say one word about it on a slip at half past five on a Thursday because a thing said on a slip at half past five on a Thursday is a thing four hundred yards from a room where eleven people cannot read it.

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it. No figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND TENTH TIME AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. THE WATER WENT INTO IT IN ELEVEN CARRIED TRIPS BECAUSE THE HOSE IS DEAD AND WILL BE DEAD UNTIL THE SPRING. TWO THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED PENCE WENT ON THAT SHEET FOR BREAD AND SALT BEEF FOR NINE MEN FOR TEN DAYS AND IT IS EIGHT HUNDRED PENCE MORE THAN THE THIRD OF DECEMBER AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY PENCE MORE THAN THE CHALK ON THE STEP AT THAT END STALL. FOUR MEN TOOK A DAY APIECE IN ADVANCE AT THE BENCH STONE AND THEIR NAMES ARE IN THE BOOK THE DAYS ARE PAID OUT OF. TWO WAGONS AND A HANDCART WENT DOWN THAT COAST ROAD WITH THAT GEAR IN THEM AND CAME BACK UP IT EMPTY BEFORE DARK AND THE BOAT DID NOT GO TO SEA AND THE TRENCH IS TWO DAYS' SAIL AWAY. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-ONE OUT OF A COAST TOWN FOUR DAYS DOWN IS SWEEPING OUT A HOLD ON THE SECOND LAY AND WAS NOT ASKED WHETHER HE WOULD COME. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-NINE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-third day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the hundred and tenth of them was placed on it at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and the figure of that hundred was not said out loud on it; and that bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at two thousand four hundred pence, being fifteen marks, being the figure a supply boat alongside the west quay is selling that article at, being eight hundred pence more than the figure the same article came to on the third of December, and that a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of both lines out loud before he wrote a figure against either, and that neither of those two lines carries the day it is written because the day it is written and the day the work was done are the same day and there is no second day on the face of either; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty thousand six hundred and ten pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-nine thousand nine hundred and seventy pence short, being two hundred and forty-nine marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved on that day by three thousand and seventy-two pence; and that two wagons and a handcart went off that quay at about half past ten in the morning with that yard's gear in them, went down the coast road and came back up it before dark the same evening empty, and did not go to sea, and that the gear is lying at the end of that road and the rest of it does not go down it until the last day of that month, and that the trench is two days' sail from that harbor; and that a man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down was in that harbor on that day and was not in that cart and was not sent for and nobody in that harbor asked whether he would come or not come, and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud at about half past ten that he was not going to be asked either; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-four, one thousand and one hundred and forty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-three, one thousand and one hundred and forty-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that article went onto that sheet at the figure a supply boat alongside the west quay is selling it at and not at the figure on the chalk at the end stall is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a buyer who takes the price off a step is a customer and has begun to think the step is the market, and that this yard is a buyer and has eleven trips of water to carry every Monday and a Thursday and is not in a position to set that step; and that the reason the man of about fifty-one was not sent for is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a man fetched up the road to stand in somebody's room is a man who has been put in a room, and that the only thing this yard has ever had that a stranger could not have had is that it has not sent for a man; and that the reason nobody was told at about half past five what that party is going down that coast for is his own, and is that a condition put to a harbor by a man of about thirty-four is either answered in a room where about eleven people cannot read it or it is not answered, and that a slip at half past five is neither; and that the reason this yard settled nothing that was owed in this harbor and advanced nothing and shut nothing on that Thursday is his own, and is that a hundred and tenth cistern is a cistern and not a debt, and that the three hundred and twenty-fifth night under that pump is a night somebody has to put a bucket under and nobody in this harbor has ever asked him to, and that the nineteen winters are on a page in an office on the west basin where nobody who works here has ever stood.**
