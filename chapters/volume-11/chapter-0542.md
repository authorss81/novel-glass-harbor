# Chapter 542

*Thursday the Thirteenth Day of January, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Sixteenth Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It and the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, Bread and Salt Beef for Nine Men for Two Days at One Thousand Four Hundred and Forty and Coal for Two Nights for the Pumps at Four Hundred and Eighty and Air for Eight Men for the One Night of Friday the Fourteenth at Two Hundred and Eighty Pence a Man and Two Thousand Two Hundred and Forty in All, and a Coil of Line and Tin and Lamp Oil for Three Lamps at Four Hundred and Forty-Eight Going Onto the Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at Four Thousand Six Hundred and Eight Pence in All, Being Twenty-Eight Marks, Ten Shillings and Eightpence, With the Twelfth and the Thirteenth Both on the Face of the First Three of Those Lines and the Thirteenth and the Fourteenth on the Face of the Fourth, Three Crews Asked in the Same Eighteen Words Before Anybody Was Off a Slip and Three Answers and One of Them Asking Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon Who Pays Him and Being Answered at the Figure in Chalk in Advance, the Air for That Night Charged at About Eleven o'Clock in the Morning in a Shipyard Eight Days Under a Roof and Nowhere Else in That Harbor, a Pump Hand of About Forty-Four Asking Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon What Happens If the Plan Fails and Being Answered in About Seventy Words and Not Asked Again, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Seventy Thousand Four Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Four Hundred and Forty Marks, Four Shillings and Tenpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the thirteenth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shipyard on that quay with a roof on it that is eight days old.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and sixteenth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Four lines went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at four thousand six hundred and eight pence in all, being twenty-eight marks, ten shillings and eightpence, with the twelfth and the thirteenth of this month both on the face of the first three of them and the thirteenth and the fourteenth on the face of the fourth. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at eighty-one thousand and ninety-eight pence out, being five hundred and six marks, eleven shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is seventy thousand four hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being four hundred and forty marks, four shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and nineteenth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-two days. A north berth of that harbor is out and has been out since the beginning of December, and the master of a boat of about sixty tons has had it in his own book as a berth since the summer and is not going to move it back into the column. A sluice gate at the back of that market has been out of reach to a boat's side since the spring and two men go in on their knees to it and neither of them is paid for it and it is not on the rota. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty days and he was in that hut from about eight in the evening until about three in the morning on Tuesday and on Wednesday and wrote four lines on the back of a sheet out of a book and has not written a fifth and has not been asked for one.**

---

The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning, and there were two men on it and neither of them is on that rota board, and the hose that is meant to take the water in at the top of it has been dead since the spring, so what went in that morning went in off the butt at the back of that market in eleven trips by hand, on a plank, and it went in at a hole in the side of it and not through that hose. The hundred and sixteenth of those is a figure a clerk on the west basin said out loud once with his own name and the hour under it, and the figure of that hundred is not going to be said out loud a second time this winter.

---

And at about ten in the morning, with the door of that shed open on the slip, the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of each of those four lines out loud before he wrote a figure against any of them, and about eleven people were on that slip.

"Bread and salt for nine men for two days, the twelfth and the thirteenth. One thousand four hundred and forty pence, being nine marks exactly, being eighty pence a head a day and nine heads. **The twelfth is against that line as well as the thirteenth, and both of them are on the face of it, and a man with a leaf and a pencil can take the two days off and find that they are the day before yesterday and today, and that is the whole of the rule and I am not going to say it again this month.**"

"Coal for two nights for the pumps, the twelfth and the thirteenth. Four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks exactly. It is bought off the same cart that has been coming up that quay since the fourth and it is not dearer than the last figure and it is not cheaper, and nobody in this market has been told either."

"Air for eight men for the one night, being Friday the fourteenth of this month, at two hundred and eighty pence a man. Two thousand two hundred and forty pence, being fourteen marks exactly, being eight men at two hundred and eighty and no second day on it, because eight men are going into that water once between half past eleven at night and five to four in the morning and not one of them goes in twice. That is the figure a day's air came in at on the thirtieth of December, being one thousand one hundred and twenty for four men, and a stranger with a pencil can divide one thousand one hundred and twenty by four and get two hundred and eighty, and this yard has not moved that figure since the thirtieth."

"And a coil of line, tin, and lamp oil for three lamps, off a chandler's counter at the head of that quay. Four hundred and forty-eight pence, being two marks, ten shillings and eightpence. Isel Marn put that figure on the counter herself and I did not argue with it and she did not come down off it."

"That is four thousand six hundred and eight pence in all, being twenty-eight marks, ten shillings and eightpence, and eighty-one thousand and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and a man with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those off this sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

---

And at about eleven o'clock in the morning eight bottles were charged in a shipyard on that quay, and there is nowhere else in that harbor to charge a bottle, and that is why they were charged there and not at a cannery on that coast road, and the man who keeps the leaf said the reason out loud before he wrote the air line against the sheet.

"That compressor is in this building and this building was not here on the fifth of this month. **The roof went on in three days and the bench went on in the same three days and there has been a fire in that room every night since the eighth, and on Friday night that fire is what makes a hundred and sixty pounds of air in eight bottles, and every one of those eight bottles is on a line on that slip and on no other line.**"

Nessa Pike, of about twenty-seven, who has that shipyard, ran the compressor herself and charged them herself and checked them herself against a gauge, and she was asked for a figure for the running of it and gave none, and she was not asked a second time and she was not thanked.

---

And at about two in the afternoon, before anybody was off any slip, a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors was asked in the same eighteen words as the other two crews whether he would take a channel of a night on the water at the second point, and he said yes, and he asked one question out loud and it was not about the eighteen words.

He had his hand flat on that bench stone and he said it in about fifty words.

"**What happens if the plan fails.** Not what happens to me. I have been in a compressor house for nineteen years and a thing fails at two in the morning and everybody in the room is a man with a job. I want to know what the eight of us do."

He was answered in about seventy words and nobody argued with him about it afterwards and he did not ask it again.

"Then you come up. **If a bell at the first point goes and the bell at the second point has not gone inside a quarter of an hour of one o'clock, and one o'clock is the hour the first window closes, then the eight of you come up and the third point does not start, and the page says so and the page was read out in this shed yesterday in front of nine people.**"

He said that was a quarter of an hour and not a plan, and that a quarter of an hour is what a man can say out loud in a room, and went off up the quay.

---

And a man of about nineteen of the second lay, who is one of the three whose names are on the third of the three, stood at the corner of that slip and asked out loud who pays him, and was answered in front of about eleven people and did not ask again.

"Who pays me and when and where is it written."

"One hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at the figure in chalk, in advance, on the day before, out of a berth fund that pays a day for a day and does not care what a line of boats is standing outside a mole. **Your name goes in the book the days are paid out of and it does not go on that sheet, and it is not going to, and I am not going to tell you why, because if I told you why you would work out that the sheet could be leaned on and then it could not.**"

He said that was fine and went and told two other men of that lay about the figure and the day before, and neither of those two men was asked for anything.

The man of about thirty-two, who is the third name down on the first of the three and is not the head of any of them, was asked the same eighteen words on that slip at about half past two and said yes in one word, and was not thanked, and did not have a word of it in his own hand on any of the three windows, because the first of those three windows is in the hand of the man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and said that the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.

"Seventy thousand four hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being four hundred and forty marks, four shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by four thousand six hundred and eight pence, and that is victual for nine men for two days, coal for two nights, air for eight men for two days, and a coil of line and lamp oil for three lamps, and **a stranger can take the four of those figures off this sheet and put them into a different order and they still make the same four thousand six hundred and eight, because that is the only property a sheet like this has and it is a poor one and it is the one that is wanted.**"

"Eight bottles of air are charged in a shed eight days under a roof and eight men are going down in about four fathoms in three separate places on Friday night, and this morning the cheapest thing in that harbor was the same as it was on Tuesday, and the lamp oil board is at four hundred and fifty pence, and the mole head is dark, and there is still no flour in that market."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Thursday, and nobody was thanked for the eight bottles and nobody was thanked for the air.

He said one more thing at that bench stone after the others had gone, once, to about four men, and nobody has asked him about it since.

"That page went down that coast road in a cart on the ninth of December and came back up on Wednesday, and a cart takes four days of that road in each direction. **A man in an open boat with a fair tide and nothing in her but a bag takes a day and a night, and I am going down on the evening tide tonight and I will be on that quay before the three boats leave it tomorrow night.** I said that out loud this afternoon in front of nine people so that nobody in this harbor has to be told it twice."

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND SIXTEENTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. FOUR LINES WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AT FOUR THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHT PENCE IN ALL, BEING TWENTY-EIGHT MARKS, TEN SHILLINGS AND EIGHT PENCE, WITH THE TWELFTH AND THE THIRTEENTH ON THE FACE OF ALL FOUR. THREE CREWS WERE ASKED IN THE SAME EIGHTEEN WORDS BEFORE ANYBODY WAS OFF A SLIP AND THREE SAID YES, AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK IS PAID IN ADVANCE ON THE DAY BEFORE AND THE NAMES GO IN A BOOK AND NOT ON THAT SHEET. EIGHT BOTTLES WERE CHARGED AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN A SHIPYARD EIGHT DAYS UNDER A ROOF BECAUSE THERE IS NOWHERE ELSE IN THAT HARBOR TO CHARGE A BOTTLE. A PUMP HAND OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR ASKED WHAT HAPPENS IF THE PLAN FAILS AND WAS ANSWERED THAT EIGHT MEN COME UP. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SEVENTY THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY MARKS, FOUR SHILLINGS AND TEN PENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the thirteenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and sixteenth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand because the hose is dead until the spring and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and that a clerk on the west basin said the figure of that hundred out loud once with his own name and the hour under it; and that four lines went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning, being bread and salt for nine men for two days at one thousand four hundred and forty pence, being eighty pence a head a day, being nine marks, with the twelfth of January and the thirteenth both on the face of that line; coal for two nights for the pumps at four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks, with the twelfth and the thirteenth on the face of it; air for eight men for the one night of Friday the fourteenth at two hundred and eighty pence a man at two thousand two hundred and forty pence, being fourteen marks, being eight men at two hundred and eighty and no second day on it, with the thirteenth of January and the fourteenth on the face of it, being the same figure a day's air came in at on the thirtieth of December for four men; and a coil of line, tin and lamp oil for three lamps off a chandler's counter at four hundred and forty-eight pence, being two marks, ten shillings and eightpence, put on that counter by the woman who keeps it and not argued with; and that those four are four thousand six hundred and eight pence, being twenty-eight marks, ten shillings and eightpence, and that the whole of each of the four was said out loud before a figure was written against it; and that eight bottles were charged at about eleven in the morning in a shipyard on that quay because that compressor is in that building and there is nowhere else in that harbor to charge a bottle, and that the person who ran it gave no figure for the running of it and was asked for none a second time; and that the three crews were asked the same eighteen words before anybody was off a slip and all three said yes, and that a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors asked out loud what happens if the plan fails and was answered in about seventy words, being that eight men come up and the third point does not start, and did not ask it again; and that a man of about nineteen of the second lay asked out loud who pays him and was told one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at the figure in chalk in advance on the day before and that his name is in a book and not on that sheet, and that two other men of that lay were told the figure and the day before and neither of them was asked for anything; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at eighty-one thousand and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is seventy thousand four hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being four hundred and forty marks, four shillings and tenpence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and seventy-five, one thousand two hundred and forty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and seventy-four, one thousand two hundred and forty-seven; and that the third column took no line and nobody has been put in it and the boy's hand is at the head of it and is not scratched out, and that the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the air for that night is charged in a shipyard and not at a cannery on that coast road is his own, and is that the figure is the same either way and the difference is whose floor it stands on, and that a bottle charged on a floor this yard pays for is a bottle somebody can point at when it is short, and that a roof eight days old is not a reason to charge air anywhere else; and that the reason the three names on the first of those three windows are not the same three men as the three on the second is his own, and is that eight men who have all said yes look like one crew on a page and are not one crew, and that a man told on a Thursday that somebody else is on the water beside him behaves differently on a Friday from a man told that the water is his alone, and that the only one of the eight who can be asked the question is the one who was asked it in a room in front of nine people while he was still dry; and that the reason a man of about nineteen was told the figure and the day and not the sheet is his own, and is that a boy who has been given a figure and a day will turn up, and a boy who has been given a page with a name at the top of it will read the top of the page first; and that the reason the man of about thirty-two had not a word of the first window in his own hand is his own, and is that a man asked to be the first name on three things at once is a man who is on all three of them by the small hours, and that three names in a column, with a hand against each of them, can be read off a sheet by anybody who cannot read a word of anything else, and that one name at the top of three asks the reader to take the rest on trust; and that on the night of the three that page is about eight men and three tides and a woman of about thirty-four standing off the third point in a boat of her own; and that the reason nothing owing to any person in that harbor was touched on that Thursday is the person whose yard it is, and is that there is not one sill under that roof and she told the yard the same on the ninth and the sill is not on this sheet and the wall and the sills and the doors and the glass are not on this sheet, and that when they are they will be a second figure and not this one, and that a roof with nothing under it is a roof that a man is standing under in the rain and calling a plan.*
