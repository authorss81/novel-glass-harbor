# Chapter 545

*Sunday the Sixteenth Day of January, a Wipe, the Eighty-Ninth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, Seven People Walking Past It Between Then and the Middle of the Day and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon and the Reason for That Entered in the Second Column in New Words and Said Out Loud Once at About Eleven, a Room over That Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Putting a Page of What This Harbor Can Move and What It Cannot on That Long Table and Reading It Out Word for Word Once With Her Own Name and the Hour Under It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Asking Her in Front of Everybody for Her Help With Two Bills and Getting an Answer on Her Own Terms, a Man of About Thirty-Five of the First Lay Asking Out Loud Whether the Three Points Belong to This Harbor and Being Told That a Page Goes Down a Coast in a Cart and Does Not Come Back, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Seventy-One Thousand One Hundred and Thirty Pence, Being Four Hundred and Forty-Four Marks, Seven Shillings and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the sixteenth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at eighty-one thousand seven hundred and seventy pence out, being five hundred and eleven marks and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is seventy-one thousand one hundred and thirty pence short, being four hundred and forty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-five days. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid on a page in an office on the west basin and nobody in that harbor has taken that page up since the spring, and the tide gang of that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the spring. A bolt of new cordage came up off a cart at the west quay on the Saturday and is standing in the passage of a store with a price chalked on the end of it in a hand nobody has read, and the price on that end of it is higher than the price on a board in that market and both of them are standing. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-three days and he was in that shed for about an hour on the Saturday morning saying nothing and was said nothing to, and he has not been to the road with the pump on it.**

---

**The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-ninth time it has come down bare, and it went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and seven people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.**

---

**And at about eleven o'clock in the morning, with the door of that shed open on the slip, the man who keeps the leaf said out loud, once, why a day of the berth fund was paid on the eighty-ninth wipe, and about four men who were on that slip at the time heard it, and it is not the reason the last wipe carried and it is not the reason the wipe before that carried.**

"This Sunday two men carried water up eleven steps to a cistern that is already full, and a third man put a wall up at an angle that is not right, and a fund that pays on five days has a choice on the sixth of not paying. **The choice is always made by somebody who is not the man who carried the water, and the figure in chalk does not know what day of the week it is, and neither does the man on the step, and that is the whole reason the ten of them are sound.**"

Nobody asked him about it again that day and nobody has asked him about it since.

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and a woman of thirty of no office, who is not on this party's crew and is not its mouth and is not restored and is not forgiven, put a page on that long table and read it out word for word, once, and her own name and the hour went at the foot of it in her own hand, and nobody in that room stopped her at any word.**

This is what she read.

1. *That nine boats of that harbor went inside a line of boats four days down this coast on the night of Friday the fourteenth and seven of them came back, and that two did not, and that both of those two were found on the ground on the Saturday morning with a hole under the forefoot of each of them and their gear in the bottom of them and nobody standing on either of them.*
2. *That nine hulls were off the head of that mole on the fifteenth and there are nine hulls off the head of that mole this afternoon, and that this harbor is not in a position to say why and is not going to guess at it in a room on a Sunday.*
3. *That there is no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and that the lamp oil board is at four hundred and fifty pence with the first of June above it, and that the mole head has been dark since the seventh of May, and that neither of those things is because of a line of boats and neither of them is better than it was on the seventh of December.*
4. *That the wage column in this harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and did not move on the sixty-fifth wage Tuesday tomorrow, being the eighteenth, and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of it, being the sixty-fifth.*
5. *That a page carrying four names and five marks went up on the boards of the floor of this market on the twenty-sixth of December and that the name of no man of this harbor is on it, and that this harbor is one body among the bodies on that coast and is not the body that decides.*

**There is a sixth line on that page and she did not read it out, and the reason she did not read it out is in the second column of that leaf in her own words and is on no wall in that room, and nobody in that room asked her about it and nobody has asked her about it since.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-two asked that woman of thirty for her help in front of about twenty people, and asked her for it and did not assume it, and she gave him an answer on her own terms, and nobody in that room thanked either of them for it.**

He stood up where the long table is and said it in about twenty-five words, and he said her name first, and about eleven people in that room could not read what was on the table and heard all of it.

"There are two bills going onto my sheet on Tuesday and on Thursday, being two boats of that port that are on the ground at the moment, and I have got a sheet that a stranger can take a figure off, and I cannot read a figure out loud to a room without making a mess of it, and I am asking you to carry both of those figures into whatever you are keeping, in your own hand, on your own page, and I am not asking you to put my name on it and I am not asking you for anything else."

She said no to the first part and yes to the second, and said both of them out loud in about forty words, and about eleven people in that room heard all of it.

"No. **I will not read your figures out in a room, because a figure a man says out loud with his mouth is his and a figure on a page is anybody's, and I have watched eleven people in this room not be told things for a year and I have made my own arrangements about it. What I will do is this.** I will write on my own page that two boats of that port were found on the ground on the fifteenth with a hole under the forefoot of each of them, and I will write the date, and I will not write a penny on it, and if a man in this room wants to know what a boat costs he can go to a bench stone four hundred yards from here and read it off a piece of paper himself, and if he cannot read then that is a fault of his and not of the figure."

He said that was fair and thanked her, and she said out loud that he was not to thank her in a room with eleven people in it, and he did not do it again that day.

---

**And a man of about thirty-five of the first lay stood up in that room at about half past two and asked out loud whether the three points belong to this harbor now, and was answered by the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay and who had put that page up on four tacks on the boards of that market on the Sunday the twenty-sixth of December with a hammer and a bit of his own sentence about it.**

"No. **That page went down that coast in a cart on the Monday and it does not come back, and a name goes on it by a person writing it there, and a man in this room cannot put a name on it from this room, and I would not take it off if he could.**"

He said that was what he had wanted to know and sat down, and a woman of about thirty-one asked him afterwards in the passage what he had wanted it for, and he said he wanted to know whether to write his daughter's name down when she was fifteen, and she said that was a different question and he said it was and went home.

Nobody in that room decided anything, and a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that room at the back and said nothing and was not asked, and a man of about thirty-eight of that harbor was in that room and kept what he is keeping and was not asked about it, and the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and is not in it.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and said that the day had not touched it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Seventy-one thousand one hundred and thirty pence short, being four hundred and forty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Saturday morning and it is not going to move tomorrow either, because tomorrow is a Monday and a Monday is a cistern and a cistern is not a cost. **A page with five lines on it was read out in a room four hundred yards from this slip today by a woman with no office on it, and not one penny of it came from that room and not one figure of it is on this sheet, and a woman of thirty stood up in front of twenty people and refused me a thing and gave me a different one and neither of those was a favour.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Sunday, and nobody was thanked for the page and nobody was thanked for the refusal and nobody was thanked for the five lines and the five lines were read once.

---

**THE WALL BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE EIGHTY-NINTH TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT UP WHOLE AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND SEVEN PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. A DAY OF THE BERTH FUND WAS PAID AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND A DAY WAS PAID AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK DID NOT MOVE AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IS IN THE BAG UNDER IT. A PAGE OF FIVE LINES WAS READ OUT ONCE IN THAT ROOM OVER THAT MARKET BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE WITH HER OWN NAME AND THE HOUR UNDER IT AND A SIXTH LINE ON IT WAS NOT READ OUT. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO ASKED THAT WOMAN FOR HER HELP IN PUBLIC AND WAS REFUSED ONE THING AND GIVEN ANOTHER AND WAS TOLD NOT TO THANK HER IN A ROOM. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT AND THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ON NO WALL IN THAT ROOM. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SEVENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the sixteenth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-ninth time at about half past ten in the morning and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and that seven people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the reason a day of the berth fund was paid on that Sunday was said out loud once at about eleven in the morning with the door of that shed open on the slip and about four men heard it, and that it is not the reason the last wipe carried and is not the reason the wipe before that carried; and that a room over the floor of that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read what is put in front of them, and that a woman of thirty of no office put a page of five lines on that long table and read it out word for word once with her own name and the hour at the foot of it in her own hand, and that about nine boats of that harbor went inside a line of boats four days down that coast on the night of the fourteenth and seven came back and two did not, and that both of those two were found on the ground on the Saturday morning with a hole under the forefoot of each of them and their gear in the bottom of them and nobody standing on either of them, and that nine hulls were off the head of that mole on the fifteenth and there are nine off the head of that mole this afternoon and that this harbor is not in a position to say why; and that there is a sixth line on that page and it was not read out and the reason is in the second column in her own words and nobody in that room asked her about it and nobody has asked her since; and that the man of about thirty-two asked that woman for her help in front of about twenty people and did not assume it, and that she refused the first part of it and agreed to the second, and that she said out loud that she would write that two boats of that port were found on the ground on the fifteenth with a hole under the forefoot of each of them and would write no penny on it and that a man who cannot read a figure can go to a bench stone and read it himself, and that he thanked her once and she told him not to do it in a room with eleven people in it and he did not do it again that day; and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay asked out loud in that room whether the three points belong to this harbor and was told that that page went down that coast in a cart on the Monday and does not come back and a name goes on it by a person writing it there; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it and nobody was asked for anything, and that that sheet stood at eighty-one thousand seven hundred and seventy pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is seventy-one thousand one hundred and thirty pence short, being four hundred and forty-four marks, seven shillings and sixpence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and seventy-eight, one thousand two hundred and sixty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and seventy-seven, one thousand two hundred and sixty-two; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that sixth line was not read out is hers and she gave it in six words to one man afterward on the stair, being that a line not said out loud is not a line, and that the five lines she did read out are the whole of what this harbor can be checked on and that the sixth one is a thing she is doing on her own page and on her own page it can wait for her; and that the reason he asked her in front of twenty people instead of asking her on a step beforehand is his own, and is that a thing asked in a room is a thing a person who cannot read has heard, and that if she had refused him on a step at half past one then there would be no record anywhere that she had been asked and no record anywhere that she had said no; and that the reason she said no to the figures and yes to the two boats is hers, and is that a figure in a mouth belongs to the man who said it and a figure on a page belongs to everybody, and that about eleven people in that room have spent eleven months being handed the first kind by people who meant well; and that the reason he thanked her once and was told not to is hers and it is not a correction and it is not a rebuke, and is that a person who leads a thing of her own in a room of about twenty is owed the standing of somebody who is not being helped, and that being helped in public is a way of being helped twice; and that the reason no sum owing to that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Sunday is his own, and is that five lines were read out in a room four hundred yards from this slip and every one of the five said the same thing in a different way, being that this harbor is poorer than it was in October and the only things on that page that moved were two boats onto a stone four days down that coast.*
