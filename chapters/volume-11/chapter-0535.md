# Chapter 535

*Thursday the Sixth Day of January, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Fourteenth Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, the Person Whose Yard It Is Saying Out Loud in About Two Hundred Yards of It at About Ten in the Morning in Eleven Words That It Is a Bill and Not a Wound and Not Saying It a Second Time and Not Being Thanked, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking Her to Say It Again for the People at the End of That Quay and Being Refused in About Nine Words, Three Days Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at Two Thousand and Sixteen Pence, Being Twelve Marks and Eight Shillings, With the Sixth of January Against It, and Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Fifty-One Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Four Pence, Being Three Hundred and Twenty-Four Marks, Two Shillings and Tenpence, the Third Lay a Berth Short This Month and the Shed at the Head of It Not a Yard Since Wednesday Morning, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the sixth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in about two hundred yards of a shipyard on that quay.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fourteenth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Three days apiece in advance to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings, with the sixth of January against it, being the day the work was begun, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at sixty-two thousand five hundred and fourteen pence out, being three hundred and ninety marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is fifty-one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four pence short, being three hundred and twenty-four marks, two shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twelfth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-five days. The third lay is a berth short this month and the shed at the head of it has not been a yard since Wednesday morning, and a berth is a length of stone and not a building and has been a length of stone since the spring. A drum of oil came ashore on the twenty-ninth of December at a figure nobody put on a board and it stands in a store on the second lay with that figure in a pocket book in a coat, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is on the board at that end stall this morning with the figure of the first of June above it and both dates on the board. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-three days and he went down the coast road on Tuesday morning in the seat of a cart and did not come back up it until after dark and nobody in this harbor sent him anywhere or asked him where he was going.**

---

**That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning by two men who carried the water to it and did not stop while they carried it, and the hundred and fourteenth of them is two days from now being a Monday, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it.**

---

**And at about ten in the morning, with about fourteen people standing in about two hundred yards of it, the person whose yard it is said eleven words out loud, and said them once, and did not say them again that month, and was not thanked for them by anybody in that harbor on that day or on any day after it.**

"It is a bill and not a wound. Say it that way round. Nobody is going to feel better and I am not asking anybody to feel better."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood about ten feet off her and asked her, in front of about fourteen people, to say it again louder so that the people at the end of that quay could hear it, and she said no in about nine words.

"You were not standing here at ten o'clock yesterday and neither was anybody at the end of that quay. **A thing said twice for the people who were not there is not said louder, it is said to the people who were, and I have got fourteen of them here and I am not going to perform it at the far end.**"

He said that was fair and he did not ask her a second time.

---

**And then she did not stop there, and what she did for the rest of that hour was the thing she is the only person in that harbor to do, and she did it in hours and material off a second slate, and the four figures and the sum of them were Tuesday's four figures and Tuesday's sum.**

"Right. What a roof is. Forty fathoms of three-inch, two hundred and forty a fathom, and the boarding dressed to it, nine thousand six hundred pence. What a bench is, three days of one man's hours at the figure in chalk, five hundred and four. Stock to keep this yard working, sixteen fathoms of two-inch, two hundred a fathom, three thousand two hundred. **Fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence, being ninety-five marks and ten shillings, and that is Tuesday's slate and it has not changed by a penny and it is not going to change because a wall is standing open to the sky.**"

"What changes is the days, and I will say them in the open because they are the part that is going to be wrong. Two men on that roof is a fortnight and three men is five days, and I have three, because two of the four went off on Wednesday night to a cannery at the end of that coast road and came back this morning and I did not ask them why and they did not tell me. **So the roof goes up on the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, and if the weather turns on the ninth I am going to stand in this yard and be wrong out loud and I am not going to buy a fourth day and pretend this week had one in it.** The bench goes in on the eighth, because a bench made under a roof that is not on yet is a bench that gets wet for nine months first, and the man whose hours those three days are is not one of the three on the roof. The stock comes off a cart on the eighth from the mill that goes off the coast road before the burn."

A man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay asked her how she knew the roof would take five days with three men, and she answered him in about six words and he did not argue.

"You have been in that yard for eleven years and you have watched me be wrong about a figure three times. This is the fourth time and you may write it on whatever you like."

---

**And the four men were hired at about ten in the morning at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day apiece for three days in advance, and the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and about fourteen people were on that slip.**

"Three days apiece in advance to the same four men. Two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings, being four men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day for the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, being the figure in chalk. **Those four men are not on that sheet and are not counted on it and their names are in the book the days are paid out of, and that sheet carries no count of persons and has never carried one.** The sixth of January is against it because that is the day the work begins and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. The roof and the bench and the stock are not on that line and are not on that sheet today and they go on on the day the work is done and not before, and if they are not on there then they were not done."

He put the pen down flat and said one more thing about it, once.

"**This yard is not asking this harbor for a penny of that roof and did not ask on Tuesday when she priced it, and the only figure that is going to move on that sheet is one that this yard is answerable for. I would rather be answerable for one line than be thanked for a hundred, and there is nobody in this harbor to thank, because nobody in this harbor has been asked.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for anything on that Thursday.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door open on the slip, and said that the day had moved it.**

"Fifty-one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four pence short, being three hundred and twenty-four marks, two shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by two thousand and sixteen pence and that is three days apiece to four men who start at eight o'clock tomorrow, and both those days are today. Sixty-two thousand five hundred and fourteen out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence is sitting on a slate in that yard and none of it is on that sheet, and that is correct, and on the day the last of that roof is on this is the day it goes on. **Nobody in this harbor is a penny better off for any part of it and nobody in this harbor is a penny worse off for any part of it, and the third lay is still a berth short and the lamp oil is still at four hundred and fifty pence and the mole head is still dark, and those three have not moved today and are not going to move on the day that roof goes on.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Thursday.

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND FOURTEENTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE PERSON WHOSE YARD IT IS SAID AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN ABOUT FOURTEEN PEOPLE THAT IT IS A BILL AND NOT A WOUND, AND SAID IT ONCE, AND WAS ASKED BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE TO SAY IT AGAIN LOUDER FOR THE PEOPLE AT THE END OF THAT QUAY AND REFUSED IN NINE WORDS BECAUSE A THING SAID TWICE FOR THE PEOPLE WHO WERE NOT THERE IS SAID TO THE PEOPLE WHO WERE. FOUR MEN WERE HIRED AT ABOUT TEN THAT MORNING AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE A DAY APIECE FOR THREE DAYS IN ADVANCE, BEING TWO THOUSAND AND SIXTEEN PENCE, AND THAT LINE CARRIES THE SIXTH OF JANUARY AGAINST IT AND IS IN THE TOTAL THIS AFTERNOON AND THOSE FOUR MEN ARE NOT ON THAT SHEET AND ARE NOT COUNTED ON IT. THE ROOF GOES UP ON THE SIXTH, THE SEVENTH AND THE EIGHTH, AND THE BENCH GOES IN ON THE EIGHTH, AND THE STOCK COMES ON THE EIGHTH, AND FIFTEEN THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE IS ON A SLATE AND NONE OF IT IS ON THAT SHEET. NOTHING WENT INTO THAT SHEET IN ANY HAND AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR ANYTHING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS ON THE BOARD AT THAT END STALL WITH THE FIGURE OF THE FIRST OF JUNE ABOVE IT AND BOTH DATES ON THE BOARD. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FIFTY-ONE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the sixth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fourteenth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that about fourteen people were standing in about two hundred yards of that yard at about ten in the morning and that the person whose yard it is said out loud, in eleven words and once, that it is a bill and not a wound, and that she said that nobody is going to feel better and that she was not asking anybody to feel better, and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked her out loud to say it again louder for the people at the end of that quay and was refused in about nine words and said that was fair and did not ask a second time; and that she gave the whole of that figure again off a second slate, being a roof at nine thousand six hundred pence, a bench at five hundred and four, a stock at three thousand two hundred, and three days apiece in advance to the same four men at two thousand and sixteen, being fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence, being ninety-five marks and ten shillings, and that she said out loud that that is Tuesday's slate and has not changed by a penny, and that she gave the days in the open and said that two men on that roof is a fortnight and three men is five days and that she has three because two of those four went off to a cannery on Wednesday night and came back on the Thursday morning and were not asked about it, and that the roof goes up on the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, and that the bench goes in on the eighth because a bench made under a roof that is not on yet gets wet for nine months first and the man whose hours those three days are is not one of the three on the roof, and that the stock comes off a cart on the eighth from the mill that goes off the coast road before the burn; and that the four men were hired at about ten in the morning at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day apiece for three days in advance at two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings, with the sixth of January against it being the day the work begins and being in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon, and that the whole of that line was said out loud before a figure was written against it and that those four men are not on that sheet and are not counted on it and that that sheet carries no count of persons; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at sixty-two thousand five hundred and fourteen pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is fifty-one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four pence short, being three hundred and twenty-four marks, two shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved by two thousand and sixteen pence on that day; and that the third lay is a berth short this month and the shed at the head of it has not been a yard since Wednesday morning; and that a drum of oil came ashore on the twenty-ninth of December at a figure nobody put on a board and that figure is in a pocket book in a coat and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is still on the board at that end stall with the figure of the first of June above it and both dates on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that sentence was said once and not again is hers and is not a modesty, and is that a sentence about a loss belongs to the morning it happened, and that by the afternoon of the day after it belongs to whoever heard it first, and that a thing repeated on a quay becomes a thing said on behalf of people, which is the one job on a coast she has never wanted; and that the reason a man who hires hands was refused a second reading is the reader's own, and is that a person who was not standing in a yard at ten o'clock on Wednesday has no claim on what was said in it, and that about eleven people in a room over a market who cannot read a thing are owed the whole of it once and not a summary of it twice; and that the reason the days were given out loud in the open and not only the money is his own, and is that this sheet has never once carried a day without a figure and has never once carried a figure without a day, and that a person who reads pence off a sheet and does not read days off it is reading half of a bill and the half that looks better; and that the reason that four men were taken on for three days at ten o'clock in the morning and not settled until the afternoon is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a day apiece in advance is the only thing in this yard that a stranger can take off a page and believe, and that the figure on that line is one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day and has been since the first of December and is not going to be two hundred for a shed with a roof on it; and that the reason not one standing matter of that harbor was touched on that Thursday is his own, and is that the rota board at the back of that market turned over on a Thursday morning exactly as it turns on every Thursday, and a board that turns is the only instrument in this harbor that has never once been argued with, and that two men of that yard being off at a cannery on Wednesday night and back on Thursday morning cost this harbor nothing and gained it nothing and a harbor that keeps a register of that is a harbor with a fifth column; and that the reason nothing was read out in the room over that market on that Thursday is his own, and is that a room is let out for a thing that has been said twice, and this had been said once, and that he had been the one who said it once and was not going to be the one who asked somebody else to say it again.
