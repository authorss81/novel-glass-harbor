# Chapter 514

*Thursday the Sixteenth Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Eighth Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still Off It, a Second Pair of Hands for Two Days Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Twenty Past Nine in the Morning at Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Two Marks, One Shilling and Fourpence, With the Sixteenth of December Against It and Not Written Into the Total at the Foot of That Sheet Until Those Two Days Are In, a Man of About Thirty-Two Asking a Woman of Thirty of No Office Out Loud in a Room for Her Help With the Record and Not Assuming It and Not Telling Her What She Would Do With It, and Her Saying Yes in One Sentence and Neither of Them Being Thanked, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Thirty-Four Thousand and Fifty Pence, Being Two Hundred and Twelve Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Thursday the sixteenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and eighth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. A second pair of hands for two days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about twenty past nine in the morning at three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixteenth of December against it, being the day those two men were asked for and the day they begin, and it is not written into the total at the foot of that sheet until those two days are in. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-four thousand six hundred and ninety pence out, being two hundred and seventy-nine marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-four thousand and fifty pence short, being two hundred and twelve marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure has not moved since Tuesday afternoon. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twelve days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fourteen; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighteenth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-four days. One berth is short on that third lay and has been short since the spring, and the lay fills on the first of April. The light at the head of that mole is out and its lens has been out since the seventh day of May, and the board at that mole head stands in the dark under it. The nineteen winters of that harbor are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and has not been asked about it and has said nothing this week and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by two men who did not stop walking, and the water went into it out of a hose that is dead until the spring and had to be carried from the butt behind that market in eleven trips, and the man who keeps the leaf was in that shed at eight with the door shut and did not go and watch it, as he does not go and watch any of the hundred and eight of them.**

"The hundred and eighth," he said, to the slate. "Same words as the hundred and seventh. The name at the top of that board is still off it and I am not going to put it back on because the number has gone past a hundred and that is not what a rota is for."

---

**At about twenty past nine in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and then said, in the same words he used on the sixth of December and the same words he will use on every day of this month where a line is not the same day as its work, why the total at the foot of that sheet had not moved.**

"A second pair of hands for two days. Two men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece for two days, being three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixteenth of December against it, being the day they were asked for and the day they begin."

"And the total at the foot of it."

"The total at the foot of it has not moved and it is not going to move until Saturday. **A line goes onto that sheet with the day the work was done against it, and the day it goes into the total at the foot of it is another day and not always the same one, and both of them are on the face of the line where a stranger can put a finger on them.** Those two men are hired for the sixteenth and the seventeenth and the work is not in until the work is in. If I move that total on a Thursday for two days that have not been worked, then in about a year of Wednesdays this sheet will be a page of counting things that were not done."

He wrote the line and put the pen down, and two men came at about half past nine and were put on the third lay with a caulking iron and a bucket of oakum and the bench that has not been level since August, and nobody thanked the man who keeps the leaf for saying it out loud a second time and he did not say it a second time in a different way.

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and a man of about thirty-two stood at the front of that room and asked a woman of thirty of no office for her help out loud, in front of all of them, and did not assume it and did not say what she would do with it.**

He had been in that room twice this month and had not spoken in it either time, and there was a great deal of talk in that room on that afternoon about what had been read out on the Wednesday and about the list that came ashore off the supply boat, and about eleven people in that room were following it in their heads and not on the paper.

The woman of thirty of no office was at the back of that room with a book under her arm that she has not been thanked for carrying in or out of any room in nine weeks.

"I am going to ask you for something," he said, "and I am going to say it in front of everybody in this room and not at you in a passage, and if you say no I will say no has been said and that is the whole of what happened here this afternoon."

**And then he stopped, and he did not say what the something was, and he did not tell her what she would be doing or how much of it there would be or who else would be in it, and about twenty people in that room waited for about four seconds.**

"I am asking you for the record."

She said it in one sentence and she did not raise her voice and she did not ask him anything back.

"Yes."

**Nobody in that room thanked her for it. Nobody thanked him for asking. Nobody came up afterwards and said to either of them that it was a good thing to have asked, and nobody has offered to, and the two of them have not spoken about it to each other since that afternoon, and there is nothing on any sheet in that yard about it and there is nothing on any page in that harbor about it except what is in this column and what the room heard.**

And at the back of that room a man of about thirty-three said that if the record was going anywhere it should be in a room, and a woman of about fifty-eight with an end stall and a slate said that a thing being in a room is a thing about eleven people can be told a thing about, and the man of about thirty-three said that was the same thing said the other way round, and the woman of about fifty-eight said it was not and that she was not going to argue about it in a stairwell, and nobody argued about it.

**Her name is on a public sheet in that market and her name is at the foot of the sheet of four heads on that wall, and nobody has taken it off either one of them and nobody has thanked her and nobody has offered to.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door open on the slip, and said the day of the week, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Thirty-four thousand and fifty pence short, being two hundred and twelve marks, ten shillings and tenpence. Unmoved since Tuesday afternoon, and it is going to be unmoved tomorrow as well, and the reason is on a line in that book with the sixteenth of December against it and it is in the total on Saturday, and I have said that rule twice this month and I am not going to say it a third time this month."

Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for anything on that day.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTH TIME AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. TWO MEN ARE ON THAT THIRD LAY WITH AN OAKUM IRON AND A BUCKET AND A BENCH THAT HAS NOT BEEN LEVEL SINCE AUGUST, AND A LINE ON THAT SHEET WITH THE SIXTEENTH OF DECEMBER AGAINST IT IS NOT IN THE TOTAL AT THE FOOT OF IT UNTIL THE EIGHTEENTH. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAID ONE WORD IN A ROOM OVER THAT MARKET ON THAT AFTERNOON AND HER NAME IS ON A PUBLIC SHEET AND ON THE SHEET OF FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL AND NOBODY TOOK IT OFF EITHER AND NOBODY HAS THANKED HER AND NOBODY HAS OFFERED TO. THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET AND NONE IS COMING THIS MONTH. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-FOUR THOUSAND AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the sixteenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and eighth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that a second pair of hands for two days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about twenty past nine in the morning at three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the sixteenth of December against it, being the day they were asked for and the day they begin, and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud in the same words he used on the sixth of December that a line goes onto that sheet with the day the work was done against it and the day it goes into the total at the foot of it is another day and not always the same one and both of them are on the face of the line, and that the total at the foot of that sheet did not move on that day and did not move on the Friday; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-four thousand six hundred and ninety pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-four thousand and fifty pence short, being two hundred and twelve marks, ten shillings and tenpence; and that a room over that market was let out at about half past two in the afternoon and about twenty people went up and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and that a man of about thirty-two asked a woman of thirty of no office out loud and in front of all of them for her help with the record and did not assume it and did not say what she would do with it or who else would be in it, and that she said yes in one sentence and was not thanked, and that he was not thanked, and that nobody came up afterwards to either of them about it and nobody has offered to; and that her name is on a public sheet in that market and at the foot of the sheet of four heads on that wall and neither name was taken off either; and that the four heads on that wall have not changed and no fifth head was added to it and the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on any wall in it; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and forty-seven, one thousand and one hundred and nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and forty-six, one thousand and one hundred and seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the ask was made out loud in a room and not in a passage is the man of about thirty-two's own, and is that a favour asked in a passage is a favour that can be granted quietly, and a favour asked in a room in front of twenty people is a thing eleven of whom cannot read has heard and cannot be argued about afterwards; and that the reason he did not say what she would be doing with it is his own, and is that the moment a man tells a person of thirty what her time is going to be spent on he has hired her, and that this yard has never hired anybody on a record and is not starting this week; and that the reason her name was left on a public sheet and on the sheet of four heads on that wall is this yard's own mouth, and is that a name taken off a page for having said one word in a room is a name taken off for agreeing with somebody, and that the four heads on that wall cannot carry a fifth and are not going to be given one, and that the two names of hers on two pages in that harbor are two pages and not a count of anything; and that the reason the rule about the day a line is written and the day the work is done was said out loud a second time in the same words is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a rule said once in a month is a rule somebody will be tempted to try, and that a rule said twice in the same words is a rule that has stopped being an opinion; and that the reason not one standing thing in that harbor was touched on that Thursday, or bettered, or shut, is this yard's own mouth, and is that a day went by in which a woman said yes to something in a room and this harbor did not shorten by one penny, and that a harbor which shortens itself every time somebody agrees with it is a harbor that is being shortened, and that this yard left every standing figure in that harbor where it was standing on that day and did not move one for a yes.*
