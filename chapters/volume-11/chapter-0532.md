# Chapter 532

*Monday the Third Day of January, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Thirteenth Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It and the Figure of That Hundred Not Said Out Loud a Second Time, Nine Men Back on a Quay About Half an Hour Ago After a Road With Nothing at the End of It and Nobody in That Harbor With Work for Them That Morning, Coal for Four Days Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at Nine Hundred and Sixty Pence, Being Six Marks Exactly, With the Third of January Against It and the Whole of That Line Said Out Loud Before a Figure Was Written Against It, a Tide Gang Four Men Short Since the Spring With Two of the Four on That Rota on a Monday and a Thursday and Neither of Them at the Head of It, a Slater of About Forty Asking Out Loud for Two Men to Carry Water and Being Told That a Rotating Board Is Not a Place to Ask At, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Nine Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Three Hundred and Eleven Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, the Three Nots Standing Against the Eighth of June Past in Their Seventeenth Week and Nobody in That Crew Saying One Word About Them, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the third day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in that shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirteenth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Coal for four days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks exactly, with the third of January against it, being the day the work was done, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at sixty thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out, being three hundred and seventy-eight marks, one shilling and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-nine thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being three hundred and eleven marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-two days. A sluice gate at the back of that market has been out of reach to a boat's side since the spring and two men go in to it on their knees and neither of them is paid for it. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are in their seventeenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them and nobody has asked. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty days and yesterday morning he told a man on a slip that he could hear a compressor going four hundred yards off on the west quay and the man he said it to has not mentioned it to anybody since.**

---

**That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning by two men who carried the water to it and did not stop while they carried it, and it went in at the top off the butt behind that market in eleven trips by hand because the hose that it went in through is dead until the spring, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and it is going to still be off it.**

---

**At about half past nine in the morning, about nine minutes after nine men had come back up a coast road with two wagons and nothing at the end of it, a man of about thirty-three who hires hands at that market was standing at the head of that third lay asking whether anybody wanted a pair of hands for a week, and four people said no to him in about two minutes and he had not asked the ninth man.**

He came along that slip to the bench stone and put it to the man who keeps the leaf directly, in front of about nine people, and he was not a man who had asked anybody else.

"Nine of you came back up that road at half past four yesterday afternoon and you are all standing on that slip and there is not one thing on this quay that wants any of you this week. I have had a week of nothing in front of me since Friday. **I am not asking you for a favour, I am asking whether this harbor has nine men on it and no work, because that is a thing that can be said out loud and I have said it.**"

The man who keeps the leaf said no to him in about six words and then said the rest of it once.

"Four of those nine are my four men and they are paid a day a day at the figure in chalk whether I want them or not, and the other five are not mine to put on anything. **What this harbor has is four men paid for a day with a boat at a berth and a day asked for by name, and a shed with nothing in it to mend, and if that is nine men and no work then that is the ninth week of it and I did not hear anything new in it this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands said that the man who keeps the leaf had said a true thing and had said it in a way that was going to make him feel fine about it, and said it out loud to about nine people, and then asked for the two men who carry water for that cistern and did not get them.

He asked a slater of about forty who was on that slip instead, who has worked on that mole head eleven times, and the slater said no to him in about four words and gave the reason himself.

"Because that board turns on a Monday and a Thursday and it is not a place to ask anybody anything, it is a place people put their names because they have to. **You put your two men on there and I will move you up if there is a day I can do it with, and if I do that then a man who has been on that board for nine years comes off it for a week and goes home without asking, and I have watched that happen twice.** Ask me again in a fortnight and ask me on a Thursday."

---

**At about ten in the morning the coal line went onto that sheet and the whole of it was said out loud before a figure was written against it, and about nine people were on that slip.**

"Coal for four days. Nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks exactly, at the figure a boat alongside that west quay is selling it at, and that is two hundred and forty pence a day for four days and that figure went up in December and has not come down. **The third of January is against it because that is the day it was done and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon, and the figure on the back of the slate at that end stall is a lamp oil figure and this is not a lamp oil figure and the two of them are never going to be on the same line.**"

He put the pen down flat.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and said the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Forty-nine thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being three hundred and eleven marks, eight shillings and twopence. Up by nine hundred and sixty pence since yesterday afternoon, being four days of coal, and both those days are today. Sixty thousand four hundred and ninety-eight out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in."

"And a man stood on this slip at half past nine this morning and said out loud in front of nine people that this harbor has nine men on it and no work, and he was right, and I have not got a single thing to put against it. **A tide gang four men short, a third lay a berth short, nineteen winters on a page nobody has answered, and four hundred and fifty pence on a board at that end stall, and this morning a cistern went up eleven trips by hand because a hose that cost less than that has been dead since the spring. None of that has been paid today and none of it has been bettered and none of it is going to be because two wagons came up a road yesterday.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Monday, including the man of about thirty-three who hires hands and including the slater who said no to him.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTEENTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT HALF PAST NINE IN THE MORNING THAT THIS HARBOR HAS NINE MEN ON IT AND NO WORK AND WAS TOLD THAT A SHED WITH NOTHING IN IT TO MEND IS NOT A WEEK OF WORK, AND WAS ASKED FOR TWO MEN TO CARRY WATER AND DID NOT GET THEM. NINE HUNDRED AND SIXTY PENCE WENT ON THAT SHEET FOR FOUR DAYS OF COAL AND THE THIRD OF JANUARY IS AGAINST IT AND IT IS IN THE TOTAL THIS AFTERNOON. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SLUICE GATE AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS OUT OF REACH TO A BOAT'S SIDE AND TWO MEN GO IN TO IT ON THEIR KNEES AND NEITHER OF THEM IS PAID FOR IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-NINE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN MARKS, EIGHT SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the third day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirteenth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood at the head of that third lay at about half past nine in the morning and said out loud in front of about nine people that this harbor has nine men on it and no work and that he had had a week of nothing in front of him since Friday, and that four people said no to him in about two minutes before he asked the ninth man, and that he was given a different answer by the man who keeps the leaf in about six words and then the rest of it once, and that he asked a slater of about forty for two men to carry water for that cistern and was refused in about four words with the reason that a board that turns is not a place to ask anybody anything and that moving a man up it once moves a man off it who has been on it nine years; and that coal for four days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks exactly, with the third of January against it being the day the work was done and being in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon, and that the whole of that line was said out loud before a figure was written against it and that the figure of two hundred and forty pence a day is the figure a boat alongside the west quay is selling coal at and has not come down, and that the figure of four hundred and fifty pence at that end stall is a lamp oil figure and is not on that sheet and is not going to be on the same line; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at sixty thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-nine thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being three hundred and eleven marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by nine hundred and sixty pence on that day; and that a tide gang of that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the spring and two of those four are on that rota board on a Monday and a Thursday and neither of them is at the head of it; and that the sluice gate at the back of that market has been out of reach to a boat's side since the spring and two men go in to it on their knees and neither of them is paid for it; and that the three nots standing against the eighth of June past are in their seventeenth week and no word has been said about them in this harbor in that time.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man who hires hands was answered with a list of what is short rather than with a job is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a man who is told that the harbor has nothing to give is owed a list of the somethings it is short of, and that the list is the only argument this shed has ever had that it is not idle, and that it is also the argument that nothing got better; and that the reason that slater refused two men for that cistern in four words is his own and was not asked for, and is that a rota is a queue with a name at the head of it and the head of that one has been off since before he started working on that mole, and that a man who is careful with a thing that belongs to everybody ends up being careful with it for the one man who has been on it longest, which is not what anybody meant and is what happens; and that the reason not one debt in that harbor was discharged, advanced, trimmed or written off on that Monday is his own, and is that a cistern charged by hand is not a cistern mended, and that a shed with nothing in it to mend is not a shed full of work, and that two men kneeling in a sluice for no pay are the most honest figure in this harbor this month and they are not on a page anywhere; and that the reason the coal was put on at the figure a boat is selling at and not at the figure this yard has paid before is the reader's own, and is that a supply that does not arrive is not a price that has gone up, it is a supply that has stopped, and that a sheet which shows both is a sheet a stranger can check, and that the lamp oil figure on that board has been standing at four hundred and fifty pence since the first of June and a person who puts a coal figure next to a lamp oil figure is making an argument that no board in that market is making; and that the reason those stairs stood empty on that Monday is his own, and is that people climb them when there is something on the step, and that the only thing on the step on that Monday was a column of wages already read on the last Tuesday and still where it was, and that a room let out for nothing is a room people stop climbing to.
