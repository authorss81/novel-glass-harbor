# Chapter 549

*Thursday the Twentieth Day of January, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Boat of About Fourteen Tons Found on the Ground off the Outer Water Four Days Down That Coast on the Morning of the Fifteenth Priced in Hours and Material at About Ten o'Clock on the Said Thursday at Nine Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Fifty-Nine Marks and Eight Shillings, With the Fourteenth Against It and Settled on the Twentieth, Being the Second of Two Bills and Not the First One Plus Something, a Room over That Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Man of About Thirty-One Who Was on the Water on Friday Night and Whose Own Boat Is on the Ground Giving His Account of What the Coast Can Now Do in About Fifty Words in That Yard's Own Mouth, and a Man of About Fifty Saying Out Loud in That Room at About Half Past Two, in Eleven Words, That This Coast Can Be Cut in One Place at a Time, Which Is Not Argued With and Is Not Thanked, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the twentieth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market with about twenty people in it.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. One line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at nine thousand five hundred and thirty-six pence, being fifty-nine marks and eight shillings, with the fourteenth of January against it and settled on the twentieth. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifty-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty-nine days. The bench under that pump at the back of that quay has been bare for three hundred and fifty-three nights and the pump is dry, and the man who mends that pump was asked for a figure in the spring last year and has not come and has not been asked again. A store on the second lay of that harbor has had its shutter up since the middle of December and the woman of about forty-four who keeps it has told two people that she is not going to take it down until there is something to sell, and the two people are her own hands. A chandler of about fifty-two at the head of that quay put lamp oil across her counter for three lamps on Tuesday at four hundred and forty-eight pence and has not put a figure on any board in that market and is not going to and has said so out loud once. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-seven days and he has not been to the road with the pump on it and nobody has sent for him.**

---

**That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and is going to still be off it.**

---

**And at about ten o'clock in the morning the person whose yard it is priced the second of the two boats, and she said every figure out loud as she wrote it, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it, and the whole of the price was said before a figure went against that line, and it is not the figure from Tuesday with something added to it.**

1. *A new hull. Thirty fathoms of two-inch at two hundred pence a fathom. Six thousand pence, being thirty-seven marks, six shillings and eightpence.*
2. *Gear, a warp, and about forty fathoms of line. One thousand three hundred and forty-four pence, being eight marks, five shillings and fourpence.*
3. *A pot, a funnel and two iron rings out of the store at the back of that yard. One hundred and seventy-six pence, being one mark, one shilling and four pence.*
4. *Twelve days of my own hours on that hull at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day. Two thousand and sixteen pence, being twelve marks and eight shillings.*

"That is nine thousand five hundred and thirty-six pence, being fifty-nine marks and eight shillings, and it is the sum of those four, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can add them. The fourteenth of January is against that line, being the day she was lost, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. **She is bigger than the other one by ten fathoms of two-inch and eight days of my hours, and those two numbers are the whole of the difference between the two bills, and a man who cannot add a fathom to a day has not understood a bill and does not need to.**"

Nobody thanked her for either figure and she was not thanked on the Tuesday and she was not thanked on the Thursday and she was not asked for a third.

---

**And at about one o'clock in the afternoon the man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay and owns the boat of about nine tons gave his account of what that coast can now do, in about fifty words, in that yard, in front of about eleven people, and he was on the water on the night of the fourteenth and he is not a member of anything and he has no seal on anything and nobody thanked him for it.**

"Nine boats went in past a line of boats on a Tuesday night in January and seven came back, and there was no Crown hull anywhere near the inside of it, and there is no Crown hull that had to agree. **Eight men went into about four fathoms in three places and three men sat in three boats on the surface and the whole of it was moved by three bells and three hours, and a man with a leaf and a pencil can take the days off that sheet and the crossings off the crossings and check both of them, and there is not one Crown figure on that coast with a day against it.**"

He stopped there and did not add anything and nobody asked him to. The man who keeps the leaf said out loud that he was not going to write one word of that on that sheet, because a sheet with money on it cannot carry what a man of about thirty-one said at one o'clock on a Thursday, and because the whole of the use of a pencil in this world is that a stranger can take a figure off it, and what that man had said is not a figure and is not anybody's to take.

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and is not in it and the two were named separately and out loud.**

A man of about fifty who owns a boat of about fourteen tons stood up at about half past two in the afternoon, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read, and said out loud, once, in eleven words, what the coast they are all standing in is.

"**This coast can be cut in one place at a time.**"

Nobody argued with him. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands opened his mouth and did not say anything and closed it again. A woman of about thirty-one who keeps a fish plate outside said out loud that she had been thinking that since the Saturday morning and had not wanted to say it in a room, and he said that that was all right and that it did not need saying again. Nobody thanked him. No figure was set against it. It went into the second column of that leaf in his own words and it was not put in the first column, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that it was going in in the order it was said and would stay in that order, and that he was not going to be asked for an opinion on it and had not got one and was not going to get one.

**He said one more thing about it, once, in about fourteen people, and he did not say it again that month.**

"That is on the page now and it is the only thing in four days that this yard has got that it would be ashamed of. **Three points and a page of hours is a coast that can be cut in one place at a time, and I would rather have that on a sheet in a room than have a man find it out on a Tuesday with a boat of his own behind him, and I am not going to argue with him about it and neither is anybody else in this room.**"

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and said that the day had moved it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. It moved this morning by nine thousand five hundred and thirty-six pence, and that is a hull of thirty fathoms, gear, a pot, a funnel and two iron rings, and twelve days of one woman's hours, and not one person is in it. One hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And the two bills together are seventeen thousand two hundred and eighty-eight pence, being one hundred and eight marks and eightpence, for two boats, and there is not one man in either of them. **A man said eleven words in a room this afternoon that nobody argued with and nobody thanked him for, and those eleven words are the reason those two bills are a small number and not a large one, and both of those things are on the same page and neither of them cancels the other.** The lamp oil board is at four hundred and fifty pence, the mole head is dark, there is no flour in that market and there has been no barley in it since the middle of September, there are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon, and the column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with a week fallen due behind the day after tomorrow."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Thursday, and nobody was thanked for the second bill and nobody was thanked for the fifty words at one o'clock and nobody was thanked for the eleven words at half past two.

---

**A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS FOUND ON THE GROUND OFF THE OUTER WATER ON THE MORNING OF THE FIFTEENTH WAS PRICED AT NINE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX PENCE, BEING FIFTY-NINE MARKS AND EIGHT SHILLINGS, AS FOUR FIGURES AND A SUM, WITH THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY AGAINST IT AND SETTLED ON THE TWENTIETH, AND IT IS NOT THE FIGURE OF THE TUESDAY BILL PLUS ANYTHING. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO WAS ON THE WATER ON THE NIGHT OF THE FOURTEENTH SAID IN ABOUT FIFTY WORDS IN THAT YARD THAT NINE BOATS WENT IN PAST A LINE OF BOATS AND SEVEN CAME BACK AND THAT THE WHOLE OF IT WAS MOVED BY THREE BELLS AND THREE HOURS AND THAT NO CROWN FIGURE ON THAT COAST HAS A DAY AGAINST IT, AND NO WORD OF THAT WENT ONTO THAT SHEET. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT ROOM AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON, IN ELEVEN WORDS AND ONCE, THAT THIS COAST CAN BE CUT IN ONE PLACE AT A TIME, AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HIM AND NOBODY THANKED HIM AND NO FIGURE WAS SET AGAINST IT AND IT WENT INTO THE SECOND COLUMN IN HIS OWN WORDS AND NOT INTO THE FIRST. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twentieth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that a boat of about fourteen tons, found on the ground off the outer water on the morning of the fifteenth with a hole under her forefoot and her gear in the bottom of her and nobody standing on her, was priced at about ten o'clock in the morning at nine thousand five hundred and thirty-six pence, being fifty-nine marks and eight shillings, as a new hull of thirty fathoms of two-inch at two hundred pence a fathom at six thousand, being thirty-seven marks, six shillings and eightpence; gear, a warp and about forty fathoms of line at one thousand three hundred and forty-four, being eight marks, five shillings and fourpence; a pot, a funnel and two iron rings at one hundred and seventy-six, being one mark, one shilling and four pence; and twelve days of one woman's hours at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at two thousand and sixteen, being twelve marks and eight shillings; and that the whole of those four was said out loud before a figure was written against that line, and that the fourteenth of January is against that line being the day the boat was lost and that it is in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon, and that it is not the figure of the boat of about nine tons with something added to it; and that a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay and owns the boat of about nine tons gave an account at about one o'clock in the afternoon in about fifty words in that yard in front of about eleven people, being that nine boats went in past a line of boats on the night of the fourteenth and seven came back and that there was no Crown hull inside that line and none had to agree, and that eight men went into about four fathoms in three places and three men sat in three boats on the surface, and that the whole of it was moved by three bells and three hours, and that a man with a leaf and a pencil can take the days off that sheet and the crossings off the crossings and check both, and that there is not one Crown figure on that coast with a day against it; and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that not one word of that is going onto that sheet and that what a man said at one o'clock on a Thursday is not a figure and is not anybody's to take; and that a room over the floor of that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read, and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about fourteen tons said out loud in that room at about half past two, once and in eleven words, that this coast can be cut in one place at a time, and that nobody argued with him, that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands opened his mouth and said nothing, that a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a fish plate outside said she had been thinking it since the Saturday morning and had not wanted to say it in a room, that nobody thanked him and no figure was set against it, and that it went into the second column in his own words and not into the first column, and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that it was going in in the order it was said and would stay in that order and that he was not going to be asked for an opinion on it and had not got one; and that the man who keeps the leaf said once that that was the only thing in four days this yard had got that it would be ashamed of and that a page of three points and three hours is a coast that can be cut in one place at a time, and that he would rather have it on a sheet in a room than have a man find it out on a Tuesday with a boat of his own behind him, and that nobody in that room argued with it; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and eighty-two, one thousand two hundred and eighty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and eighty-one, one thousand two hundred and eighty-two; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that eleven words went in at about half past two in the afternoon in a room with about twenty people in it and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, in the mouth of a man who owns a boat of about fourteen tons that is on the ground four days down that coast, and that is this: that this coast can be cut in one place at a time. That it is not answered here and is not answered anywhere in this yard and is not going to be answered this winter, and that the man who keeps the leaf is not going to be asked for an opinion on it and has not got one and is not going to get one, and that a sheet that cannot answer a thing is a sheet that can be relied on for the things it can answer, and that is the reason the sentence is in the second column and not the first; and that the reason the man of about thirty-one's fifty words at one o'clock did not go onto that sheet is his own, and is that a sheet with money on it cannot carry a man's account of what a coast is, and that a man who has said a true thing out loud in a yard and then had it written down by a person who keeps a leaf has been given a page with a voice on it, and a page with a voice on it is a page that gets argued with inside a year, and the only page in that harbor that anybody can lean on is a page of figures with a day against each of them; and that the reason nobody thanked the man of about fifty is his own, and is that a man who is thanked for saying a thing in a room is a man who will say it a second time, and that this one is worth more said once by a man with a boat on the ground than said every week by a man who keeps a leaf, and that the whole of the argument of that yard is that it is not a head and a man in a room who is not a head is the only instrument in it that works; and that the reason nothing owed by that harbor or by anybody in it was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Thursday is his own, and is that three days after a night of eight men in four fathoms there is a figure for the second of two boats on a piece of paper and a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a store on the second lay has still not taken her shutter down, and that those two facts are in the same room this afternoon and neither of them is going to alter the other one, and that the harbor is poorer on the twentieth day of this month than it was on the third day of December and the only thing that is different about it is that there are two boats on the ground four days down that coast and a page in a room that says so.*
