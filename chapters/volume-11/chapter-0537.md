# Chapter 537

*Saturday the Eighth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Roof Going Up on a Shipyard on That Quay in Three Days on the Sixth, the Seventh and the Eighth With Two Days on the Face of One Line and the Last Board on at About Four in the Afternoon, a Man of About Fifty Who Owns a Boat of About Twenty-Two Tons Offering the Whole of That Yard a Fortnight's Work at a Figure and Being Refused in Nine Words, Three Lines Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten in the Morning on the Saturday Being a Roof at Nine Thousand Six Hundred Pence, a Bench at Five Hundred and Four and a Stock of Sawn Fir at Three Thousand Two Hundred, Being Thirteen Thousand Three Hundred and Four Pence in All, Being Eighty-Three Marks and Two Shillings, Every One of Them With the Day the Work Was Done Against It and a Stranger With a Leaf and a Pencil Able to Take the Days and the Figures Off It and Find That They Agree, a Slate Carrying Fifteen Thousand Three Hundred and Twenty Pence Coming Off That Bench Stone at About Two in the Afternoon and Going Into the Drawer Under It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Sixty-Five Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Four Hundred and Eleven Marks, Seven Shillings and Sixpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the eighth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shipyard on that quay with a roof on it.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Three lines went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning, being a roof at nine thousand six hundred pence, being sixty marks exactly; a bench at five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings; and a new stock of sawn fir at three thousand two hundred pence, being twenty marks exactly; which are thirteen thousand three hundred and four pence, being eighty-three marks and two shillings. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out, being four hundred and seventy-eight marks and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fourteenth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-seven days. A thing about the length of his own forearm, cut out of a carrot in about two hours by a man of about thirty-four of that port on the afternoon of the twenty-fourth of December, stands on the sill of the room over that market and nobody has asked him what it is for. The four men of that yard have been in that yard since about eight o'clock on the morning of the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, and they were paid on the Thursday for all three of those days before the first of the three had begun. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-five days and he is four days into a second week at that listening station and nobody in either harbor has said a word to him about it and he has not asked anybody for anything since the Friday.**

---

**At about ten in the morning three lines went onto that sheet, and the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of each of them out loud before he wrote a figure against any of them, and about nine people were on that slip, and the roof was not on yet and would not be until about four in the afternoon.**

"**A roof. Nine thousand six hundred pence, being sixty marks exactly, being forty fathoms of three-inch fir at two hundred and forty pence a fathom and the boarding dressed to it.** The sixth of January and the seventh of January are both on the face of this line, being the days the work was done, and it goes into the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because that is the day I am settling it, and that is the rule this yard works to and I said it out loud on the sixth of December and I am not going to say it again this month."

"**A bench. Five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings, being three days of one man's hours at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at the figure in chalk.** The eighth of January is against it and that is the day it was done and it is the same day I am settling it on, so this one carries one day and the roof carries two, and a man with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those days off these three lines and find that they are the sixth, the seventh and the eighth and that the first two days are on the roof because that is where the roof was."

"**A stock of sawn fir for that yard to keep working off. Three thousand two hundred pence, being twenty marks exactly, being sixteen fathoms of two-inch at two hundred pence a fathom off a cart from the mill that goes off the coast road before the burn.** The eighth of January is against it and that is the day it was done."

"And that is thirteen thousand three hundred and four pence, being eighty-three marks and two shillings, added to what was on that sheet before this morning, and **forty fathoms is a number that a man can count with a stick and three days is a number that cannot be counted with a stick at all, and the three days is the part that cost the most and I have put it on that sheet in hours at a figure anyway, because that is the only way a man who was not in that yard on Friday can see any of it.**"

---

**And at about a quarter to eleven in the morning a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons put his hand flat on that bench stone and offered that yard a fortnight's work at a figure, in front of about nine people, and was refused in nine words, and he was not argued with and he was not thanked and he has not been asked about it since.**

He offered a fortnight at two hundred and forty pence a day for his own boat and two men, which is above the figure in chalk, and he said that he was not doing it for the sake of it and that he was doing it because his boat had been out of the water since Wednesday and he had nothing to put in it.

The man who keeps the leaf said no in nine words.

"**This sheet is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference and it is never going to be.**"

He said the rest of it once and did not say it again.

"You have a boat with nothing in it. I have a yard with a roof going on it and four men in it. Two hundred and forty pence a day from you would come onto that sheet as a figure and there would not be one line on that sheet this winter that a man could take a pencil to, because the day somebody starts paying for a thing here the thing stops being a thing anybody can check. **I would rather be short by sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence and be right about every one of them than be four thousand pence better off and have nobody able to say what the fortnight was for.** Keep your two hundred and forty and take your boat out on Monday and if you want to be useful then take three of the men who came up that road on Friday out with you at the figure in chalk and pay them yourself and write that in your own book and not on this one."

Nobody thanked the man of about fifty for offering. Nobody thanked the man who keeps the leaf for refusing. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that a sheet nobody can check is just a diary with a bad head on it, and nobody disagreed with him and nobody agreed with him either and he did not say it again.

---

**The last board went on at about four in the afternoon, and at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip for the first time since the Wednesday before, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence. It moved this morning by thirteen thousand three hundred and four pence and that is a roof and a bench and sixteen fathoms of two-inch. Seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"**That roof is on and nobody in this harbor is a penny better off and nobody in this harbor is a penny worse off, and the price of it is fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence over three days, and it is on that sheet with the sixth, the seventh and the eighth of this month against it in three places, and there is not one person's name on any of those three lines.** The slate with Tuesday's figure on it came off that bench stone at about two this afternoon and went into the drawer under it, and the figure on the slate and the figure on that sheet are the same figure, and the drawer has never been locked in this yard."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Saturday, and nobody was thanked for the roof and nobody was thanked for the choice and nobody was thanked for the slate.

---

**A ROOF WENT UP ON A SHIPYARD ON THAT QUAY IN THREE DAYS, ON THE SIXTH, THE SEVENTH AND THE EIGHTH, WITH TWO DAYS ON THE FACE OF ONE LINE, AND THE LAST BOARD WENT ON AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. THREE LINES WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AT THIRTEEN THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FOUR PENCE IN ALL, BEING EIGHTY-THREE MARKS AND TWO SHILLINGS, AND EVERY ONE OF THEM CARRIES THE DAY THE WORK WAS DONE AGAINST IT AND NO LINE OF THEM CARRIES A NAME. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WHO OWNS A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-TWO TONS OFFERED THAT YARD A FORTNIGHT'S WORK AT TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE A DAY AND WAS REFUSED IN NINE WORDS BECAUSE THIS SHEET IS NOT MADE TO BALANCE BY ASKING THIS HARBOR FOR THE DIFFERENCE. A SLATE CARRYING FIFTEEN THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE WENT INTO THE DRAWER UNDER THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE DRAWER HAS NEVER BEEN LOCKED IN THAT YARD. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SIXTY-FIVE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the eighth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that three lines went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning, being a roof at nine thousand six hundred pence, being sixty marks exactly, with the sixth of January and the seventh of January both on the face of that line being the days the work was done and being in the total at the foot of that sheet that afternoon; a bench at five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings, being three days of one man's hours at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, with the eighth of January against it being the day the work was done and being settled on the same day; and a new stock of sawn fir at three thousand two hundred pence, being twenty marks exactly, being sixteen fathoms of two-inch at two hundred pence a fathom off a cart from the mill that goes off the coast road before the burn, with the eighth of January against it being the day the work was done; and that those three lines are thirteen thousand three hundred and four pence in all, being eighty-three marks and two shillings, and that the whole of each of the three was said out loud before a figure was written against it, and that a man with a leaf and a pencil can take three days off those three lines and find that they are the sixth, the seventh and the eighth of this month; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it moved by thirteen thousand three hundred and four pence on that day; and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons offered that yard a fortnight's work with his own boat and two men at two hundred and forty pence a day in front of about nine people at about a quarter to eleven in the morning, and was refused in nine words, and that the reason given out loud was that this sheet is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference and never will be, and that he was not argued with, was not thanked and has not been asked about it since; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that a sheet nobody can check is a diary with a bad head on it and said it once and nobody disagreed with him and nobody agreed with him; and that the last board went on that roof at about four in the afternoon; and that a slate carrying fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence came off that bench stone at about two in the afternoon and went into the drawer under it, and that the figure on the slate and the figure on that sheet are the same figure and that the drawer has never been locked in that yard; and that not one person's name is on any of those three lines and those three lines carry no count of persons.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that fortnight was refused in nine words and not argued with for half an hour is his own, and is that an offer from a man in this harbor is the only kind of money this sheet has ever been short of and it has been short of it since the middle of September, and that the difference between being short and being unreadable is about four days of work by four men and that four days of work by four men is two thousand six hundred and eighty-eight pence and that he would rather carry that than the fortnight, and that a man who has offered twice will be offered at by somebody who has never been refused; and that the reason that slate went into a drawer instead of the back of a shed is hers and was not asked for, and is that a figure which was spoken out loud in a yard on a Tuesday and was refused by everybody and then turned out to be the exact figure that went onto a sheet three days later is the only kind of record a person who cannot read can be given, and that the back of a shed is where a number goes when somebody wants it not to be checked; and that the reason the roof line carries two days and the other two carry one is his own and is not an error, and is that the sixth and the seventh are one piece of work and the eighth is another and the bench and the stock arrived on the eighth, and that a line with a day on it that is not the day it was settled is a line a stranger can check and a line with a date a yard chose is a line a stranger cannot; and that the reason no debt of that harbor moved on that Saturday is his own and is not the same words as any other day of this month, and is that a roof went on in three days and the wage column in that harbor has stood where it stood since the twenty-fourth of November through five wage Tuesdays of this winter, and that this yard paid a man for a day he spent sawing and did not pay a man a penny toward a week that nineteen winters ago, and both of those went out of the same hand on the same Saturday; and that the reason that nobody was thanked for the choice is the reader's own and is not a complaint, and is that a thing a man is not thanked for is a thing he did for the sheet and not for the room, and that the sheet has never once thanked anybody either and is not going to start.
