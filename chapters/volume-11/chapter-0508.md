# Chapter 508

*Friday the Tenth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Page Carrying Eleven Sentences at the Head of It Written Before Anybody Was Asked and Read Out Word for Word Eleven Times and Not Varied Once, About Eleven Local Crews Asked One at a Time and Seven of Them Putting a Name to It and Four of Them Not, No Reason Given by Any of the Four, No Such Four Named in Any List and Counted With Nobody and Not Asked Again on Any Day After That One, a Man of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets Refusing in About Two Sentences a Reading of Those Four as a Position on Anything, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Asking to Read the Four Refusals Into the Record of That Room and Being Refused in About Two Sentences, a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eleven in the Morning at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, With the Ninth of December Against It and Written Into That Sheet on the Morrow, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Twenty-Eight Thousand and Sixty-Six Pence, Being One Hundred and Seventy-Five Marks, Five Shillings and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Friday the tenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of a fish market.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. A day apiece in advance to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the ninth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-eight thousand seven hundred and six pence out, being two hundred and forty-one marks, twelve shillings and two pence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-eight thousand and sixty-six pence short, being one hundred and seventy-five marks, five shillings and sixpence, and that figure has not moved since the Wednesday. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and six days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eight; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-six days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twelfth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-fifth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundredth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventeenth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty-eight days. A woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay was in that room that afternoon and put a name to the page. A case stands on a table in the room over that market with its lock shut and the key in the keeping of the woman who wrote the minute, and nobody in that harbor has been shown what is in it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and no figure is set against that anywhere and nobody has made it into anything.**

---

**A page went round that room at about two o'clock in the afternoon with eleven sentences at the head of it, and the eleven sentences were written on the page before anybody was asked anything, and they were read out word for word eleven times and they did not vary once.**

The eleven sentences are these, and they are the whole of what anybody in that room was asked.

1. *This is a line of local crews and it is not a company and it has no head.*
2. *A crew that puts a name to it agrees to go out to the mark off that third lay once a month and to answer for it.*
3. *Answering for it means going out and looking at it and writing down what is wrong with it.*
4. *It does not mean going out in weather, and it does not mean going out at night, and it does not mean going out when the officer outside that mole says not to.*
5. *Nobody in this room is paid by this page and nobody is paid for the day he loses coming up these stairs.*
6. *A crew that puts a name to it may take the name off it in front of anybody in this room and needs to give no reason.*
7. *A crew that does not put a name to it is not asked again this month or any month after it.*
8. *The name on this page is the name of the crew and not the name of the man who owns her.*
9. *This page is not a list of anybody who has refused and no such list exists in this harbor.*
10. *Nobody's name goes on the four heads on that wall because of this page, and this page does not go on that wall.*
11. *What goes into the book afterward is what the reader says out loud and not what he makes of it going to it.*

Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, read them out from the top of that page eleven times, one time for each crew, with a hand laid flat on the page between times, and about eleven people in that room heard all eleven readings and the eleventh was word for word the first.

---

**About eleven local crews were asked, one at a time, and not in front of each other, and seven of them put a name to that page and four of them did not.**

Seven names went on. A woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay was the second, and she read the eleven sentences back to herself under her breath and then wrote the name of her crew in her own hand and put the pen down and said nothing about it to anybody.

Four did not write. **None of the four gave a reason and none of the four was asked for one, and their names do not appear on that page or on any other paper in that harbor, and they are not counted with anybody, and nobody in that room and nobody in that yard has asked any of them anything since, and is not going to.**

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon a man at the front of that room said the thing that is said, and Hobb Lee refused it in about two sentences and did not raise his voice for it.**

"So that is four of them against it."

"No it is not. Four of them did not write a name on a piece of paper and that is the whole of what happened in this room this afternoon, and if you write *against* on that page about four people then you have made a list, and the eleventh sentence before this one says that there is not going to be one."

"Those are the same thing."

"They are not the same thing and a person who cannot read this page is not going to be able to tell you that they are not, and that is exactly why nobody is writing your word on it." He put his thumb on the page. "If you want to say it, say it about yourself and say it about your own crew, and I will put your name and your crew and the hour against it and read it back to the room, and then there is one man in this harbor who has said a thing about himself and not four men in a room where a thing has been said about them."

Nobody took him up on it. About twenty people were in that room and the sentence was heard by all of them and none of the four heard themselves described.

---

**And at about ten past three in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office asked to have the four refusals read into the record of that room, and she asked for it once and in about eleven words, and the man who mends nets refused it in about two sentences, and he has never argued with her about anything.**

"Read the four into the book."

"No. The book in this room takes down what is on a page and what a person says about themselves, and there is no page with the four on it and no person in this room has said anything about themselves, and I am not going to invent the second of those two things so that I can write down the first. I have not argued with you about anything in nine weeks and I am not going to start over a list of four men who did not write their names down."

"That is a decision and you have not said whose it is."

"It is mine, because I am the one with the book, and I will say so every time anybody asks me for one of them in this room, and you may put that in the record in my words if you have a page of your own for it."

Nobody wrote the four anywhere. There is no list of them in that room, in that yard, or in that harbor, and there is not going to be one, and the four of them were not named in any list and were not counted with anybody and were not argued with and are not going to be asked again.

---

**At about eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud, and it went on with the ninth of December against it and not on with the tenth.**

"A day apiece in advance to the same four men, being four men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the ninth of December against it, being the day they worked. I am paying them on Friday for the Thursday they have already done, and the line goes into the total at the foot of that sheet on the day I write it, which is tomorrow, and the day the work was done and the day it is written are two different days and both of them are on that sheet and you can take either of them off it with a pencil."

Nobody argued with him about it and nobody asked him why the four men are the same four men they were on the third of December, and he did not say, and it is the same four men.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, and it had not moved for two days running and he said that too.**

"Twenty-eight thousand and sixty-six pence short, being one hundred and seventy-five marks, five shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since Wednesday afternoon. A line went on this morning with Monday's date on it and it is not in the total at the foot of this sheet until tomorrow, and that is why nothing moved, and I have said why and I am not going to say it again this month."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it. Nobody was paid for going up those stairs. The page with the eleven sentences at the head of it went home with the man who mends nets and it is not on any wall in that harbor and it is not in that yard and it is not in the book of the room over that market except the seven names and the hour.

---

**SEVEN NAMES WENT ONTO A PAGE IN THAT ROOM ON THAT AFTERNOON AND FOUR DID NOT AND NONE OF THE FOUR GAVE A REASON AND NONE OF THE FOUR HAS BEEN ASKED FOR ONE AND THEIR NAMES ARE ON NO PAGE IN THAT HARBOR AND THEY ARE NOT COUNTED WITH ANYBODY. THE ELEVEN SENTENCES AT THE HEAD OF THAT PAGE WERE WRITTEN BEFORE ANYBODY WAS ASKED AND WERE READ OUT WORD FOR WORD ELEVEN TIMES. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL HAVE NOT CHANGED AND A FIFTH HEAD IS NOT GOING ON IT. A DAY APIECE IN ADVANCE TO THE SAME FOUR MEN WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AT SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE WITH THE NINTH OF DECEMBER AGAINST IT. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-EIGHT THOUSAND AND SIXTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS BEEN ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Friday the tenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that a page carrying eleven sentences at the head of it, written before anybody was asked anything, was read out word for word eleven times in the room over that fish market between about two o'clock and about half past two in the afternoon, one time for each crew, and did not vary once in the eleven readings; and that about eleven local crews were asked one at a time and not in front of each other and seven of them put a name to that page and four of them did not, and that none of the four gave a reason and none of the four was asked for one, and that no such four is named on any list in that harbor and they are not counted with anybody and none of them has been asked anything since; and that a man at the front of that room said at about half past two that those four were against it and was refused in about two sentences, and that he was not taken up on it and none of the four heard themselves described; and that the woman of thirty of no office asked at about ten past three to have the four refusals read into the record of that room and was refused in about two sentences by a man who has never argued with her about anything, and that he said the decision was his and would say so again, and that nobody wrote the four anywhere; and that a day apiece in advance to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the ninth of December against it, and that it is written into the total at the foot of that sheet on the morrow, and that it is the same four men as on the third of December; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-eight thousand seven hundred and six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-eight thousand and sixty-six pence short, being one hundred and seventy-five marks, five shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Wednesday.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the eleven sentences were written at the head of the page before anybody was asked is the man who mends nets' own, and is that a page whose terms are read out after a man has been asked has been written for the man who asked the question, and that in this harbor about eleven people in that room can only be asked a thing that is already on paper in front of them; and that the reason four crews did not write a name is not in this leaf and was not asked for and is not going to be in any leaf, and is that the seventh of those eleven sentences promised them that before they were asked and that this yard intends to keep that promise for as long as this yard is standing and not for one afternoon; and that the reason the word *against* was refused is the reader's own, and is that a page with four names and the word *against* beside them is a page a man with a boat can be shown to a man with an office, and that this harbor has not got a list of the people who put nothing in and is not going to make one by accident in a warm room with nine people in it; and that the reason the woman of thirty of no office was refused in two sentences rather than argued with for an hour is the reader's own, and is that she has asked this room for one thing in nine weeks and she was refused it, and that the cheapest way for a room to keep the trust of a person who writes down everything is to be the same with her on the second thing as on the first; and that the reason a day apiece in advance went onto that sheet with the ninth against it and the tenth against nothing is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a day in advance is a day a man has already done, and that a sheet which dates such a line by the day it was paid is a sheet that is a day wrong every Tuesday in the year.*
