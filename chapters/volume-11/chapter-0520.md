# Chapter 520

*Wednesday the Twenty-Second Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Page Carrying the Crossing of the Twenty-First on It and Four Names Against It Read Out Word for Word Once by the Man of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets on the Third Lay With His Own Name and the Hour Under It, and One Line on That Page That Is Not a Name, Being That a Third of Three Crews Asked at About Ten Minutes to Three Did Not Take a Channel, With No Name Against It and No Reason Against It, Nobody in That Room Being Asked to Read Out His Own Refusal and Nobody Being Asked to Say Anything About It, the Sheet That Carries the Money Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in the Afternoon in That Yard's Own Mouth at Thirty-Six Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Eight Pence, Being Two Hundred and Thirty Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, and Not Answered by Anybody, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Nobody Thanked*

Wednesday the twenty-second day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-seven thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out, being two hundred and ninety-seven marks, one shilling and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-six thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being two hundred and thirty marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighteen days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twelfth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty days. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and there has been none since the middle of November, and the woman with that end stall and that slate has four hundred and fifty pence on the front of her board with February's figure visible above it and both dates on the board. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay has a hole in the middle of a Tuesday and has it still, and it is not a thing anybody in that harbor has asked him about since the spring and no figure is set against it anywhere. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fourteen days and it has not been made into anything by anybody.**

---

**A page went round that room over that market on the afternoon of the twenty-second and it carries the crossing of the twenty-first on it, and four names against it, and one line that is not a name.**

The four names are the man of about fifty who said yes in that shed on the morning of the twenty-first for a crew of about six men out of the west quay, the man of about thirty who said yes in that shed at about half past eleven for a crew of five out of the first lay, Ivo Sark of about twenty-four who was the named tender on the surface and was not in the water at any hour of that afternoon, and the woman of about twenty-seven of that yard who was on the compressor with him.

The line that is not a name says this and says only this.

*That a third of three crews asked at about ten minutes to three in the afternoon of the twenty-first, in the shed at the head of that third lay, did not take a channel.*

**There is no name against that line and there is no reason against that line either, and it is on that page, and it is the only line on that page that is not a name.**

**Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, read that page out once, word for word, at about two o'clock in the afternoon, with his own name and the hour under it, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it and heard all of it, and nobody in that room stopped him at any word.**

The page carries the day and the water and the two hundred yards off that third lay and the forty minutes from about four o'clock to about twenty to five, and it carries four boats that went up that water, and it carries that the float was taken up on its own line at about ten to five and that the bell was struck once and was not struck twice, and it carries the four names, and it carries the one line that is not a name.

It does not carry the fifth boat. **That boat is not on that page and its owner is not on that page and there is no other page in that harbor with either of them on it, and nobody in that room asked about it, and nobody has asked since.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-eight was not in that room, and nobody asked him to come, and nobody asked him to read that line out, and nobody asked him to say one word about it, and nobody in that harbor has argued with him about it, and nobody in that harbor has thanked him for it.**

A woman of about fifty-eight with an end stall and a slate said one thing about that line after the reading, out loud, in about eleven words, and she was not thanked for it either.

"That is on a page and it has nobody's name on it and that is the only way that goes on a page without hurting somebody."

And a man of about thirty-three said that a line with no name against it was a line nobody could check, and Hobb Lee said that it could be checked as well as anything else on that page, being that a stranger with a pencil could take the hour off it and the fact off it and find that it was there, and that a name would have made it a different fact and a worse one.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone in that shed with the door open on the slip, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Thirty-six thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being two hundred and thirty marks, eight shillings and twopence. Forty-seven thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and the day the work was done and the day it was written are on the face of every one of the seven lines this yard has opened in ten days, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

**Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it. No figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise. Nothing on that sheet carries a count of persons and nobody in that yard has ever written one on it and nobody is going to.**

The man who keeps the leaf's wage is four marks and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag and the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall has not moved since the spring and the wage column four hundred yards away has not moved since the twenty-fourth of November and there is no flour in that market.

---

**And somebody at the end of that slip said that he had said a thing on the Sunday the twelfth about that, and he said that he had, and that it was on the leaf, and that it was not going to be said again this month.**

Nobody in that harbor has said it a second time. The proof is on a page in a room over that market with four names against it and one line that is not a name, and what the proof is, is that it did not stop, and **that is a smaller thing than it looks and nobody in that harbor says so and nobody is going to.**

---

**THERE ARE NINE HULLS LYING ABOUT TWO SEA MILES OFF THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND DAY OF DECEMBER THERE WERE STILL NINE OF THEM. A FLOAT OF TARRED FIR IS LYING IN ABOUT FOUR FATHOMS OFF THE HEAD OF THAT THIRD LAY WITH AN EYE PUT INTO A STONE UNDER IT ON THE TWENTY-FIRST AND FORTY FATHOMS OF TWO-INCH STUFF COILED ON THE THWART OF A DINGHY. A CASE STANDS ON A LONG TABLE IN THE ROOM OVER THAT MARKET WITH ITS LOCK SHUT. A CHEST WITH TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT IS IN A ROOM AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AND ITS LID IS SHUT. THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL WITH FEBRUARY'S FIGURE VISIBLE ABOVE IT AND BOTH DATES ON THE BOARD. THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET AND NONE IS COMING THIS MONTH. THE WAGE COLUMN IS TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND THE SIXTY-FIRST WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE. A BAG UNDER A SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-SIX THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-second day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that nothing went onto the sheet that carries the money in any hand in any line on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-seven thousand five hundred and thirty-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-six thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight pence short, being two hundred and thirty marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Tuesday, and that seven lines went onto that sheet in the ten days from the thirteenth to the twenty-first of that month and nothing went into it in any hand in any of those ten days; and that a page carrying the crossing of the twenty-first and four names against it was read out once in the room over that market at about two o'clock in the afternoon by the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay, with his own name and the hour under it, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it, and that one line on that page is not a name and carries no name and carries no reason, being that a third of three crews asked at about ten minutes to three on the twenty-first did not take a channel; and that the man of about thirty-eight was not asked to read that line out and was not asked to say anything about it and was not argued with and was not thanked; and that the fifth boat of that afternoon is on no page in that harbor and its owner is on no page in that harbor; and that the four heads on that wall have not changed and no fifth head was added to it and the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on any wall in it; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-three, one thousand and one hundred and thirty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-two, one thousand and one hundred and thirty-seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the line that is not a name was put on that page and the name was not is the reader's own, and is that a page which can be read out loud to twenty people is a page that has to be true in the mouths of people who cannot read it, and that a refusal with a name against it is a thing that gets said back in a market over a winter, and that a refusal with no name against it is a thing that happened and that a man who wants to know it happened can look at the page; and that the reason a man of about thirty-eight was not asked to read his own refusal out is this yard's own mouth, and is that a man asked to read his own no back to a room has to stand there and be a thing that room has an opinion about, and that this yard was not going to make one of its own into that; and that the reason the fifth boat of that afternoon is on no page at all is his own, and is that a boat that did not go is not a fact about the water and putting it on a page would make a harbor that has been told no about a boat that never tried; and that the reason the shortfall was said out loud in that yard's own mouth at half past five and not in that room is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a deficit is a figure against a yard and a page read out in a room is a page about a room, and that a man who carries one into the other at the end of a month has stopped being answerable for anything at all; and that the reason no standing figure in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that Wednesday is this yard's own mouth, and is that a page with four names on it and a line with no name on it is a page about ten days, and that the price of lamp oil has not come down and the mole head is still dark and there is still no flour in that market and the sixty-first week stands fallen due, and that this yard left every account in that harbor open on that Wednesday, knocked nothing down, shut nothing, and is not going to begin on anything that happened in those ten days.*
