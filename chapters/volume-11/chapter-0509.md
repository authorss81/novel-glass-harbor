# Chapter 509

*Saturday the Eleventh Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Room over That Fish Market Let Out on a Monday and a Tuesday and a Wednesday and a Thursday and a Friday and Not on This One Because It Is a Harvest Week and About a Dozen People in It Anyway, a Working Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands at That Market Putting Out Loud in That Room a Thing That Is Going Round That Harbor About a List of the People Who Put Nothing In and a Ropemaker of About Sixty-One Refusing It in About Four Words and Then Saying Out Loud Once, and Not a Minister and Not on Any Sheet and Not a Member of Anything, the Whole of the Difference Between a Roster of Boats and a Schedule of Who May Go Out and When and a Roll of People, a Fair Copy of a Schedule of Which Boats May Go Out of That Harbor and on Which Hour Come Ashore on the Thursday and Going Round That Market, a Buoy a Bell and a Line at Six Hundred and Forty Pence With the Seventh of December Against It and a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence With the Ninth of December Against It Both Written Into the Fitting-Out Sheet on This Day With Both Days on the Face of Each Line, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Twenty-Nine Thousand Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Eighty-Three Marks, Eight Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Saturday the eleventh day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of a fish market that is not let out that day.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. The buoy, the bell and the line went into the total at the foot of the sheet that carries the money on that day at six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, with the seventh of December against it, and a day apiece in advance to the same four men went into that total at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the ninth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty thousand and eighteen pence out, being two hundred and fifty marks, one shilling and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence short, being one hundred and eighty-three marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirteenth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighteenth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty-nine days. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and nobody sent for him and nobody thanked him. The man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a chart in a bag was in that harbor that day and nobody asked him for it and nobody was put in a room with him. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and no figure is set against that anywhere and nobody has said anything to him about it.**

---

**The room over that fish market is let out on a Monday and a Tuesday and a Wednesday and a Thursday and a Friday, and it is not let out on a Saturday, and this is a harvest week, and about a dozen people were in it anyway at about half past two in the afternoon and the man with the key and the pail was not there and had not been asked to be there.**

They had come up those stairs because there is nowhere else in that harbor to sit down indoors on a Saturday in a harvest week, and about half of them said so out loud on the stairs and the other half did not.

**A fair copy of a schedule came ashore on the Thursday and has been going round that market since.** It is a fair copy in a clerk's hand of which boats may go out of that harbor and on which hour and on which tide, and it carries a seal at the head of it and no figure on it, and the man who hires hands at that market has a copy of the boat column of it in his own coat and has read it out twice in that market.

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-three who hires hands put the other thing to the room, and he put it out loud and he put it to everybody in it and not to one person.**

"Somebody came to me on Wednesday in that market and asked me for a list of the people in this harbor who have put nothing into that sheet over the fish market," he said. "A man I did not know and who would not give his name and who was not in a coat with anything on it. He said it was worth something and he was not lying about the worth, because a name that is on no paper anywhere is a name that can be come back to twice, and a name that is on a paper can only be read. He said it would make the line outside that mole easier to run and he said it the way a man says a thing he has been told rather than thought of."

Nobody in that room said anything for a moment. About a dozen people were in it and about five of them cannot read the schedule that was read out in that market on the Thursday.

**A ropemaker of about sixty-one, who is not in her room on a Sunday and has not been in one for two years, and who is not a member of anything, and who is not on a list of anything, and whose name is not on that sheet on any line, said four words from about nine feet back.**

"Nobody is keeping that."

**And then she said the rest of it out loud, once, and it is the whole of what she said that afternoon, and she is not a minister and she is not an officer and she was not on that party's crew and nobody thanked her for saying it.**

"Now listen, because there is a thing going round this market this week and it is going to get itself confused with a thing that came ashore on Thursday, and those two things are not the same thing and if anybody in this room cannot tell the difference then somebody else will tell it for them and it will cost somebody a job."

"That schedule is a list of **work**. It says which boat may go out of this harbor and on which hour and on which tide. It does not say who does it. It came from an office and it is written in a clerk's hand and it is about seventy boats and it does not have a person's name on it anywhere on it, and the harbor could have been given that list on paper in a morning by a man who owes nothing to anybody in it, and a man with an office could have asked for it and got it and said thank you."

"A roll of people who put nothing in is not a list of work. **It is a list of what a person has not got money for.** And there is not one particle of that which any office in this world can be given. It has to be written by somebody here, out of what he knows about his neighbours, and the only people who can write it are the people who did not put in, and they are the last people in this harbor who are going to write it, because writing it is putting it in a second time and paying for it in something you cannot put on a slate."

"So a man can bring a list of boats into this room and go out with a list of boats. And nobody can bring a list of names into this room at all, and I have said that in this room before and I am saying it again, and the last time I said it a man said *then somebody will be asked twice*, and yes, and I would rather somebody was asked twice by one man with a bad memory than that the whole of this harbor had a book about what it has not got."

**She put nothing in. Not one penny of hers is on that sheet on any line and nobody asked her for one and nobody asked her twice, and she came up those stairs on a Saturday in a harvest week when the room was not let out, and nobody thanked her for that either.**

---

**And at about ten past three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of the two lines out loud before he wrote either figure into the total at the foot of that sheet, and he said the rule out loud once, and about six people were standing on that slip and listened to all of it.**

"The buoy, the bell and the line, six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and the seventh of December against it, being the day the two men started on it. The days apiece in advance to the same four men, six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and the ninth of December against it, being the day they worked and not the day I paid them. Both of those are in the total now and they have been in the total since about half past eleven this morning and I have not said out loud what I was doing, so here it is: **a line goes into that sheet with the day the work was done against it and is written into that sheet on the day it is written, and both of those days are on the face of the line where a stranger can put a finger on them.** That is the whole of the rule and it is four months old and it has never been said out loud before because it has never before mattered to anybody that a week was crossed."

Nobody argued with him about it. Nobody asked why those two lines and not the others. The other lines are on that sheet with their own day against them and the day they went in is on that sheet against them too, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three figures off it and find that they agree.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, and it had moved by a thousand three hundred and twelve pence that day for the first time since the Wednesday, and he said where every one of those pence came from.**

"Twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence short, being one hundred and eighty-three marks, eight shillings and twopence. It moved this morning by a thousand three hundred and twelve pence at about half past eleven and it has not moved since, and that is one buoy and four days of four men, and nothing has gone into that sheet since the sixteenth day of October and nobody in this harbor has been asked for a penny of it and nobody is going to be."

---

**A ROPEMAKER OF ABOUT SIXTY-ONE CAME UP STAIRS INTO A ROOM THAT WAS NOT LET OUT ON A HARVEST SATURDAY AND REFUSED IN FOUR WORDS A THING THAT HAD BEEN ASKED FOR IN THAT MARKET ON THE WEDNESDAY, AND THEN SAID OUT LOUD ONCE THAT A ROSTER OF BOATS AND A SCHEDULE OF WHICH HOUR A BOAT MAY GO OUT IS A LIST OF WORK AND A ROLL OF PEOPLE WHO PUT NOTHING IN IS A LIST OF WHAT A PERSON HAS NOT GOT MONEY FOR, AND THAT ONE OF THE TWO CAN BE HANDED TO AN OFFICE AND THE OTHER HAS TO BE WRITTEN BY THE PEOPLE WHO WOULD BE ON IT. NOBODY WROTE IT DOWN. THE BUOY AND FOUR DAYS WENT INTO THAT TOTAL WITH THE SEVENTH AND THE NINTH AGAINST THEM AND THE DAY EACH WAS WRITTEN ON THE FACE OF THE LINE. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-NINE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK. A MAN NAMED TOBIAS WREN, WHO IS SIXTY-ONE, SAT ON THE CROWN OF THAT MOLE FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR WITH ELEVEN PIECES OF A BOAT BEHIND HIM AND NOBODY SENT FOR HIM.**

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the eleventh day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the room over that fish market is let out on a Monday and a Tuesday and a Wednesday and a Thursday and a Friday and was not let out on that Saturday because it was a harvest week, and that about a dozen people were in it anyway at about half past two in the afternoon and the man with the key and the pail was not there and had not been asked to be there; and that a fair copy of a schedule of which boats may go out of that harbor and on which hour and on which tide came ashore on the Thursday with a seal at the head of it and no figure on it, and that it carries no person's name on it anywhere on it; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands at that market put it out loud in that room at about half past two that a man he did not know and who would not give his name had asked him on the Wednesday for a list of the people in that harbor who have put nothing into that sheet, and that the ropemaker of about sixty-one refused it in about four words and then said out loud once that a schedule of boats and hours is a list of work and a roll of people who put nothing in is a list of what a person has not got money for, and that the first of those can be handed to an office and the second has to be written by the people who would be on it; and that she put nothing in and is on that sheet on no line and was not thanked; and that the buoy, the bell and the line went into the total at the foot of the sheet that carries the money at about half past eleven in the morning at six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, with the seventh of December against it, and that a day apiece in advance to the same four men went into that total at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the ninth of December against it, and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once that a line goes into that sheet with the day the work was done against it and is written into that sheet on the day it is written and that both of those days are on the face of the line; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty thousand and eighteen pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence short, being one hundred and eighty-three marks, eight shillings and twopence, and that the running out on it moved by one thousand three hundred and twelve pence on that day and nothing came in against it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a ropemaker was in a room on a harvest Saturday when the room was not let out is her own, and is that a room over a market is the only roof in this harbor that is not owned by the man who owns the boat, and that the woman who ropes has been the person in this harbor who could afford a Saturday for eleven years and has not had one since; and that the reason a schedule of boats was left in that market on the Thursday without anybody in this yard objecting to it is the yard's own mouth, and is that a list of work is a thing a harbor can be handed and a list of people is a thing a harbor has to make, and that this yard has spent four months keeping the two apart and would rather be annoying about it for a year than see them confused in a fortnight; and that the reason the rule about the day a line is written and the day the work was done was said out loud for the first time in four months is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that it stopped being a habit on the eighth of December and became a thing somebody could be asked about, because a buoy went into four fathoms of water on a Wednesday and four men were paid on a Friday for a Thursday, and that a rule a stranger cannot ask about is a rule that only the man who keeps it knows the reason for; and that the reason nothing standing in that harbor was touched on that Saturday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a schedule of boats can be handed to an office and a standing account cannot be handed to anything at all, and that the first of those two things came ashore on the Thursday and the second of them has not come ashore anywhere and is not going to, and that a harbor which lets the first of two things through its door on a Thursday has not thereby let the second of them out;  and that the reason no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor on any of those ten days is this movement's own, and is that the sheet is short by twenty-nine thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence against that yard's own book and the whole of that deficit is carried by one yard against one crew, and that a page which is made to balance by asking a harbor for the difference is a page that will be balanced once and will be asked for again.*
