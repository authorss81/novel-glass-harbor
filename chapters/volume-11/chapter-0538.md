# Chapter 538

*Sunday the Ninth Day of January, a Wipe, the Eighty-Eighth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, Six People Walking Past It and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, a Day of the Berth Fund Paid at Ten in the Morning and a Day Asked for by Name Paid at Four in the Afternoon and the Reason for the First of Those Entered in the Second Column in New Words and Said Out Loud Once at the Bench Stone, a Slater of About Forty Coming Back to That Yard at About Eleven in the Morning and Saying Out Loud That He Is Not Going to Be the Man Who Puts a Slate on a Roof He Has Not Seen in the Spring, and Nobody Asked Him for a Figure and He Gave None, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Walking Out to the Head of That Mole at About Two in the Afternoon and Standing at the Rail for About Ten Minutes for the Reason That His Own Boat Is on the Other Side of That Line and for No Other Reason and Nobody Asked Him for Another, Nothing Going Out and Nothing Going In and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Sixty-Five Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Four Hundred and Eleven Marks, Seven Shillings and Sixpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the ninth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in that shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. No charge was placed on that cistern on it and none is placed on any Sunday. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out, being four hundred and seventy-eight marks and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-six days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty-eight; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-six days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-second night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifteenth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirtieth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-seventh; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy-eight days. The cistern at the back of that market is charged tomorrow and not today, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and is going to still be off it tomorrow. The nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole were still nine on that Sunday and nobody in that harbor counted them twice that day. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-six days and he was five days on that coast and came back up that road on the Saturday morning in a cart with a crate of salt cod in it and the crate is in the store on the second lay and it is his and it is not on any sheet.**

---

**The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-eighth time it has come down bare, and it went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and six people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.**

---

**At about ten in the morning, with the door of that shed open on the slip, the man who keeps the leaf said out loud, once, why a day of that berth fund was paid on the eighty-eighth wipe, and about four men were on that slip at the time and heard it, and it is not the reason the last wipe carried and it is not the reason the wipe before that carried.**

"There was a roof on that shed on Wednesday morning and there is a roof on it now and this yard lost eleven years of shavings and a stock of fir and two benches in between, and the reason I am saying this is that a man standing on this slip this morning has noticed that the berth fund paid a day yesterday for a day when four men were on a roof instead of on a boat, and he is right that it did and he is wrong about what it means. **That fund pays for a day of work and it does not know and does not care what a man did with the day, and the second a day is paid for the using of the day then this yard is a harbor office with a slate in it, and I would rather be a yard with twenty-eight pence in a bag than that.** A man cut a roof up on Saturday with his own hands because he was hired in advance on Thursday and that is what the day was for, and I did not know he would use it on a roof and I would not have stopped him, and if he had gone out on the water instead I would have paid him exactly the same and been exactly as glad about it."

Nobody asked him about it again that day and nobody has asked him about it since.

---

**And at about eleven in the morning the slater of about forty came back to that yard and stood in the gap of the doorway for about ten minutes and looked up at the underside of a roof that had been finished about six hours before, and then he said what he came to say, and nobody asked him for a figure and he did not give one.**

"You put a roof on in three days on a Tuesday slate and it is a good roof and I am not saying it is not. There is not one sill under any of it. **I am not going to be the man who puts a slate on that roof in the spring, and I want that said out loud in that yard today so that nobody in this harbor spends the winter thinking a man has been found to do it.** There are about a yard of old stone at the east end and nothing at the west and a slate wants a sill and a wall and a man, and I will come and look at it in the spring when there is something to look at, and that is the whole of what I have come to say."

Nobody in that harbor asked him what it would cost, or offered him anything, or asked him whether he wanted the work, and he was not thanked for coming and he has not been asked about it again.

---

**And at about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-three who hires hands walked out along that mole to the head of it and stood at the rail for about ten minutes and came back in and said one thing about it, and nobody asked him for a second reason and he did not give one.**

"A line is a line and there are nine of them and they were nine of them on Saturday and they are nine of them now. I went out and looked because **my boat is on the other side of that and that is the only reason I have got, and I am not dressing it up as anything else, because it is a thing that takes bread off nine families in this market and not a thing that anybody should make a speech about.**"

He said that and went back to the west quay and did not say it a second time.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door open on the slip, and said that the day had not touched it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Saturday morning and unmoved now, and the only thing that happened between those two hours was that a man who has worked on that mole head eleven times came into a yard and told me there is no sill under a roof I paid for on Saturday, and that is now on this slip and not on that sheet, because a man who says he will look at a thing in the spring is not a figure anybody can take off a page."

"And **it is a Sunday, and a wall went down at half past ten this morning and went up again at an angle, and this is the eighty-eighth time, and that is the number this harbor has moved in seven days and not the number anybody will hear about.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Sunday, and nobody was thanked for the roof.

**THE WALL BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE EIGHTY-EIGHTH TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT UP UNREPAIRED AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND SIX PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. A DAY OF THAT BERTH FUND WAS PAID AT TEN AND A DAY ASKED FOR BY NAME WAS PAID AT FOUR AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK DID NOT MOVE. A SLATER OF ABOUT FORTY STOOD IN THAT DOORWAY AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT THERE IS NOT ONE SILL UNDER THAT ROOF AND THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO BE THE MAN WHO PUTS A SLATE ON IT, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM FOR A FIGURE AND HE GAVE NONE. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WALKED OUT TO THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND STOOD AT THE RAIL FOR ABOUT TEN MINUTES AND SAID THAT HIS OWN BOAT IS ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THAT LINE AND THAT IS THE ONLY REASON HE HAS GOT. NOTHING WENT OUT ON ANY LINE AND NOTHING WENT INTO IT AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR ANYTHING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SIXTY-FIVE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the ninth day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-eighth time at about half past ten in the morning and was walked on by none of it and went up unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and that six people of that harbor walked past it and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that a day of the berth fund was paid on that Sunday and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once and in words of its own, why, being that that fund pays for a day of work and does not know and does not care what a man did with the day, and that the second a day is paid for the using of the day then this yard is a harbor office with a slate in it; and that a slater of about forty came back to that yard at about eleven in the morning and stood in that doorway for about ten minutes and said out loud that there is not one sill under that roof, that about a yard of old stone is left at the east end and nothing at the west, that he is not going to be the man who puts a slate on it, and that he wants that said out loud in that yard that day so that nobody in this harbor spends the winter thinking a man has been found to do it, and that nobody asked him what it would cost, nobody offered him anything, nobody asked him whether he wanted the work and he was not thanked for coming and has not been asked about it since; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands walked out to the head of that mole at about two in the afternoon, stood at the rail for about ten minutes and said that there are nine hulls off that point and that they were nine on Saturday, and that he went out to look because his own boat is on the other side of that line and that is the only reason he has got and he did not dress it up as anything else, and that nobody asked him for a second reason and he did not give one and did not say it a second time; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Saturday; and that the cistern at the back of that market is charged tomorrow and not today; and that a man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat was five days on that coast and came back up that road on the Saturday morning in a cart with a crate of salt cod in it and that crate is in the store on the second lay and is his and is not on any sheet.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that fund paid on the eighty-eighth wipe, in words that are neither the last two wipes nor the twentieth, is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that four men were on a roof on Saturday instead of on a boat, and that a fund which begins asking what a day was spent on ends up asking what a man is worth, and this yard has never once asked a man what he is worth and is not going to begin on a Sunday when everybody is watching; and that the reason that slater was not asked for a figure is his own, and is that a man who says he will come back in the spring has offered a season and not a price, and a price taken off him this week would be a price for the spring and would sit on that sheet in June with a day on it that nobody could stand behind; and that the reason the man who hires hands was let stand on his own reason is the reader's own, and is that nine hulls is a number a man can get wrong twice in a day and one reason is a number of one, and a man with one honest reason in a market of nine reasons is a man who has made the other eight of them look like excuses, and that is not this yard's doing and is not this yard's to argue with; and that the reason not a single standing matter of that harbor was carried forward, trimmed or shut on that Sunday is his own, and is that the cistern is not charged today and will be tomorrow and the board at the back of that market does not know what was done to a shed two hundred yards away, and that a rota that turned on the fact that there had been a fire would be a rota that could be turned off again by the next person who was cross about something; and that the reason that the eighty-eighth figure was the one this yard said out loud at the end of that Sunday is his own, and is that it is the only count in this harbor a man can check without a pencil, that it goes up by one every Sunday whether anybody does anything or not, and that a harbor which pays attention to the number that always moves is a harbor that will not be tempted to believe the numbers that only move when somebody wants them to.
