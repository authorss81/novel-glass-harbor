# Chapter 524

*Sunday the Twenty-Sixth Day of December, a Wipe, the Eighty-Sixth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, Five People Walking Past It and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon and the Reason for That Entered in the Second Column in New Words and Said Out Loud Once, a Page Going Up on a Wall of the Floor of That Market at About Half Past One in the Afternoon With the Names of the Local Crews at the Head of It and the Marks They Answer For Against Each Name and the Name of the Man of About Thirty-Two Nowhere on It and He Not Asking to Have It on It and Nobody in That Room Suggesting That It Ought to Be, the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed and No Fifth Head Added and the Fitting-Out Sheet on No Wall in That Room, Nothing Going Out on Any Line and Nothing Going into It, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Forty-Three Thousand Four Hundred and Ninety Pence, Being Two Hundred and Seventy-One Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the twenty-sixth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-four thousand one hundred and thirty pence out, being three hundred and thirty-eight marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-three thousand four hundred and ninety pence short, being two hundred and seventy-one marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixteenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-four days. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and the hundred and eleventh of them is on the day after this one. The tide gang of that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the spring, and two of those four are on that gang on a Monday and Thursday and neither of them is at the head of that rota board. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-two days and he was on the west quay for about two hours that morning and said one word to a man he was working beside and has not said anything about it since.**

---

**The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-sixth time it has come down bare, and it went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and five people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.**

---

**And at about ten in the morning, with the door of that shed open on the slip, the man who keeps the leaf said out loud, once, why a day of the berth fund was paid on the eighty-sixth wipe, and about four men who were on that slip at the time heard it, and it is not the reason the last wipe carried and it is not the reason the wipe before that carried.**

"Because that fund pays a day for a day, and the day it is paying is the day the work was done, and a wipe is a day a wall comes down and a man puts it up again at an angle. **A man who put that wall up on Sunday is owed his Sunday by whoever he put it up for, and he is not going to be told by me that his Sunday depends on a day of the week.** I have said that once and I am not going to say it again this month and neither is anybody else in this yard."

Nobody asked him about it again that day and nobody has asked him about it since.

---

**And at about half past one in the afternoon a page went up on a wall of the floor of that market, on the boards, and it is a buoys list and it is not on the wall of the room over that market and it is not the sheet that carries the money and it does not carry a figure of a penny on it.**

The names are at the head of it, and against each name are the marks that name answers for, and the marks are the marks on the coast four days down and not in this harbor.

1. *Jory Anse, master of the Osyra, nine men, the first mark and the ground tackle to it, and every month he goes out and looks at it and writes down what is wrong with it.*
2. *Bel Hesk, master of the Cordle, six men, the second mark and the line to it from the first, and every month he goes out and looks at it and writes down what is wrong with it.*
3. *Tam Rieve, master of the Sennet, four men, the third mark, which is a bell and not a float, and every month he goes out and looks at it and writes down what is wrong with it.*
4. *The crews of that port together, five marks between them and the putting of any of them, and every month one of the four above goes and one of the four above stays.*

**The name of the man of about thirty-two is not on that page and it is not under it and it is not on the back of it either, and he did not ask to have it on it, and nobody in that market suggested that it ought to be, and nobody was thanked for the page going up.**

Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, was the one who put it up with four tacks and a hammer at about half past one and he said out loud, to the people standing round it, that a list of who answers for what is a list of what people have agreed to answer for and that anybody who had not agreed to answer for anything was not on it and was not going to be put on it and was not going to be asked whether they wished they were.

A woman at the end stall with a slate asked him out loud, in front of about nine people, whether a person from this harbor could put a name on that list, and Hobb Lee said that she could put a name on it tomorrow morning and that the list would be tacked up again with a fifth line and that nobody in that market could stop her, and that what would happen to her is that the crews of that port would find out, because that page goes down that coast in a cart on Monday and does not come back.

The woman said that she knew that, and that her son was nineteen and did not want to answer for anything, and that she was not asking for a mark. Nobody asked her about the son.

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over that market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and the thing on the wall of that room was the four heads and the four standing lines under them and the eleven names at the foot of the sheet and it had not changed, and no fifth head went on it on account of a commonwealth, a condition, a list, a buoy or a page.**

The man who keeps the leaf stood where the long table is and said out loud that the sheet that carries the money is not in that room and is not on any wall in it, and that the four heads on that wall are the four heads on that wall, and that a buoys list with four names on it went up on the boards of that market an hour ago and is a different thing and is not this room, and that anybody who wanted either of those two things said out loud in a room like this one could ask for it and it would not be that afternoon.

Nobody in that room asked for either.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone, and said that the day had not touched it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Forty-three thousand four hundred and ninety pence short, being two hundred and seventy-one marks, ten shillings and tenpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Saturday morning and not going to move tomorrow either, because tomorrow is Monday and Monday is a cistern and a cistern is not a cost. **A page on a boards with four names and five marks on it cost this harbor nothing at all and bought this harbor less than nothing, and a person who works it out as a bargain has misunderstood both the page and the harbor.**"

Nobody was thanked on that Sunday, and the wall went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the man of about thirty-four of that commonwealth slept in that barn at the second lay for the fourth night and was on the first lay in the morning, and nobody sent for him and nobody thanked him and he was not on the list with the four names on it.

---

**THE WALL BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE EIGHTY-SIXTH TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT UP WHOLE AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND FIVE PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. A DAY OF THE BERTH FUND WAS PAID AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND A DAY WAS PAID AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK DID NOT MOVE AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IS IN THE BAG UNDER IT. A PAGE WITH FOUR NAMES AND FIVE MARKS WENT UP ON FOUR TACKS ON THE BOARDS OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT HALF PAST ONE IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE NAME OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO IS NOT ON IT AND HE DID NOT ASK TO HAVE IT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ON NO WALL IN THE ROOM OVER THAT MARKET AND THE FOUR HEADS ON THE WALL OF THAT ROOM DID NOT CHANGE. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-THREE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-sixth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-sixth time at about half past ten in the morning and was walked on by none of it and went up unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and that five people of that harbor walked past it and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-four thousand one hundred and thirty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-three thousand four hundred and ninety pence short, being two hundred and seventy-one marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Saturday; and that a day of the berth fund was paid on that Sunday and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once and in new words why, being that a fund pays the day the work was done and a man who put a wall up on a Sunday is owed that Sunday; and that a page carrying four names of local crews and the five marks they answer for went up on the boards of the floor of that market at about half past one in the afternoon on four tacks, and that the name of the man of about thirty-two is not on that page and he did not ask to have it on it and nobody suggested that it ought to be and nobody was thanked; and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read what is put in front of them, and that the four heads on the wall of that room did not change and no fifth head went on it and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and is not in it, and that nobody in that room asked for either of those two things; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-seven, one thousand and one hundred and fifty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-six, one thousand and one hundred and fifty-seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that fund paid on the eighty-sixth wipe, in words that are neither of the two the last wipes carried, is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a fund which is asked to explain itself is a fund that has been given a condition, and that this yard has kept that book dull on purpose for thirteen weeks and is not going to be the one that first makes it interesting; and that the reason the buoys list went up on the boards and not on the wall of the room over that market is the reader's own, and is that a list of who answers for what is a thing a market does and a wall of claims is a thing a room does, and that the two were put in the same sentence out loud in that room and named separately so that nobody in that market could go away thinking that a name under the four heads is the same as a name under a mark, and that they are not; and that the reason no name of this yard's is on that page at all is his own and was said in a room on the Saturday and not this morning, and is that a person who has said in front of twenty people that he does not set a buoy has to be able to be seen not to be on a list, and that a list with a name of about thirty-two on it that he did not ask for would be a list this yard put him on against his own mouth; and that the reason not a single debt in that harbor was touched, bettered, carried forward or shut on that Sunday is this yard's own mouth, and is that a Sunday with a page on the boards of that market is still a Sunday with no flour in it and the lamp oil still standing at four hundred and fifty pence on a board at an end stall, and that the four heads on that wall are four and are not going to be five, and a week of that winter is tomorrow being fallen due and this yard is not going to be in a position to do one thing about it.**
