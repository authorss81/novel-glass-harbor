# Chapter 503

*Sunday the Fifth Day of December, a Wipe, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench in That Harbor Coming Down Bare for the Eighty-Third Time at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, Four People Walking Past It and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and the Same Figure Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon on a Sunday, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Entering the Reason for That in the Second Column in New Words and Saying Out Loud Once That a Berth Fund Pays a Day for a Day and Does Not Care What Is Standing Outside That Mole, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Fitting-Out Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock With a Bowl on the Step and About Twenty People Up Those Stairs in the Half Hour After It and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Page Written in That Yard's Own Hand Carrying on Its Face the Rule That the Reader's Own Name Goes Under What He Reads and the Hour Under His Name and That Anybody in That Room May Stop Him at Any Word He Is Saying, Read Out Twice by Hobb Lee of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets on the Third Lay at Two o'Clock and at Twenty-Five Minutes Past Two, Nobody Stopping Him in Either Reading, the Shortfall Standing at Twenty-Seven Thousand One Hundred and Ninety Pence, Being One Hundred and Sixty-Nine Marks, Twelve Shillings and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Sunday the fifth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room four hundred yards from that quay over the floor of a fish market.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon for a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-seven thousand eight hundred and thirty pence out, being two hundred and thirty-six marks, five shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-seven thousand one hundred and ninety pence short, being one hundred and sixty-nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that figure has not moved since the Saturday. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eightieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninety-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twelfth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty-three days. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest, and fifty-eight weeks of it stand fallen due and no further week fell due on that Sunday. There is one berth short on that third lay and there has been for a month. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head, and nobody in that harbor has said anything to him about it in either direction and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

**The wall behind a reeve's bench came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-third time it has come down bare and gone up unrepaired, and it went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and four people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.**

The man who keeps the leaf did not walk past it at all. He was in that shed with the door shut and the door propped and nothing on the bench stone but a slate and a pen.

"A wipe," he said, to nobody. "It is the eighty-third. I have written it in the first column on every one of them since the first of October and I have written in the same words on every one of them, and I am not going to vary it now because a line of boats has come outside that mole and made the eighty-third into something that sounds like a reason."

---

**And the day came in and the day went out, both at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, on a Sunday, and he said out loud once, in that yard's own mouth, why a day is paid on a day when nobody in that harbor is on the water at all.**

"You are going to ask me why the figure in chalk is the same today as it was on Friday," he said. "Here is the whole of it, and it is one sentence and then I am going back to the slate. **A berth fund pays a day for a day.** It does not care what is standing outside that mole. It was not written by anybody in this room and it does not know about a line of boats, and if it did know about a line of boats it would pay the same, because a day is a day and a day is a day whether the tide is making or the boats are going nowhere."

The four men on that slip heard him. One of them said, at the door, that he had not asked.

"You did not have to," he said. "I said it out loud so that a man who did not ask has got it the same as a man who did."

---

**At about two o'clock in the afternoon the room over that fish market was let out and a bowl went down on the top stone of the step and about twenty people went up in the half hour after two, and about eleven of them could not read. It is the first time a page has been read out in that room since the twenty-eighth of November.**

The sheet had been written the day before in the yard's own hand, being the account of what was done in that harbor on the fourth of December and what was not done in it. At the head of it, under the account and above it, there is a rule, and the rule was written before the account and not after it, and it is on the face of the paper in the same hand as the account, and it says that the reader's own name goes under what he reads and the hour goes under his name, and that anybody standing in that room may stop him at any word he is saying and put their own words against his, and that what goes into the book afterward is what is on the paper and not what he makes of it going to it.

Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay and has done since he was nine, read it out twice. He did not raise his voice for the second and he did not go slower at it. He put his own name and the hour under each of the two readings, being two o'clock and twenty-five minutes past two, in his own hand.

Nobody stopped him in the first reading. Nobody stopped him in the second.

**And at about half past three a woman of about thirty-one of no office stood at the back of that room and asked him one question, and it was not about the account and he answered it and nobody argued with either of them.**

"Is the room going to say that on Sundays now."

"No," he said. "This room is a room over a fish market and it is let out to anybody who comes up the stairs and there is a bowl on the step when it is wanted and there is not one when it is not, and that is the whole of how it is decided and the decision is made by a man with a key and a pail. A thing being read out in here twice a day would be a different thing and it would want about nine of you to agree to it and I have not got nine of you and I am not going to count them in a doorway."

Nobody counted anybody in a doorway. The woman of thirty of no office went down those stairs first, and she has not asked for that room twice in any week since, and she is not on that party's crew and she is not its mouth.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, standing at that bench stone, and it had not moved all day and he said so.**

"Twenty-seven thousand one hundred and ninety pence short, being one hundred and sixty-nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence. It is the same figure it was at four o'clock yesterday and it is the same figure it was at ten o'clock this morning, and there is not a line on that sheet with a Sunday against it, and that is not a thing I have decided. I did not decide it. Nobody asked me."

---

**THE WALL CAME DOWN BARE BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH FOR THE EIGHTY-THIRD TIME AND FOUR PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NOBODY PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT AND IT WENT UP UNREPAIRED AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH. ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE CAME INTO THAT YARD ON A SUNDAY AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE WENT OUT OF IT ON THAT SAME SUNDAY, AND THE FIGURE IN THE CHALK DID NOT MOVE, AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID THE REASON OUT LOUD ONCE. A PAGE WAS READ OUT TWICE IN A ROOM OVER THAT FISH MARKET BY A MAN WHO MENDS NETS AND WHO WAS PAID NOTHING FOR IT AND WAS NOT THANKED, AND THE RULE THAT ANYBODY IN THE ROOM MAY STOP HIM AT ANY WORD WAS ON THE FACE OF THE PAPER AND WAS USED BY NOBODY THAT AFTERNOON. THERE ARE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN A CHEST AND FIFTY-EIGHT WEEKS OF IT STAND FALLEN DUE AND A FURTHER WEEK FALLS DUE ON TUESDAY. ONE BERTH ON THAT THIRD LAY IS SHORT. THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS CHARGED ON A MONDAY AND A THURSDAY AND WAS NOT CHARGED ON THAT SUNDAY.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the fifth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the eighty-third time at about half past ten in the morning and was walked on by none of it and went up unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back, and that four people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it on that Sunday, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-seven thousand eight hundred and thirty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-seven thousand one hundred and ninety pence short, being one hundred and sixty-nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the day before; and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud once, in that yard's own mouth, that a berth fund pays a day for a day and does not care what is standing outside that mole, and that the man who heard it at the door had not asked; and that a room over that fish market was let out on that Sunday with a bowl on the step at about two o'clock and about twenty people went up in the half hour after it and about eleven of them could not read, and that a page written the day before in the yard's own hand, carrying the rule on its face, was read out twice by Hobb Lee of about forty-six at two o'clock and at twenty-five minutes past two with his own name and the hour under each, and that nobody stopped him at any word in either reading; and that a woman of about thirty-one of no office asked at about half past three whether that room was going to read a page twice a day, and was told no, and that nobody counted anybody in that doorway.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that wall was written down in the same words for the eighty-third time is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a thing that varies its account of itself as it goes along is a thing nobody can go back and read, and that the eighty-three of them are worth more to a stranger as eighty-three identical lines than as eighty-three different ones; and that the reason no Sunday was put against that sheet in any line is the yard's own mouth, and is that a day on which a day's work was done and paid for is not a day that cost anything, and that writing a cost against a day the harbor did not lose would be the first figure on that sheet that this yard had made up; and that the reason the room over that fish market was not turned into a room that reads twice a day is the man who mends nets' own, and is that a room which reads on a rota is a room with an office in it, and this room has a key and a pail and a stove that does not draw and about eleven people in it who cannot read, and those three facts together are a floor and a set of stairs and nothing else; and that the reason the hearing of a man of about twenty-nine was not put in the first column of that leaf on that Sunday, when it came back on the Saturday and when a Sunday is exactly the kind of day a harbor writes a good thing down on, is the yard's own mouth, and is that a yard which entered the day a man got his hearing back in the first column would be a yard that had begun keeping a book of what people are becoming; and that the reason nothing standing in that harbor was settled on that Sunday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a Sunday is the day a harbor finds out what it has and not the day it begins paying any of it, and that this yard wrote a wipe down in eighty-three identical lines and did not write one line against a thing that has been owing since before the first of them;  and that the reason the page of the fourth of December was read out on the fifth and not on the sixth is the woman of about twenty-seven's own, and is that a page read a week later is a page about the week and this harbor has about eleven people in that room who would hear a Friday's business on a Monday and take it for a Monday's.*
