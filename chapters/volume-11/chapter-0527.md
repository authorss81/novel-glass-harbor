# Chapter 527

*Wednesday the Twenty-Ninth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Day's Hire of a Boat and Two Men to Run the Buoys Going Onto the Sheet at Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Two Marks, One Shilling and Fourpence, With the Twenty-Ninth of December Against It, the Whole of That Line Said Out Loud Before a Figure Was Written Against It, Including That the Two Men on That Boat Are Not on That Sheet and Are Not Counted on It and Their Names Are in the Book the Days Are Paid Out Of, Five Sentences Written at the Head of a Page at About Half Past Eight in the Morning Before Anybody Was Asked Anything and Not Altered After That Hour on That Day, Three Men Asked Out Loud One at a Time and Not in Front of the Other Two Whether They Will Each Hold One Channel of the Same Stretch of About Four Fathoms Off That Third Lay, Each One Told Before He Was Asked That He May Stop It and That Nobody Will Ask Him Which Channel It Was and Each One of the Three Hearing That Before He Answers and Not After, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Six Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Eight Pence, Being Two Hundred and Ninety-Two Marks, One Shilling and Sixpence, the Four Heads Not Changed, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. A day's hire of a boat and two men to run the buoys went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the twenty-ninth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-seven thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence out, being three hundred and fifty-eight marks, eight shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-six thousand seven hundred and thirty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-two marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and nineteenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-seven days. A register copy of what the harbor authority has been given is kept in a drawer in an office on the west basin and it is a copy and not the thing, and nobody in that harbor has ever been shown it. A drum of oil came ashore off a boat alongside the quay on that Wednesday at a figure nobody in that market has written on a board and about nine people heard the figure and one of them wrote it in a pocket book and no board in that harbor has a new figure on it this month. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-five days and he was on the head of that third lay for about two hours that afternoon and heard a man ask for a thing in a voice and heard him say it.**

---

**At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"A day's hire of a boat and two men to run the buoys. Three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence. Off a boat that is not the berth fund's and not this yard's, with the twenty-ninth of December against it, being the day the work was done, and it is in the total at the foot of that sheet this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. **Those two men are not on this sheet and they are not counted on it. Their names are in the book the days are paid out of, and that book is a different book with a different set of names in it, and this sheet carries no count of persons and has never carried one.** They are being paid at the figure in chalk for a day's work with a boat and two men and they are not a line of this yard's work and they are not going to be."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked whether the two men knew what they were being hired to do, and the man who keeps the leaf said that he had told them in one sentence what he was hiring them for and that they had both been doing it for years for other people and that he was not going to put a description of a day's work on a sheet that carries money and no persons.

---

**And at about half past eight in the morning, before any of that and before anybody had been asked anything, five sentences went onto the head of a page on that bench stone, and they were not altered after that hour on that day by anybody.**

1. *This is a stretch of about four fathoms off the head of that third lay, about two hundred yards long, and there is a float of tarred fir lying in it with a bell under a board on it and a mark of a Crown man's on the stone underneath it, put there on the twenty-first of December.*
2. *There are three of them asked and there are three things to hold, one each, and the thing each of them holds is one end of a channel of that water, and there is no name on any of the three and the man asked will not be told what any of them is called.*
3. *Nobody in this room is going to be asked why. Nobody is going to be asked whether he is sure and nobody is going to be asked to hold a particular one, and if a man takes one he will not be told afterwards which one he took.*
4. *A man asked may stop it. He may stop it at any hour and he will not be asked when he stopped it or why he stopped it, and the whole of it will be run with two ends of it or one end of it or with nobody in it at all, and it will be recorded that way and not any other way.*
5. *Nobody is paid for this and nobody in this room can send anybody anywhere, and if the answer is no this afternoon nobody will ask again this month or any month after it.*

**Every one of the three was told the fourth sentence and the fifth sentence before he was asked anything at all, and every one of the three heard both of them before he answered and not after, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud at about half past eight that he was going to read all five to each of them in order and would not read them a second time to anybody.**

---

**And three men were asked out loud, one at a time, and not in front of the other two, and the five sentences were read out word for word to each of them before each of them was asked anything else, and the two other men were not in that shed at the hour either of them was asked.**

**The first was a man of about fifty-one who crews four out of the first lay and owns none of them.** He heard all five sentences and then said, in about nine words, that he wanted to know whether the bell would be struck and by whom, and he was told that the tender on the surface would strike it and that the tender would be a named man in a dinghy who was not going into the water at any hour, and he said yes after that, and he did not ask a second question and was not asked one.

**The second was a man of about thirty-three who runs a lighter of about eleven tons on the west quay with two men.** He heard all five sentences and said yes in one word, and then said out loud, on his own account and before anybody asked him, that if he stopped it he would say so in the room and not on the water, and that he wanted that written down, and it was written down with the hour against it.

**The third was a man of about thirty-eight who works that compressor.** He heard all five sentences and then asked one question and got one answer, and then said yes, and he is the man who said no on the twenty-first of December about a channel and nobody in that shed connected the two things and nobody was going to.

"Will there be three of you in that water at any hour."

"There will be one at a time and this yard's man will be on the surface in a dinghy with a cutoff and on the compressor and not in the water, and he is not one of the three and is not going to be."

"Will anybody be asked which one he took."

"Nobody will be asked which one he took and I am going to read the fourth sentence to him again after he has said it and I am not going to read the other four."

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone, and said that the day had moved it by three hundred and thirty-six pence, and said one more thing out loud, once.**

"Forty-six thousand seven hundred and thirty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-two marks, one shilling and sixpence. It moved this morning by three hundred and thirty-six pence and that is a boat and two men for a day and those two men are not on this sheet and are not counted on it and their names are in a book the days are paid out of, and this sheet carries no count of persons and never has."

"And this morning at about half past eight I wrote five sentences at the head of a page and I read them to three men one at a time and not in front of each other, and each of the three of them heard that he may stop it and that nobody will ask him which one he took before he said anything, and one of the three said he wanted that second part written down and it is written down with the hour against it, and one of the three asked a question about a bell and got an answer and then said yes. **Those five sentences are not on this sheet either. They are on a page with no figure of mine on it, and they are in the book on this bench stone with the hour against them, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count the money on this sheet and cannot count the men in that book, and that is the correct order.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for anything on that Wednesday, and the third lay had two pressure cases in it and a slate with nothing written on it yet on a bench stone, and the five sentences went into the second column of that leaf in the order they were said.

---

**A DAY'S HIRE OF A BOAT AND TWO MEN TO RUN THE BUOYS WENT ON THAT SHEET AT THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE AND THE TWO MEN ON THAT BOAT ARE NOT ON THAT SHEET AND ARE NOT COUNTED ON IT AND THEIR NAMES ARE IN THE BOOK THE DAYS ARE PAID OUT OF. FIVE SENTENCES WERE ON THE HEAD OF A PAGE ON THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT HALF PAST EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND THREE MEN WERE ASKED IN THEM ONE AT A TIME AND NOT IN FRONT OF EACH OTHER, AND EVERY ONE OF THE THREE WAS TOLD HE MAY STOP IT AND THAT NOBODY WILL ASK HIM WHICH ONE HE TOOK BEFORE HE ANSWERED AND NOT AFTER, AND ALL THREE ANSWERED YES, AND ONE OF THEM ASKED FOR THAT PART OF IT TO BE WRITTEN DOWN AND IT WAS. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-SIX THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that a day's hire of a boat and two men to run the buoys went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, with the twenty-ninth of December against it being the day the work was done, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it and said out loud that the two men on that boat are not on that sheet and are not counted on it and that their names are in the book the days are paid out of, and that that sheet carries no count of persons; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-seven thousand three hundred and seventy-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-six thousand seven hundred and thirty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-two marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that the running out on it moved by three hundred and thirty-six pence on that day; and that five sentences were written at the head of a page on that bench stone at about half past eight in the morning and were not altered after that hour on that day by anybody, and that three men were asked out loud one at a time and not in front of the other two whether they will each hold one channel of that same stretch of about four fathoms off that third lay, and that every one of the three was told before he was asked that he may stop it and that nobody will ask him which channel it was, and that all three of them heard that before they answered and not after, and that all three said yes, and that one of the three asked for that second part to be written down and it was written down with the hour against it, and that one of the three asked who would strike the bell and was told that a named tender on the surface would strike it and would not be in the water at any hour, and that the five sentences are not on the sheet that carries the money and are in the book on that bench stone with the hour against them; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and sixty, one thousand and one hundred and seventy-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-nine, one thousand and one hundred and seventy-two.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason those five sentences went on a page with no figure on them and not onto the sheet that carries the money is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a sheet with money on it is a thing a harbor can check and a page with a question on it is a thing a person has to answer for, and that putting a question to three men on the same paper that pays for the compressor is how a man gets told that answering is a favour done to the yard; and that the reason the fourth of those five sentences was read to every one of the three before he was asked anything, and the fifth, and not the first three, is his own, and is that a man has to know he can stop a thing before he is asked whether he will do it, and that a man told he can stop it afterwards has been told a different thing; and that the reason a man of about thirty-three wanted the second half of the fourth sentence written down is his own and is that a man who has been told nobody will ask him which one he took has a right to have that in a book instead of in somebody's memory, and that a book is the only place a promise like that survives a winter; and that the reason the whole of that morning went into this column and no part of it went into the first is his own, and is that three men saying yes is a thing a stranger cannot count from a mole and five sentences written before anybody was asked is a thing a stranger can read, and only one of those two belongs on a page with figures on it; and that the reason this yard neither paid nor advanced nor wrote off nor closed one item of what that harbor owes on that Wednesday is his own, and is that a drum of oil came ashore this morning at a figure nobody in that market has put on a board and a pocket book in a coat is not a board and is not going to be, and that a figure nobody has written on a board is not a figure in that harbor, and that the sixty-third Tuesday is four days off, and this yard's wagons go down a coast road tomorrow.**
