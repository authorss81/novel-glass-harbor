# Chapter 530

*Saturday the First Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Only Thing Anybody in That Harbor Says About the Day Being the First of a Month Being That It Is a Saturday and That a Day Asked for by Name Is Paid on a Saturday at the Figure in Chalk, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read What Is Put in Front of Them, a Page Carrying the Crossing of the Thirtieth on It and the Names of Three Men on It and the Refusal of One Channel on It and No Name Against That Refusal Read Out Word for Word Once by the Man of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets on the Third Lay With His Own Name and the Hour Under It, the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed and No Fifth Head Added and the Four Standing Lines the Four Standing Lines and No Route and No Panel and No Account Represented by Anybody Who Has Not Asked to Be Represented, the Sheet That Carries the Money Said Out Loud in That Yard's Own Mouth at Forty-Seven Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Two Hundred and Ninety-Nine Marks, One Shilling and Sixpence, and Nobody in That Harbor Asked for the Rest of It and No Figure Asked of Any Person in That Harbor to Make It Otherwise, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the first day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-eight thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out, being three hundred and sixty-five marks, eight shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-nine marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and thirty; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and thirty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and twenty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventy days. A slip on the north side of that harbor is under water at the top of the flood and dry at half ebb and a lighter of about eleven tons lies at it in any weather and the master of that lighter has said that he would rather have a berth on the third lay and there is one berth short on the third lay. A register copy of what that harbor authority has been given is kept in a drawer in an office on the west basin and it is a copy and not the thing, and the four heads on the wall of that room over that market are not that copy and are not on that copy. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty-eight days and he was in that shed on that Saturday for about half an hour and nobody spoke to him and he did not speak to anybody.**

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, read out one page word for word with his own name and the hour under it, and about eleven people in that room could not read it and heard all of it.**

The page carries the crossing of the thirtieth and the names of three men on it and the refusal of one channel on it, and the reader's name and the hour are at the foot of it.

1. *That on the thirtieth day of December three men went into about four fathoms off the head of the third lay, one at a time, and each of them held one channel of the same stretch of that water, and the three of them were asked in the same words before they were asked anything and each of them was told before he was asked anything that he might stop it and that nobody would ask him which channel it was.*
2. *That before any of the three went down, one of the three asked out loud for one of the three channels to be dropped, and the drop was made, and nobody argued with him about it, and nobody asked him which channel it was, and he was not thanked and no figure is set against him.*
3. *That the three of them said three accounts at about half past four in the afternoon and that the three accounts do not agree about one thing, and that the one thing is whether that float was held by its line or was loose in that water, and that the three accounts were not settled and nobody was asked to agree with anybody.*
4. *That the stretch of that water now held is about a hundred and ten yards, and that about ninety yards at the north end of it is water that nobody in this harbor has looked at, and that it is going to stay that way until somebody looks at it.*
5. *That all three of the men came up, and that all three of them said out loud in that shed on the evening of the thirtieth that they had the same thing in their mouths, and that it was still in their mouths on the thirty-first, and that no figure has been set against any of it and none of the three of them is a member of anything.*

**There is no name against the second of those five lines, and there is no reason against it either, and the man who asked for that channel to be dropped is not on that page in any other place, and nobody in that room asked about it after the reading and nobody has asked about it since.**

A woman of about thirty-one asked out loud, in that room, whether the man who had given up a channel was the man who had been asked for a date by a Crown officer in a room on the west basin on the ninth of December, and Hobb Lee said that he was not going to say, and that the man who keeps the leaf had written it on that page as it happened and had not written a name against it because a name against it would have made it a different fact, and that the woman was welcome to think whatever she liked about it after she had gone up those stairs.

Nobody in that room stopped the reader at any word, and he read it once, word for word, and he did not read it twice, and the reader's own name and the hour went at the foot of it in his own hand.

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood up in that room and asked, out loud, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read, who reads out in that room when a thing is on a page in that shed, and nobody in that room answered him for about five seconds.**

Hobb Lee said that the man who keeps the leaf reads out whatever that yard has on its own slip, and that the fitting-out sheet that carries the money is not on any wall in that room and was not read out in that room on that day and is not going to be read out in that room this winter, and that the four heads on that wall are claims and refusals and restricted records and a public correction and that they are four and that they are not going to be five.

"Then who is it that decides," the man said.

"Nobody in this room decides where a boat goes and that includes the man who keeps the leaf and it includes the man who was in that water on Thursday. **No route and no account and nothing of that kind is on that wall for anybody who has not asked to be represented, and no man in this harbor has asked, and the man who gave up his buoys on the twenty-fifth of December has his name nowhere on that page and is not on any of the four lines and did not ask to be.**"

The man of about thirty-two was not in that room on that Saturday and was not sent for and has not been asked to come.

The only thing anybody in that market said about the day, all day, was that it was a Saturday, and that a day asked for by name is paid on a Saturday at the figure in chalk. A man at the end stall said it twice to two different people and to nobody else, and a clerk on the west basin said it once at about ten o'clock and put his own name and the hour under the saying of it.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise.**

"Forty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-nine marks, one shilling and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Thursday morning. Fifty-eight thousand four hundred and ninety-eight out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree, and that a figure of a hundred and ten yards of water is in three men's heads and is on that sheet nowhere, and that the only place it is written down is the second column of that leaf in three men's own words, and that is the correct order and I am not going to change it."

He put the pen down and said one more thing out loud, once, in that yard's own mouth, and then he said it once and not again.

"**A commonwealth said yes to one condition and the condition has been kept and this yard is not thanked for it and is not thanked for anything and no part of that condition has put a shilling cheaper on a table in this harbor.** The lamp oil is at four hundred and fifty pence and there is no flour in that market and there is no barley in it and there has been no barrel of lamp oil since the twenty-ninth day of August and the mole head has been dark since the seventh of May and the hose will be dead until the spring and the tide gang is four men short and the third lay is a berth short and nineteen winters are on a page in an office on the west basin and the wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the sixty-third week fallen due on Tuesday. And this yard put a mark in four fathoms, asked three people to hold it, and gave it up, and cannot be told what a route on that coast is doing. That is what this month was. Nobody in this harbor has any cheaper food because of it and nobody in this harbor has any dearer food because of it either and the two of those are the same thing."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it. No figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise. Nobody was thanked for anything on that day and nobody had been thanked for anything on any of the ten days before it.

---

**A PAGE WAS READ OUT IN THAT ROOM OVER THAT MARKET AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON CARRYING THE CROSSING OF THE THIRTIETH AND THE NAMES OF THREE MEN ON IT AND THE REFUSAL OF ONE CHANNEL ON IT WITH NO NAME AGAINST THAT REFUSAL AND THE READER'S OWN NAME AND THE HOUR AT THE FOOT OF IT, AND ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM COULD NOT READ IT AND HEARD ALL OF IT. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT AND THE FOUR STANDING LINES UNDER THEM ARE THE FOUR STANDING LINES. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ON NO WALL IN THAT ROOM AND WAS NOT READ OUT IN IT. THERE ARE NINE HULLS LYING ABOUT TWO SEA MILES OFF THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AND ON THAT SATURDAY THERE WERE STILL NINE OF THEM. THE WAGE COLUMN IS TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND THE SIXTY-THIRD WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-SEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS, ONE SHILLING AND SIXPENCE, AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the first day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that it was a Saturday, and that a day asked for by name is paid on a Saturday at the figure in chalk and that this yard took a day in at ten in the morning and gave a day out at four in the afternoon; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-eight thousand four hundred and ninety-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being two hundred and ninety-nine marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Thursday, and that there has been nothing on that sheet in any line since the twenty-eighth of December and nothing has gone into it in any hand since the sixteenth of October; and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read what is put in front of them, and that a page carrying the crossing of the thirtieth and the names of three men on it and the refusal of one channel on it was read out once word for word by the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay with his own name and the hour under it, and that there is no name against that refusal and no reason against it, and that nobody stopped him at any word and nobody asked about it after the reading; and that the four heads on the wall of that room did not change and no fifth head went on it and the four standing lines under them are the four standing lines, and that no route and no account is represented on that wall by anybody who has not asked to be represented, and that the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and was not read out in it; and that the man of about thirty-two was not in that room and was not sent for and has not been asked to come; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and sixty-three, one thousand and one hundred and eighty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and sixty-two, one thousand and one hundred and eighty-seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a page with a refusal on it and no name against it was read out in that room and a sheet with money on it was not is the reader's own, and is that a sheet a yard keeps for itself is a sheet that answers to the yard and a page a room keeps is a page a room has to be able to hear, and that the yard's own sheet has not been read out in that room this winter and is not going to be, and that the difference between those two things is the whole of what this month was; and that the reason a name is nowhere on that page and is on none of the four lines under those four heads and was not put on one by anybody is his own, and is that a man who gives up a thing in a room in front of twenty people and eleven of whom cannot read has already been given the only standing he wanted, and that a name added to a wall after that is a name somebody put on a wall, and that the difference between a man who asks to be on a page and a man who is put on one is the whole of the difference and this harbor has not been asked the second question by anybody; and that the reason a hundred and ten yards of water in three men's heads was said out loud at the bench stone in that yard's own mouth and was not written on that sheet is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a thing three men said at half past four on a Thursday in a shed is theirs and not this yard's, and that this yard put thirty feet of three-inch on its own weight and a figure of twenty-five minutes and a compressor and a cutoff against it and is answerable for those four things and for nothing else, and that a man who writes three men's account on his own sheet does not get a bigger number, he gets a smaller page; and that the reason not a thing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, bettered, carried forward or shut on that Saturday, and the reason this yard did not ask a person in this harbor for one penny of the rest of it, is his own, and is that a commonwealth kept a condition and this yard kept its side of it and those two things together are worth nothing on any board in this town, and the lamp oil is at four hundred and fifty pence, there is no flour in that market, the mole head is dark, and a week of that winter is on the fourth and nothing this yard did this month has moved one penny of it, and the first column of that leaf has taken an entry on every single one of these ten days and the read-back of that leaf is not due until the last page of the fifty and not before.**
