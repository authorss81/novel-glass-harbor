# Chapter 540

*Tuesday the Eleventh Day of January, the Sixty-Fourth Wage Tuesday, the Wage Column in That Harbor Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest and Read Out on the Step at the Back of That Market at Ten o'Clock in the Morning in Front of About Forty People of Whom About Twenty Cannot Read the Column on the Lid and Not Moving, Nobody Paid a Penny Out of That Chest, a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Being the Sixty-Fourth, a Sheet of Paper Carrying a Date and a Place and Five Lines of Rule and a Seal at the Foot of It and a Name Under That Seal Nailed to the Board at the Head of That Mole at About a Quarter Past Eleven in the Morning Under a Light That Is Out, a Room over That Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, Isel Marn, of About Fifty-Six, Who Keeps a Chandler's Counter at the Head of That Quay, Reading It Out Word for Word Once and Stopping at the Foot of It Where the Seal and the Name Are and Not Reading the Name and Putting Her Own Name and the Hour Under the Reading, a Man of About Fifty Asking Out Loud for the Name at the Foot of It to Be Read So That He Could Put It on a Slate and Take It Up the Coast and Being Refused, the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed and No Fifth Head Added on Account of a Declaration, the Fitting-Out Sheet on No Wall in That Room and the Two Named Separately and Out Loud, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Sixty-Five Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Four Hundred and Eleven Marks, Seven Shillings and Sixpence, the Paper With a Seal on It and No Figure on It From the Third of December Still Nailed to That Board and Not Opened, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the eleventh day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and it was the sixty-fourth wage Tuesday. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the sixty-fourth. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out, being four hundred and seventy-eight marks and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and forty; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and seventy-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and forty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventeenth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and thirty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and forty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eighty days. The paper that was nailed to the board at the head of that mole on the third of December is still on that board, and it has not been taken off it, and it was not opened then and it has not been opened since, and nobody in that harbor has a key to it and nobody is going to be given one. The berth fund paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence into that shed at about ten in the morning and will pay one hundred and sixty-eight pence on the Wednesday and nobody has asked it to do anything else. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been thirty-eight days and he went out on the west quay at about six this morning with two men and a handline and came in at about ten with about forty fish in it, and nobody in this harbor has said a word to him about it and he did not say a word to anybody.**

---

**At about ten o'clock in the morning a clerk of that harbor authority read that column out on the step at the back of that market and put his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading, and about forty people were on the boards in front of that step and about twenty of them cannot read the column on the lid, and the column did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four and nobody asked to have it opened, and the sixty-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of that day.**

---

**And at about a quarter past eleven in the morning a boat's boat came up inside that mole with a man in it and a bag and a maul, and that man nailed a sheet of paper to the board at the head of that mole and he did not put the maul down while he did it and he did not say one word to anybody while he did it, and he was back in that boat's boat and out of the harbor inside a quarter of an hour, and the light that is meant to stand over that board is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May.**

There is a date at the head of it and a place at the head of it, and there are five lines of rule between those two things and a seal pressed into the paper at the foot of it and a name written under that seal. **There is no figure of any kind on the whole of it.**

The man who keeps the leaf read it standing up with his hands behind his back and did not take it off that board, and read it about four times, and came back down that mole to his own shed and put no line against it on any sheet in that yard and said two things about it out loud, and neither of the two things was about what it means.

"That is the second one in four weeks nailed to that board and I am not copying this one either. **A sheet this yard keeps is a sheet this yard is answerable for, and I am not answerable for one word of that, and that paper is answerable to any man in this harbor who walks up and reads it in the dark, which is the only reason it was nailed up in the dark.**"

"And can anybody say what is on it."

"Nobody has said so and I am not going to be the first. There is a date at the head of it and a place at the head of it and five lines of rule and a seal at the foot of it and a name under the seal, and **that name is the only thing on that paper that would let anybody put a hand on a person, and it is written and not spoken, and a name that is written is a fact and a name that is said out loud in a room is a rumour with a coat on.**"

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and Isel Marn, of about fifty-six, who keeps a chandler's counter at the head of that quay and is not on anything and is a head of nothing, read that paper out word for word, once, and stopped where the seal is.**

This is what she read, and she read it in the words it is written in.

1. *That from the hour set down at the head of this paper, every stretch of that sea set down at the head of this paper is under martial custody.*
2. *That no boat of any size shall go past a mark, into or out of that sea, without a permit signed at a coast office, and that a permit is not a thing a coast office has agreed to give anybody.*
3. *That victual, coal, timber, oil, glass and tin may be landed only at the places set down in the second column of the schedule, and that that schedule is not fastened to this paper and is held at a coast office.*
4. *That no person shall leave that coast while this paper is in force without a permit, and that a permit is not given twice to the same person in one month.*
5. *That this paper may be read aloud in any room on any coast, and that nobody shall be charged any figure for hearing it read.*

**She stopped there, with her finger about an inch above the seal, and she did not read the name under it, and she said out loud why, and nobody in that room asked her a second question about it.**

"There is a name under that seal and I am not going to read it. I have read papers in this room three times this year and the names at the bottom of them are never the part anybody can act on, and the minute a name is in a room it is in about forty mouths by the middle of the night and then it is not a thing that was written on a paper, it is a thing that somebody in this market said about somebody. **It is written and it stays written and I will read the day and the place and the five lines and the seal, and that is all of that paper there is.** My own name and the hour go under mine and I will read those out at the end, because those two are mine and I know what they are for."

Nobody stopped her at any word and she read it once and she did not read it twice, and the reader's own name and the hour went at the foot of the reading in her own hand.

---

**And a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons stood up in that room at about ten past two in the afternoon and asked her, out loud, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read, whether his boat may leave on Thursday, and she answered him in about thirty words out of the five lines and not out of her own head.**

"Line two says no boat of any size goes past a mark, into or out of, without a permit signed at a coast office. Line four says a permit is not given twice to the same person in one month. **I can tell you what that paper says. I cannot tell you what will happen on Thursday and neither can anybody standing in this room, and anybody who stands up here and tells you that you may or may not is not reading a paper, he is making one up in front of twenty people.**"

He said that was not much of an answer and she said that it was the only one that was on the paper, and he sat down, and he was not thanked and nobody was asked anything on his account.

---

**And a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood up at about a quarter to three and asked Isel Marn to read the name at the foot of that paper so that he could put it on a slate and take it up the coast to about eleven people in a room over a fish market four days down that coast, and she refused him in about twenty words, and he did not argue with her and he did not ask again and nobody in that room backed him up.**

"No. **You have not told me what the slate is for and I am not going to put a man's name in this room's mouth so that it can be carried up a coast road in somebody's coat.** If it is about a permit then line two and line four tell you what a permit is and where one is signed, and that is on the paper and you can copy that off with a pencil. If it is about who did it then it is not going out of this room this afternoon, and if it is about anything else then put it to me without a name on it and I will read that out for you in front of these twenty people."

He said that was fair and he sat down and did not put it to her again that afternoon.

---

**The four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it, and there is no head of anything on that wall, and about nine people in that room said one thing out loud, one at a time and in about six words each, that they were going to do about it, and not one of the nine said they would comply with it and not one of the nine said they would not.**

The man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said he was going to the west quay and tell two boats. A woman of about thirty-one who keeps a fish plate outside said she was going to put a bucket under that wall tomorrow morning. A man of about fifty-eight of the second lay said he was going to write nothing down, and he wrote nothing down. The man of about thirty-three who hires hands said he was going to go out on Thursday and find out, and he did not say what finding out looked like.

The man who keeps the leaf stood where the long table is and said out loud, in front of all of them, that the sheet that carries the money is not in that room and is not on any wall in it; that the four heads on that wall are the four heads on that wall; that the paper they have just heard read out is a paper and not a decision and there is nobody in that room who has decided anything today; and that there is not one man in that room who is only a name or a seal or an office, and that the woman who read it out has a chandler's counter at the head of that quay and will open it in the morning whatever that paper says.

Nobody in that room asked him for anything and nobody thanked anybody for anything, including the reader, who was not thanked for reading and put her own name at the foot of it.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone with the door open on the slip, and said that the day had not touched it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since ten o'clock on Saturday morning. **A sea was declared in that room this afternoon and it did not move a penny on this sheet, and the reason is that a declared sea is not a bill and this sheet is a bill, and there are only two things that move it and both of them happened before the third of this month.**"

"And this afternoon the sixty-fourth wage Tuesday went past in that harbor with a column at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, twenty envelopes in a chest, and a week standing fallen due at the close of today. **There is no lamp oil in that market at any figure because of that paper and no flour and no cheaper anything and the lamp oil board is still at four hundred and fifty pence with the first of June above it, and the nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole are nine this afternoon and were nine on Saturday.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Tuesday, and nobody was thanked for the reading and nobody was thanked for the reader.

---

**A SHEET OF PAPER CARRYING A DATE AND A PLACE AND FIVE LINES OF RULE AND A SEAL AT THE FOOT OF IT AND A NAME UNDER THAT SEAL WAS NAILED TO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST ELEVEN IN THE MORNING UNDER A LIGHT THAT IS OUT AND WAS NOT OPENED AND WAS NOT COPIED AND THE NAME UNDER THE SEAL WAS NOT READ OUT ALOUD IN ANY ROOM. A ROOM OVER THAT MARKET WAS LET OUT AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON WITH ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE UP THOSE STAIRS AND ABOUT ELEVEN OF THEM WHO CANNOT READ WHAT IS PUT IN FRONT OF THEM, AND ISEL MARN, OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX, READ THAT PAPER OUT WORD FOR WORD ONCE AND STOPPED AT THE SEAL AND PUT HER OWN NAME AND THE HOUR UNDER HER OWN READING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM DECIDED ANYTHING. THE WAGE COLUMN IN THAT HARBOR IS TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS WITH TWENTY ENVELOPES IN A CHEST AND THE SIXTY-FOURTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE. THE PAPER WITH A SEAL ON IT AND NO FIGURE ON IT FROM THE THIRD OF DECEMBER IS STILL NAILED TO THAT BOARD AND WAS NOT OPENED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ON NO WALL IN THAT ROOM AND IS SIXTY-FIVE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the eleventh day of January at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that it was the sixty-fourth wage Tuesday, and that the wage column in that harbor stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move and that nobody was paid a penny out of that chest and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of that day, being the sixty-fourth, and that the column was read out on the step at the back of that market at about ten o'clock in the morning by a clerk of that harbor authority with his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading in front of about forty people of whom about twenty cannot read the column on the lid, and that the chest stood shut behind that door from ten until four and nobody asked to have it opened; and that a boat's boat came up inside that mole at about a quarter past eleven in the morning and a man in it nailed a sheet of paper to the board at the head of that mole and did not put the maul down while he did it and said nothing to anybody while he did it, and that the light over that board is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May and about eleven people stood in front of that board in the dark between eleven and four and about eleven of them could not read what was on it; and that that paper carries a date and a place at the head of it and five lines of rule and a seal at the foot of it and a name under that seal and no figure of any kind, and that the man who keeps the leaf read it standing up about four times and did not take it off that board and put no line against it on any sheet in that yard and said out loud that a sheet this yard keeps is a sheet this yard is answerable for and that a name that is written is a fact and a name that is said out loud in a room is a rumour with a coat on; and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read what is put in front of them, and that Isel Marn, of about fifty-six, who keeps a chandler's counter at the head of that quay and is not on anything and is a head of nothing, read that paper out word for word once and stopped at the seal and did not read the name under it and said out loud why, and put her own name and the hour at the foot of the reading in her own hand, and that nobody stopped her at any word; and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons asked out loud whether his boat may leave on Thursday and was answered out of the second and the fourth of the five lines and not out of anybody's own head, and was told that anybody who says he may or may not is making a paper up in front of twenty people, and that he sat down and was not thanked and nobody was asked anything on his account; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked that reader to read the name at the foot of the paper so that he could put it on a slate and take it up the coast to about eleven people in a room four days down that coast, and was refused in about twenty words because he had not said what the slate was for, and did not argue and did not ask again and nobody in that room backed him up, and was told that if it is about a permit the two lines on permits can be copied off with a pencil and if it is about anything else he can put it to her without a name on it; and that about nine people in that room said one thing out loud, one at a time and in about six words each, that they were going to do about it, and that not one of the nine said he would comply with it and not one of the nine said he would not; and that the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and the two were named separately and out loud in that room; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at seventy-six thousand four hundred and ninety pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pence short, being four hundred and eleven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the Sunday; and that the paper nailed to that board on the third of December is still on that board and was not opened then and has not been opened since and there is no key to it in that harbor; and that a declared sea is not a bill and this sheet is a bill; and that the first column of that leaf has taken one entry against each of the ten days that have gone since the first of January and not one of those ten days has gone without an entry against it, and that the second column has taken five against each of them, three before the hour and two after, and that the third took none on any of the ten and nobody has been put in it and the boy's hand is at the head of it and it is still there, and that the fourth took none on any of the ten and stands at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither shut nor struck, and that the read-back of that leaf is not due until the last page of the fifty and not before.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that name was not read out in that room is the reader's own and is hers as well and they gave the same reason in different words, and is that a name written under a seal is the only part of a document that can be used against a person and a name said in a room cannot be taken back out of it, and that this room four hundred yards from that bench stone has about eleven people in it who cannot read what is put in front of them and the only protection those eleven have is that whoever is standing at the front is choosing what to say out loud; and that the reason the man of about fifty was not told what would happen on Thursday is his own, and is that nobody in that harbor knows and that a man who is told a thing he can act on will act on it and a man who is told the truth has to sit with it, and this yard is not in the business of making a market comfortable; and that the reason a slate was refused is hers, and is that four crews on this coast put no name down in December and have not been asked about it since, and that the day somebody in this room carries a written name up a coast road in a coat is the day this room starts being a thing with an office behind it, and that a room with an office behind it stops being a place where about eleven people who cannot read find out what was decided about them; and that the reason not a single thing owing to any person in that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Tuesday is his own, and is that a paper with five lines and a seal changed nothing that this harbor can measure and that the two things it did change are both worse, being that a man of about fifty cannot get an answer about Thursday and that nine people went down those stairs each saying one small sentence about what they were going to do, and that neither of those is on any page and that is the correct arrangement; and that the reason the wage Tuesday and the declaration were both put under one figure at the end of that Tuesday is his own, and is that a column and a chest and a week that has fallen due is a thing a harbor finds out about itself once a week and does not pay itself, and that a paper saying a sea is under martial custody is a thing a harbor finds out about somebody else once and does not pay either, and that this sheet has moved four times in eleven days and every one of those four times was a thing that somebody had actually done with a boat or a coal or a roof or a room.
