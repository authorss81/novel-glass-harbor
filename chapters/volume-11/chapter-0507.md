# Chapter 507

*Thursday the Ninth Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Sixth Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, a Close-Sea Officer of About Forty-Four Asking in a Room in the Harbor Authority's Office on the West Basin at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon a Man of About Fifty Who Owns a Boat of About Twenty-Two Tons for a Date, and His Not Giving One and Saying Out Loud Once in About Four Sentences and in His Own Mouth Why, and the Reason Being Neither a Threat Nor a Speech, Nobody Thanking Him and No Figure Being Put Against Him, the Whole of That Room Written Into the Second Column at About Half Past Four in the Afternoon With the Hour Against It and Nothing Asked of Him About Any Part of It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Out, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Twenty-Eight Thousand and Sixty-Six Pence, Being One Hundred and Seventy-Five Marks, Five Shillings and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Thursday the ninth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room with a stove in it in the harbor authority's office on the west basin.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and sixth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on the eighteenth of November and is not said out loud a second time. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that is the second day running that sheet has not moved and it moved by nine hundred and seventy-six pence on the Wednesday. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-eight thousand seven hundred and six pence out, being two hundred and forty-one marks, twelve shillings and two pence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-eight thousand and sixty-six pence short, being one hundred and seventy-five marks, five shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and forty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eleventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its eighty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninety-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixteenth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty-seven days. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is still on a board at an end stall with the figure of the first of June visible above it and both dates on that board, and that figure did not come down on that Thursday and cannot be taken off again. The harbor authority's office on the west basin keeps a register copy of everything it is given, and nobody in that harbor has ever been shown that register. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and nobody in that harbor has said anything to him about it and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning for the hundred and sixth time and was walked on by none of it, and the man who keeps the leaf was in that shed and did not go out to it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it.**

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon Iven Sallow, of about forty-four, a close-sea officer, asked a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons for a date, in a room in that office, and the room had a table in it and a stove in it and a window that looks over the west basin, and there was nobody else in the room but the two of them and a clerk who came in at a quarter past and went out again.**

He had not taken his coat off. He had a docket book open on the table and he wrote in it before he asked, which the man of about fifty noticed and mentioned afterwards.

"I have four boats in that line and I am required to have a date from every owner of a boat of over fifteen tons inside this harbor," he said. "I am asking you for yours. I am not asking you to agree to anything and there is nothing in this room that can make you agree to anything."

The man of about fifty looked at the docket book and then at the window for a while.

"Four sentences," he said, "and then I am going back down to my slip, and I am not saying them to be clever and I am not saying them at anybody, because you are the man who has to write it down and you are the man it is for."

"Go on."

"**I have got a boat of about twenty-two tons and there is a line of nine boats outside my mole and I am not going to give you a day.** If I give you a day, that is a day I have given away, and a day I have given away is a day somebody can come back and ask me for and say that I promised. **I have not said no to you and I am not going to say no to you, because a no is a thing that goes in a book and a thing in a book is a thing that can be used twice.** You have a list and I can see from here that it is a list, and I can see there are four names in the margin of it, and I know what a margin is for. **If you want a day out of me then you are going to have to come out to that slip and stand on it with me, and not send anybody.**"

He did not raise his voice at any of it. It took him about forty seconds and it was four sentences and it was not a threat and it was not a speech, and he had said it once in his own mouth, and he did not say a fifth thing.

---

**The officer did not argue with him and he did not put it in the docket book in front of him and he did not thank him and no figure of any kind was put against him anywhere in that harbor, on any paper, in any column, in any book.**

"That is fair," the officer said. "That is a fair answer and I am going to write it down as you said it, which is that you have declined to give a date and have given four reasons and one of them is a condition and the condition is that the asking be done on the slip. I am not going to write down the word *refused* because you did not use it and because it is not the word. I am going to write down *declined to give a date* and the hour, and the four reasons will go in the book because the book is where reasons go."

He wrote for about a minute and a half and turned the book round and pushed it nine inches across the table so the man of about fifty could see his own words, and the man of about fifty read them and did not ask for a word of it to be changed, which is a thing he has never done in this harbor about any paper.

"That will be held," the officer said. "It is a register copy and nobody in this harbor will ever be shown it, and in about a month there will be a man in an office a long way from here with a copy of that page and your slip will be on a list with about eleven other slips, and I cannot stop that and I have no power to stop that and I am not going to pretend to you this afternoon that I have."

**Nobody thanked him for it. He put his coat on, which he had not taken off, and went down the steps of that office, and he has not been thanked for it and nobody in that harbor has put a figure against him and nobody has asked him a second question about it.**

---

**And at about half past four in the afternoon, at the head of that third lay, the man of about fifty told the whole of it to the man who keeps the leaf in that yard's own mouth, standing at the bench stone, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the whole of it into the second column of that leaf with the hour of half past four in the afternoon of the ninth of December against it, and asked him nothing about any part of it.**

"Anything you want to put against it."

"No," said the man of about fifty. "You have got it. I am not going to stand in that shed and say he was right or that he was wrong, and I am not going to tell you what I am going to do on Monday, and you are not going to ask me."

"I am not going to ask you."

**And the man who keeps the leaf wrote the whole of the room into the second column of that leaf, the four sentences as they came out of him, the condition, the hour the officer put the docket book round the table, the hour the man read them, and the two things the officer said he could not stop, and he wrote no name against any part of it except the man's own name against the four sentences, and it is in the second column and not in the first, and it is not checkable from a mole, and it is the only thing this yard has got about that room.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, and it had not moved for a day and a night, and he said so and nobody said anything about it.**

"Twenty-eight thousand and sixty-six pence short, being one hundred and seventy-five marks, five shillings and sixpence. Unmoved since yesterday afternoon and nothing has gone out of that sheet in any hand in any line today and nothing has gone into it, and I have got a room full of nothing in the second column and a shed full of nothing on the slip, and neither of those is a figure and neither of them is going on that sheet."

---

**A CLERK CAME INTO A ROOM IN THAT OFFICE AT A QUARTER PAST TWO AND WENT OUT AGAIN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY SAID FOUR SENTENCES IN ABOUT FORTY SECONDS AND SAID NO FIFTH THING, AND THE FOUR SENTENCES WENT INTO A BOOK AND THE BOOK WAS TURNED ROUND AND PUSHED NINE INCHES ACROSS A TABLE AND READ AND NOT ASKED TO BE CHANGED. NOBODY THANKED THE OFFICER AND NO FIGURE WAS PUT AGAINST HIM AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM A SECOND QUESTION ABOUT IT. THE WHOLE OF THAT ROOM WENT INTO THE SECOND COLUMN OF THAT LEAF AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON WITH THE HOUR AGAINST IT AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF ASKED NOBODY ANYTHING. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-EIGHT THOUSAND AND SIXTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THE HARBOR AUTHORITY KEEPS A REGISTER COPY OF WHAT IT IS GIVEN AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS EVER BEEN SHOWN IT.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the ninth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and sixth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-eight thousand seven hundred and six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-eight thousand and sixty-six pence short, being one hundred and seventy-five marks, five shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the day before; and that a close-sea officer of about forty-four asked a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons for a date in a room in the harbor authority's office on the west basin at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and that the man of about fifty did not give one and said four sentences in about forty seconds in his own mouth and said no fifth thing, and that the reason was neither a threat nor a speech and one of the four sentences was a condition, being that the asking be done on the slip; and that the officer wrote the four sentences and the hour into a docket book and turned the book round and pushed it nine inches across the table, and that the man of about fifty read them and did not ask for a word of it to be changed, and that nobody thanked the officer and no figure was put against him anywhere in that harbor and nobody has asked him a second question about it; and that the whole of that room was written into the second column of that leaf at about half past four in the afternoon with the hour against it and the man who keeps the leaf asked the man of about fifty nothing about any part of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and the four sentences of the ninth of December are entered in here and they are entered whole: that the reason a room with two people in it was written down in this leaf at all is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a refusal made in a room that keeps a register of it is going to be read out of that register in about a month by a man a long way off, and that this yard is the only place in this harbor where what was said in that room is written down by somebody who is not the office, and that if it is not written down here then it is only in the office; and that the reason nobody was asked a question about it afterwards is the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who has said four sentences in a room and has come down a slip afterwards and has told them to a bookkeeper is a man who has just spent the whole of what he came to spend, and that asking him a second question would only be asking him whether he had meant it, and that a man who has put a condition in the middle of a refusal has already told this yard everything there is to know; and that the reason the officer was not thanked is that harbor's own, and not this yard's, and it is that nobody in this harbor thanks a man for writing down a thing that was said to his face, because being written down is what is done to people in harbors and not what is done for them; and that the reason no figure has been set against the man of about fifty on that sheet is the yard's own mouth, and is that a figure against a man's boat is a figure against the man's trade, and that a man who has declined to give a date has not changed his trade and has not changed his boat and is on that slip tomorrow with the same men on her, and that this sheet carries what a person has put into it and not what a person is worth.*
