# Chapter 515

*Friday the Seventeenth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Line, Glass, Tin and Gaskets for a Pressure Case Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Half Past Ten in the Morning at Nine Hundred and Sixty Pence, Being Six Marks, With the Seventeenth of December Against It, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying the Whole of What That Line Is Before He Wrote a Figure Against It and Nobody Arguing With Him About Any Part of It, a Pump Hand of About Forty-Four With Nineteen Years of Compressors and of Nobody's Crew and of No Thing at All Asking Out Loud in Front of Nine People in a Shed What Happens When the Plan Fails, and Being Given the Whole of the Answer by Five People in Turn and in Working Words, and Not Being Thanked and Having No Figure Set Against Him and Being Sent For on No Day After That One, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Thirty-Five Thousand and Ten Pence, Being Two Hundred and Eighteen Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Friday the seventeenth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Line, glass, tin and gaskets for a pressure case went onto the sheet that carries the money at about half past ten in the morning at nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks, with the seventeenth of December against it, being the day the work was done and the day it is written, which are the same day this once. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-five thousand six hundred and fifty pence out, being two hundred and eighty-five marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-five thousand and ten pence short, being two hundred and eighteen marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and thirteen days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifteen; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and nineteenth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and thirty-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-five days. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that market since the twenty-ninth day of August and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is on the board at that end stall with February's figure visible above it and both dates on the board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down and cannot be taken off. There has been no barley in that market since the middle of September. A man of about thirty-eight of that party is keeping what he is keeping and has not been asked about it and has not said what it is, and nobody in that harbor has set a figure against him for it and nobody has. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and has said nothing about it this week.**

---

**At about half past ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and nobody argued with him about any part of it, and about nine people were in that shed and heard all of it.**

"Line, glass, tin and gaskets for a pressure case. Nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks, with the seventeenth of December against it, being the day the work is done and the day it goes into the total at the foot of this sheet, and those two are the same day this once. That is thirty feet of two-inch stuff, a pane of glass about the size of two hands laid flat, a ring of tin, and gaskets enough for four seats. It is not a new case. It is the case we have been in four times this month with the pane reseated and the head ring packed, and the pane in it now is scratched in about the middle where a man's glove has gone over it every time, and a scratched pane in four fathoms at the top of a flood is a thing that gets harder to see through and not easier."

"And what is not on that line."

"What is not on that line is a new case and what is not on that line is anybody's hours. I have not put a figure of mine on any part of it and I am not going to put my hours on it this month when I cannot pay myself out of it. It is timber and glass and tin and rubber and a boy's two days carrying it down the slip, and that is the whole of it and you can take all three of those figures off this sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

---

**And at about eleven in the morning, with the door propped and about nine people in that shed, a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors asked a question out loud, in front of all of them, and he asked it once and it was not argued with and it was not talked over and about nine people gave him the whole of the answer between them.**

He was in nobody's crew. He was a member of no thing in that harbor. He is not on the sheet that carries the money on any line, and no figure has been set against him on any page in that harbor, and he was not paid on that day and he was not paid on any other day of that month, and nobody in that yard has sent for him since.

"I have run compressors for nineteen years," he said. "I have not been asked for anything this week and I am not asking for anything. And I am not going to be able to tell anybody in this shed one single thing about air that they do not already know, and that is the truth and it is not a complaint. So I am asking about the other end of it. **What happens when the plan fails.**"

Nobody said anything for about two seconds and then five people in that shed answered him, one after another, and they did not take turns because they had agreed to take turns and nobody agreed to anything.

---

**The woman of about twenty-seven of that yard answered first and what she said was about glass and it was not a speech.**

"I have been asking that question every working day since I was seventeen and the answer is always the same one and it is never a good one. It does not fail at the end. It fails at the middle, at about the fourth minute, when the man in it is doing the thing he came for and is not yet finished, and the case is doing the thing it came for and is not yet finished either. A pane of glass in a ring of tin with a gasket in it holds water at four fathoms all day. What it does not do is stop being touched. Every man who has been in this case has put a glove across that pane and every glove has put something on it, and one day there is going to be a mark on that pane that spreads from the edge, and on the day it spreads the case is not a thing you can sit in a boat about and wait for. It will go. And the glass is not the worst of it and the glass is what I can buy."

---

**A man of about thirty-two answered second and what he said was about the water and it was not long.**

"The plan is on the making of a tide and the tender on the surface has the hour and not me. There is a cutoff on a weight and it comes out of that water without anybody asking it to and I do not get a vote in it. There is a slate with a figure of minutes on it and my reason in my own words on it and the hour, and the slate is written before I am off the boat and it is on the boat when I go over the rail, and it does not go in the water with me. If the plan fails because I am slow I will be told so by a man on the surface and I will come up. If the plan fails because the glass has gone then the water is the thing that kills a man and not the plan, and the only thing this plan can do about that is to have a man on the surface who is not me and a line that is not my line and a bell on a buoy that anybody can hear."

---

**The man who keeps the leaf answered third and what he said was about the sheet and it was the shortest thing said in that shed that day.**

"It fails on paper first and it fails on paper worse than anywhere else. The line does not go up. There is no line in this world for the cost of a thing that went wrong. The four men paid a day apiece are paid a day apiece on the fourth of this week with the water as flat as it is now and they will be paid on the eleventh with the water however it is. The air goes on the sheet before it goes in the water and not after. And there is nothing standing in this harbor that this yard would take money out of to make a rescue come out cheaper, and if there were, I would be the one who had to say so out loud, and I have said it out loud this month once and I am not going to have to say it again."

---

**Ivo Sark, of about twenty-four, one of the master's two men, and the named tender of the eighth of December, answered fourth, and he said the only thing in that shed that was about himself and he said it in nine words.**

"I pulled it once. You do not have to want to."

---

**And then the pump hand of about forty-four said the thing that was the actual question underneath the one he had asked out loud, and it was about eleven words long and it was not written for him by anybody.**

"Then somebody comes up, every time, and it is not me and not him, and it is somebody neither of you has to be brave about."

"Every time," the man of about thirty-two said. "In this harbor, with the boats we have got, in four fathoms, in weather we would call bad in a bigger place. And I have not got an answer for what happens past that and I am not going to give you one, because there is not one and anybody who gives you one has not been in the water."

Nobody thanked him. Nobody thanked anybody else who spoke. A man of about thirty-eight, who is one of the four men paid a day apiece, was in that shed and did not say anything at any hour of that morning, and nobody in that shed asked him anything at any hour of that morning, and he is not on that sheet on any line and no figure is set against him anywhere in that harbor.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud, and he said what the day had done to it, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Thirty-five thousand and ten pence short, being two hundred and eighteen marks, ten shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by nine hundred and sixty pence and that is a pane of glass and thirty feet of stuff and a ring of tin and some rubber, and that is the whole of the crossing as far as this sheet is concerned, and the crossing is not on this sheet and has never been on it and is not going to be."

A man at the end of that group said that was the first time anybody in that yard had said out loud that the crossing would not be on the sheet, and the man who keeps the leaf said it would not be on the sheet because a crossing is not a thing that is bought, and that a page that carried what a crossing cost would be a page that other people could be handed.

---

**A PUMP HAND OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAT IN A SHED AT THE HEAD OF THAT THIRD LAY AND ASKED ONE QUESTION OUT LOUD AND WAS ANSWED BY FIVE PEOPLE IN TURN AND WAS NOT THANKED AND IS NOT ON ANY LINE OF ANY SHEET IN THAT HARBOR AND HAS NOT BEEN SENT FOR SINCE. A PANE OF GLASS ABOUT THE SIZE OF TWO HANDS LAID FLAT IS GOING INTO A CASE THAT HAS BEEN IN FOUR FATHOMS FOUR TIMES THIS MONTH AND IS SCRATCHED IN THE MIDDLE WHERE A GLOVE GOES OVER IT EVERY TIME. THERE IS NO LINE ON THAT SHEET FOR THE COST OF A THING THAT WENT WRONG. FOUR MEN ARE PAID A DAY APIECE ON THE EIGHTEENTH AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH WITH THE WATER HOWEVER IT IS. THERE HAS BEEN NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT MARKET SINCE THE TWENTY-NINTH DAY OF AUGUST AND THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON THE BOARD. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-FIVE THOUSAND AND TEN PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK.**

---

*First column, entered on Friday the seventeenth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that line, glass, tin and gaskets for a pressure case went onto the sheet that carries the money at about half past ten in the morning at nine hundred and sixty pence, being six marks, with the seventeenth of December against it, being the day the work was done and the day it is written, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it and put no figure of his own hours on any part of it; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-five thousand six hundred and fifty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-five thousand and ten pence short, being two hundred and eighteen marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved by nine hundred and sixty pence on that day; and that a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors, who is in nobody's crew and a member of no thing and on no line of that sheet, asked out loud at about eleven in the morning in front of about nine people in that shed what happens when the plan fails, and was answered by five people in that shed in turn and in the words of their own trades, being glass, water, a sheet, a cutoff and a man on the surface, and that nobody argued with him and nobody talked over him and nobody thanked him and no figure was set against him and he was not paid on that day and has not been sent for since; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and forty-eight, one thousand and one hundred and fourteen, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and forty-seven, one thousand and one hundred and twelve.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that question was answered by five people in turn and not by one is this yard's own mouth, and is that a question like that has five ends to it and a man who answers one of them has answered a fifth of it and knows it, and that this yard does not use one mouth for a thing with five ends because one mouth would have been kinder to the man who asked and less true; and that the reason the man who asked it was not thanked and no figure was set against him is this yard's own mouth, and is that a man of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors is in this harbor and is not on this slip and is not in this crew and has not put a penny in, and that the day this yard put a figure against a man for having asked it a question in a shed would be the day a man in that harbor stopped asking questions in sheds, and that he is on no line of this sheet and that is the whole of what this sheet says about him; and that the reason there is no line on that sheet for the cost of a thing that went wrong is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a page that can be made to carry a loss becomes a page that can be made to carry a win, and that the only honest total is the one that counts what went out; and that the reason the man of about thirty-eight said nothing in that shed that morning and was not asked is this yard's own, and is that a man keeping a thing of his own is not a man who has been given nothing, and that the afternoon a yard starts asking one is the afternoon it stops being a place where a man can sit in it; and that the reason this yard did not touch, better, reduce or shut one old figure in that harbor on that Friday is this yard's own mouth, and is that nineteen winters are disputed in a list being made up in a city and no figure of that list is in this harbor to be moved by a good morning with a glass in it, and that this yard left every old figure in that harbor where it was on that day and would have been wrong to move one.*
