# Chapter 518

*Monday the Twentieth Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Ninth Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, a Day Apiece to the Same Four Men Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Half Past Ten in the Morning at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, With the Twentieth of December Against It and the Running Out at the Foot of That Sheet Moving by the Same Figure, a Working Person of a Fisher Commonwealth at That Port of About Thirty-Two Asked Out Loud in a Room to Sign a Sheet and Saying No in About Four Sentences in His Own Mouth, and Not Being Argued With and Not Being Asked a Second Time on Any Day After That One, Nobody Reading That No as a Position on Anything, the Three Who Said No on the Sheet of the First Ten Days of That Month Standing on It at the Same Size as the Contributions and Not Being Asked Again and Not Being Counted With the Four Crews That Did Not Write a Name, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at Thirty-Six Thousand and Eighteen Pence, Being Two Hundred and Twenty-Five Marks, One Shilling and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Nobody Thanked*

Monday the twentieth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed at the head of that third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and ninth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. A day apiece to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about half past ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the twentieth of December against it, being the day those four men are asked for and the day they work, and the running out at the foot of that sheet moved by that same six hundred and seventy-two pence on that day. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at forty-six thousand six hundred and fifty-eight pence out, being two hundred and ninety-one marks, eight shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirty-six thousand and eighteen pence short, being two hundred and twenty-five marks, one shilling and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixteen days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighteen; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fifty-six days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-second night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-fifth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and tenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and twenty-seventh; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifty-eight days. There is a bar of sand across the middle of the mouth of that harbor that carries about four foot at the top of the flood and about nine foot at the half ebb, and it has moved half a chain seaward since the spring and the man who keeps the leaf does not know it and the tide gang does not know it either. A man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons is on that quay with his boat's boat over the side and he has not been asked for a date by anybody with an order since the ninth of December, and he has not been thanked and nobody is going to. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twelve days and he has said nothing about it and nobody in that harbor has asked him.**

---

**The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by two men who did not stop walking, and the water went into it out of a hose that is dead until the spring and had to be carried from the butt behind that market in eleven trips, and the man who keeps the leaf was in that shed at eight with the door shut and did not go and watch it.**

"The hundred and ninth," he said, to the slate. "Same words as the hundred and eighth. The name at the top of that board is still off it and it will be off it on the hundred and tenth, and I would put a figure on my own two hands before I put a name back on that board."

---

**At about half past ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said it out loud, and the total at the foot of that sheet moved on the same morning, and he said both of those things and nobody argued with him about either.**

"A day apiece to the same four men. Four men at one hundred and sixty-eight pence apiece, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the twentieth of December against it, being the day they are asked for and the day they work. That line is in the total at the foot of this sheet this morning because the day on the line and the day it is written are the same day today, and I am going to enjoy that about as much as anybody else and I will not be writing any more of it in that vein."

The four men took their day at the third lay at about half past three and were not thanked for it and their names are in the book the days are paid out of and are not on that sheet and are not counted on it.

---

**And at about half past one in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and two men with a store on the west quay had a sheet going round that harbor since the Saturday before.**

The sheet has a head on it and two columns under the head. In the first column is a name. In the second is a figure, and the figure is the figure of the winter's victual at the figure the market is charging that week, being fourteen marks for nine men for ten days, and under the two columns at the foot of the sheet is a line that says that a man who puts his name in that column is not to go past the line of boats standing off that mole head to fetch anything.

The two men with that store are not in any office and are not answerable to anybody in that town and they have not asked for anybody's money and they have not offered anybody a price lower than the one on that board at the end stall.

**A working person of a fisher commonwealth at that port of about thirty-two was asked, out loud, in front of about twenty people, to sign that sheet, and he said no, in about four sentences, in his own mouth, and nobody argued with him and nobody asked him a second question and nobody asked him why.**

He had a boat of about fourteen tons inside that mole since before the third of December and he has not been out of it since the ninth and he is not going to be out of it this winter, and he holds a share in the boats of that commons and not in the shares themselves, and the eleven men of that commons are eleven miles up that coast and they are not in that room and they did not know that he was in it.

"I will not sign that."

"Then it is a no to the winter's victual, and the figure is on the board in the market and anybody can come and take it."

"It is a no to a piece of paper and I will tell you what is in it. **There are eleven of us on that sheet at that port and I hold a share in the boats and not in the shares, and if I put my name to a figure on that page I have put a figure against eleven men who are not in this room and did not know I was in it.**"

"And if the figure goes up, they all carry it."

"They all carry it, and I am the one who put his name to it, and I am not going to carry that for them in the middle of the winter with eleven miles of coast and a line of boats in it. You can write down that I would not sign it and you can put the hour against it, and I am going back down to the slip."

Somebody at the back of that room said that it was a no to the line, and a man of about thirty-three said it was a man saying he would not sign a piece of paper, and nobody in that room argued about it further and the sheet went back into the pocket of the man with the store and it was not carried round that harbor again.

**Nobody asked him a second time on that day, and nobody has asked him a second time on any day since, and nobody in that harbor is going to, and the no has not been read as a position on anything by anybody.**

The man who keeps the leaf did not write it on the sheet that carries the money. It is not on that sheet and there is no line of that kind on it and there is not going to be one, and the reason he gave for that, out loud, in that shed, at about four o'clock, was that a sheet of that kind carries what a person did with their own money and not what a person would not put their name to.

**And the three who said no on that sheet in the first ten days of that month stand on it at the same size as the contributions and at the same size as each other, and none of the three has been asked again since the day they said it, and they are not counted with the four crews that did not write a name in that room on the tenth, and they are not on any list with anybody, and there is no list of the people who put nothing in in that market, in that yard or in that town.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone, and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it.**

"Thirty-six thousand and eighteen pence short, being two hundred and twenty-five marks, one shilling and sixpence. It moved this morning by six hundred and seventy-two pence and that is four men for a day and it is the only kind of line this yard puts on that sheet that anybody in that market would understand at a glance, and it does not make the other figure any smaller."

A man at the end of that group asked whether the man who would not sign ought to be put on the sheet. The man who keeps the leaf said no, and said it in about eleven words, and did not say it again that month.

"**No. That sheet is what people did with their own money and it is not going to be a place where this harbor keeps the names of the men it did not get anything from.**"

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND NINTH TIME AND IS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. FOUR MEN ARE ON THAT THIRD LAY THIS AFTERNOON AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK HAS NOT MOVED AND THEIR NAMES ARE IN A BOOK AND NOT ON A SHEET. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WHO HOLDS A SHARE IN THE BOATS OF A COMMONWEALTH ELEVEN MILES UP THAT COAST SAID NO IN FOUR SENTENCES IN A ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT SHED AND WENT BACK DOWN TO THE SLIP AND HE HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AGAIN AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO ASK HIM. THERE IS NO LIST IN THAT TOWN OF ANYBODY WHO PUT NOTHING IN. A BAR OF SAND ACROSS THE MOUTH OF THAT HARBOR HAS MOVED HALF A CHAIN SEAWARD SINCE THE SPRING AND NOBODY KNOWS IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THIRTY-SIX THOUSAND AND EIGHTEEN PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twentieth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and ninth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that a day apiece to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about half past ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, with the twentieth of December against it, being the day they are asked for and the day they work, and that the running out at the foot of that sheet moved by that same figure on that day; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at forty-six thousand six hundred and fifty-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is thirty-six thousand and eighteen pence short, being two hundred and twenty-five marks, one shilling and sixpence; and that a sheet with a name in one column and a figure in the other went round that harbor from the Saturday, offered by two men with a store on the west quay at the figure the market was charging that week, and that a working person of a fisher commonwealth at that port of about thirty-two was asked out loud in the room over that market at about half past one in the afternoon, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read the sheet, to sign it, and said no in about four sentences in his own mouth, the third of them being that there are eleven men on that sheet at that port and that he holds a share in the boats and not in the shares; and that nobody argued with him and nobody asked him a second question and nobody asked him why, and that the no was not read as a position on anything by anybody in that room or since, and that the sheet went back into the pocket of the man with the store and was not carried round that harbor again; and that that refusal is not on the sheet that carries the money and no line of that kind was opened on it; and that the three who said no on that sheet in the first ten days of that month stand on it at the same size as the contributions and none of the three has been asked again and they are not counted with the four crews that did not write a name, and that there is no list in that market, in that yard or in that town of anybody who put nothing in; and that the third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-one, one thousand and one hundred and twenty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty, one thousand and one hundred and twenty-seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a refusal in that room was not written onto that sheet is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a page which carries what a person put in and what a person would not put a name to becomes a page with two kinds of thing on it, and that a stranger with a pencil cannot take a no off it and get anything he can use, and that the only honest total on a page of that kind is the total of what went out; and that the reason nobody argued with the man of about thirty-two is this yard's own mouth, and is that he was asked one question and he gave four sentences and every one of the four was about eleven men eleven miles away, and that a man who is made to defend his own refusal in a room ends up defending it instead of having given it, and that what a harbor needs from a no is that it was said once and that it stays said; and that the reason the three who said no in the first ten days were not counted with the four crews that did not write a name is his own, and is that one of those said no to a page of money and four of those said nothing to a page of names, and that a harbor which puts them in one number has built itself a count of the people who did not come, and that this yard is not going to build one; and that the reason a day apiece to the same four men went on that sheet on the day they were asked for and the total moved the same morning is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a rule is worth more when it is inconvenient than when it is convenient, and that this one costs him the pleasure of a tidy column once a week; and that the reason no account standing in that harbor was settled on that Monday, in either direction, and none was shut, is this yard's own mouth, and is that a hundred and ninth cistern charge and a no in a room and a day of four men are three things that happen to a harbor and none of them is a settlement, and that this yard put every standing figure in that harbor back where it was that Monday and moved none of them for a page that went back into a pocket.*
