# Chapter 522

*Friday the Twenty-Fourth Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Working Person of About Thirty-Four of a Fisher Commonwealth at a Port Four Days Down That Coast Coming Up That Coast Road in a Cart With a Chest of Tools and a Bag of Rope and Not a Name on Anything, a Room over a Fish Market Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People Up Those Stairs and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, One Condition Stated Out Loud in That Room in About Four Sentences and Not Said Twice, Coal for the Pump for Ten Days Going Onto the Sheet at Two Thousand Four Hundred Pence, Being Fifteen Marks, With the Twenty-Fourth of December Against It, the Whole of That Line Said Out Loud Before a Figure Was Written Against It, the Four Heads on That Wall Not Changed and No Fifth Head Going On It on Account of a Commonwealth or a Condition or a Figure, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Forty-Two Thousand Three Hundred and Seventy Pence, Being Two Hundred and Sixty-Four Marks, Ten Shillings and Tenpence, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the twenty-fourth day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Coal for the pump for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at two thousand four hundred pence, being fifteen marks, with the twenty-fourth of December against it, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at fifty-three thousand and ten pence out, being three hundred and thirty-one marks, four shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is forty-two thousand three hundred and seventy pence short, being two hundred and sixty-four marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and twenty days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and twenty-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and sixty days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and twenty-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and forty-four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its ninety-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fourteenth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and thirty-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and sixty-two days. The four heads on the wall of that room and the four standing lines under them and the eleven names at the foot of the sheet are the ones that were on that wall before any of this began, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and has not been on one. There are nine hulls lying about two sea miles off the head of that mole and on that Friday there were still nine of them and the count has not been taken twice by anybody in that harbor. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been twenty days and nothing anybody has done has made it into a thing he is better at.**

---

**At about half past nine in the morning a cart came up that coast road into that harbor with a man of about thirty-four on the driving seat and a chest of lashed tools and a bag of rope under the seat behind him, and he was not stopped at that mole and nobody in that harbor knew who he was until he said his business.**

He works and he crews. He is not a name on anything, he is not a head of anything, and he did not come up that coast road with a paper. He had slept the Thursday night in a barn at the second lay because he had the use of one, and he had come in on a cart because a cart was cheaper than a passage and the road was passable.

He was at the head of that third lay at about eleven o'clock, and the man who keeps the leaf was at the bench stone, and the two of them looked at each other for about four seconds without either of them saying anything, and then the man of about thirty-four said that he was not going to say it on a slip and that he was going to wait for the room.

---

**At about eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before he wrote a figure against it, and about nine people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Coal for that pump for ten days. Two thousand four hundred pence, being fifteen marks. Off a boat alongside the quay and not off a deck somebody said something on, and with the twenty-fourth of December against it, being the day the work was done, and it will be in the total at the foot of that sheet at about half past four this afternoon because I am settling it this afternoon. **A line goes onto that sheet with the day the work was done against it and it is not in the total at the foot of that sheet until the day this yard settles it, and where those two days are not the same day both of them are on the face of the line.** On one line in ten days those two days are not the same day and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take both of them off the face of it."

He put the pen down flat and did not say one word more about it, and the coal went up from the slip in a barrow and a man of about thirty-four watched it go up and said nothing.

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that fish market was let out, and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and the four heads on that wall and the four standing lines under them and the eleven names at the foot of the sheet were on that wall and had not changed and no fifth head went on it.**

A man of about thirty-four stood up in about the middle of that room with his hands behind him and said that he had come up that coast road in a cart to say one thing and that he was going to say it once, and that he was not a name on a wall or a list or a sheet of anybody's, and that he worked and crewed and that was all he was.

He said it in about four sentences and he did not say it twice, and about eleven people in that room could not read a page and heard every word of it.

"The crews of that port will take victual and coal off that coast the way they have always taken it, and they will put a mark on that coast and name it and keep it and answer for it. **And a person from outside our commonwealth does not hold a buoy and does not go out to it.** That is the one condition and there is not another, and I have not come up here to be thanked for it and I am not going to be thanked for it."

Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him whether that condition was acceptable to anybody, and nobody in that room said anything at all for about four seconds, and a woman at the back of it said out loud that a person from outside that commonwealth was a person and was she not a person, and he said yes, and that the condition was not a statement about what a person was, it was a statement about who went out to a float in the water in the dark on a tide, and that his own crew had put that to him and he had put it to them and he had not come up that coast road to argue it.

Somebody said that this harbor had eleven crews that had written a name to a page and four that had not, and the man of about thirty-four said that those fifteen crews were in this harbor and not in that commonwealth and that the four that had not written a name were not less than the seven that had and that he had not come to count anybody, and that if those four wanted their names on a page at that port they could put them there themselves and that no one of them would be asked twice.

---

**And the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and was not named in it, and nobody in that room was asked to sign anything and nobody was handed anything to sign.**

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, in about eleven words, that the four heads on that wall could not send anybody anywhere and that whatever this party decided about that condition it would not be decided by anybody standing in front of that wall, and the man who keeps the leaf said yes, and that is why the man of about thirty-two was not answering it on a slip on Thursday and is going to answer it in that room on Saturday when about twenty people will be up those stairs and about eleven of them cannot read it.

The man of about thirty-four said that he would be in that room on Saturday and that he would not read it again, and that a condition that has to be read twice is not a condition, it is a doubt, and that if it is a doubt then they can all go back to their own water and this yard can go down the coast road with its two wagons and see what it finds.

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at the bench stone with the door of that shed open on the slip, and about eleven people were on that slip and heard all of it.**

"Forty-two thousand three hundred and seventy pence short, being two hundred and sixty-four marks, ten shillings and tenpence. It moved this morning by two thousand four hundred pence and that is one line of coal and it is on the sheet with the twenty-fourth of December against it and it is in the total at the foot of it as of half past four this afternoon, and those two days are the same day today and I have said why they are not always the same day and I am not going to say it again this month."

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it. No figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked on that Friday, and the man of about thirty-four slept in that barn at the second lay again and the man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down did not speak to him and was not asked to.

---

**A CART CAME UP THAT COAST ROAD IN THE MORNING WITH A CHEST OF LASHED TOOLS AND A BAG OF ROPE AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR ON THE DRIVING SEAT WHO IS NOT A NAME ON A WALL OR A LIST OR A SHEET OF ANYBODY'S. A ROOM OVER THAT MARKET WAS LET OUT AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON AND ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE WENT UP THOSE STAIRS AND ABOUT ELEVEN OF THEM CANNOT READ WHAT IS PUT IN FRONT OF THEM. FOUR SENTENCES WERE SAID IN THAT ROOM AND THEY WERE NOT SAID TWICE AND THE THIRD OF THEM WAS THAT A PERSON FROM OUTSIDE THAT COMMONWEALTH DOES NOT HOLD A BUOY AND DOES NOT GO OUT TO IT. NOBODY WAS ASKED WHETHER THAT CONDITION WAS ACCEPTABLE TO ANYBODY AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TO SIGN ANYTHING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. TWO THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED PENCE WENT ON THAT SHEET FOR COAL FOR A PUMP FOR TEN DAYS. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS FORTY-TWO THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-fourth day of December at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that coal for the pump for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at two thousand four hundred pence, being fifteen marks, with the twenty-fourth of December against it being the day the work was done, and that the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of that line out loud before he wrote a figure against it and said out loud that a line goes onto that sheet with the day the work was done against it and is not in the total at the foot of that sheet until the day this yard settles it, and where those two days are not the same day both of them are on the face of the line, and that on every line of that sheet since the thirteenth of that month those two days have been the same day and there has not yet been a line of that sheet where they were not the same day; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at fifty-three thousand and ten pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is forty-two thousand three hundred and seventy pence short, being two hundred and sixty-four marks, ten shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved by two thousand four hundred pence on that day; and that a man of about thirty-four of a fisher commonwealth at a port four days down that coast came up that coast road in a cart in the morning with a chest of lashed tools and a bag of rope and was at the head of that third lay at about eleven o'clock and refused to say his business on that slip, and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon and about twenty people went up those stairs and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and that that man stated one condition in about four sentences and did not say it twice, being that the crews of that port will put a mark on that coast and name it and keep it and answer for it, and that a person from outside that commonwealth does not hold a buoy and does not go out to it; and that nobody in that room was asked whether that condition was acceptable to anybody, and nobody was asked to sign anything, and the four heads on that wall did not change and no fifth head went on it and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room; and that the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and took no line and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and fifty-five, one thousand and one hundred and forty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the hour of that day's reading at two hundred and fifty-four, one thousand and one hundred and forty-seven.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that man was not answered on a slip and was waited for in a room is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a condition said at the head of a slip is said to about nine men and about half of them cannot read and the other half will remember it wrong on purpose, and that about eleven people who cannot read a page are the only reason in this harbor that a page gets read out twice, and that a man who has come four days up a coast road to say one thing is entitled to have it said where he can be stopped at any word by anybody standing in the room; and that the reason he was not thanked is his own, and is that he said so himself before anybody had a chance to and that a man who has told a room in advance that he is not going to be thanked has saved that room about a quarter of an hour and every day of that week is eleven weeks short of paying a wage; and that the reason no fifth head went on that wall is this yard's own mouth, and is that four heads are claims, refusals, restricted records and public correction, and that a fifth head for a commonwealth would be a head for a condition, and a condition is a thing somebody said once and not a thing a wall holds, and that the day a wall grows a head for a condition is the day a man at that port has a thing on a wall four hundred yards from his own hard and no way to take it off; and that the reason not a penny of anything standing in that harbor was touched in either direction on that Friday is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a condition is a thing said out loud in a room in about four sentences and a figure is a thing somebody owes for a thing somebody did, and that a man who has come four days up a coast road to put a condition on a harbor has not earned a penny of anybody's and has not been given one, and that the front of that end stall's board still carries a figure of four hundred and fifty pence with the figure of the first of June above it, and that two more days stand between this harbor and the next Tuesday on which that column is read out on a step.**
