# Chapter 805

*Monday the Third Day of October, the Forty-Fifth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-Ninth Time, That Cistern Charged at About Nine and Carried in Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Figure Against That Slate at About One and Not Read Out, Four New Crews on Their Fifteenth Day Given to the Man of About Thirty-Two at About Ten and Saying Out Loud in About Four That He Is Slower at It Than the Men He Is Teaching, no Wage Tuesday and No Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Monday the third day of October, with that market open from six and a hard westerly still in it and spray going over that mole head twice between seven and nine, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-ninth time, that cistern charged at about nine in fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of them. The figure went against that rota slate at about one and was not read out, and there is no charge on that cistern tomorrow. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short being the figure at that foot against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Monday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighteenth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-fifth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and third day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and fifth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-third day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare six hundred and ninth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and twenty-seventh days and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-second day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood four hundred and fourteenth day. The six names stay shut at four hundred and forty-five days, and the man of about fifty-one was behind that end stall all morning with his back to them. The empty middle column has been headed by that boy's hand of twenty-two since the spring and is headed by it still, and the seventh week of his being off the water began on the twenty-ninth. The fourth of those four still carries one hundred and twenty-five and no hundred and twenty-sixth line, and a column with nothing in it is not a column that has been closed. The nine hulls off that mole head have not been counted this week and are not going to be counted this week, and the count of January was nine. Three hundred and three days is the length of that hearing and the length has nothing to do with anything this yard does, and he is not on the water.**

---

That butt was charged at nine the way it is charged on a Monday and on a Thursday, and the fourteen men who did it were the same fourteen as on Thursday, and not one of them put a foot on it. The water came up over the rim twice before it stopped, and the cover was chained over at about half past eleven and the chain was put on by two men who had finished their own work at nine, and the butt stood short at the end of it and nobody drank out of it and nobody said that it was bad.

The ordinary work of that Monday was four boats off that mole before noon and a coil of rope measured against a gauge and marked with a piece of chalk at two places, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against a coil of rope that has not been used.

The four new crews were on their fifteenth day and were given to the man of about thirty-two at about ten, and he had them until about half past two and got through less of it than the man of about twenty-seven gets through in about an hour and a quarter. He said so out loud himself, at about half past two, in about four people's hearing, and what he said was that he is slower at it than the men he is teaching and that the difference is not a matter of the work being hard.

About four of that nine said out loud, that same afternoon, that the four crews are quicker at it than he is, and about four said the opposite and about one said nothing, and a thing about four people say in a shed is not a finding and is not written down as one.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with a page of her own under her arm and did not come into that shed, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about four with the page of routes in his hand. Neither of them said one word to the other that day, and neither of them signed the other's page, and neither of them did the other's work. She turned four pages in an hour and he read the page of routes the whole of his three hours and wrote nothing in the book of hours, and about four of that nine saw both halves of that and said nothing about either.

The five lines of the rule made on the thirtieth are still in the inside of the door of that room and they are not on any wall in this harbor and not on any board in this lane. The keeper said out loud to about nine, at about three, that a rule is not a wall and a wall is not a rule, and that about four of that nine had come in on that Monday with the notion that they were going to be asked for a copy of something, and that nobody is going to be asked for a copy of it because nobody in this yard keeps a copy of anything that is not a page.

Nothing has come back down that coast road about the page that went up it on Saturday, and nobody in that yard has asked after it, and the keeper said out loud that not asking is correct and that a yard which goes after a page the day it leaves is a yard that expects the page to be doing something for it.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-fifth day of that run of days, and there is no figure against him and there never has been.

---

The two sheets at that end stall were on their thirty-sixth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirty-first day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen. The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on it and nothing came down off it, and the four heads in the room over that fish market are claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records, and all four of them have been used and there is nothing else on that wall.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and eight words, and the charge came first in it and the teaching came in the middle of it, and nothing was said in it about any cost.

"**That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and eighteenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads an empty column that has been empty since the spring, that boy's surname is on no page, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie off that mole head and were not counted this week. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head for the three hundred and third day, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the cistern or for anything the man of about thirty-two said about himself, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

**A ROTA DAY AND THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINTH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO TAUGHT FOUR CREWS THIS AFTERNOON AND WAS SLOWER AT IT THAN THEY WERE, IN HIS OWN MOUTH, AND ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE SAID THE SAME THING AND IT IS NOT A FINDING. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTEENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man in his own mouth is the only account of how fast he is that this yard will take is that the only two persons who can say what a man's work costs in time are the man himself and the men he is working over the top of, and that both of those have a reason to shade it; that the reason the keeper would not be asked for a copy of the rule is that the fifth line gives anybody who has to work under it the right to repair it, and a right cannot be repaired off a copy that one place keeps and the other does not have; that the reason nobody went after the page that went up that coast road is that a page which is sent is not owed anything back and a page which is chased is a thing this harbor has started expecting to be paid for, and the woman of about twenty-nine who wrote it was not paid for it and said so by coming; that the reason the charge on that cistern is walked on by nobody is that a cistern is charged for a market and not for a man, and the fourteen who carried it did not drink from it and were not thanked for it and neither of those two things is a complaint.*
