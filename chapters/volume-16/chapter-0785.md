# Chapter 785

*Tuesday the Thirteenth Day of September, the Twenty-Fifth Day, the Ninety-Eighth Wage Tuesday and the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in Four Minutes at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, the Chest with the Twenty Envelopes Shut from Ten until Four, a Copying Book Brought Down off That Room over the Fish Market at About Half Past Eleven and Read Out at the Flat of That Stone in About Nine People, the Four Parts True and the Total False and the Honest Figure in a Drawer in That Record Room Which Was Not Opened Today, Three Marks Going onto the Face of That Page Before the Book Went Back up Those Stairs, a Further Week Fallen Due at the Close, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighteen and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Tuesday the thirteenth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a flat brown morning with a haze on the water that took the far side of that mole away by ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The ninety-eighth wage Tuesday and the fourth of the seven in this run of days, and the next one is the twentieth of September. The column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and a further week is fallen due at the close of this day. Not a rota day and not a wipe, and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, so that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is one hundred and ninety-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-fifth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-third day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-fifth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-third day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and eighty-ninth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and seventh days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-second day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-seventh day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-fourth day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and twenty-fifth day of saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. Under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, there is nothing and has never been anything, and nobody has been set over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. One hundred and twenty-five is the figure in that fourth column and no line has gone into it, and it is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls, as there were on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-third day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

The column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten and it took four minutes, and it was read by the keeper and not by anybody else, and about four of that nine could read it themselves and about four stood where they could not.

It stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks. It did not move. The chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and the keeper had the key on him the whole of that time and did not once put it in his pocket to get at it.

The man of about forty-nine of the west end said out loud, in about six, that it is the fourth Tuesday running that a person could put a week's wage in that step out of what is in it, and that nobody in that market has said that out loud before today and that he was saying it now because the next one is a fortnight off.

---

At about half past eleven the keeper went up to the room over that fish market and came back down with a copying book under his arm, and he carried it the way a man carries something that is not his, with both hands and without opening it on the stairs.

It is the book the copyist of that room keeps, and it is kept for what goes in it and for what comes out of it, and every page in it that has anything on it has been copied out of something else by somebody, and nobody in this harbor buys a copy of anything from that trade at less than a penny a sheet.

The woman of thirty of no office came down with him and stood at the back of that shed and did not sit, and the man of about thirty-two was not in that yard today and had gone out at about eight with two of the crews off that mole and was not back in it before six.

---

The keeper opened that book at about half past twelve at the flat of that bench stone and put a bit of tape at the top of the page as a marker, and about nine people stood round it.

He read it out in the yard's own mouth and he read it in the order it is written in and he read the four figures first and then the fifth line under them and then the note in the second hand against the fifth line, and then he read the three words at the top of that page, which are at the top of the page and not at the top of that part of it.

The four parts are one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence; four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, being four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence, being ten shillings and eightpence; coal for one day at twenty pence, being one shilling and eightpence; and thirty yards of line at three pence the yard, being ninety pence, being seven shillings and sixpence.

---

He read the fifth line out loud, and the fifth line is a single figure and it is smaller than the four of them make, and nobody in that shed had that figure on any page of theirs.

Against that fifth line there is a note in a second hand and the note is one line long and says that the figure was asked for in writing by a place that had read that page and asked for a smaller one, and that the figure given is the figure asked for.

Then the keeper said out loud that the honest figure is on a sheet in a drawer in that record room and that the drawer was not opened today and is not going to be opened by him this week, and that nobody in that shed has seen that sheet and that nobody in that shed is going to be shown it out of a drawer as a favour.

---

About four of that nine said nothing at all.

A man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store, who is the man in this harbor that counts words on a sheet when a sheet is disputed, said out loud that every one of those four parts is true, and that a man does not have to be a fool to make a total of four true parts that is false, and that this page was not made by a fool.

A man of about forty-nine of the west end said out loud that a smaller figure came back on a desk from a place that had read the page and asked for a smaller figure, and that a place that reads a page and asks for a smaller figure is a place that has read the three words at the top of it and not the four lines under them.

---

The keeper said out loud, to about nine and not to anybody in particular, that the man who wrote that page out wrote what came back.

He said it once and he did not say it twice, and he said it without any name attached to it, and he said that the copyist of that room is a trade and not a person anybody in that shed has any standing to be sorry for or to be satisfied about, and that he keeps his trade and charges his penny a sheet and has done so every week of this run of days.

Then he said out loud the last of it, and he said it slowly, and he said that in the whole of the working life of the man who writes in that book, he has never once written down a page nobody asked him to copy.

Nobody in that shed said that the man was forgiven and nobody in that shed said that the man was ruined, and both of those words were looked for by about four people and neither of them arrived.

---

At about two three marks went onto the face of that page of that book before the book went back up those stairs.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one against the fourth of the four lines. A man of about forty-nine of the west end put one against the note in the second hand. And a man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store put one against the fifth line and said out loud that he wanted it on the record that a smaller figure can be true, and that a man who cannot hold two true figures at once on one sheet is not careful, he is short of a column.

They are three marks and three hands and none of them is the hand that wrote that page, and a mark is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries three against this day.

---

At about four the woman of thirty of no office said out loud, to about six and not to the nine, that a record which is wrong is a thing with a way of being found out, and that what this yard has now is a page on which nothing is wrong and a drawer in which the right thing is, and that she is the one who keeps that record and that she is not able to put the second on the first without somebody opening the drawer, and that opening a drawer in that room is not hers to do.

The man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that that is the fault of the three words at the top of that leaf and not of anybody in that room, and that nobody has to be wicked to be standing in the way of a page that asks the wrong question.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the thirteenth day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the ninety-eighth wage Tuesday and the fourth of the seven in this run of days, and that the next is the twentieth of September; that the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and that a further week is fallen due at the close of the said day; that at about half past eleven the keeper brought down out of the room over that fish market the copying book kept by the copyist of that room, and that the woman of thirty of no office came down with him and stood at the back of that shed and did not sit, and that the man of about thirty-two was not in that yard; that at about half past twelve the keeper read that page out at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people, in the yard's own mouth and in the order it is written in; that the four parts on that page are one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence, being ten shillings and eightpence, coal for one day at twenty pence, being one shilling and eightpence, and thirty yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety pence, being seven shillings and sixpence, and that every one of the four is true; that the fifth line under them is a single figure smaller than the four of them make and that no figure of it was read out loud and that nobody in that shed holds it; that against that fifth line there is a note in a second hand, one line long, saying that the figure was asked for in writing by a place that had read that page and asked for a smaller one and that the figure given is the figure asked for; that the honest figure on that page is on a sheet in a drawer in that record room and that the said drawer was not opened on the said day; that the keeper said out loud that the man who wrote that page out wrote what came back, once, with no name attached to it, and that the copyist of that room keeps his trade and charges his penny a sheet, and that in the whole of his working life he has never once written down a page nobody asked him to copy; that nobody in that shed said that the man was forgiven and nobody said that the man was ruined; that at about two three marks went onto the face of that page before the book went back up those stairs, against the fourth of the four lines, against the note in the second hand, and against the fifth line, and that one mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote the page and is not an approval and is not a signature and is not a person, and that the page of days carries three against the said day; that at about four the woman of thirty of no office said out loud that she keeps that record and cannot put the drawer onto the page without somebody opening it; and that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that book was carried down with both hands and not opened on the stairs is that a book of copies is the only property in this harbor that a person who is not the owner of it is trusted with in a closed room, and a man who opens it in a passage has already told everybody on that passage what is in it; that the reason the four parts were read before the fifth line is that about four of that nine could not read and would have taken the fifth line for the whole of the page, and a smaller figure first and four true figures after it is the order in which that page has been misread ever since it was written; that the reason no figure of the fifth line was read out loud is that a figure nobody in that room has holds a page together and a figure said out loud in a yard of nine goes out of that yard in a week, and the figure is the only part of that page this harbor has any reason to keep; that the reason the drawer was named out loud and not opened is that a drawer nobody has mentioned can be a rumour and a drawer named and not opened is a fact, and this coast has spent nine years being told that the wrong thing about a page is sitting in a drawer somewhere, and the fact is worth more than the opening.*
