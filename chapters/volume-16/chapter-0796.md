# Chapter 796

*Saturday the Twenty-Fourth Day of September, the Thirty-Sixth Day, a Notice Off the Tide on the Friday Naming a Sitting Inland on the Eighth Day of October, a Document Read Out Once in That Shed at About Four by a Working Person in Front of About Twenty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read It, This Harbor Deciding to Be Represented by a Person Who Expects to Lose the Vote, no Charge and No Wipe and No Wage Movement, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Nineteen*

Saturday the twenty-fourth day of September, with that market open till six and a busy Saturday with three boats off that mole before noon and a woman of about thirty-seven of that market standing at the back of that shed from about ten with her hands behind her, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday and the next charge is on the morning of Monday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Saturday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-sixth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and ninety-fourth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-sixth day of a chest nobody has opened, and it stays shut. Those two objects nobody owns on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-fourth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has stood bare six hundredth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and eighteenth day and no figure of money is set against either. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and fifth day. It is four hundred and thirty-six days since a man of about fifty-one said anything at all to anybody about a page of six names. There is a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of the middle of those four columns and there has never been a line under it, nobody has been appointed over it, that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor, and he is off the water. The fourth of those four remains at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken nothing into it, and has been neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls lying, which is what lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. The two hundred and ninety-fourth day is what a man of about twenty-nine carries in the left side of his head, where nothing has been heard, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

A notice came off the tide on the Friday evening and it was lying on that bench stone at ten on the Saturday morning, and it names a sitting of a house inland on the eighth day of October and says that the treaty and the schedule that goes with it are to be spoken to, and that a harbor touched by the schedule may send one person to be heard. It was folded once and it had been folded once before it came out of the water, and there is a mark of a thumb at the top of the fold that is not the thumb of anybody in this shed.

About four of that nine read it before it was read out and about eleven of the twenty who came into that shed over the course of the day could not read it at all, and about five more could read the words and not the frame. The woman of about thirty-seven read it twice before anybody said anything about it and then folded it back the way it had been folded, which she had not been asked to do and which about four of that nine noticed.

---

At about two a woman of about thirty-seven of that market said out loud, to about nine, that this harbor has to decide before the Saturday is out whether it sends anybody to that sitting, and that nobody in this harbor may send anybody because a person asked to go is not a person who has asked to go. She stood at the back and she waited until about four of that nine had gone out of that door and back in again before she said it, and the finding out that followed took about nine minutes and there has never been a shorter one in this harbor.

The keeper said out loud that a route, a panel and an account are only represented by somebody who has asked to be represented, and that about nine people in that shed had to say whether they wanted somebody there at all before this harbor could speak to a woman of about thirty-seven about it.

About four said yes and about four said it did not matter and one said nothing, and that is the whole of the finding out and it took about nine minutes. The one who said nothing was a man of about thirty-eight of that landing and he has said nothing on the subject since, and about four of that nine read his face in about four minutes and read nothing in it that they would put on a page.

---

At about four the keeper read that notice out once in that shed in about twenty people and did not read it out again, and he read it in the yard's own mouth and he read the head of it first and the paragraph about the schedule last. He read it standing, because there is nowhere in that shed to sit down with twenty people in it, and he turned the sheet round once so that the men at the back could see the head of it and did not turn it round a second time.

No office arrived in that shed and nobody came into that shed with anything out of a room inland, and there is a name under the seal on that paper and nobody in that harbor read it out and nobody in that harbor said it.

That body sits inland, decides no contested claim, binds nobody who has not signed anything, and cannot send anybody anywhere, and what it comes to on the eighth of October is not on any page of this run of fifty days. A person in that lane can put the eighth day of October in a calendar without being told to and can copy a schedule off a board, and what nobody in that shed can do is make that body do anything it has not already decided to do, and nobody there tried.

---

That harbor has decided to be represented, and the person it is represented by is the woman of about thirty-seven, and she said out loud at about half past four that she expects to lose the vote and that she is going anyway. She said yes in about half a second, which she has said out loud since was not a thing anybody expected from a woman of about thirty-seven of a market that has about four hundred people in it.

She said that about two hundred and forty people sit in that house inland and that not one of them has ever been under this water, and that the entry about the thing the people of the Cinder Bell gave to a route is going to be read out and voted on by itself, and that somebody has to be in the room when it is read out, and that being in the room losing is worth more to that place than being outside it and right. She said that she has a house with four walls and a man in it and a boat of her own and none of that is on that schedule, and that the thing that is on it is the only thing in this harbor that nobody can find out by asking them a question, and she is going to be in the room when it is read out and that is all she has been asked for.

About four of that nine said nothing at all and nobody said that she had bettered anything by saying it, and it is not a victory and it is not the end of a thing. A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said afterwards that it was the best thing anybody had said in that shed in his time and he did not say it out loud in that shed and it is on no page, and that is the whole of what came of it.

---

At about five a sheet went up that coast road on the evening boat for the harbor authority on the west basin with the notice on it and a copy of the schedule on it, and a man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store and a man of about forty-nine of the west end put one mark each on the face of it against the day and not against a word. The copy of that schedule went into an envelope and the envelope went into the same boat with the rest of that day's post, and about four of that nine stood at that stone until it had gone round the point and there was nothing to see.

Two marks and two hands and neither of them the hand that wrote it, and the page of days carries two against this day. Neither of those two hands is the hand that wrote that sheet, and the man of about forty-nine of the west end put his mark against the day and not against a word because he cannot make out more than about four words of it.

Nobody was thanked for the notice or for the woman who is going inland to lose, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason not one person in that shed put the question to her is that asking a person whether they will go is a way of making a person a representative without their having asked, and the only standing this harbor has is over its own work; that the reason that notice was read out once and once only is that a document read twice in a shed of twenty is a document that has begun to be an argument, and it is a piece of paper and not an argument; that the reason the entry about the Cinder Bell is the entry this harbor is sending a person about is that a place's own giving is the one thing on that schedule nobody inland can find out by asking a question, and it is in a list because it was a copy and not because it was looked at; that the reason nobody said that going inland to lose is worth doing is that a harbor which only speaks when it expects to win does not speak at all in about nine years out of ten; and that the reason no figure of the vote at that sitting is written down anywhere in that yard is that a figure written before a thing has happened is a thing this yard has been wrong about before.*
