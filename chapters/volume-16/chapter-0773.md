# Chapter 773

*Thursday the First Day of September, the Thirteenth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eightieth Charge on That Cistern, Fourteen Trips by Hand Between About Nine and About One and Walked on by None of It, a Man of About Twenty-Six of the Harbor Authority Putting Out Loud in About Nine People at About Eleven Whether Anybody in That Shed Could Speak for the Cinder Bell and Nobody in That Shed Speaking for the Cinder Bell, an Ordinary Working Day Otherwise and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Six and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Four*

Thursday the first day of September, with that market open from about nine and a bright morning with the wind gone into the north-west and the tide making about half past seven and a good deal of cloud coming up over the point after noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eightieth charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand between about nine and about one, and it was walked on by none of them. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and the next wipe is tomorrow week and the next wage Tuesday is the sixth of September. A question was put out loud in about nine people at about eleven and it was not answered in that shed, and nobody in that shed stood up to speak for a place four days up that coast road. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and the first line of this run of days goes onto it tomorrow, so that foot still stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out, being seven hundred and eighty marks and one shilling, and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, being seven hundred and thirteen marks, seven shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence goes into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight comes out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the last tin in that shed was opened on the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and seventy-first day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and seventy-third days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and eleventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-seventh night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninety-fifth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and fiftieth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixty-fifth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighty-second day. And four hundred and thirteenth days have gone by with a man of about fifty-one saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. There is a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of the middle one of those four columns and there is nothing under it and nobody over it; his surname is on no page and he is off the water. One hundred and twenty-five is what the fourth of those four stands at, and it has taken no line into it and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. Two hundred and seventy-one days is how long a man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head, one more than the day before, with no figure set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern at the back of that market was charged between about nine and about one and it took fourteen men and fourteen trips and the water went in at the top and came out at the bottom into a butt.

The keeper wrote the hundred and eightieth against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it out, and that was the hundred and eightieth and the next of them is on Monday.

---

A man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority came into that yard at about eleven with a sheet of his own under his arm and stood a step off the flat of that bench stone and read one line of it out loud, being the line that carries the name of that place.

Then he said out loud, to about nine, that a list of what is unclaimed has come to this coast and that there is a place named on it four days up this road, and he asked out loud whether anybody in that shed could speak for the Cinder Bell.

---

The keeper said out loud, before anybody else in that yard had found a place to stand, that nobody in that shed may speak for a place that has not asked to be spoken for, and that a route and a panel and an account are not represented in this harbor by anybody who has not asked to be represented.

Nobody in that shed spoke for the Cinder Bell.

The man of about twenty-six said out loud that a harbor cannot be a party to a thing by having its man say nothing, and the keeper said out loud that a harbor can be a party to a thing by having its man say nothing for long enough for that place to send somebody of its own choosing.

---

The man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that yard the whole of that morning and stood against the wall by the door from about nine until about one, and when the reading of that one line had finished the man of about twenty-six looked round that shed and nobody asked him whether he could read it.

At about half past eleven a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud to about four, not asking anybody anything, that there are two people in this yard who cannot read that line and that neither of them was asked.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that yard at about twelve and said out loud one sentence, being that a place four days up that road does not need this harbor to speak for it, and that if it wants a word said it will send somebody who can be asked a question.

The woman of thirty of no office said out loud that the page of that week carries the question and the answer to it in that order and that the answer is on the same line, and that a page which printed the question without the answer would be a page that only a body could use.

---

Nothing went onto the face of anything in that yard today, and the page of days carries no figure in the second-hand column against this day, and a day with no figure in it is not a zero and is nowhere on it.

Nobody was thanked for the charge and nobody was thanked for the question that was not answered, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the cistern came first in the reading, as it does on a rota day, and after it there was a question that had been put out loud in that yard eight hours before and had not been answered, and the whole of that went out at that stone in about seven hundred and seventy-four words to about nine people, and one of that nine had said nothing all day.

"**The cistern at the back of that market was charged between about nine and about one and took fourteen men and fourteen trips, the water going in at the top and coming out at the bottom into a butt. The keeper wrote the hundred and eightieth against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it out, and that was the hundred and eightieth and the next of them is on Monday.**"

Nobody in this shed spoke for the Cinder Bell. At about half past eleven a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud to about four, asking nobody anything, that there are two people in this yard who cannot read that line and that neither of them was asked.**"

"**And the rest of it. Nothing went onto the face of anything in that yard today and that page of days carries no figure against this day in the second-hand column, and a day with no figure in it is not a zero and is nowhere on it. The fitting-out sheet has no settlement standing against it yet today, and its foot is still one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence goes into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight comes out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred comes to twenty-eight. What is under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. That is one hundred and eighty-six days this yard has gone without a tin in it. The third of those four columns carries nothing under it and nobody over it, a boy's hand of twenty-two being at the head of it, his surname on no page, and he is off the water, and one hundred and twenty-five is what the fourth of those four stands at, and it has taken no line into it and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls are lying about two sea miles off that mole head this evening and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody here has said that any of them is riding easier. Two hundred and seventy-one days is how long a man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head, one more than the day before, with no figure set against it, and he is not on the water. And the hearing that came back to him on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the charge or for the question that was not answered, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on.

**A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX OF THE HARBOR AUTHORITY ASKED OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AT ABOUT ELEVEN WHETHER ANYBODY IN THAT SHED COULD SPEAK FOR THE CINDER BELL, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED MAY SPEAK FOR A PLACE THAT HAS NOT ASKED TO BE SPOKEN FOR, AND NOBODY DID. TWO PEOPLE IN THAT SHED CANNOT READ THAT LINE AND NEITHER OF THEM WAS ASKED. THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTIETH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN THIS MORNING AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the first day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and the hundred and eightieth charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand between about nine and about one, walked on by none of them, and that the keeper wrote the hundred and eightieth against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it out; that the said day was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the next wipe is tomorrow week and the next wage Tuesday is the sixth of September; that a man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority came into that yard at about eleven with a sheet of his own under his arm, read one line of it out loud, being the line that carries the name of that place, and said out loud to about nine that a list of what is unclaimed has come to this coast and that there is a place named on it four days up that road, and asked out loud whether anybody in that shed could speak for the Cinder Bell; that the keeper said out loud, before anybody else in that yard had found a place to stand, that nobody in that shed may speak for a place that has not asked to be spoken for, and that a route and a panel and an account are not represented in that harbor by anybody who has not asked to be represented; that nobody in that shed spoke for the Cinder Bell; that the man of about twenty-six said out loud that a harbor cannot be a party to a thing by having its man say nothing, and that the keeper said out loud that a harbor can be a party to a thing by having its man say nothing for long enough for that place to send somebody of its own choosing; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that yard the whole of that morning and stood against the wall by the door from about nine until about one; that when the reading of that one line had finished the man of about twenty-six looked round that shed and nobody asked him whether he could read it; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason that question was not answered is that a man who answers it in a shed has answered it for a place that is not in the room, and a man who has answered for a place that is not in the room will go on answering for it, and the day this harbor finds that out the answer will be four weeks old and will have a seal on it that nobody in this yard put there; that at about half past eleven the reason the man of about thirty-one said the number of people in that yard who cannot read that line out loud to about four is that a man who cannot read a line is invisible to a man asking a room a question, and the only thing that puts him back in the room is somebody else saying a number in front of everybody; that at about twelve the reason the answer was written on the same line as the question is that a question standing on its own line for eleven days is a question somebody will put a body under, and this yard has watched that happen twice and does not intend to write the third one down; and that at about nine the reason the hundred and eightieth was not shortened by the two men who were late is that a cistern charged by a rota and not by the men who turn up is a cistern that can be argued about on the morning it is wanted, and the two men who were late are two men who will be late again and will be argued with again, and a yard that argues about its cistern does not charge it.*
