# Chapter 794

*Thursday the Twenty-Second Day of September, the Thirty-Fourth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-Sixth Time, the Cistern Charged at About Nine in Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Drawer in That Record Room Opened at About Eleven and the Honest Total Under Nine Sheets and Nine Pennies Paid to a Man of About Fifty-Seven and Taken by Him, Six Sentences Said in That Shed at About Half Past Three in About Nine People, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Back in Service at Daybreak and Seen by Nobody Here, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Nine*

Thursday the twenty-second day of September, with that market open from six and rain coming in off the water in long sheets and the wind round in the north-west by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-sixth time, that cistern charged at about nine and taken in fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of them, the figure written against that rota slate at about one and not read out, and there is no charge on that cistern tomorrow. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Thursday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventh days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and ninety-second day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-fourth day of not being asked anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-second day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninety-eighth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and sixteenth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-first day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood four hundred and third day. The four hundred and thirty-fourth day went by with a man of about fifty-one saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. A boy's hand of twenty-two is the head of the middle of those four columns, under which nothing has ever stood and over which nobody has ever stood, and his surname is on no page of this harbor and he is off the water. The fourth of those four has never taken a line into it, stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie off that mole head at about two sea miles, the same nine as lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine has now gone two hundred and ninety-two days with nothing heard in the left side of his head, no figure is set against that, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged at about nine and took fourteen men and fourteen trips and was walked on by none of them, and the butt stood short at the end of that and had a skin on it by about one, and nobody drank it and nobody said that it was bad.

The bell of the Cinder Bell went back into service at daybreak that morning, four hundred yards and three fields off, and nobody in this harbor saw it seated and nobody in this harbor will see it seated, and no figure of that day is on any page of this yard.

---

At about eleven the keeper went up into the room over that fish market with the man of about fifty-seven of that market who keeps that copying book, and the drawer in that room was opened at about eleven and not before and not after.

There were nine sheets in it above the one underneath, and the man of about fifty-seven lifted the nine off in his own hand one at a time and put them on the table and charged a penny a sheet for the lifting of them and took the money, and the money was nine sheets at a penny a sheet, being nine times one penny, for nine pence, being nine of the twelve pence in a shilling, and that money came out of the pocket of the man who keeps the leaf, and about four of that nine said out loud that the harbor ought to be paying that.

He said that he had paid it and that it was nine pence of his own and it was not going onto that sheet, and that a sheet is a record of work done and that opening a drawer is not work done.

---

Under the nine sheets there was one sheet and on that sheet were four lines and a figure at the foot of them, and that figure was four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence.

The four parts on it are one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence; four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence, being ten shillings and eightpence; coal for one day at twenty pence, being one shilling and eightpence; and thirty yards of line at three pence the yard at thirty times three for ninety pence, being seven shillings and sixpence. One hundred and sixty-eight added to one hundred and twenty-eight, and twenty added to ninety, being one hundred and ten, and two hundred and ninety-six added to one hundred and ten, being four hundred and six.

The sheet that was above it on the pile is the one that came back, and it carries a figure that is smaller than that one by one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and that is a day of a boat at the figure in chalk.

---

At about half past three that man of about fifty-seven stood at the back of that shed in about nine people and said six sentences, and nobody asked him one word before them or during them or after them.

The first of the six was that he wrote out what came back off that desk and that he charged his penny a sheet for the writing of it out.

The second was that the figure which came back had been asked for in writing by a place that had read that page and had asked for a smaller one.

The third was that the sheet with the true total on it was in that drawer under the nine sheets and that he had known that since the day he wrote the other one out.

The fourth was that he did not know the four parts did not make that total until the keeper read the four of them out in that shed on the thirteenth of September with a pencil in his hand.

The fifth was that he has never in his working life written down a page nobody asked him to copy, and that he wrote that one.

The sixth was that he has taken his penny a sheet for it, that he is not giving it back, and that he will charge the same penny a sheet on the next page anybody in this harbor wants written.

Nobody in that shed said the word forgiven and nobody said the word ruined, and about four of that nine looked for both of those words and neither of them arrived, and he went back up those stairs at about four and was not sent for again on any day of this run.

---

At about four a copy of that sheet was written out for each of the two places that planned on the lower figure, and a man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark on the face of each of them against the line and neither of them against any figure.

Two marks and two hands and neither of them the hand that wrote the sheet, and the page of days carries two against this day.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said at that stone standing up, in about one thousand one hundred and sixteen words, and the drawer came first in it and the nine pennies came in the middle of it and the six sentences were not said again.

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-fifth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twentieth day a step to the left of the refusal, and the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at that end stall at fourteen.**"

"**The foot of that fitting-out sheet stands this evening at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and nothing settled into it today. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard has gone two hundred and seventh days without a tin opened in it. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two and beneath it there is nothing, that boy's surname is on no page, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five, holds no line, and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head nine hulls lie, as nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine has gone two hundred and ninety-two days with nothing heard in the left side of his head, one more than the day before, no figure is set against that, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the nine pennies or for the six sentences or for the sheet that was under the other nine, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

**THE DRAWER IN THAT RECORD ROOM WAS OPENED AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND THE HONEST TOTAL IS FOUR HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE, BEING TWO MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND NINE SHEETS WERE ABOVE IT AND A PENNY A SHEET WAS PAID AND TAKEN. SIX SENTENCES WERE SAID IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE AND WERE NOT SAID AGAIN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nine pence was paid out of the keeper's own pocket and not out of that sheet is that a sheet of this yard is a record of work done for this yard and that nine pence paid to a man for opening a drawer is a thing between two men in a room and is not work done for this harbor; that the reason the nine sheets were lifted one at a time and not together is that a man lifting nine sheets together is a man who does not know what is under them, and that this yard wanted a man who knew; that the reason the six sentences were said standing at the back of that shed and not written out and read is that a written confession can be carried up that coast road and read by people who were not in the room, and a thing said in a shed of nine is in a shed of nine; that the reason nobody said the word forgiven and nobody said the word ruined is that both of those words put a man on a step, and a step in a yard of about nine is a thing this yard has no authority to build; and that the reason he is not sent for again is that a man who has said the six sentences of it has said them, and asking a seventh thing of him would be a second occasion and this harbor does not make a second occasion out of a first one.*
