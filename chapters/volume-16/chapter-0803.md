# Chapter 803

*Saturday the First Day of October, the Forty-Third Day, the Correction Standing Where It Went Up on the Twenty-Ninth of August and the Refusal Standing Beside It at the Same Size and Neither Taken Down, the Honest Total of Four Hundred and Six Pence Going onto the Own Page of the Lowfen Reed in That Community's Own Hand at About Two with the Woman of About Twenty-Nine on That Page of It and That Page Going up That Coast Road on the Four O'Clock Boat with Ten Hands on Its Face, no Charge on That Cistern and No Wage Movement, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Saturday the first day of October, with that market open from six and a hard bright morning with the wind in the north and no cloud on that mole head until about two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Saturday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and there is a charge on that cistern on the morning of Monday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence being all that has ever gone in, and that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Saturday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixteenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and first day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and third day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-first day. That bench under that pump has stood bare six hundred and seventh night running. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and twenty-fifth day and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eightieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twelfth day. That man of about fifty-one stands at four hundred and forty-three days of not opening his mouth over six names, and he passed that lane twice that day. The middle of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with an empty space under it, and the forty marks whole that stand against that boy have not gone and he is not on the water. One hundred and twenty-five is what the fourth of those four has said for the whole of this run and it says nothing else, and neither shutting it nor striking it has been proposed by anybody. Those nine hulls are still about two sea miles off that mole head and nothing has been said about them in this harbor this week, as in every week since the twenty-first of January. The left side of the head of the man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing for the three hundred and first day, and he is not on the water and nobody has put a price on it.**

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was three boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by four, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of that. The four new crews were on their thirteenth day and did the afternoon's work in about two hours under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about four with the page of routes in his hand and did not go into the end of that mole once in all that time.

At about ten the keeper went up the stairs of that fish market to the long room and opened that drawer himself and took nine sheets off it one at a time and set them on the table in the order they came off, and the fourth one down was the sheet with the four parts on it. He carried it down the stairs in his own hand and charged nobody anything for it, because that sheet is not a copy and nobody copied it, and the man of about fifty-seven of that market was not asked to open that drawer on this day and did not open it.

---

He read the four parts out once in that shed at about eleven with his multiplications against them, and about nine people stood there and about five of them could not read the sheet and heard it and believed the figures or did not. The first part is one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence. The second is four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, four times two times sixteen, for one hundred and twenty-eight pence, being ten shillings and eightpence. The third is coal for one day at twenty pence, being one shilling and eightpence. The fourth is thirty yards of line at three pence the yard, thirty times three, for ninety pence, being seven shillings and sixpence.

The woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed did that sum out loud herself, in her own words and in that order, at about half past eleven. One hundred and sixty-eight added to one hundred and twenty-eight is two hundred and ninety-six. Twenty added to ninety is one hundred and ten. Two hundred and ninety-six added to one hundred and ten is four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence. She said it once and she said it slowly and about four of that nine wrote it on the back of their own hand, and nobody read it out after her.

At about two she took that community's own page out of the inside of her coat and laid it flat on her knee and held the near corner down with the heel of her hand, and she wrote the four parts first and put the total under them, and the hand that wrote the refusal on that end stall on the twenty-eighth of August is the hand that wrote this one. Her own hand wrote it and she is the one who took it, and there is no name against any of it because there is no name on her community's page to put one in. She said out loud that this is the first figure anybody in this harbor has put in front of her that she can spend, and that a figure somebody can spend is a figure and not an argument.

---

And then she said the other half of it out loud, and this is what she said and nobody took it off her. That it does not withdraw what is standing on that end stall, because what is standing on that end stall is not about a figure, and because a refusal is a thing a community says about what a total is for, and putting a spendable total on a page does not turn that refusal into nothing. About four of that nine said out loud that her reason was correct, which is what about four of that nine said on the twenty-eighth of August as well, and about four said nothing and one said that she had bettered something and about four said it was not a bettering of anything.

At about four that page of hers went up that coast road with her on the boat, carried in her own hand, and it went with ten hands on the face of it besides hers, put there by ten people in that yard who were not the person who wrote what the hands went against, and the page of days carries ten against this day. The sheet carrying the refusal stayed on that end stall, where it has stood since the twenty-eighth of August, and the correction stayed where it went up on the twenty-ninth of August, and the two of them are the same size they have been for a month and a hand's breadth apart and the wrong figure is left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out.

She said out loud, to about nine, that nobody is to take that refusal down on the strength of a figure, and that if anybody in this harbor takes it down on the strength of a figure she will come down that road again and put it up, and about four of that nine said nothing at all and nobody asked her one question about it.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about four with the page of routes in his hand, and the woman of thirty of no office was not in that harbor and was two days up that coast road, and neither of them signed the other's page that day and neither of them said one word to the other and neither of them gave up an inch of their own work. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-third day of that run of days.

The two sheets at that end stall were on their thirty-fourth day and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twenty-ninth day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen with nothing on it moved. Nothing went up on the wall of the room over that chandler's store and nothing came down off it, and no fifth thing was added to the four heads in the room over that fish market on account of a figure being right.

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the honest total went up that coast road on the Saturday boat in her own hand instead of being copied and sent is that a copy of a figure somebody has to sign for is a different kind of object from a figure a person wrote with her own hand and is carrying home herself; that the reason nobody here wrote her reason down on any page of this yard is that the reason is hers and it is on her community's page and it has been standing on that end stall since the twenty-eighth of August in her own hand where anybody can read it; that the reason nobody took the refusal down when the figure that can be spent went onto her own page is that a refusal is not a complaint about a number, and that the two of those things have been kept apart in this harbor since the first of them was put up, which is the only reason both of them are still standing; that the reason ten hands went onto that page and none of them went onto the refusal is that the four parts are a thing about a day of work that nine people in that shed can say they watched being done, and a refusal is a thing about what a total is for and no person in that shed has standing to put a mark on it.*
