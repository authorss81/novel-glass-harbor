# Chapter 774

*Friday the Second Day of September, the Fourteenth Day, Line Three Going onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Two in the Afternoon With the Friday on Its Face as the Day the Work Was Done and the Sunday on Its Face as the Day It Settles, Four Parts Read With Their Multiplications Before Any Figure Was Written, a Sheet of Newsprint Carrying Two Column Headings and the Count of Two Hundred and Eleven Made at About Three and One Mark Going onto the Face of It Before It Left That Yard on the Saturday, an Ordinary Working Day and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Nine*

Friday the second day of September, with that market open from about nine and a fine morning with the wind in the north-west going lighter towards noon and a good deal of cloud coming up over the point after noon and the glass steady, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, and nothing goes on that cistern until Monday morning and no wipe comes before Sunday, and the next wage Tuesday is the sixth of September. Line three went onto that fitting-out sheet at about two in the afternoon with the day the work was done on its face and the day it settles on its face, the two being two days apart, and the four parts were read with their multiplications before any figure was written down. Nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today, so the foot of it stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out, being seven hundred and eighty marks and one shilling, and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, being seven hundred and thirteen marks, seven shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and the sum of that line goes down on Sunday and the foot is read after it. Into that yard at about ten goes a hundred and sixty-eight pence and out of it at about four goes a hundred and sixty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and nothing has been taken out of that tin since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fourteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and seventy-second day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and seventy-fourth days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twelfth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-eighth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninety-sixth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and fifty-first day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixty-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighty-third day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for four hundred and fourteenth days. The middle of those four columns has nothing in it and nobody over it, and the hand at the head of it is a boy's of twenty-two whose surname is written nowhere, and he is off the water. The fourth of those four remains at one hundred and twenty-five with no line taken into it and with neither a shut mark nor a strike across it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine has now gone two hundred and seventy-two days without hearing anything out of the left side of his head, one more than the day before, and nothing is set against that and he is not on the water.**

---

The yard did its ordinary work and the work was fitting out, being two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, coal and oil for those two days, ninety yards of new line, and three men on two days.

The ninety yards came off a coil in that shed and went into a bin by the bench stone and were cut into lengths against a gauge, and four panes went into that lamp earlier in the week and were not in this line and are on the next one.

---

The keeper wrote the third line onto that fitting-out sheet at about two with the pen and not with a charcoal, and both days went onto the face of it, the Friday as the day the work was done and the Sunday as the day it settles.

He said both of those days out loud in about nine people before he wrote any figure at all, and he said them in that order, being the day the work was done and then the day it settles.

---

The four parts, with the multiplication of each one said out loud before the product.

Two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence. Coal and oil for two days is forty pence. Ninety yards of line at three pence the yard is ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence. Three men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day is three times two times sixteen for ninety-six pence.

Three hundred and thirty-six and forty and two hundred and seventy and ninety-six is seven hundred and forty-two pence, being four marks, eight shillings and sixpence.

---

At about three the keeper made a sheet of newsprint and wrote on it the two headings at the top of that fourth sheet and the count of the two hundred and eleven underneath them, and nothing else from the inside of that copy went onto it.

A man of about forty-five of that market put one mark on the face of that newsprint at about half past three, against the count and not against the headings, and said out loud that the headings are the part that can be wrong in a way a man cannot see and the count is the part that can be checked, and that a sheet which is going out of this yard ought to carry a mark against the part that can be checked.

One mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote the sheet. It is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries one against the said day.

---

The man of about thirty-eight of that landing came into that yard at about eleven and said out loud, to about four, that he goes up that coast road tomorrow morning and back on the Thursday, and that he is not carrying anybody's case and is not anybody's mouth, and that a thing said to him in this yard he will repeat in a yard four days up that road word for word and with the day and the hour on it and with his own name under it as the man who carried it.

Nobody asked him a single thing.

---

At the end stall the correction and the refusal were standing at the same size and a hand's breadth apart on their fifth day, and the wrong figure was still showing underneath the correction, and neither sheet had been touched.

Nobody in that yard said anything about either of them, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

---

About half past five on that Friday a line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about two with four parts and two days on its face, and at half past five the whole of that Friday went out at that stone with the multiplication of each part read before the sum and not after, in about nine hundred and twenty-seven words, to about nine people, four of whom could add a column in their heads and five of whom could not.

"**And the rest of it. At the end stall the correction and the refusal were standing where they were put on Monday, at the same size and a hand's breadth apart on their fifth day, and the wrong figure was still showing underneath the correction and neither sheet had been touched, and nobody in that yard said anything about either of them. Nothing goes into the foot of that fitting-out sheet until Sunday, and that foot is standing at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Into this yard at about ten goes a hundred and sixty-eight pence and out of it at about four goes a hundred and sixty-eight, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight. That slate is holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. There has been no tin opened in that shed for one hundred and eighty-seven days. The third of those four columns has nothing in it and nobody over it, the hand at the head of it being a boy's of twenty-two whose surname is written nowhere, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four remains at one hundred and twenty-five with no line taken into it and with neither a shut mark nor a strike across it. This evening there are nine hulls about two sea miles off that mole head, the same nine that were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has kept its mouth shut about them. A man of about twenty-nine has now gone two hundred and seventy-two days without hearing anything out of the left side of his head, one more than the day before, and nothing is set against that and he is not on the water. And what he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the line or for the mark on the newsprint, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on.

**LINE THREE WENT ONTO THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET AT ABOUT TWO WITH THE DAY THE WORK WAS DONE ON ITS FACE AND THE DAY IT SETTLES ON ITS FACE, THOSE TWO DAYS BEING TWO DAYS APART, AND FOUR PARTS WERE READ WITH THEIR MULTIPLICATIONS BEFORE ANY FIGURE WAS WRITTEN, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO PENCE, BEING FOUR MARKS, EIGHT SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE. THE SUM OF THAT LINE GOES DOWN ON SUNDAY AND THE FOOT IS READ AFTER IT. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FIVE OF THAT MARKET PUT ONE MARK ON THE FACE OF A SHEET OF NEWSPRINT GOING OUT OF THAT YARD AGAINST THE COUNT AND NOT AGAINST THE HEADINGS. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the second day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the yard did its ordinary work on the said day and that the work was fitting out, being two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, coal and oil for those two days, ninety yards of new line, and three men on two days; that the keeper wrote the third line onto that fitting-out sheet at about two with a pen and not with a charcoal, and that both days went onto the face of it, the Friday as the day the work was done and the Sunday as the day it settles, those two days being two days apart, and that he said both of those days out loud in about nine people before he wrote any figure at all; that the four parts of that line are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, ninety yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence, and three men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at three times two times sixteen for ninety-six pence; and that the sum of those four parts is seven hundred and forty-two pence, being four marks, eight shillings and sixpence, which goes down into the foot of that sheet on Sunday and that foot is read after it; that at about three the keeper made a sheet of newsprint and wrote on it the two headings at the top of that fourth sheet and the count of the two hundred and eleven underneath them, and nothing else from the inside of that copy went onto it; that at about half past three a man of about forty-five of that market put one mark on the face of that newsprint against the count and not against the headings, and said out loud that the headings are the part that can be wrong in a way a man cannot see and the count is the part that can be checked, and that a sheet going out of that yard ought to carry a mark against the part that can be checked; and that the man of about thirty-eight of that landing came into that yard at about eleven and said out loud, to about four, that he goes up that coast road on the Saturday morning and back on the Thursday, and that he is not carrying anybody's case and is not anybody's mouth, and that nobody asked him a single thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two the reason both days went onto the face of that line before the figures is that a line with one day on it is a line nobody can check, because a man cannot tell from a bill whether the work was done late or whether the bill was written late, and this yard was wrong about that in June and the two were not the same mistake; that at about two the reason the multiplication of each part was said out loud before its product is that a product on its own is a figure anybody would have to trust, and a product with its count and its rate standing next to it is a figure a man can do again on a slate in front of the man who wrote it; that the reason the mark went against the count and not against the two headings is that a mark against a heading says only that a man can read, and a mark against a number says a man has added up what he read, and this yard has more men who can read than men who add up what they read; and that at about eleven the reason the man of about thirty-eight said out loud what he would carry and what he would not before anybody asked him is that a man who volunteers the terms of an errand in a working place cannot afterwards be asked whether he consented to it, and this yard has been ruined twice this summer by a man who said yes before anybody had said what was being asked.*
