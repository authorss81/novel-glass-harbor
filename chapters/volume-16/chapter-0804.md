# Chapter 804

*Sunday the Second Day of October, the Forty-Fourth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Sixth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One Not Whole, Forty-Three and Thirty and Forty-Three Being One Hundred and Sixteen the Whole of It, a Minute Longer Each Way Again and Nobody in This Harbor Has Said Why and Nobody Asked Again, That Market Shut With the Bars Down Until About Six and Four New Crews In at About Eleven, no Charge on That Cistern and No Wage Movement, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Sunday the second day of October, with that market shut and the bars down until about six and a cold bright morning with the first of a hard westerly in it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twenty-sixth time. That wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare thirty minutes and went back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being forty-three and thirty and forty-three and one hundred and sixteen the whole of it. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and the first charge to that cistern after this wipe is on the morning of Monday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventeenth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and second day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and fourth day of nobody asking him anything, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-second day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and eighth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and twenty-sixth day and no figure of money is set against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-first day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirteenth day. Nobody asked the man of about fifty-one anything on that Sunday about his page of six names, being the four hundred and forty-fourth day, and that has been the arrangement without anybody ever making it. That boy's hand of twenty-two is still the head of an empty column and nothing was put under it on that Sunday, and nobody said his name, because there is not one on a page. That fourth column stood at one hundred and twenty-five through the wipe and at the close of that Sunday, and no line was ruled against it and it is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie where nine lay in January off that mole head, and a man of about thirty-eight of that landing looked at the water over them for about a minute on that Sunday and said nothing about them. That man of about twenty-nine is on the three hundred and second day of hearing nothing on his left and is not on the water, and this run of fifty days has not once set a figure against that hearing.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare thirty minutes, and the three men who do that work took forty-three minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than last Sunday, and nobody in this harbor has said why and nobody said why this morning either. They go out with the barrow at about ten and come back with the barrow and put it against the same wall they took it off, and it is the ninth year that wall has been taken off and put back and not one year of the nine has had it done in the time it is now done in.

At about half past ten the new render came off in one piece at the top corner and the second man caught it on his knee and put it back where it was and nobody said anything about that either, and there is a place on that wall where the render has run down onto the floor in a place the size of a hand and it has not been scraped up and is not going to be.

A man of about thirty-eight of that landing stood at that bare wall through the whole of the thirty minutes and said out loud, to about four and then to about five, that it is a minute longer each way again and that he has asked three times this year and been told nothing three times. He said it at about eleven and he said it again at about twelve and he did not say it once and then stop, and the second time there were about five people instead of about four and about two of them had come in out of the weather and were standing at the back with their coats still on.

The keeper said nothing at all about that minute on that Sunday, and about four people noticed that he said nothing, and it is the first Sunday in this run of days that he has had nothing to say about it, and about nine of that nine have not asked him about the silence and are not going to.

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday was four new crews in at about eleven and the afternoon's work done in about two hours under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about four with the tally slate and the book of hours and was given nothing else to do, and he wrote four lines in the book of hours and they were four lines of hours and no figure of money is against any of them.

The five lines of that rule are still in the inside of the door of that room and they have not gone anywhere since the Friday, and the keeper said out loud on that Sunday, to about four, that a rule in a keeper's own keeping is not a rule anybody is held to yet, and that anybody who comes into that room and asks to read it may read it standing at the door, and about four have asked and about four have not and nobody in that yard has sent for it.

A market shut and the bars down until about six means that about half the people in that market were not in it on that Sunday at all, and that is the ordinary condition of a Sunday and is not on any page of this harbor.

---

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about eleven until about three and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-fourth day of that run of days, and he has been on that bench every one of those days without a break. That man has not asked this yard for a figure since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard has not offered him one, and there is no arrangement between them of any kind.

The two sheets at that end stall were on their thirty-fifth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirtieth day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen with nothing ruled on it and nothing entered against it. The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on it and nothing came down off it and the four heads in the room over that fish market are claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and thirty-two words, and the wall came first in it and the minute nobody has explained came in the middle of it, and nothing was said out loud about the rule.

"**That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and seventeenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of an empty column with nothing under it, that boy's surname is on no page, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and this harbor has said nothing about any of them. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head for the three hundred and second day, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the thirty minutes that wall stood bare or for the minute that has not been explained, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**A WIPE AND THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIXTH CAME AND WENT AT FORTY-THREE AND THIRTY AND FORTY-THREE, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THE WHOLE OF IT, AND A LADDER IS A UNIFORM THREE MINUTES AND THAT WALL IS NOT A LADDER. NOBODY HAS SAID WHY AND NOBODY ASKED AGAIN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTEENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the keeper said nothing about the minute is that he has said the same thing about it on six Sundays of this run and about four people in that market can now say it without the sheet, and a thing said six times in the same words has stopped being an answer and become a noise; that the reason the three men are still asked to take that wall off and put it back unrepaired and not whole is that it is a job and the job is done and no figure of money is set against either side of it by anybody who has looked at the wall since the twelfth of January; that the reason the five lines are in the inside of a door and not in a keeper's pocket is that a thing in a pocket is a thing nobody can find, and a rule that cannot be found cannot be repaired by anybody who has to work under it, which is what the fifth line says it may be; that the reason no sheet left any building in that harbor on the said day is that the market was shut and the bars were down and the carrier does not run on a Sunday, which is the ordinary condition of a harbor and is not a column of anything.*
