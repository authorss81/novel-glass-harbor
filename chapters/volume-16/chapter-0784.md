# Chapter 784

*Monday the Twelfth Day of September, the Twenty-Fourth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-Third Charge on That Cistern at About Nine, Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Hundred and Eighty-Third Written Against That Rota Slate at About One and Not Read Out, a Sheet of This Yard's Own Coming Down off a Board at About Eleven and Going Back up at About Twelve to the Same, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Fifth Day of Fourteen, no Wage Movement and no Wipe and Nothing into the Foot of That Fitting-Out Sheet, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventeen and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Monday the twelfth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a hard blue morning after a night of wind out of the north-east and no sea at all inside the mole, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-third charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand, and it was walked on by none of them, and the keeper wrote the hundred and eighty-third against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it, and that rota has now run one hundred and eighty-three times without a charge being put off it and without one being put on twice. There is no charge on that cistern tomorrow and the next is on Thursday morning. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, so that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is one hundred and ninety-seventh days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-second day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-fourth day of not being asked anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-second day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and eighty-eight nights running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and sixth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-first day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-third day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and twenty-fourth day of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. There is nothing under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and nobody has been set over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. That fourth column is standing at one hundred and twenty-five with nothing taken into it, and it is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-second day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern went at about nine and took fourteen men and fourteen trips, and it was walked on by none of them, and the last man down came back with the empty can and did not look at the gauge, and the butt stood short at the end of it again and had a skin on it by about one.

The keeper wrote the hundred and eighty-third against the day on that rota slate at about one with a stub of pencil and did not read it out, and about four of that nine were in the shed at that hour and heard the pencil and not the figure.

---

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay came in at about ten and stood against the wall by the door until about two and was not asked anything by anybody in that time, and he brought a folding chair with him and did not open it.

He is a man who is in this yard most days and is asked nothing on any of them, and about four people in that shed have noticed that and none of them has ever mentioned it to him or to anybody else.

---

A sheet of this yard's own came down off the board at about eleven.

The man of about thirty-one of the second lay put it up himself at about nine that morning, at the top of the board and to the left of the three sheets that have been standing there since the twenty-ninth of August, and it was a sheet with a day and an hour on it and a figure against one line of it, and it went up with a pin through one of four holes at the top of that board.

At about eleven he took it down off that board, folded it once along the middle, and put it in his own coat, and about six people in that yard and on that step watched him do it and none of them said anything.

---

He put it back up at about twelve.

The same sheet, the same way round, on the same board, in the same place, with the same figure on it and the same day and the same hour on it, and the pin went through the third hole instead of the first.

He gave his reason at about four and not before, and he gave it to about nine people and not to the six who had watched him, and he said that the board there stands about a foot out from the wall behind it and that a sheet pinned at the top hole lies back against the plaster and cannot be read by anybody standing on that step unless they are as tall as he is, and that he had not known that when he put it up and that he had read it himself every day since he first put it up.

Nobody in that shed said that the sheet had been wrong, and nobody said that it had been right either, and nobody said the word mistake.

---

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud at about half past four that the page of days in this shed carries a column headed WITHDRAWN which has stood empty since this run of days began, and that a sheet came off a board in this yard this morning and went back on it at twelve, and that he had not asked what that column counts and does not intend to ask this week.

A man of about forty-nine of the west end said out loud that he had three sheets of his own at home and had taken one off his own wall in June and put it back the same day and had never once thought of it again, and that whether that is the same thing is not a question anybody has asked him.

---

At about two the keeper copied the day and the hour off that sheet into the first column of that sheet and copied nothing else, and at about three he wrote the hour at which that sheet had gone up and the hour at which it had come back into the first column of that week and no figure of money at either hour.

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the fifth day of being out of service and nobody in this harbor has rung it, and the fourteen days of it run to the twenty-second of September and are written out as fourteen days and not as a week and a day.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was put out at that stone in about one thousand and sixty-one words, and the charge came first in it, because it came first in the yard, and the sheet that came off that board came last, and nothing was read out twice.

"**And the rest of it. At about two the keeper copied the day and the hour off that sheet into the first column of that sheet and copied nothing else, and at about three he wrote the hour at which that sheet had gone up and the hour at which it had come back into the first column of that week and no figure of money at either hour. The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the fifth day of being out of service and nobody in this harbor has rung it. No mark went onto the face of any page on this day and the column of the second hand carries nothing against it, and a day with no figure on it is not a zero and is nowhere on it. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard has gone one hundred and ninety-seventh days without a tin in it. A boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and no figure has ever gone into it and no person has ever been named over it, that boy's surname on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no line taken into it and nothing struck. Nine hulls are lying about two sea miles off that mole head this evening where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and that is the two hundred and eighty-second day of it, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against that figure and he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the charge or for the sheet going up again or for the reason it went up again, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THIRD CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN THIS MORNING AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. A SHEET OF THIS YARD'S OWN CAME DOWN OFF THAT BOARD AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND WENT BACK UP AT ABOUT TWELVE THROUGH A DIFFERENT HOLE, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED SAID THAT IT HAD BEEN WRONG. THE COLUMN HEADED WITHDRAWN ON THAT PAGE OF DAYS HAS STOOD EMPTY SINCE THIS RUN OF DAYS BEGAN AND WAS NOT ADDED UP TODAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twelfth day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day, being the hundred and eighty-third charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand, walked on by none of them, and that there is no charge on that cistern tomorrow and the next is on Thursday morning; that the said day was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that yard from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody in that time; that at about nine a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put a sheet of his own up on that board with a pin through the first of four holes at the top of it; that at about eleven he took the same sheet down off that board and folded it once along the middle and put it in his own coat, and that about six people in that yard and on that step watched him do it and none of them said anything; that at about twelve he put the same sheet back up on the same board in the same place with the same figure and the same day and the same hour on it, the pin going through the third hole instead of the first; that he gave his reason at about four and not before, being that that board stands about a foot out from the wall behind it and a sheet pinned at the top hole lies back against the plaster and cannot be read from that step; and that nobody in that shed said that the said sheet had been wrong and nobody said that it had been right and nobody said the word mistake; that the keeper wrote the hour at which that sheet went up and the hour at which it came back into the first column of that week and no figure of money at either hour; that the column on that page of days headed WITHDRAWN has stood empty since this run of days began and was not added up on the said day; and that the bell of the Cinder Bell is on the fifth day of being out of service.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the reason was given at about four and not at about eleven is that a reason given at the moment a thing is done is a reason for the doing, and a reason given three hours afterwards is a reason a man has had to stand in front of while about nine people decide whether to believe him, and the second of those is worth more than the first on every board in this harbor; that the reason nobody in that shed used the word mistake is that a page taken off a board by the person who put it there and put back on it before the day is out has not been a mistake and has not been a correction and the two of those words belong to offices and not to boards; that the reason no figure of money was written at either hour is that the money in this yard moves by lines on a fitting-out sheet and a sheet going off a board and on again is not a boat and not a coil and not a man-day, and a yard that writes money against an hour it cannot name the work of has stopped being a yard and started being something else.*
