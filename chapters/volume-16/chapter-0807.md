# Chapter 807

*Wednesday the Fifth Day of October, the Forty-Seventh Day, an Ordinary Working Day With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement, the Correction and the Refusal Both Still on the Wall at That End Stall on Their Thirty-Eighth Day at the Same Size and a Hand's Breadth Apart and Neither of Them Taken Down, Four Heads Over That Wall and Nothing Else on It and the Wall of the Room Over That Chandler's Store Bare Since the Fourteenth of August, the Man of About Thirty-Two at the End of That Mole From About Seven and the Woman of Thirty of No Office at the Flat of That Bench Stone From About Ten until About One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Wednesday the fifth day of October, with that market open from six and a fine morning with the wind in the north and the water slack at eight and the whole of that mole head in view from about seven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday the sixth of October and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the ninth of October, both of which are outside this run of days. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and nothing else in it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Wednesday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twentieth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-seventh day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and fifth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and seventh day of nobody asking him anything, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-fifth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare six hundred and eleventh night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and twenty-ninth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-fourth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood four hundred and sixteenth day. Four hundred and forty-seven days of that man's silence about six names is what this run of days has carried, and about four people in that market could name the page and none of them did. A boy of twenty-two heads the third of those four columns and no figure has ever gone under his hand and his surname is on no page of this harbor. The fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five and has been at that figure since before this run of days began, and it stands open and unruled. Nothing was said about those nine hulls by anybody in that yard today, and they were there about two sea miles off that mole head in the morning and in the afternoon. Nobody in that shed put anything against the left side of that man's head on the three hundred and fifth day, and he is not on the water.**

---

The two sheets at that end stall were on their thirty-eighth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and neither of them has been out in the weather because both of them are under the awning of that stall and always have been. Nobody in this harbor has said which of the two of them is right, and nobody has taken either of them down, and the four heads over that wall in the room over that fish market are claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records, and all four of them have been used and there is nothing else on that wall.

The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August and nothing went up on it today and nothing came down off it. A person standing in that lane on that Wednesday would have seen an empty wall and a bench and a visitor's door and nothing else, and about four people in that market know that there is a rule in the inside of the door of that room and about four do not.

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was the mole-head light turned by hand with a spanner and a cloth and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet, and the cloth went back into the drawer it came out of.

---

The man of about thirty-two was at the end of that mole from about seven with the four new crews and the book of hours, and they were on their seventeenth day and did the morning's work in about two hours and a half under him, which is slower than the man of about twenty-seven and about four of that nine have said so and it is not a finding. The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and sat at the flat of that bench stone with it on her knees and wrote in it the whole of that time.

At about one he came back up that mole and she was still at that stone, and they said eleven words to each other about a hatches and about a boat at about one o'clock in the afternoon with about four people standing within hearing of it. Not one of those eleven words was about the rule and not one of them was about the sheet that went up that coast road on Saturday, and neither of them has written any of them down, and about four of that nine have not repeated any of it and are not going to.

The keeper said out loud to about nine, at about three, that the rule has now been in the inside of that door for five days and that in those five days nobody has asked to have it repaired and nobody has asked to have it taken down, and that a rule nobody has come back to is either a good rule or a rule that was never wanted, and that this yard does not know which of those two it is and is not going to find out.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-seventh day of that run of days, and he has been on that bench every day of it.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirty-third day a step to the left of that refusal and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen with nothing on it moved.

Two hundred and twentieth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the third of those four columns and no figure has ever gone under it, that boy's surname is on no page, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five and stands open and unruled and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and nobody in that yard said a word about them today. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head for the three hundred and fifth day, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the two sheets that are still on that wall or for eleven words said at a bench stone, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it.

NO FIFTH THING IS ON ANY WALL IN THAT HARBOR. NO COST OF ANYTHING WAS ON A PAGE OF THIS YARD TODAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTIETH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the two sheets have been left standing for thirty-eight days without anybody saying which is right is that a person who says it is right is answering for a page belonging to somebody else, and the only person who could answer for it is the one who keeps it and she is four days up a coast road; that the reason the keeper is not going to find out whether that rule is a good one or a rule nobody wanted is that finding out means asking a person who works under it, and this yard does not ask people questions it has no answer to and no standing to use the answer for; that the reason the eleven words at that bench stone are on no page is that a page which printed what two people said to each other over a hatches would be a page about them, and there is no page in this harbor that is about either of them; that the reason a hatches out of the water and a boat are what they said about is that a hatches out of the water and a boat are the two things they both know and the two things they both did this morning, and a person who has worked nine days out of ten with somebody knows what to say to them at one o'clock.*
