# Chapter 777

*Monday the Fifth Day of September, the Seventeenth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-First Charge on That Cistern, Fourteen Trips by Hand Between About Nine and About One, a Woman of About Fifty-Two of the Cinder Bell Saying Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People That Her Community Has Decided to Let That Bell Go for a Fortnight From the Eighth of September to the Twenty-Second, Being Fourteen Days in the Middle of a Working Season, and That the Whole of It Is Paid Out of That Community's Own Page and Not Out of Any Sheet in This Yard, One Mark Going onto the Face of That Page at About Three Before It Goes Down the Road on the Thursday, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ten and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Monday the fifth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a clear bright morning with the wind gone into the north and the sea going down off that bar after the weekend swell, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-first charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand between about nine and about one, and it was walked on by none of them. A fortnight of a bell out of service was settled on that community's own page at about two this afternoon and no figure of it went onto any sheet in this yard. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and the next wage Tuesday is tomorrow. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and the foot of it stands where it stood yesterday at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence went into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight came out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and there has been no tin opened in that shed since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this seventeenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-fifth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and eighty-seventh days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-fifth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and ninety-first night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and ninth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and sixty-fourth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-ninth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-sixth day. And a man of about fifty-one has gone four hundred and twenty-seven days without telling anybody anything about a page of six names. The middle one of those four columns has a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it and has never had anything under it and never had anybody over it, and that boy's surname is on no page and he is off the water. The fourth of those four has gone this quarter and every quarter at one hundred and twenty-five, with no line in it and with no shut mark and no strike on it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head for two hundred and seventy-five days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against that number and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern at the back of that market was charged between about nine and about one for the hundred and eighty-first time, fourteen men and fourteen trips, the water going in at the top and coming out at the bottom into a butt.

The keeper wrote the hundred and eighty-first against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it out, and the next charge of that rota is on Thursday.

---

At about two the woman of about fifty-two was in that yard with about nine people round the flat of that bench stone, and she had said out loud that her community had decided a thing at four o'clock on Friday morning before she left, and that what it had decided was that the bell on that stretch is to be let go.

She said out loud that let go means out of service and not rung, and that it begins on Tuesday the eighth of September and ends on Tuesday the twenty-second of September, being fourteen days.

---

She gave her reason out loud and she gave it once and in the same words twice over because the second time was for about four men at the back who had come in late.

She said out loud that a bell in service answers a signal, and that a bell which answers a signal cannot be compared with anything, and that two places have two figures about that stretch and that the two figures cannot be set against each other while there is a bell on it answering whoever rings it.

Nobody in that yard put a second reason to hers.

---

She said out loud that the whole of it is paid out of the page of the people of that place and out of nothing else, and that there is no figure of it on any sheet in this yard and that she would not give one if it were asked for.

The man of about thirty-two said out loud one sentence, being that a fortnight of a bell out of service in the middle of a working season is a real cost and that he is not going to write it on a sheet that has no column for it, and that a sheet with thirteen lines on it is not going to grow a fourteenth because somebody wants a day in it.

---

A man of about forty-five of that market said out loud at about half past two, to about nine, that a cost with no column in it is a cost that will be denied in three weeks, and that in June this yard had a cost with no column in it and it was denied in three weeks, and that the woman of about fifty-two ought to know that before she goes back up that coast road and not after.

And the woman of about fifty-two said out loud that she knows it, and that her community entered it on Saturday in its own hand before she left, and that it is going up the road with me in the morning with my own name under it as the person who carried it.

---

At about three a man of about forty-five of that market put one mark on the face of that page at the line where the fortnight goes in, above the six hands and below the count of the season, and he gave his reason out loud, being that a page which leaves this harbor with a cost written on it and no mark on it is a page that arrives at the other end with nothing on it that anybody can check.

One mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote it, and it is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries one against the said day.

---

Nothing went onto the face of anything else in that yard, and the man of about thirty-four of the second lay was not in that yard today and nobody sent for him and nobody said anything about where he was.

The keeper wrote the fourteenth into that community's page in that woman's own hand and not in his, and he did not enter it in the first or second column of anything, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

---

About half past five on that Monday nothing was read out loud at that stone until half past five, and when it was it went on for about nine hundred one words, and the cistern was in it at the front and a fortnight of a bell was in the middle and there was no figure of money anywhere in it.

No settlement was made into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, and that foot is where it was yesterday, at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence went into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight came out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight. That slate carries twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and it is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and ninety days without a tin in it, and the last tin in it was opened on the twenty-ninth of April past. The third of those four columns has never had anything under it and never had anybody over it, a boy's hand of twenty-two standing at the head of it, his surname on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four has gone this quarter and every quarter at one hundred and twenty-five, with no line in it and with no shut mark and no strike on it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this evening and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head for two hundred and seventy-five days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against that number and he is not on the water. And the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is never set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the charge or for the fortnight or for the mark, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO OF THE CINDER BELL SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT TWO IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE THAT HER COMMUNITY HAS DECIDED TO LET THAT BELL GO FROM THE EIGHTH OF SEPTEMBER TO THE TWENTY-SECOND, BEING FOURTEEN DAYS IN THE MIDDLE OF A WORKING SEASON, AND THAT THE WHOLE OF IT IS PAID OUT OF THAT COMMUNITY'S OWN PAGE. A FORTNIGHT OF A BELL OUT OF SERVICE IS THE FIRST COST IN THIS RUN OF DAYS THAT IS NOT A FIGURE ON ANYBODY'S SHEET, AND NO FIGURE OF IT WAS SET AGAINST ANYTHING. THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FIRST CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN THIS MORNING. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two the reason she gave her reason twice over and in the same words is that this yard has eleven people out of twenty who cannot read and about four more who can read slowly, and a reason heard once in a yard where half the room is late is a reason that about four men will have against them for the rest of the month; that the reason the man of about thirty-two said one sentence and not two is that the second sentence would have been advice, and advice from that man to a woman four days down a coast road is a thing this coast has mistaken for an offer twice this summer; that at about three the reason that mark went on the line of the fortnight and not on the six hands again is that the six hands have already been answered for by two marks and a third would be counting the same thing, and the fortnight is a new line and a new line is the one a stranger will want to check; and that at about one the reason that charge ran with fourteen men whatever the hour is that a rota which is a list of names is a rota which gets shorter every time a man is ill, and a rota which is a list of trips is a rota which costs the same on a bad morning as on a good one, and the whole of this harbor is built on the second of those.*
