# Chapter 761

*Saturday the Twentieth Day of August, the First Day, a Man of About Forty of the Ashtide Run at the Door of One Room Over a Chandler's Store at About Two in the Afternoon Reading a Board Beside a Copy Instead of the Copy and Going Away Again Without Saying What He Came For, an Ordinary Working Day and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Paper With a Mark on It Standing Unsigned on the Step of That Shed Since About Eleven, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Forty-Four*

Saturday the twentieth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a low grey morning with the wind in the north-east and the sea running short over that bar, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past. A paper with a mark on it came ashore in a boat's boot at about eleven and is standing on the step of that shed with the print side out and has not been read out to anybody and is not read out on this day. The first column takes one entry and the second takes five, and no page of that week was written on this day in a hand that has been used on any other day of this month.**

**Nine counters were carried through the morning here. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and fifty-ninth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-one days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and ninety-ninth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and sixty-fifth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-third days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and thirty-eighth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-third day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventieth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and one days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The third of those columns is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two standing at the head of it, that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor, and he is off the water. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-nine days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lane that runs behind the row of chandler's stores is not a street and has no name on any plan of this harbor, and it is four hundred yards from that quay and four hundred yards is a distance across a harbor and not a distance down a coast.

At about two that afternoon a man of about forty came up it to one door and stood on the step and did not knock, and the door is the door of a witness house, being one room over a chandler's store with a bench in it and a visitor's chair and a copy on the wall.

---

The copy on that wall is the public copy and it is in three pieces tied in oilcloth and it went up there on the thirteenth day of August with four nails at the height a person of about five foot four could read standing.

Beside it is a board of the same size put up the same afternoon in the same hand saying what is not known, and the board is the same size as the copy's own heading and the two of them are the same distance from the floor.

---

He read the board first and read it all the way down, and he read it with his hand not on the rail, and he did not put his hand on the copy at all.

The board says that the manifest of a ferry was altered in the spring of a year not written on the board, that it is not known whose hand altered it, at whose instance or with what intent, that no person has been charged with it and no person has been cleared of it, that the room is not the whole of what is known, and that a person who comes in there is not to take the wall for the coast.

---

He said out loud to nobody in particular that somebody should have to be able to read the copy first, and then the board, and not the other way about, and a chandler of about fifty-eight who was in that store at the time said out loud that it is the same size and the same height and a man with his eyes on the copy never sees the board at all.

And the man of about forty said that a man who has come to argue with a wall does not want the wall to be fair at him, and that he had come to say a thing about the copy and had not said it, and he went down that lane the way he came.

---

The paper on the step of that shed came ashore in the boot of a Crown boat at about eleven and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put it on the step print side up because it was wet, and it has a mark on the foot of it and a line of type along the top and nobody has read it out to anybody and it stood on that step all day in the rain and the print ran a little at the bottom and nobody turned it over.

The keeper came out at about three, looked at it, and said out loud that it would keep until Monday, and that it would keep better on a step than in a hand, and went in again.

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine and the stove drawing by ten and a lighter out of that quay at about half past eleven with four men in her and no coal in her at all.

The glass case in the corner of that shed has four panes in its frame and the front of it is not yet lit at this hour, and the second lay were cutting glass for it until about one and then stopped because the light went off that bench, and about five people of that party cannot read what is on that bench and were working off the marks of a man's hand at the end of the day.

---

At about half past four the man of about thirty-two came in off that quay with a coil of rope over his shoulder and asked the keeper, in front of about four, whether anybody had been up that lane.

The keeper said a man had and had gone, and the man of about thirty-two asked what he wanted, and the keeper said he did not know and had not asked, and the man of about thirty-two said that was right and put the coil down.

---

He cannot hear out of the left side and has not said so to anybody in that shed this month, and there is a figure of money against nothing he has done in fourteen volumes and there is one now.

The woman of thirty of no office was in the room over the fish market until about two and came down the steps to that yard at about half past four, and she asked the same question about the lane and got the same answer, and she wrote the lane and the hour and the man of about forty and the fact that he did not say what he came for into her own book and not into anybody else's.

---

About half past five on that Saturday the yard's own mouth put the day out at that stone, in about eight hundred and forty-two words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had not been in that shed since the Friday before last.

"**The lane behind that row of chandler's stores is not a street and has no name on any plan of this harbor, and it is four hundred yards from this quay. A man of about forty came up it at about two this afternoon to one door and stood on the step and did not knock, and that door is the door of a witness house, being one room over a chandler's store with a bench in it and a visitor's chair and a copy on the wall.**"

"**The copy on that wall is the public copy and it went up there on the thirteenth with four nails at the height a person of about five foot four could read standing. Beside it is a board of the same size in the same hand saying what is not known, and the two of them are the same distance from the floor. He read the board first and read it all the way down and did not put his hand on the copy at all, and a chandler of about fifty-eight who was in that store said out loud that a man with his eyes on the copy never sees the board at all.**"

At about half past four I asked the keeper in front of about four whether anybody had been up that lane, and the keeper said a man had and had gone, and I asked what he wanted, and the keeper said he did not know and had not asked, and I said that was right and put the coil down.**"

"**A paper with a mark on it came ashore in the boot of a Crown boat at about eleven and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put it on the step print side up because it was wet, and it has a mark on the foot of it and a line of type along the top and nobody has read it out to anybody. The glass case in the corner has four panes in its frame and the front of it is not lit at this hour, and the second lay were cutting glass for it until about one and then stopped because the light went off that bench, and about five of that party cannot read what is on that bench and were working off the marks of a man's hand at the end of the day.**"

"**And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is now one hundred and seventy-four days without a tin being opened in it and the last one was on the twenty-ninth of April past. The third of those four columns carries nothing and nobody over it, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor, and he is off the water, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five having taken no line and is neither shut nor struck and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-nine days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the paper or for the lane or for the reading, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that paper with a mark on the foot of it was still on the step print side up.

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY CAME UP A LANE THAT HAS NO NAME ON ANY PLAN OF THIS HARBOR AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON TO THE DOOR OF A WITNESS HOUSE AND READ THE BOARD BESIDE THE COPY INSTEAD OF THE COPY AND WENT AWAY AGAIN WITHOUT SAYING WHAT HE CAME FOR. A PAPER WITH A MARK ON IT CAME ASHORE IN A BOAT'S BOOT AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND IS STANDING UNREAD ON THAT STEP. NOBODY HAS READ IT OUT AND IT WILL KEEP UNTIL MONDAY. THE ORDINARY WORK WENT ON AND WAS NOT STOPPED BY ANY OF IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the twentieth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was put on that cistern and its covers lay on it from nine until four; and that a man of about forty came up the lane behind that row of chandler's stores at about two in the afternoon to the door of a witness house and stood on the step and did not knock; and that he read the board beside the copy all the way down and did not put his hand on the copy, and that a chandler of about fifty-eight said out loud that a man with his eyes on the copy never sees the board at all; and that the man of about forty said a man who has come to argue with a wall does not want the wall to be fair at him, and that he had come to say a thing about the copy and had not said it; and that a paper with a mark on the foot of it came ashore in the boot of a Crown boat at about eleven and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put it on the step print side up because it was wet, and that nobody read it out to anybody and that the keeper said at about three that it would keep until Monday and keep better on a step than in a hand; and that the pump ran clean from about nine and the stove drew by ten and a lighter went out of that quay at about half past eleven with four men in her and no coal in her; and that the second lay were cutting glass for the case until about one and stopped because the light went off that bench; and that at about half past four the man of about thirty-two asked the keeper in front of about four whether anybody had been up that lane and was told a man had and had gone, and asked what he wanted, and was told the keeper did not know and had not asked; and that the woman of thirty of no office asked the same question at about half past four and wrote the lane and the hour and the man of about forty and the fact that he did not say what he came for into her own book and into nobody else's.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two the reason the man of about forty did not say what he came for is that a man who has read a board saying what is not known is a man holding a thing he came without, and a thing said in that condition is a thing said to win an argument and not to get at a figure; that at about three the reason that paper was left on the step and not brought in is that a paper brought into a working room at about three on a Saturday is read out by whoever picks it up, and a paper left on a step is a paper nobody has agreed to be the reader of; that at about half past four the reason the man of about thirty-two did not ask what the man wanted is that a question put to a keeper about a man who has not been named to him is a question about somebody's business, and a keeper who answers it is a keeper keeping a list of who came up that lane; and that the reason the second lay stopped cutting glass at about one is that glass is cut against a light and not against a mark, and about five of that party work off a man's hand and not off a light, and a pane cut off a hand in a low light is a pane with a flaw in it that nobody sees until it is in a frame.
