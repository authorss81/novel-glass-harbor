# Chapter 769

*Sunday the Twenty-Eighth Day of August, the Ninth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-First Time and the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Twenty-Five Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One and Not Whole, Thirty-Eight and Twenty-Five and Thirty-Eight Being One Hundred and One the Whole of It, the Page Coming Off the Wall of the Witness House at About Four in the Afternoon as the Day Printed on the Face of Line Two, a Woman of About Twenty-Nine of the Lowfen Reed Putting a Refusal on the End Stall at About Eleven in Her Own Hand and on Her Own Community's Own Page, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Two and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Sunday the twenty-eighth day of August, with that market shut and the bars down until about six and a flat morning with the swell long and slow outside the bar, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twenty-first time, the wall behind that reeve's bench off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-five minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January and not whole, and thirty-eight and twenty-five and thirty-eight being one hundred and one the whole of it. The page came off the wall of the witness house at about four in the afternoon, which is the day printed on the face of line two, and it went into a bundle and down a lane and not into any room in this harbor. A woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed put a refusal on the end stall at about three in her own hand and on her own community's own page. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and a charge is put on that cistern on Monday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of that Sunday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-seventh day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-nine days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and seventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-third night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninety-first days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-sixth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixty-first day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-eighth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and nine days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. Nothing stands in the third of those columns and no person has been named to it, and at its head is a boy's hand of twenty-two, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of them has stood at one hundred and twenty-five without taking a line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-seven days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare twenty-five minutes with a longer swell running outside the bar than there was on Friday, and the three men who do that work took thirty-eight minutes each way and about a man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority stood at the end of the bare time and looked at the chalk and did not touch it and did not write anything.

Nobody in that harbor stood open from five until twelve and the lane behind that row of chandler's stores was shut with a bar across it from about ten until about one.

---

At about eleven a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed came up that lane for the second time in two days and came to the end stall and not to the shed, and she put a sheet on that stall in her own hand and did not give it to anybody.

The sheet is a refusal. It says that the Lowfen Reed has read a copy of a page in this harbor and has read the correction on the face of it, and that the Lowfen Reed is not able to accept either, and that the reason is not a disagreement about a day of a boat.

The reason is given in two lines and a figure. It is that the Lowfen Reed has one bell on that stretch and the bell is paid for out of a page, and the page carries the lower figure, and the bell was engaged in June on the lower figure, and if the honest figure goes onto that page in October then the bell that was engaged in June was engaged at a price that was not there, and a contract made in June on a page that was wrong in June is a contract that stands.

---

The keeper read that sheet out at about half past three in about nine people, and about five of those nine could not read it, and the woman of about twenty-nine read it out again herself afterwards in the same words and in the same order, which is the practice on that wall.

About four of that nine said out loud that her reason was correct, and about four said nothing, and nobody said she was wrong.

---

The man of about forty of the Ashtide Run was in that yard at about half past three and said out loud that his own page is being corrected and that he signed the correction against himself on the Saturday, and that a man who signs a correction against himself has no business telling another place what it owes.

And the woman of about twenty-nine said out loud that that is the first sensible thing anybody has said to her since Friday, and that it changes nothing on that sheet and will not change anything on it.

---

A man of about twenty-six of that market put three marks on the face of that refusal, one beside the first line of the reason, one beside the figure, and one at the foot, and the reason he gave out loud for three was that a refusal is the one thing on that wall that everybody can agree is there, and that a refusal with one hand on it is a complaint and a refusal with three is a thing a stranger can find out where to stand.

The page of days carries three against the said day and those three are marks and are not approvals and are not signatures and are not people.

---

The page came off the wall of the witness house at about four, the copy and the board and the four nails, and a chandler of about fifty-eight and two of the second lay did it in about twenty minutes, and the copy was rolled with the board inside it and went into a bundle and down that lane to a man who would carry it four days up that road to the Lowfen Reed and nobody in that harbor read it again on the way out.

The woman of about twenty-nine put her own refusal into the same bundle herself and did not let anybody else do it, and said out loud that it goes with the copy because the copy is what it is a refusal of.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud in about eight hundred and sixty-three words at that stone in the yard's own mouth to about nine people, and the page it was about was still standing at the end stall with the morning's sun on the back of it and the sun had gone off it by four.

"**And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four on a Sunday as on every other day, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and eighty-two days without a tin in it. Nothing stands in the third of those four columns and no person has been named to it, and at its head is a boy's hand of twenty-two, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four has stood at one hundred and twenty-five without taking a line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-seven days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the wipe or for the refusal or for the three marks, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE OF THE LOWFEN REED PUT A REFUSAL ON THE END STALL AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN HER OWN HAND AND ON HER OWN COMMUNITY'S OWN PAGE, AND THE REASON IS A BELL ENGAGED IN JUNE ON A FIGURE THAT WAS WRONG IN JUNE, AND A CONTRACT MADE ON A PAGE THAT WAS WRONG IN JUNE STANDS. ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE SAID OUT LOUD THAT HER REASON WAS CORRECT AND NOBODY SAID SHE WAS WRONG. THREE MARKS WENT ONTO THE FACE OF THAT REFUSAL. THE PAGE CAME OFF THAT WALL AT ABOUT FOUR AND WENT FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST ROAD. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-eighth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and the hundred and twenty-first time, the wall behind that reeve's bench being off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-five minutes and back up at about a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being thirty-eight and twenty-five and thirty-eight and one hundred and one the whole of it; and that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and that a charge is put on that cistern on the twenty-ninth; and that a man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority stood at the end of the bare time and looked at the chalk and did not touch it and did not write anything, and that the lane behind that row of chandler's stores was shut with a bar across it from about ten until about one; and that at about eleven a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed put a sheet on the end stall in her own hand and did not give it to anybody, and that the sheet is a refusal and says her community has read a copy of a page in this harbor and has read the correction on the face of it and is not able to accept either; and that the reason is given in two lines and a figure, being one bell on that stretch paid for out of a page, engaged in June on the lower figure, and that a contract made in June on a page that was wrong in June is a contract that stands; and that the keeper read that sheet out at about half past three in about nine people of whom about five could not read it, and that the woman of about twenty-nine read it out again herself in the same words and the same order; and that about four of that nine said out loud that her reason was correct and about four said nothing and nobody said she was wrong; and that the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run said out loud that a man who signs a correction against himself has no business telling another place what it owes, and that she said out loud that that changes nothing on that sheet; and that a man of about twenty-six of that market put three marks on the face of that refusal at about half past three; and that at about four the copy came off the wall of the witness house with the board and the four nails in about twenty minutes and went rolled into a bundle and down that lane to a man carrying it four days up that road, and that the woman of about twenty-nine put her own refusal into the same bundle herself.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason she came to the end stall and not to that shed is that a stall is a place a person may stand without being received, and a shed is a place a person may be answered in, and a woman who has come four days up a road to say no to a page does not want a room; that at about three the reason she read the sheet out again herself after the keeper had read it is that a refusal read by the person it refuses and a refusal read by the person it is refused by are two different documents, and this coast has only ever had the second one; that at about half past three the reason three marks went onto the face of that refusal and not one is that a refusal with one hand on it is a complaint and is answered in a room, and a refusal with three is a thing a stranger can find out where to stand, and the standing is the only part of a refusal anybody else needs; and that at about four the reason that refusal went into the same bundle as the copy is that a copy travelling without the refusal attached to it is a copy on its way to becoming a fact, and this coast has spent fourteen volumes learning that a page which travels alone arrives as a page nobody has refused.
