# Chapter 787

*Thursday the Fifteenth Day of September, the Twenty-Seventh Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-Fourth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine, Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, a Woman of About Twenty-Nine of the Lowfen Reed Coming into That Market at About Nine After Four Days on That Coast Road With Nothing Sent For and Nobody Asked Her to Stay, One Question Asked Once in About Nine People at About Half Past Three and Answered by Nobody, One Mark Going onto the Face of Her Own Page at About One, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Eighth Day of Fourteen, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Thursday the fifteenth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a bright cold morning with the wind gone into the north-east again and the first whitecaps outside that bar by ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-fourth charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand, and it was walked on by none of them, and the keeper wrote the hundred and eighty-fourth against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it. There is no charge on that cistern tomorrow and the next is on the Monday morning. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, so that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-fifth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-seventh day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-fifth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninety-first night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and ninth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-fourth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-ninth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-sixth day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and twenty-seventh day of saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. There is nothing under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and nobody has been set over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. The fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line into it and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-fifth day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern went at about nine and took fourteen men and fourteen trips and was walked on by none of them, and the butt stood short at the end of it and had a skin on it by about one, and nobody drank it and nobody said that it was bad.

A woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed came into that market at about nine on the same morning with a bundle of her own on her back and nothing at all in the bag on her shoulder.

She had set out on the Sunday and had walked that coast road in four days, and nobody sent for her and nobody carried anything for her and nobody asked her to stay, and about four people in that market watched her come in and none of them said anything to her about it.

---

She came into that yard at about half past ten and stood at the back of it and did not sit, and she stayed until about four, and she was not given anything to do and did not ask for anything to do.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay was in it all day and was not asked anything by her either.

She put her own page on the flat of that bench stone at about one and squared it up the way a person squares up a thing that has been carried four days in a bundle, and about six people came and looked at it and nobody read it out.

---

At about one a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark onto the face of that page of hers, against the one line of it that carries the fourteen days, and he gave no reason out loud and nobody asked him for one.

He said afterwards, to about four and not to about nine, that a mark on a page that has come four days to be here is not much of a thing to ask a man to explain, and that he would have said something out loud if it had been anybody else's page.

One mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote it, it is not an approval, it is not a signature, and it is not a person, and the page of days carries one against this day.

---

At about half past three she asked one question in that shed, and she asked it once, and about nine people heard it, and nobody answered it.

She said out loud that a correction that is right, and that makes two places poorer this month, is still owed, and that the method has a rule for refusing a record and no rule at all for refusing a correction, and that nobody in that shed knows who says a correction is owed, and that nobody in that shed knows what a person is owed when the page they have been working from turns out to have been wrong.

Then she stopped, and she did not ask it a second time in those words, and she did not ask it in any other words for the rest of that afternoon.

---

Nobody answered it.

The keeper said out loud that he had heard it and that he was not going to answer it in that shed on a Thursday, and that a man who answers a question like that in a yard of nine has answered it for four hundred people he cannot see.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that the question was a good one and that he had not got an answer to it and that he would not make one up between now and Saturday, and that anybody who wanted to write it down was welcome to write it down on a sheet of his own and not on anything in this yard.

Of that nine, four said nothing whatever at all and two of them went out of the door and stood in the lane.

---

Nobody used her question for anything else that afternoon.

A man of about forty-nine of the west end came in at about four and said out loud that he wanted the foot of that money sheet read to him again and got it read to him again, and he did not mention what she had said, and the foot he asked for was the same foot he has asked for every day this week.

A man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store said out loud that a copy is a copy and that four weeks of arguing has produced nothing anybody can spend, and that if the question she asked has an answer then somebody with a ledger has to go and look for it, and that if it has not got an answer then this harbor is going to be wrong about something it has not thought about yet.

---

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the eighth day of being out of service, and the fourteen days of it run to the twenty-second of September, and nobody in this harbor has rung it and nobody in this harbor has asked anybody else to do without it.

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their eighteenth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart, and a step to the left of the refusal the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirteenth day, and nobody said which of the two of them is right.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said at that stone in about one thousand and eighty-three words, and the charge came first in it, and one question came in the middle of it, and no answer to it came out at that stone.

She said it in her own mouth and in the yard's own hearing, and she said it out loud to that nine and not to one person, and then she stopped, and she did not ask it a second time in those words, and she did not ask it in any other words for the rest of that afternoon. What she asked is not written onto any page of this harbor and is not entered in any column of the page of days and does not go out at that stone.**"

The keeper said out loud that he had heard it and that he was not going to answer it in that shed on a Thursday, and that a man who answers a question like that in a yard of nine has answered it for four hundred people he cannot see. A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that the question was a good one and that he had not got an answer to it and that he would not make one up between now and Saturday. About four of that nine said nothing at all.**"

"**Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard has gone two hundred days without a tin in it. A boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and what is under it is nothing at all, and that boy's surname is on no page and he is off the water, and that fourth column is still at one hundred and twenty-five, still empty of any line, and still neither shut nor struck. That mole head has nine hulls lying about two sea miles off it and it had nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing whatever about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and that is the two hundred and eighty-fifth day of it, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against that figure and he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the charge or for the four days of road or for the question, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it.

A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE OF THE LOWFEN REED ASKED ONE QUESTION IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE AND WAS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND THE QUESTION IS NOT ON ANY PAGE OF THIS HARBOR. ONE MARK WENT ONTO THE FACE OF HER OWN PAGE AT ABOUT ONE AGAINST THE LINE THAT CARRIES THE FOURTEEN DAYS. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the mark went onto her page and not onto a page of this yard's is that a mark is a second hand and the only second hand that had anything to say to that page this week was one that did not need to say it, and a yard that puts its own hand on a page belonging to a place four days up that road has begun to keep that place's record and that is a thing this harbor does not do; that the reason no reason was given out loud at the time is that the reason is that he was ashamed of not having an answer and a man who says that out loud in a yard of nine has said something true and has also lost the mark he might have got on a page next month, and about four people in that shed have known both of those things about him for years; that the reason no answer was given is not in this column and is not on any page of this harbor, and a column of that kind does not exist and this yard has not got one.*
