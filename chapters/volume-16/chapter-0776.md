# Chapter 776

*Sunday the Fourth Day of September, the Sixteenth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Second Time and the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Twenty-Six Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One and Not Whole, Thirty-Nine and Twenty-Six and Thirty-Nine Being One Hundred and Four the Whole of It, Line Three Settled into the Foot of That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Four With the Four Parts Read Before the Sum Went Down and the Foot Read After It at One Hundred and Twenty-Five Thousand Five Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence Out, an Ordinary Working Day Otherwise and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Nineteen*

Sunday the fourth day of September, with that market shut and the bars down until about six and a grey morning with the wind gone back into the west and a long swell outside the bar that came in on the flood and put water over the slip about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twenty-second time, the wall behind that reeve's bench off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-six minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being thirty-nine and twenty-six and thirty-nine and one hundred and four the whole of it. Line three settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four and the four parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down, and the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of the Monday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and there has been no tin opened in that shed since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this sixteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and seventy-fourth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and seventy-sixth days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fourteenth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and eightieth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninety-eighth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and fifty-third day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixty-eighth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighty-fifth day. Four hundred and sixteenth days have gone with a man of about fifty-one not saying one word to anybody about a page of six names. Nothing is entered under the middle of those four columns and nobody is set over it; a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of those four has still taken no line and still stands at one hundred and twenty-five and is still neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. It is two hundred and seventy-four days today since a man of about twenty-nine heard anything out of the left side of his head, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare twenty-six minutes, and the three men who do that work took thirty-nine minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than they took last Sunday and which nobody in this harbor has said why.

A man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority stood at the end of the bare time and looked at the chalk and did not touch it and did not write anything on anything.

No shop in that harbor stood open from five until twelve, and the lane behind that row of chandler's stores was barred across from about ten until about one.

---

The water came over that slip about eleven and went off it about half past twelve and the floor of that shed was wet at about one and stayed damp until about four.

The yard did the ordinary work of a Sunday and that work was cutting line against a gauge with the door open on the damp and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet, because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line.

---

At about four line three settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet, and the four parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down.

Two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence. Coal and oil for two days is forty pence. Ninety yards of line at three pence the yard is ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence. Three men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day is three times two times sixteen for ninety-six pence. And three hundred and thirty-six and forty and two hundred and seventy and ninety-six is seven hundred and forty-two pence, being four marks, eight shillings and sixpence.

One hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve and seven hundred and forty-two is one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four, and the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and not before it.

---

The foot stands at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

About five of that nine could not read those two figures off that sheet and the keeper read them out loud at the end of it in about half a minute, and nobody wrote them down in that shed and they went onto no slip.

---

That leaf from the Cinder Bell was still standing at the end stall where it went on Saturday, with the sun gone off the back of it about two and the six hands on it not legible after that.

Nobody took it down, and the correction and the refusal were beside it at the same size, and the three of them stood in a row on that board and were not read out that day and were not in that shed.

---

About half past five on that Sunday, with that market shut and the floor of that shed damp and about nine people standing rather than sitting, the day was said at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and ninety-five words, and the wipe came first in it and the settlement came last and nothing was read out twice.

"**The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare twenty-six minutes, and the three men who do that work took thirty-nine minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than they took last Sunday and which nobody in this harbor has said why. A man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority stood at the end of the bare time and looked at the chalk and did not touch it and did not write anything on anything. Nobody in that harbor stood open from five until twelve.**"

"**The water came over that slip about eleven and went off it about half past twelve and the floor of that shed was wet at about one and stayed damp until about four. The yard did the ordinary work of a Sunday and that work was cutting line against a gauge with the door open on the damp, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet, because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line.**"

"**At about four line three settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and the four parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down. Two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence. Coal and oil for two days is forty pence. Ninety yards of line at three pence the yard is ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence. Three men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day is three times two times sixteen for ninety-six pence. And three hundred and thirty-six and forty and two hundred and seventy and ninety-six is seven hundred and forty-two pence, being four marks, eight shillings and sixpence. One hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve and seven hundred and forty-two is one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four, and the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and not before it.**"

"**The foot stands at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. About five of that nine could not read those two figures off that sheet and the keeper read them out loud at the end of it in about half a minute, and nobody wrote them down in that shed and they went onto no slip.**"

"**And the rest of it. That leaf from the Cinder Bell was still standing at the end stall where it went on Saturday, with the sun gone off the back of it about two and the six hands on it not legible after that, and nobody took it down, and the correction and the refusal were beside it at the same size and the three of them stood in a row on that board and were not read out that day and were not in that shed. The sum of that line went down into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at four o'clock, and the foot of that sheet now reads one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten on a Sunday as on every other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight. Under that slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. That shed has now gone one hundred and eighty-nine days without a tin in it. The third of those four columns has nothing entered under it and nobody set over it, a boy's hand of twenty-two being at the head of it, his surname on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four has still taken no line and still stands at one hundred and twenty-five and is still neither shut nor struck. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off that mole head this evening where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and nothing about their easing has been said in this harbor. It is two hundred and seventy-four days today since a man of about twenty-nine heard anything out of the left side of his head, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water. And the hearing that came back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the wipe or for the settlement, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on.

**THE WALL BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND WENT UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE AND STOOD BARE TWENTY-SIX MINUTES, THIRTY-NINE AND TWENTY-SIX AND THIRTY-NINE BEING ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR THE WHOLE OF IT, AND THE LADDER IS A MINUTE LONGER EACH TIME AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. LINE THREE SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR AND THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET WAS READ AFTER THE SETTLEMENT AND STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN PENCE SHORT. THE THREE SHEETS AT THAT END STALL HAVE NOT BEEN TAKEN DOWN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the fourth day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and the hundred and twenty-second time, the wall behind that reeve's bench being off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-six minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being thirty-nine and twenty-six and thirty-nine and one hundred and four the whole of it; that the three men who do that work took thirty-nine minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than they took on the Sunday before, and that nobody in this harbor has said why; that a man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority stood at the end of the bare time and looked at the chalk and did not touch it, and that nobody in that harbor stood open from five until twelve; that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday; that the water came over that slip about eleven and went off it about half past twelve and the floor of that shed was wet at about one and stayed damp until about four, and that the yard did the ordinary work of a Sunday and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet, because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line; that at about four line three settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and the four parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down; that those four parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, ninety yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence, and three men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at three times two times sixteen for ninety-six pence, the sum being seven hundred and forty-two pence, being four marks, eight shillings and sixpence; and that one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve and seven hundred and forty-two is one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four, and that the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and not before it and stands at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that about five of that nine could not read those two figures off that sheet and the keeper read them out loud at the end of it, and nobody wrote them down in that shed; and that that leaf from the Cinder Bell was still standing at the end stall with the sun gone off the back of it about two and the six hands on it not legible after that, and that nobody took it down, and that the correction and the refusal were beside it at the same size and the three of them stood in a row on that board.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about four the reason the foot of that sheet was read after the sum went down and not before it is that a total read before the sum goes down is a number this yard can be asked about all afternoon and has not yet paid for anything, and this coast has lost three arguments to that order of reading and has won none of them; that at about half past four the reason those two figures were read out loud to about five people who could not read them is that a figure nobody in the room can see has not been published, and publication on this coast is not a printing, it is a man saying it out loud with four other men in hearing; that at about one the reason no line went onto that sheet on the Sunday is that a boat hired on a Sunday and settled on a Tuesday is a line whose two days are both days a man can remember doing nothing, and this yard has one line of that kind on that sheet from May that it cannot now account for; and that at about two the reason nobody took that leaf down off the end stall when the sun went off the back of it is that a leaf which has been unreadable since two o'clock is the ordinary condition of every board in this market after two o'clock, and taking a page down because the light has gone off it is taking it down because a man did not get to it in time.*
