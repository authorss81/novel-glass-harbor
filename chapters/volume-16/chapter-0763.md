# Chapter 763

*Monday the Twenty-Second Day of August, the Third Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Seventh Charge on That Cistern, Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, a Chandler of About Fifty-Eight Saying Out Loud at About Ten That He Copied the Middle Copy From a Copy and That the Middle Copy Is in a Room Over a Fish Market in This Harbor, a Second Mark Going onto the Face of the Page of That Week at About Two, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Monday the twenty-second day of August, with that market open from about nine and a fine hard morning with the wind still in the west and the tide at the turn about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and seventy-seventh charge, fourteen trips by hand, and it was walked on by none of it. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge is put on that cistern tomorrow. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried through this Monday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-first day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-three days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and first day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and sixty-seventh night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-fifth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and fortieth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-fifth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-second day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and three days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The third of those columns has a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it and has never had anything entered under it, nobody having been appointed to it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-one days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern at the back of that market was charged on the Monday between about nine and about one and it took fourteen men and fourteen trips and the water went in at the top and came out at the bottom into a butt, and the last of them walked the whole of the way back with the empty can in his hand and not one of the fourteen looked at the gauge.

The keeper wrote the hundred and seventy-seventh against the day on the rota slate at about one and did not read it out.

---

A chandler of about fifty-eight came into that yard at about ten with a fathom of line he had bought on the Saturday and wanted to know why the yard had not answered him on it, and the keeper gave him his figure and he paid it and stood there after he had paid it.

And he said out loud, to about four, that he had been in that room over the fish market in March with a page to copy, and that a page came to him and then the page went on, because the page was not the one to copy, the one to copy was in that room, and what he copied was off the one that went on.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard at about ten and heard that, and did not say anything for about a minute, and then said out loud that she would go up and see.

And the man of about thirty-two said out loud, before she had gone, that nobody goes up into that room on the strength of a chandler's account of what he saw in March, and that if a page in that room is a copy of a copy then that is a thing to find out and not a thing to be told.

---

She went up at about eleven with about four other people and came down at about half past one, and the door of that room is shut and there is a table in it and a copying book on the table, and there is one page in that book with a number on it that a man of about fifty-eight of that market wrote out in March.

A copying book is a book of things that have been copied and the price of them is a penny a sheet and the man who keeps it is a trade and not an office and anybody may bring him a page and get a copy of it and nobody may look in the book itself.

---

What she brought down was the page, and a sheet of newsprint to put it on, and the newsprint is that shop's own and it is the width of the page and it is not folded.

The page is one leaf and it is a leaf of the ratified record and it is headed with the name of a place and with the name of a route and it carries what one crossing of that route cost, in four parts, and the four parts are on the face of it and the total is at the foot of it.

---

The man of about forty of the Ashtide Run was in that yard at about two when the page was laid out, and he took out of his coat the back of his own book with the date of that June in it, and did not open it further, and said out loud that he had a copy of that page himself and that his was given to him in April by a man who has since gone four days down that coast road.

And the man of about thirty-two said out loud that the copy in his hand is of a copy of a page and that a copy of a copy is the ordinary condition of this coast and that nobody is in trouble for it yet.

---

The paper on the step of that shed was still there at about two and it was read out at about three to about eleven people of whom about six could not read it, by the keeper, in the yard's own mouth, and it says that a treaty has been signed between the Dominion and the coastal communities, that it ends the custody of unclaimed vessels by the Crown, and that a schedule goes with it and the schedule is at the circuit office and may be had there.

Nobody in that yard said a word about it when it was read out and the keeper put it back on the step print side out and it was still standing on that step at six.

---

At about two a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put a second mark on the face of that page, being the mark of a man whose own hand is not the hand that wrote the four parts, and the reason he gave out loud was that a page that has been read out in a working place has been in two mouths and not one and that a page that has been in one mouth has not been checked at all.

That mark is the first of the fifty days of this run with a figure against it on the page of days, and it is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person.

---

About half past five on that Monday the whole of the day went out loud in the yard's own mouth at that stone, in about eight hundred and ninety-seven words, to about nine, and one of the nine was a man who has counted words on a sheet in this shed in June and had not come back into it since.

And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks. This yard is one hundred and seventy-six days without a tin in it. The third of those four columns has nothing in it and nobody over it and the hand at the head of it is a boy's of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-one days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the charge or for the page or for the mark, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it.

---

**A CHANDLER OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT TEN THAT HE COPIED THE MIDDLE COPY OFF A COPY THAT WENT ON, AND THE MIDDLE COPY IS A LEAF IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET IN THIS HARBOR, AND IT CARRY WHAT ONE CROSSING OF ONE ROUTE COST IN FOUR PARTS WITH THE TOTAL AT THE FOOT OF IT. THE TREATY WAS READ OUT ONCE AT ABOUT THREE AND NOBODY SAID A WORD ABOUT IT AND IT IS STILL ON THAT STEP. A SECOND HAND WENT ONTO THE FACE OF THAT PAGE AT ABOUT TWO AND IT IS NOT AN APPROVAL AND IT IS NOT A PERSON. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten the reason the chandler of about fifty-eight stood there after he had paid is that a man who has been given a thing and has not been given the use of it will stand about four minutes after he has paid and will say it out loud to whoever is nearest, and that whoever is nearest was four people and one of them kept the record; that at about eleven the reason the man of about thirty-two said nobody goes up on the strength of a chandler's account is that a room is a room and a man's memory of a room from four months back is a man's memory, and a page taken out of a room on the strength of a memory is a page nobody can put back the way it was; and that at about two the reason a second mark went on that page and not a signature is that a signature says a man stands behind the figure and a mark says a man has looked at it, and the two are different things and a stranger with a pencil can only check the second one; and that at about three the reason nobody said a word about the treaty is that a paper read out in a working place on a Monday afternoon is a thing about four people will remember and a thing about fifteen will not, and this yard has learned that the hard way and has written the fact down.
