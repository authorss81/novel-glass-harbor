# Chapter 788

*Friday the Sixteenth Day of September, the Twenty-Eighth Day, an Ordinary Working Day and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Two Pages of This Harbor's Own Coming Down off That End Stall Board on One Morning at About Ten and One on One Afternoon at About Two by Two Different Men Who Put Them Up Themselves, One Mark Going onto the Face of a Sheet That Goes out of That Yard on the Evening Boat at About Five, the Woman of About Twenty-Nine of the Lowfen Reed Still in That Yard and Asked Nothing, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Ninth Day of Fourteen, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Friday the sixteenth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a mild morning with the wind gone into the west and the water inside that mole flat and grey, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day and not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge goes on that cistern until the morning of Monday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, and that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and first days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-sixth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-eighth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-sixth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninety-second night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and tenth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-fifth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eightieth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-seventh day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and twenty-eighth day of saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. The head of the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and nothing has ever been entered under it and nobody has ever been set over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. That fourth column is at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no line and has not been shut and has not been struck. Nine hulls are lying off that mole head at about two sea miles, where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about their easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-sixth day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

Two pages of this harbor's own came down off that end stall board on one day, and they came down at about ten and about two, and they were put up by two different men and they were taken down by those same two men.

Nobody in that yard said the word mistake at any point on this day, and nobody in that yard said the word corrected either, and about four of that nine noticed both of those absences.

---

The first of the two was a sheet of the man of about forty-nine of the west end, and he had put it up himself on the Thursday morning at about nine, and it was one of the three sheets of his own with the three headings on it in his hand, being coal, and oil, and line.

He took it down at about ten, folded it once along the middle, put it inside his coat against the other two, and did not put it back up at any hour of that day.

He said out loud to about nine that he had a letter on Thursday morning offering him coal at a figure under the one on that sheet, and that the sheet was his yard's business and not a notice of this harbor, and that a notice of this harbor is not a place for a man to put his coal on.

---

The second of the two was a sheet of the man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store, and he had put it up himself at about ten that morning, at the right-hand end of that board and below the three sheets that have stood there since the twenty-ninth of August.

He took it down at about two and put it back up at about three through the first hole, with the same figure on it and the same day and the same hour on it.

He gave his reason out loud at about three and said that he had stood at that end stall for an hour at about eleven that morning with a man of about forty of the Ashtide Run in front of it and had read the wrong line on it, and that a page a man reads wrong in front of a witness is not a page, it is a rumour with a nail in it.

---

The man of about forty-nine of the west end said out loud at about half past three that a sheet taken down and not put back is not the same thing as a sheet taken down and put back in an hour, and that he was not going to say out loud in that yard what the difference was because he did not know, and that there is a column on the page of days headed WITHDRAWN and that it has stood empty since this run of days began and that he had not asked what it counts.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that neither of the two of them was a mistake and that he was the wrong man to ask and that about half the yard had noticed both of them and had said nothing.

---

At about five the keeper made a sheet for the harbor authority on the west basin and put it in a folded paper against the tide, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark on the face of it before it went.

He put it against the day and the hour and not against any figure, and he said out loud that a figure of nothing goes on a page about nothing, and that if that sheet comes back with a hand on it then the two of them can be compared and if it comes back without one then it was a sheet and not a notice.

A mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote it, it is not an approval, it is not a signature, it is not a person, and the page of days carries one against this day.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed was in that yard from about ten until about two and was not asked one question about Thursday and said nothing about Thursday at any hour of that day.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud, at about one and to about four and not to her, that whatever she asked on that Thursday is on nobody's page and is going onto nobody's column, and that the column headed WITHDRAWN does not carry questions and does not carry answers and carries pages.

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the ninth day of being out of service and nobody in this harbor has rung it.

---

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their nineteenth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its fourteenth day a step to the left of the refusal on the same board at the same height, and neither of the three has been taken down at any hour since the one that went down and came back on Monday.

---

Nobody was thanked for either page or for the letter on the coal or for the sheet that went out on the evening boat, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**TWO PAGES CAME DOWN OFF THAT BOARD ON ONE DAY AND WERE PUT UP BY THE TWO MEN WHO TOOK THEM DOWN. ONE OF THEM WENT BACK UP IN AN HOUR AND ONE OF THEM WENT INSIDE A COAT AND DID NOT COME BACK, AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD SAID THE WORD MISTAKE AND NOBODY SAID THE WORD CORRECTED. THE COLUMN HEADED WITHDRAWN WAS NOT ADDED UP TODAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that at about ten a man of about forty-nine of the west end took down off that end stall board one of the three sheets of his own that he had put up himself on the Thursday at about nine, folded it once along the middle, put it inside his coat against the other two, and did not put it back up at any hour of the said day, and gave his reason out loud to about nine; that at about ten a man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store put up a sheet of his own at the right-hand end of that board and below the three sheets that have stood there since the twenty-ninth of August; that at about two that man took that sheet down off that board and at about three put it back up through the first hole with the same figure and the same day and the same hour on it, and gave his reason out loud; that the said two men put those two pages up themselves and took them down themselves; that nobody in that shed said the word mistake on the said day and nobody said the word corrected; that at about five the keeper made a sheet for the harbor authority on the west basin and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark on the face of it against the day and the hour and not against any figure, and that one mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote it and is not an approval and is not a signature and is not a person, and that the page of days carries one against the said day; and that the correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their nineteenth day and the leaf from the Cinder Bell on its fourteenth day, and that none of those three has been taken down since the one that went down and came back on Monday.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason one of those two sheets went back up in an hour and the other did not go back up at all is not written down anywhere in this harbor and was asked for out loud by the man who took the second one down and was not answered by anybody, and that two men doing two different things with two sheets of their own on one board in one afternoon is what a board is for and is not a fault of either of them; that the reason the mark went against the day and the hour and not against a figure is that a sheet about a day and an hour is the only kind of page in this harbor that a man can be asked to agree with without agreeing to a number, and this harbor has four heads on a wall and no column anywhere for a person's opinion; that the reason nobody in that shed asked that woman anything about the day before is that a question asked twice is a question a man has answered twice, and about four people in that yard worked out in under a minute on the Friday who was going to ask her and neither of them did.*
