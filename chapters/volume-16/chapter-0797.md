# Chapter 797

*Sunday the Twenty-Fifth Day of September, the Thirty-Seventh Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Fifth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Twenty-Nine Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One Not Whole, Forty-Two and Twenty-Nine and Forty-Two Being One Hundred and Thirteen the Whole of It, a Minute Longer Each Way Again and Nobody in This Harbor Has Said Why, the Sheet at That End Stall Going from Fourteen Gaps to Fifteen at About Two, no Charge and No Wage Movement, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Sunday the twenty-fifth day of September, with that market shut and the bars down until about six and a fine morning with the wind in the north-east and a cold edge on the water at eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twenty-fifth time, that wall behind that reeve's bench off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-nine minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being forty-two and twenty-nine and forty-two and one hundred and thirteen the whole of it. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and there is no charge on that cistern until the morning of Monday and that is the first charge in the days after this wipe. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and tenth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-seventh day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past has now reached its four hundred and ninety-fifth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-seventh day of being nobody's question. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-fifth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and first night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and nineteenth day and no figure of money is set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and sixth day. Four hundred and thirty-seven days have now passed with a man of about fifty-one saying nothing whatever to anybody about a page of six names. Under the head of the middle of those four columns, being a boy's hand of twenty-two, there has never been anything whatever entered and nobody has ever been set above it, and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor and he is off the water. One hundred and twenty-five is what the fourth of those four stands at, and there is no line in it, and it is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls still lie about two sea miles off that mole head, where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing at all out of the left side of his head and that is the two hundred and ninety-fifth day of that, and no figure is set against that figure and he is not on the water.**

That wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare twenty-nine minutes, and the three men who do that work took forty-two minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than last Sunday, and nobody in this harbor has said why and nobody said why this morning either. The three men who do that work go out with the barrow at about ten and come back with the barrow and put it against the same wall they took it off, and there is a place on that wall where the new render has run down onto the floor in a place the size of a hand and nobody has scraped it up.

The plaster behind it showed a rectangle of a different colour and the second man wiped his hand across the bottom of the new render before he stepped down, and the wind had got into that slip in the night and the water came over it about two and the floor of that shed was wet by about three. It is the ninth year that wall has been taken off and put back and the floor under it has been wet more times in that nine years than in the twenty before it, and nobody has put a figure on any of that because nobody asked for one.

---

A man of about thirty-eight of that landing stood at the bare wall through the whole of the twenty-nine minutes and said out loud to about four, on the fourteenth time he has said it, that it is a minute longer each way again and that he has asked twice this year and been told nothing. He stood there with his hands behind his back for the whole of it and he did not say it once and then stop, he said it at about eleven and again at about twelve, and the second time there were about five people instead of about four.

The keeper said out loud that the ladder is a uniform three minutes and that a ladder gets longer the way a man gets slower and that nobody in this harbor is going to write down which of those two it is, because the minute is a minute and not a figure of money. He said that he has asked twice this year and been told nothing twice and that he is not going to be the man who asks a third time in a market where everybody can hear him ask, and that is the last of what he said about it on that Sunday.

---

At about two the sheet at that end stall was ruled a line against that Sunday and nothing was entered against the line, and that sheet has now fifteen gaps in it where it had fourteen, and it had carried fourteen since it was ruled. The line went on with a rule and not with a pen and it is the only line on that sheet that has been drawn with a rule, and the hand that drew it is the hand of a woman of about thirty-four of that market and she has not said why she was at that stall at about two on a Sunday.

Nobody in that market said why and about four people looked at it and nobody put a figure against it, and that is the whole of the fifteenth gap and it is not a count of anything and it is not entered in any column of the page of days. There are three people in this market who could give a reason for it and all three of them have given a different one to about four other people in the last two years, and none of those three has ever been asked to put either of them on a page.

---

About nine people stood in that shed rather than sat in it and the ordinary work of that Sunday was a length of line being cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line. There is no tide worth the name in that yard on that Sunday and the sea was flat from about eight, and that flatness is the ordinary condition of a market that opens at six and shuts at six and is not a harbour that works on a tide.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the forty-seventh day of this run of days on which that has happened, and about half of that nine have stopped counting it and about half have not. That man of about thirty-four has been on that bench every day of this run since the first and has not once looked at the fifteen gaps on the sheet at that end stall, and nobody in this yard has ever wondered out loud whether he can read it.

---

The four new crews came in at about eleven on that Sunday and did the afternoon's work in about two hours under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about four with the tally slate and the book of hours and was given nothing else to do. They came in through that door in ones and twos and stood against the back wall until somebody moved aside for them, which is what a man does on a Sunday in a shed where he has been given nothing to do all morning.

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-eighth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twenty-third day a step to the left, and nobody in that shed said which of the two of them is right. The wrong figure is still showing underneath the correction on both of them and it has been showing underneath for twenty-eight days, and about four people in this market have read that particular figure and worked out what it changes and about four have not, and neither of those four has ever said which of the two of them is which.

Nobody was thanked for the wipe or for the fifteen gaps or for the two hours the man of about twenty-seven gave those four crews, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.
