# Chapter 806

*Tuesday the Fourth Day of October, the Forty-Sixth Day, the Hundred and First Wage Tuesday and the Seventh and Last of the Seven in This Run of Days, That Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes Shut from Ten until Four, a Further Week Fallen Due at the Close of the Day and the Next Wage Tuesday Outside This Run of Days, Eleven Hands on Four Sheets That Left Buildings in That Harbor, no Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Tuesday the fourth day of October, with that market open from six and a grey morning with the wind gone round to the north-east and no rain in it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The hundred and first wage Tuesday of this run and the seventh and last of the seven in it, and that column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten this morning in four minutes and stands exactly where it stood yesterday, being twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks. The chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and a further week is fallen due at the close of this day. The next wage Tuesday is the eleventh of October and is outside this run of fifty days and this yard keeps no page of anything outside this run of fifty days. Not a rota day and not a wipe, and there is no charge on that cistern until the morning of Thursday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and nineteenth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and fourth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and sixth day of not being anybody's question, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-fourth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and tenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and twenty-eighth day and no figure of money is set against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-third day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and fifteenth day. He is on the four hundred and forty-sixth day, and the page of six names was in the same room as him for four hours that morning and neither of them was looked at. Under the head of that boy's hand of twenty-two there is still nothing, and it was still nothing at the close of that Tuesday, and he is not on the water for the seventh time. That fourth column was read past like the others and stood at one hundred and twenty-five afterwards, and nobody shut it and nobody struck it. About two sea miles off that mole head nine hulls are lying and nothing whatever was said about them in that shed on the last wage Tuesday of this run. The hearing in the left side of the man of about twenty-nine is on the three hundred and fourth day and is not improved by a wage Tuesday, and no figure of money is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

That column was read out standing with the lid up against his chest and it took four minutes and the fourth minute is the one in which he gets to the end of the twenty-sixth line and does not move his mouth, and that fourth minute has been the same length on every Tuesday of this run. He read it as a man reads out the weather and not as a man asking anybody for anything, and the chest stayed shut behind him from ten until four and nobody knocked on it and nobody asked who keeps the key, and there is no page anywhere in this harbor that says who keeps the key.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of that. The four new crews were on their sixteenth day and did the afternoon's work in about two hours under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that afternoon, and a wage Tuesday is the day a harbor finds out what it owes and the finding out is the thing that travels. The sheet of hours for that week went up the coast road, the sheet carrying the four minutes of that column went to the room over that fish market, the own page of the Cinder Bell went back up that road to that community, and a notice of that morning went up on a board at that end stall in a keeper's hand, and every one of those eleven hands is the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

The notice of the hundred and first went up on the board at that end stall at about eleven in a keeper's hand, and that board stands out in the market lane with the correction and the refusal and the leaf from the Cinder Bell already on it, and it is not the plaster inside that room over that fish market which takes the four heads, and nothing a person pins to that board turns it into a fifth kind of thing on any wall. It says that the column was read out in four minutes and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and that a further week is fallen due. About four people in that market stood in front of it and about five could not follow it, and nobody took it down during the whole of that day and nobody will take it down before the next Tuesday, which is outside this run.

Nobody has sent for that rule and nobody has asked for it to be taken down. About four people in that market have asked to read it and about four have not, and the keeper reads it out standing at the door of that room to anybody who asks and asks nobody to sign it and nobody has signed it. About four of that nine have said out loud in the last three days that a rule with nobody's name on it is not worth the paper, and the keeper said out loud, to about nine, that a rule that decided anything would not be worth the paper either and that the whole of what it does is make a person write down what they mean when they refuse something.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about four with the page of routes in his hand and the tally slate in front of him, and the woman of thirty of no office was in the room over that fish market from about ten until about two and came down those stairs at two and went up that coast road at about three. Neither of them said one word to the other that day and neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them did the other's work and neither of them has surrendered an inch of it.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-sixth day of that run of days, that run having begun on Wednesday the tenth day of August.

The two sheets at that end stall were on their thirty-seventh day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirty-second day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen. Nothing went up on the wall of the room over that chandler's store and nothing came down off it, and there is nothing else on the four heads in the room over that fish market, which are claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records.

Two hundred and nineteenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. Nobody was thanked for the hundredth and first wage Tuesday or for the four minutes it takes to read out what this harbor owes, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason no figure of money went onto that sheet on a day when the finding out of an owing is the loudest thing in a harbor is that the fitting-out sheet is a record of what this yard has done and has not settled, and what a harbor owes is not the same thing as what a yard has spent; that the reason nobody has asked for the rule to be repaired since it was made is that the only person who has said out loud that it needs repairing came four days down a road, said it once and went home, and a rule that is repaired by whoever happens to be in the room is not a repair and is a different rule every week; that the reason nobody asked who keeps the key to that chest is that a key is a thing a person looks after and asking about it puts it in a mouth, and this yard has not put one thing in a mouth in fifty days that it did not have to; that the reason the fourth minute has never once been a different length is that the twenty-sixth line has been the same line for nine weeks, and a man reading the same line nine weeks running stops being able to hurry it, which is not a figure of money and is not on any page.*
