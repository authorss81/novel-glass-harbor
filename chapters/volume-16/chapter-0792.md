# Chapter 792

*Tuesday the Twentieth Day of September, the Thirty-Second Day, the Ninety-Ninth Wage Tuesday, That Column Read at About Ten in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, the Chest with the Twenty Envelopes Shut from Ten until Four, Four New Crews in That Shed All Day and None of Them on That Column, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Thirteenth Day of Fourteen, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Tuesday the twentieth day of September, with that market open from six and a flat calm and the glass falling all morning and rising again after two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wage Tuesday, the ninety-ninth of this run and the fifth of the seven in it, that column on the lid of that step read out at about ten in four minutes and standing at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and not moved, the chest with the twenty envelopes in it shut from ten until four, and a further week fallen due at the close of this day. Not a rota day and not a wipe, and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Tuesday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters stand at the close of this thirty-second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now reached its four hundred and ninetieth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-second day of a chest nobody has opened, and it stays shut. Those two objects nobody owns on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirtieth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has stood bare five hundred and ninety-sixth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fourteenth day and no figure of money is set against either. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and first day. A man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and thirty-second day of not saying one word to anybody about a page of six names. Nothing stands under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and no page of this harbor carries that boy's surname, and he is off the water for the seventh time and has been asked nothing about it. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five with nothing entered against it, and it has not been closed and it has not been struck. There are nine hulls lying about two sea miles off that mole head and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said here. It is the two hundred and ninetieth day that a man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head, no figure is set against that day, and he is not on the water.**

---

That column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes by the man who keeps the leaf and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and a further week was fallen due at the close of that day. He read it standing on the step with one hand on the lid and he turned the lid back when he had finished, and the four minutes were as long as they have ever been.

About four of that nine stood up and listened to those four minutes and the other five went on with what they were doing, and that has been the ordinary of a wage Tuesday in this harbor for as long as anybody in that shed can remember. The man of about thirty-four of that market, who has a berth of his own and is owed nothing on that column, counted the twenty-six lines with his finger on his own apron while it was read, and got as far as the eleventh line and stopped counting there.

---

The four new crews were in that shed all day and not one of the ten of them is a line on that column and nobody put one of them there.

The keeper said out loud, to about nine, that a wage column is a column of work already done and owed for, and that a man who has been in a yard two days has not done work already done, and that the four of those crews will come onto that column on whatever day the work they have done is owed for and not before, and that this is not a favour to them and is not a slight on them.

---

Those four crews were put in front of the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay at about two in the afternoon and did the two hours of work they had not managed on the Monday, and about four of that nine watched them do it and said nothing about the man of about thirty-two, who was at the far end of that shed with a coil of line. He turned the coil twice in the whole of it and neither time did he look up.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was a length of sounding line being marked off in fathoms against that board and the tally slate lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it from about ten until about four.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about one and was not asked anything by anybody, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay was in it all day and was not asked anything by anybody either. They were at opposite ends of the same shed for three hours on the same morning and neither of them went out of the door while the other was in it.

---

At about three a woman of about thirty-four of that market came to the door of that shed and stood at it and did not come in, and she said out loud to about four that she had come to hear the column read and had heard it from the lane, and that she was going to say the number of Wednesdays again on the next one and was not going to say anything else.

Nobody asked her anything and she went away at once, and nothing was written down about it. She had a shawl over her arm and she put it on at the door before she said any of it, in the manner of a person who has decided what she is going to say before she arrives at the place where she says it.

---

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the thirteenth day of being out of service and the fourteen days of it run to the twenty-second of September, and nobody in this harbor has rung it.

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-third day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its eighteenth day a step to the left, and the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at that end stall at fourteen.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and eighty-four words, and the column came first in it and the four minutes came first in that.

"**That fitting-out sheet took nothing into its foot today, and that foot reads one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and fifth days is how long that shed has gone without a tin standing open in it. There is a boy's hand of twenty-two over the middle of those four columns with nothing whatever under it, that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line into it, and is neither shut nor struck. Off that mole head, about two sea miles out, nine hulls are lying where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing in the left side of his head for the two hundred and ninetieth day, a day on top of the one before, and no figure is set against that number, and he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the four minutes or for the four crews being left off that column, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

---

**THE NINETY-NINTH WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND WENT AND THAT COLUMN STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DID NOT MOVE, AND A FURTHER WEEK IS FALLEN DUE. FOUR NEW CREWS STOOD IN THAT SHED ALL DAY AND NONE OF THEM IS A LINE ON IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the reading of that column takes four minutes and is not shortened is that the four minutes are the finding out and a harbor that has shortened the finding out has stopped finding out; that the reason those four crews are not on that column is that a column is a debt and a debt is a thing somebody is owed, and writing a man onto it before the work is done makes the column a thing this yard promises and not a thing it can be checked on; that the reason the woman of about thirty-four was not asked anything at that door is that a person who has said a thing three times and been answered three times by a date is owed the fourth time on the day it falls and not before, and this yard has no standing to take a thing away from her that she has not offered; that the reason the four minutes were not read out a second time is that this yard does not read a page aloud twice, and the one time is in the yard and not in the lane.*
