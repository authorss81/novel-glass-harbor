# Chapter 809

*Friday the Seventh Day of October, the Forty-Ninth Day, an Ordinary Working Friday With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement, Eleven Hands on Four Sheets That Left Buildings in That Harbor, a Man of About Forty-Three of That Market Asking at About Three When the Column of the Second Hand Will Be Added Up and a Keeper Saying Out Loud That It Is Added Up Once and Not Before the Last Day, the Correction and the Refusal Both on the Wall at That End Stall on Their Fortieth Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Friday the seventh day of October, with that market open from six and a fine morning with the wind dropping away to nothing after eight and the bar quiet by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Friday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and there is no charge on that cistern at all inside this run of days now and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the ninth of October, outside it. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence being all that has ever gone into it, and that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Friday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-second days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and seventh day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and ninth day of nobody asking him anything, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-seventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirteenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-first day and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-sixth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and first day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and eighteenth day. Nothing was said to him about the six names on the four hundred and forty-ninth day and he said nothing about them, as he has on every day of this run. The middle column of those four is empty under a boy's hand of twenty-two as it has been every day of this run, and this harbor is not the office that would put him back on the water. The fourth of those four still stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no line against it and nothing struck, and there is a hundred-and-twenty-sixth line nobody has drawn. Nine hulls off that point, nine hulls in January, and no figure of them and no figure of what came off them is on any page of this run of fifty days. That man of about twenty-nine heard nothing at all out of the left side of his head for the three hundred and seventh day today, and he is not on the water.**

---

That Friday's ordinary work was three boats off that mole before noon with the bar quiet, and a hatches scrubbed down and a coil of rope stowed, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against any of that. The four new crews were on their nineteenth day and did the day's work in about two hours and a quarter under the man of about twenty-five of the second lay, who stood in for the man of about twenty-seven that day because the man of about twenty-seven is four miles up that coast road, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about four with the page of routes and the tally slate and wrote four lines in the book of hours.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that afternoon, being the sheet of hours for that week, the own page of the Cinder Bell going back up that coast road, a sheet of the costs of one community being off one shared stretch going to a third room, and the sheet of that market's own, and every one of those eleven hands is the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries eleven against this day and this yard does not add them together.

---

At about three a man of about forty-three of that market asked the keeper in about nine, out loud and in front of everybody, when the column of the second hand would be added up. The keeper said out loud that it is added up once and not before the last day of this run of days, and that a sum taken day by day in a shed is an argument, and that a sum taken once at the end of a run is a figure a stranger can take off a page with a pencil.

He said out loud that a man who wants to check a figure with a pencil can come back on the Saturday, read it off the page of days himself, and write it in his own book, and that this yard will not say it out loud first in case a man who has come to check a figure is given one instead of the page. About four of that nine said that was fair and about four said nothing, and nobody in that shed put the figure in a mouth, and the man of about forty-three wrote nothing down and came back at six and asked for nothing.

The rule has been in the inside of that door for eight days now and nobody has asked to have it repaired, nobody has asked to have it taken down, and nobody has asked for a copy of it. The keeper said out loud at about three that he expects all three of those to be true of a rule made on a Friday in about nine people and that he does not know of one that is not, and that a drawer in the corner of that room is not a place a thing is kept because somebody wants it looked at.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that market at that end stall from about ten until about twelve with a book of her own and not a page of that yard, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed and was not in that lane, and neither of them saw the other one and neither of them signed the other's page.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-ninth day of that run of days, that run having begun on Wednesday the tenth day of August.

---

The two sheets at that end stall were on their fortieth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirty-fifth day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen with nothing on it moved. About four people in that market have read both of those sheets every day since the twenty-ninth of August and about four have not, and the two groups have been in that lane for forty days without either of them saying a word to the other about either of them.

The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on it and nothing came down off it, and there is nothing else on the four heads in the room over that fish market, which are claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records.

---

Nobody was thanked for a question asked in about nine people or for the answer to it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

NO REMAINDER FOR ANYBODY WAS PRINTED ON ANY PAGE TODAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SECOND DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**
