# Chapter 799

*Tuesday the Twenty-Seventh Day of September, the Thirty-Ninth Day, the Hundredth Wage Tuesday, That Column Read at About Ten in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, the Chest with the Twenty Envelopes Shut from Ten until Four, the Cost of the Cinder Bell Being Off That Shared Stretch on the Page as a Bell Out of Service at Fourteen Days and Six Pence a Day and a Boat's Hire Lost and a Figure of Money, Every One of the Three in Chalk and Not One of Them a Line on That Fitting-Out Sheet, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of September, with that market open from six and rain in from the south-west from about two and the ground in that yard soft to the door, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The hundredth wage Tuesday of this run and the sixth of the seven in it, and that column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes this morning and stands exactly where it stood yesterday, being twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and a further week is fallen due at the close of this day. This day is not a rota day and is not a wipe, and nothing went into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and not one line of these ten days was made on it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Tuesday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twelfth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters stand at the close of this thirty-ninth day. Nothing has been said by a man of about fifty-one about a page of six names for four hundred and thirty-nine days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past has now reached its four hundred and ninety-seventh day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-ninth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects nobody owns on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-seventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump has stood bare six hundred and third night running. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and twenty-first day and no figure of money is set against either. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-first day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and eighth day. A boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns, and what is under that head is nothing, and nobody has been set over it, and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor and he is off the water. The fourth of those four takes no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and it has not been shut and it has not been struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls lying, and nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said here. A man of about twenty-nine is on the two hundred and ninety-seventh day of hearing nothing out of the left side of his head, no figure is set against that figure, and he is not on the water.**

That column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and a further week was fallen due at the close of that day. He read it standing with the lid up against his chest, and the fourth minute is the one in which he gets to the end of the twenty-sixth line and does not move his mouth, and that fourth minute has been the same length every Tuesday of this run.

This is the hundredth wage Tuesday of this run and there is one of them left in it, being the fourth of October. The one after that is the fourth day of October, and after that one there is no date in this run of fifty days and this yard does not know whether there is a date, and it is not the yard's business to know.

---

At about one the keeper read out in that shed the three figures of what the Cinder Bell being off that shared stretch has cost, and every one of the three is in chalk on somebody else's board and not one of them is a line on that fitting-out sheet. He wrote the three of them on the back of a sheet that had been used and turned it round so that the yard could see them, and about four of that nine could add and about four could not, and neither of those four said anything about it while he was standing there with a piece of chalk.

The first of the three is the boat's hire lost. That shared passage is one boat for one day at the figure in chalk, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and the money of it was divided between the two places that use that stretch, so that each of them stood at eighty-four pence, being seven shillings, and one hundred and sixty-eight divided between two is eighty-four. From the twenty-seventh the Saltmarch takes that boat alone, one boat for one day at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, against eighty-four pence before it, and the difference between those two figures is eighty-four pence, being seven shillings. The Cinder Bell hires a boat of its own for that same stretch on that same day at the same figure in chalk, one boat for one day at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, against eighty-four pence before it, and the difference there is eighty-four pence, being seven shillings. He said that the figure of one hundred and sixty-eight pence has been on that chalk in three places since the spring and that nobody has ever come down that coast road to argue with it, and that is the whole of the standing of a number that a stranger can check with a pencil.

---

The second of the three is a bell out of service. The bell of the Cinder Bell was not rung on that stretch from the morning of the twenty-seventh, because that bell is rung to call that boat onto that stretch and there is now no boat of that community coming onto it, and that community's own page carries fourteen days of that bell at six pence a day, being fourteen times six for eighty-four pence, being seven shillings. The bell of that community is rung by a man who is paid six pence a day for it out of that community's own page, and that is not on any page of this harbor and has never been and is not going to be.

About two people in that market still want the other fortnight entered at fourteen days and about two still want it entered at twenty-one, and neither of them has said the other of them is wrong, and nobody in that shed compared this figure with that one and nobody settled that argument. About two people in that market want it at fourteen and about two want it at twenty-one and both of those pairs have had their own figure in their own heads for two seasons, and there is no third person in this shed who could settle it and nobody asked for one.

---

The third of the three is the whole of it, and the whole of it is three lots of eighty-four pence, being seven shillings, which is two hundred and fifty-two pence, being one mark, seven shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and sixty-eight added to eighty-four, being two hundred and fifty-two. He said it three times, which is twice more than a figure needs, and he said it the same way each time, and about four of that nine could see that the three figures were the whole of it and about four could not, and neither of those four wrote anything down.

That figure is written in chalk on two boards in two places and on the page of the Cinder Bell, and it is not on that fitting-out sheet and it did not go into the foot of it, and that foot stands where it stood yesterday. Two of those boards are in two rooms a hundred yards apart and the third is four miles up a coast road, and a person in this harbor could walk to all three of them in a morning and has not, and is not going to, and there is no figure anywhere in this yard for the doing of it.

---

One mark went onto the face of the sheet carrying those three figures at about half past three, in the hand of a man of about thirty-one of the second lay, against the first of the three and not against the second and not against the third, and the page of days carries one against this day. He made that mark with the same stub of pencil a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay used on the Monday before and neither of them has ever asked the other where it came from and it is the same stub.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about eleven until about three and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the forty-ninth day of this run of days on which that has happened. That man has not asked this yard for a figure since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard has not offered him one, and there is no arrangement between them of any kind, and neither of them would call the other an arrangement.

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason those three figures are in chalk and not on that sheet is that this yard's sheet carries what this yard has done and not what it means to do, and the money of it was spent by two places and by one community and this harbor is not paying any part of it; that the reason the whole of the three is put down as a sum is that a person who has lost two lots of seven shillings and a fortnight of a bell at sixpence a day has lost two hundred and fifty-two pence, being one mark, seven shillings and eightpence, and that a sum somebody can be told is the difference between a cost and an annoyance; that the reason nobody in that shed compared the fourteen days on that community's page with the fourteen days on the other bill is that the two are for two different things and putting them side by side would be a way of settling by arithmetic an argument that has to be settled by two people saying a thing out loud; and that the reason nobody argued about the withdrawal yesterday is that the three figures read out today are the argument, and an argument that is made of money is one a person can look at instead of one a person has to have.*
