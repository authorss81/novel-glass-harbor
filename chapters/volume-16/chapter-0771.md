# Chapter 771

*Tuesday the Thirtieth Day of August, the Eleventh Day, a Man of About Thirty-Eight of That Landing Coming Up That Coast Road in the Middle of the Morning With a Bundle of Oiled Cloth and Eleven Sheets Tied With a String in It and the Fourth of Those Sheets a List, Two Marks Going onto the Face of the Top Sheet at About Two in the Afternoon, the Ninety-Sixth Wage Tuesday and the First of the Seven in This Run of Days, an Ordinary Working Day Otherwise and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Four and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Tuesday the thirtieth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a soft morning with the wind come round into the south-west and a haze standing on the water that did not go off it until about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The ninety-sixth wage Tuesday and the first of the seven in this run of days, and the next one is the sixth of September. Nothing is put on that cistern before Thursday and no wipe comes before Sunday. Eleven sheets came up that coast road inside oiled cloth at about eleven in the morning and the fourth of them is a list, and the head of the top one says in as many words that what is inside is a copy and that the copy is the thing a harbor works from. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, so that foot stands where it stood on Monday at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out, being seven hundred and eighty marks and one shilling, and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, being seven hundred and thirteen marks, seven shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and nothing has been taken out of a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this eleventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-ninth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and seventy-first days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and ninth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-fifth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninety-third days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-eighth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixty-third day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eightieth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and eleventh days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The middle of those four columns stands empty with nobody set over it; a boy's hand of twenty-two is written at the head of it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of the four stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line into it, and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-nine days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The man of about thirty-eight of that landing came up that coast road and into that yard at about eleven with a bundle of oiled cloth under one arm, and he had been four days on the road with it and had not slept in a room with it.

He put the bundle down on the flat of that bench stone and unwrapped it himself, and inside the cloth there were eleven sheets tied with a string through two holes in the top one, and he cut the string and put it in his own pocket and did not throw it down.

---

The head of that top sheet carries four printed lines and they are the whole of the printing on it. The first says that what is inside is a copy. The second says that the copy was made at a place on this coast in the month of June and that where a harbor works from a copy the copy is what it works from. The third says that the binding is of eleven sheets and that the list is the fourth of them counted from the top. The fourth is at the foot of that sheet, and it carries a seal with a name written under it.

Nobody in that yard read that name out. The keeper laid his thumb flat on the line beneath it, took it off again, and wrote nothing about it on the page of that week.

---

At about ten the column on the lid of that step was read out in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and it stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and it does not move.

The chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and about two people came to the door of that office in that hour and one of the two went away again.

A further week stands fallen due at the close of that day and would fall due again on the sixth, and nothing was paid and nothing was advanced and nothing was cut back.

---

The man of about thirty-eight said out loud, to about four, that the man who put the bundle into his hands on the Monday had said out loud that nobody can send anybody anywhere with a piece of paper, and that a copy is the thing a harbor works from, and that a harbor wanting the other one will be sitting a long time and will not be doing anything else while it sits.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard at about half past eleven and said out loud that a thing nobody can be sent for is a thing that has to be worked from as it stands, and that she would rather have eleven sheets in a drawer in this shed than not have them.

---

The man of about thirty-two said out loud that he was not going to have the inside of that bundle read out in a shed with nine people standing in it at eleven in the morning, and that a list of that kind is read once in a working place and is not read a second time anywhere afterwards.

Nothing was read out of it that day. The keeper wrote the four printed lines from the head of that top sheet onto the page of that week in his own hand and wrote nothing else, and the eleven sheets went into a drawer in that shed with the string laid in beside them.

That drawer is not the drawer on a hill and it is not on any page, and it is not the drawer in that room over a fish market, and nobody in that yard opened it again before the bell.

---

At about two two marks went onto the face of that top sheet, one above the other and neither of them near the foot of it.

The first is a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and he gave his reason out loud, being that a sheet which has come into this harbor and been looked at by nobody is a sheet that will be described out of memory in a month, and that memory is a poor copy of anything.

The second is a man of about forty-five of that market and he gave his reason out loud, being that he put his hand on the head of the thing and not on the list, because the head is the part that says what the thing is, and if the head is wrong everything printed under it is a list of nothing.

Two marks are two hands that are not the hand that printed it. A mark is not an approval and a mark is not a signature and a mark is not a person, and the page of days carries two against the said day.

---

The wall of that room over a chandler's store stood empty all day with the nail-holes in it, and the board that hangs beside that wall stood empty from the same hour, and both have stood empty since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on either of them and nothing was said about either of them and neither is on any page.

That shed did its ordinary work and nobody in it touched that fitting-out sheet, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day was given out at that stone a piece at a time, in about one thousand and fifty-nine words, and the piece about the bundle was the longest of the pieces and nobody in that shed had heard any part of it before, and about six of that party could not read the four printed lines at the head of that top sheet and had them given out instead.

"**The man of about thirty-eight of that landing came up that coast road and into this yard at about eleven with a bundle of oiled cloth under one arm, and he had been four days on the road with it. He put it on the flat of that bench stone and unwrapped it himself, and inside there were eleven sheets tied with a string through two holes in the top one, and he cut the string and put it in his own pocket.**"

"**And before any of that, at about ten the column on the lid of that step was read out in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and does not move, the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and about two people came to the door of that office in that hour and one of the two went away again, and a further week stands fallen due at the close of that day.**"

"**And the rest of it. The wall of that room over a chandler's store stood empty all day and the board beside it stood empty from the same hour, and both have stood empty since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on either and nothing was said about either and neither is on any page. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today. That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight. Under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and eighty-four days without a tin in it. The third of those four columns carries nothing and has nobody over it and a boy's hand of twenty-two stands at the head of it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line into it, and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and nothing has been said in this harbor about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-nine days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water. And the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the bundle or for the two marks on it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN FOUR MINUTES AND STANDS AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DOES NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT WAS SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR. ELEVEN SHEETS CAME UP THAT COAST ROAD IN OILED CLOTH AND THE HEAD OF THE TOP ONE SAYS THAT WHAT IS INSIDE IS A COPY AND THAT THE COPY IS WHAT A HARBOR WORKS FROM. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF IT ON THE DAY IT ARRIVED AND THE NAME UNDER THE SEAL AT ITS FOOT WAS READ BY NOBODY. TWO MARKS WENT ONTO THE FACE OF THAT TOP SHEET AT ABOUT TWO AGAINST THE HEAD OF IT AND NOT AGAINST THE LIST. THE WALL OF THAT WITNESS HOUSE STOOD EMPTY ALL DAY AND NOTHING WENT UP ON IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the thirtieth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that a man of about thirty-eight of that landing came up that coast road with a bundle of oiled cloth and was in that yard at about eleven, having been four days on the road with it; that inside the cloth were eleven sheets tied with a string through two holes in the top one, and that the string was cut and kept and not thrown down; that the head of the top sheet carries four printed lines and they are the whole of the printing on it, the first saying that what is inside is a copy, the second saying that the copy was made at a place on this coast in June and that where a harbor works from a copy the copy is what it works from, the third saying that the binding is of eleven sheets and that the list is the fourth of them from the top, and the fourth being at the foot of that sheet and carrying a seal with a name written under it; that nobody in that yard read that name out, and that the keeper laid a thumb on the line beneath it and wrote nothing about it on that page; that the man of about thirty-eight said out loud, to about four, that the man who gave him the bundle on the Monday said out loud that nobody can send anybody anywhere with a piece of paper and that a copy is the thing a harbor works from; that the woman of thirty of no office said out loud that a thing nobody can be sent for is a thing that has to be worked from as it stands; that the man of about thirty-two said out loud that he was not going to have the inside of that bundle read out in that shed with nine people in it at eleven in the morning and that a list of that kind is read once in a working place and is not read a second time anywhere afterwards; that nothing was read out of it on that day, that the keeper wrote those four printed lines onto that page in his own hand and nothing else, and that the eleven sheets went into a drawer in that shed with the string laid in beside them, and that nobody in that yard opened that drawer again before the bell; that at about two two marks went onto the face of that top sheet, one above the other and neither of them near the foot of it, one of them a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and the other a man of about forty-five of that market, the first for a sheet nobody in that harbor has looked at and the second against the head of the thing and not against the list; and that two marks are two hands and not the hand that printed it, and that a mark is not an approval and a mark is not a signature and a mark is not a person; and that the wall of that room over a chandler's store stood empty all day and the board beside it stood empty from the same hour, both having stood empty since the fourteenth of August, and that nothing went up on either of them and neither is on any page; and that the said day was the ninety-sixth wage Tuesday and the first of the seven in this run of days and was not a rota day and was not a wipe; that the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and does not move; that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and that about two people came to the door of that office in that hour and one of the two went away again; that a further week stands fallen due at the close of the said day and would fall due again on the sixth, and that the column did not move and nothing was paid and nothing was advanced and nothing was cut back; and that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the eleven sheets were put in a drawer in that shed and not on the table is that a thing nobody can be sent for stops travelling the moment it stops and has to be found in the same room next morning, and this yard has lost four days of work in June to a sheet that was on a table on a Friday and in a drawer on a Monday; that at about two the reason both marks went onto the head of that sheet and neither went near the foot is that the head is the only part of it anybody in a working place will be asked about, and a mark put against a list says the man has read eleven sheets and a mark put against a head says the man has read one line and knows what kind of thing it is; that at about eleven the reason nothing was read out of it on the day it came is that a bundle opened in front of nine people at eleven in the morning is read out badly in the morning and read out worse in the afternoon, and a list of what is unclaimed is a list where the order of the lines decides which line a man looks at first; and that at about eleven the reason nothing was hung on the wall of that witness house is that there is nothing to hang on it that anybody here has a right to hang, and an empty wall is a true thing about that room this month and would be a false one in October.*
