# Chapter 783

*Sunday the Eleventh Day of September, the Twenty-Third Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Third Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Twenty-Seven Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One at the Angle It Has Held Since the Twelfth of January and Not Whole, Forty and Twenty-Seven and Forty Being One Hundred and Seven the Whole of It and a Minute Longer Each Way Than Last Sunday and Nobody in This Harbor Having Said Why, Two Marks Going onto the Face of One Copy of the Page of the Crossing at About Two, the Correction and the Refusal on Their Fourteenth Day at the End Stall and the Leaf from the Cinder Bell on Its Ninth, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixteen and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Sunday the eleventh day of September, with that market shut and the bars down until about six and a morning with a low sun in the east that came over the roof of that shed at about eight and made the whole of the west wall white, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twenty-third time, that wall behind that reeve's bench off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-seven minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being forty and twenty-seven and forty and one hundred and seven the whole of it. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is one hundred and ninety-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried into the close of this twenty-third day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past has now reached its four hundred and eighty-first day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-third day of being nobody's question. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-first day. The bench under that pump is on the five hundred and eighty-seventh night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifth day and no figure of money is set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixtieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the three hundred and ninety-second day. Four hundred and twenty-third days have gone by with a man of about fifty-one not saying one word to anybody about a page of six names. There has never been a figure under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and nobody has ever been named over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. The fourth of those four has taken no line into it and stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has been neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and nothing has been said about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and that is the two hundred and eighty-first day of it, one more than the day before, no figure set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare twenty-seven minutes, and the three men who do that work took forty minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than they took last Sunday, and nobody in this harbor has said why.

The plaster behind it showed a rectangle of a different colour and the second man wiped his hand across the bottom of the new render before he stepped down, the same as he does, and the whole lane smelled of it for about two hours after the bar went up.

---

The lane behind that row of chandler's stores was barred across with a length of batten and a nail at each end from about ten until about one, and nothing whatever was written on it, and there has been a bar across that lane at every one of the bare times this summer and there was one at every one of them this year.

A man of about twenty-six of the harbor authority stood at the end of the bare time with his hands behind him and looked at the chalk and did not touch it and wrote nothing on anything.

---

The yard did the ordinary work of a Sunday, and about nine people stood in it rather than sat in it, because the floor was cold and the sun had gone off the west wall by about one.

The work was cutting line against a gauge with the door propped and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet, and a man of about forty-nine of the west end came in at about half past one and said out loud, to about nine, that it was a minute longer each way again, and that it had been a minute longer each way on the two Sundays before this one, and that he would like to know what a minute a week is coming to by Christmas, and that nobody had ever told him and he had asked twice.

About four of that nine said nothing at all and about four said out loud that a man who mends a wall is not a man who keeps a figure, and that if the minute is going somewhere it is going into the wall and not into a book, and that was the whole of the answer and it was not an answer.

---

The correction and the refusal were standing at the end stall on their fourteenth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure showing underneath the correction and not struck out and a line drawn round it, and nobody said which of the two of them is right.

A step to the left of the refusal, on the same board and at the same height, the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its ninth day, and its own fold had gone deeper than it was when it went up on the Saturday, and nobody in this harbor has said which of the two season counts printed at the feet of those two sheets is the season of the stretch they are both standing on.

---

At about two a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark onto the face of a copy of the page of the crossing, and at about a quarter past two a man of about forty-nine of the west end put one more onto the same face, and both marks went against the fourth of the four lines and neither went against the three words at the top.

They were two marks and two hands and neither of them is the hand that wrote that page, and it is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries two against this day.

A man of about forty-nine gave his reason out loud and said that a line is bought by the yard and thirty yards came out of a hundred and twenty bought in June for three crossings, and that ten yards to a crossing is a division this coast has used for two hundred years and that a division which has been used for two hundred years is not made false by being written on a page which asks a different question.

---

In the room over that fish market the four heads on that wall were all in use before two o'clock, being claims in the first place and corrections put up in public in the second and refusals in the third and restricted records in the fourth.

About four claims came in on paper over the month and about four went up. About two corrections were put up in that place in the month and one of those two was the one at the end stall. About four refusals stood on that wall in the month and three of them were the ones this harbor knows about. And the fourth of those four heads took about nine sheets into a drawer in that room and gave a receipt for them with the day on it.

Nothing was added to that wall on account of anything else that happened this month, and those four heads are four, and no person in that room has the standing to take anything off that wall.

---

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the fourth day of being out of service, and on the fourth day of it a woman of about twenty-seven of the Ashtide Run came into that market and asked out loud, in about six people, whether the Ashtide Run has lost its signal while that bell stands, and was told out loud by the keeper that a signal on a bell is that route's own page and not this harbor's, and that this harbor has not got a column for it and is not going to make one.

She said out loud that she had not come to have anything and had come to have said that she had asked, and that is the whole of what a person can do on a Sunday in a market.

---

that the wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare twenty-seven minutes and went back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, the three men who do that work taking forty minutes each way, being one minute longer each way than the Sunday before, and that nobody in this harbor has said why; that at about two a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark onto the face of a copy of the page of the crossing against the fourth of the four lines, and that at about a quarter past two a man of about forty-nine of the west end put one more onto the same face against the same line and not against the three words at the top, and that one mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote the page and is not an approval and is not a signature and is not a person, and that the page of days carries two against the said day; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the minute was put into that reading in the middle of a wipe is that a wipe is the only day in seven on which three men stand in front of that wall and count, and a figure nobody will say out loud on six days out of seven gets said on the seventh by somebody who did not mean to say it; that the reason about four of that nine said nothing when the minute was asked about is that a man who answers a question like that in a yard of nine is making a finding, and no man in that yard has the standing to make a finding about a wall he does not mend; that the reason those two marks went against the fourth line and not against the three words is that a mark against the three words would be a mark saying that the page asked the wrong question, which is a thing four people in that shed believe and which nobody in that shed can prove with the page in front of them, and a mark against a line is a thing that can be proved in a minute.*
