# Chapter 780

*Thursday the Eighth Day of September, the Twentieth Day and the Last of This Run, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-Second Charge on That Cistern, Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, Line Four Settled into the Foot of That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Four With the Five Parts Read Before the Sum Went Down and the Foot Read After It, Line Five Going onto That Sheet at About Two Carrying the Thursday on Its Face and the Tenth Day of September on Its Face for the Day It Settles, Two Figures in Two Places' Own Hands Printed a Step Apart on the Same Board and the Difference Between Them, Being Seven Days, Stated Out Loud at About Half Past Five by a Man of About Thirty-Nine Off a Boat That Was In for Stone, Two Marks Going onto the Face of a Sheet of Three Figures Pinned Beside Those Two at the Same Size at About Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirteen and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Thursday the eighth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a rising wind in the north-east and a short hard sea that put spray over that mole before eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-second charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand, and it was walked on by none of them. Line four settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four, the five parts being read with their multiplications before the sum went down, and the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and ninety-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks, twelve shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and fifteen thousand seven hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-three marks, six shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Line five went onto that sheet at about two carrying the Thursday as the day the work was done and the tenth day of September as the day it settles, and it has not settled and is owed to a day outside this run. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe. A hundred and sixty-eight pence goes into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight comes out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is one hundred and ninety-third days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twentieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and seventy-eighth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and eightieth days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and eighteenth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and eighty-fourth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and second days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and fifty-seventh day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-second day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighty-ninth day. A man of about fifty-one has now said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for four hundred and twentieth days. The third of those four columns is empty and unappointed and carries nothing at all under the head of it, where a boy's hand of twenty-two stands, his surname being on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five exactly as it stood on the first day of this run, with no line taken into it and neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and seventy-eight days today, one more than the day before, and no figure stands against that figure and he is not on the water.**

The cistern went at about nine and took fourteen men and fourteen trips, and it was walked on by none of them, and the keeper wrote the hundred and eighty-second against the day on that rota slate at about one and did not read it, and that rota has now run one hundred and eighty-two times without a charge being put off it and without one being put on twice.

---

At about two the keeper wrote the fifth line onto that fitting-out sheet and both days went onto the face of it, the Thursday as the day the work was done and the tenth day of September as the day it settles.

He said both of those days out loud in about nine people before he wrote any figure, and the second of the two is a day that has not happened yet and he said so out loud as well.

Two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence. Coal and oil for two days is forty pence. Thirty yards of line at three pence the yard is thirty times three for ninety pence. A case and four nails at thirty pence is thirty pence. Six men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day is six times two times sixteen for one hundred and ninety-two pence.

Three hundred and thirty-six and forty and ninety and thirty and one hundred and ninety-two is six hundred and eighty-eight pence, being four marks and four shillings, and that sum is not in the foot of that sheet tonight and is not in it until the Saturday.

---

At about four line four settled into the foot of that sheet, the five parts being read with their multiplications before the sum went down.

Three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence. Coal and oil for three days is sixty pence. Four panes of sheet glass at forty-eight pence is forty-eight pence. Two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence is two times eighteen for thirty-six pence. Four men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day is four times three times sixteen for one hundred and ninety-two pence. And five hundred and four and sixty and forty-eight and thirty-six and one hundred and ninety-two is eight hundred and forty pence, being five marks, three shillings and fourpence.

One hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four and eight hundred and forty is one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and ninety-four, and the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement.

---

A man of about thirty-nine came into that market on the Monday off a boat that was in for stone and was in that yard on the Thursday at about ten and had a pencil in his coat and nothing else in the yard worth having.

He had been standing at that end stall for about half an hour with his back to it, and at about half past five he turned round and put the back of a bill on the ledge at that end stall and wrote three figures on it and read them out loud to about eleven people.

The three figures are two hundred and thirty-eight, two hundred and thirty-one, and seven. The first is at the foot of the leaf from the Cinder Bell and the second is at the foot of the refusal, and the third is the difference between them and he wrote nothing above the two that gave it.

Then he said out loud, once, that two hundred and thirty-eight over fourteen comes out at seventeen and that two hundred and thirty-one over fourteen comes out at sixteen and a half, so that a day is worth more of the shorter of those two seasons than it is of the longer one, and that he did not know which of the two places was right and that he was not going to find out because he goes on the tide tonight.

He was gone by six and his name is on no page of this harbor.

---

About two people in that yard wanted the fortnight of that bell entered at fourteen days and about two wanted it entered at twenty-one, and neither of them said out loud that the other of them was wrong.

A man of about forty-five of that market said out loud that the season is the season of the place the fortnight belongs to and that a fortnight is fourteen days whatever a stranger's arithmetic says.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that the page of days in this yard will be asked in a month why two printed figures a step apart on one board do not agree, and that a stranger with a pencil has already answered that question and the answer is on a bill going out on the tide tonight.

---

The keeper made a sheet at about five with the three figures on it in his own hand and pinned it beside those two at the end stall at the same size and at the same height, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and a man of about forty-five of that market put a mark each on the face of it before it was pinned, against the seven and not against the other two.

Two marks are two hands and not the hand that wrote it, and the page of days carries two against this day. That column carried a figure on six of the ten days of this run and the four days it stands bare on are nowhere on it, and those ten days are not added together here and are not added together in this movement, and the whole of that column is added up once in this run of fifty days and on no day before it.

---

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud at about ten past six that the column of the withdrawn carries nothing against any of these ten days and nowhere on it, and that a page belonging to a place four days up a coast road does not become a notice of this harbor by standing on this harbor's board for six days, and that nobody took anything down off that board this week and nothing came off that wall in the room over the fish market and the four heads on that wall are still four.

The correction and the refusal were still standing at the end stall at the same size and a hand's breadth apart on their eleventh day with the wrong figure showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and nobody said which of the two is right, and this run of ten days closed.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past five the reason a stranger wrote the difference on the back of a bill is that a man who has a pencil and two minutes is a cheaper clerk than any office four days up a coast road, and a figure written by a stranger with no stake in either of the two places is the one figure on that board this year that nobody in this yard can be accused of having made up; that at about half past five the reason nothing was written above the two figures that gave the seven is that a bill with a heading is a bill that belongs to somebody, and a bill with three figures on it and no heading belongs to whoever picks it up, and that is the whole of the difference between a notice and a sum; that at about two the reason the Saturday was said out loud as a day that has not happened yet is that this yard has twice been wrong about a day printed on a line before it arrived, and both times it was wrong by being right on the Friday and wrong on the Monday, and a yard that names the day it does not yet know about cannot be caught doing that a second time; and that at about ten past six the reason nothing came off that end stall this week is that a board in a market is not a locker and a thing left on it for six days is a thing that has been left there, and this yard has taken two sheets down this month for being wrong and has not once taken one down for being inconvenient, and it is going to go on being the first of those and not the second.*
