# Chapter 770

*Monday the Twenty-Ninth Day of August, the Tenth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Ninth Charge on That Cistern and the Last of This Run, Fourteen Trips by Hand, Line Two Settled into the Foot of That Sheet at About Four With the Five Parts Read Before the Sum Went Down and the Foot Read After It, the Correction and the Refusal Going Up at the End Stall at About Three at the Same Size With the Wrong Figure Left Showing Underneath the Correction, the Count of the Copies Out Taken Once Off that Page of Days at About Half Past Four and Standing at Thirteen, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Three and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Monday the twenty-ninth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a rising wind in the south-west and white water on the mole at about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and seventy-ninth charge, fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of it, and it is the last charge on that rota before the run breaks and the next one is on a Thursday three days after the twenty-ninth. Line two settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four, the five parts being read with their multiplications before the sum went down, and the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short. The correction and the refusal went up at the end stall at about three at the same size, and the wrong figure was left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this tenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-eighth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and seventy days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and eighth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-fourth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninety-second days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-seventh day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixty-second day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-ninth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and ten days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The third of those four columns carries nothing and has nobody over it, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-eight days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern went at about nine in the rain and it took nine men instead of fourteen because four of them were on the mole with a line, and the keeper wrote the hundred and seventy-ninth against the day and did not shorten the figure for the four men who were elsewhere.

That is the practice on that rota and no figure of it is set against the four men.

---

The correction went up at the end stall at about three and the refusal went up beside it at about three, and the two sheets are the same size of paper and the two are at the same height on that board and the two are the same distance apart as the two sheets in the market behind them, being a hand's breadth.

On the correction is one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, one hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, the ninth day of June, the name of the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run under the total, and underneath the correction the wrong figure, not struck out, with a line drawn round it and a hand's writing beside it saying that the wrong figure is left showing.

On the refusal is a bell engaged in June on a lower figure, and a contract made on a page that was wrong in June is a contract that stands, in the hand of a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed, and three marks on the face of it.

---

The keeper read both sheets out at about three in about eleven people of whom about six could not read, first the correction and then the refusal, and did not read either one twice.

Then a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that the two of them are the same size and that is not a courtesy and that he would know it in a month if they were not, and about four of that eleven said that was the only reason to do it and about four said nothing.

Nobody in that yard said which of the two was right and nobody said the other one was wrong, and the man of about thirty-two was not in that yard and was in that shed at about six and was told about both of them and said one sentence about them and it was that both of them are on the board and that is a thing that happened and not a thing he arranged.

---

At about four line two settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and the five parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down, being two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, four panes of sheet glass at forty-eight pence, a case and four nails at thirty pence, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence.

A boat at the figure in chalk for two days comes to three hundred and thirty-six pence, and with forty of coal and oil, forty-eight of glass, thirty of a case and four nails, and one hundred and twenty-eight for four men at two days, the five of them come to five hundred and eighty-two pence, which is three marks, eight shillings and sixpence.

The foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out, being seven hundred and eighty marks and one shilling, and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short, being seven hundred and thirteen marks, seven shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

---

At about half past four the keeper took the page of days off the bench and wrote the count of the copies out against the ten days of that run, and did not read it out in that shed and did not put it in any slip.

The ten days stand at zero, one, zero, two, one, zero, three, one, one and four, and the sum of those is thirteen, and the base was zero on every day of it and the count is of copies and is not of pages and is not of sheets and is not of people and is not of signatures.

And the column of the second hand carries a figure on six of the ten days, being the second, the third, the fourth, the sixth, the seventh and the ninth, against two, one, two, three, two and one, which is eleven, and that column is not summed in this shed and is not summed in this movement and is summed once in this run of fifty days and on no day before it.

---

About half past five on that Monday the whole of that tenth day went out loud at that stone in the yard's own mouth, in about nine hundred and eighty-six words, to about nine people, and one of those nine had counted ten days of a column that afternoon and put the figure on the top corner of his own copy.

The copies out for the ten days stand at zero, one, zero, two, one, zero, three, one, one and four, and the sum of those is thirteen, and the base was zero on every day of it, and that is a count of copies and not of pages and not of sheets and not of people. The second hand carries a figure on six of those ten days, being the second, the third, the fourth, the sixth, the seventh and the ninth, against two, one, two, three, two and one, which is eleven, and that column is not summed in this shed and is summed once in this run of fifty days and on no day before it.**"

"**And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and eighty-three days without a tin in it and the last one was on the twenty-ninth of April past. The third of those four columns carries nothing and has nobody over it and at the head of it is a boy's hand of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-eight days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for either sheet or for the count or for the settlement, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

**THE CORRECTION AND THE REFUSAL WENT UP AT THE END STALL AT ABOUT THREE AT THE SAME SIZE AND A HAND'S BREADTH APART, AND THE WRONG FIGURE WAS LEFT SHOWING UNDERNEATH THE CORRECTION AND NOT STRUCK OUT. NOBODY SAID WHICH OF THE TWO WAS RIGHT AND NOBODY SAID THE OTHER ONE WAS WRONG. LINE TWO SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR AND THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET WAS READ AFTER THE SETTLEMENT AND STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND TWELVE PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE SHORT. THE COUNT OF THE COPIES OUT IS TAKEN ONCE AND STANDS AT THIRTEEN AND IS NOT SAID IN A ROOM AND IS NOT IN THIS SLIP. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-ninth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and the hundred and seventy-ninth charge, fourteen trips by hand, taken at about nine in the rain by nine men because four of them were on the mole with a line, the figure not shortened for the four men; and that the said day was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe; and that at about three the correction and the refusal went up at the end stall at the same size and the same height and a hand's breadth apart, the correction carrying one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, the ninth day of June, the name of the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run under the total, and the wrong figure left showing underneath it and not struck out with a line drawn round it; and that the refusal carries a bell engaged in June on a lower figure and the statement that a contract made on a page that was wrong in June is a contract that stands, in the hand of a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed, with three marks on its face; and that the keeper read both sheets out at about three in about eleven people of whom about six could not read, first the correction and then the refusal, and did not read either one twice; and that a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that the two of them are the same size and that that is not a courtesy; and that nobody in that yard said which of the two was right and nobody said the other one was wrong; and that at about four line two settled into the foot of that sheet, the five parts read with their multiplications before the sum went down, being five hundred and eighty-two pence, being three marks, eight shillings and sixpence, and that the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand eight hundred and twelve pence out and one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and seventy-two pence short against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; and that at about half past four the keeper took the page of days off the bench and wrote the count of the copies out against the ten days of that run and did not read it out in that shed and did not put it in any slip.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about nine the reason the figure on that rota was not shortened for four men who were on the mole with a line is that the cistern is charged by the rota and not by the men, and a rota that flexes with the weather is a rota that a man can argue with on the morning it is wanted, and a rota that cannot be argued with is the only kind worth walking on; that at about three the reason two sheets were put up a hand's breadth apart is that a hand's breadth is the smallest distance a man can read two things at, and a correction and a refusal which cannot be read together are not on the same board whatever the board is called; that at about three the reason neither sheet was read out twice is that reading one twice is a courtesy to the man who wrote it and reading it twice in two rooms is a record, and a courtesy is cheap and a record is not, and this yard has two of those this month and cannot afford a third; and that at about half past four the reason the count was not read out in that shed is that a count of what left a room is a count of what this harbor has given away, and a harbor that knows what it has given away is a harbor somebody can ask to give something else, and this harbor has not decided whether it can afford that.
