# Chapter 802

*Friday the Thirtieth Day of September, the Forty-Second Day, a Rule Made in Public at a Working Place and Out Loud in About Nine People at About Two and Saying on Its Own Face That It Was Made Under Pressure by People With No Stake and That It Can Be Repaired, a Woman of About Twenty-Nine of the Lowfen Reed in That Shed Saying Out Loud That It Is a Bad Rule, Eleven Hands on Four Sheets That Left Buildings in That Harbor, the Man of About Thirty-Two in That Shed Until One and the Woman of Thirty of No Office in That Yard From Two and Neither of Them in a Room With the Other, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Friday the thirtieth day of September, with that market open from six and rain coming in off the water in patches after one and the boards of that yard wet by three, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Friday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the second of October and the next wage Tuesday is on the fourth of October. The bell seat and its board went up that morning in the cart at six and were put into the crossing before eight. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it, one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence being the figure at the out side of that foot, which is seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, which is seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifteenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now reached the five hundredth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and second day of not being anybody's question, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fortieth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and sixth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and twenty-fourth day and no figure of money is set against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and eleventh day. Four hundred and forty-two days now on a page of six names, and the man of about fifty-one has not broken that silence in either direction, and nobody has come near him about it. Nobody has been set above that boy's hand of twenty-two in the middle of those four columns and nothing has been entered under it, and he stays off the water. That fourth column is at one hundred and twenty-five with nothing entered against it and nothing ruled off it, and it is not shut and it is not struck. About two sea miles off that point there are nine hulls lying, and nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody on this coast has offered an opinion about them. Two hundred days is what that man of about twenty-nine has now gone without hearing anything in the left side of his head, and nothing in this run of days has done about it and no figure stands against it.**

---

The ordinary work of that Friday was four boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet. The bell seat cut on Thursday went into the crossing at eight in the morning, which is a thing done on a Thursday and put in on a Friday, and the two halves of it are on no page of this harbor and are not going to be on one.

At about two the keeper made a rule out loud in that shed in front of about nine people, and he made it standing and not from a paper, and there was no paper in his hand when he began. He said it once and he said it slowly and he did not say it a second time, and about nine people heard every word of it and about five of those nine could not read it and would not have known from looking at it that it was a rule.

The rule is five lines long. The first of the five says that a correction is not free, and that what a correction costs is taken off the two places that spent on the figure being corrected. The second says that any person of a place that has spent on that figure may refuse a correction, and that a person who refuses one is not asked why. The third says that where one is refused, the refusal and the figure it was given on are written down in a keeper's hand. The fourth says that a refusal is as large as the correction and goes up on the same wall beside it, and that the figure it was given on is not struck out. The fifth says that the rule was made in that shed on the thirtieth day of September at about two by about nine people, that it was made under pressure by people with no stake in it, and that anybody who has to work under it is free to repair it.

---

A woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed was in that shed at about two and had come down that lane on the Wednesday and was going back up it on Saturday. She came down four days and has come down twice this year and this is the third time, and she said out loud in about nine people that the rule is a bad rule, and she said why, and it was that the rule agrees with her.

She said that the only people who can refuse a correction under that second line are the people who spent on the figure being corrected, and that the whole of the cost of a correction falls on the person being corrected by it, and that a refusal costs a sentence and a line in a keeper's hand and nothing else at all. She said that a rule that lets the person it charges refuse the charge and does not ask why is not a rule about cost at all, and that the fifth line is the only honest part of the five, because it says on its own face that it was made under pressure by people with no stake in it. She said that she is not going to repair it today and that she is not going to say it is right either.

About four of that nine said out loud that it was a bad rule and about four said nothing and one of that nine said that it was better than no rule, and nobody said that her reason was wrong and nobody said it was right.

The keeper then said out loud, in about nine, what that rule is not. It is not a jurisdiction and decides no contested claim. It binds nobody who has not signed it. It cannot send anybody anywhere. It is not an office and it gives nobody a title. It is not a victory for anybody, and nobody in that shed said anything to contradict that, and about four of that nine had come expecting one thing and got another and have not said which they wanted.

---

At about half past two the keeper wrote those five lines out fair on one sheet in his own hand in about eleven minutes, and about four of that nine watched him do it and about four did not. That sheet went into the inside of the door of that room at about three and did not go onto any wall and was not sent anywhere and left no building, and about four people in that market have heard of it and about four have read it and about four have not heard of it at all.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about one with the page of routes in his hand and went up that coast road at about one, and the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about two until about four with a page under her arm. Both of them were in that yard on the same day and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page, and neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them said one word to the other.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-second day of that run of days.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that afternoon, and every one of those eleven was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries eleven against this day and no running figure is printed against any of them. Three of those four sheets went up that coast road on the Friday boat and the fourth went to the room over that fish market, and about four of that nine put one of the eleven and the rest of them were put by people who came through that door over the day and not all at one hour.

---

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their thirty-third day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twenty-eighth day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at that end stall at fifteen. The wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August, and no fifth thing is on it, and no fifth thing was put on it on account of a rule.

**A RULE WAS MADE OUT LOUD AT A WORKING PLACE IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND SAYS ON ITS OWN FACE THAT IT WAS MADE UNDER PRESSURE BY PEOPLE WITH NO STAKE IN IT AND THAT IT CAN BE REPAIRED. IT IS A BAD RULE AND THE PERSON IT COSTS MOST SAYS SO IN THAT SHED AND NOBODY SAYS HER REASON IS WRONG. IT DECIDED NOTHING AND SENT NOBODY ANYWHERE AND WENT ON NO WALL. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTEENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the rule carries the words about pressure and no stake on its own fifth line instead of leaving them out is that a rule put up by persons with nothing at stake in the answer has somebody's hand behind it that nobody standing in the room can see, and that a page naming who made it and what they had at stake is worth reading on a morning when somebody wants it to mean more than it means; that the reason the second line lets any person of a spending place refuse a correction without being asked why is that this yard had nothing else to offer on the twenty-eighth of August, and that having nothing to offer is not the same thing as having nothing to be ashamed of, and that is the whole of the difference between the two rules and both of them are honest; that the reason nobody in that shed repaired it on the day it was made is that the woman who named the fault came four days down a road to say it and she did not come down a road to be argued out of the room by nine people who are not paying for the correction; that the reason the sheet went into the inside of a door and not onto a wall is that a wall in this harbor takes four kinds of thing and this is not a fifth kind, and that a page that has never been on a wall can go into a drawer without anything having to be taken down first.*
