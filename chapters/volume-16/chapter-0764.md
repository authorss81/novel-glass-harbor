# Chapter 764

*Tuesday the Twenty-Third Day of August, the Fourth Day, the Ninety-Fifth Wage Tuesday and the First of the Seven in This Run of Days, Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and the Column Not Moved, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten until Four, and a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of It, Two Copies of One Page Laid Side by Side on the Flat of That Bench Stone at About Three and the Difference Named Out Loud in About Nine People, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Tuesday the twenty-third day of August, with that market open from about nine and a hot still morning with no wind at all in the harbour and the bar flat, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The ninety-fifth wage Tuesday, and the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and does not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and a further week stands fallen due at the close of it and would fall due again on the thirtieth. Not a rota day and not a wipe, and no charge is put on that cistern. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried through this Tuesday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-second day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-four days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and second day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and sixty-eighth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-sixth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-first day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-third day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and four days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. Under the third of those columns there is nothing at all and nobody set over it, and at the head of it stands a boy's hand of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of those columns has stood at one hundred and twenty-five without taking a line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-two days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The column was read out at about ten in four minutes and it is twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay said the two figures out loud at the end of it the way he says them every Tuesday and nobody wrote them down in that shed.

The chest was shut from ten until four and it is a long flat thing against the wall of the office with twenty envelopes in it and about five of the people who came in that morning could not read the figure on the lid of the step and did not know what was being read.

---

A woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch came up that coast road on the Monday and put a page of her own on the end stall and it is the outside copy, being the one that came to this harbor in April and the one that was read out in a working place in that community, and it is in a hand that is not the hand on the middle copy.

The woman of about thirty-four did not come herself. What came was a bundle of the page with a string on it and a note in the hand of a woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch, and the note says that the Saltmarch has a bell to seat this week and is paid out of what that crossing was said to have cost, and that the figure on that page is what the Saltmarch has been working from since April, and that if the figure is going to be different the Saltmarch would like to know before the bell is seated and not after.

---

The two pages were laid on the flat of that bench stone at about three, the middle copy on the newsprint and the outside copy on the boards at the back of that market, and the two of them are the same leaf with the same heading and the same four parts on the face of them.

The man of about thirty-two was in that yard at about three and did not touch either of them and stood about two feet back with his hands behind him, and the man of about thirty-eight was not in that harbor and was two days of water and a night away and nobody sent for him.

---

The four parts are the same four parts on both pages. What differs is the total at the foot, and the difference is one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.

The middle copy carries two days of hire. The outside copy carries one.

---

The chandler of about fifty-eight was in that yard at about three and said out loud, to about nine, that a boat is hired for the day she starts and that a boat which starts on the second morning and comes in that afternoon has started a second day whatever the tide did, and that he would not have argued it in March and would not argue it now.

And the man of about thirty-two said out loud that a man of fifty-eight is not going to lose a trade over a day of a boat, and that the figure on the page is the figure on the page and that the question is not who wrote it.

---

The woman of thirty of no office stood at that stone for about ten minutes and did not say anything until about a quarter to four, and then she said out loud, in about nine, that a page that has been read out in two working places is not a record any more, it is a letter, and that a letter that agrees with itself is worth nothing to anybody who was not in either room.

And about four of that nine agreed with her and about four said nothing, and nobody said either was wrong.

---

At about four a man of about twenty-six of that market, who is a second hand and not a keeper, put two marks on the outside copy, one at the foot of the total and one beside the fourth part, and wrote the two figures against them in his own hand, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence and one day.

That man put two marks on that copy and a page of days carries the two against the day, and a mark is not an approval and a mark is not a signature and a mark is not a person.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was put out loud at that stone, in about eight hundred and eight words, and about five of that party could not read the sheet it was about and heard it instead.

The chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and a further week stands fallen due at the close of this day and would fall due again on the thirtieth.**"

"**And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and seventy-seven days without a tin in it and the last one was on the twenty-ninth of April past. There is nothing in the third of those four columns and no person appointed to it, and a boy's hand of twenty-two stands at the head of it, that boy's surname being on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those columns has stood at one hundred and twenty-five without taking a line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-two days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the column or for the two copies or for the two marks, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**THE NINETY-FIFTH WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND WENT WITH THE COLUMN AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND NOT MOVED, THE CHEST SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND A FURTHER WEEK FALLEN DUE. TWO COPIES OF ONE PAGE WERE LAID ON THAT STONE AND THE DIFFERENCE NAMED OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, BEING ONE DAY OF A BOAT HIRED AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK, ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, ONE MARK AND EIGHTPENCE. THE MIDDLE COPY CARRIES TWO DAYS OF HIRE AND THE OUTSIDE COPY CARRIES ONE. A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THE SALTMARCH HAS A BELL TO SEAT THIS WEEK AND IS PAID OUT OF WHAT THAT CROSSING WAS SAID TO HAVE COST. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-third day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the ninety-fifth wage Tuesday and the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of the said day and would fall due again on the thirtieth; and that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe and that no charge was put on that cistern; and that a woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch sent up that coast road on the Monday a bundle with a string on it and a note in her own hand, saying that the Saltmarch has a bell to seat this week and is paid out of what that crossing was said to have cost, and that the figure on that page is what that community has been working from since April, and that if the figure is going to be different that community would like to know before the bell is seated and not after; and that the two pages were laid on the flat of that stone at about three, the middle copy on the newsprint and the outside copy on the boards at the back of that market, and that the four parts on their faces are the same four parts; and that the difference is in the total at the foot, being one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, one hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, the middle copy carrying two days of hire and the outside copy carrying one; and that a chandler of about fifty-eight said out loud to about nine that a boat is hired for the day she starts and that one which starts on the second morning and comes in that afternoon has started a second day whatever the tide did; and that the man of about thirty-two said out loud that a man of fifty-eight is not going to lose a trade over a day of a boat and that the question is not who wrote it; and that the woman of thirty of no office said out loud at about a quarter to four in about nine that a page read out in two working places is not a record any more but a letter, and that a letter which agrees with itself is worth nothing to anybody who was not in either room, and that about four of that nine agreed with her and about four said nothing; and that at about four a man of about twenty-six of that market put two marks on the outside copy, one at the foot of the total and one beside the fourth part, and wrote the two figures against them in his own hand.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten the reason the two figures of the column are said out loud and not written down is that a figure written down in that shed on a Tuesday is a figure somebody in that shed can be asked about in nine days, and the figure of what this harbor owes is a thing a harbor says it does not know; that at about three the reason the two pages were put on the flat of that stone and not on the boards is that a page on a board is a claim and a page on a stone with four people either side of it is a thing being looked at, and a thing being looked at can be found to be two things; that at about three the reason nobody touched either page is that a man with his hands on a page is a man who has an interest in which of the two figures is on it, and there are two figures and one of them is wrong and the man in front of them is the wrong one to be the one holding them; and that at about four the reason a second mark carries the two figures written against it and not the two figures alone is that a figure on a page with a name beside it is a claim, and a figure on a page with another man's hand beside it is a figure somebody has looked at, and those two things are not the same and only the second of them can be checked by a stranger with a pencil.
