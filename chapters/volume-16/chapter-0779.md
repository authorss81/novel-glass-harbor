# Chapter 779

*Wednesday the Seventh Day of September, the Nineteenth Day, a Woman of About Fifty-Two Going up That Coast Road at About Seven in the Morning With a Bundle of Her Own on Her Back and Nothing in Her Bag and Nobody Sending for Her and Nobody Asking Her to Stay, the Fortnight Entered on That Page in That Community's Own Hand on the Tuesday Night With Two Figures Against It That Are Not Figures of Money, Being Fourteen Days and Two Hundred and Thirty-Eight Days, and Fourteen of Two Hundred and Thirty-Eight Being One Day in Seventeen, One Mark Going onto the Face of That Page at About Eleven in the Morning and That Leaf Left Standing on That Board, a Man of About Thirty-Eight of That Landing Back Down That Coast Road at About Four in the Afternoon, an Ordinary Working Day and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twelve and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Wednesday the seventh day of September, with that market open from about nine and a hard bright morning with a light wind come up in the north-east and a short sea outside that bar, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, and no charge goes on that cistern until the morning of Thursday, no wipe comes before Sunday, and the next wage Tuesday is the thirteenth of September. The fortnight of that bell was entered on that community's own page in that community's own hand with two figures against it which are not figures of money, being fourteen days and two hundred and thirty-eight days, and one mark went onto the face of that page at about eleven, and that leaf was left standing on that board. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and the foot of it stands where it stood on the Sunday at one hundred and twenty-five thousand five hundred and fifty-four pence out, being seven hundred and eighty-four marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and fourteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen pence short, being seven hundred and eighteen marks, two shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the last of that tin was opened on the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this nineteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and seventy-seventh day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and seventy-ninth days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and seventeenth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and eighty-third night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and first days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and fifty-sixth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-first day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighty-eighth day. And four hundred and nineteenth days have passed with a man of about fifty-one saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. A boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and nothing has ever been entered under it and nobody has ever been set over it, and that boy's surname is on no page and he is off the water. The fourth of those four has not been touched this week and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, no line taken into it and nothing struck across it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and seventy-seven days today, a day further than yesterday, no figure set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The woman of about fifty-two went up that coast road at about seven that morning with a bundle of her own on her back and nothing at all in the bag on her shoulder, and about four people in that market watched her go and none of them said anything to her about it.

Nobody sent for her and nobody asked her to stay and nobody carried anything for her.

---

The entry was made on the Tuesday night in a room over that market, with that leaf carried off that board for the evening and set back on it after, and it is in her hand and not in the keeper's and the keeper did not write any of it.

It carries the two dates, being the eighth of September and the twenty-second of September, and it carries the fourteen days off it written out in her hand as fourteen days and not as a week and a day.

And at the end of that entry there are two figures against it and neither of them is money. The first is two hundred and thirty-eight days, being the season as that community keeps it. The second is a line of arithmetic in her own hand, being that fourteen days out of two hundred and thirty-eight days is one day in seventeen.

---

At about eleven a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark on the face of that page against the fourteen and not against the six hands and not against the count of the season.

He gave his reason out loud, being that the six hands have been marked three times now and that a hand marked three times is a hand nobody counts again, and that the fourteen has been marked once and that a fortnight which has cost a place a fortnight of a bell is the one line on that page a stranger will want to see has not been argued with.

One mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote it. It is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries one against the said day.

---

The keeper wrote no figure of that fortnight into the first column of that leaf or into the second column of that leaf, and he wrote nothing about it on the page of that week except the hour at which the page left, and he read no part of it out in that shed.

A man of about forty-five of that market said out loud at about half past eleven that the figure of what a fortnight costs the people of that place is that community's own figure and is on that community's own page and is not going to be copied into a yard four days away, and that if this yard copies it the yard will own it in a month and will be asked about it in a year.

---

At about half past two the keeper went along to that end stall with about six people and read the foot of the refusal that a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed wrote in her own hand on Monday of last week, because the light was on it and nobody had looked at the foot of it.

There is a figure printed at the foot of that refusal, in that woman's hand, and it is a count of a season on that stretch as the Lowfen Reed keeps it, and it is two hundred and thirty-one days.

About a step to the left of that refusal, on the same board and at the same height, the leaf from the Cinder Bell carries two hundred and thirty-eight days.

Nobody in that six said anything about the two figures and nobody put the two sheets together and the keeper said out loud one thing, being that he had not known both of them were printed.

---

At about four the man of about thirty-eight of that landing came back down that coast road into that yard and stood by the flat of that bench stone and put nothing on it.

He said out loud, to about nine, that the sheet of newsprint arrived on the Monday and was read out in a yard three days up that road at about three in the afternoon to about twenty people of whom about eleven could not read it, and that it was not read out again, and that a man of about twenty-six of that market up there said out loud that a count is a count and that nobody on this coast has been asked for anything and that those two things are the good news and the bad news in one sentence.

Nobody asked him anything at all, and he said out loud that he expected that and had come anyway.

---

Nobody was thanked for the entry or for the mark or for the four days of road, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it.

NO FIGURE OF IT WAS COPIED INTO ANY COLUMN IN THIS YARD. A MARK WENT ONTO ITS FACE AT ABOUT ELEVEN AGAINST THE FOURTEEN. AT THE FOOT OF THAT REFUSAL THERE IS A FIGURE IN ANOTHER PLACE'S OWN HAND, BEING TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-ONE DAYS, A STEP AWAY FROM THE FIRST ON THE SAME BOARD, AND NOBODY PUT THE TWO SHEETS TOGETHER. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the mark went against the fourteen and not against the six hands is that a mark is a way of counting how many people have looked at a line, and a line with three marks on it has been looked at and settled and a line with one mark on it is a line somebody may still come back to, and a fortnight is a line this coast has not looked at twice in nine years; that the reason no figure of that fortnight was copied into this yard is that a copy is a claim of the right to say what a thing is, and this yard has no standing whatever to say what a fortnight costs the people of a place four days down a coast road, and the one thing it can do honestly is carry a sheet with somebody else's figure on it; that the reason nobody put the two sheets together is that those two communities do not keep one calendar between them and have never been asked to, and a yard which puts two calendars side by side and prints the difference is a yard which has decided that the difference is a finding, and it is not a finding, it is two places being two places; and that at about four the reason he came back into that yard with nothing on him is that a man who comes back from three days up a coast road with a story in his mouth has come back to be listened to, and a man who comes back with a figure in his hand has come back to be checked, and this yard only wanted the first of those and he had brought the second and neither of them was wrong.*
