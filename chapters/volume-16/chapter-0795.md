# Chapter 795

*Friday the Twenty-Third Day of September, the Thirty-Fifth Day, an Ordinary Working Day With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement, the Six Sentences of Thursday Not Said Again and the Man of About Fifty-Seven Not Sent for Again, the Woman of Thirty of No Office in That Shed From About Eleven With a Page Under Her Arm, the Man of About Thirty-Two Two Miles Up That Coast Road at That Hour and Not in It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Fourteen*

Friday the twenty-third day of September, with that market open from six and a fine soft morning and the water slack and no wind at all, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and there is no charge on that cistern tomorrow either and the next is on the morning of Monday, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Friday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters stand at the close of this thirty-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one has now gone four hundred and thirty-five days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and ninety-third day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-fifth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-third day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninety-ninth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and seventeenth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood four hundred and fourth day. The middle of those four columns is still headed by the hand of a boy of twenty-two, still empty, still unappointed, and that boy's surname is on no page here and he is off the water for the seventh time. One hundred and twenty-five is all the fourth of those four carries, no line has ever gone into it, and it is neither shut nor struck. That mole head keeps nine hulls about two sea miles off it and kept nine on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. Two hundred and ninety-three days is how long a man of about twenty-nine has had nothing in the left side of his head, no figure stands against that number, and he is not on the water.**

---

Nobody sent for the man of about fifty-seven of that market on that Friday, and nobody in that shed asked about the six sentences of the day before, and about four of that nine looked all morning for a reason to bring them up and did not find one. He came down about eleven and went up that coast road again at about two with the book under his arm, and about four of that nine watched him go and about four of that nine did not.

That is the ordinary of a yard that has had its first thing said in it and is getting on with the rest of the day, and it is not a favour to him and it is not a matter of his standing. The one thing he said out loud that day was that a penny a sheet was a penny a sheet, and about four of that nine agreed with him on that and said nothing else on it, and nobody has said since whether they agreed with him on the rest of it.

---

The woman of thirty of no office came into that yard at about eleven with a page under her arm and stood at the back of that shed with the page in her hands until about half past two, and the man of about thirty-two was not in that yard and was not expected in it and was two miles up that coast road at that hour. She put the page on that bench stone when she came to it, squared it up with the heel of her hand, and picked it up again when about four of that nine came over, and held it against her chest while they looked, which is what a person does with a page that is hers and not the yard's.

She was not asked about the drawer and she was not asked about Thursday and she said nothing about either, and about four of that nine looked at the page under her arm and looked away again. She had her own reasons for coming down on a Friday and none of them were on that bench stone, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay offered her a stool and she took it and did not sit on it for more than a quarter of an hour.

Neither of them signed the other's page that day because they were not in a room together, and neither of them gave up an inch of their own work, and that is the whole of what is known about the two of them in this run of days. She went up that coast road at about half past two and he came into that yard at about ten the next morning, and neither of them has ever put down on any page what the other one was doing on that road.

---

The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has been bare since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on it on that Friday and nothing has gone up on it on any day of this run of fifty days, and that page is not on any page of this harbor. It is not a bare wall because there is nothing to go on it. There is a nail in that plaster at about the height of a man's shoulder that has been in the plaster since the fourteenth of August and has no head on it, and about four people in this harbor know that nail is there.

The ordinary work of that Friday was the outer board of that mole being planed and a length of line cut to length, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line. The planing was done on the outside face and the shavings went into the water, and about half of them came back on that tide, and nobody put a second pass on that board that day because a mole head is not a thing a man has to be proud of before the winter.

---

The four new crews were on their fifth day and did the day's work in about three hours under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten and was given the tally slate and the book of hours and nothing else to do all day. He wrote the hours down as they went and he did not go near the bench, and about four of that nine looked at the two ends of that shed for about a minute and then stopped looking, which is what a yard does with a thing it has already decided about and not one word.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about one until about four and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the forty-fifth day of this run of days on which that has happened. He came in at about one and worked to about four and did not speak, and there is no figure against any of that afternoon and there never will be, and that is the whole of what this yard knows about what that man does with his hands.

---

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-sixth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twenty-first day a step to the left, and the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at that end stall at fourteen with nothing on it moved. About four people in this market read both of them every day of the three years and can tell anybody who asks which of the two of them is the older by the state of the nail at the top left, and about four have never read either, and the two groups have not spoken to each other about it and are not going to.

The bell of the Cinder Bell has been back in service since daybreak on the twenty-second and is rung by the people of that place and not by anybody in this harbor, and no figure of it is on any page here. There is no hour of any day of this run in which a person in this harbor could have gone up that coast road and watched it happen, and the fortnight it was out of service is on no page here at all and is not on any page of that community's either except as fourteen days at six pence a day.

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-third day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was an ordinary working day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that there is no charge on that cistern on the Saturday and that the next is on the morning of Monday, and that the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the six sentences were not asked about again is that a thing said once in a yard of nine becomes a second occasion if it is asked about, and the whole of what this harbor wanted out of that day was the saying of it and nothing since; that the reason nobody troubled her over that drawer is that the record is hers and it is her work, and a person who keeps a record is not asked about the papers of a room over a fish market as though the record were a door into it; that the reason that wall has stood bare since the fourteenth of August is that a wall a person can put something on is the easiest thing in this harbor to fill and the hardest to empty, and nothing has gone up on it because nothing in these fifty days was a thing this harbor was willing to hang; and that the reason the second hand column of that page of days is bare against the said day is that there was no page leaving that yard, a mark being a hand on a face before a page goes out of a building and there was no page going out of a building.*
