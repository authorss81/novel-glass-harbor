# Chapter 790

*Sunday the Eighteenth Day of September, the Thirtieth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Fourth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Twenty-Eight Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One at the Angle It Has Held Since the Twelfth of January and Not Whole, Forty-One and Twenty-Eight and Forty-One Being One Hundred and Ten the Whole of It, Two Marks Going onto the Face of One Copy of the Page of the Crossing at About Two, the Count of the Withdrawn Taken Once at About Half Past Four off the Page of Days and Written Down and Not Read Out and Not in That Slip, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Eleventh Day of Fourteen, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Sunday the eighteenth day of September, with that market shut and the bars down until about six and a wet morning with the wind in the south-west and spray over that mole before ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twenty-fourth time, that wall behind that reeve's bench off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-eight minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being forty-one and twenty-eight and forty-one and one hundred and ten the whole of it. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and that is the first charge in the days after this run. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and third days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirtieth day, and that is the last time in this run of days that this yard has stood a man of about thirty-one of the second lay in front of a page of days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-eighth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninetieth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-eighth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninety-fourth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and twelfth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-seventh day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-second day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-ninth day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and thirtieth day of saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. The head of the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two and there is nothing under it and nobody over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. Of the fourth of those four, one hundred and twenty-five is the figure and no line has been taken into it, and it is neither shut nor struck. Off that mole head, at about two sea miles, lie nine hulls where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor says nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-eighth day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one and stood bare twenty-eight minutes, and the three men who do that work took forty-one minutes each way, which is a minute longer each way than last Sunday, and nobody in this harbor has said why and nobody said why this morning either.

The plaster behind it showed a rectangle of a different colour and the second man wiped his hand across the bottom of the new render before he stepped down, and the water came over that slip about eleven and the floor of that shed was wet by about one.

---

About nine people stood in that shed rather than sat in it and the ordinary work of that Sunday was cutting line against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fortieth day of this run of days on which that has happened and about half of that nine have stopped counting it and about half have not.

---

At about two two marks went onto the face of one copy of the page of the crossing, and both of them went against the first of the four lines, and one of them is the hand of a man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store and the other is the hand of a man of about forty-nine of the west end.

The man of about fifty-eight gave his reason out loud and said that the first of those four lines is the only line on that page that is written differently on the two copies of it, and that a mark on a line that differs is a mark saying that this coast has been carrying two figures for a day of a boat for a year and that the two of them have only this month been laid beside each other.

The man of about forty-nine gave his reason out loud and said that he wanted it on the record that a line which differs is worth more than a line which does not, because a line which does not differ can be argued about for years and a line which differs has to be answered inside a month.

They are two marks and two hands and neither of them is the hand that wrote that page, and it is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries two against this day.

---

The woman of thirty of no office came into that yard at about half past twelve with a page under her arm and stood at the back of it with the page in her hands, and the man of about thirty-two came in at about twenty past one and stood at the other side of that shed with his back to the door.

They were in the same yard for about fifty minutes and there was a page in the room the whole time, and neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them said one word to the other, and about four of that nine watched the pair of them stand the width of a door apart for fifty minutes and said afterwards that they had been doing that for years.

Neither of them gave up an inch of their own work over it. She kept the record and it was her record and he kept the routes and they were not his, and the two of them have never once in this run of days been in a room together with no page in it, and about four of that nine would like it recorded that nobody in that shed has any standing to make a finding about a marriage.

---

About two people in that yard still wanted that fortnight of that bell entered at fourteen days and about two still wanted it entered at twenty-one, and neither of them said out loud that the other of them was wrong.

They said it in the ordinary way, one to the other, at the same time, and then both of them said nothing for about a minute and a half, the same as they said it on the Thursday of the week before.

A man of about forty-five of that market said out loud that the season is the season of the place the fortnight belongs to and that a fortnight is fourteen days whatever a stranger's arithmetic says, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that two printed figures a step apart on one board do not agree and that the difference between them is seven days and is on a bill that went out on the tide a week ago.

Neither of those two men said anything else about it, and it is not settled on any of these ten days.

---

At about half past four on that Sunday the keeper took the page of days off the bench and wrote the count of the withdrawn against the ten days of that run, and he did not read it out in that shed and did not put it in any slip.

The ten days stand at zero, zero, zero, one, zero, zero, zero, two, zero and zero, and the sum of those is three, and the base was zero on every day of it.

The count is of pages taken back off a public board in this harbor on that day by the person who put them there, and a page taken down and put up again the same hour is one withdrawal, and it is not a count of the people who changed their minds about a page, and it is not a count of mistakes, and it is not a count of corrections.

And the column of the second hand carries a figure on six of the ten days of this run, being the first, the third, the fifth, the seventh, the eighth and the tenth, against one, two, three, one, one and two, which is ten, and the four days it stands bare on are nowhere on it, and those ten days are not added together in this shed and are not added together in this movement, and the whole of that column is added up once in this run of fifty days and on no day before it.

---

The drawer in that record room was not opened on any of the ten days of this run and the sheet in it is not on any page of this harbor, and the man of about fifty-seven who keeps that copying book has taken his penny a sheet for every page of it and is not ruined and is not forgiven and has not been asked anything else about it.

The correction and the refusal were still standing at that end stall at the same size and a hand's breadth apart on their twenty-first day with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its sixteenth day a step to the left, and nobody in that shed said which of the two is right.

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the eleventh day of being out of service and goes back into service on the twenty-second of September, and the fourteen days of it are written out as fourteen days and not as a week and a day.
