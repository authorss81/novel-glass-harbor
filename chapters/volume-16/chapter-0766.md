# Chapter 766

*Thursday the Twenty-Fifth Day of August, the Sixth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Eighth Charge on That Cistern, Fourteen Trips by Hand, the Woman of About Thirty-Four of the Saltmarch on the End Stall at About Eleven With a Figure of What a Bell to Seat This Week Is Paid Out Of and the Difference Out Loud, a Wage Tuesday Yesterday and No Figure of That Hundred Said a Second Time, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Thursday the twenty-fifth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a wind come up in the north in the night and the sea running over that bar again at about four in the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and seventy-eighth charge, fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of it, and it was charged between about eight and about twelve because the men were wanted early. The woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch stood at the end stall from about eleven with the figure of what that bell to seat this week is paid out of, and said the difference out loud in about eleven people of whom about six could not read it. Not a wipe and not a rota day tomorrow and not a wage Tuesday, and the column on the lid of that step was read out yesterday and has not been read out again today and no figure of that hundred is said a second time. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried through this Thursday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-fourth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-six days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fourth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventieth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-eighth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-third day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-eighth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-fifth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and six days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The third of those columns is empty and nobody has been put in it, and at the head of it is a boy's hand of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-four days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern went at about eight because the tide was in the fish market gutter and the men who do that work wanted it done before the boats came, and it took fourteen of them and fourteen trips and the last man walked back with the empty can and did not look at the gauge.

The keeper wrote the hundred and seventy-eighth against the day at about one and read it out to nobody, and the hundred and seventy-ninth is on Monday and is the last of this run.

---

At about eleven the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch put a sheet of her own on the end stall and stood beside it and did not hand it to anybody.

On that sheet are three figures, being what the Saltmarch has set aside for a bell to seat this week, what that sheet was said to come out of, and what is left. The middle figure is the outside copy's figure and the last figure is the middle copy's figure with one day of a boat hired taken out of it, and she had worked the last one out herself on the Wednesday night and had it written out in her own hand before anybody in this harbor had said a word about it.

The difference is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.

---

She said out loud, to about eleven people, that a bell to seat is not a thing a place buys, it is a thing a place pays for twice, once in the metal and once in the days the men are off the water, and that the days are the part that is paid out of what a crossing was said to have cost.

And she said out loud that the Saltmarch is not asking this harbor for anything and is not asking that harbor to be wrong, and that the bell is going to be seated on the Wednesday whatever is on that leaf, and that the eleven shillings and the food of three men for two days is coming out of a page that is being corrected and the people of that place are the ones who are paying for the correction.

---

A man of about thirty-eight of that market said out loud that a correction that is right is right in a room and not in a harbour, and that the Saltmarch may be the only place on this coast that has said out loud what a wrong figure costs it, and that is not a complaint.

And about four of that eleven said nothing at all and about four said it was a complaint, and nobody said either of those was wrong.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that yard at about two and read the three figures on that sheet twice and did not touch it, and then said out loud, in about nine, that he is not going to be the man who hands a community a different number.

And a chandler of about fifty-eight who was in that market said out loud that a man who will not hand anybody a number is a man who has decided the number is not his, and that is a thing a man can decide and it is a thing he can be wrong about.

---

The man of about thirty-two said, to about nine, that the number is not his and that the leaf is not his and that the people who wrote the leaf four days ago are the people who should be asked what the leaf is for, and that a man who has been on this water for fourteen volumes does not get to decide which page a harbour is allowed to be ashamed of.

And then he did the other thing, which is that he said out loud that he would go up that coast road himself on the Monday and would take nobody's figures with him and would ask the two places what the figure is for.

---

At about four the sheet of the woman of about thirty-four went back into a bundle with the string on it and went down the quay to a man who would carry it four days up that road, and she paid him out of her own hand and not out of that stall and not out of that yard.

A page of days carries no figure of copies leaving a room on this day and the sheet she carried went out of this harbor and not out of a room in it.

---

A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and seventy-nine days without a tin in it. The third of those four columns is empty and unappointed and carries at its head a boy's hand of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-four days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the cistern or for the three figures or for the reading, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

**THE FIGURE ON THAT LEAF IS A ROOF. THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THE SALTMARCH PUT THREE FIGURES ON THE END STALL AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND STOOD BESIDE THEM AND HANDED THEM TO NOBODY, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING ONE MARK AND EIGHTPENCE, AND SHE HAD WORKED IT OUT HERSELF ON THE WEDNESDAY NIGHT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT SAID OUT LOUD THAT A CORRECTION WHICH IS RIGHT IS RIGHT IN A ROOM AND NOT IN A HARBOUR. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO HAND A COMMUNITY A DIFFERENT NUMBER AND THAT HE WILL GO UP THAT COAST ROAD HIMSELF ON THE MONDAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-fifth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and the hundred and seventy-eighth charge, fourteen trips by hand, walked on by none of it, charged between about eight and about twelve because the tide was in that gutter; and that the said day was not a wipe and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the column on the lid of that step was read out on the twenty-third and has not been read out again and no figure of that hundred is said a second time; and that the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch put a sheet of her own on the end stall at about eleven and stood beside it and did not hand it to anybody, and that on that sheet are three figures, being what that community has set aside for a bell to seat this week, what that sheet was said to come out of, and what is left, and that the middle figure is the outside copy's figure and the last is the middle copy's figure with one day of a boat hired taken out of it, and that she had worked it out herself on the Wednesday night in her own hand; and that the difference is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence; and that she said out loud that a bell to seat is paid for twice, once in the metal and once in the days the men are off the water, and that the Saltmarch is not asking that harbor for anything and is not asking it to be wrong, and that the bell will be seated on the Wednesday whatever is on that leaf; and that a man of about thirty-eight of that market said out loud that a correction which is right is right in a room and not in a harbour, and that the Saltmarch may be the only place on this coast that has said out loud what a wrong figure costs it, and that that is not a complaint, and about four of that eleven said nothing and about four said it was a complaint; and that at about two the man of about thirty-two read the three figures twice and did not touch that sheet and said out loud in about nine that he is not going to hand a community a different number, and that the people who wrote the leaf are the people who should be asked what the leaf is for, and that he will go up that coast road himself on the Monday and take nobody's figures with him; and that at about four that sheet went back into a bundle and went down the quay to a man carrying it four days up that road, paid for out of her own hand.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason she stood beside that sheet and did not hand it to anybody is that a sheet handed to a yard is a sheet the yard is responsible for and a sheet standing on a stall is a sheet a stranger may read, and the whole of the difference between those two things is about six words on a piece of paper; that at about two the reason the man of about thirty-two read the figures twice and touched nothing is that a hand on a sheet is a claim on it, and he is a man a coast has decided not to take claims from, and there is no sense in him making one with his hands; and that at about two the reason he said he would go up the road himself is that a man who sends a figure up a coast road is a man who has sent the figure and not the question, and he wants to know what the figure is for and only one of the two places can tell him that; and that at about four the reason she paid the man who carried that sheet out of her own hand is that a community that asks a harbor to be wrong and then lets the harbor pay the carriage is a community that has asked the harbor for something after all.
