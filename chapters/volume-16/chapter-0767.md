# Chapter 767

*Friday the Twenty-Sixth Day of August, the Seventh Day, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud in About Nine People at About Two That He Is Not Going to Write the Correction and Giving the Reason for It, the Woman of Thirty of No Office Refusing It for the Same Reason and Saying It Is Her Own Work and Not His Decision, Line One Settled into the Foot of That Sheet at About Four With the Five Parts Read Before the Sum Went Down and the Foot Read After It, a Rota Day on Monday and a Wipe on Sunday, a Second Mark Going onto the Face of the Outside Copy at About Three, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Friday the twenty-sixth day of August, with that market open from about nine and the wind still in the north and a cold bright morning with the glassy look that this coast gives about two days after a blow, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**At about two the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine people that he is not going to write the correction, and gave the reason for it out loud, and the reason is that a correction in his hand is a correction this coast will believe because he is in it, and he is in it. At about half past two the woman of thirty of no office said the same thing about herself for the same reason and said out loud that it is her work and not his decision. Line one settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four, the five parts being read with their multiplications before the sum went down, and the foot of that sheet is read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand two hundred and thirty pence out and one hundred and thirteen thousand five hundred and ninety pence short. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and a charge is put on that cistern on Monday and a wipe on Sunday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried through this Friday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-fifth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-seven days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-first night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-ninth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-fourth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-ninth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-sixth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and seven days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. There is nothing in the third of those columns and nobody in charge of it, a boy's hand of twenty-two is set at the head of it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water. Nothing has gone into the fourth, which stands at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-five days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The two copies were on that bench stone again from about ten, the middle copy on its newsprint and the outside copy on the boards, and the four parts on their faces are the same four parts and the totals at their feet are one day of hire apart.

The man of about thirty-two came in at about two off that quay and did not sit down and asked the keeper for the pen and then did not take it.

---

He said the thing out loud in about nine that he is not going to write the correction, and the reason is that a correction in his hand is a correction this coast will believe because he is in it, and he is in it, and that in fourteen runs of days he has been a man people believe things because of and has not once used that, and he is not going to start on a day when a community is out of one hundred and sixty-eight pence because of him.

About four of that nine said nothing and about four said that was right and nobody said either was wrong.

---

The woman of thirty of no office came down the steps at about half past two and heard the whole of it twice because she was on the top step when the first half of it was said.

She said out loud, in about nine, that she is the keeper of that record and that a correction in her hand is a correction the record made on itself, and that the four heads on the wall in that room have never had a correction on them that anybody in this harbor had to live with, and that they are going to get one.

And she said out loud that this is her work and not his decision and that he is not to speak for her about it, and he said he would not, and neither of them said anything else about it in that shed.

---

The man of about forty of the Ashtide Run was in that yard at about two and heard both refusals and said out loud that he had come on the Monday to tell a room something and that he was not going to be the one to put it in writing for them.

And then he said out loud that his route is one of the two that has been working from the outside copy since April, and that his route's page is the page that carries the wrong figure, and that if nobody in this harbor will write it then he will write it himself, and that he will write it against his own page and not anybody else's.

And about four of that nine said that was the first sensible thing anybody had said in three days, and about four said nothing.

---

A man of about twenty-six of that market put a third mark on the face of the outside copy at about three and the mark is against the second part and not against the total, and the reason he gave out loud is that the second part is the part that is right, and that a mark on the right part of a page is the only mark on it that says something anybody can use.

At about half past three the man of about forty wrote his own name on the outside copy in the space under the total, in a hand that is not the hand of the four parts, and that name is on no other page in that harbor and is on no ledger and is not in a column.

---

At about four line one settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and the five parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down, being three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence, sixty pence of coal and oil for three days, ninety yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence, two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence at thirty-six pence, and six men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at six times three times sixteen for two hundred and eighty-eight pence.

Four men at three days, and four times three times sixteen, is two hundred and eighty-eight pence, and against that a hundred and sixty-eight three times over for a boat is five hundred and four, and sixty of coal and oil and two hundred and seventy of line and thirty-six of two seats make the sum of one thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence, which is seven marks, three shillings and twopence.

---

The foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand two hundred and thirty pence out, being seven hundred and seventy-six marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and thirteen thousand five hundred and ninety pence short, being seven hundred and nine marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

And a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about four and a hundred and sixty-eight had come into it at about ten and under that slate is twenty-eight pence.

---

About half past five on that Friday the reading took longer than the days either side of it, in about eight hundred and ninety-six words said at that stone at about half past five to about nine people, and about four of those nine had not said anything all day.

"**The two copies were both on that bench stone again from about ten and the four parts on their faces are the same four parts and the totals at their feet are one day of hire apart. The man of about thirty-two came in at about two off that quay, did not sit down, asked the keeper for the pen and then did not take it.**"

"**He said out loud in about nine that he is not going to write the correction, and the reason is that a correction in his hand is a correction this coast will believe because he is in it, and he is in it, and that in fourteen runs of days he has been a man people believe things because of and has not once used that and is not going to start on a day when a community is out of one hundred and sixty-eight pence because of him. And about four of that nine said nothing and about four said that was right and nobody said either was wrong.**"

"**The woman of thirty of no office came down the steps at about half past two and heard the whole of it twice, and said out loud that she is the keeper of that record and that a correction in her hand is a correction the record made on itself, and that the four heads on that wall have never had a correction on them that anybody in this harbor had to live with, and that they are going to get one. And she said out loud that this is her work and not his decision and that he is not to speak for her about it, and he said he would not, and neither of them said anything else about it in that shed.**"

"**The man of about forty of the Ashtide Run said out loud that his route is one of the two that has been working from the outside copy since April, and that his route's page is the page that carries the wrong figure, and that if nobody in this harbor will write it then he will write it himself against his own page and not anybody else's.**"

"**At about three a man of about twenty-six of that market put a third mark on the face of that copy against the second part and not against the total, and said out loud that the second part is the part that is right and that a mark on the right part of a page is the only mark on it that says something anybody can use. At about half past three the man of about forty wrote his own name on that copy in the space under the total in a hand that is not the hand of the four parts, and that name is on no other page in this harbor and is on no ledger and is in no column.**"

"**At about four line one settled into the foot of that sheet and the five parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down. Three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is five hundred and four pence, coal and oil for three days is sixty pence, ninety yards of line at three pence the yard is two hundred and seventy pence, two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence is thirty-six pence, and six men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day is two hundred and eighty-eight pence, and the sum of those is one thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence, being seven marks, three shillings and twopence. The foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand two hundred and thirty pence out and one hundred and thirteen thousand five hundred and ninety pence short against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.**"

"**And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard is one hundred and eighty days without a tin in it. A boy's hand of twenty-two stands at the head of the third of those four columns, which is empty and unappointed and has nobody over it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-five days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for either refusal or for the correction or for the settlement, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it.

---

**TWO PEOPLE WHO KEEP THE THINGS REFUSED TO MAKE THE CORRECTION IN THEIR OWN HANDS, EACH GIVING THE SAME REASON OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND THE REASON IS THAT A CORRECTION IN A HAND THIS COAST BELIEVES IS A CORRECTION THAT MAKES NOBODY ANSWER FOR IT. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY OF THE ASHTIDE RUN WROTE IT HIMSELF AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE AGAINST HIS OWN ROUTE'S PAGE. LINE ONE SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR AND THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET WAS READ AFTER THE SETTLEMENT AND STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-sixth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that a charge is put on that cistern on the twenty-ninth and a wipe on the twenty-eighth; and that at about two the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine that he is not going to write the correction, and that the reason is that a correction in his hand is a correction this coast will believe because he is in it; and that the woman of thirty of no office said at about half past two that she is the keeper of that record and that a correction in her hand is a correction the record made on itself, and that the four heads on that wall have never had a correction on them that anybody in this harbor had to live with, and that they are going to get one, and that this is her work and not his decision and that he is not to speak for her about it, and that he said he would not; and that the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run said out loud that his route is one of the two that has been working from the outside copy since April and that if nobody in this harbor will write it then he will write it himself against his own page and not anybody else's; and that a man of about twenty-six of that market put a third mark on the outside copy at about three against the second part and not against the total, and said out loud that the second part is the part that is right; and that at about half past three the man of about forty wrote his own name on that copy in the space under the total in a hand that is not the hand of the four parts; and that line one settled into the foot of that sheet at about four, being one thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence, the five parts read with their multiplications before the sum went down; and that the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-four thousand two hundred and thirty pence out and one hundred and thirteen thousand five hundred and ninety pence short against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two the reason he asked for the pen and did not take it is that a man who asks for a pen in a working room is a man who has said he is going to write, and a man who has said he is going to write and then does not is a thing this coast will remember for a year and use at a time of its own choosing; that at about half past two the reason she said she was the keeper of that record and not the person who would correct it is that a record which corrects itself has no one to complain of, and a record with nobody to complain of is a record a person can be run over by; that at about half past three the reason one man in that shed put his own name under a total on a page that carried the wrong figure is that a name under a wrong figure is the only thing on the paper that is harder to take back than the figure, and a man knows that about his own name and does not about a stranger's; and that at about four the reason the foot of that sheet was read after the sum went down and not before is that a sheet read before its own settlement is a sheet about yesterday and this yard keeps a sheet about today.
