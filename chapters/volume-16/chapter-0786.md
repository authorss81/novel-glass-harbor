# Chapter 786

*Wednesday the Fourteenth Day of September, the Twenty-Sixth Day, an Ordinary Working Day and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Man of About Forty of the Ashtide Run Coming into That Yard at About Eleven With a Copy of the Page of the Crossing That He Has Had Since April and Laying It Beside the Page in That Record Room at About Two and the Two of Them Not Being the Same Page, the Difference of One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Being in the Hire and Not in the Question, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Seventh Day of Fourteen, Nothing into the Foot of That Fitting-Out Sheet and Nothing onto the Face of Any Page, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Nineteen and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Wednesday the fourteenth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a grey morning with the wind round in the west and a short sea outside that bar doing nothing to it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day and not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge goes on that cistern until the morning of Thursday and there is a charge on that cistern tomorrow. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, and that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is one hundred and ninety-ninth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-fourth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-sixth day of not being asked anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-fourth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninetieth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and eighth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-third day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and seventy-eighth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-fifth day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and twenty-sixth day of saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. Nothing has ever been entered under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and nobody has ever been set over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. The fourth of those four carries one hundred and twenty-five and has carried no line, and it stands neither shut nor struck. Off that mole head there lie nine hulls at two sea miles, where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said here. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-fourth day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The yard did the ordinary work of a Wednesday and the only thing out of the ordinary in it was that a man came in at about eleven who had not come in since the twenty-ninth day of August, and that he came with a page rolled inside his coat.

A man of about thirty-eight of that landing was in that yard from about one until about four and was not asked anything by anybody, and about four of that nine have now stopped noticing that he is there and about four have not.

---

A man of about forty of the Ashtide Run came into that market at about eleven and put his hand into his coat and took out a rolled sheet and unrolled it on the flat of that bench stone and weighted the two ends with two pieces of line.

He said out loud that he has had that sheet since April and that he brought it because a thing was read out of a book in this shed on the Tuesday which was not on that sheet, and that he did not come to argue with anybody and had brought it to lay two pages together.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that laying two pages together is what a man does when he wants one of them to be wrong, and that the two of them had been laid together on the twenty-ninth of August by the man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store and had not agreed then either.

---

The keeper went up at about one and came down at about two with the page in that room on a board, and he brought it out face down and turned it over on the flat of that bench stone beside the other one, and about nine people stood round two sheets of the same size with the same four lines on them.

The man of about forty said out loud, before anybody read anything, that they are not the same page.

And then he said out loud that he was not saying that either of them was false, and that he was saying that they are two pages and that one of them is a copy of the other and that a copy is allowed to be different from the thing it is copied from in one line only and that this is not that line.

---

He said that the two of them differ in the first of the four lines and in nothing else, and that his carries one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and that the one out of that room carries two days of it, being two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, and that the difference between those two figures is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.

And he said out loud that both of those are true, and that a boat is hired for the day she starts and a boat that starts again on the second morning has started a second day whatever the tide did, and that he does not know which of those two pages is the first one and neither does anybody in that shed.

---

A man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store put his own hand flat on the two sheets at the same time, one under each, and said out loud that he had said on the twenty-second day of August that the middle copy was copied out of a copy, and that this is what that means and no more than this.

He said out loud that a page that has been copied out of a page has no middle left in it, and that what it has instead is a first, which is somebody's guess about which of the two the other one came from, and that a guess is not a lie and is not a copy and is not any of the four heads on any wall in this harbor.

About four of that nine said nothing and about four said that a guess on a page is as good as anything else on a page.

---

The keeper read the top of both sheets out loud at about half past two, and the top of both is the same three words, and they are not a question with a number in it and they are not a heading either.

They ask what that crossing cost.

And when he had read them he put his finger on them on one sheet and then on the other and said out loud that they were on both of them, and that a man of about fifty-eight had been copying a page for a year and a day and had never once looked at the three words at the top of what he was copying, and that neither had the man of about forty when he took his sheet in April.

---

A woman of about thirty-four of that market, who is not the woman of the Saltmarch, said out loud, at about a quarter past three, that the fourth of those four lines is thirty yards of line at three pence the yard, and that those thirty yards came out of a hundred and twenty bought in June for three crossings, and that ten yards to a crossing is what it works out at, and that a yard's purchase is not a crossing's.

The man of about forty said out loud that she had said the whole of it in four lines and that he had had that page in his coat since April and had not known it.

---

He said out loud that the fault is in the three words at the top of that leaf and that the four lines under them are all of them true whichever of the two pages they are on, and that a crossing is a thing a boat does and is not a thing that can be added up into one number, and that a man who writes three words like that at the top of a page has asked four true questions a question no page can answer.

The keeper said out loud that the drawer in that record room is not the drawer that would answer it, and that the copyist of that room keeps his own papers and that this harbor has no standing over them, and that nobody in that shed was going to be shown anything out of anybody else's drawer this week.

---

At about four the man of about forty rolled his sheet up and put it inside his coat again and said out loud that he was taking it back up that coast road on the Saturday with the rest of a week of work, and that he was not going to have anybody change one word of it, and that the three words were his now in the way that a bad stair is a man's after he has fallen down it twice.

Nobody in that shed said that the man of about forty was right and nobody in that shed said that the keeper was right, and about four of that nine said nothing at all and let it stand there.

---

**TWO PAGES OF THE SAME SIZE WITH THE SAME FOUR LINES ON THEM ARE NOT THE SAME PAGE AND NEITHER OF THEM IS FALSE. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THEM IS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AND IT IS IN THE HIRE. THE THREE WORDS AT THE TOP OF THAT LEAF ARE ON BOTH OF THEM AND BOTH OF THEM ASK A QUESTION THAT NO SET OF FOUR TRUE LINES CAN ANSWER. NOTHING WENT ONTO THE FACE OF ANY PAGE TODAY AND THE COLUMN OF THE SECOND HAND CARRIES NOTHING AGAINST THIS DAY.**

that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that at about eleven a man of about forty of the Ashtide Run laid on the flat of that bench stone a sheet he has had since April, and that at about two the keeper brought down out of the room over that fish market the page in that room and turned it over beside the other one, and that about nine people stood round two sheets of the same size with the same four lines on them; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the two pages were laid down and turned over before either of them was read is that a man who reads first and lays second has already decided which of the two is the true one and is only looking for the other to agree with him, and about four of that nine can read a figure and about four cannot, and the four who cannot are the reason this yard reads before it looks; that the reason the third of those three words was named rather than read as a question is that about four of that nine would have called it a question if it had been read as one, and a man who calls a page a question has stopped reading it and started answering it, and that is the second of the two faults this page has had in two years; that the reason nobody was shown anything out of the drawer in that record room is that a drawer opened once for a man who came with a page in his coat becomes a drawer that is opened for the next man who comes with a page in his coat, and the day after that the people who own the papers in it find out from somebody else that they have been read.*
