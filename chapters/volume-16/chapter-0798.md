# Chapter 798

*Monday the Twenty-Sixth Day of September, the Thirty-Eighth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Eighty-Seventh Time, That Cistern Charged at About Nine in Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, a Man of About Thirty-Six of the Cinder Bell Down That Coast Road at About One Saying in About Nine in That Community's Own Hand and on That Community's Own Page That the Cinder Bell Is Off That Shared Stretch from the Twenty-Seventh, Two Marks on the Face of That Page, no Wage Tuesday and No Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Monday the twenty-sixth day of September, with that market open from six and a moderate swell on the bar and the first of a dirty sky in the west, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and eighty-seventh time, that cistern charged at about nine and taken in fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of them, the figure written against the rota slate at about one and not read out, and there is no charge on that cistern tomorrow. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Monday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eleventh days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-eighth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and ninety-sixth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-eighth day of not being asked anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-sixth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare six hundred and second night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and twentieth days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-fifth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninetieth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood four hundred and seventh day. A man of about fifty-one stands on the four hundred and thirty-eighth day of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The middle of those four columns stands headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, has never had anybody appointed over it, and that boy, whose surname is on no page of this harbor, is off the water for the seventh time. That fourth column has stood at one hundred and twenty-five through all of this with no line entered into it and is neither shut nor struck. That mole head has nine hulls lying about two sea miles off it where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. Nothing has been heard in the left side of the head of a man of about twenty-nine for the two hundred and ninety-sixth day, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern went at nine as it goes on a Monday and on a Thursday, fourteen men and fourteen trips, and not one of the fourteen put a foot on it, and the butt stood short at the end of that and had a skin on it by about one, and nobody drank it and nobody said that it was bad.

The figure went against that rota slate at about one in the hand of the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay and was not read out.

---

At about one a man of about thirty-six of the Cinder Bell came down that coast road with a page in his own hand in his coat and put it on the flat of that bench stone, and about nine people came and looked at it.

The four words at the top of it are that the Cinder Bell is off that shared stretch from the twenty-seventh day of September, and they are in that community's own hand on that community's own page, and about four of that nine cannot read them and heard them read out once.

---

The keeper said out loud, to about nine, that a community may come off a shared stretch and that it takes one person of that community to say it, and that this yard has never once had a turn given to anybody who has not asked for a turn.

He said that the man of about thirty-six had come down that coast road of his own account on his own money and that nobody in that shed had sent for him, and that about nine people in that shed could have stopped him and none of them did and that this harbor has no standing to un-say a thing a person of a place has said on that place's page.

Nobody asked that man anything else and nobody put him on a step and he said nothing else himself, and he went back up that coast road at about half past two on the boat that took him.

---

Two marks went onto the face of that page at about half past two, both of them against the four words at the top of it and neither of them against the rest of the sheet, and they are the hands of a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay and a man of about fifty-eight of the chandler's store.

A mark is a second hand and is not an approval and is not a signature and is not a person, and the page of days carries two against this day.

---

The consequence of that falls on the twenty-seventh and not today, and the keeper said out loud that this yard does not put a cost on a page before the day the cost falls on it, and that anybody who wants to know what it will be has one more day of not knowing.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about four with the page of routes in his hand and was not asked anything about the Cinder Bell by anybody, and about four of that nine noticed that he did not say one word and said nothing about that either.

---

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-ninth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twenty-third day a step to the left of the refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at that end stall at fifteen with nothing on it moved.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was said at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and sixty-nine words, and the charge came first in it and the four words of that community came in the middle of it and no figure of a cost was in it at all.

The figure went against that rota slate at about one in the hand of the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay and was not read out.**"

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-ninth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its twenty-third day, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at that end stall at fifteen.**"

"**That foot was not written into today, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. That shed is two hundred and eleventh days without a tin opened in it. The middle of those four columns stands headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, has never had anybody appointed over it, that boy's surname is on no page, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five with no line entered into it and is neither shut nor struck. That mole head has nine hulls lying about two sea miles off it where nine lay on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. Nothing has been heard in the left side of the head of a man of about twenty-nine for the two hundred and ninety-sixth day, one more than the day before, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the four words at the top of that page or for the two marks or for the man who came down that road on his own money, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

---

**A ROTA DAY AND THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SEVENTH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN THIS MORNING AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. ONE PERSON OF ONE COMMUNITY HAS SAID IN HIS OWN HAND AND ON HIS OWN COMMUNITY'S PAGE THAT THAT COMMUNITY IS OFF THAT SHARED STRETCH FROM THE TWENTY-SEVENTH, AND THE COST OF IT IS NOT YET ON ANY PAGE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-sixth day of September at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day, being the hundred and eighty-seventh charge on that cistern, fourteen trips by hand, walked on by none of them, and that there is no charge on that cistern on the Tuesday; that the said day was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe and nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that at about one a man of about thirty-six of the Cinder Bell came down that coast road with a page in his own hand in his coat and put it on the flat of that bench stone, and that about nine people came and looked at it; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the cost was not written down on the said day is that a cost written down before it has fallen is only a guess, and this yard has been called wrong about a guess for nine years; that the reason the two marks went against the four words and not against the rest of that sheet is that the rest of that sheet is a community's own arithmetic and no person in this harbor has standing to put a mark on another place's arithmetic, and the four words are the one part of that page about which somebody here can say they have seen it; that the reason nobody in that shed tried to stop him is that a right to withdraw that this harbor can stop is not a right, and a right somebody here could have stopped would have been a favour with a word on it; and that the reason he was asked nothing about it is that the routes are his and what a community does with its own water is not a route of his to answer for, and asking him would have been asking him to hold the whole of that community against his own judgment in front of nine people.*
