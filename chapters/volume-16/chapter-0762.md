# Chapter 762

*Sunday the Twenty-First Day of August, the Second Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twentieth Time and the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off the Wall at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Twenty-Four Minutes and Back up at a Quarter to One and Not Whole, Thirty-Seven and Twenty-Four and Thirty-Seven Being Ninety-Eight the Whole of It, the Same Man of About Forty Round the Back of That Bench in the Bare Time Reading the Chalk on the Wall Underneath, a Wage Tuesday Tomorrow and No Charge on That Cistern Today, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Sunday the twenty-first day of August, with that market shut and the bars of the sheds down until about six and a hard clean morning with the wind gone into the west and a low swell that came up the harbour side and broke on the slip at about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and the hundred and twentieth time, the wall behind that reeve's bench off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-four minutes and back up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January and not whole, and thirty-seven and twenty-four and thirty-seven being ninety-eight the whole of it. Not a wage Tuesday and not a rota day, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it all day. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and the reason is entered in the second column, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of that Sunday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixtieth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-two days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundredth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and sixty-sixth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-fourth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and thirty-ninth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-fourth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-first day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and two days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. Nothing stands under the third of those four columns and nobody has ever been appointed to it, and the hand at its head is a boy's of twenty-two whose surname is on no page of this harbor, and he is off the water, while the fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in this run of days or in the one before and is neither shut nor struck and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one, and the three men who do that work did it in thirty-seven minutes each way and stood in the bare twenty-four minutes with a pot of tea and nothing else to do, and the wall was bare from about half past ten until about a quarter to one and nothing in that harbor stood open from five until twelve.

Under the plaster of that wall there is chalk, because the plaster has been off and on and off and on since the twelfth of January and the chalk a reeve's clerk wrote on the stone in the spring is still on the stone under it.

---

The man of about forty came round the back of that bench at about eleven, on his own foot and not asked, while the wall was bare, and read the chalk for about three minutes without touching it.

The chalk is a row of figures and no words, being a day of a boat hired and a day paid at a figure in chalk against it, and the day of the boat is the ninth of June and the day paid is the same day, and the figure is the figure in chalk that the berth fund pays for a day's work with a boat and two men.

---

A reeve's clerk of about twenty-six came out of the office at about eleven and found him at the wall and asked him what he was doing, and the man of about forty said he was reading, and the clerk said that was not on the bench and was not on the wall, and the man of about forty said it was on the wall and the wall was off.

Then he said out loud, to the clerk and not to anybody else, that the ninth of June was a Tuesday and that a boat hired on a Tuesday and paid on a Tuesday is one day and not two, and that somebody four days down that coast road has been carrying two.

---

The clerk of about twenty-six said that the figure in chalk is the figure in chalk and that a reeve's bench does not settle what a coast road settles, and went back in.

The man of about forty wrote the date of that June in the back of his own book with a pencil and nothing else against it, and no name, and put the book in his coat, and went away down the lane and not up the coast road.

---

The three men who put that wall up did it at a quarter to one and it went up at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole, and there is a strip of new plaster at the left-hand end about a hand's breadth wide that is a different colour from the rest of it and everybody in that harbor knows it and nobody has said anything about it.

Nobody has said why the wall is a minute longer each way than it was the time before, and nobody has asked.

---

The man of about thirty-two was not in that yard on that Sunday and was in that shed at about six in the evening, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that he had been on that wall in the spring and that a Tuesday hire is a Tuesday hire whatever a coast road says.

And the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about four that the figure in chalk on that wall is right and that a thing being right in one room and wrong in another is not a fault in either and is the ordinary condition of a coast with nine pages on it.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said at that stone a little before six in the evening, in about nine hundred and nine words, with about nine people standing about a yard off the flat of it because the floor of that shed was wet from the swell that came up the harbour side and broke on the slip at about eleven.

"**The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and went up at a quarter to one, and the three men who do that work did it in thirty-seven minutes each way and stood in the bare twenty-four minutes with a pot of tea and nothing else to do, and nothing in this harbor stood open from five until twelve. Under the plaster of that wall there is chalk, because the plaster has been off and on and off and on since the twelfth of January and the chalk a reeve's clerk wrote on the stone in the spring is still on the stone under it.**"

"**The man of about forty came round the back of that bench at about eleven, on his own foot and not asked, while the wall was bare, and read the chalk for about three minutes without touching it. The chalk is a row of figures and no words, being a day of a boat hired and a day paid at a figure in chalk against it, and the day of the boat is the ninth of June and the day paid is the same day, and the figure is the figure in chalk that the berth fund pays for a day's work with a boat and two men.**"

"**A reeve's clerk of about twenty-six came out at about eleven and found him at the wall and asked him what he was doing, and the man of about forty said he was reading, and the clerk said that was not on the bench and was not on the wall, and the man of about forty said it was on the wall and the wall was off. Then he said out loud, to the clerk and not to anybody else, that the ninth of June was a Tuesday and that a boat hired on a Tuesday and paid on a Tuesday is one day and not two, and that somebody four days down that coast road has been carrying two.**"

The man of about forty wrote the date of that June in the back of his own book with a pencil and nothing else against it, and no name, and put the book in his coat, and went away down the lane and not up the coast road. The wall went up at a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and there is a strip of new plaster at the left-hand end about a hand's breadth wide that is a different colour from the rest of it, and nobody has said why the wall is a minute longer each way than it was the time before.**"

"**And the rest of it. The man of about thirty-two was not in that yard today and was in that shed at about six in the evening, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that he had been on that wall in the spring and that a Tuesday hire is a Tuesday hire whatever a coast road says, and the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about four that the figure in chalk on that wall is right, and that a thing being right in one room and wrong in another is not a fault in either and is the ordinary condition of a coast with nine pages on it.**"

"**And a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, a Sunday being no different in that respect from any other day, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin has been opened in this shed since the twenty-ninth of April past and this is the one hundred and seventy-fifth day without one. A boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of the third of those columns and nothing has ever been entered under it and nobody has been appointed to it, that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor, and he is off the water. Nothing has gone into the fourth, which stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in this run of days or the one before and is neither shut nor struck and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. Two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls at this hour and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said a word about any of them easing. Out of the left side of his head a man of about twenty-nine hears nothing and it is two hundred and sixty days today, while the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it; and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and carries no figure.**"

Nobody was thanked for the wipe or for the chalk or for the reading, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

---

**THE WALL BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND WENT UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE, THIRTY-SEVEN AND TWENTY-FOUR AND THIRTY-SEVEN BEING NINETY-EIGHT THE WHOLE OF IT, AND IT DID NOT GO UP WHOLE. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY READ THE CHALK UNDER IT FOR ABOUT THREE MINUTES AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT A BOAT HIRED ON A TUESDAY AND PAID ON A TUESDAY IS ONE DAY AND NOT TWO, AND THAT SOMEBODY FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST ROAD HAS BEEN CARRYING TWO. A REEVE'S CLERK OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX SAID THAT A BENCH DOES NOT SETTLE WHAT A COAST ROAD SETTLES, AND HE WAS RIGHT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIVE DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-first day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and the hundred and twentieth time, the wall behind that reeve's bench being off the wall at about half past ten and standing bare twenty-four minutes and back up at about a quarter to one at the angle it has held at since the twelfth of January and not whole, being thirty-seven and twenty-four and thirty-seven and ninety-eight the whole of it; and that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was put on that cistern and its covers lay on it all day; and that under the plaster of that wall there is chalk, a row of figures and no words, being the ninth day of June and the same day paid, at the figure in chalk that the berth fund pays for a day's work with a boat and two men; and that the man of about forty came round the back of that bench at about eleven on his own foot and not asked and read the chalk for about three minutes without touching it; and that a reeve's clerk of about twenty-six asked him what he was doing and was told he was reading, and told that was not on the bench and was not on the wall, and heard that it was on the wall and the wall was off; and that the man of about forty said out loud to the clerk and to nobody else that the ninth of June was a Tuesday and that a boat hired on a Tuesday and paid on a Tuesday is one day and not two, and that somebody four days down that coast road has been carrying two; and that the clerk said the figure in chalk is the figure in chalk and that a reeve's bench does not settle what a coast road settles, and went back in; and that the man of about forty wrote the date of that June in the back of his own book with a pencil and no name against it and put the book in his coat; and that the wall went up not whole and there is a strip of new plaster at the left-hand end about a hand's breadth wide; and that at about six in the evening a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said a Tuesday hire is a Tuesday hire whatever a coast road says, and the man of about thirty-two said the figure in chalk on that wall is right and that a thing being right in one room and wrong in another is the ordinary condition of a coast with nine pages on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the man of about forty went round the back of that bench while the wall was bare is that a wall is the only paper in this harbor that comes off twice a month and a man who wants to see what is under one has about twenty-four minutes to do it in and no other chance in a fortnight; that at about eleven the reason a reeve's clerk of about twenty-six did not ask him to move on is that a man standing at a wall that is off is not a man standing at a bench, and the bench is the thing the office has and the wall is the thing the office does not have; that at about six the reason the man of about thirty-one of the second lay said a Tuesday hire is a Tuesday hire is that a man who was on that wall in the spring has seen the figure in chalk twice and has never seen the page it is on, and a man who has seen a thing twice believes it the way he has seen it; and that the reason the wall gets a minute longer each way than it was the time before is entered in a clerk's book in this harbor and not in this yard, and this yard has not seen that book and is not going to ask for it.
