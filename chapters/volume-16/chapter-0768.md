# Chapter 768

*Saturday the Twenty-Seventh Day of August, the Eighth Day, the Correction Going onto the Outside Copy at About One With the Name of the Man Who Wrote It Under the Total and the Wrong Figure Left Showing Underneath It and Not Struck Out, Line Two Going onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Two Carrying the Twenty-Eighth on Its Face as the Day the Page Comes Off That Wall and Both Days Said Aloud in About Nine People Before Any Figure Was Written, a Wipe Tomorrow and a Charge on That Cistern on Monday, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and One and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Saturday the twenty-seventh day of August, with that market open from about nine and a flat grey morning with the wind gone back into the west and a long swell outside the bar that came in on the flood, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The correction went onto the outside copy of that leaf at about one, being one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, with the name of the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run under the total and the wrong figure left showing underneath it and not struck out. Line two went onto that fitting-out sheet at about two, and the day printed on the face of that line is Sunday the twenty-eighth, being the day the page comes off that wall, and both days were said out loud in about nine people before a single figure was written, being five hundred and eighty-two pence, being three marks, eight shillings and sixpence, in five parts. Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and there is a wipe tomorrow and a charge on that cistern on Monday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried through this Saturday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and sixty-sixth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty-eight days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and seventy-second night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and ninetieth days and no figure is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and forty-fifth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and sixtieth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and seventy-seventh day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred and eight days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The third of those columns has nothing in it and nobody appointed to it, and the hand at the head of it is a boy's of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth of those columns stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-six days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

A lane behind that row of chandler's stores came down to that quay at about noon and a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed came up it on her own foot with a bundle and stayed in it, and she did not come to the end stall and she did not come to that shed.

She came at about one because the man of about forty had sent word up the road on the Friday night and not this morning, and the word was that a correction was going onto a page in this harbor and that a place which had been working from that page was welcome to send a hand.

---

At about one the correction went onto that copy in the yard's own mouth, and the words were that the total at the foot of that leaf carries one day of hire and not two, that the day is the ninth of June, and that the four parts above the total are each true and are not touched.

And under that, on the same face, in the same hand, was the name of the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run, and the wrong figure was left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, because a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that a figure a stranger cannot see has been corrected and a figure a stranger can see has been corrected in public, and that those are two different things and this coast has only ever done the first one.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed read that copy from end to end at about half past one and did not touch it, and then she said one thing out loud and it was that her page is not that page.

And the keeper said out loud, in about nine, that the Lowfen Reed is on the chain of that crossing and that the Lowfen Reed has a bell of its own on that stretch, and that a copy that has the name of a place on the top of it has that place's name on it and not the name of the place that got it copied.

And the woman of about twenty-nine said out loud that she is not here about the total and is here about the page, and that the page of that route is not kept in that room and is not in any room in this harbor.

---

At about two the keeper wrote line two onto that fitting-out sheet and read the two days against it out loud in about nine people before he wrote a single figure, and the two days are the twenty-seventh for the work and the twenty-eighth, being the day the page comes off that wall, which is a day that has not happened yet and is a day a woman of that other community has put on a calendar four days up that road.

Then he wrote the five parts, being two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, four panes of sheet glass at forty-eight pence, a case and four nails at thirty pence, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence.

Three hundred and thirty-six plus forty plus forty-eight plus thirty plus one hundred and twenty-eight is five hundred and eighty-two pence, being three marks, eight shillings and sixpence.

---

A man of about twenty-six of that market put two marks on the face of the correction itself at about half past two, one under the first line of it and one beside the name, and the reason he gave out loud was that a correction with one hand on it is a sentence and a correction with a second hand under it is a thing somebody has been shown.

The page of days carries two against the said day and those two are marks and not approvals and are not signatures and are not people.

---

About half past five on that Saturday the yard's own mouth gave the day up at the flat of that stone, in about eight hundred and forty-six words, to about nine people, and one of the nine had signed a piece of paper in the morning.

"**A woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed came up that lane at about noon on her own foot with a bundle and stayed in it, and did not come to the end stall and did not come to this shed. She came at about one because the man of about forty had sent word up the road on the Friday night and not this morning, and the word was that a correction was going onto a page in this harbor and that a place which had been working from that page was welcome to send a hand.**"

"**At about one the correction went onto that copy in this yard's own mouth, and the words were that the total at the foot of that leaf carries one day of hire and not two, that the day is the ninth of June, and that the four parts above the total are each true and are not touched. Under that, on the same face and in the same hand, was the name of the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run, and the wrong figure was left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, because a man of about thirty-one of the second lay said out loud that a figure a stranger cannot see has been corrected and a figure a stranger can see has been corrected in public.**"

"**The woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed read that copy from end to end at about half past one and did not touch it, and said one thing out loud and it was that her page is not that page. The keeper said out loud in about nine that the Lowfen Reed is on the chain of that crossing and has a bell of its own on that stretch, and that a copy which has the name of a place on the top of it has that place's name on it and not the name of the place that got it copied. And she said out loud that she is not here about the total and is here about the page, and that the page of that route is not kept in that room and is not in any room in this harbor.**"

"**At about two the keeper wrote line two onto that sheet and read the two days against it out loud before he wrote a single figure, being the twenty-seventh for the work and the twenty-eighth for the day the page comes off that wall, which is a day that has not happened yet and is a day a woman of that community has put on a calendar four days up that road. The five parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, four panes of sheet glass at forty-eight pence, a case and four nails at thirty pence, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at one hundred and twenty-eight pence, and the sum of those is five hundred and eighty-two pence, being three marks, eight shillings and sixpence.**"

"**At about half past two a man of about twenty-six of that market put two marks on the face of that correction, one under the first line of it and one beside the name, and said out loud that a correction with one hand on it is a sentence and a correction with a second hand under it is a thing somebody has been shown.**"

"**And the rest of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks. This yard is one hundred and eighty-one days without a tin in it and the last one was on the twenty-ninth of April past. Nobody has been appointed to the third of those four columns and nothing has been entered in it, and the hand at its head is a boy's of twenty-two whose surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and sixty-six days today, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the correction or for the hand that came four days up that road, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**THE CORRECTION WENT ONTO THAT COPY AT ABOUT ONE WITH A NAME UNDER IT AND THE WRONG FIGURE LEFT SHOWING UNDERNEATH IT AND NOT STRUCK OUT. A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE OF THE LOWFEN REED CAME FOUR DAYS UP THAT ROAD ON HER OWN FOOT AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HER PAGE IS NOT THAT PAGE AND THAT IT IS NOT IN ANY ROOM IN THIS HARBOR. LINE TWO WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT TWO CARRYING THE TWENTY-SEVENTH AND THE TWENTY-EIGHTH ON ITS FACE, THE TWENTY-EIGHTH BEING A DAY THAT HAS NOT HAPPENED YET, AND BOTH DAYS WERE SAID ALOUD BEFORE ANY FIGURE WAS WRITTEN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-seventh day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that there is a wipe on the twenty-eighth and a charge on that cistern on the twenty-ninth; and that a woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed came up the lane behind that row of chandler's stores at about noon on her own foot with a bundle and stayed in it, and did not come to the end stall and did not come to that shed; and that at about one the correction went onto the outside copy in the yard's own mouth, being that the total at the foot of that leaf carries one day of hire and not two, that the day is the ninth of June, and that the four parts above the total are each true and are not touched, and that the name of the man of about forty of the Ashtide Run went under the total in the same hand on the same face, and that the wrong figure was left showing underneath the correction and not struck out; and that the woman of about twenty-nine read that copy from end to end at about half past one and did not touch it and said out loud that her page is not that page, and that the keeper said out loud in about nine that the Lowfen Reed is on the chain of that crossing and has a bell of its own on that stretch, and that she said out loud that she is not here about the total and is here about the page and that the page of that route is not kept in that room and is not in any room in this harbor; and that at about two line two went onto that fitting-out sheet carrying the twenty-seventh and the twenty-eighth on its face, both days having been said out loud in about nine people before any figure was written, in five parts, being five hundred and eighty-two pence; and that at about half past two a man of about twenty-six of that market put two marks on the face of that correction, one under the first line of it and one beside the name.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about one the reason the wrong figure was left showing under the correction is that a page on which a stranger cannot see what was there before cannot be argued with, and a page a stranger can argue with can be argued with by the people it hurts as well as the people it helps; that at about half past one the reason the woman of about twenty-nine said her page is not that page is that a copy of a page is a copy of the page and not of the book the page was in, and a community whose book is not in this harbor cannot be shown a correction to a copy of one of its leaves and be asked to sign for the book; that at about two the reason the twenty-eighth is on the face of that line is that the twenty-eighth is the day a page comes off a wall, and a sheet that carries a day a thing is going to happen on is a sheet a stranger can hold against the calendar afterward; and that at about half past two the reason two marks went onto the face of the correction and not one is that one mark says a man has looked at a thing and two marks say a man has looked at it and another man has been shown it, and the second of those is the only one of the two that a community can be asked to rely on.
