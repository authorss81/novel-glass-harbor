# Chapter 789

*Saturday the Seventeenth Day of September, the Twenty-Ninth Day, an Ordinary Working Day With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement and Nothing into the Foot of That Fitting-Out Sheet, a Boat of That Route's Own Hired at the Figure in Chalk at About Ten Whether Anybody Was Going Out on Her or Not, the Woman of About Twenty-Nine of the Lowfen Reed Going up That Coast Road at About Seven in the Morning With a Bundle of Her Own on Her Back and Nothing in Her Bag, a Woman of About Thirty-Four of the Saltmarch Coming in at About Three and Standing Beside Three Figures of Her Own for About Half an Hour, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Four Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Saturday the seventeenth day of September, with that market open from about nine and a fine still morning with the wind in the north and the first of that fog coming up the water at about eleven and gone by one, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, and there is no charge on that cistern until the morning of Monday, no wipe comes before Sunday, and the next wage Tuesday is the twentieth of September. A boat of that route's own was hired at about ten at the figure in chalk and was paid for whether anybody was going out on her or not, and that hire is a figure in chalk and not a line on that fitting-out sheet. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, and that foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and second days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and eighty-seventh day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and eighty-ninth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and twenty-seventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare five hundred and ninety-third night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and eleventh days and no figure of money stands against either of them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and sixty-sixth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-first day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninety-eighth day. And a man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and twenty-ninth day of saying nothing at all to anybody about a page of six names. Nothing has ever been entered under the head of the middle of those four columns, which is a boy's hand of twenty-two, and nobody has ever been set over it, that boy's surname being on no page of this harbor and his being off the water. The fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line into it and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears nothing out of the left side of his head and it is the two hundred and eighty-seventh day of that, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed went up that coast road at about seven that morning with a bundle of her own on her back and nothing at all in the bag on her shoulder, and about four people in that market watched her go and none of them said anything to her about it.

Nobody sent for her and nobody carried anything for her. She had been in that yard three days and had been given nothing to do and had asked for nothing, and she went up the road with the fortnight of that bell written in her own hand on her own page and with a mark on the face of it that she did not put there.

---

A boat of that route's own was hired at about ten at the figure in chalk and was paid for that day whether anybody went out on her or not, and nobody went out on her.

The keeper wrote that figure in chalk on the board inside the door and read it out loud to about four people and no fifth person heard it, and about four of that nine at half past five said out loud that it is the cheapest day this harbor buys and that a day asked for by name is a day that gets paid for whatever the water does.

---

The yard did its ordinary work and the ordinary work of that day was the whole of it, being line against a gauge and a length of coil cut down and a kettle set on the brick at the back for the men that come in at four.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about one and was not asked anything by anybody, and about four of that nine have now stopped counting how often he is in there.

At about eleven the keeper went up to the room over that chandler's store with a jug of water and came back down in four minutes, and that wall is bare and has been bare since the fourteenth of August and the board beside it has stood empty from the same hour since the same day, and nothing went up on either of them and neither is on any page of this harbor.

---

A woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch came into that yard at about three and put three figures of her own on the end stall and stood beside them and did not hand them to anybody.

They were the same three figures, in the same order, in her own hand, as she had put them on the twenty-fifth day of August, and the middle one of the three is the figure that was on the outside copy and the last one of the three is the figure with one day of a boat hired taken out of it.

She said out loud, to about nine, that she has been told three times since the twenty-fifth of August that the bell is going to be seated on a Wednesday, and that there have been three Wednesdays, and that the bell is standing in a room at the Saltmarch leaning against a wall with nobody's mark on it, and that she is not accusing anybody and has come to say the number of Wednesdays.

---

A man of about forty-nine of the west end said out loud that the correction is on that board at the end stall and the refusal is on that board at the end stall and neither of them has been taken down, and that the Saltmarch is not asking this harbor to be wrong and is not asking it to be right, and that a place which has been right three weeks ago and is still right three weeks on is not waiting for an answer, it is waiting for a Wednesday.

The keeper said out loud that the days the men are off the water are that community's own figure and are not on any sheet in this yard, and that he has no column for them and has never had one, and that this harbor has not got a thing to give her and has not got a thing to take from her either.

---

The woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch said out loud, before she went, that she knew that, and that she had said it three times at that end stall and would say it a fourth time on the next Wednesday, and that the saying of it costs her the walk and costs nobody else anything at all.

She picked her three figures up off the end stall at about half past three and folded them once and put them inside her coat, and about nine people watched her do that as well, and nobody in that shed said a word about it.

---

Nobody put a mark on the face of anything that day and the column of the second hand carries nothing against it, and a day with no figure on it is not a zero and is nowhere on it.

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twentieth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its fifteenth day a step to the left, and nobody said which of the two of them is right.

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the tenth day of being out of service and the fourteen days of it run to the twenty-second of September.

---

Nobody was thanked for the hire of that boat or for the three figures or for the fourth Wednesday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

**THREE WEDNESDAYS HAVE COME AND GONE SINCE THE TWENTY-FIFTH DAY OF AUGUST AND A BELL IS STANDING IN A ROOM AT THE SALTMARCH LEANING AGAINST A WALL WITH NOBODY'S MARK ON IT. A BOAT WAS PAID FOR TODAY WHETHER ANYBODY WENT OUT ON HER OR NOT. NO MARK WENT ONTO THE FACE OF ANY PAGE TODAY AND THE COLUMN OF THE SECOND HAND CARRIES NOTHING AGAINST THIS DAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SECOND DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that there is no charge on that cistern until the morning of Monday and no wipe comes before Sunday and the next wage Tuesday is the twentieth of September; that a boat of that route's own was hired at about ten at the figure in chalk and was paid for that day whether anybody went out on her or not, and that nobody went out on her, and that that hire is a figure in chalk and not a line on that fitting-out sheet; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that the wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has been bare since the fourteenth of August and the board beside it has stood empty from the same hour since the same day, and nothing went up on either of them; that at about three a woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch put three figures of her own on the end stall and stood beside them and did not hand them to anybody, being the same three figures in the same order in her own hand as she had put them there on the twenty-fifth day of August; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that hire is written in chalk on a board inside the door and read out loud to about four people and no fifth is that the men who pay it out of their own pockets are not this harbor's men and are owed by this harbor nothing, and the only thing a yard can honestly give a man who pays his own way is to say the figure out loud so that he has not had to say it twice; that the reason she folded those three figures up and took them off that end stall instead of leaving them standing is that a sheet which has been standing for three Wednesdays is not a notice any more, it is a piece of furniture, and a place whose own page has become furniture in another place's market has stopped being heard at all; that the reason three figures of her own stood on that end stall from about three until about half past three with no hand on any one of them is that about four of that nine had spent the whole day waiting to see whether anybody would put a mark against a figure that is not theirs and was never going to be, and that a man who puts his hand on a figure he has no standing over would be the second man to do it in this harbor this month, and this yard would rather be the first of the two than the second.*
