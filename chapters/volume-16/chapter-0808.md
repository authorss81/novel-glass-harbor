# Chapter 808

*Thursday the Sixth Day of October, the Forty-Eighth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninetieth Time, That Cistern Charged at About Nine in Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It and the Last Charge on That Cistern in This Run of Days, the Figure Written Against That Slate at About One and Not Read Out, a Keeper Saying Out Loud at About Three That a Page Which Says It Can Be Repaired by Anybody Has to Be Kept Where a Person Can Find It, the Man of About Thirty-Two up That Coast Road and the Woman of Thirty of No Office in That Record Room, no Wage Tuesday and No Wipe, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Thursday the sixth day of October, with that market open from six and a hard north-easterly from about seven with white water on the bar and the mole head not visible from the yard at eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and the hundred and ninetieth time, that cistern charged at about nine and taken in fourteen trips by hand and walked on by none of them, and that is the last charge on that cistern in this run of days, the next one falling on the morning of Monday the tenth of October, outside it. The figure went against that rota slate at about one and was not read out. Not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short being what stands at the foot of it against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, with one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Thursday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-first days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and sixth day. That bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and eighth day of not being anybody's question, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-sixth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twelfth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirtieth day and no figure of money is set against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-fifth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundredth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and seventeenth day. The man of about fifty-one came to the four hundred and forty-eighth day of not speaking about six names and wiped down a bench he had no need to wipe. That boy's hand stands at the head of nothing whatever on the forty-eighth day, and he has not been on the water since the spring and nobody has asked him about it. Nothing has gone into that fourth column, which stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and no line of any kind was ruled against it this day. The nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head as they lay on the twenty-first of January, and this run of fifty days has never once said a word about what they are doing. Three hundred and six days is where that hearing stands at the end of this week and one more than the day before is all this run of days has done with it.**

---

That cistern was charged at nine in fourteen trips with a hard north-easterly coming over the top of that market and the water going sideways into the yard, and not one of the fourteen stood on the butt. The butt was chained over at about eleven and the two men who chained it were the same two who chain it every time and neither of them has ever said why they were there at eleven on a morning when their own work was done at nine.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was three boats off that mole before noon with the bar across the mouth of it, and a coil of rope cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet because the bar was across that mole and there is no hour against any of that work. The four new crews were on their eighteenth day and did the day's work in about two hours under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay.

The man of about thirty-two went up that coast road at about seven in the morning and was not in that yard at all that day, and the woman of thirty of no office was in the room over that fish market from about ten until about two with the leaf of that room on the table in front of her, and neither of them saw the other one and neither of them signed the other's page.

---

It is the tenth morning of the Saltmarch taking that shared boat alone and of the Cinder Bell hiring one of its own for that same stretch, and that is now an ordinary arrangement between two places and is written in chalk on two boards a hundred yards apart, and not one of the three figures of it is on any page of this yard. About four people in this market could say that the bell of the Cinder Bell has not been rung on that stretch since the twenty-seventh and about four could not, and neither of those four has asked the other why.

At about three the keeper said out loud, to about nine, that a page which says on its own face that anybody who has to work under it is free to repair it has to be kept where a person can find it, and that there is a drawer in the corner of that room over the chandler's store for pages and that a thing kept in a drawer has to carry on the outside of that drawer what is in it and the day it went in. He said that nobody here has ever had to write that on the outside of anything because there has never been anything here to write it on, and about four of that nine said nothing and about four said they had never thought about where a thing like that went.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about one and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fifty-eighth day of that run of days, that run having begun on Wednesday the tenth day of August.

The notice of the hundred and first wage Tuesday is still on that board at that end stall on its second day and nobody has taken it down, and it is the only notice on that board and it will be the only notice on that board when the next one goes up on a Tuesday outside this run of days. About five people in that market cannot read it and about four have read it and one of those four has copied it out into a book of his own, and that book is not in this yard and is not on any page here and was not asked for by anybody.

The two sheets at that end stall were on their thirty-ninth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its thirty-fourth day a step to the left of that refusal, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stood at fifteen with nothing on it moved. The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August, and nothing went up on it and nothing came down off it, and there is no figure of that wall or of that drawer on any page of this harbor.

---

"**That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and twenty-first days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads nothing at all and nothing has gone under it, that boy's surname is on no page, he is off the water for the seventh time, and the fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five with no line ruled against it and is neither shut nor struck. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head where nine lay on the twenty-first of January and this run of days has said nothing about what they are doing. A man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head for the three hundred and sixth day, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two has not come back and is not priced.**"

Nobody was thanked for the last charge on that cistern in this run of days or for a drawer nobody has written on yet, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**A ROTA DAY AND THE HUNDRED AND NINETIETH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND IT IS THE LAST CHARGE IN THIS RUN OF DAYS. A KEEPER HAS SAID OUT LOUD THAT A PAGE SAYING IT CAN BE REPAIRED BY ANYBODY HAS TO BE KEPT WHERE A PERSON CAN FIND IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason not one of the fourteen put a foot on that butt is that a cistern in the back of a market is charged so that the market has water and not so that anybody in particular does, and the fourteen who carried it are the same fourteen every Monday and Thursday and have never asked to be thanked for it and have never been thanked for it; that the reason the rule has not gone onto that wall is that the plaster in that room receives claims and corrections put up in public and refusals and restricted records, and a thing a keeper keeps for six days in the inside of a door is none of those four and putting it there would make it a fifth; that the reason two boards a hundred yards apart carry the same figure in chalk is that the two places have agreed between themselves what a boat costs and neither of them needs this harbor to write it down twice, and that a figure in chalk on two boards is still a figure and not a page; that the reason nobody in that shed said what it is to keep a page for a year is that a year is a thing this run of days has never once had to think about, and the fifty days it has had are forty-eight.*
