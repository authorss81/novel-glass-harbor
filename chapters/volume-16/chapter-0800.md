# Chapter 800

*Wednesday the Twenty-Eighth Day of September, the Fortieth Day, an Ordinary Working Day With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement and the Next Charge on the Morning of Thursday, the Man of About Thirty-Two in That Shed From About Ten With the Page of Routes in His Hand and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Not in It, the Correction and the Refusal Both Still on the Wall at That End Stall on Their Thirty-First Day at the Same Size and a Hand's Breadth Apart and Neither of Them Taken Down, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Thirty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Wednesday the twenty-eighth day of September, with that market open from six and a hard clear morning with the wind in the east and the whole of that mole head in view from about seven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday, the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the second of October and the next wage Tuesday is on the fourth of October. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Wednesday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirteenth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fortieth day, and that is the last time in this run of fifty days that this yard has stood a man of about thirty-one of the second lay in front of a page of days. That fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five in it and has never had anything else in it, and it is neither shut nor struck. Over the middle of those four columns there is a boy's hand of twenty-two and nothing has ever been set above it, that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor, and he is off the water for the seventh time. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and ninety-eighth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundredth day of nobody asking him anything and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-eighth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare six hundred and fourth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone four hundred and twenty-second days and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventy-seventh day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and ninety-second day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street has stood four hundred and ninth day. A man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fortieth day of not saying one word to anybody about a page of six names. There are nine hulls lying about two sea miles off that mole head, as there were on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. It is the two hundred and ninety-eighth day, one more than the day before, that a man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head, no figure is set against that figure, and he is not on the water.**

---

That correction and that refusal were both still on that wall at that end stall on their thirty-first day, at the same size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. They are the same two sheets that were on that wall on the twenty-ninth of August and they are the same size they were on the day they went up, and neither of them has been out in the weather because both of them are under the awning of that stall and always have been.

Nobody in this harbor has said which of the two of them is right, and nobody in this harbor has taken either of them down, and the four heads over that wall in the room over that fish market are claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records, and all four of them have been used and there is nothing else on that wall. About four people in this harbor have read both of them and about four have read one and not the other and about four have read neither, and the three of those groups are all in that lane most days of a week and have never said one word to each other about any of the three.

Nobody has put a fifth thing on that wall on account of a correction, a withdrawal, a treaty, a vote or a rule, and the wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has been bare since the fourteenth of August. The board beside that wall is on no page of this harbor and has not been on one since the fourteenth of August, and a person standing in that lane would have to be told by somebody that there is a board there at all.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was the mole-head light being turned by hand and a coil of rope being measured, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet because a line with an hour against it and no boat against it is not a line. The light was turned by a man of about thirty-eight of that landing with a spanner and a cloth and he did it in about a quarter of an hour, and the cloth was put back in the same drawer it came out of, and there is no figure against any part of that hour.

The four new crews were on their tenth day and did the day's work in about two hours and a half under the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay, and about four of that nine said out loud that they were quicker than the man of about thirty-two had been on his tenth day, which is a thing about four people said and not a finding. The man of about thirty-two was given the book of hours with them at about ten and wrote nothing at all in it for the whole morning, and about four of that nine saw the whole of that morning and have not said anything about it since and are not going to.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about four with the page of routes in his hand and the tally slate in front of him, and the woman of thirty of no office was not in that yard and was three miles up that coast road, and neither of them signed the other's page that day and neither of them said one word to the other and neither of them gave up an inch of their own work. He read that page of routes the whole of that day and turned one corner of it over at about three and put it back the same way up, and a man is not paid for turning a corner of a page and this yard is not a place where a man is paid for not turning one either.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the fiftieth day of the run of days on which that has happened, that run of days having begun on Wednesday the tenth day of August, ten days before this run of fifty days began. He has been on that bench every day of those fifty days without a break and has not been asked one question, and there is no figure against him and there never has been, and the two of those things have been in this yard at the same time since the first of them.

---

The column of the withdrawn was not taken in these ten days and is not taken anywhere on this page, and the column of the second hand carries a figure on five of the ten days of this run, being the third, the fourth, the sixth, the eighth and the ninth, and those five figures are not added together in that shed and are not added together in this movement. On each of those five days a page left this harbor with a second person's hand on its face, and on the other five of these ten days no page left any building in this harbor at all.

The drawer on that hill was not opened in these ten days and no digit of the three numbers in that room is on any page, and the claim against the sealed record stands released and is not exercised and is not argued about. Nobody went up that hill in these ten days, and the road up it takes about half an hour at a walking pace, and there is nobody in this harbor who has not at some time walked up it for a reason that was not that, and not one of those reasons is on any page.

This yard has gone two hundred and thirteenth days without a tin in it. Nobody was thanked for the ten days or for the two sheets that are still on that wall, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the two of those sheets are described here and are kept out of the running sheets of that yard altogether is that the three columns on that leaf are a tally of what this yard itself did and those two sheets are two things two places did, and this harbor keeps no tally of what other people do with their own pages; that the reason no figure of that column of the second hand was added up in these ten days is that a sum taken day by day in a shed is an argument and a sum taken once at the end of a run of fifty days is a figure a stranger can check with a pencil; and that the reason the wrong figure is left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out is that striking it out would leave a hole in a board that other people read every day, and a hole is a thing that a person standing in that lane at six in the morning has to decide what it means; and that the reason nobody said which of the two of them is right is that each was put up in a hand of its own at the same size by people who had read the other one, and that saying it here would be saying it with a page out of the room.*
