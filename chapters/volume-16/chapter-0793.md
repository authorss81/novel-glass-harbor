# Chapter 793

*Wednesday the Twenty-First Day of September, the Thirty-Third Day, an Ordinary Working Day With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement, Four New Crews Third Day, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud in About Four That He Is Worse at It Than the Men He Is Teaching, the Bell of the Cinder Bell Out of Service on Its Fourteenth and Last Day of Fourteen, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Twenty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Four*

Wednesday the twenty-first day of September, with that market open from six and a hard south-wester that had got up by nine and a sea over the coping of the mole by eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working day, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe and no wage movement in it, and there is no charge on that cistern until the morning of Thursday and the next wipe is tomorrow morning. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Wednesday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried into the close of this thirty-third day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past has now reached its four hundred and ninety-first day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the four hundred and ninety-third day of being nobody's question. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and thirty-first day. That bench under that pump is on the five hundred and ninety-seventh night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifteenth day and no figure of money is set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and seventieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and eighty-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and second day. Nothing whatever has been said by a man of about fifty-one about a page of six names for four hundred and thirty-three days. The middle of those four columns is headed in a boy's hand of twenty-two and has never had a figure entered under it, nobody has been set over it, and that boy is off the water and is on no page by his surname. The fourth of those four has taken no line into it and stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has been neither shut nor struck. Off that mole head, about two sea miles out, nine hulls are lying, and nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. The left side of the head of a man of about twenty-nine has been silent for the two hundred and ninety-first day, a day more than the day before, nothing is set against that figure, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was a coil of line being served out and two seats being faced for a lamp that is not being set this month, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four and nothing else about money happened in that yard at all. The wind had got up by nine and the sea was over the coping of that mole by eleven, so the two seats were faced with the door propped on a brick and both of those jobs were done inside with the door standing open anyway.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that shed from about eleven until about four and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the forty-third day of this run of days on which that has happened. Some of that nine had stopped keeping the count of it and some had not, and neither of those two facts had ever been said out loud in that yard.

---

The four new crews were on their third day and the man of about thirty-two was still standing at the back of that bench with them and talking, and at about eleven he stopped and stood still and then said it out loud to about four and not to about nine. He had a hand on the back of a chair and he took it off it before he began, and the rain that had been coming in off the water in long sheets since before nine went on against that door while he said it and nobody went to shut it.

He said that he was worse at that work than the men he was teaching, and that he had known it since the Tuesday, and that on Monday the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay had done in about a minute and a half on that bench the thing he himself had needed a fortnight on in the spring of this year, and that he was not going to be put in front of those four crews again on anything that can be done properly by one of the men standing in it. Nobody in that shed had asked him for it and he gave it anyway, and what he said out loud was that he would rather be at the far end of that shed with a coil of line than at the head of that bench.

About four of that nine heard all of it and nobody said anything to him about it, and one of those four was the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay and he said afterwards that it was the truth and that it was not news. He said it to about four and not to the man it was about, and he wiped his hands down his legs before he said it, and he did not look over at the far end of that shed while the other man was standing in it.

---

The man of about thirty-two went and worked at the far end of that shed from about half past eleven until about four, and the four new crews were put in front of the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay at about twelve and did in about two hours what they had not managed in two days.

Nobody in that yard called that a change of anything and nothing standing was given away, moved, closed or struck by it. The keeper wrote one line in the middle of that column and no more, and what went onto it was the hour and the day, and he has never been able to say afterwards which of the two of them he would have given up first.

---

At about one a sheet of hours for those four crews was written out and it went up that coast road on the evening boat at about five for the harbor authority on the west basin, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay put one mark onto the face of it against the day and the hour and not against any figure.

One mark is one hand and not the hand that wrote it, it is not an approval and it is not a signature and it is not a person, and the page of days carries one against this day. That mark went onto the head of that sheet and not against any of the hours on it, which is a thing a man does when he wants a page to be received and not read into.

---

The bell of the Cinder Bell is on the fourteenth day of being out of service, which is the last day of that fortnight this harbor will see, and it goes back into service at daybreak on the morning of the twenty-second of September, and nobody in this harbor is going to see any part of it. There is no hour tomorrow that a man in this yard will hear anything at, and the day after that there will be one again, and nothing has been written down about the fortnight in between.

The correction and the refusal were standing at that end stall on their twenty-fourth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell was on its nineteenth day a step to the left, and the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at that end stall at fourteen.

---

Nobody was thanked for what the man of about thirty-two said at about eleven or for the two hours the man of about twenty-seven gave those four crews, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

ONE MARK WENT ONTO THE FACE OF ONE SHEET OF HOURS AND AGAINST THE DAY AND THE HOUR AND NOT AGAINST ANY FIGURE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that hand was set against the hour and the day on that sheet and against none of its figures is that this yard has had a mark go against a figure before and the figure was carried to another harbor as a figure and arrived as an argument, and a mark on a day and an hour is a mark that cannot be carried as anything else; that the reason no note of what he said at about eleven went onto any page is that a man who says a thing about himself in about four people has not made a finding and this yard does not write other men's findings down; that the reason those four crews were put in front of the man of about twenty-seven without asking him is that a man who has been asked to take four crews on a Wednesday has said yes to four crews and not to a fifth thing, and putting the question again would have been asking him to say it twice; and that the reason the two hours those four crews then took is not on any sheet of this yard is that hours put in by a man who was not asked to put them in are the hours of the man who put them in and not of the four.*
