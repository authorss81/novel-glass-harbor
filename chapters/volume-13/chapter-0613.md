# Chapter 613

*Friday the Twenty-Fifth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, at About Eleven in the Morning on the Boards at the Back of That Market, in About Nine People of Whom About Four Can Read, in Front of That Harbor's Own Page of Answers Still on the Boards, a Woman of About Thirty-Nine Who Mends Net There and Has Mended Net There for Nineteen Years Saying No Out Loud in About Two Hundred and Eighty-Nine Words, and Naming the Position as One of the Four, and Giving the Ground of It Once and Not a Second Time, Nobody Answering It and Nobody Arguing With Her and Nobody Thanking Her and No One Asking Her a Second Time, a Man of About Thirty-Two Standing at the Back of Those Boards for About Eleven Minutes Afterwards and Saying Nothing at All, the Figure One Entered in the Refused Column Against That Day With the Hour of Eleven Against It and Nothing Rubbed Out and Nothing Struck, the Refused Column Carrying Three Rows Where It Carried Two, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Six and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Four*

Friday the twenty-fifth day of March, that market working underneath a sky that had not decided, the boards at the back of it standing full from about ten in the morning, and a yard below with a shed and a bench stone in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Friday with nothing on it that anybody put there: no charge on that cistern, its covers lying on it from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. That page of that harbor's own answers was still on the boards where it was nailed on Wednesday, and about nine people stood in front of it this morning, and about four of them could read what was on it. At about eleven a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards said no in about nine people, and she said which of the four she is standing in, and she gave the ground of it once. Nobody answered her. Nobody argued with her. Nobody thanked her. Nobody is going to ask her a second time in this harbor and that is not a promise this yard has broken about anything before. The money sheet took nothing in any hand and gave nothing back, and a word said out loud in a market is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. That yard's day rate is a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and the same amount away at four in the afternoon, and the twenty-eight pence lying under that slate at the end of it, being two shillings and fourpence, is six hundred and twelve pence behind a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, which is three marks eleven shillings. What has gone out of this yard on that sheet is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and what has ever come into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and what is not on it at all is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine durations went on a day each this week and nobody went looking for any of them. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-one days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and eleven. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and seventeenth night. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirteen. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-three days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-five. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninetieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fifth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-second. Five papers stand on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. In that boathouse the basket stands where it has stood since the second of this month with the paper still in it and the basket empty, and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and nobody has said that they have eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eleven days, one more than it was yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven on that Friday a woman of about thirty-nine who has mended net on those boards for nineteen years stood up in about nine people in front of a page of this harbor's own answers and said no.

She had said the word on the sixteenth and had changed it on the eighteenth, both times in that same place and in front of that same number of people, and neither of those two times had anybody asked her.

"**No, and I am saying it out loud so that it is on those boards and not in a kitchen. This harbor said opened on the sixteenth and said restricted on the eighteenth and I was the mouth of both of those and I am the mouth of this one, and the word is refused.**

**Every word that anybody in this harbor can ever say, printed in one place where any stranger can climb up and read it, cannot mean that everybody is out in the open. The day it means that, it has stopped being a record and it is a wall with a clerk standing behind it, and I will not be on that wall.**

**I will give every hour of that night except mine.** I have been at this end of these boards since before eleven o'clock and I have said so once in eleven weeks and I am not going to say it again today. Every other thing in this harbor can be checked by anybody who turns up, and an hour is the smallest thing a person can keep and it is the largest thing this method runs on. **The day my hour goes on that page is the day every hour in this harbor that anybody is ashamed of is a figure somebody can add up, and then it is not a set of people talking to each other at all. It is an inventory with a date on it.**

**Refused. Nobody is to argue with me about it and nobody is to ask me a second time and nobody is to thank me for it, because I have had a fortnight of being right in public and I am tired of it.**"

That is about two hundred and eighty-nine words and nobody asked her for one of them, and about four people on those boards heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody argued and nobody answered it and nobody thanked her and nobody said her name out loud after she had sat down.

---

The man who keeps the leaf did it at about ten past twelve with a pencil and with the whole of that nine watching and he did not consult anybody.

He wrote the figure one in the refused column against Friday the twenty-fifth with the hour of eleven standing against that figure, and under the figure he wrote the word refused. He did not strike the figure one in the restricted column against the eighteenth and he did not strike the figure one in the opened column against the sixteenth, and nothing was rubbed out and nothing was blotted and no day moved.

**The refused column of that page carried two rows at ten past twelve this morning and it carries three now, and two of those three rows have been standing since the fifteenth and have never been asked anything, and the third one went in this afternoon with the hour of eleven against it.** A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on all three and cannot tell from the page alone which of them means what, and that is the first thing that has gone wrong with that page since it was made and it is not anybody's fault.

---

A man of about thirty-two stood at the back of those boards for about eleven minutes after that with his hands in his coat and said nothing at all, and he did not write anything down, and he did not go into the water that day, and at about one o'clock he was at the bench stone in that yard doing ordinary work with a wedge and his hands were not as good on it as they were in the autumn.

Nobody in that yard went and stood next to him and nobody said one word to him about what had just been said on those boards, and that was arranged by nobody and it happened anyway.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and thirty-eight words, and nobody in that shed was asked what an hour is worth.

"**Somebody said no in this harbor this morning in nine people and I am not going to say her word back in this shed tonight, because writing it on a page is a different act from saying it in a market and I have done the second one already.** The figure on the face of that sheet is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Twenty-eight pence is under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten this morning and the same figure went out of it at four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**A figure went into the refused column of that page at about ten past twelve this afternoon with the hour of eleven against it, and nothing was struck and nothing was rubbed out, and that column carries three rows where it carried two this morning, and two of the three have been standing since the fifteenth and have never been asked one thing.**

**Here is what I have not done and what I am not going to do.** I have not asked her anything. I am not going to ask her anything on Monday. I am not going to ask her anything when this harbor has a bad week and needs her to say a different word. **A community is not asked which position it is in twice, and that rule was mine, and a rule of mine that I only keep when it is convenient is not a rule, it is a habit.**

And here is the other thing. That page was made to be a page about one thing and it is now a page about two things, and a page about two things is a page somebody has to be given a key to, and nobody in this harbor has a key. **A word in a column used to carry one meaning and it carried two meanings this afternoon, and that is not her doing and it is not anybody's doing, it is what happens when four words are asked to do the work of five, and I have known that since Wednesday and I have said it out loud for the first time tonight and I said it here and not in that shed this morning.**

The sheet with the fourteen things on it was not touched today and I read nothing out of it and no figure on it has moved and none of those fourteen has been added to and none taken off.

I entered a line on that first column at about six this evening and five on the second, and that leaf has not been read out loud in this harbor since the twelfth day of this month. Three hundred and forty-six stands against the first of those two figures tonight and one thousand six hundred and four against the second, the third took nothing, and the fourth is still at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a word said out loud in a market that was trading underneath it, and the shed was empty again by about six and the door stood on its latch with a slip on it and nobody on the slip. That page was still on the boards at the back of that market when the market shut, with three rows in one column of it instead of two.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE WHO MENDS NET ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET, AND HAS MENDED NET THERE FOR NINETEEN YEARS, SAID NO AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN FRONT OF THAT HARBOR'S OWN PAGE OF ANSWERS, AND NAMED THE POSITION AS ONE OF THE FOUR, BEING REFUSED, AND GAVE THE GROUND OF IT ONCE, AND NOBODY ANSWERED HER AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HER AND NOBODY THANKED HER AND NO ONE IS GOING TO ASK HER A SECOND TIME. THE FIGURE ONE WENT IN THE REFUSED COLUMN AGAINST THAT DAY WITH THE HOUR OF ELEVEN AGAINST IT, AND THE TWO FIGURES ALREADY STANDING WERE NOT STRUCK AND NOTHING WAS RUBBED OUT, AND THAT COLUMN CARRIES THREE ROWS WHERE IT CARRIED TWO AND TWO OF THE THREE HAVE NEVER BEEN ASKED ANYTHING. NO LINE OF THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WAS READ ALOUD AND NOT ONE FIGURE ON IT WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE, AND NOBODY PUT A PEN ON THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY. SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE IS WHAT THAT SHEET HAS COST THIS HARBOR, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE. SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE IS ALL THAT ANYBODY PUT INTO IT, BEING TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE. SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS IS WHAT IT IS SHORT, BEING NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*Second column, entered in that yard's own mouth on the said Friday at about six in the evening and not checkable from a mole, and no name is against it: that at about ten past twelve in the afternoon the reason I did not strike the figure in the restricted column is that a strike means one word was taken back under pressure, and that this morning was not one word being taken back, it was a second word said in a different column on a different day in front of the same nine people, and that two words on two days is a record and one word struck out of two is an argument somebody has had with himself; and that at about half past twelve the reason I have written down that a stranger cannot tell from that page which of those three rows means what is that in eleven weeks every page this yard has made has been a page about one thing, and that this page has just become a page about two things, and that a page about two things is a page somebody has to be given a key to, and that nobody in this harbor has a key and that is the fault of the page and not of the three rows; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not answer her is that she did not ask a question and a thing that is not a question cannot be answered without turning it into one, and that in eleven weeks every time somebody has answered a refusal this harbor has ended up arguing about the refusal instead of about the thing refused, and that I have seen four men do that and I am not going to be the fifth; and that at about one o'clock in the afternoon the reason I have written down that a man of about thirty-two stood at the back of those boards for eleven minutes and that nobody went and stood next to him is that he is a man who gets worse at a thing when a second pair of hands comes near it, and that there were four people in that nine who wanted to go over there and did not, and that wanting to is not doing and that the four of them did the harder thing and none of them will ever be told they did it; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says that a word said out loud in a market is not a line on the money sheet is that I have been asked by nobody this fortnight and I keep checking it anyway, and that a check that nobody asked for is the cheapest kind of honesty and I would rather be caught at that than at the other thing; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have not written in this column what her ground was is that she said it once, in a market, out loud, and that the two of us have now put it in three places today, and that a thing said once and written three times is three times as true and not one bit more checkable, and that this column is the one place in this harbor where a person can be wrong in private and nobody has to be told about it.*
