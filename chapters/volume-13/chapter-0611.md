# Chapter 611

*Wednesday the Twenty-Third Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Page of That Harbor's Own Answers Taken Out From the End of That Stall and Nailed Up on the Boards at the Back of That Market at About Ten in the Morning and Read Out Twice in About Nine People of Whom About Four Can Read It, Three Figures Standing on It and One of the Three Struck and Still Readable, a Woman of About Thirty-Nine Who Mends Net on Those Boards Standing in That Nine for the Whole of Both Readings and Saying Nothing at Either of Them and Not Saying Anything on Those Boards for Four Days Afterwards, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking One Question at About Half Past Eleven and Getting One Answer, Nothing Read Out of the Sheet of the Fourteen Gaps and No Figure on It Moved by One, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and the Same Figure Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Four and the Second at One Thousand Five Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Wednesday the twenty-third day of March, with that market trading all morning and about nine people standing on the boards at the back of it from about ten, and a yard four hundred yards off that market with a shed in it and a bench stone on the flat of it, and about nine people in that yard at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Wednesday carrying nothing that anybody has put on a calendar: the cistern at the back of that market untouched, its covers lying on it from eight until four, no rota day in it, no wage Tuesday in it and no wipe in it. A page came off the end of that stall at about ten in the morning and went up on those boards on a nail that was already in the wood, and it is the page this harbor keeps its own answers on and it is not the sheet under the stone at the head of those boards and it is not the sheet with the fourteen things on it and those three were named apart out loud twice while that page was being read. Three figures stand on it and one of those three has a stroke through it and is still readable under the stroke, and a figure on it is a count of positions and not a count of persons. Nothing went onto the money sheet in any hand and nothing came off it, and a nail in a board is not a line on that sheet. That yard takes a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning, which is one mark and eightpence, and hands the identical figure back out of it at four in the afternoon, and what the slate in that shed is lying on at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, which is two shillings and fourpence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so that slate is six hundred and twelve pence behind it, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the distance between those two figures is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and nobody in this harbor is going to be asked for the difference. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and not one penny of that page and those two nails has come out of that bag.**

**The nine durations in this harbor have gone on another day each and not one of them has been looked for. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and nine days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and eleven, and his chest stays shut and it is not a gift and it has not been bettered. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-nine days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and fifteenth night, and that is the one of the nine a stranger can see from a road. Those two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-three days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-eighth day, and there are two other papers standing beside that sheet this morning and none of the three is the sheet with the fourteen on it. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and third. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twentieth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-one days without a word to anybody about a page of six names, and that is the roundest of the nine and nobody has said so out loud. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody, and the answer to the offer of four lines is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and nine days, a day more than it was yesterday, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The page came off the end of that stall at about ten in the morning in the hand of the man who keeps it and it went up on those boards on a nail that was already in the wood above the head of the second one, and about nine people stood in front of it, and about four of them could read what was on it.

The woman of thirty of no office read it out twice, in the same words, and stopped at the foot of it both times without being asked to stop.

She read the three columns with something in them. She read the stroke through the figure one in the opened column against Wednesday the sixteenth, and the word opened standing under that figure, and then the figure one in the restricted column against Friday the eighteenth with the hour of eleven standing against that figure, and the word restricted under it with the word opened written again under that with one stroke through the word and not through the figure.

She read that both figures were standing there and neither of them had been rubbed out, and that neither of the two days had moved.

---

And the woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards stood in that nine for the whole of both readings and said nothing at either of them.

She had mended net on that set of boards for nineteen years and she was the mouth of this harbor's word on the sixteenth and on the eighteenth, and on this Wednesday she stood at the back of her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and let about nine people look at a page that said what she had said twice and let nothing come out of her.

Nobody asked her anything. About four people noticed that she had not said a word on those boards since the Friday and about five of the nine did not notice at all.

---

At about half past eleven a man of about thirty-three who hires hands came up those boards and asked one question and got one answer, and the question was what a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could do with a page of three figures in it.

The answer came from the woman of thirty of no office and it was about a hundred and fifty-nine words and it went to about four people.

"**That he can do the same thing with it twice and get the same figure twice, and that is the whole of what I have ever wanted a page to do.** He can go down the columns of the last day and add what is standing in them, and then he can go down the figures that have not been struck and count those, and the two numbers will be the same number, and if they are not the same number then one of us has written something down that we cannot stand behind. **A page that can be checked twice is worth a page that can be read aloud four times, and this harbor cannot read anything aloud more than twice.** The struck figure is not a gap and the three figures are not three people and the sheet with the fourteen things on it is a different sheet and I said so out loud twice this morning."

Nobody argued with that and nobody took it further, and nobody thanked her for reading a page out twice in a market that was trading underneath it.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and seventeen words, and about nine people were in that shed and none of them asked him what a missing thing looks like.

"**There is a page in this harbor with this harbor's own answers on it and it went up on those boards this morning and it was read out twice, and I have taken nothing off it and I have put nothing on it.** The figure of the gap in that yard is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag under the slate, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that has been true on every day of this fortnight including the Sunday that wall came down.

**Three figures stand on that page and one of the three has a stroke through it and is still readable, and a figure on that page is a position and not a person, and about four people standing at this bench stone can read all three of those figures and about five of the nine of us cannot read any of them.**

And here is the thing I have not said out loud to anybody this week. That page has been this harbor's own answers since the sixteenth and it has carried three figures on it since the eighteenth, and there has been one thing absent from it for four days, and I have watched that absence for four days and I have not asked about it, because a community is not asked which position it is in twice, and that rule is mine and I wrote it.

**I am not going to name what is absent tonight.** There is a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards and has mended net there for nineteen years, and on the Friday she stood up in nine people and changed this harbor's word, and she has said nothing on those boards since, and four days is not a long time and it is also four days.

The sheet with the fourteen things on it stands at the end of that stall where it stood, and I have read nothing out of it this fortnight, and no figure on it has moved by one, and none of those fourteen has been added to and none has been taken off, and a page nobody can see the edges of is a page somebody else fills in for you inside three years.

One line went in on the first of those four columns at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and that leaf was not read out loud. Three hundred and forty-four stands against the first of those two figures and one thousand five hundred and ninety-four against the second, the third is empty, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a page nailed to a board, and that shed stood empty by about six with the door standing open on a slip with nobody on it. That page was still on the boards at the back of that market when the market shut, with the stone and the sheet under the stone a yard and a half away at the head of them and nobody having moved either of them into a drawer.

**THE PAGE THIS HARBOR KEEPS ITS OWN ANSWERS ON WENT UP ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING ON THAT WEDNESDAY AND WAS READ OUT TWICE IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT FOUR CAN READ IT, AND IT CARRIES THREE FIGURES AND ONE OF THE THREE HAS A STROKE THROUGH IT AND IS STILL READABLE UNDER THE STROKE, AND A FIGURE ON IT IS A COUNT OF POSITIONS AND NOT OF PERSONS. THE SHEET WITH THE FOURTEEN THINGS ON IT WAS NOT READ OUT OF AND NO FIGURE ON IT MOVED BY ONE AND NO ONE OF THE FOURTEEN WAS ADDED TO AND NONE WAS TAKEN OFF. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED. THAT SHEET CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. UNDER THE SLATE IN THAT SHED IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-third day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it unbroken from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the page this harbor keeps its own answers on came off the end of that stall at about ten in the morning in the hand of the man who keeps it and went up on those boards on a nail that was already in the wood above the head of the second one, and that about nine people stood in front of it and about four of them could read what was on it; and that the woman of thirty of no office read it out twice in the same words and stopped at the foot of it both times without being asked to stop; and that what she read was the three columns with something standing in them, the stroke through the figure one in the opened column against Wednesday the sixteenth with the word opened under that figure, and then the figure one in the restricted column against Friday the eighteenth with the hour of eleven against that figure and the word restricted under it and the word opened written again under that with one stroke through the word and not through the figure, and that both figures stand and neither of the two days moved; and that a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards and has mended net there for nineteen years stood in that nine for the whole of both readings and said nothing at either of them, and that she was the mouth of this harbor's word on the sixteenth and on the eighteenth, and that nobody asked her anything, and that about four people noticed that she had said nothing on those boards since the Friday and about five of the nine did not notice at all; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question at about half past eleven and got one answer of about a hundred and fifty-nine words from the woman of thirty of no office, and that the question was what a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could do with a page of three figures in it, and that the answer was that he can do it twice and get the same figure twice, and that a page that can be checked twice is worth a page that can be read aloud four times, and that this harbor cannot read anything aloud more than twice; and that the sheet with the fourteen things on it was not read out of and no figure on it moved by one and no one of the fourteen was added to and none taken off; and that the sheet that came into that harbor under a stone was not opened and was not moved and the stone was still on it at the close of the day; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and seventeen words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second, and that the first of those figures is three hundred and forty-four and the second is one thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and that the third took nothing and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it anywhere, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month is the eleventh.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I nailed that page to those boards instead of holding it up is that four people in this harbor get shown things and about nine are able to compare them, and that the four are always the ones who get shown things, and that a page lying flat on a board at the height of a man's shoulder is the only kind of showing this yard has that does not pick who is looking; and that at about half past eleven the reason I have written down that a woman of thirty-nine said nothing twice this morning is that in eleven weeks every other thing said on those boards has had a word in it, and that a silence in a market in front of nine people is not a mood, and that I have found no way at all to write a silence down except to write down that it was one, and that is what this is; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says that four days is not a long time and that it is also four days, and says nothing else about it, is that a man of about thirty-two has been standing about nine feet from that woman every morning for a fortnight and has not once put his hand on her shoulder, and that the rule against a second question is a rule about words and not about shoulders, and that I do not know which of the two he is respecting and I am not going to find out this week; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not read out one line of the sheet with the fourteen things on it is that those fourteen are a set of things people are entitled to keep and not a set of answers, and that the moment a keeper starts reading them out loud in a market they stop being gaps and become a list, and that I have got enough lists in this harbor and there is a page of six names in a drawer that nobody in that room has been told about; and that at about six o'clock the reason I put the ninth of those nine durations into this slip in the place it is in, and not in the order I have used all fortnight, is that a figure at the end of a list stops being a list by about the fourth day and becomes a thing somebody is carrying, and that is the only reordering in this slip and I have made it once.*
