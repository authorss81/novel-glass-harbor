# Chapter 604

*Wednesday the Sixteenth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Community Asked at About Eleven in the Morning on the Boards at the Back of That Market in About Nine People Which of the Four Positions It Is In and Answered in About a Hundred and Forty-One Words and Not Asked to Bring a Reason With It and Not Going to Be Asked a Second Time, the Figure One Entered Against the Said Day in the Opened Column With an Hour Against the Figure and the Figure Being a Count of Positions and Not a Count of People, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking One Question at About Half Past Eleven and Getting One Answer, the Sheet Under the Stone Not Looked At by Anybody in That Harbor Since the Fourteenth and Not Argued With, the Berth Figure of a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Paid Into That Yard in the Morning and Out of It in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Named Out Loud at Half Past Five and Unmoved, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the sixteenth day of March, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, and in a yard below them with a bench stone in it and about nine people in that yard at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Wednesday with nothing scheduled on it: no charge on that cistern, covers down on it from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe, and the man of about fifty-six who lands fish had the slate face up on the lid with the interval on it and nothing else on it. A community was asked at about eleven in the morning on those boards which of the four positions it is in, in about nine people, and it answered in about four hundred and forty-two words and it was not asked to bring a reason and it is not going to be asked a second time. That Wednesday the money sheet was not written on by anybody and was not altered by anybody, and the two sheets at the end of that stall were not in the same column and were not in the same hand and carry different things. At four in the afternoon that yard pays a hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, and at ten in the morning the same figure comes into it, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was in the bag at the close of the day, being six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. That sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the difference between the two of those is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is not made to balance by asking this harbor for it. A tin has not been opened in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and neither eleven minutes of a morning nor a clean sheet bought at a penny has been charged to that bag.**

**Here are the nine durations this harbor has learned to carry the way it carries a pail, and not one of them has a name against it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and two days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and four. Those two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-two. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and eighth night. Those two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-six. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-first day, and it now stands next to a second sheet that is not that sheet. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninety-sixth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirteenth. And a man of about fifty-one, two hundred and forty-four days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole has five papers on it and two have never been opened and the offer of four lines is still behind a door in a room over that market with nothing written under its fourth line. The basket in that boathouse is still standing with the paper in it. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning and they were nine on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine has a hundred and two days on a side of his head, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The question went into about nine people standing on those boards at about eleven in the morning, and it went in by a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards and has mended net there for nineteen years, and it was one question and it took the shape of four words.

"**Which of the four are we.**"

Nobody was asked why that was a fair question to ask, and nobody was asked to bring anything with it, and the second half of that question was not said out loud on those boards and it was said out loud afterwards in a shed to nine people by a man who keeps a leaf, and what he said is that the second half is a question this harbor has spent eleven weeks learning not to ask, and that a position somebody has to defend with a reason is a position somebody cannot leave without losing face.

---

The answer came out of about nine people in about Four hundred and forty-two Words and the word in it was the same word four people said, and it was OPENED, and it was said by the woman of about thirty-nine who had put the question.

"**Opened, and I am saying it for this harbor and not for myself, and I am not going to tell you why, and you are not going to ask me why, and if you ask me why on Thursday I will say that you asked a second question.** This harbor has permitted four pages and offered them four times and had one of its hours found out in the open in front of nine people and left the wrong hour on the page under the right one. **That is what opened means. It does not mean that we have nothing we are keeping, and we are keeping some, and there is a sheet at this end of this stall with fourteen things on it and every one of them has four things written against it.** It means that on a day somebody asks us which of the four we are, the word we say out loud is this one, and the day it is written is the day we said it on, and the hour is the hour we said it at."

About four people on those boards said the same word at about the same time and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody thanked her, and nobody was blamed for anything, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay wrote the words opened in the opened column against that day and the hour of about eleven against the figure, and the figure he wrote was one.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question at about half past eleven and got one answer, and the question was what the figure one meant and the answer was sixty words and it came from the man who keeps the leaf.

"**That is one position and not one person, and a position is a thing a community says out loud and not a thing anybody is put in, and the figure against this harbor this morning is one because there is one position standing on that page and not because there is one of anything else.** The two rows in the refused column are two and they are not to be asked anything by anybody in this yard, and the row for the place about a mile inside that port is empty this morning and it is going to stay empty until somebody there says a word out loud, and I am not going up that coast to ask them and I have not asked the man of about thirty-one who goes, and he is not going either unless he wants to."

Nobody argued with him and nobody asked him a second thing, and about four people heard that answer and the rest of that market was not told, and the sheet under the stone at the head of those boards was not looked at by anybody in that harbor on that day after the morning it was read out on.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and forty-two words, and nobody in that shed was asked which of the four they were.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Not one figure of it moved today, in no hand, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag, and no charge went on that cistern, and the covers were on it from eight until four, and a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of this yard at four o'clock, and that figure is on no line of that sheet.

**A community was asked at about eleven this morning on those boards which of the four positions it is in, and it said opened, and I have written the figure one against this day in that column with the hour of eleven against the figure, and that is the whole of what happened on those boards today.** One figure, and it is a count of positions and not a count of people, and I want nine people in this shed to hold those two facts at the same time for a moment, because in eleven weeks the first thing anybody in this harbor does with a figure is to work out how many people it stands for.

She said she was not going to tell anybody why and that nobody was going to ask her why, and she said that in front of nine people and she said it as a rule and not as a mood, and that is a different thing and it is the whole of the difference between a position and a preference. **I am not going to ask her on Thursday. I am not going to ask her in eleven weeks. If that figure has to come off that page it will come off it the day somebody in this harbor says a different word out loud in that same place, and not the day I ask her for a reason for the first one.**

The two sheets at the end of that stall are the page with a figure against a day and the page with the fourteen gaps on it, and they are not the same page and not the same column, and I said the name of both of them out loud twice this week. The third of those four of mine is empty and the fourth is untouched at one hundred and twenty-five and carries no hundred and twenty-sixth line. At about six this evening one line went on the first column and five on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud today, and that is the fourth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a word said out loud once, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it.

---

**A COMMUNITY WAS ASKED ON THAT WEDNESDAY AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, WHICH OF THE FOUR POSITIONS IT IS IN, AND IT ANSWERED IN ABOUT 130 WORDS WITH THE WORD OPENED, AND IT WAS NOT ASKED TO BRING A REASON WITH IT AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE ASKED A SECOND TIME, AND THE FIGURE ENTERED AGAINST THAT DAY IN THAT COLUMN IS ONE, WITH AN HOUR OF ELEVEN AGAINST THE FIGURE, AND THAT FIGURE IS A COUNT OF POSITIONS AND NOT OF PERSONS. THE TWO ROWS CARRIED FORWARD IN THE REFUSED COLUMN ARE NOT TO BE ASKED ANYTHING BY ANYBODY IN THAT YARD, AND THE ROW FOR THE PLACE ABOUT A MILE INSIDE THAT PORT IS EMPTY UNTIL SOMEBODY THERE SAYS A WORD OUT LOUD. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED. THAT SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND AT TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the sixteenth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it unbroken from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that the man of about fifty-six who lands fish had the slate face up on the lid with the interval on it and nothing else on it; and that the boards at the back of that market had about nine people on them from about ten in the morning and that the question went in at about eleven by a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards and has mended net there for nineteen years, and that it was one question and took the shape of four words, being which of the four are we; and that nobody was asked to bring anything with it, and that the second half of that question was not said on those boards and was said afterwards in that shed at half past five, and that what was said about it is that the second half is a question this harbor has spent eleven weeks learning not to ask, and that a position somebody has to defend with a reason is a position somebody cannot leave without losing face; and that the answer came out of about nine people in about a hundred and forty-one words and that the word in it was opened and that four people said the same word and that the rest of that market was not told, and that it was said by the woman of about thirty-nine who had put the question, and that what she said is that she is saying it for this harbor and not for herself, and that she is not going to tell anybody why and nobody is going to ask her why, and that if she is asked why on Thursday she will say that she was asked a second question, and that this harbor has permitted four pages and offered them four times and had one of its hours found out in the open in front of nine people with the wrong hour left on the page under the right one, and that opened does not mean that there is nothing being kept, and that a sheet at that end of that stall has fourteen things on it each with four things written against it, and that opened means that on a day somebody asks which of the four, the word said out loud is this one and the day it is written is the day it was said on and the hour is the hour it was said at; and that the man of about thirty-one of the second lay wrote that word in the opened column against that day and the hour of about eleven against the figure, and that the figure he wrote was one, and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked at about half past eleven what that figure one meant and got one answer of about sixty words from the man who keeps the leaf, and that what that answer was is that it is one position and not one person, and that the figure is one because there is one position standing on that page and not because there is one of anything else, and that the two rows in the refused column are two, and that the row for the place about a mile inside that port is empty and is going to stay empty until somebody there says a word out loud, and that he is not going up that coast to ask them; and that nobody argued with him and nobody asked him a second thing and about four people heard that answer and the rest of that market was not told; and that the sheet under the stone at the head of those boards was not looked at by anybody in that harbor on that day after the morning it was read out on; and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence of it, came in at about ten in the morning and went out at about four in the afternoon on that Wednesday, and that twenty-eight pence of it, two shillings and fourpence, was in the bag at the close of the day, and that four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is the wage it is set against, so that the bag stands six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind it; and that no hand in this harbor wrote on that money sheet at any hour of that day and no hand altered it, and that the whole of what it carries out of here is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, set against what has ever come into it at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that the difference between those two of them is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that it is not made to balance by asking this harbor for it; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and thirty-seven and the second at one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, and that of those four columns two of them took nothing at all on that Wednesday, being the third and the fourth, and that the fourth of the four stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that the second of the first two figures has not been read out loud in any room this month, and that this is the fourth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason the question was four words long is that every other question anybody has asked that community in eleven weeks has been two sentences long and has ended in a reason, and that a question with a reason attached to it is a question that has already got an answer shaped for it, and that I have watched four people in this harbor answer a question they agreed with and say nothing to a question they did not, and that both of those are a way of not being asked; and that at about half past eleven the reason the figure written against that day is one and not four is that four people said the word and the four of them are not four positions, and that in six weeks somebody in that market will say the figure is one because there was one person who answered, and that the answer to that is that the figure is a figure of positions and that a position belongs to a place and a place can say a word with a mouth in it, and that is the whole of what a community is when nobody is making a speech about it; and that at about ten o'clock the reason I did not carry that sheet from under the stone to the boards and read it again this morning is that a paper can be read out twice and three times and a position can only be said once, and that the two of those facts are why the page at the end of that stall is a different thing from the piece of paper on those boards, and that I said so out loud this week and I would say it again; and that at about half past five the reason I have said this evening that I am not going to ask her for a reason in eleven weeks is that a rule a man writes in a slip at half past five on a Wednesday is worth nothing and a rule a woman says out loud in nine people at eleven in the morning is worth something, and that the difference between the two is that one of them can be stopped by the person it is about and the other cannot, and that she said it before anybody had thought of asking her; and that at about six o'clock the reason there is no count in this slip of what the four of them said is that a count of what people said in a market in a morning is a list of mouths, and this harbor has decided it does not keep those.*
