# Chapter 642

*Saturday the Twenty-Third Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered from Nine Until Four and Untouched, Six Men at Five Hours Each Being Six Times Five Is Thirty Hours at Every Part of It on a Page of Hours in That Shed With the Multiplication Printed at Every Part of It and the Six Anchors Brought In and Their Lines Inspected Inside Those Thirty Hours and Not One Hour Outside Them, No Penny of It on the Fitting-Out Sheet and No Line on It and No Minute Carrying It, and No Movement Closing on It, the Two Lights at About a Mile Off That Point at Two Hundred and Sixty-Four Days and the Man of About Fifty-One at Two Hundred and Eighty-Two Days, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Five and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Saturday the twenty-third day of April, with the boards at the back of that market busy from about nine in the morning and the wind coming round into the north-east about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Saturday carrying nothing at all: that butt covered from nine until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. A page of hours was looked at in that shed this morning for the first time in six weeks, and it carries six men at five hours each, and the multiplication is printed at the head of it, and six times five is thirty hours, and thirty hours is the figure on it at every part of it. The six anchors came down off those shelves at about ten and their lines were inspected by those six men and every one of the six finished inside those thirty hours and not one hour outside them, and the page says what an inspection costs in hours and it says it in hours. It is priced in hours and not in money, and no penny of it went onto the money sheet and no line went onto that sheet for it and it is not in any minute, and it is not one of the three counts this yard keeps and no count of it was taken today and none will be taken. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a page of hours is not a line on that sheet, and six men at five hours each is thirty hours and thirty hours is not a penny out of anybody's hand. That berth fund paid that yard at the figure in chalk, a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and what the slate was holding at the close of the day was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence; set against four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, the difference is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet of money is carrying its one line and is not going to be given a second one this month. The figure it has cost this harbor is one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, the whole of what has ever been put into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the figure it is still short of is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today.**

**The nine went on a day each and the fifth of them is a pair of lamps on a point about a mile off that mole which somebody has wanted to be told about for a very long time. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-four days. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-two. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-sixth night. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and nineteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-fourth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-first day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-two days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of the five have not been opened by anybody in this harbor. Those four lines are still behind that door in an upper room over that market with nothing under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has looked inside it. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and no man in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten in the morning six anchors came down off the shelves in the corner of that shed, one at a time, on the shoulders of two men apiece, and they were set on a bench along the north wall where the light from the door comes in.

They are working anchors and they are not glass and they are not the same thing as a record of anything. Each one has a line of a particular length on it and the line is what has to be looked at, and what has to be looked at on a line is the splice at the top and the seizing where it passes the eye and the wear where it has been on a bottom.

A woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm was one of the six, and she said one thing out loud about it at about eleven in the morning, in about 145 words, and nobody in that yard argued with her about it.

"**Thirty hours is thirty hours and I want it written on that page with the six of us and the five hours each against it and not with my name on it, because my name on that page turns six people into one man with a crew, and I have crew enough.**

**And the part I want said is this. I have looked at eleven of these lines since the spring and eight of them were cut and seized by somebody who was in a hurry, and a splice made in a hurry is a splice that will let go in a sea, and a man who is not in a hurry now will be gone in two winters. That is what the five hours a man is for. It is not for thinking. It is for standing still with a line in both hands.**"

Nobody thanked her for saying it and nobody put her name on that page and nobody asked her a second thing about it.

---

The six of them worked until about two in the afternoon and came out of those thirty hours with every one of the six lines either sound or marked, and two of the six were marked, and the marks are on the page and not on any other surface in that yard.

At about half past three in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two of this harbor stood at the flat of that bench stone and read that page from end to end, twice, and he was in it nowhere. His name is not on it and his name is not going on it, and he said nothing about what the thirty hours meant and nobody in that yard asked him.

"**Six of them and five hours each and it comes to thirty, and it is written at every part of it, and two of the six lines are marked and the marks are on the page.**

**I am not going to stand here and tell nine people what that is for, because I do not know, and a man who puts a meaning on a page of hours in nine people is doing the thing this whole yard spent the winter getting away from, which is deciding what other people's arithmetic means.**

**What I will put in front of nine people is the plain part. That page is a figure. A figure can be checked by anybody with a leaf and a pencil. And it is not on the money sheet and it will not be, and it is not in any minute, and it is not one of the three things this yard counts, and no count of it is being taken today.**"

Nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him for reading a page twice.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 620 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what thirty hours is worth.

"**Thirty hours, and it is on a page in new noun phrases and the multiplication is printed at the head of it, and the six anchors are on the bench along that wall and two of the six lines are marked and the marks are on the page.**

**Here is what I want nine people to hear about it, and it is short. That page is not money, it is not a line on the money sheet, and it is not in any minute, and it is not one of the three counts this yard keeps. It is a fourth thing and it carries no figure against any day in this month and it is not being closed on this month and it will not be closed on by me saying so. Nobody has asked me to close on it and I have not asked anybody.**

Six men at five hours each is thirty hours and the six of them were not this party and were not paid out of that bag, and two of those six lines are marked for cutting and a man who is in a hurry now will be gone in two winters.

A bearing was put on the back of an old slate in that shed on Friday and this morning the nine on the casting of that bell went up a day to two hundred and eighty-two, and neither of those two figures is on the sheet of days, and both of them are written down.

The money sheet did not move and it carries one line and there will not be a second one this month. That sheet carries one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and a gap of one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. **Not one standing thing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence stayed where it is on that board.**

That sheet of the fourteen was not read out of today, not one figure on it moved by a single one, and not one of those fourteen was added to and not one was taken off. That count of positions was not taken a second time and is not going to be taken this month. That fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and carries no figure against any day. The three columns of that page of days were not counted today, and that count is not in this slip and is not going to be in one.

Under that slate lie twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

That first column took one entry at about six this evening and the second took five, and I did not read them back. Three hundred and seventy-five stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and forty-nine against the second, the third of those four is nothing at all, and the fourth has not moved off one hundred and twenty-five since before the frost."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for thirty hours. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the six anchors were back on their shelves along that wall and the page of hours was on the bench with the two marks on it.

**SIX MEN AT FIVE HOURS EACH, BEING SIX TIMES FIVE IS THIRTY HOURS, ON A PAGE OF HOURS IN THAT SHED WITH THE MULTIPLICATION PRINTED AT THE HEAD OF IT AT EVERY PART OF IT, AND THE SIX ANCHORS BROUGHT DOWN OFF THOSE SHELVES AND THEIR LINES INSPECTED INSIDE THOSE THIRTY HOURS AND NOT ONE HOUR OUTSIDE THEM, TWO OF THE SIX LINES MARKED FOR CUTTING, AND THAT PAGE IS PRICED IN HOURS AND NOT IN MONEY AND NO PENNY OF IT WENT ONTO THE FITTING-OUT SHEET AND NO LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET FOR IT AND IT IS NOT IN ANY MINUTE, AND IT IS A FOURTH THING AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE COUNTS AND NO COUNT OF IT WAS TAKEN AND NO MOVEMENT IS CLOSING ON IT. A BEARING OFF THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE READ ON FRIDAY IS ON THE BACK OF AN OLD SLATE IN THAT SHED AND IS NOT ON THE SHEET OF DAYS. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT SHEET OF MONEY CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. THE FIGURE IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS EVER BEEN PUT INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND UNDER IT, AS ON EVERY DAY OF THIS RUN, LIES TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not put that woman's name against the thirty hours is that her name on that page turns six people into one man with a crew, and that this yard has spent thirteen weeks learning that the difference between a fact and an account of a fact is whether a stranger can check it, and that a name is not a check; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I wrote nothing in this column about what a page of hours means is that I do not know and that a keeper of a page who puts a meaning on six people being six people is a keeper deciding what other people's arithmetic says, and that the man of about thirty-two stood in that page twice today and said nothing about what it was for and that was the correct thing for him to do and I am not going to write down that he did it well; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason those two marks are on that page and nowhere else is that a mark that appears on a wall gets argued with and a mark that appears in a book gets found, and that this harbor has wanted to be found out on things before and got its wish; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted today, and will not be counted on any day this month but one, is that a count taken twice in a month is a count somebody is waiting for, and that this yard is not a place where anybody waits; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on the money that did not move and not on the thirty hours is that a page of hours is the fourth thing this yard keeps and the money sheet is the first, and that the first one is older and the fourth one is newer, and that a slip that ended on the newer thing would be a slip that had learned to be pleased with itself.*
