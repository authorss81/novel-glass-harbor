# Chapter 637

*Monday the Eighteenth Day of April, a Rota Day, That Cistern Behind That Market Filled by Hand for the Hundred and Forty-Third Time, Two Men and a Bucket Up a Ladder Eleven Times at a Little After Eight in the Morning and Nothing Walked on, Its Covers Back on It by a Quarter Past Nine and Its Water to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Claim in One Line Written on a Separate Sheet of a Working Place at About Three in the Afternoon in About Nine People and the Four Things That Go Under It Said Out Loud in the Mouth of the Man It Is About, Being That It Is a Claim and Not a Witness, That It Is Not a Jurisdiction and Binds Nobody, That It Is Not Exercised and the Record Is Not Opened and Nothing Inside It Is Named or Counted, and That It Stands Until He Ends It and He Has Named Nobody, and a Day and an Hour and a Hand Against It and No Name on It, a Sheet That Is Not the Sheet of the Fourteen and Not the Sheet of Positions and Not the Fitting-Out Sheet and Not the Four Heads on That Wall Above the Bench Stone, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Monday the eighteenth day of April, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before that market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about three in the afternoon and about nine in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Monday rota day, and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe. That butt was filled by hand for the hundred and forty-third time at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips and nothing walked on, the covers went back on it before a quarter past nine, and it stood at the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. At about three this afternoon, in about nine people at the flat of that bench stone, a line went onto a separate sheet and under it went four things, and every one of those four things was said out loud in the mouth of the man that line is about, in his own words and not read off anything. **That sheet is not the sheet of the fourteen at the end stall and is not the sheet of positions and is not the fitting-out sheet and is not the four heads on that wall above this stone, and all four of those were named separately and out loud this afternoon in front of nine people, and a sheet that carries a claim is not a page of positions and a page of positions is not a count of anybody.** Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a line written on another sheet about a thing nobody has asked anything of is not a line on the money sheet. That yard drew in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and let the same sum go out of it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the day's end, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. The figure that sheet carries is unchanged, and it is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, with ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, as the whole of what has ever gone into it, and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, as what it does not carry. Not one tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing this afternoon came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the eighth of them is the only one of the nine nailed to a wall in a street with a seal on it that no one in this harbor has ever spoken about. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-sixth day. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-five days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-five days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-seven. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-first night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-nine days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and fourteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-ninth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-seven days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. On that board are five papers, and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. Behind a door in an upper room over that market there are still four lines, and under the fourth of those four there is nothing written, and the answer to all of it remains no on four out of six with the fifth not put to them. A basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off that mole head nine hulls are lying this afternoon and nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-five days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about three in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two of this harbor put one line on a sheet of his own and said the four things under it out loud in about nine people, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the line down with the day and the hour against it and wrote his own hand against it, and there is no name on that sheet.

The line says that a man of about thirty-two of this harbor holds a claim, in his own family, over one sealed record held elsewhere, and that it is a claim and not a hearing and not a decision and not a permission.

---

He said the four things and nobody read anything to him and nobody read anything out of that sheet, and about four of the nine could read the sheet themselves and read it as it was being written.

"**It is a claim and not a witness. It has not been asked anything this afternoon and it is not going to be asked anything by anybody in this yard, ever, and if a thing cannot be asked anything then it cannot answer anything, and that is what it is.**

**It is not a jurisdiction. It binds nobody, it decides nothing, and it is not a delegation, and there is no office behind it and no one in this harbor is standing here under it.**

**It is not exercised and the record is not opened. Nothing inside that record is named on this sheet or on any other, and no figure of what is inside it is on this sheet or on any other, and not one page of this yard carries a figure of it, and nobody is going to be able to read what it is off this table this afternoon.**

**And it stands until I end it or until a person I name ends it, and I have not named anybody, and that is the fourth thing and it is the one I would ask somebody to check me on in twenty years.**"

Nobody arbitrated. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody asked him a single question about any of the four, and about four people in that nine heard all of it and about five were not told until the following morning that it had happened, and nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody congratulated him on a sheet.

The keeper did not ask him to sign it and he was not asked to sign anything, and that sheet went behind the four heads on that wall and is not on the sheet of the fourteen and is not on the sheet of positions and is not on the money sheet and is not on the board at the head of that mole.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing, and about nine people were at the flat of that bench stone, and about four of them could read what was on the wall above it.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 712 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what is inside a record that nobody has opened.

"**A hundred and forty-three charges have gone past that cistern, eleven trips by two men this morning, the covers back on it before a quarter past nine, and that butt up to the rim of its second hoop until about four this afternoon, and the name at the head of that board is still off it and I am not going to say anything else about that butt this month.**

**A line went onto a separate sheet at about three this afternoon and four things went under it and every one of those four things was said by the man it is about, in his own mouth, in nine people, and about four of those nine could read the sheet as it was being written and nobody read it out to anybody.** That sheet is not the sheet of the fourteen and it is not the sheet of positions and it is not the fitting-out sheet and it is not the four heads on that wall, and all four of those were named separately and out loud this afternoon in front of nine people, and I have written that down here because this yard has put two different things on two different surfaces in thirteen weeks and one of them was called the same thing as the other.

**A claim is not a witness and cannot testify and cannot answer a question and is not a jurisdiction and binds nobody and decides nothing and is not a delegation, and it has not been asked one question this afternoon and it is not going to be asked one by anybody in this yard.** It is not exercised and the record is not opened and nothing inside it is named on any page in this harbor and no figure of it is on any page in this harbor, and no page in this harbor is going to carry one, and that is not a promise of mine about a locked drawer, it is a fact about what is written on paper.

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the figure at the head of that money sheet and it is where the twenty-sixth of last month left it and it has not moved a halfpenny in thirteen weeks. Twenty-eight pence is lying under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**And the ninth of those nine days is tomorrow and the line goes onto that money sheet tomorrow afternoon and it is going to carry the days the work was done on it and the day this yard settles it, and every part of that line is going to carry its own multiplication and its own days, and I am not going to write a figure of it this evening and I am not going to say a figure of it in nine people this evening.**

Nobody at the end of that stall was asked one thing this afternoon. The fourteen on that sheet at the end stall were not read out of and no figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. That count of positions has not been taken again this month, and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and no figure is set against it for any day.

At about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and seventy stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and twenty-four against the second, my third is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, a boy's hand being at the head of it and his surname being on no page and he not being on the water, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four things said out loud. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that separate sheet was behind the four heads on that wall and had a day and an hour and a hand on it and no name.

**THAT CISTERN BEHIND THAT MARKET WAS FILLED BY HAND FOR THE HUNDRED AND FORTY-THIRD TIME, TWO MEN AND A BUCKET GOING UP THAT LADDER ELEVEN TIMES AT A LITTLE AFTER EIGHT IN THE MORNING, AND NOT ONE BUCKET OF IT WAS WALKED ON, THE COVERS BACK ON IT BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE AND THE WATER AT THE RIM OF ITS SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A CLAIM IN ONE LINE WAS WRITTEN ON A SEPARATE SHEET AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR AND THE KEEPER'S HAND AGAINST IT AND NO NAME ON IT, AND THE FOUR THINGS THAT WENT UNDER IT WERE SAID OUT LOUD IN THE MOUTH OF THE MAN THAT LINE IS ABOUT, BEING THAT IT IS A CLAIM AND NOT A WITNESS, THAT IT IS NOT A JURISDICTION AND BINDS NOBODY AND DECIDES NOTHING AND IS NOT A DELEGATION, THAT IT IS NOT EXERCISED AND THE RECORD IS NOT OPENED AND NOTHING INSIDE IT IS NAMED OR COUNTED ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR, AND THAT IT STANDS UNTIL HE ENDS IT AND HE HAS NAMED NOBODY. THAT SHEET IS NOT THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN AND NOT THE SHEET OF POSITIONS AND NOT THE FITTING-OUT SHEET AND NOT THE FOUR HEADS. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. NOTHING HAS COME OFF THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET AND NOTHING HAS GONE ON IT. WHAT IT CARRIES IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SPENT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE PUT IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND LONG UNDER THAT SLATE LIES TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the eighteenth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day, was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, that butt behind that market having been filled by hand for the hundred and forty-third time, a little after eight in the morning, by two men and a bucket going up that ladder eleven times before that market opened, and that not one of those eleven buckets was walked on and nothing was carried up it on any other errand, and that the covers were back on that butt by a quarter past nine, and that it stood to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that at about three in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two of this harbor put one line on a sheet of his own and said the four things under it out loud in about nine people, and that the man who keeps the leaf wrote the line down with the day and the hour against it and wrote his own hand against it, and that there is no name on that sheet; and that the line says that a man of about thirty-two of this harbor holds a claim, in his own family, over one sealed record held elsewhere, and that it is a claim and not a hearing and not a decision and not a permission; and that the four things he said were that it is a claim and not a witness and has not been asked anything and is not going to be asked anything by anybody in this yard and cannot therefore answer anything, that it is not a jurisdiction and binds nobody and decides nothing and is not a delegation and there is no office behind it, that it is not exercised and the record is not opened and nothing inside that record is named on any page in this harbor and no figure of what is inside it is on any page in this harbor, and that it stands until he ends it or until a person he names ends it and that he has named nobody; and that nobody arbitrated and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody asked him a single question about any of the four, and that about four people in that nine heard all of it and about five were not told until the following morning that it had happened, and that nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody congratulated him on a sheet; and that the keeper did not ask him to sign it and he was not asked to sign anything, and that that sheet went behind the four heads on that wall and is not on the sheet of the fourteen and is not on the sheet of positions and is not on the money sheet and is not on the board at the head of that mole; and that at about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked not one thing; and that the fourteen on that sheet at the end stall have not been read out of in thirteen weeks, and that no figure on that sheet has moved by one in either direction, and that none of them was added to and none was taken off; and that that count of positions has not been taken again this month, and that the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and no figure is set against it for any day; and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted this afternoon and that count is not said out loud in this slip; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 712 words in about nine people; and that the Morrow Line has not gone out of the held column of that sheet of days and has not been struck and is still one of the nine names on that sheet; and that at about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March is the thirty-seventh, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and seventy and the second is one thousand seven hundred and twenty-four, and that the third of those four took no line of any kind, a boy's hand being at the head of that column and his surname being on no page and himself being off the water, and that the fourth of them remains at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that a separate sheet with a day and an hour and a hand on it and no name was behind the four heads on that wall at six that evening.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about three in the afternoon the reason I wrote that line down with my own hand against it and with no name on it is that a claim with a name on it is a thing a person can be frightened into withdrawing and a claim with a hand on it is a thing a person can be frightened into rewriting, and that neither of those is what this yard is for; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason I named four other surfaces out loud in nine people this afternoon is that this harbor has spent thirteen weeks finding that two different things carried the same name on the same wall, and that the whole of the trouble of the last three months came out of a sheet being called a sheet, and that naming the four of them separately took nine seconds and cost nothing; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have not written one word in this column about what is in that record is that I do not know, that nobody in this yard knows, that I have not climbed that hill this year and I am not going to, and that the number of things in this harbor that are not known to be in that record is one hundred and thirty-eight days of a man's hearing on a different page altogether; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I have not said the word witness out loud in a yard this month except to say that a claim is not one is that the last time this yard gave a thing a name it gave a name to a man who was keeping a page, and that has not gone down well anywhere; and that at about ten in the morning the reason the ninth of those nine days is tomorrow and not today is that a day is settled on an afternoon and not in a morning, and that a line written this morning would carry a day the work was not done on, and that a line which carries a day the work was not done on has to say so on its own face in this yard's own mouth before a figure goes on it, and that I have one such a day in this month and I am not going to have two; and that at about six this evening the reason I have put nothing on that money sheet this afternoon is that there is nothing to put on it until tomorrow and that a sheet which moves twice in one week is a sheet nobody trusts by the second time, and that this sheet has not moved since the twenty-sixth of last month and it is going to move once in this month and once is enough.*
