# Chapter 645

*Tuesday the Twenty-Sixth Day of April, the Seventy-Ninth Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Seventy-Ninth Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-Seventh and the Seventy-Eighth and the Seventy-Ninth Weeks All Standing Fallen Due and the Seventy-Ninth the Last of the Seven, a Boy of Twenty-Two on the Stool at the End of That Stall at About One in the Afternoon With a Board Across His Knees and Off the Water and Nobody in That Market Having Said One Word to Him About It and No Figure Kept by Anybody, a Woman of About Forty-Four With a Chandler's Counter in That Front Street Down at the Flat of That Bench Stone at About Three in the Afternoon Saying One Thing Out Loud in About Nine People, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of April, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning and a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The seventy-ninth wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind that market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. On the lid of that step the column stood at twenty-six lines and the last of the twenty-six came to one hundred and four marks, and a man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten this morning and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four. The seventy-seventh, the seventy-eighth and the seventy-ninth weeks are all three standing fallen due behind that door at the close of today, and the seventy-ninth is the last of the seven that came due this spring, and there is no eighth one on the board and nobody in that market has asked what happens in the summer. At about one this afternoon a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall with a board across his knees and a book open on it, and he is not on the water, and nobody in that market has said one word to him about it and no figure of it is kept by anybody in this harbor. At about three this afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter in that front street came down the boards to the flat of that bench stone and said one thing out loud, in about 176 words, and nobody in that yard asked her where a paper was coming from and nobody thanked her. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a column that does not move is not a line on that sheet, and a woman with a shop is not a line on that sheet. A hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight was paid out of it at about four, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the close of the day, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. The money sheet carries one line and is not going to carry a second this month. It stands at one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today.**

**The nine went on a day each and the third of them is two objects on a crown that nobody will claim and that have not been asked after in twenty months. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty-three days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty-three days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-five. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-ninth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-seven days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twenty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-seventh. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-fourth day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-five days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. There are five papers on that board at the head of that mole, and two of those five are still shut. Those four lines are still shut behind a door in an upper room over that market, nothing under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. A basket stands in that boathouse with a paper in it, and nobody has ever put a second thing in that basket. That mole head has nine hulls lying about two sea miles off it, it had nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no one here has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-three days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten in the morning the man of about thirty-one of that second lay got his finger to the end of the twenty-sixth line on the lid of that step and stopped on it and gave the figure and gave the fact that it had not shifted, and about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of it from where they were standing.

The seventy-ninth week joins the seventy-seventh and the seventy-eighth behind that door at the close of today, and there is nothing on that board for an eightieth Tuesday, and nobody in that market has said a word about that.

---

At about one in the afternoon the boy of twenty-two was on that stool at the end of that stall with his board across his knees and his book open, writing weights in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and he is not on the water.

Nobody in that market said one word to him about the water and nobody asked him where he had been and nobody kept a figure of it, and there is no column in this harbor for how many times a boy of twenty-two has not gone out, and the keeper of the leaf has stopped writing it down in the second column on the grounds that a habit written down every week for thirteen weeks is not a record, it is a complaint.

---

At about three in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter in that front street came down the boards from the west end and stopped at the flat of that bench stone with her arms in her sleeves and said one thing out loud in about nine people, and nobody had asked her for anything and nobody asked her anything after.

"**I have kept a counter for twenty-two years and I will tell you exactly what a counter is for, because I think this yard has a different idea of it. A counter is not where paper gets read out. A counter is where a person who cannot read signs for the thing they have taken, and the paper comes to them and not the reading. If a paper is going to be read out in this market then somebody is going to stand up in front of twenty people and open their mouth, and the mouth has to belong to a person who works here, and not to a diver and not to a clerk and not to whoever came down that road last.**

**I am not saying I will do it. I am saying that whoever does it is going to be asked to say out loud where they stop, and that stopping is a thing a person decides before they start and not in the middle of it. Mine would be at the seal.**"

Nobody in that yard argued with her and about four people heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody thanked her for it, and nobody in that yard has asked her since whether she will do it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 677 words, and nobody in that shed was asked where a paper was coming from.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks off that lid at ten this morning in four minutes for the seventy-ninth time, and it did not move, and twenty envelopes stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and the seventy-ninth week joins the other two behind that door at the close of today. There is no eightieth Tuesday on that board and I am not going to invent one and I am not going to say a word about what comes after the frost.**

**A woman of forty-four with a chandler's counter in the front street came down here at three this afternoon and said a thing out loud in nine people about what a counter is for, and she is right, and I have written down that she is right and I have not written down that she will do it, because she did not say she would and this yard is not going to put a word in her mouth on a Wednesday.**

**What she said is that a paper gets read out by a working person and not by a diver and not by a clerk, and that whoever reads it decides before they begin where they stop, and that hers would be at the seal. I am not going to say what that means tonight because I do not know yet and a slip is a bad place to guess in.**

A boy of twenty-two was on that stool at one this afternoon and is not on the water and nobody said a word to him about it, and I have stopped writing it in this column, and that is a change and I want it in a column somewhere, so it is in this one.

The money sheet stood still today and the one line on it is the only line on it. One hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence is what that sheet has carried out of this harbor, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence is the whole of what has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence is what it does not carry, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. **Nothing that stands in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today, and that board is still carrying the figure of four hundred and fifty pence.**

The sheet at the end of that stall with the fourteen things on it was not read out of, not one figure on it went up or down by one, and not one of those fourteen was added to and not one was taken off. No count of positions was taken again, and that count belongs to the page of days and not to this yard. The fifth word is not a column, and no figure is set against it for any day of this month. The three columns of that sheet of days stand on that wall uncounted today, and that count is not in this slip.

Twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, is what lies under that slate, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight came out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-three days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

This evening at about six that first column took one and the second took five, and the reading is not done and will not be done before it is due, and the figures are three hundred and seventy-eight and one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four, with a third column that has never had anybody in it and a fourth standing at one hundred and twenty-five."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for twenty-two years of a counter. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the boy of twenty-two was still on that stool at six with his book shut and his board across his knees.

**TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS STOOD ON THE LID OF THAT STEP AT ABOUT TEN THIS MORNING AND DID NOT MOVE FOR THE SEVENTY-NINTH TIME, AND TWENTY ENVELOPES STOOD SHUT BEHIND THAT DOOR FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-SEVENTH, THE SEVENTY-EIGHTH AND THE SEVENTY-NINTH WEEKS ARE ALL THREE STANDING FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, AND THE SEVENTY-NINTH IS THE LAST OF THE SEVEN THAT CAME DUE THIS SPRING. A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WITH A CHANDLER'S COUNTER IN THAT FRONT STREET CAME DOWN TO THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON AND SAID ONE THING OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 176 WORDS IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, BEING THAT A COUNTER IS NOT WHERE PAPER GETS READ OUT BUT WHERE A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ SIGNS FOR THE THING THEY HAVE TAKEN, THAT IF A PAPER IS GOING TO BE READ OUT IN THAT MARKET SOMEBODY WHO WORKS HERE IS GOING TO HAVE TO OPEN THEIR MOUTH IN FRONT OF TWENTY PEOPLE, AND NOT A DIVER AND NOT A CLERK, AND THAT WHOEVER READS IT DECIDES BEFORE THEY BEGIN WHERE THEY STOP, AND THAT HERS WOULD BE AT THE SEAL, AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD ASKED HER SINCE WHETHER SHE WILL DO IT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. NOTHING WENT ONTO THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET TODAY AND NOTHING CAME OFF IT AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE. WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, WHAT HAS EVER BEEN PUT INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND LONG UNDER THAT SLATE LIES TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the seventy-ninth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was put on that cistern upon it, the covers lying on it from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the lid of that step was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay, and that it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of it from where they were standing; and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four, and that the seventy-seventh and the seventy-eighth weeks have been standing fallen due behind that door and that the seventy-ninth week joins them at the close of that day, and that the seventy-ninth is the last of the seven that came due this spring, and that there is no eightieth Tuesday on that board and that nobody in that market has said a word about what comes after the frost; and that at about one in the afternoon a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall with a board across his knees and a book open on it writing weights in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and that he is not on the water, and that nobody in that market said one word to him about the water and that nobody asked him where he had been and that nobody kept a figure of it, and that there is no column in this harbor for how many times a boy of twenty-two has not gone out, and that the keeper of the leaf has stopped writing it in the second column on the grounds that a habit written down every week for thirteen weeks is a complaint and not a record; and that at about three in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter in that front street came down those boards and stopped at the flat of that bench stone with her arms in her sleeves and said one thing out loud in about 176 words in about nine people, being that she has kept a counter for twenty-two years and that a counter is not where paper gets read out but where a person who cannot read signs for the thing they have taken, and that the paper comes to them and not to the reading, and that if a paper is going to be read out in that market then somebody who works there is going to have to stand up in front of twenty people and open their mouth, and that the mouth has to belong to a person who works here and not to a diver and not to a clerk and not to whoever came down that road last, and that she is not saying she will do it, and that whoever does it is going to be asked to say out loud where they stop, and that stopping is a thing a person decides before they start and not in the middle of it, and that hers would be at the seal; and that nobody in that yard argued with her, that about four people heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, that nobody thanked her for it, and that nobody in that yard has asked her since whether she will do it; and that the count of positions was not taken again, that the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and carries no figure against any day, and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted on that Tuesday and that count is not in that slip; and that the sheet of the fourteen at the end of that stall was not read out of, that not one figure on that sheet moved by one, and that none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 677 words in about nine people; and that the Morrow Line stands in the dormant column of that sheet of days, not struck, still one of the nine, and still at Orison; and that one line went in on that first column at about six that evening and five on the second, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the forty-fifth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and seventy-eight and the second is one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four; the third of those four is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand being at the head of it with his surname on no page and himself not on the water, and the fourth of them is unmoved at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that four hundred and fifty pence did not come down any board on that Tuesday, and that nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about three in the afternoon the reason I did not ask that woman one question after she had finished is that she told nine people a thing about her own trade and this yard does not interrogate people about their own trade, and that if I had asked her whether she would do it I would have turned a working person into a candidate and a candidate is a thing a yard has chosen, and that choosing people is the one thing this harbor has been getting away from since the winter; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have stopped writing a boy's thirteen weeks of not being on the water into this column is that a figure somebody keeps about a person they live with is a roster, and this harbor keeps no roster of anybody, and that it took thirteen weeks for me to notice that I had built one out of good intentions and a second column; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason there is no eightieth Tuesday written on that board is that a board with a gap in it is a board that can be looked at, and that filling the gap with a figure would be inventing a wage Tuesday that nobody in this harbor has had, and that the last of the seven is the last of the seven; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I wrote nothing in this column about that woman coming down here is that what she said is hers and not mine and that a keeper of a page who repeats a working person's words back to nine people has made them into the yard's, and that the yard does not get to have her mouth; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on four columns and not on a seal is that a seal is on a piece of paper that has not come ashore yet, and that a slip is not a place to get in front of a thing.*
