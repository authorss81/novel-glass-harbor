# Chapter 643

*Sunday the Twenty-Fourth Day of April, a Wipe, the Hundred and Third Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Off Its Holders at About Ten Past Ten and Bare at About Half Past Ten and Up Again at About Ten to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole, Twenty Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Seven Standing Bare and Twenty to Get It Back, Being Forty-Seven Minutes the Whole of It, a Minute Longer Each Way Than the Seventeenth and a Minute Longer Bare, and Nobody Having Said Why, the Two Men Who Take a Bearing Off That Frame Standing at an Empty Sill at About Eleven and Setting Nothing Down on It and Going, a Man of About Fifty-Three With a Boat of About Fourteen Tons Out of That Harbor on Saturday Morning at About Seven and Back In at About Six and Not Asked Where He Had Been and Not Saying, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything on the Fifth Sunday Running, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Six and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Sunday the twenty-fourth day of April, with that market trading all morning under a sky that had gone back to grey about seven, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Sunday, and the hundred and third time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down off its holders, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it, and that butt covered from nine until four with nothing put on it. Two men began to take that wall down at about ten past ten and it was bare by about half past ten and it was back on its holders at about ten to eleven, sitting at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame is still not whole. Twenty minutes to get it off the wall, seven minutes standing with it bare, twenty minutes to get it back, which is forty-seven minutes the whole of it. That is a minute longer each way than the seventeenth of this month and a minute longer standing bare than the seventeenth, and a minute longer each way than the tenth, and nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and this yard has stopped asking the two men who do it. A page of hours was looked at yesterday and thirty hours is written on it with the multiplication printed at the head of it, and no line of that page went onto the money sheet and none will. Nothing went onto that sheet today and nothing came off it, and a wall is not a line on that sheet and a man going out of a harbor on a Saturday is not a line on that sheet. A day's work with a boat and two men at a berth went into that yard at about ten in the morning at a hundred and sixty-eight pence and the same sum left it at about four, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was under that slate at the day's end, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet of money did not move on that Sunday and it is still on one line. Its three figures are one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. No tin in that shed has been opened this month and the last time one was opened was the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine went on a day each and the first of them is the oldest thing this harbor carries and it has not moved since the frost. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty-one days. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-three days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty-one days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-three. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-seventh night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-five days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twentieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-fifth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-second day. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. Those four lines are still behind a door in an upper room over that market and nothing has ever been written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. The basket standing in that boathouse has a paper in it and has never held anything else. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this morning and nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-one days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten past ten on that Sunday two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare at about half past ten, for the hundred and third time, and about five people near enough to see it happen went on with what they were doing.

They had it back on the holders at about ten to eleven at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and it did not go up whole. Twenty minutes off the wall, seven standing bare, twenty back, which is forty-seven minutes from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven.

The frame behind it is an empty stone sill and a gap and the sky over that mole, and that is the easiest sightline anybody on this coast has.

---

At about eleven, with that wall bare and about six minutes to go on it, the two men who take a bearing off that frame every Sunday morning came up the steps and stood at the sill and looked down the mole head, and neither of them set a slate down on that stone, and neither of them said a figure out loud.

They were not on the frame at about ten to six and they went down the steps and that is the whole of what they did, and it is the first Sunday in nine years that neither of them has put anything on that sill, and nobody in this harbor asked either of them about it and nobody is going to.

---

At about four in the afternoon a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came into that harbor on the flood with a warp over his own bow and a man in his cockpit who was not one of his crew, and he was not asked where he had been and he did not say.

He had gone out of that harbor on Saturday morning at about seven and had been in the water in two places that nobody here knows the names of, and he came back in at about six on Saturday evening and was alongside that west quay again at about four this afternoon, and about nine people at that market saw him do it and about four said nothing about it and the rest of that market was not told.

He has had eleven words on a page in this harbor since before the winter and nobody has answered them and nobody has thanked him and nobody has told him he was wrong, and that is the same this evening as it was last winter, and this yard is not going to use a boat coming in to make itself feel like a yard that is paying attention.

---

At about four in the afternoon as well, a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net stood at her own stall at the end of that market with her hands in the bib of her apron and worked, and nobody in this harbor came to that stall and asked her a single thing.

That is the fifth Sunday running. She said no on the twenty-fifth in nine people and gave a ground for it once, and nobody has argued with her and nobody has thanked her and nobody has asked her since, and this morning she was not asked whether she agreed with a word in any column, and she is not going to be asked this month.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 692 words, and nobody in that shed was asked to say the hundred and third time out loud twice.

"**Twenty minutes to get it off the wall, seven standing bare, twenty minutes to get it back, and forty-seven minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven. I am going to say the minutes and I am not going to say the number this evening. A yard that counts a thing out loud for an audience has started performing it, and that frame has had enough of this harbor's performances.**

**Seven minutes standing bare against six last Sunday and five the Sunday before, and twenty each way against nineteen and nineteen, and nobody has said why and this yard has stopped asking the two men who do it.**

**And this morning those same two men came up to that sill with the wall off it and neither of them set anything down. Nine years of Sundays and neither of them has ever come up there and left without a slate on the stone, and this morning they came and looked and went, and nobody asked them, and I am not going to ask them, and I am writing it down because in a month somebody will ask me what happened and I will not remember.**

A man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came in on the flood at four this afternoon off two days of water in two places whose names this yard does not have, and nobody asked him where he had been and he did not say, and he has had eleven words on a page in this harbor since before the winter and nobody has answered them and nobody has thanked him and nobody has told him he was wrong.

Thirty hours is on a page in that shed with the multiplication printed at the head of it and no penny of it went on the money sheet and no line will.

Nothing came onto that sheet of money and nothing came off it, and it carries one line. The three figures on that sheet are one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence spent, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence put in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence not yet got, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. **Nothing standing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence did not come down any board.**

Nobody at the end of that stall was asked one thing this morning and that is the fifth Sunday running.

The fourteen on that sheet at the end of that stall were not read out of, not one figure on it went up or down by one, and not one of those fourteen was added to and not one was taken off. The count of positions was not taken again, and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and has no figure against any day. The columns of that sheet of days were not added up today, and that sum is not in this slip.

There are twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, under that slate at the close of that Sunday, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-one days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

A single line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and I did not read either of them out. Take them off the page yourself: three hundred and seventy-six, one thousand seven hundred and fifty-four, nothing, and one hundred and twenty-five with no line under it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a slate that was not put down. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that wall stood at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and the woman of about thirty-nine was still at that market at six.

---

**THAT WALL CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK TWENTY MINUTES OFF THE WALL, SEVEN STANDING BARE AND TWENTY TO GET IT BACK, BEING FORTY-SEVEN MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT, AGAINST NINETEEN AND SIX AND NINETEEN ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. THE TWO MEN WHO TAKE A BEARING OFF THAT FRAME CAME UP TO AN EMPTY SILL AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND PUT NOTHING DOWN ON IT AND SAID NO FIGURE OUT LOUD AND WENT, AND THAT IS THE FIRST SUNDAY IN NINE YEARS THAT NEITHER OF THEM HAS LEFT A SLATE ON THAT STONE, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED EITHER OF THEM. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS CAME IN ON THE FLOOD AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS NOT ASKED WHERE HE HAD BEEN AND DID NOT SAY. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET HAS ONE LINE ON IT AND IS NOT GOING TO CARRY A SECOND THIS MONTH. ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS EVER BEEN PUT INTO IT, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT THAT SLATE IS HOLDING.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe, being the hundred and third time that wall has come down, that it was no rota day and no wage Tuesday, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that two men began to take that wall off its holders at about ten past ten, it was bare by about half past ten, and it was back on the holders at about ten to eleven at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and it did not go up whole, and that it took twenty minutes to get off the wall, seven standing bare and twenty to get it back, which is forty-seven minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven, and that that is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the seventeenth of this month and a minute longer each way than the tenth, and that nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and that this yard has stopped asking the two men who do it; and that at about eleven, with that wall bare and about six minutes to go on it, the two men who take a bearing off that frame every Sunday morning came up the steps and stood at that sill and looked down the mole head, and that neither of them set a slate down on that stone and that neither of them said a figure out loud, and that they were not on the frame at about ten to six and went down the steps, and that this is the first Sunday in nine years that neither of them has put anything on that sill, and that nobody in this harbor asked either of them about it and that nobody is going to; and that at about four in the afternoon a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came into that harbor on the flood with a warp over his own bow and a man in his cockpit who was not one of his crew, and that he was not asked where he had been and that he did not say, and that he had gone out of that harbor on Saturday morning at about seven and had been in the water in two places whose names this yard does not have, and that he came back in at about six on Saturday evening and was alongside that west quay again at about four that afternoon, and that about nine people at that market saw him do it and about four said nothing about it and the rest of that market was not told, and that he has had eleven words on a page in this harbor since before the winter and that nobody has answered them and that nobody has thanked him and that nobody has told him he was wrong, and that this yard is not going to use a boat coming in to make itself feel like a yard that is paying attention; and that at about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing, and that that is the fifth Sunday running, and that she said no on the twenty-fifth in nine people and gave a ground for it once, and that nobody has argued with her and that nobody has thanked her and that nobody has asked her since, and that she is not going to be asked this month; and that the page of hours looked at on the Saturday carried six men at five hours each being thirty hours with the multiplication printed at the head of it and that no penny of it went onto that money sheet and that no line went onto that sheet for it; and that nothing was read out of the fourteen at the end of that stall on that Sunday, that not one figure on that sheet moved by one, and that none of those fourteen went on and none came off; and that the count of positions was not taken a second time, that the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and has no figure set against it for any day, and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted on that Sunday and that count is not in that slip; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 692 words in about nine people; and that the Morrow Line is still in the dormant column of that sheet of days at the close of that day and is not struck and is still one of the nine and is still at Orison; and that one line went in on that first column at about six that evening and five on the second, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the forty-third, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and seventy-six and the second is one thousand seven hundred and fifty-four; the third of those four took nothing, that a boy's hand is at the head of it, his surname on no page and him off the water, and the fourth of them is unmoved at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line; and that the figure of four hundred and fifty pence did not come down any board on that Sunday, and that nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I said the minutes and not the number is that I said the hundredth a fortnight ago to about five people and told them it would not be the last, and that a figure said to an audience gets said differently the second time, and that seven bare minutes today against six last Sunday is a figure this yard will have to say something about one day and this is not that day; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not go up to those two men with their empty hands is that a yard that asks a man why he left his slate off a stone is a yard that has decided a slate belongs there, and that nine years of Sundays is not a rule of this harbor, it is a habit of two men, and that a habit of two men is theirs; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written down that a man of about fifty-three came in off two days of water in two places whose names this yard does not have is that I do not know where he was and that the not knowing is the honest figure, and that his eleven words have been on a page since before the winter and that a boat coming in is not a reason to go and look at them, and that this yard has been wrong about that man twice already and is not going to be wrong about him a third time this month; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason the fifth Sunday running is in this slip and nowhere else is that a thing a harbor does to a woman is not a thing that belongs in a column, and that saying it every week has stopped it being said and started it being a habit, and that a habit about a woman is worse than a column about her because a column can be read by her; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on four columns of a leaf and not on a wall is that a wall comes down and goes up and a leaf does not, and that the one that comes down is the one that gets counted out loud and the one that does not is the one that gets read.*
