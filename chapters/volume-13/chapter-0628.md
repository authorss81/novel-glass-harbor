# Chapter 628

*Saturday the Ninth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, the Word Restricted Going in Against the Ternwatch Passage at About Ten in the Morning on the Sheet of Days in That Shed With the Day and the Hour Against It and the Word Held Left Standing Above It and Still Readable, Being the Third of Three in One Week and the Third of the Three That Came Down That Coast Road, and the Three Columns of That Sheet Not Counted on This Day, a Man of About Forty-Five Who Crews Out of Ternwatch Saying It Out Loud at About a Quarter to Ten in About Nine People in About Ninety-Six Words and Nobody Asking Him a Second Time and Nobody Thanking Him, a Man of About Thirty-Two and a Woman of Thirty of No Office Arguing Out Loud at About Four in the Afternoon at the Flat of That Bench Stone in About Nine People in About Two Hundred and Seventy-Three Words and About Two Hundred and Forty-Three Words and About One Hundred and Sixty Words About What a Person Is Allowed to Carry and Nobody Arbitrating and Nobody Agreeing Out Loud and Neither of Them Giving Anything Up and Nobody Thanked and the Two of Them Still Married, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-One and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Saturday the ninth day of April, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, and a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Saturday with nothing standing against it: that cistern covered and untouched from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. At about ten this morning the third of the nine names on that sheet of days had a word put under another word, being restricted under held against the Ternwatch Passage, with the day and the hour of ten against the new word, and the word held is still on that sheet and is still readable above it. Three words in seven days, put there by three different people in three different mouths, and none of those three was asked by anybody in this harbor which position they were in. The third of the three went in at ten this morning, and the three columns of that sheet were not counted today and are not going to be counted this month by anybody in this yard, and the nine names are on that sheet and can be read off it one at a time. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a route crew's own word about its own run is not a line on that sheet and a married pair arguing on a bench stone is not a line on that sheet either. At ten that yard was handed a hundred and sixty-eight pence and at four that afternoon it handed a hundred and sixty-eight pence back, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, stayed on the slate through all of it. Set under a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, that is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. One hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, is the whole of what that sheet has carried out of this harbor; ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, is the whole of what has ever been carried into it; and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is what lies between those two figures. There has been no tin opened in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and this afternoon put nothing into that bag and took nothing out of it.**

**The nine went on again this morning and the seventh of them is the one in a back room over that market and the eighth of them is the only one of the nine that is nailed to a wall with a seal on it. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twentieth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty-six days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-eight. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty-six days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-second night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-eight days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and fifth day. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened by anybody here. The answer to those four lines is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked and there is still nothing written under the fourth one of them. That basket in that boathouse stands with the paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty-six days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about a quarter to ten a man of about forty-five who crews out of Ternwatch came down the boards from the end of that market with a coil of line over his shoulder, and put the coil down at the flat of that bench stone, and said one thing out loud in about nine people.

He had come up that coast road on Friday night and he had not been in that yard before that morning, and about nine people were standing round that stone and about four of them could read what was nailed to the wall above it.

"**The Ternwatch Passage is restricted from today, and I have come up that coast road to say it in a yard and not on a paper, and I am the one going back down it on Monday.** It is not coming back into held until the four of us say so in one of our own mouths on a day of our own choosing, and nobody in this harbor is going to come up that coast and ask me a second time about it. Put the word down and leave the rest of it with us."

That is about ninety-six words and nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him he was wrong, and about four people in that yard heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told at all.

He put the coil back on his shoulder and went back up the boards and did not wait to hear whether anybody had understood it.

---

At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf put one word on the sheet of days in that shed against the Ternwatch Passage, with Saturday the ninth of April and the hour of ten against it, and nobody in that shed had asked that man anything at any point in eleven weeks and he had not been in that yard before that morning.

The word held is still on that row and it is still readable and it sits above the word that is true now, and nobody in that shed can take it off. Three words have gone into that sheet in seven days against three different names and every one of them was put there by a person who came from that run and said it in their own mouth, and not one of those three was asked which position it was in, and that sheet decides nothing.

---

At about four in the afternoon, in about nine people at the flat of that bench stone with the market trading underneath, a man of about thirty-two and a woman of thirty of no office argued out loud for about eleven minutes and neither of them lowered their voice and neither of them gave anything up.

She went first and it was about two hundred and seventy-three words.

"**Three words in seven days. Three runs, three small numbers of people, and not one name on that board, and I want to say the half of this argument that nobody in this yard has said yet and then I want the other half said back to me in front of these nine.** The page of days is carrying three sentences about eleven men, a woman of forty-one, six people on a reed and four boats out of Ternwatch, and it is going to be nailed to that wall in forty years after every single one of them is dead, and it says about them that they stopped.

And we did not ask them. **We decided a run speaks for itself and so nobody was asked, and a run is not a person.** A run does not have a wife expecting in the spring. A run does not have a boy of fifteen who is not on the number. Those are people and they are going to walk past those boards in ten years with their grandchildren and read a word about themselves in a column of three with nobody's name against it, put there by a man who was not one of them and was not sent by them.

**What is a person allowed to carry.** Not on this page, that is the other question and we did that one on the thirtieth and I have not settled it either. On their own backs. A word with no name on it, forever, in a column, and no way at all of answering it, because you cannot answer a thing nobody has named."

He answered and it was about two hundred and forty-three words.

"**Then that sheet is a description of the weather.** A word about a run with nobody's name on it cannot be argued with by anybody, and it is not held up there because it is true and it is not held up there because it is careful, and if a stranger comes to this harbor in thirty years and reads that a run was restricted on the ninth of April, there is not one soul alive he can go and be angry at, and a page that cannot be got at has not protected anybody, it has only stopped the record from being a record.

**And I am not asking those eleven men a single thing, so do not put it on me that I want them asked. A run that has put itself down has put itself down and we were not going to ask it and we were right not to.** What I am saying is that the hand that wrote the word and the day and the hour are both on that sheet already, and you have been printing both of them for eleven weeks, and the name is not on it because nobody in this yard has the name and anybody who tells you otherwise is selling it. Print the hand. Print the hour. Stop writing a page with a sentence on it and no man behind it, because that is not modesty, that is a page about weather."

She answered back and it was about one hundred and sixty words.

"**And then that page has forty hands on it and forty hours against them, and the day one of those forty asks to be taken off it you will do it, and I will have to argue it in nine people at half past five, and that is the whole of what I am for and it is not a thing I am ashamed of.**

You want the sentence to have somebody behind it. I want the sentence to be about something. There is a difference and it is the same difference as the one we had on the thirtieth and it is not going to be settled this month and it is not going to be settled in nine people at a bench stone on a Saturday, and I am not going to settle it in a kitchen either, and anybody in this yard who says otherwise is telling you a thing about two people that is not true."

Nobody arbitrated. Nobody said she was right and nobody said he was right, and about four people in that yard heard all three of those things and the rest of that yard was not told, and nobody thanked either of them for anything and about five of that nine were not told that the argument had happened at all.

At about half past four a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said one thing out loud in about nine people and it was about thirty-eight words and nobody answered it.

"**I have been standing in nine people in this yard for four Saturdays and I have got nothing on the page and nobody has asked me for anything. Put that in a column and I will take it.**"

Nobody answered it and about four people in that nine did not hear it at all and nobody thanked him for saying it.

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and fifty-eight words, and nobody in that shed was asked which half of that argument was the half that settles it.

"**Two people who are married to each other argued out loud in nine people at that stone this afternoon for about eleven minutes and I was standing four paces away with a book under my arm and I have not written one word of any of it into a column, and I am going to say that out loud and let the nine people decide whether that was right of me.**

The number at the head of this slip is the only one I answer for, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, sitting where the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month left it. Lying under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and went out of it at four at that same figure, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**That sheet of days has three columns on it and nine names down the side of it and I did not count those columns tonight and I am not going to, and that count is not in this slip and is not going to be in one this month.** Anybody with a leaf and a pencil can read those nine names off that board one at a time and can add a column up whenever this harbor decides to do it, and neither of those is a thing nine people need to hear said at half past five.

And the other thing. Three words in seven days and every one of them said by a person who came from that run, and about four of the nine people who heard them can say out loud what the other six thought, and nobody asks the other six.

**A man of about thirty-three said thirty-eight words at that stone at half past four this afternoon and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him and about four people in that nine did not hear it.** I am putting that in a slip because in eleven weeks this harbor has written down four things that were said out loud by somebody nobody asked, and three of them have been by people who were old hands here, and this is the first one by a man who works for a living and has no reason to be standing in this yard at all, and it will not be the last one, and this yard has no column for it.

Nobody read out of the fourteen at the end of that stall today and not a figure on that sheet moved by one in either direction. The count of positions is not being taken again and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and nothing on that page says settled.

That first column has one entry on it from about six this evening and the second has five, and I have not stood in this shed and read them out. Three hundred and sixty-one stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and seventy-nine against the second, my third is empty, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for thirty-eight words at half past four. That shed was bare by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and those two people went up that coast road together and were in that market on Sunday morning and were still married.

A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ARGUED OUT LOUD AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN ABOUT 273 WORDS, ABOUT 243 WORDS AND ABOUT 160 WORDS ABOUT WHAT A PERSON IS ALLOWED TO CARRY, AND NOBODY ARBITRATED AND NOBODY AGREED OUT LOUD AND NEITHER OF THEM GAVE ANYTHING UP AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND THEY ARE STILL MARRIED. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID THIRTY-EIGHT WORDS AT THAT STONE AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR AND NOBODY ANSWERED HIM. NO ENTRY WAS TAKEN OUT OF ANY GAP AND EVERY FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN STOOD EXACTLY WHERE IT STOOD AT TEN THIS MORNING. ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT THE MONEY SHEET HAS COST THIS HARBOR, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT WENT INTO IT, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IS UNDER THAT SLATE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**
