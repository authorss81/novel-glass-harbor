# Chapter 601

*Sunday the Thirteenth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Ninety-Seventh Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Up Again at About a Quarter to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole and About Fourteen Minutes Both Ways Against Thirteen on the Sixth and Eleven on the Twenty-Seventh of Last Month and Nobody Having Said Why, a Sheet of Days Lying Under a Stone on the Boards at the Back of That Market When They Were Swept, Wet on the Top of It and Dry Underneath, With Days Down One Edge and Four Columns at the Head of It and a Place Filled In Against Every One of Those Days for Two Communities, Four Lines at the Head of It Saying What It Is and Four More Saying What It Does Not Do, Read Out Twice in About Nine People and Stopped Above the Seal at the Foot of It, Nobody Having a Name for Whoever Is Putting That Sort of Thing Out, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Paid Into That Yard at Ten in the Morning and Paid Out Again at Four in the Afternoon, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five and Not Moved, Twenty-Eight Pence Under the Slate, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the thirteenth day of March, in a market that trades on a Sunday, with a bench stone in a yard below it and a shed with a roof on it, and the boards at the back of that market swept at about seven in the morning.

**A wipe, and the ninety-seventh time that wall has come down, and no charge placed on that cistern on that day, and its covers lying on it at nine in the morning and still lying on it at four in the afternoon, and no rota day in it and no wage Tuesday in it either. Something came into this harbor in the night and is lying under a stone on those boards, and there is no name in this harbor for whoever is putting that sort of thing out, and that is the first thing on this page and it is a thing on paper and not a face. Not one line of the sheet that carries the money was written on in any hand that day and not one line was struck out of it, and the last hand to touch that sheet was on Saturday the twenty-sixth of last month and has not touched it since. One hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence has gone out of that yard since the beginning, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has come into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the space between those two figures is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and nobody in this harbor has been asked to make that space smaller and nobody is going to be. A hundred and sixty-eight pence, that is one mark and eightpence, was paid in at ten in the morning and the same figure was paid out at four in the afternoon, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, at the close of the day, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so that the bag is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of it. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not a penny of that night's cost was taken out of the bag under the slate, and none out of any boat's owner, and none out of the man with two papers, and none out of the Crown, and that includes a sheet of days, a wall, and a thing nobody has opened.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood two hundred and ninety-nine days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and one days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and thirty-nine days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and fifth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and twenty-three days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its hundred and seventy-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its hundred and ninety-third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and tenth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and forty-one days. At the head of that mole there are still five papers on that board and two of the five have never been opened by anybody. In the boathouse the basket stands where it has stood since the second of this month with the paper in it and nothing in the basket, and nobody has put a hand in to look. Two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine came home on that boat and can hear out of the left side of his head; it is ninety-nine days, no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

It came in with the rain of the night and it was under a lump of stone at the head of those boards, and when the boards were swept at about seven the stone came away and left the top of the sheet wet and the bottom of it dry, and that is the whole of how anybody knows when it arrived.

It is a sheet of days. The days run down the left edge in a printed hand and they begin on Monday the fourteenth of this month and they run to the end of April. Across the top of it there are four columns, and the four columns are OPENED, REFUSED, RESTRICTED and WITHDRAWN. Down the second column from the left there are two names of places and no names of persons at all, and against every one of those days there is a word standing in one of the four columns, and the word is the same word in every one of those days for each of the two of them.

At the head of the sheet there are eight lines, four of which say what the sheet is and four of which say what it did not do. At the foot of it there is a seal, and under that seal there is a name, and nobody in that market read the name under that seal out loud and nobody asked to and nobody is going to be asked.

---

And at about a quarter past ten on that Sunday two men began to take that wall down off its holders, and it was bare by about half past ten for the ninety-seventh time, and about four people near enough to see it happen saw it happen and did not stop.

It went back on at about a quarter to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole. Fourteen minutes to get it off the wall, one minute standing with it bare, and fourteen minutes to get it back, being twenty-nine minutes the whole of it, from about a quarter past ten to about a quarter to eleven, and that is longer than the thirteen minutes both ways of the sixth and longer than the eleven of the twenty-seventh of last month, and neither of the two men who do that work has said one word about it and neither has said a word about it since the thirteenth of last month.

---

At about eleven a woman of thirty of no office read that sheet out on those boards, twice, in the same words, with about nine people standing there, and she stopped at the seal at the foot of it and did not read the name under it, and nobody asked her to go on past it.

The four lines that say what it is are these.

1. *That this is a sheet of days, and that against every one of those days there is a place for one community, and that the four words at the head of it are opened, refused, restricted and withdrawn.*
2. *That a community which has taken on the counter-plan stands in one of those four on a day of this sheet, and that the word it stands in is the word that community said out loud on that day, and that the hour is the hour it said it at.*
3. *That a community may stand in the same column on as many days as it likes, and that nothing is asked of it for standing in the same column twice.*
4. *That this is a place to put a word and not a place to put a reason, and that no community is asked for a reason on it.*

The four lines that say what it did not do are these, and they are the four that have never been on anything else that has come out of a drawer on this coast.

1. *That it did not ask any community anything.*
2. *That it does not name a person.*
3. *That it does not carry a figure of persons and says so on its own face, and will not.*
4. *That it does not say who has set it out, and does not say what any of the four words at the head of it means.*

---

At about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and eighty-six words, to about nine people, and he had read that sheet twice himself in the middle of the day and had not answered it and nobody in that shed had answered it either.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Unmoved since Saturday afternoon, untouched in every hand that day, and the slate bag holds twenty-eight pence, and that cistern was not charged and the covers were lying on it all day, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that is true on every day of this week including the day that wall came down.

**A wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten this morning for the ninety-seventh time and went up again at about a quarter to eleven and it did not go up whole, and it took fourteen minutes to get it off the wall, one minute standing with it bare, and fourteen minutes to get it back, which is twenty-nine minutes the whole of it, and that is a minute longer each way than the sixth of this month and three minutes longer each way than the twenty-seventh of last month.** Nobody has said why it is getting slower and I have not asked either man who does it and I am not going to.

And there is a piece of paper in this harbor that nobody wrote. **That is the cleanest thing that has been put on this coast since the board came down with four hundred and fifty pence on it, and it is cleanest because it is not wrong about anything.** It says what it is and it says what it does not do, and the second of those two is the part that has not happened here before, and it has a seal on it and a name under the seal and the name is in nobody's mouth, and it costs a woman of thirty of no office eleven minutes of a morning to read out eight lines twice.

**And here is what I have not got an answer to and am not going to get one to today. A method built on visible gaps has to be run at a speed at which the edges of the gaps stop being visible, and a person who cannot see a gap is a person who has been handed a smooth page.** That is not chalk on a wall. Chalk on a wall is a page somebody has to climb up and count. **A calendar is a page nobody has to climb up.** It has four columns and it has a day against every day, and it asks a community to be in one of four places every morning of the month, and there is no place on it for a community to stop being in one of them, and that is not a fault in it, and that is the thing I want somebody else to be angry at instead of me, because I am not going to say it out loud in this shed and I am not going to say it twice.

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eight lines read out twice, and the shed stood empty by about six with the door standing open on a slip nobody was on. and that paper was still under the stone at the head of those boards with the stone put back on it by somebody who did not say they had done it."

**A SHEET OF DAYS CAME INTO THAT HARBOR IN THE NIGHT AND LAY UNDER A STONE ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WITH DAYS DOWN ONE EDGE AND FOUR COLUMNS AT THE HEAD OF IT, BEING OPENED, REFUSED, RESTRICTED AND WITHDRAWN, AND TWO NAMES OF PLACES DOWN THE SIDE OF IT AND NO NAME OF ANY PERSON, AND A WORD FILLED IN AGAINST EVERY DAY FOR BOTH OF THOSE PLACES, AND THE SAME WORD IN EVERY ONE OF THOSE DAYS FOR EACH OF THEM. IT HAS EIGHT LINES AT THE HEAD OF IT, FOUR OF WHICH SAY WHAT IT IS AND FOUR OF WHICH SAY WHAT IT DID NOT DO, AND IT WAS READ OUT TWICE IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND STOPPED AT THE SEAL AT THE FOOT OF IT WITH THE NAME UNDER THAT SEAL IN NO MOUTH, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS A NAME FOR WHOEVER IS PUTTING THAT SORT OF THING OUT. THE WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN FOR THE NINETY-SEVENTH TIME AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK ABOUT FOURTEEN MINUTES BOTH WAYS AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY IT IS GETTING SLOWER. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND NO LINE HAS GONE ONTO IT IN ANY HAND SINCE THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF LAST MONTH. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirteenth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the ninety-seventh time that wall has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the covers were lying on it at nine in the morning and at four in the afternoon; and that something came into that harbor in the night and was lying under a stone on the boards at the back of that market when those boards were swept at about seven, and that the top of it was wet and the bottom of it was dry, and that that is the whole of how anybody in that harbor knows when it arrived; and that it is a sheet of days with the days down its left edge running from Monday the fourteenth of this month to the end of April, and that across the head of it are four columns headed opened, refused, restricted and withdrawn, and that down the side of it are the names of two places and no name of any person, and that against every one of those days a word stands in one of those four columns for each of the two of those places, and that the word is the same word in every day for each of the two; and that at the head of the sheet are eight lines, four of which say what the sheet is and four of which say what it does not do, and that it does not require any community to open anything, and that it does not say what any of the four words at the head of it means, and that it does not name a person, and that it is not an account of what anybody has done, and that it does not say who has set it out; and that at the foot of it is a seal and under that seal is a name, and that nobody in that market read that name out loud and nobody asked to and nobody is going to be asked to; and that a woman of thirty of no office read that sheet out twice on those boards at about eleven in the morning in about nine people, in the same words both times, and stopped at the seal and did not go past it; and that at about half past ten the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the ninety-seventh time with about four people near enough to see it happen, and that it went up again at about a quarter to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and that it did not go up whole, and that it took fourteen minutes to get it off the wall and one minute standing with it bare and fourteen minutes to get it back, being twenty-nine minutes the whole of it from about a quarter past ten to about a quarter to eleven, and that that is longer than the thirteen minutes both ways of the sixth of this month and longer than the eleven minutes of the twenty-seventh of last month, and that neither of the two men who do that work has said a word about it and neither has said a word since the thirteenth of last month; and that the berth fund paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence into that yard at about ten in the morning, being one mark and eightpence of it, and drew the same hundred and sixty-eight pence out of it again at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight pence of that day's work, two shillings and fourpence of it, was lying under the slate when that day shut, and that the wage it is measured against is four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, so that what is under the slate is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind it; and that no hand in this harbor set a figure in any line of that money sheet on that Sunday morning and no hand lifted one out of it, and that nothing has been set in any line of it since Saturday the twenty-sixth of last month, and that the whole of what that sheet carries out of this yard is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, set against what has ever come in at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that the gap between those two is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that it is not made to balance by asking that harbor for the difference; and that the man who keeps the leaf said that shortfall out loud at that bench stone at about half past five in about five hundred and eighty-six words, and that in the middle of it he said that a calendar is a page nobody has to climb up, and that a method built on visible gaps has to be run at a speed at which the edges of the gaps stop being visible, and that a person who cannot see a gap is a person who has been handed a smooth page, and that the smooth page in this harbor is not chalk on a wall; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and thirty-four and the second at one thousand five hundred and forty-four, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the first day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March and not from the day this harbor last counted a thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about seven in the morning the reason I have written down that the top of that sheet was wet and the bottom of it was dry is that a thing which arrived some time in the night is a thing nobody in that harbor had to be caught doing, and that a paper is a very different thing to catch a man with, and that I wrote the wet and the dry down because it is the only part of the arrival that anybody can check in a week, and that a man in this harbor who told me he had seen who put it there would have told me something about himself and not about the paper; and that at about eleven o'clock the reason a woman of thirty of no office read those eight lines out twice on those boards and stopped at the seal is that the second reading is for the people who came in after the first one, and that there are always people coming in after the first one, and that she did not read the name under that seal because a name under a seal is a thing that is on the paper and not a thing that is anybody's to hand round at a fish stall, and that I have not asked her what she thought the four words at the head of it meant and I am not going to; and that at about a quarter past ten the reason I took the fourteen minutes of that wall down twice this morning is that it is the fifth figure I have for that wall and the other four were taken by men standing at it with a watch, and that a man standing at a wall with a watch on a Sunday is a man who has decided the wall matters more than the four things he came out for, and that I did it with the fifth one and this is the last one I am going to do; and that at about half past five the reason I said out loud in nine people that a calendar is a page nobody has to climb up, and that I said it at half past five and not at eleven in the morning, is that the morning is the hour this harbor is on the boards and the evening is the hour it stands in a shed with a door open, and that a thing about a calendar is not a thing nine people can argue with on a wet morning at a fish stall and is a thing nine people can stand still about at the end of a day; and that at about six o'clock the reason nobody in that harbor has a name for whoever is putting that sheet out is that a name is the only thing anybody could ask that sheet and there is nobody in it to ask, and that I have not written that down as a fault of the sheet and I have written it down as a fact about this harbor, which is that this harbor has spent eleven weeks learning to say what it does not know out loud and has not once in that time had to say it about a piece of paper.*
