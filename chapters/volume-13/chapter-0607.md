# Chapter 607

*Saturday the Nineteenth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Five Men of a Route Crew That Keeps the Ashtide Run in the Harbor With Their Own Boat at the End of That Stall and Never Asked for Anything by Anybody in This Harbor Before, Asked at About Ten in the Morning at the Head of That Mole to Hold One Mark for One Night and Saying No in About Two Hundred and Eighty-Eight Words on the Ground That a Night Is a Short Unit of Time to Be Given Away, and Saying Something Else Instead in About Two Hundred and Seventy-Six Words, Which Is to Sound Their Own Mark at the Hour the Paper Under the Stone Gives and to Put What They Hear at the End of That Stall Before Nine in the Morning, Nobody Argued With and Nobody Asked Again, the Crossings of That Mark Not Parted With and No Buoy Held and None Asked For, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and the Same Figure Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Figure of the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five, Twenty-Eight Pence in the Bag, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the nineteenth day of March, on the quay and at the head of the mole, and in a yard below with a bench stone in it and about nine people in that yard at half past five in the afternoon.

**Nothing scheduled on that Saturday at all: the cistern untouched and covered from eight until four, and no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe on it. Five men of a route crew that keeps the Ashtide Run had their own boat at the end of that stall from Friday evening and were still in this harbor on Saturday, and they have never been asked for anything by anybody in this harbor before today. They were asked at about ten in the morning at the head of that mole to hold one mark for one night, and they said no, and the reason one of them gave is that a night is a short unit of time to be given away, and they said something else instead. The crossings of that mark were not parted with, no buoy is held here and none was asked for, and the answer to that ask was not argued with and is not going to be asked a second time. That Saturday the sheet of money sat where it has sat since the twenty-sixth of last month and no hand went near it, and an answer to a question is not a line on that sheet. A hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, comes into that yard at about ten in the morning and the same figure goes out of it at about four in the afternoon, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, at the close of the day against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind. What that money sheet carries out of this yard is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, that ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever come into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence of it, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is not there. A night asked for by a man and refused by a crew has not put a penny on that sheet or taken one off it. No tin has been opened in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and that is the whole of the cost of that Saturday, and it includes a man walking out to the head of a mole at a quarter past nine in the morning and a man walking back.**

**The nine figures below are the ones this harbor has stopped counting out loud and has not stopped keeping. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and forty-seven days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The bench under that pump, the four hundred and eleventh night. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and five days. The two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-five. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-fourth day, and it now has a second sheet beside it. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and seven. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-nine days. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and sixteenth. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninety-ninth. That board at the head of the mole this morning carried five papers and two of them have never been opened, and it is a hundred yards from where five men of a route crew said no to a question this morning. The basket in that boathouse has the paper still in it and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is a hundred and five days, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

A man of about thirty-two walked out to the head of that mole at about a quarter past nine in the morning and asked five men of that crew one question, and he asked it once, and the five of them were sitting on their own transom with their own line coiled on the deck in front of them.

"**We are not asking for a buoy and we are not asking for the crossings of anything, and we know the answer before you do, and I am asking anyway because a thing that is not asked is a thing that is not known.** The paper that came into this harbor on Sunday has an hour standing against your mark on it, and we would like to know what your mark does at that hour, and the only way to know what a mark does at an hour is to have somebody on it at that hour, and we have not got anybody, and we are not going to take it off you. That is the whole of the ask. One mark. One night. Starting about nine tonight and standing down at first light."

---

A man of about forty-nine in that crew answered in about four hundred and one words, and he had not been asked anything before that morning by anybody in this harbor, and the other four of them said nothing at all while he said it.

"**A night is a short unit of time to be given away.** That is the whole of my answer and I am not going to dress it up. You have come out here to ask me for the part of my day that nobody sees, and you have asked me for it in the same breath as you have said you are not going to take anything off me, and both of those are true and only one of them is a favour. **I have five men here and two of them have not been on the water since a Thursday and one of them is nineteen, and there is no such thing as a small night in a house where somebody is nineteen.** So no, and you are not going to argue with me about it, and if you come out here on Monday with the same question in a different coat I will say the same word in the same number of words.

**And here is what I will do instead, and you can have it or not have it. We will sound our own mark at the hour that paper gives, from our own crossing, with our own hands, and we will come in at first light and we will put what we hear at the end of that stall before nine in the morning. If it answers, we will say what it answered and at what hour. If it does not answer, we will say that nothing answered, and that is a thing a stranger can write down and a stranger can ask us about next week.** That is not a night. That is a morning we were going to be up for anyway."

---

Nobody argued with him. Calder Marr did not ask a second question and did not ask any of the other four, and about four people standing inside the mole heard all of it and the rest of that quay was not told, and nobody thanked that crew for the morning they offered and nobody thanked them for the night they did not give.

The crossings of that mark stayed where they have always been, which is in the hands of the six people who keep them, and no buoy was carried up to that mole head by anybody from that yard and none was asked for and the offer of four lines is still behind a door in a room over that market with nothing written under its fourth line.

---

And a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall from about seven in the morning with a board across his knees, writing weights into a book in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and he is not on the water and nobody in that market has said a word to him about the water and nobody put a name to the third column of anybody's leaf this week.

---

At about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about Three hundred and ninety-eight Words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a night costs.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Nothing on it moved, and twenty-eight pence is in the bag, and no charge went on that cistern, and a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of this yard at four o'clock and is on no line of that sheet at all.

**Five men of a crew that keeps the Ashtide Run were asked at about ten o'clock this morning at the head of that mole to hold one mark for one night, and they said no, and one of them gave a reason in about two hundred and eighty-eight words and then gave a different thing instead in about 90 words, and both of those are on a page at the end of that stall if anybody wants them and neither of them is on a sheet.** No buoy is held here. None has been asked for. The crossings of that mark are in the hands of the six people who keep them and they are in the hands of the six people who keep them this evening, and a thing somebody gave up in the open is on no line of that money sheet, because that sheet is a list of what eleven people did with their own money.

**A night is a short unit of time to be given away, and that is the first sentence anybody has said out loud in this harbor in a fortnight that I have wanted to keep, and I am not going to say it a second time in this shed, and I have not written it on anything.** A man of about thirty-two asked once and took the answer and did not ask the other four, and that is the whole of what he did today and I have not put one word of it in this slip about how he looked while he was doing it.

One line went in on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and I did not read that leaf out loud, and this is the seventh day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth. The third of those four columns is empty and the fourth has not moved from one hundred and twenty-five, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it anywhere."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a morning offered to a shed four hundred yards off, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it.

**FIVE MEN OF A ROUTE CREW THAT KEEPS THE ASHTIDE RUN, NEVER ASKED FOR ANYTHING BY ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR BEFORE, WERE ASKED AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE TO HOLD ONE MARK FOR ONE NIGHT, AND SAID NO IN ABOUT 140 WORDS ON THE GROUND THAT A NIGHT IS A SHORT UNIT OF TIME TO BE GIVEN AWAY, AND SAID SOMETHING ELSE INSTEAD IN ABOUT 90 WORDS, BEING THAT THEY WILL SOUND THEIR OWN MARK AT THE HOUR THE PAPER UNDER THE STONE GIVES AND PUT WHAT THEY HEAR AT THE END OF THAT STALL BEFORE NINE IN THE MORNING, AND NOBODY WAS ARGUED WITH AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE ASKED AGAIN. NO BUOY IS HELD THERE AND NONE WAS ASKED FOR AND THE CROSSINGS OF THAT MARK WERE NOT PARTED WITH. THAT MONEY SHEET IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, AND THE FIGURE IT IS SHORT BY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about a quarter past ten in the morning the reason a man of about thirty-two asked a question he said he knew the answer to is that in six weeks this harbor has had four things said out loud that nobody had asked about and not one thing found out that had been asked, and that both of those are useful and only the second of them is a habit, and that a question is a thing a man finds out with and I would rather he was found out wrong in the open this month than not at all; and that at about ten o'clock the reason I have written down that a man of about forty-nine said that a night is a short unit of time to be given away, in his own words, at the head of a mole, is that it is the first sentence in six weeks that has cost a man something to say and come out of a mouth, and that everything else this harbor has said out loud in that time has cost about eleven minutes of a morning, and that a thing which costs a man something is a thing that can be asked about next week and a thing which costs eleven minutes cannot; and that at about half past three the reason I am not going to say in this slip what that crew offered this morning is that what they offered was a morning they were going to be up anyway, and that if I write it down here as a favour it becomes one, and that it was not one and the man who offered it was careful to say so twice; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says the crossings of that mark are in the hands of the six people who keep them and was careful to say it twice, at ten this morning and again this evening, is that in eleven weeks the only thing anybody in this harbor has been careful about twice is a thing that belongs to somebody else, and that a mark is not ours, and that a paper with an hour against our mark on it is a different thing from a mark; and that at about six o'clock the reason there is no figure in this slip about a night is that a night is not priced in this country and a man of about forty-nine said so in a better sentence than any figure I could put under it, and that a page of prices is what this harbor has been trying not to be since the autumn.*
