# Chapter 622

*Sunday the Third Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Hundredth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Off Its Holders at About a Quarter Past Ten and Bare at About Half Past Ten and Up Again at About Ten to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole, Seventeen Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Four Standing Bare and Seventeen to Get It Back, Being Thirty-Eight Minutes the Whole of It from About a Quarter Past Ten, a Minute Longer Than the Third of Last Month and Two Longer Than the Twelfth of March and Nobody Having Said Why, Two Men Not the Two Who Do That Wall Taking a Bearing Off the Mole Head Off Its Empty Frame at About Eleven in the Morning While It Stood Bare, the Crown Vessel of About Ninety Tons Showing Her Bottom at Low Water About Two Hundred Yards Off That Bar and Not Being Gone and Not Being Looked at from the Beach, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-Five and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Sunday the third day of April, with that market trading all morning and a flat sea under it, and a yard below that market with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Sunday and the hundredth time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down. That butt at the back of the market was covered from nine until four and no charge went on it, and there is no rota day in this and no wage Tuesday in it. Two men who are not the two who do that wall took a bearing off the head of that mole at about eleven this morning while the wall stood bare, and the wall has been bare four minutes longer this month than it was bare last month, and nobody in this harbor has said why and this yard has stopped asking the two men who do it. A Crown vessel of about ninety tons showed her bottom at low water this afternoon about two hundred yards off that bar for about an hour and a half and did not go anywhere and was not looked at from the beach by anybody. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a bearing taken off an empty frame is not a line on that sheet and a hull showing her bottom at low water is not a line on that sheet either. Into that yard at about ten in the morning went a hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, and out of it at about four in the afternoon went the identical figure, and what stayed lying under that slate was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. Set against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, that is a gap of six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Out of this harbor on that sheet has gone one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; into it has ever come ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and what it never carries is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing that happened this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine have gone on again and the first of them is the oldest thing this harbor carries and the fourth of them is the only one of the nine that is a thing on a stone. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-two. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-sixth night, and there is nothing under it. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-four days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its one hundred and ninety-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fourteenth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-first. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-two days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers stand on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened by anybody here. Four lines are still behind a door in an upper room over that market, nothing is written under the fourth of them, and the answer to those four lines is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. The basket in that boathouse is where it has been since the second of this month with the paper inside it and nothing else. About two sea miles off the head of that mole there lie nine hulls this afternoon, and nobody has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty days, one more than the day before it, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about a quarter past ten on that Sunday two men began to take the wall behind that reeve's bench down off its holders, and it was bare by about half past ten for the hundredth time, and about five people near enough to see it happen did not stop them and did not stand and watch either.

It went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole. Seventeen minutes to get it off the wall, four minutes standing with it bare, seventeen minutes to get it back, which is thirty-eight minutes the whole of it from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven. That is a minute longer than the third day of last month and two longer than the twelfth of March and nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower, and this yard has stopped asking, and neither man who does it has ever been asked by anybody about it.

**It is the hundredth time and it is the first time anybody in this harbor has said so out loud.** It was said by the man who keeps the leaf, to about five people, at about half past ten, standing in front of the frame with his hands at his sides.

---

At about eleven, with the wall still bare and about nine minutes to go on it, two men this harbor does not pay and does not roster put a slate flat on the stone sill of that frame and took a bearing off the head of that mole from it, and one of them called a figure out loud and the other one wrote it on the back of his hand.

They were taking the bearing of a lump of stone above the mole head that two boats coming in from the south west use, and they had done it that way every Sunday for as long as either of them could remember, because there is nothing else at that end of that mole to take a bearing from at that hour, and a wall that is not there is the easiest sightline anybody has ever had.

They were off the frame at about eleven and the two men who do that wall came back with the holders at about a quarter to eleven and put it up at the angle and not whole. Nobody asked the two men with the slate what their figure was and nobody wrote it down and it is not on any page in this harbor.

---

The Crown vessel of about ninety tons showed her bottom for about an hour and a half this afternoon at low water, about two hundred yards off that bar of hard sand, and she did not move and nothing came off her and nobody on that beach looked at her.

About four people in that market stopped what they were doing for about a minute and looked out and about five of that market did not, and about nine people in the yard below did not look up from the bench stone at all, and that man of about thirty-two was not one of the nine who looked.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and sixty-five words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a bare frame is good for.

"**Thirty-eight minutes. Seventeen off the wall, four standing bare, seventeen back, from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven, and it is a minute longer than the third of last month and two longer than the twelfth of March, and that is a hundredth time and I am the first man in this harbor to have said so out loud and I am not going to say it again this year.**

I am not taking a new figure of that wall. This yard has printed six of those since the autumn and five of them were taken by men with watches and I printed the sixth and I am not printing a seventh. The clock closes and it closes in the same words it has closed in every one of those six and there is nothing in the hundredth of it that is different from the ninety-ninth except that somebody counted it.

**Here is what those four bare minutes are for, and I have had it in front of me all morning and I did not write it down until now.** Two men this harbor does not pay and does not roster take a bearing off that mole head off that sill every Sunday morning at about eleven, and they have done it that way for longer than I have been in this yard, and this morning it was the hundredth time that sill has been there empty at eleven o'clock and they used it, and they were off it at about a quarter to eleven before the holders came back. I have not asked them for their figure and I am not going to. A yard that asks a man for his bearing is a yard that has decided it knows what a bearing is for.

The figure at the head of this slip is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is where the twenty-sixth of last month left it. There are twenty-eight pence under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and a berth fund pays a day for a day and does not care what a sea is under martial custody or what a paper says.

**A hull of about ninety tons showed her bottom this afternoon for about an hour and a half and about four people in that market looked at her and about five did not and nobody went down to her and nobody has gone down to her since Saturday and nobody is going to.** That is a fact about four people and five people and it is not a figure and I am not turning it into one. What is on that sand is a season's work and a hull and six men, and I printed that in a slip on Saturday and I am not printing it again today, and the second time a thing is printed it starts to look like it is being counted, and it is not being counted, it is just sitting there.

The fourteen on that sheet at the end of that stall were not read out of this morning and no figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen has been added to and none taken off.

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud. Three hundred and fifty-five stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and forty-nine against the second, my third is empty, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four minutes of an empty frame. That shed was bare by about six with the door open on a slip nobody was standing at, and the wall behind that reeve's bench stood at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and not whole.

**THE WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE ON THAT SUNDAY AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN, BEING THE HUNDREDTH TIME, AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE. IT TOOK SEVENTEEN MINUTES TO GET OFF THE WALL, FOUR STANDING BARE, AND SEVENTEEN TO GET IT BACK, BEING THIRTY-EIGHT MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT FROM ABOUT A QUARTER PAST TEN TO ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN, AGAINST SIXTEEN BOTH WAYS ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF LAST MONTH AND FOURTEEN ON THE THIRTEENTH, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. TWO MEN NOT ON THAT ROTA BOARD TOOK A BEARING OFF THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE OFF ITS EMPTY SILL AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND WERE OFF IT AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN, AND NOBODY ASKED THEM FOR THEIR FIGURE AND IT IS ON NO PAGE. THE CROWN VESSEL OF ABOUT NINETY TONS SHOWED HER BOTTOM AT LOW WATER FOR ABOUT AN HOUR AND A HALF AND NOBODY WENT TO HER. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NOT ONE FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IS ALL THAT HAS EVER COME INTO THAT SHEET, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT HAS COST, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, IS WHAT IT NEVER CARRIED. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the third day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the hundredth time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that at about a quarter past ten two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten with about five people near enough to see it and nobody stopping them and nobody standing and watching; and that it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and that it took seventeen minutes to get it off the wall, four minutes standing with it bare and seventeen minutes to get it back, which is thirty-eight minutes the whole of it from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven, and that that is a minute longer than the third day of last month and two longer than the twelfth of March, and that nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and that this yard has stopped asking and that neither man who does it has ever been asked by anybody about it; and that at about half past ten the man who keeps the leaf said out loud in front of about five people that this was the hundredth time, and that he is the first man in this harbor to have said so out loud and that he is not going to say it again this year; and that at about eleven two men this harbor does not pay and does not roster put a slate flat on the stone sill of that frame and took a bearing off the head of that mole from it, one calling a figure out loud and the other writing it on the back of his hand, and that they had done it that way every Sunday for as long as either of them could remember because there is nothing else at that end of that mole to take a bearing from at that hour and a wall that is not there is the easiest sightline anybody has ever had, and that they were off that frame at about eleven and the two men who do that wall came back with the holders at about a quarter to eleven and put it up at the angle and not whole, and that nobody asked either of those two men what their figure was and nobody wrote it down and it is on no page in this harbor; and that the Crown vessel of about ninety tons showed her bottom for about an hour and a half this afternoon about two hundred yards off that bar at low water and did not move and nothing came off her, and that about four people in that market stopped what they were doing for about a minute and looked out and about five did not, and that about nine people in the yard below did not look up from that bench stone at all and that the man of about thirty-two was not one of the nine who looked; and that the sheet with the fourteen things on it was not read out of and no figure on it moved by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none taken off; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about six hundred and sixty-five words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March is the twenty-second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and fifty-five and the second is one thousand six hundred and forty-nine, and that the third took nothing, and that the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I said the hundredth time out loud to five people is that six figures have been taken of that wall since the autumn and none of them ever had a number to sit next to, and that a thing that has happened a hundred times and has never been counted out loud is not a hundred things, it is one thing a hundred times, and that the difference matters to me because I write things down and this is the first figure on that wall that anybody in this harbor owns; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not ask those two men for their bearing is that I have been in this yard eleven weeks and this is the first time I have understood that a wall coming down on a Sunday is not only a thing being taken off a wall, and that those two men have had that sill to themselves every Sunday morning since before I came here and did not know that they had it, and that if I had asked one of them for his figure this morning he would have given it to me and neither of them would have used that sill again; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason I have written down that a hull showed her bottom for about an hour and a half and that about four people looked and about five did not is that four and five is the only number in this slip about anybody and it is a number about looking and not about people, and that I will not write it a second way; and that at about two o'clock in the afternoon the reason I have not written one word in this column about what a bottom looks like is that I did not go and look and a man of about fifty-six who goes down that beach every day of the week looked at it and went on doing what he was doing, and that I have known for eleven weeks that the difference between a thing and a fact about a thing is whether the man writing it went and looked; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason this slip closes on a Sunday at the same hour it closes on a Wednesday is that a berth fund pays a day for a day, and that the day asked for by name went out of that yard at the figure in chalk at four this afternoon whether anybody was on the water or not, and that there are two men in this harbor who have not been on the water in a fortnight and the figure did not ask them.*
