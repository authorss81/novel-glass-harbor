# Chapter 619

*Thursday the Thirty-First Day of March, a Rota Day, That Cistern Behind That Market Charged for the Hundred and Thirty-Eighth Time in Eleven Trips by Two Men at About Eight in the Morning With Nothing Carried up It on Any Other Errand, the Covers Back Before a Quarter Past Nine and the Water at the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Woman of About Thirty-Nine Coming Down Those Boards at About Eleven in the Morning and Standing at the Flat of That Bench Stone for About Six Minutes and Reading the Back of That Page With Her Own Eyes and Then Going Back to Her Own Stall, Nobody Asked Her Anything and Nobody Stopped Her and Nobody Followed Her and a Man of About Thirty-Two in That Nine Who Did Not Follow Her Either, the Fifth Word Not Yet Written and Friday the First Day of April Not Moved by Anybody, no Line of a Gap Read Out and no Figure Shifted by One, the Shortfall Given Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Slate Holding Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-Two and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Thursday the thirty-first day of March, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before that market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about eleven in the morning.

**A Thursday rota with no wage Tuesday and no wipe in it. That cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-eighth time in eleven trips by hand and walked on by none of it, the covers back before a quarter past nine and the butt full to the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. A woman of about thirty-nine came down those boards at about eleven, stood at the flat of that bench stone for about six minutes, read the back of that page with her own eyes and went back to her own stall, and nobody asked her one thing and nobody stopped her and nobody followed her and nobody has said a word about it since. The fifth word is not on that page yet and Friday the first day of April was not moved today by anybody. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a woman standing at a bench stone for six minutes is not a line on that sheet. That yard's day was a hundred and sixty-eight pence at ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence at four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was under that slate when the day shut, being six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. What that sheet has sent out of this harbor is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and what it has ever taken in is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and what it has never had is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Nobody in this harbor has opened a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of what happened on this morning came out of what is lying under that slate.**

**The nine went on again and the eighth of them is the only one of the nine that is nailed to a wall in a street with a seal on it. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-eighth day. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-seven days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and seventeen. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and nineteen. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-third night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-nine days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-sixth day, and three papers stand at that end of that market this month and they are three different papers. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and eleventh. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and the offer of four lines has not left the upper room over that market it is in, there is still nothing written under its fourth line, and the answer to it is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. Nobody in this harbor has emptied that basket in that boathouse, moved it, or looked into it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventeen days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

It was charged a few minutes after eight in the morning, eleven trips up that ladder by two men, and nothing carried up it on any other errand. The covers were back on the butt before a quarter past nine, the water stood at the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon, and not one bucket was taken out of it in all that time, and the name at the head of that board is still off it.

---

At about eleven a woman of about thirty-nine came down those boards from her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and stood at the flat of that bench stone for about six minutes.

She did not touch the page. She put her hands behind her back and she read the back of it with her own eyes, which carries the two drafts from Monday with one above the other and the two lines from yesterday under both of them, and there are about nine people standing round that stone and about four of them could read what she was reading.

Then she went back up to her own stall and mended net. Nobody asked her one question and nobody told her what the fifth word was going to be and nobody asked her whether she agreed with Monday's drafts or with yesterday's argument, and nobody in that yard followed her across that yard, and about five people in that nine did not notice her at all and about four noticed every second of it.

A man of about thirty-two was in that nine and did not follow her either, and that is the second time in six days he has been standing in a nine where the thing he wanted to do was not done.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and eighty-seven words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a woman sees when she reads a page she is not asked about.

"**A woman stood at that stone for about six minutes this morning and read the back of that page and then went back to her own stall and mended net, and I have been in that yard since half past eight and I did not go over, and neither did anybody else, and I am putting that in a slip because in about eleven weeks nobody in this harbor has ever written down a thing somebody did not do.** The figure I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is where the twenty-sixth of last month left it, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, is lying under this slate, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

Now the thing I want said out loud in nine people tonight, and it is not about her and it is about that page.

**Three of us have written on that page since Monday and she has read it, and on Friday there is going to be a fifth word on the back of it, and nobody in this yard has the right to say that word is hers.** She did not write it, she was not asked for it, and she came and stood at the flat of that stone this morning for six minutes and went back to her stall. If that page goes on Friday with a word on it that she has not spoken, then that word is this yard's, and it is going to say so in a line of its own under the word itself, in a hand that is not hers, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil will be able to see the two hands and tell which is which.

And I will not be asking her. She has been asked what she thinks three times in nine days and none of those three times by anybody in this yard, and a person who has said no to this harbor three times in nine days has earned the right to stand at a bench stone and say nothing at all, and the right to say nothing is the only thing any of us has got left.

**The two drafts from Monday are both still standing on that page with one above the other and neither struck, and the two lines from Wednesday are under both of them and neither of those is struck either, and there are four things on that page now that disagree with each other and a fifth word going on the top of the lot of them.** That is not a page being revised. That is a page being argued with in ink, and it is the most honest object in this harbor and I have not read one line of it out loud and I am not going to.

At about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second and I did not read that leaf out loud. My first of those figures is three hundred and fifty-two and my second is one thousand six hundred and thirty-four, my third is empty, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for six minutes at a bench stone. That shed stood empty by about six with the door open on a slip with nobody on it. The cistern behind that market was still up to the rim of the second hoop and the name was still off that board, and that page was still turned back to Monday.

---

**THAT CISTERN WENT UP ON THAT THURSDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHTH TIME, ELEVEN TRIPS BY TWO MEN AND NOT ONE BUCKET OF IT WALKED ON, THE COVERS BACK BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE, THE BUTT AT THE RIM OF ITS SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD STILL OFF IT. A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE STOOD AT THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT ELEVEN FOR SIX MINUTES, READ THE BACK OF THAT PAGE WITH HER OWN EYES, AND WENT BACK TO HER OWN STALL, AND NOBODY ASKED HER ANYTHING AND NOBODY FOLLOWED HER AND THE FIFTH WORD IS NOT ON THAT PAGE YET AND NOBODY MOVED FRIDAY. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, IS WHAT THAT SHEET HAS SENT OUT OF THIS HARBOR; TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, IS ALL THAT HAS EVER COME INTO IT; AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, IS WHAT IT HAS NEVER HAD. UNDER THE SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the thirty-first day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern behind that market went up on its ladder for the hundred and thirty-eighth time, eleven trips by two men before the market opened and nothing carried up it on any other errand, that the covers went back on before a quarter past nine, that the water stood at the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon with no bucket out of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that at about eleven a woman of about thirty-nine came down those boards from her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and stood at the flat of that bench stone for about six minutes, and that she did not touch the page and put her hands behind her back and read the back of it with her own eyes, and that the back of that page carries the two drafts from Monday with one above the other and the two lines from the Wednesday under both of them, and that about nine people were standing round that stone and about four of them could read what she was reading; and that she then went back up to her own stall and mended net, and that nobody asked her one question and nobody told her what the fifth word was going to be and nobody asked her whether she agreed with Monday's drafts or with the Wednesday's argument, and that nobody in that yard followed her across it, and that about five people in that nine did not notice her at all and about four noticed every second of it; and that a man of about thirty-two was in that nine and did not follow her either, and that that is the second time in six days he has been standing in a nine where the thing he wanted to do was not done; and that the fifth word is not on that page yet and Friday the first day of April was not moved today by anybody in that harbor; and that no word of the sheet of the fourteen was said out loud in that market this month, that its figures did not move by one, and that it was not touched; and that the sheet that describes a made thing and is not the thing was not looked at by anybody in that harbor that day; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and eighty-seven words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and fifty-two and the second is one thousand six hundred and thirty-four, and that the third of those four is empty and that the fourth of them has not moved from one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month is the nineteenth.*

*Second column, entered in that yard's own mouth on the said Thursday at about six in the evening and not checkable from a mole, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason nobody followed her across that yard is that a woman who came and read a page and went back to her stall has told this harbor everything she came to tell it, and that the three people in that yard who wanted to follow her wanted to follow her for our reasons and not hers, and that a man of about thirty-two has not been on the water for three days and has had one hour and a quarter of it on a quay with a wedge at his feet and a man nine feet off him, and that wanting to follow somebody is the symptom and not the cure; and that at about half past eleven in the morning the reason I did not ask her what she thought of the two drafts is that she has been asked what she thinks on the sixteenth and on the eighteenth and on the twenty-fifth, three times in nine days, none of them by anybody in this yard, and that a woman who says no to this harbor three times in nine days has earned the right to stand at a bench stone and say nothing, and that the right to say nothing is the only thing any of us have got left; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have written down that that page was still turned back to Monday when I shut that shed is that three of us have added to it since Monday and she has added nothing, and that a page which four people write on and one person only reads is a page that will end up describing four people, and that I have known that since Wednesday afternoon and I have not told anybody and I am going to put it on that page on Friday in a line of its own if nobody else does; and that at about eight in the morning the reason that cistern is the first line of this slip on the last rota day of this month is that a hundred and thirty-eight charges have gone past and the name at the head of that board is still off it, and that this yard has been printing eleven trips every rota day for three weeks and has printed nothing at all about the two men who carry it, and that on Friday I am going to stop printing the cistern for a week and print the page instead, and that is the only thing in this slip that is a plan and it is not a promise; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have not written one word in this column about whether I am glad she came or not is that I am not, and that I would have written the fifth word this morning if she had stood there for a quarter of an hour instead of six minutes, and that the whole of the method says I was wrong to want that, and that this column is the one place in this harbor where a man is allowed to want something and is not allowed to have it.*
