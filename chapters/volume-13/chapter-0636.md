# Chapter 636

*Sunday the Seventeenth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Hundred and Second Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Off Its Holders at About Ten Past Ten and Bare at About Half Past Ten and Up Again at About Ten to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole, Nineteen Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Six Standing Bare and Nineteen to Get It Back, Being Forty-Four Minutes the Whole of It from About Ten Past Ten to About Ten to Eleven, a Minute Longer Than the Tenth of This Month and Two Longer Than the Third, and Nobody Having Said Why, Two Men Not the Two Who Do That Wall Taking a Bearing Off the Mole Head Off Its Empty Frame at About Eleven in the Morning While It Stood Bare, a Cistern Covered from Nine Until Four and Untouched, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything on the Fourth Sunday Running, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Nine and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Nineteen*

Sunday the seventeenth day of April, with that market trading all morning under a sky that had finally stopped going down, a yard below it with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Sunday and the hundred and second time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and no charge on that cistern with its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it. Two men began to take that wall off its holders at about ten past ten and it was bare by about half past ten and it was back against the holders at about ten to eleven, sitting at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame was still not whole. Nineteen minutes to get it off the wall, six minutes standing with it bare, nineteen minutes to get it back, which is forty-four minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven. That is a minute longer than the tenth of this month, two longer than the third, three longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and this yard has stopped asking the two men who do it. The wind came back into the west on Saturday and the glass stopped going down at half past twelve yesterday and has stood since, and the sea outside that mole head came down a foot and a half between two o'clock yesterday and two o'clock this morning on no wind at all. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a wall is not a line on that sheet, and a sea coming down is not a line on that sheet either. That yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of it at about four in the afternoon, and what lay under that slate at the day's end was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which against four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. What this harbor has spent on that sheet, up to the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month, comes to one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and the whole of what anybody has ever put into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and those two figures leave ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence outstanding, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. There is no tin in that shed that anybody has opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and nothing this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on again and the second of them is the only one of the nine that is a man who has not been asked for a figure in thirteen weeks. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-six days. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-four days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-four. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and fortieth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-eight days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and thirteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-eighth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-six days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are still on that board at the head of that mole and this harbor has not opened two of them at any time this year. Those four lines are still behind a door in an upper room over that market, nothing has ever been written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse there is a basket with a paper in it and no other thing in that basket. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this morning, there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-four days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten past ten on that Sunday two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten for the hundred and second time, and about five people near enough to see it happen went on with what they were doing.

They had it back against the holders at about ten to eleven at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame was still not whole. Nineteen minutes off the wall, six standing bare, nineteen back, which is forty-four minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven. That is a minute longer than the tenth of this month and two longer than the third and three longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and nobody has said why, and two men have now been asked by nobody at all.

---

At about twenty to eleven, with that wall bare and about nine minutes to go on it, two men this harbor does not pay and does not roster set a slate flat on the stone sill of that frame and took a bearing off the head of that mole from it, the way they have done every Sunday morning for as long as either of them can remember, because a wall that is not there is the easiest sightline anybody has ever had.

One of them called a figure out loud and the other one wrote it on the back of his hand. They were off the frame a few minutes before the two men who do that wall came back with the holders at about a quarter to eleven and put it up at the angle and not whole.

Nobody asked those two men what their figure was and nobody wrote it down and it is not on any page in this harbor.

---

At about eleven as well, a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market stood at her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and worked, and nobody in this harbor came to that stall and asked her a single thing.

That is the fourth Sunday running. She said no on the twenty-fifth in nine people and gave a ground for it once, and nobody has argued with her and nobody has thanked her and nobody has asked her since, and this morning she was not asked whether she agreed with a word in any column, and she is not going to be asked this month.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 613 words, and nobody in that shed was asked to say that figure out loud twice.

"**Forty-four minutes. Nineteen off the wall, six standing bare, nineteen back, from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven, and that is the hundred and second time, and I am not going to say the hundred and second out loud this evening. I said the hundredth a fortnight ago to about five people and I said it would not be the last, and a man who counts a thing for an audience has started performing it and that is the last thing that frame has needed.**

Six minutes standing bare today against five last Sunday and four the Sunday before and three the one before that, and nineteen each way against eighteen last Sunday and seventeen the Sunday before, and nobody in this harbor has said why and this yard has stopped asking.

And the wind has gone round into the west and the glass has stood since half past twelve yesterday and the sea came down a foot and a half overnight on nothing at all, and **I have said twice in nine people this week that I do not know what that weather was, and I am not going to say anything this evening about what it was or what made it, and a page is not the place to find out.**

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, stands at the head of that money sheet and has not moved off that mark since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**Eight days of this work have not been settled on that sheet and there are eight and the last of them is the twentieth, and when that line goes on it is going to carry the days the work was done on it and the day this yard settles it, and both of those will be on the face of it, and I am not going to write a figure on it this evening on a Sunday with a ledger open and a boy asleep on the stool behind me.**

Nobody at the end of that stall was asked one thing this morning and that is the fourth Sunday running and I am going to keep saying that in this shed until somebody stops me and the somebody is not going to be me.

Nobody read anything out of the fourteen at the end of that stall this morning, and not one figure on that sheet moved by a single one, and not one of those fourteen was added to and not one was taken off. The count of positions is not being taken again, and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and stands against no day with a figure.

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and sixty-nine stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and nineteen against the second, my third has taken nothing into it, a boy's hand being at the head of it, his surname being on no page and he not being on the water, and my fourth is where it has always been, at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a bearing taken off a bare frame. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that wall stood at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and the woman of about thirty-nine was still at that market at six.

---

**THAT WALL CAME DOWN BARE ON THAT SUNDAY AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN, BEING THE HUNDRED AND SECOND TIME, AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE. IT TOOK NINETEEN MINUTES TO GET OFF THE WALL, SIX STANDING BARE, AND NINETEEN TO GET IT BACK, BEING FORTY-FOUR MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT FROM ABOUT TEN PAST TEN TO ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN, AGAINST EIGHTEEN BOTH WAYS AND FIVE BARE ON THE TENTH OF THIS MONTH AND SEVENTEEN AND FOUR ON THE THIRD, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. TWO MEN THIS HARBOR DOES NOT PAY AND DOES NOT ROSTER TOOK A BEARING OFF THAT MOLE HEAD OFF THAT EMPTY SILL AT ABOUT TWENTY TO ELEVEN AND SAID A FIGURE OUT LOUD AND WROTE IT ON THE BACK OF A HAND AND IT IS NOT ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR. NOBODY WENT TO THE STALL OF THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE THIS MORNING AND ASKED HER ONE THING, AND THAT IS THE FOURTH SUNDAY RUNNING. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT SHEET OF MONEY IS WHERE THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF LAST MONTH LEFT IT. WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS EVER BEEN PUT INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT THAT SLATE IS HOLDING.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the seventeenth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the hundred and second time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that at about ten past ten two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten, and that it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and that it took nineteen minutes to get it off the wall, six minutes standing with it bare and nineteen minutes to get it back, which is forty-four minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven, and that that is a minute longer than the tenth of this month, two longer than the third and three longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and that nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and that this yard has stopped asking the two men who do it; and that at about twenty to eleven, with that wall bare and about nine minutes to go on it, two men this harbor does not pay and does not roster set a slate flat on the stone sill of that frame and took a bearing off the head of that mole from it the way they have done every Sunday morning for as long as either of them can remember, one of them calling a figure out loud and the other one writing it on the back of his hand, and that they were off the frame a few minutes before the two men who do that wall came back with the holders at about a quarter to eleven and put it up at the angle and not whole, and that nobody asked those two men what their figure was and nobody wrote it down and it is not on any page in this harbor; and that at about eleven a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market stood at her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and worked and nobody in this harbor came to that stall and asked her a single thing, and that that is the fourth Sunday running, and that she said no on the twenty-fifth in nine people and gave a ground for it once and nobody has argued with her and nobody has thanked her, and that she was not asked this morning whether she agreed with a word in any column; and that the wind came back into the west on Saturday and the glass stopped going down at half past twelve yesterday and has stood since, and that the sea outside that mole head came down a foot and a half between two o'clock yesterday and two o'clock this morning on no wind at all; and that nobody read anything out of the sheet of the fourteen at the end of that stall this morning, and that not one figure on it was altered by one, and that none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off; and that the count of positions is not being taken again, and that the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and stands against no day with a figure; and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted on this Sunday and that count is not in this slip; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 613 words in about nine people, and that he said in it that he had said twice in nine people that week that he does not know what that weather was and was not going to say anything about it on a Sunday; and that he said in it that eight days of that work had not been settled on that sheet and that there are eight and the last of them is the twentieth; and that the Morrow Line stands in the held column of that sheet of days where it has stood since before the winter, and is not struck and is still one of the nine; and that one line went in on that first column at about six that evening and five went in on the second, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the thirty-sixth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and sixty-nine and the second is one thousand seven hundred and nineteen, and that the third of those four is as empty as it was, a boy's hand at the head of it, his surname on no page, and he not on the water, and that the fourth of them is where it has always been at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that the woman of about thirty-nine was still at that market at six that evening.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I did not say the hundred and second time out loud is that I said the hundredth a fortnight ago to about five people and told them it would not be the last, and that a figure said to an audience gets said differently the second time, and that six bare minutes this week against five last week is a figure that this yard will have to say something about one day and today is not the day; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have put nothing in this column all week about that weather is that I said on Tuesday in nine people that I do not know what it was, and that a man who has said he does not know does not spend a week finding out, and that the three bells and their sum are on a wall and are the only thing about that week this yard can hand to a stranger, and that they are a count of a gap and not a report of a cause; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not go up to the two men with the slate is that a yard that asks a man for his bearing is a yard that has decided it knows what a bearing is for, and that a hundred and second frame is not a place to start a habit; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason I have said nothing this week about a boy asleep on a stool at the end of that stall is that he has been off the water six times in thirteen weeks and that six is a number about him and not a number about this yard, and that this yard has no column for it and is not going to make one, and that his hand is still at the head of my third column and his name is on no page; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason I am going to say the fourth Sunday running in this slip and nowhere else is that a thing a harbor does to a woman is not a thing that belongs in a column, and that the only place it can be said out loud in a form other than an insult is a shed at half past five, and that in eleven weeks nobody has stopped me; and that at about seven in the evening the reason there is no figure of money on that sheet on a Sunday is that a day is settled when a yard settles it and this yard settles days on working afternoons, and that the eighth of those eight days is the twentieth of this month and that day is going to carry the days the work was done on it and the day it was settled, and that I have not written one penny of this fortnight out of anybody's hand and I am not going to.*
