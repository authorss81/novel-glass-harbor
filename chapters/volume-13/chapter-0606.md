# Chapter 606

*Friday the Eighteenth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Community Saying Out Loud at About Eleven in the Morning on the Boards at the Back of That Market in About Nine People That It Is in a Different Position, Unasked and Unthreatened and Without Being Asked a Second Time, the Position Taken Back Off Wednesday the Sixteenth by the Man Who Keeps the Leaf in His Own Hand, the Figure in the Opened Column Struck and Left Readable and the Figure One Written Underneath It in the Restricted Column With the Hour of Friday Against It, Nobody Asked Why and Nobody Blamed and Nobody Thanked, a Man of About Thirty-Two Standing at the Back of Those Boards for About Nine Minutes and Saying Nothing at All, the Berth Figure of a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Into That Yard in the Morning and Out of It in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Named Out Loud at Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence*

Friday the eighteenth day of March, on the boards at the back of that market at about eleven in the morning with about nine people standing on them, and in a yard below with a bench stone in it and a flat of bare stone where a canvas bag had stood the day before.

**A Friday carrying nothing but the day's own work, no cistern charge, covers lying on that cistern from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday, no wipe, and the page of positions at the end of that end stall has three figures standing on it this evening and one of those three was struck and left readable this morning and not rubbed out. The money sheet that day received nothing, in any hand, and gave nothing back, and a word changed on one page is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. Four o'clock took a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard and ten o'clock put a hundred and sixty-eight pence into it, one mark and eightpence each way, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, at the close of the day, being six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. Of that sheet, what has gone out of this yard is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; what has ever come into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and what is not on it at all is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. It has not been moved this week and it is not going to be moved by a word anybody says on a set of boards. The tin in that shed has been shut since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and a man changing his own position in the open costs nothing and is charged for by nobody, and is not to be thanked either.**

**The nine durations this harbor is carrying this week are these and they have not been looked for. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and fourth day. Those two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-four days. A bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and six. The bench under that pump, the four hundred and tenth night with nothing under it. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-third day. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-eight days. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifteenth. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninety-eighth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and forty-six days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That mole head board carries five papers, and two of the five have never been opened. The offer of four lines is still behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing written under its fourth line, and the answer to it is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In the boathouse the basket stands with the paper in it and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning. A man of about twenty-nine has a hundred and four days on the side of his head that hears out of the left, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven on that Friday a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards stood up in about nine people and said a word she had not been asked for, and nobody had put the question to her and nobody had threatened her with anything and nobody was going to ask her a second time.

"**Restricted, and I am saying it on a Friday and nobody asked me, and I want that said before anybody in this market starts anything.** On Wednesday we said opened and we meant it, and nothing in this harbor has changed since Wednesday. What has changed is that there is a piece of paper in this harbor that wants a word standing against every day, and a word against every day is a word somebody has to be able to hold up the morning after, and a community that has to hold a word up every morning does not get to change its mind on a Tuesday without somebody in that market calling it a lie. **I am not saying that paper is a lie. I am saying I do not want to live in a place where my own word is something I have to carry out in front of people every morning of the month. That is the whole of why it is restricted.**"

That is about a hundred and sixty-six words and nobody asked her for one of them, and about four people on those boards heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody thanked her and nobody blamed her and nobody said her name out loud after she had sat down.

---

The man who keeps the leaf did it at about half past eleven with a pencil and with nine people watching, and he did not consult anybody and he did not read anything out of a book to find out what to do.

He drew one stroke through the figure one where it stood in the opened column against Wednesday the sixteenth, and he left the figure standing there under the stroke with the word opened written under it, and he took nothing off that day. Wednesday the sixteenth is still on that sheet and the hour of eleven is still against it and no sixteenth day has been lost and no second line has been opened. Then he wrote the figure one in the restricted column against Friday the eighteenth with the hour of eleven against it, and under that figure he wrote the word restricted, and under the word restricted he wrote the word opened again with one stroke through it.

The two figures are not on one line and they are not in one column, and the old one is readable and the new one is readable, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on each of them in turn and see that they do not agree with each other, and neither of them has been rubbed out and neither of them has been blotted, and neither of the two days has moved.

---

A man of about thirty-two stood at the back of those boards for about nine minutes with his hands in his coat and said nothing at all, and he did not write anything down, and he did not go into the water that day and he did not ask anybody one question after it, and at about one o'clock he was at the bench stone in that yard doing ordinary work with a wedge and his hands were not as good on it as they were in the autumn and nobody in that yard said so and he did not say so.

At about half past three a man of about thirty-three who hires hands came up those boards and asked one question and got one answer and it was eighty-one words long and it came from the man who keeps the leaf.

"**That is one position and it is in a different column than it was in on Wednesday, and this harbor is one position and not one person, and there is a stroke through Wednesday and there is Wednesday.** I have not asked her why and I am not going to, and if you ask her why I will say that you were the second person to do it today, and I would rather say that to you than not say it."

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and forty-five words, and about nine people were in that shed and one of them had been standing on the boards at eleven with a pencil in his hand and had not said a word.

"**The sheet of money is as far off as it was this morning, and the distance between its two sides is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** It has not shifted by a penny, and nobody's hand went near it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and no charge went on that cistern and the covers were on it from eight until four.

**A word came off a page this morning that I did not ask for and I have left both of the words where they were.** Wednesday the sixteenth is still on that sheet with the hour of eleven against it and the figure one in the opened column has a stroke through it and the word opened is written under that figure, and the figure one in the restricted column is new this morning and stands against Friday the eighteenth with the hour of eleven on it, and the word restricted is written under that new figure with the word opened written again under it and one stroke through that. **Nothing is rubbed out. A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on both figures and they do not agree and that is the honest state of them.**

And here is why the old one is still on the page. **A page where somebody has changed their mind and the earlier thing has been taken away cleanly is a page nobody can check, and a page nobody can check is worth exactly what a word with a seal on it is worth.** In eleven weeks the only thing this harbor has had that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can do something with is the things that stay on the page after they have stopped being true. That is not a rule. It is the reason I did not use an eraser and it is the reason I did not start a new line.

Nobody asked her why and nobody blamed her and nobody thanked her, and I have not written in this slip why she did it, and I heard it and it is on a page at the end of that stall and not in here. **At about six this evening I entered one line on the first column and five on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud, and that is the sixth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth.** My third column is empty and my fourth is unmoved at one hundred and twenty-five, and no hundred and twenty-sixth line has ever been entered in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a word said out loud twice in a week, and by about six nobody was left in that shed and its door stood open on the slip with no name against it, and the bench stone outside it was bare and had a scratch on it about the size of a hand.

**A COMMUNITY SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING ON THAT FRIDAY, ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, UNASKED AND UNTHREATENED, THAT IT WAS IN A DIFFERENT POSITION, BEING RESTRICTED, AND THE POSITION WAS TAKEN BACK OFF THE DAY IT WAS WRITTEN BY THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF IN HIS OWN HAND, AND THE FIGURE IN THE OPENED COLUMN AGAINST WEDNESDAY THE SIXTEENTH WAS STRUCK AND LEFT READABLE UNDER THE STROKE, AND THE FIGURE ONE WAS WRITTEN IN THE RESTRICTED COLUMN AGAINST FRIDAY THE EIGHTEENTH WITH THE HOUR OF ELEVEN AGAINST IT, AND NEITHER DAY MOVED AND NOTHING WAS RUBBED OUT AND NOBODY WAS ASKED WHY AND NOBODY WAS BLAMED AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND WHAT IT IS SHORT BY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, THAT LAST BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past eleven in the morning the reason I struck the figure instead of taking it off the page is that I have got a page of days on that sheet with a figure standing against some of them and nothing standing against others, and that if I lift a figure off a day and leave the day there, then inside a month a stranger with a leaf and a pencil will come to a day with nothing against it and will not be able to tell whether that day had nothing on it or whether somebody took something off it, and that those are two quite different things and that only one of them is a gap this harbor is entitled to; and that at about eleven o'clock the reason I did not read the two words out one after the other with a date between them is that reading them out makes a sequence, and a sequence of a thing being said and then unsaying is a story, and that this is not a story and that the two of them happened on two different days in front of two different nine people and that is all; and that at about half past three the reason I told a man of about thirty-three that I would say that he was the second person to ask today is that I have watched four people in this harbor be careful on a Monday and careless by Wednesday, and that the way to stop that is not to be angry with anybody and it is to say the sentence out loud, and that saying a sentence out loud costs nothing and has never once in eleven weeks not worked; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason a man of about thirty-two said nothing on those boards for nine minutes and I did not go over and stand next to him is that he is not a person who is helped by being stood next to, and that in six weeks I have watched two men in this yard get worse at a thing by being given a second pair of hands near them, and that the one thing I can do today for him is not do the second thing, and that not doing a thing is a decision and I have made it; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says how many figures are standing on that page and does not say what they add to is that a slip at half past five is said out loud to nine people and that a sum said out loud in nine people on a Friday evening is a thing nine people carry home, and that I have decided this month that this page is not a thing nine people carry home, and that the person who will want to add it up is a stranger with a leaf and a pencil and that person can have it on a Tuesday when the figures stop moving.*
