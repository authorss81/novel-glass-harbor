# Chapter 608

*Sunday the Twentieth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Ninety-Eighth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Up Again at About Ten to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole and About Fifteen Minutes Both Ways Against Fourteen on the Thirteenth, Nobody Having Said Why, the Four Heads on the End Wall of the Room Over the Floor of That Market Standing Where They Have Stood Since the Autumn and No Fifth Going on It on Account of a Paper With a Seal on It, a Woman of About Thirty-One of the First Lay Saying Out Loud in About seventy Words that a Paper Which Names What It Did Not Do Is Not a Public Correction and Is Not Going on That Wall, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the twentieth day of March, with that market trading all morning and the room over the floor of it full by a quarter to eleven, and a yard below with a bench stone in it and about nine people in that yard at half past five in the afternoon.

**The ninety-eighth wipe, that wall down bare for that many times now, no cistern charge with the covers on it from nine until four, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in that Sunday. The wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten and went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole, and that is about fifteen minutes both ways, which is a minute longer than the thirteenth and two longer than the sixth. The four heads on the end wall of the room over the floor of that market are the four heads and there is no fifth head on that wall and no space cut for one and no nail above the fourth of them, and a paper with a seal on it is not going to put one there. On that Sunday the money sheet was not touched in any hand, and not a figure of it moved, and four heads on a wall are not a line on that sheet. At about four in the afternoon a hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, goes out of that yard, and at about ten in the morning the same figure comes in, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, at the close of the day, being six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. That sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and a wall coming down does not move that by a penny. The tin in that shed is still the tin of the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of two Sundays has come out of that bag, and the reason for that is set down in a slip at half past five as a sentence and not as a figure.**

**Here are the nine, and they are the ninth Sunday this harbor has not improved one thing on. The bellwright of that harbor, not asked for, three hundred and eight days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and six. The two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-six. The bench under that pump, the four hundred and twelfth night, and that is the one of the nine that a stranger can see from a road. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-fifth day, and there is a second sheet beside it that is not that sheet. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundredth day. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and seventeenth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and forty-eight days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of the mole carried five papers this morning and two of them have never been opened, and the demand for the returns is in a basket in that boathouse with the paper still in it and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is a hundred and six days today, and that is not a gift and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

And at about half past ten on that Sunday the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the ninety-eighth time, and about five people in that harbor were near enough to see it happen, and the two men who do it said nothing to each other and nothing to anybody else.

It went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole, and that is about fifteen minutes both ways. That is a minute longer than last Sunday and two minutes longer than the Sunday before that and eight longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and nobody in that harbor has said why it is getting slower and this yard has not asked and is not going to.

---

The four heads are on the end wall of that room at the height of a man's shoulder and under each of them there is a line in a small hand saying what that head is, and they are a claim, a refusal, a restricted record and a public correction, in that order, and they have been there since the autumn.

A woman of about thirty-one of the first lay came up those stairs at about half past ten with her own particular still in her own pocket and said one thing out loud in about 70 words to about nine people, and she was not asked to say it and she said it because a piece of paper came into this harbor on Sunday week and somebody had already begun talking about putting something on that wall for it.

"**A paper that says what it did not do is not a public correction, and that is the whole of what I have come up here to say.** A correction is for something somebody has said out loud in a working place and got wrong, and I have read that sheet twice and nobody in this harbor has said a word of it out loud anywhere except the eight lines on it, and you cannot correct a thing that has not been said. **And it is the fourth of those four heads, and the fourth has been the one nobody has ever used, and there is no fifth head on this wall and no space cut for one and no nail above the fourth of them, and a paper with a clean seal on it is not going to be the thing that changes that.**"

Nobody argued with her and nobody agreed with her out loud and about four of those nine had not come up those stairs to hear about a wall and went back down them, and nobody was thanked and nobody was blamed.

---

And at about eleven on that Sunday a man of about thirty-six from a boat that had come in on Saturday stood at the head of those boards with the sheet under the stone in his two hands and read all eight lines of it at the head of it, once, and then stood there with it, and said one thing out loud in about 88 words to about nine people.

"**This is the first paper anybody has put in front of me in two years that I cannot argue with, and I have argued with a great many things in two years.** I am not saying it is wrong. I am saying that there is nobody in it. You cannot say to a sheet of days that your week was a hard week, and you cannot get a sheet of days to move a figure, and there is nobody at the foot of it I can be angry at except a seal and a name, and that is the cleanest thing I have seen since I came into this trade and it is the coldest thing I have seen since I came into this trade. **I am going to be angry at it for a long time and I would rather be angry at it than at a man.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody asked him what his week had been like, and about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told, and he put the sheet back under the stone himself and nobody asked him to.

---

At half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 460 words, and nobody in that shed was asked why a wall is getting slower.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** That is where it has stood since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month, and the bag holds twenty-eight pence, and no charge went on that cistern and the covers were on it from nine until four.

**A wall came down bare this morning at about half past ten for the ninety-eighth time and went up again at about ten to eleven and did not go up whole, and it took about fifteen minutes both ways.** That is the fifth figure I have for that wall and the three before it were taken by men with watches and the fourth by me, and I am not taking another one, and the fifth is a minute longer than the fourth and nobody in this harbor has said why and I have not asked and I am not going to ask.

The four heads on the end wall of that room are the four heads. **A woman of about thirty-one of the first lay came up those stairs this morning and said in about 70 words that a paper which says what it did not do is not a public correction, and she was not asked to and she had a reason of her own and that reason is hers.** I have not written that reason on anything. There is no fifth head on that wall and there is no space cut for one.

A man of about thirty-six read the eight lines at the head of that sheet once this morning and said in about 88 words that it is the first paper in two years he cannot argue with, and that you cannot say to a sheet of days that your week was a hard week, and that he is going to be angry at it for a long time and would rather be angry at it than at a man. **Nobody answered him and nobody asked him about his week, and I have not asked him anything, and I am not going to.**

At about six this evening I entered one line on the first column and five on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud, and that is the eighth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth. The third of my four is empty. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line, and the reason for that is not on a page of mine."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four heads standing on a wall, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it. and the wall behind that reeve's bench stood at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and not whole.

**THE WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY, BEING THE NINETY-EIGHTH TIME, AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK ABOUT FIFTEEN MINUTES BOTH WAYS AGAINST FOURTEEN ON THE THIRTEENTH AND THIRTEEN ON THE SIXTH, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY IT IS GETTING SLOWER. THE FOUR HEADS ON THE END WALL OF THAT ROOM ARE THE FOUR HEADS, BEING A CLAIM, A REFUSAL, A RESTRICTED RECORD AND A PUBLIC CORRECTION, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THE FIRST LAY SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 70 WORDS THAT A PAPER WHICH NAMES WHAT IT DID NOT DO IS NOT A PUBLIC CORRECTION, AND THERE IS NO FIFTH HEAD ON THAT WALL AND NO SPACE CUT FOR ONE AND NO NAIL ABOVE THE FOURTH OF THEM. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twentieth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the ninety-eighth time that wall has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the covers were on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that that market traded all morning on that Sunday and that the room over the floor of it had about nine people in it by a quarter to eleven; and that the four heads are on the end wall of that room at the height of a man's shoulder, and that under each of them is a line in a small hand saying what that head is, and that they are a claim, a refusal, a restricted record and a public correction in that order, and that they have been there since the autumn, and that there is no fifth head on that wall and no space cut for one and no nail above the fourth of them; and that at about half past ten the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the ninety-eighth time with about five people in that harbor near enough to see it happen, and that the two men who do it said nothing to each other and nothing to anybody else, and that it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and that it took about fifteen minutes both ways, and that that is a minute longer than the thirteenth and two longer than the sixth and eight longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and that nobody in that harbor has said why and that this yard has not asked and is not going to; and that a woman of about thirty-one of the first lay came up those stairs at about half past ten with her own particular still in her own pocket and said one thing out loud in about 70 words to about nine people, and that she was not asked to say it and that she said it because a piece of paper came into that harbor on Sunday week and somebody had already begun talking about putting something on that wall for it, and that what she said is that a paper that says what it did not do is not a public correction, and that a correction is for something somebody has said out loud in a working place and got wrong, and that she has read that sheet twice and nobody in that harbor has said a word of it out loud anywhere except the eight lines on it, and that a thing which has not been said cannot be corrected, and that it is the fourth of those four heads and that the fourth has been the one nobody has ever used, and that a paper with a clean seal on it is not going to be the thing that changes that; and that nobody argued with her and nobody agreed with her out loud, and that about four of those nine had not come up those stairs to hear about a wall and went back down them, and that nobody was thanked and nobody was blamed; and that at about eleven a man of about thirty-six from a boat that had come in on Saturday stood at the head of those boards with the sheet under the stone in his two hands and read all eight lines at the head of it once and then said one thing out loud in about 88 words, and that what he said is that it is the first paper anybody has put in front of him in two years that he cannot argue with, and that he has argued with a great many things in two years, and that he is not saying it is wrong, and that there is nobody in it, and that there is no way to say to a sheet of days that a week was a hard week and no way to get a sheet of days to move a figure, and that there is nobody at the foot of it he can be angry at except a seal and a name, and that that is the cleanest thing he has seen since he came into the trade and the coldest, and that he is going to be angry at it for a long time and would rather be angry at it than at a man; and that nobody answered him and nobody asked him what his week had been like, and that about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told, and that he put the sheet back under the stone himself and nobody asked him to; and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was in the bag at the close of the said day, and that the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and the bag is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind that wage; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on the said day and nothing came off it, and that four heads on a wall are not a line on that sheet, and that that sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that a wall coming down does not move that by a penny; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and forty-one and the second at one thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the eighth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I have written that the two men who take that wall down said nothing to each other is that in six weeks two of them have been asked by three separate people in this market what it is getting slower, and that they have each answered a different one of those three people with a different sentence, and that I know that because I have been the third person they said it to and I have not written down what they said to me and I am not going to; and that at about ten to eleven the reason I have stopped taking the figure of that wall is that the fifth time anybody counted it the person who counted it was the only one in that harbor who cared what the answer was, and that a number a man has taken about a thing he cares about is not a number anybody can use, and that I would rather have four figures taken by four people who do not care than five taken by me; and that at about half past ten the reason a woman of about thirty-one said a thing on those stairs that she was not asked to say is that somebody in that market had been talking for a fortnight about putting something on that wall for the paper under the stone, and that the fourth of those four heads is a public correction and a paper nobody has said out loud is not a thing a correction can stand behind, and that she said it before anybody had built the argument and that is the only part of it I have written down here; and that at about eleven o'clock the reason I have written down that a man of about thirty-six would rather be angry at a sheet than at a man is that it is the first thing anybody in this harbor has said about that paper that was not a defence of it or an attack on it, and that both of those have been said in this yard six times this week and both of those are ways of not looking at it; and that at about six o'clock the reason there is nothing in this slip about a ninth day of anything is that a berth fund pays a day for a day and does not care what a sea is under martial custody or what a sheet of days says, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out on that Sunday as it does on a Tuesday, and that that is the only line of this slip that has been the same every day of this week and I have let it be the same on purpose.*
