# Chapter 638

*Tuesday the Nineteenth Day of April, the Seventy-Eighth Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Seventy-Eighth Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-Eighth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, a Boy of Twenty-Two on the Stool at the End of That Stall With a Board Across His Knees and Off the Water for the Sixth Time in Thirteen Weeks and Nobody Having Said a Word to Him About the Water, a Crown Paper in a Bagged Canvas Slip on the Table in That Shed at About Two in the Afternoon With a Seal at the Foot of It and a Name Under That Seal Which Is on the Paper and Is in No Mouth, an Offer of a Forced-Witness Line Through the Private Access Signal on the Morrow Line Which Would Make Him the Central Witness and Would Expose a Route Signal and Not the Original Scene, Refused Out Loud at About Half Past Three in About Nine People in About Three Hundred and Ninety-Seven Words, and Not Thanked and Not Given a Word For It and Not Put on Any Wall, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-One and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Tuesday the nineteenth day of April, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning and a yard below it with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The seventy-eighth wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. The lid of that step carried twenty-six lines and the last of the twenty-six came to one hundred and four marks, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay who has read that lid every Tuesday since the twenty-fifth of January got to the end of it in four minutes this morning, and the figure did not shift, and the twenty envelopes behind that door stayed shut from ten until four. The seventy-eighth week stands fallen due at the close of today. At about one this afternoon a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall with a board across his knees, writing weights into a book in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and he is not on the water, and that is the sixth time in thirteen weeks, and it is not a punishment and nobody in that market has said one word to him about it and nobody put a name in the third column of anybody's leaf. At about two this afternoon a paper in a bagged canvas slip was put on the table in that shed by a man who works on the west basin and is not a Crown officer and is not a messenger, and it was not nailed to the board at the head of that mole and it was not put on that board and it was not read out, and it was offered out loud to one man in about nine people and refused out loud by the same man in about nine people. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a Crown paper on a table is not a line on that sheet, and a refusal is not a line on that sheet. A hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the end of the day, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet of money carries one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the sum it does not carry is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Not one tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing this afternoon came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the first of them is the oldest thing this harbor carries and the last is a man who has said nothing for a very long time. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-six days. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-eight days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-six days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-eight. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-second night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and fifteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirtieth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-seventh day. There are five papers on that board and two of them have never been opened by anybody here. The four lines have not come out of that upper room over that market, nothing at all has been written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. That basket in that boathouse has not been looked inside by anybody, and the paper is in it and nothing else is. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon, there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody here has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-six days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about two in the afternoon a paper in a bagged canvas slip was put on the table in that shed and the bag was taken off it and left folded on the corner, and about nine people stood round that table.

It is a document and not a man. It carries a seal at the foot of it and a name under that seal, and that name is on the paper and is in no mouth, and nobody in that shed read it aloud and nobody will. It says on its own face what it does and it says on its own face what it does not establish, and it is the second document in thirteen weeks in this harbor that has done the second of those things.

It offers a forced-witness line run through the private access signal on the Morrow Line, and it says in its own words that a person who accepts it becomes the central witness, and it says in its own words that what would be exposed by that line is a route signal and not the original scene, and it says in its own words that it does not establish what the signal was made from, and it says in its own words that nothing in it is testimony and nothing in it is evidence and nothing in it may be asked a question.

---

At about half past three that afternoon the paper was put in front of the man it was offered to, in about nine people, and he read it himself and nobody read it to him, and then he said one thing out loud and it was about three hundred and ninety-seven words and nobody answered him.

"**No.**

**And I want nine people to have the whole of it in one mouth, because that paper is on that table and it is going to be on that table after today and I am not going to leave my half of it in a gap. This is the part where I refuse it, and the first thing is that it would make me the middle of it. Every record in this harbor for two winters has been kept on the understanding that no one man is the middle of it, and a paper on a table that offers to put me in the middle of it is a paper that is offering to end the whole thing this yard spent the winter learning, and it is offering it to me and not to anybody else, which is a thing I am not going to let it do.

The second thing is that the second line on that paper says in its own words that what comes out of that signal is a route signal and not the scene. **So it does not get me my brother. It gets me a fact about a route, and it gets it by making me the only place in this coast that fact can be spoken from, and the only place a thing can be spoken from is the only place a thing can be argued with.**

And the third thing is that a thing made of paper cannot be asked anything and cannot answer anything, and that is not a criticism of that paper, it is a fact about every paper ever made, and if a person puts himself inside one he has stopped being a person who can be asked and has become a line in a column.**

**I will not do it. Not this week, not on that day, and not in a year, and if I ever change my mind I will say so out loud in nine people at that stone and not on a paper.**

Nobody in that shed said anything about that at the time, and nobody thanked him for it, and nobody in this harbor has called him anything for it, and no word has been put against it on any page in this harbor, and it has not been written on a wall and it is not going to be."

---

Nobody went near the stall of a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market at about four this afternoon, and about nine people stood at the flat of that bench stone, and of those nine about four could read what was on the wall above it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 638 words, and nobody in that shed was asked to say the name under that seal out loud.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks off that lid at ten this morning in four minutes for the seventy-eighth time and it did not move, and twenty envelopes behind that door stood shut from ten until four, and the seventy-seventh week has been fallen due since Friday and the seventy-eighth week joins it at the close of today.**

**A paper was put on that table at about two this afternoon and it was offered out loud to one man in about nine people and refused out loud by the same man in about nine people, and I am not going to say his name in this slip because his name is not on the paper either.** That paper carries a seal at the foot of it and a name under that seal, and that name is on the paper and in no mouth, and it was not read and it is not going to be read by anybody in this yard, and a name under a seal is not read out by a man with a slate at half past five any more than it is read out by anybody else.

That paper says on its own face that what it would expose is a route signal and not a scene. **And it says on its own face that nothing in it is testimony and nothing in it is evidence and nothing in it may be asked a question, and a man who puts himself inside a thing like that has stopped being somebody who can be asked and become a line in a column, and this yard has spent thirteen weeks refusing to make anybody a line in a column.**

Nobody has thanked him for that. Not one person in this harbor thanked him for that and not one is going to, and nobody is going to give him a word for it, and it is not on a wall, and the twenty-six lines on that lid this morning are still twenty-six lines.

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is at the head of that money sheet and has not moved off that mark since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. What is lying under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight was paid out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. Tomorrow afternoon the eighth of those eight days goes onto that sheet and it goes on once and it carries its own days and its own multiplications, and it is the first line that has gone onto that sheet in this month or in fifty days.

The fourteen standing at the end of that stall were not read out of today, not one figure on that sheet went up or down by one, and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. The count of positions was not taken again, and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and no day in this month has a figure against it.

That first column took a single entry at about six this evening and that second one took five, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and seventy-one stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and twenty-nine against the second, my third is empty with a boy's hand at the head of it and his surname on no page and he off the water, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and takes no hundred and twenty-sixth line."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for three hundred and ninety-seven words at a table. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the paper was still folded in its slip on the corner of that table, and the boy of twenty-two was still on the stool at the end of that stall with his board across his knees and nobody had said a word to him about the water.

**TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS STOOD ON THE LID OF THAT STEP AT ABOUT TEN THIS MORNING AND DID NOT MOVE FOR THE SEVENTY-EIGHTH TIME, AND TWENTY ENVELOPES STOOD SHUT BEHIND THAT DOOR FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-EIGHTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF TODAY. A CROWN PAPER IN A BAGGED CANVAS SLIP WAS PUT ON THE TABLE IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND OFFERED OUT LOUD TO ONE MAN IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND REFUSED OUT LOUD BY THE SAME MAN IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND IT WAS NOT READ OUT AND IT WAS NOT NAILED TO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE. THAT PAPER CARRIES A SEAL AT THE FOOT OF IT AND A NAME UNDER THAT SEAL WHICH IS ON THE PAPER AND IS IN NO MOUTH, AND IT SAYS ON ITS OWN FACE THAT WHAT IT WOULD EXPOSE IS A ROUTE SIGNAL AND NOT THE ORIGINAL SCENE, AND THAT NOTHING IN IT IS TESTIMONY AND NOTHING IN IT IS EVIDENCE AND NOTHING IN IT MAY BE ASKED A QUESTION. NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NOBODY WAS CALLED ANYTHING. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET HAS NOT MOVED BY SO MUCH AS A PENNY. WHAT STANDS ON IT AS OUTGOINGS IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. WHAT STANDS ON IT AS INGOINGS IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT THAT SLATE IS HOLDING IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about one in the afternoon a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall with a board across his knees writing weights into a book in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and that he is not on the water and that that is the sixth time in thirteen weeks, and that it is not a punishment and that nobody in that market has said one word to him about it and that nobody put a name in the third column of anybody's leaf; and that the paper is a document and not a man, that it carries a seal at the foot of it and a name under that seal which is on the paper and is in no mouth, and that nobody in that shed read it aloud, and that it says on its own face what it does and says on its own face what it does not establish, and that it is the second document in thirteen weeks in this harbor that has done the second of those things; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason I did not ask that man one question after he had finished speaking is that the whole of what this yard has done since the winter is to let a person say a thing in their own mouth in front of other people and then stop, and that a keeper of a page who follows a refusal with a question is a man who thinks the refusal needs tidying; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have not written the name under that seal into this column is that it is on a paper and in no mouth and that is going to be the condition of it for as long as this harbor holds it, and that a name that gets repeated in a ledger is a name that has started travelling; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing in this column about that paper being a Crown instrument is that it is a document and not a man, and that a document cannot be thanked and cannot be refused by anybody but the person it is offered to, and that not one man has come through a door of this harbor in two years and none came this afternoon and none is coming this month; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I have put nothing on that fitting-out sheet today is that a paper on a table is not a cost and a refusal is not a cost, and that tomorrow afternoon the eighth of those eight days goes onto it and it goes on once, and that a sheet that moved on a day when a document came would be a sheet that prices the weather of the week instead of the work of it; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason I have said nothing this week about that boy on that stool is that he is off the water for the sixth time in thirteen weeks and six is a figure about him and this yard has no column for it, and that his hand is still at the head of my third column and his name is on no page, and that a harbor which asks a boy why he is not in a boat has stopped being the thing it spent the winter building; and that at about eight in the evening the reason I have not written one word about the Morrow Line in nineteen days is that a name is still the first thing on that sheet of days and it is still in the held column, and that the signal on that line is still there this afternoon, and that a signal nobody has asked to lose is a signal nobody has any business losing, and that I have not the least idea what any of that means and I am not going to find out before tomorrow.*
