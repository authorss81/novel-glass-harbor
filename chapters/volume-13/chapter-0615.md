# Chapter 615

*Sunday the Twenty-Seventh Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Ninety-Ninth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Up Again at About Ten to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole, Sixteen Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Three Standing Bare and Sixteen to Get It Back, Being Thirty-Five Minutes the Whole of It From About a Quarter Past Ten, a Minute Longer Than the Twentieth and Two Longer Than the Thirteenth and Nobody Having Said Why, a Man of About Forty-Seven Who Makes Barrels at the Top of Fish Street Saying Out Loud in About Nine People at About Eleven That Four Words Are Not Enough and Nobody Having Asked Him, the Revision of the Counter-Plan Named for Friday the First Day of April and Not for a Sunday, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Eight and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Fourteen*

Sunday the twenty-seventh day of March, with that market trading all morning and about nine people in a yard below it at half past five in the afternoon.

**The ninety-ninth wipe, and no cistern charge with the covers lying on that butt from nine until four, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it. At about a quarter past ten on that Sunday two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten for the ninety-ninth time, and it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole. Sixteen minutes to get it off the wall, three minutes standing with it bare, and sixteen minutes to get it back, which is thirty-five minutes the whole of it, from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven, and that is a minute longer than the twentieth of this month and two longer than the thirteenth and nobody in this harbor has said why and this yard has stopped asking. At about eleven on that Sunday a man of about forty-seven who makes barrels at the top of Fish Street said out loud in about nine people that four words are not enough, and nobody asked him and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him. The money sheet was not written on and not altered and a sentence said in a market is not a line on it. That yard paid four in the afternoon at a hundred and sixty-eight pence and was paid at ten in the morning at the identical figure, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the close of the day, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence above it, being three marks eleven shillings. Six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence is what that sheet has cost this harbor, being one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence; sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence is all that has ever been put into it, being ten thousand six hundred and forty pence; and what is not there is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nobody in that yard has been on the water for two days and that is not a punishment and nobody has asked him about it.**

**Here are the nine, and the second of them is the one a stranger could put a date to from a church book. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirteen days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and fifteen. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-three. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and nineteenth night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-five days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-seven days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and seventh. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-fourth. That board at the head of that mole carried five papers this morning and two of the five have never been opened, and the demand for the returns is still in that basket in that boathouse with the paper in it and no hand in it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirteen days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about a quarter past ten on that Sunday two men began to take the wall behind that reeve's bench down off its holders, and it was bare by about half past ten for the ninety-ninth time, and about five people in that harbor were near enough to see it happen and nobody stopped them.

It went up again at about ten to eleven and it did not go up whole. Sixteen minutes to get it off, three standing bare, sixteen to get it back, thirty-five minutes from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven, and that is the sixth figure anybody has taken of that wall and the first five were taken by men with watches, and nobody has said why it is getting slower and this yard is not going to ask either man who does it.

---

At about eleven, with the market trading underneath and about nine people standing in that market, a man of about forty-seven who makes barrels at the top of Fish Street said one thing out loud and he had not been asked for it by anybody.

"**Four words are not enough, and that is not an opinion, it is arithmetic.** Opened, refused, restricted, withdrawn. That is four places for a community to stand on a day, and a community stood in one of them on the Friday and meant it, and this morning I have been turning that over on the way down here and the trouble is not that she said refused. The trouble is that refused already meant something else on that page before Friday and it means something else still, because two rows have been standing in that column since the fifteenth with nothing against them but a place. **So the word is doing two jobs. A word doing two jobs is not wrong. It is just not checkable, and there is a difference and this harbor is the one that taught it.**

I am a man who makes barrels and I am not going to be asked to write the fifth word down and I am not going to be thanked for saying there ought to be one."

That is about a hundred and seventy-six words and about four people in that market heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody thanked him and nobody said he was wrong.

---

At about twenty past eleven a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that this cannot be done on a Sunday, and he said it to about nine people and it went to about four of them and it was about a hundred and eight words.

"**Not on a Sunday, and I do not care what day anybody prefers. A page gets written by whoever turns up, and on a Sunday the people who turn up are the people who were in that shed on Friday and nobody else. Friday the first of April. Working morning, market open, the boards standing there with about nine of us in front of them, and if a fifth word is going in then it goes in in front of the people who are going to have to keep to it.**

And nobody has to agree with anything. It is going on a page, not in a minute."

Nobody argued with that and nobody took it further, and the woman of thirty of no office wrote the day on the corner of that page in her own hand and nothing else.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and twenty-nine words, and nobody in that shed was asked which day a page ought to be written on.

"**Six figures have been taken of that wall since the autumn and I have printed them all and I am not going to take a seventh.** It came down this morning at about a quarter past ten and it was bare by about half past ten for the ninety-ninth time and it went up again at about ten to eleven and it did not go up whole. Sixteen minutes to get it off, three standing bare, sixteen to get it back, thirty-five minutes from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven. It is a minute longer than the twentieth and two longer than the thirteenth and nobody has said why and I have not asked and I am not going to.

**The figure at the head of this slip is the one I am answerable for and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** It has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. There are twenty-eight pence in the bag under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and a berth fund pays a day for a day and does not care what a sea is under martial custody.

Now the thing this yard has got in front of it, and I am putting it in a slip because a slip is said out loud to nine people and that is the last place in this harbor where anything still gets argued about.

**Four words are not enough.** A man who makes barrels at the top of Fish Street said that in about nine people this morning at about eleven and nobody had asked him and he is not going to be thanked for it. The word refused is standing in a column with two rows under it that were never asked for anything and mean something else entirely, and one of those two things was said at eleven o'clock on Friday and the other two were carried forward, and a man with one book and one hand found that out on Saturday and told us in about nine people and was not thanked and was not told he was wrong.

So there is going to be a fifth word, and there is going to be four things written against it the way there are four things written against every one of the fourteen on the sheet at the end of that stall, being what is not said, who is not saying it, what they are entitled to keep, and what would have to happen for it to be opened. **That sheet is not being added to and none of the fourteen is being taken off and I have not read one line of it out loud this week and no figure on it has moved by one.**

It is going on Friday the first of April, in a working morning, in about nine people, on a page and not in a minute. Nobody has to agree with anything and nobody is going to be thanked for it.

At about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second and I did not read that leaf out loud. My first of those figures is three hundred and forty-eight and my second is one thousand six hundred and fourteen, my third is empty, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four words being not enough, and nobody was in that shed after about six and the door stood open on a slip that nobody had signed and nobody stood at. The wall behind that reeve's bench stood at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and not whole.

**THE WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY, BEING THE NINETY-NINTH TIME, AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK SIXTEEN MINUTES TO GET OFF THE WALL, THREE STANDING BARE, AND SIXTEEN TO GET BACK, BEING THIRTY-FIVE MINUTES FROM ABOUT A QUARTER PAST TEN TO ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN, AGAINST FIFTEEN BOTH WAYS ON THE TWENTIETH AND FOURTEEN ON THE THIRTEENTH, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SEVEN WHO MAKES BARRELS SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 176 WORDS IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE THAT FOUR WORDS ARE NOT ENOUGH, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE NAMED FRIDAY THE FIRST DAY OF APRIL FOR THE REVISION AND NOT A SUNDAY, AND NOBODY WAS THANKED FOR EITHER. NOT ONE OF THE FIGURES BELOW MOVED ON THAT SUNDAY AND NONE OF THEM IS ON ANYTHING BUT A SHEET. THE OUT SIDE OF IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; THE IN SIDE IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND THE GAP BETWEEN THE TWO SIDES IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, LAY UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the ninety-ninth time that wall has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that nothing was placed on that cistern on it and the covers were lying on it at nine in the morning and at four in the afternoon; and that at about a quarter past ten two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten with about five people near enough to see it and nobody stopping them, and that it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and that it took sixteen minutes to get it off the wall, three minutes standing with it bare and sixteen minutes to get it back, which is thirty-five minutes the whole of it from about a quarter past ten to about ten to eleven, and that that is a minute longer than the twentieth and two longer than the thirteenth and that nobody in this harbor has said why and that this yard has stopped asking either man who does it; and that at about eleven, with that market trading underneath, a man of about forty-seven who makes barrels at the top of Fish Street said one thing out loud in about nine people that nobody had asked him for, being that four words are not enough, and that what he said is that opened, refused, restricted and withdrawn are four places for a community to stand on a day, and that a community stood in one of them on the Friday and meant it, and that the trouble is not that she said refused but that refused already meant something else on that page before Friday and means something else still, because two rows have been standing in that column since the fifteenth with nothing against them but a place, and that a word doing two jobs is not wrong and is not checkable, and that there is a difference and this harbor is the one that taught it, and that he is a man who makes barrels and is not going to be asked to write the fifth word down and is not going to be thanked for saying there ought to be one, and that it was about a hundred and seventy-six words and about four people heard it and the rest of that market was not told and nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody thanked him and nobody said he was wrong; and that at about twenty past eleven a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud in about nine people that this cannot be done on a Sunday and that a page gets written by whoever turns up, and that Friday the first day of April was named as the day, being a working morning with the market open and the boards standing there with about nine people in front of them, and that nobody has to agree with anything and that it is going on a page and not in a minute, and that the woman of thirty of no office wrote that day on the corner of that page in her own hand and nothing else; and that the fourteen on that sheet stand exactly where they stood in this harbor eleven weeks ago, that no figure on it moved by one and none of them was added to and none taken off; and that the sheet under the stone was not opened and the stone was not moved and that the page of that harbor's own answers was still on its nail; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about six hundred and twenty-nine words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and forty-eight and the second is one thousand six hundred and fourteen, and that the third of those four columns took nothing, and that the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it anywhere, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month is the fifteenth.*

*Second column, entered in that yard's own mouth on the said Sunday at about six in the evening, not checkable from a mole, and with no name against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I have not written one word in this column about a man who makes barrels saying that four words are not enough is that he said it better than I would have said it and in about a hundred and seventy-six words instead of the six hundred this slip is, and that there is a kind of man in this harbor who can say the whole of a thing in a morning and there is a kind who cannot, and that both of them are needed and that I have been one of the second kind every day for eleven weeks; and that at about twenty past eleven the reason the day was put on the corner of that page in a woman's hand and not in a minute is that a minute is a thing that goes into a book that somebody else keeps, and that a corner of a page with a date on it is a thing that stays in the hands of the people who can climb up and read it, and that this harbor has been wrong about minutes twice in two winters and both times by putting something in one that belonged on a page; and that at about half past ten the reason I have printed those thirty-five minutes out again this month is that a wipe is the only thing on a Sunday that this harbor has measured six times, and that in eleven weeks nobody has asked to have the seventh taken and nobody has asked for the sixth either, and that a figure nobody wants is the only kind of figure in this yard that I would swear to twice; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written down that two days have gone by with nobody in that yard on the water is that a man of about thirty-two is not a man who goes in the water when a thing has gone wrong in his own hearing, and that I have watched two men in this yard stop for a week after a week like this one and neither of them lost anything by it and one of them came back better and one of them came back exactly the same, and that I do not know yet which one that will be and I am not going to guess in a slip; and that at about six o'clock the reason I put that notice of the seventeenth of May past at the head of those nine this evening is that it is the oldest one of the nine and that on a Sunday morning the oldest thing in a list ought to be read first, and that there is no reason and I have put it there because I felt like it and this column is the only place in this harbor where a man is allowed to be wrong in private.*
