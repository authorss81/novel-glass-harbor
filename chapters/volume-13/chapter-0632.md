# Chapter 632

*Wednesday the Thirteenth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, Low Water at Nethermorrow at About Ten Past Six and Slack Until About Half Past Seven, a Slate Written in the Diver's Own Words With the Hour at the Foot of It Before Anybody Went Down, an Air Limit of Twenty-Five Minutes Written on That Slate, a Tender of About Twenty-Six in the Tender Boat and Not in the Water and an Independent Line Run Off That Boat's Own Winch and a Cutoff She Could Pull Without Asking, One Man Down at About Ten Past Six and Up at Half Past Six, Being Twenty Minutes of the Twenty-Five, in About Three and a Half Fathoms, and a Man of About Forty-Four Asking at Six and Twenty-Eight Minutes to Come Up and Nobody Arguing With Him, One Cylinder Off the Rack and Forty Yards of Line Gone and the Coal in the Pump, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Five and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Wednesday the thirteenth day of April, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five.

**A Wednesday with nothing standing against it: that butt covered from eight until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. Low water at Nethermorrow came at about ten past six this morning and the water there was slack until about half past seven, and nobody went into the water on the top of that flood or on any other part of it. About twenty to five a slate was written on in a boat shed at the end of the west quay in the words of the man who was going down on it, with the hour at the foot of it, and it stood on the thwart in that boat from then until about half past seven. It carries the air limit of twenty-five minutes and it carries a reason, and it names a man of about twenty-four as the tender, and the tender was in her own boat on the surface the whole time and not one foot of her was in the water. A second line was cut and made up off that boat's own winch and was not on the working boat's fall at all, and a cutoff was bent into the ring of it that could be pulled without asking anybody. One man went down at about ten past six in about three and a half fathoms and was up at about half past six, which is twenty minutes of the twenty-five on that slate, and at about six and twenty-eight minutes he asked to come up and nobody argued with him about it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a slate is not a line on that sheet, and a dive is not a line on that sheet until a yard settles the day it was done on. That yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and handed the same sum back out of it at about four in the afternoon, and what stayed on that slate under the yard's own slate was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. A wage of four marks comes to six hundred and forty pence and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of it, being three marks eleven shillings. One hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, is what has gone out of this harbor on that sheet; ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, is everything that has ever gone into it; and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, lies between those two. Not one tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the fourth of them is a thing on a stone with nothing under it. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-sixth night. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-two. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-four days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-fourth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-first day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-two days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened. Those four lines are still behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with the paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, they were nine on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about twenty to five in the morning a slate was written on in that boat shed with a stick of whitewash and it was read out loud twice before it went out to the west quay, and about four people heard it read and the rest of that quay was asleep.

It said, in the words of the man who was going to hold it down on the way, that there was a man of about forty-four of Nethermorrow in the water under the wheelhouse of a boat of about nine tons lying broadside on that bar, in about three and a half fathoms, and that he was going to put a line on that man and bring him up.

It said that the air was twenty-five minutes and no more, and that nobody goes in twice in a day, and that nobody goes in at the top of a flood, and that anybody in that boat who wanted to come up was to say so and would not be argued with. It gave the hour at the foot of it, being twenty to five on Wednesday the thirteenth, and it gave the name of the man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm who was going to be in the tender on the surface, and it gave the name of the man of about thirty-two who was going down.

Nobody in that shed asked him to write it differently and nobody said a word about how it read.

---

A woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm stood in that tender from half past four and did not put one foot over the side of her, and a second line came off her own winch and was cut clear of the working boat's fall, and she bent a cutoff into the ring of it with her own hands at about ten to five and gave the loose end of the wire to nobody and kept it herself.

The air came up off a rack in that shed at about ten past six. It was one cylinder off the rack and forty yards of line gone and the coal in the pump, and Calder Marr was in the water at about ten past six and on the bottom in about three and a half fathoms at about a quarter past six, in the dark, with the sea going over the wheelhouse of that boat in a way he could hear and not see.

At about six and twenty-eight minutes the man under that wheelhouse said he wanted to come up, and the woman in the tender did not ask him why and did not ask the man below whether there was anything else worth having, and he was on the steps of that tender at about half past six, which is twenty minutes and not twenty-six.

---

The wind had gone into the north-west in the night and by about ten in the morning the rain had set in and stayed set in, and about nine people were on those boards, and about eleven of about forty people in that market could not read a line of what was on the wall above them and had not been told what was on it.

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing, and nobody in this harbor has put a state on any of the nine names on that sheet of days, and the three columns of that sheet were not counted today.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 543 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what twenty-five minutes is for.

"**A man of about forty-four of Nethermorrow came up the steps of a tender boat at about half past six this morning, and he was in the water twenty minutes and the slate on the thwart said twenty-five, and he asked to come up at six and twenty-eight minutes and nobody argued with him about it, and that is the whole of the best hour this harbor has had in thirteen weeks.**

**The air was twenty-five minutes and the tender was on the surface the whole time and the line she used was her own and not the working boat's, and she had a cutoff bent into that line at ten to five in the morning with her own hands that she could pull without asking one single person.** I did not go into the water and I was not asked to, and I was the man who wrote the reason on that slate at twenty to five because the reason has to be in the words of the man who goes down and not in the words of the man who stays up.

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the figure at the head of this slip and it has not shifted off that mark in eleven weeks. Twenty-eight pence is lying under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**There is a boat of about nine tons lying on a bar four hours' sail out with two people still in her, and nobody has been in the water twice today and nobody is going to be.** One cylinder off that rack this morning, forty yards of line gone and gone, the coal in that pump, three on that tender with victual at about seven, and a day asked for by name at the figure in chalk, and not one of those five things is on that money sheet yet because a yard settles a day when it settles it and this yard has not settled one day of it.

Out of the sheet of the fourteen at the end of that stall nothing was read today, not one figure on that sheet went up or down by one, and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. The count of positions was not taken again and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and carries no figure against any day.

That leaf took one entry on the first column at about six this evening and five on the second, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and sixty-five stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and ninety-nine against the second, my third is empty, a boy's hand being at the head of it, his surname being on no page and he not being on the water, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for twenty minutes in three and a half fathoms. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the wet slate from that boat shed was still lying on the thwart of that tender with the figures on it, and the man who had written it was not in that yard at six.

**A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR OF NETHERMORROW CAME UP THE STEPS OF A TENDER BOAT AT ABOUT HALF PAST SIX ON THE THIRTEENTH OF APRIL, HAVING BEEN IN THE WATER TWENTY MINUTES AGAINST A LIMIT OF TWENTY-FIVE WRITTEN ON A SLATE AT TWENTY TO FIVE, AND HE ASKED TO COME UP AT SIX AND TWENTY-EIGHT MINUTES AND NOBODY ARGUIED WITH HIM. THE TENDER STOOD ON THE SURFACE THE WHOLE TIME AND THE LINE SHE USED WAS HER OWN AND NOT THE WORKING BOAT'S FALL, AND THE CUTOFF IN THE RING OF IT WAS BENT BY HAND BEFORE ANYBODY WENT DOWN. THE REASON WAS IN THE WORDS OF THE MAN WHO WENT DOWN AND THE HOUR WAS AT THE FOOT OF IT. NOBODY WENT IN TWICE IN A DAY AND NOBODY WENT IN AT THE TOP OF A FLOOD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THE FITTING-OUT SHEET CARRIES NO LINE FOR ANY OF IT YET AND STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the thirteenth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was put on that cistern upon it, the covers lying on it unbroken from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that low water at Nethermorrow came at about ten past six in the morning and the water there was slack until about half past seven, and that nobody went into the water at the top of a flood or at any other part of it; and that at about twenty to five in the morning a slate was written on in a boat shed at the end of the west quay in the words of the man who was going down on it, with the hour at the foot of it, being that there was a man of about forty-four of Nethermorrow in the water under the wheelhouse of a boat of about nine tons lying broadside on that bar in about three and a half fathoms, and that he was going to put a line on that man and bring him up, and that the air was twenty-five minutes and no more and nobody goes in twice in a day and nobody goes in at the top of a flood and anybody who wants to come up is to say so and will not be argued with, and that the slate named a woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm as the tender on the surface and a man of about thirty-two as the one going down, and that it was read out loud twice before it went out to that quay and about four people heard it read; and that that woman stood in that tender from about half past four and did not put one foot over the side of her, and that a second line came off her own winch and was cut clear of the working boat's fall, and that she bent a cutoff into the ring of it with her own hands at about ten to five and kept the loose end of the wire herself; and that one man went down at about ten past six and was on the bottom in about three and a half fathoms at about a quarter past six, and that at about six and twenty-eight minutes the man under that wheelhouse said he wanted to come up and nobody asked him why and nobody argued with him, and that he was on the steps of that tender at about half past six, which is twenty minutes and not twenty-six; and that the cost of that hour was one cylinder off the rack, forty yards of line gone and gone, the coal in that pump, victual for three on that tender at about seven, and a day asked for by name at the figure in chalk, and that not one of those five things is on that money sheet yet; and that at about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing; and that nobody in this harbor put a state on any of the nine names on that sheet of days and that the three columns of that sheet were not counted today; and that nobody read out of the sheet of the fourteen at the end of that stall in ten days, and that not one figure on that sheet moved by one in either direction, and that none of those fourteen was put on and none was taken off; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 543 words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March is the thirty-second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and sixty-five and the second is one thousand six hundred and ninety-nine, and that the third of those four columns took nothing whatever at all, a boy's hand being at the head of it and his surname being on no page and himself not being on the water, and that the fourth of them stands exactly where it stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that the man who wrote that slate at twenty to five was not in that yard at six that evening.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about twenty to five in the morning the reason I wrote my own reason on that slate and did not have a clerk write it out of a book is that a reason written by the man who is going down is the only kind anybody can check afterwards, because a keeper of a page who writes a reason for a man is a man who decides what another man went down for, and this yard has spent eleven weeks finding that out; and that at about ten to five in the morning the reason that woman bent that cutoff into that ring herself and kept the wire is that a cutoff somebody has to ask permission to pull is not a cutoff, it is an opinion, and a man under a wheelhouse in three and a half fathoms cannot hear anybody ask a question; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing at all in this column about the town of Nethermorrow is that that town is four hours off this quay and this yard has no page for it, and that a man of fifty came up that road yesterday with a line burnt round his hand and told us three people were in a boat and I wrote three and a fourth unaccounted for, and that the fourth was found on that town's own mole at about nine o'clock on Monday night by that town's own people and nobody in this harbor was sent for and nobody was asked about it; and that at about half past one in the afternoon the reason I did not stand on that quay this morning is that a man on a quay is a man who makes the man in the water late, and that I have been that man twice in my working life and both times it was me and not him who decided the time; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason nothing has gone onto that money sheet this morning is that eighteen cylinders of air and six hundredweight of coal and two hundred and twenty yards of line and eight days asked for by name are going to go onto it before the end of this month with the days the work was done on the face of the line and the day this yard settles it, and that I have not written a figure on that sheet this morning because there is no figure to write and a figure written before a yard settles a day is a guess wearing a coat; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I have not written one word about that Morrow Line in nine days running is that it is still in the held column and it is still not struck and it is still one of the nine, and that the word a boat going out of a harbour to pick three men out of the water puts on it is no word at all.*
