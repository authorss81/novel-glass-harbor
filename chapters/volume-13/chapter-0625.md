# Chapter 625

*Wednesday the Sixth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, a Woman of About Forty-One Who Crews a Boat Out of Ashtide Saying Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People in About Two Hundred and Ninety-Four Words That the Ashtide Run Is Restricted from Today and Nobody Asking Her a Second Time and Nobody Agreeing With Her and Nobody Thanking Her, the Ashtide Run Taken Out of the Held Column on the Sheet of Days in That Shed at About Half Past Five With the Day Against It and the Figure One Left Readable and No Figure Struck and Nobody in This Harbor Having Asked That Crew a State Before, Eight Held Words No, Four in the Held Column and One in the Restricted Column and Four in the Dormant Column, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-Eight and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Wednesday the sixth day of April, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, and a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Wednesday with nothing standing on it at all: that cistern covered and untouched from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. At about two in the afternoon a woman of about forty-one who crews a boat out of Ashtide said out loud in about nine people that the Ashtide Run is restricted from that day, and nobody asked her a second time and nobody agreed with her and nobody thanked her, and she said it once and she had not been asked for it by anybody in this harbor. That crew was asked in the winter to hold one mark for one night and said no, and it is not a second crew and it is not a second question and nobody in this yard has put it that way. The state went on the sheet of days in that shed at about half past five with the day against it, and the word held that had stood against that run since before the winter was left where it was and still readable, and no figure was struck and nobody asked that crew anything. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a crew of a route saying one word about its own run is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out at four this afternoon, and there were twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lying on that slate at the close of it. Four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so that slate is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. What this harbor has spent on that sheet is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, what it has ever been given is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the sum it has never had is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. A tin in that shed has not been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of this afternoon came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the second of them is the one a stranger could put a date to from a church book. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty-three days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-five. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty-three days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-ninth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-seven days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and seventeenth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-fourth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-five days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have not been opened by anybody. Those four lines are still behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. That basket in that boathouse has the paper still sitting in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole are nine hulls this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty-three days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about two in the afternoon a woman of about forty-one who crews a boat out of Ashtide and had come up that coast road on Tuesday night for a boatyard matter said one thing out loud in about nine people at the flat of that bench stone, and nobody had asked her for it.

She had her hands in the bib of her apron and she had not been in that yard before that morning and about nine people were standing round that stone and about four of them could read what was nailed to the wall above it.

"**The Ashtide Run is restricted from today, and I am the mouth of that and nobody here asked me and nobody up that coast is going to ask me a second time either.**

You asked us in the winter to hold one mark for one night and we said no, and that is still standing and it is not what I have come about. **What has come about is that a sheet went round four of the harbors on this coast in the last fortnight and it has a day and an hour on it and nothing else on it, and it does not say what it did not do.** It is a clean sheet. It is the cleanest thing that has come into this harbor in eleven weeks and I want nine people in this yard to sit with that for a minute before I say the rest of it.

**We are four boats and eleven men and I have a crossing at the third of the ebb and two of my eleven are down with something in their chest and one of them has a wife who is expecting in the spring, and a sheet with a day on it does not know any of those three things and it does not have to. It only has to be right.** So the run is restricted from today and not one of those eleven is coming home and asked why by anybody in this yard, and it stays restricted until the four of us say otherwise, in one of our own mouths, on a day of our own choosing.

**And I am not going to be thanked for it and I would rather nobody in this yard repeated one word of it back to that port.** There is a word that gets said when a crew takes its own run down and it is the wrong word, and it is the word that makes the next crew say it for the wrong reason."

That is about three hundred and thirty-four words and nobody agreed with her out loud and nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her and nobody told her she was wrong, and about four people in that yard heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told at all.

---

At about half past five the man who keeps the leaf put one word on the sheet of days in that shed against the Ashtide Run with Wednesday the sixth of April and the hour of two against that word, and he did not strike the word held that had been standing against that run since before the winter opened.

The word held is still on that sheet and it is still readable and nobody in that shed can take it off and nobody in that shed is going to, because the only hand in this country entitled to take a word off that row is the hand of the crew that put it there, and this yard has not asked that crew anything at any point in eleven weeks.

**The held column of that sheet now carries three names and the restricted column carries two and the dormant column carries four, and three plus two plus four is nine, and the nine names are still nine and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can read them all off and add the three columns and get nine again.**

He did not read that sheet out loud. He did not tell the nine people in that shed what the columns now said and about four of them found out before seven o'clock and about five did not.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and seventy-four words, and nobody in that shed was asked whether one crew had been asked a state before.

"**One of the nine names on that board changed at half past five this evening and I put the word down, and I want nine people in this shed to hold three things at once about it.**

The figure at the head of this slip is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**One, that woman was not asked.** She came up that road last night for a boatyard matter, she had not been in that yard before this morning, and she said a word about her own run in front of nine people she had met four hours earlier. Nobody here put that word in her and nobody here is going to ask her a second time and there is no version of this week in which anybody in this shed goes up that coast road and asks that crew whether they meant it.

**Two, that I did not strike the word held out of that row and I am not going to.** A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on two words on one line of that sheet and read both of them, and the second one is the true one now and the first one is still there because a yard that rubs out a crew's word the day a second crew's word arrives is a yard that is collecting words. That is the whole of what a keeper of a page is for and it is not a large job.

**Three, and this is the one I have been carrying since two o'clock: nobody in this harbor has put a state on any of those nine names. Not a storm. Not a loss. Not a Crown instrument, and there is a paper on a board at the head of that mole with a seal on it and I have not read one word of it and I am not going to.** What came down that road today was a woman telling me her own run is restricted and giving me three reasons in her own mouth, and one of those reasons is a man down with something in his chest and one of them is a wife expecting in the spring, and neither of those reasons is mine to print on that sheet and neither of them is going on it.

And here is the thing about the clean sheet that came in under a stone a month ago, and she said it in nine people before I had said it in one, and I am not going to take it from her and I am not going to restate it in those words. **A sheet that carries a day and an hour and nothing else does not have to be wrong about anything, and that is the difficulty with it, and this harbor has spent eleven weeks teaching people to read the edges of a thing and there are no edges on that paper at all.**

The fourteen on that sheet at the end of that stall were not read out of and no figure on it went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off.

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud. Three hundred and fifty-eight stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and sixty-four against the second, my third is empty, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a woman taking her own run down. That shed was bare by about six with its door open on a slip nobody was on, and that sheet of days stood against that wall with three in one column and two in another and four in the third, and about five people in that market went home not knowing any of it.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE WHO CREWS A BOAT OUT OF ASHTIDE SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ON THAT WEDNESDAY, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND IN ABOUT 294 WORDS, THAT THE ASHTIDE RUN IS RESTRICTED FROM THAT DAY, AND NOBODY HAD ASKED HER AND NOBODY AGREED WITH HER AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HER AND NOBODY THANKED HER AND NOBODY TOLD HER SHE WAS WRONG. THAT CREW WAS ASKED IN THE WINTER TO HOLD ONE MARK FOR ONE NIGHT AND SAID NO, AND THAT IS A DIFFERENT THING FROM THIS AND IS NOT A SECOND QUESTION. THE STATE WENT ON THE SHEET OF DAYS IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR OF TWO AGAINST IT, AND THE WORD HELD THAT HAD STOOD AGAINST THAT RUN SINCE BEFORE THE WINTER OPENED WAS LEFT WHERE IT WAS AND IS STILL READABLE, AND NO FIGURE WAS STRUCK AND NOBODY ASKED THAT CREW ANYTHING. THE THREE COLUMNS OF THAT SHEET NOW CARRY THREE, TWO AND FOUR, AND THREE PLUS TWO PLUS FOUR IS NINE. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NOT ONE FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THE MONEY SHEET HAS SENT OUT OF THIS HARBOR HAS PAID OUT ON THAT SHEET ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; EVERYTHING PUT INTO IT COMES TO TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND THE SUM IT HAS NEVER HELD IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past five in the evening the reason I left the word held standing on that row is that I have been in this harbor eleven weeks and in that time I have struck one thing out of one page and it was mine and it was right and it is still the most frightening thing I have done here, and that a page which carries only the latest word off a crew is a page that has no memory and a page with no memory cannot be argued with and this harbor has told people for eleven weeks that the whole of what a gap is is that it can be argued with; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not write one word in this column about what she said is that she said it better in three hundred and thirty-four words than this slip says it in six hundred and seventy-four, and that a keeper of a page who answers a thing that was said in a market is answering it in a shed and that is the one move in this house I have never made and I am not going to make it in a week when nine people are waiting to see what I think; and that at about ten in the morning the reason I have not written a word about the crew that said no to one mark for one night is that she named it herself in the first hundred words and then went on to a different thing, and that a keeper who goes back to the winter in the middle of a woman's sentence about her own chest is a keeper who wants the winter to be about him; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason this slip does not say which of the other eight names is going to move this week is that this yard has been wrong three times in six weeks about what other people are about to do, and that a yard that guesses out loud at half past five is a yard that puts a state on a run that a crew has not put a state on; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason nothing that happened this afternoon went on that sheet of money is that a crew taking down its own run costs four boats and eleven men and about six weeks of crossings and there is no line on that sheet for a crossing, and that a man of about forty-one who came up that road last night will go back down it on Friday and that road takes a cart four days each way and a boat a day and a night, and that nobody in this harbor has a figure for what a boat's two days of not being there costs and this yard is not going to invent one tonight.*
