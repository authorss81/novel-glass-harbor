# Chapter 610

*Tuesday the Twenty-Second Day of March, the Seventy-Fourth Wage Tuesday, the Column Read Out on the Lid of That Step at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes by a Man of About Thirty-One of the Second Lay Who Has Read It Seventy-Four Times, Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, the Chest with the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-Fourth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, the Count of the Positions Taken Off the Page of Days at About Two in the Afternoon in That Yard With the Door Standing Open and Taken Twice and the Two Ways Agreeing at Three, and Not Said Out Loud in Nine People and Not Said in the Half-Past-Five Slip, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying in About a hundred and twenty Words Why He Is Not Going To Say It, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Three and the Second at One Thousand Five Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Tuesday the twenty-second day of March, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, and a yard below it with a shed, a bench stone, and about nine people in that yard at half past five in the afternoon.

**The seventy-fourth wage Tuesday, and neither a rota day nor a wipe, and that cistern left alone with its covers on from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon. The column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the seventy-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of that day behind the seventy-third and behind the seventy-second. The count of the positions was taken off the page of days at about two in the afternoon in that yard with the door standing open, and it was taken twice, and the two ways of taking it agree and give three, and it was not said out loud in that yard and was not said in the slip at half past five. Ten days of a schedule went by and not one of them put a mark on the money sheet, and a count is not a line on that sheet. A hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, arrives in that yard at about ten and leaves it at about four, and on each of the ten days of this run it does both, and across the ten days of this run one thousand six hundred and eighty pence has gone in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence has gone out, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, is in the bag now against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of it. That sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and ten days of a schedule and ten days of a cistern have not moved it by a penny. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and nothing at all on any of the ten days of this run was paid for out of the bag under the slate, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown,

**The nine durations are these and they are the last nine of the first ten days of this run and the man who keeps them has not added one figure to them this fortnight. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty days without a word to anybody about a page of six names, and that is the roundest of the nine and nobody has said so out loud. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and eight days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and ten, and it is a round figure this month and it is not a gift and it has not been improved. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-eight days. The bench under that pump, the four hundred and fourteenth night, and that is a figure a stranger can see from a road and there is nothing to be done about it. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-two days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and second. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and nineteenth. That board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the demand for the returns is in a basket in that boathouse with the paper still in it and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is a hundred and eight days today, and one day more than it was yesterday, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The column was read out on that step from ten o'clock in the morning and it took four minutes, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday read it for the seventy-fourth time, and about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of that lid from where they were standing.

He read twenty-six lines off the lid of that step and the last of the twenty-six came to one hundred and four marks, and there his finger stopped, and he gave the figure and he gave the fact that it had not shifted, and behind that door twenty envelopes lay shut from ten until four, and that is the whole of his reading.

The seventy-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of that day. Nobody said a word about it but the man reading it, and about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told.

---

And at about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the count of the positions off that page of days in that yard, with the door standing open and about nine people in the yard, and he did not say it out loud and nobody in that yard was told what it was and it is not on the slip at half past five and it is not in a minute and it is not on a wall in that harbor.

He took it twice. Once he went down the four columns of the last day on that page and added what was standing in them, and once he went down the sheet and counted the figures that have not been struck. The first of those two is two and one and none and none, being two in the refused column and one in the restricted column, and that is three. The second of those two is the same three figures standing on that page, and the figure in the opened column against Wednesday the sixteenth has a stroke through it and is not one of the three, and the fourth community on that page has nothing standing against any day and is not one of the three either. Three and three, and the two ways agree.

He wrote it in the yard's own book at about ten past two and about four people watched him do it and the rest of that yard was not told, and about half past four the man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked what he had written and was told that he had written a figure and that the figure was on the page and that the page was on that bench stone and that he was standing at it, and he went and looked at it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 540 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what he had written in the book at two o'clock.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Ten days have gone past it and it is exactly where the twenty-sixth of last month left it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and this is the last day of ten I have put in a slip without saying a word about it, and I am not going to say the word either.

**The column was read at about ten in four minutes and it is twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move for the seventy-fourth time, and there are twenty envelopes in a chest that has been shut from ten until four today and every Tuesday since the twenty-fifth of January.** The seventy-fourth week stands behind the seventy-third. Across these ten days a hundred and sixty-eight pence has gone into this yard a day and the same figure has come out of it a day, and that is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and that has been true on the Sunday and it is true tonight.

**And here is what I am not going to say out loud in nine people, and I am saying that I am not going to say it, which is a different thing from saying it quietly.** I took a figure off that page of days at about two this afternoon and I took it twice and the two ways agree, and I am not putting the figure in this slip and I am not going to put it in this slip tomorrow, and the reason is that **a number said out loud in front of nine people stops being a number and becomes a thing nine people are standing behind, and in eleven weeks this harbor has turned three numbers into positions and I am not handing a fourth one over at half past five on a Tuesday.** It is in the book, and the book is on this bench stone, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can add the four columns of that page and get my figure twice and neither of those two things needs me to say it at all.

**A cistern was charged three times in ten days and a wall came down twice and a column was read twice and one of those ten days had a man come four days up a coast road with a thing he took with him, and not one of those five things has put a figure on that sheet, and the four days of that man's boots is not a line on it and is not a penny out of that bag.** The four heads on a wall in a room over the floor of that market are on no sheet of mine. At about six this evening I entered one line on the first column and five on the second and I did not read that leaf out loud today, and the first of my four figures is three hundred and forty-three and the second is one thousand five hundred and eighty-nine."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a figure taken twice out of nine people who were not told it, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it. and the book was on that bench stone with a page turned back to the thirteenth day of March.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES ON THAT TUESDAY, BEING THE SEVENTY-FOURTH TIME, AND IT STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND IT DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-FOURTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. THE COUNT OF THE POSITIONS WAS TAKEN OFF THAT PAGE OF DAYS AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN THAT YARD WITH THE DOOR STANDING OPEN AND WAS TAKEN TWICE, DOWN THE FOUR COLUMNS OF THE LAST DAY AND DOWN THE FIGURES THAT HAVE NOT BEEN STRUCK, AND THE TWO WAYS AGREE AND GIVE THREE, AND THE OPENED COLUMN IS EMPTY AND THE REFUSED COLUMN HAS TWO AND THE RESTRICTED COLUMN HAS ONE AND THE WITHDRAWN COLUMN IS EMPTY, AND THE FIGURE IN THE OPENED COLUMN AGAINST WEDNESDAY THE SIXTEENTH HAS A STROKE THROUGH IT AND IS STILL READABLE. THAT COUNT WAS NOT SAID OUT LOUD IN NINE PEOPLE AND WAS NOT SAID IN THE SLIP AT HALF PAST FIVE. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND NO PAGE AND NO COUNT IS A LINE ON IT. THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-second day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday and was the seventy-fourth of them and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the covers were on it from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday and has read it seventy-four times, and that about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of that lid from where they were standing, and that he read twenty-six lines off the lid of that step and the last one came to one hundred and four marks and there he stopped, and that he said the figure and said that the figure had not moved, and that behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and that the seventy-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of the said day behind the seventy-third and behind the seventy-second, and that nobody said a word about it but the man reading it and that about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told; and that at about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the count of the positions off that page of days in that yard with the door standing open and about nine people in the yard, and that he did not say it out loud and nobody in that yard was told what it was and that it is not on the slip at half past five and is not in a minute and is not on a wall in that harbor; and that he took it twice, once by going down the four columns of the last day on that page and adding what was standing in them and once by going down the sheet and counting the figures that have not been struck, and that the first of those two is two and one and none and none, being two in the refused column and one in the restricted column, and that that is three, and that the second of those two is the same three figures standing on that page, and that the figure in the opened column against Wednesday the sixteenth has a stroke through it and is not one of the three, and that the fourth community on that page has nothing standing against any day and is not one of the three either, and that three and three and the two ways agree; and that he wrote it in the yard's own book at about ten past two and that about four people watched him do it and the rest of that yard was not told, and that at about half past four a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked what he had written and was told that he had written a figure and that the figure was on the page and that the page was on that bench stone and that he was standing at it, and that he went and looked at it; and that the page of days began on Wednesday the sixteenth and carries this harbor's word in the opened column against that day with the hour of eleven against the figure, and that on Friday the eighteenth the same position was taken back off that day, the figure in the opened column being struck and left readable and the word opened under it struck and left readable, and the figure one being written in the restricted column against the same day with the word opened written under it with a stroke through it, and the day itself did not move and the hour of eleven against it did not move and no day was lost and no figure was rubbed out; and that across these ten days a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, came into that yard a day and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it a day, and that one thousand six hundred and eighty pence is ten marks six shillings and eightpence, and that twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and that the bag holds twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and the bag is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind it; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on the said day and nothing came off it, and that a count is not a line on that sheet, and that that sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that ten days of a schedule and three charges of a cistern have not moved it by a penny; and that a cistern was charged on the fourteenth, the seventeenth and the twenty-first of this month and on no other day of the ten, being the hundred and thirty-third, hundred and thirty-fourth and hundred and thirty-fifth; and that a wall came down bare on the thirteenth and on the twentieth, being the ninety-seventh and ninety-eighth times, and went up again both times unrepaired and not whole; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and forty-three and the second at one thousand five hundred and eighty-nine, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the tenth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I have written the whole of that step out again this month and it is the second time is that a wage Tuesday is the day this harbor finds out what it owes, and that in about eleven weeks a man of about thirty-one has read that column seventy-four times and has not once been asked what he thinks of it, and that this slip is not the place to ask him and that I am not going to ask him; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I took that count twice and not once is that a count taken once is a thing a man did, and a count taken twice down two different directions and landing on the same figure is a thing anybody can check, and that the two directions were chosen for that reason, and that one of them goes across the four columns of the last day and one of them goes down the figures that have not been struck, and that those are the only two ways anybody would think of to take it and that is exactly why I took them; and that at about half past four the reason a man of about thirty-three was told that the figure was on the page rather than that it was three is that he did not ask what the figure was, and that a man who asks what a figure is and is answered with a figure will remember the figure, and that a man who asks and is sent to a page will remember going, and that I am aware of what that is and I did it anyway and I have put it in here so that a stranger can decide for himself; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says in about 120 words that I am not going to say that number, and does not say the number, is that a slip is said out loud to nine people and that nine people who have heard a number are nine people who will repeat it, and that in eleven weeks three numbers have been said out loud in this yard and all three of them have ended up being something other than what they were when they were said, and that the one that was said on that step in January is standing behind about forty people this morning and it is not a wage column and it never was; and that at about six o'clock the reason there is nothing in this slip about the ten days as a whole is that a run of days is a thing this harbor has been counting since the twenty-second of January and that I have said everything about it that a slip at half past five can say without it becoming a speech, and that the arithmetic of ten days is on the first column and not in here, and that is where arithmetic belongs on a Tuesday.*
