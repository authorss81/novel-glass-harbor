# Chapter 617

*Tuesday the Twenty-Ninth Day of March, the Seventy-Fifth Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes by a Man of About Thirty-One of the Second Lay and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Seventy-Fifth Time, the Chest with the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-Fifth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, a Boy of Twenty-Two on the Stool at the End of That Stall With a Board Across His Knees and Off the Water and Nobody Having Said a Word to Him About the Water, a Man of About Thirty-Two and a Man of About Twenty-Nine Sitting About Nine Feet Apart on That Quay at About Two in the Afternoon for an Hour and a Quarter and Neither of Them Doing Any Work and Neither of Them Saying Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure on That Sheet Moved by One, the Shortfall Named Out Loud at About Half Past Five, Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Under the Slate, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of March, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, and a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**The seventy-fifth wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that cistern covered and untouched from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon. On the lid of that step the column stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it was read out at about ten by the man of about thirty-one of the second lay and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four. The seventy-fifth week stands fallen due at the close of that day behind the seventy-fourth and behind the seventy-third, and a man has now read that lid seventy-five times and nobody in this harbor has ever asked him what he thinks of it, and that is not going to be put right this week. Two men sat on that quay at about two in the afternoon for an hour and a quarter and did no work, and there is no figure anywhere in this country that prices an hour and a quarter of two men sitting down. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it. Read out at ten this morning and paid out at four this afternoon, both at a hundred and sixty-eight pence, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lying under that slate at the close of the day, which is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, on the wrong side of a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. The sheet at the head of that yard carries one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence on the out side of it, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence on the in side of it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the two sides of it are ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence apart, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**These are the nine and none of them has a name against it and none of them has been improved. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-five days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and fifteen. A bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and seventeen. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-first night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-seven days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-nine days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and ninth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-sixth. That board at the head of that mole is still carrying five papers, and two of the five have never been opened by anybody. The answer to the offer of four lines is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. Nothing has been taken out of that basket in that boathouse, and the paper in it has not been looked at. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head, and that is one hundred and fifteen days, a day more than yesterday, and it is not a gift and no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water, and he was not on it today either, and nobody has sent for him and nobody is going to.**

---

The column was read out on the lid of that step at about ten in the morning and it took four minutes, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay who has read that lid every Tuesday since the twenty-fifth of January read it for the seventy-fifth time, and about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of it from where they were standing.

He read twenty-six lines and the last of the twenty-six came to one hundred and four marks, and there his finger stopped, and he gave the figure and he gave the fact that it had not shifted, and behind that door twenty envelopes lay shut from ten until four.

Nobody said a word about the seventy-fifth week but the man reading it, and about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody has ever asked that man what he thinks of a lid he has read seventy-five times and nobody is going to start this week.

---

At about one in the afternoon a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall with a board across his knees, writing weights into a book in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and he was not on the water, and that is the fourth time he has been off the water in eleven weeks, and it is not a punishment, and nobody in that market has said one word to him about the water and nobody put a name to the third column of anybody's leaf.

---

At about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two and a man of about twenty-nine sat on that quay about nine feet apart for an hour and a quarter and neither of them did any work at all.

The man of about thirty-two had a wedge and a drift in a canvas roll at his feet and he did not take them out. The man of about twenty-nine had his hands in his pockets and he was looking at the water and he was listening to it out of the left side of his head and that is the only thing he has been doing for a hundred and fifteen days and it is not a gift and no figure is set against it.

About four people walking that quay saw two men sitting on it and about five did not, and nobody went over, and neither of those two men said one word to the other in an hour and a quarter, and neither of them went into the water that day or the day before it or the day before that.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and fourteen words, and nobody in that shed was asked why a lid gets read seventy-five times.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, read out at ten this morning in four minutes for the seventy-fifth time, and it did not move, and behind that door twenty envelopes stood shut from ten until four.** The seventy-fifth week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the seventy-fourth and behind the seventy-third, and about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of that lid from where they were standing, and the man who reads it has read it seventy-five times and nobody in this harbor has ever asked him what he thinks of it and I am not going to be the first.

**The figure I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** It is where the twenty-sixth of last month left it and it is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference and the man whose yard that is says so out loud. Under that slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

And here is the thing I want to say out loud in nine people tonight and nowhere else, because I have said it in a book once and it is the most useful sentence anybody has produced in this yard in six weeks. **It is not his column.** That is what a man of about forty-four of the second lay said in this shed at half past five on the fifteenth of this month when somebody asked him a question about it, and it has been in every slip I have written since, and it applies to the man who reads that lid exactly as it applies to him, and he has not been asked.

**Two men sat on that quay for an hour and a quarter this afternoon about nine feet apart and neither of them did any work and neither of them said one word to the other.** One of them had a wedge and a drift in a canvas roll at his feet and did not take them out. I have not written a word in this slip about that and I am not going to and I want that on the record. There is no figure in this country that prices an hour and a quarter of two men sitting down, and there is not going to be one, and if a person somewhere wants to put a number on it I would rather they put it on a page of their own where somebody can argue with them about it than in a slip at half past five where nine people would have to carry it home.

The sheet with the fourteen things on it was not read out of today and no figure on it has moved and none of those fourteen has been added to and none taken off.

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud, and tonight of all I would not have read it out loud. Three hundred and fifty and one thousand six hundred and twenty-four are the first two of those figures, the third is empty, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for two men sitting down, and the shed was clear of everybody by about six with its door standing open on a slip with nothing on it. The chest with the twenty envelopes in it was still shut at six, and the column on that lid was still twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES ON THAT TUESDAY, BEING THE SEVENTY-FIFTH TIME, AND STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-FIFTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE SAT ABOUT NINE FEET APART ON THAT QUAY AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON FOR AN HOUR AND A QUARTER AND DID NO WORK AND SAID NOTHING TO EACH OTHER, AND THERE IS NO FIGURE ANYWHERE IN THIS COUNTRY THAT PRICES THAT. A BOY OF TWENTY-TWO WAS NOT ON THE WATER AND HIS SURNAME IS ON NO PAGE. THE FOURTEEN WERE NOT READ OUT OF AND NOT ONE FIGURE ON THAT SHEET WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. WHAT THAT SHEET IS SHORT BY STANDS AT NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS; WHAT HAS EVER COME INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND WHAT HAS GONE OUT OF THIS HARBOR ON IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. IT DID NOT MOVE ON THAT TUESDAY ANY MORE THAN IT MOVED ON ANY OTHER DAY OF THIS FORTNIGHT. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, LIES UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the seventy-fifth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it unbroken from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in the morning and that it took four minutes and that the man who read it is of about thirty-one and of the second lay and has read that lid every Tuesday since the twenty-fifth of January, being seventy-five times, and that about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of that lid from where they were standing, and that he read twenty-six lines and the last of the twenty-six came to one hundred and four marks and there his finger stopped, and that he gave the figure and gave the fact that the figure had not shifted, and that behind that door twenty envelopes lay shut from ten until four, and that the seventy-fifth week stands fallen due at the close of the said day behind the seventy-fourth and behind the seventy-third, and that nobody said a word about it but the man reading it and about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told, and that nobody in that harbor has ever asked that man what he thinks of a lid he has read seventy-five times and that nobody is going to start this week; and that at about one in the afternoon a boy of twenty-two was on the stool at the end of that stall with a board across his knees writing weights into a book in a hand that gets smaller as the afternoon goes on, and that he was not on the water, and that that is the fourth time he has been off the water in eleven weeks and that it is not a punishment, and that nobody in that market has said one word to him about the water and nobody put a name to the third column of anybody's leaf and his surname is on no page; and that at about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two and a man of about twenty-nine sat on that quay about nine feet apart for an hour and a quarter and neither of them did any work, and that the man of about thirty-two had a wedge and a drift in a canvas roll at his feet and did not take them out, and that the man of about twenty-nine had his hands in his pockets and was looking at the water and was listening to it out of the left side of his head, and that about four people walking that quay saw two men sitting on it and about five did not and nobody went over, and that neither of those two men said one word to the other in an hour and a quarter and that neither of them went into the water that day or the day before it or the day before that; and that nothing was read out of a gap and no figure on the sheet of the fourteen moved by one and that sheet was not touched; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about six hundred and fourteen words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and fifty and the second is one thousand six hundred and twenty-four, and that the third of my four took no entry, and that the fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month is the seventeenth.*

*Second column, entered in that yard's own mouth on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening and not checkable from a mole, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have written down that two men sat on a quay for an hour and a quarter and said nothing to each other is that I have been in that yard since half past eight this morning and I noticed it at about half past three and I did not go over, and that the reason I did not go over is that one of them has been on that quay every day this week and the other one has been on it twice, and that a man who has stopped is not a thing to be improved by a man with a book; and that at about ten in the morning the reason this slip does not say what that man thinks of that lid after seventy-five readings is that I asked a different man in this yard the same question in a shed at half past five on the fifteenth of this month and got an answer in about sixty words, and that the answer was that it is not his column, and that I have carried that answer into every slip since and it has been the most useful thing anybody has said in this yard in six weeks, and that it applies to that man exactly and that I am not going to be the one to put it to him; and that at about ten past ten the reason I have written down that about eleven people could not see a line of that lid from where they were standing is that this is the number of people in this harbor that a notice cannot reach by being read, and that the counter-plan was taught out loud for exactly that reason and that the method's page is read out twice for the same reason, and that the column on that lid has been read seventy-five times to a room that half of it cannot see; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have not written in this column a single word about what a wedge does or does not do in a man's hands this week is that it is not mine and that the last man who wrote about his hands in a slip like this one was told to stop by about four people, and that I am not going to be the one that does it twice in one month; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have put the boy on the stool at the end of that stall into this column and not the seventy-fifth wage Tuesday is that in eleven weeks a man standing still in the same place is a thing I have stopped writing down, and that a boy of twenty-two who has been off the water four times in eleven weeks and has still got a stool and a book on his knees is the only figure in this harbor that has not gone down, and that there is no name for him anywhere and that is the correct state of it and not a gap in the work.*
