# Chapter 623

*Monday the Fourth Day of April, a Rota Day, That Butt Filled by Hand Before That Market Opened for the Hundred and Thirty-Ninth Time, Eleven Trips of a Bucket and Not One of Them Walked on, the Covers Back on It by a Quarter Past Nine and the Water Standing at the Rim of Its Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It and Nobody Having Offered to Put It There, the Count of Positions Not Taken Again and That Sheet Standing Beside the Sheet of the Fourteen With the Count on It of Three, Two in a Refused Column and One in a Restricted Column, the Back of That Page Carrying a Fifth Word and Four Things Against It and Two Drafts Above It and Two Lines Under Those and Nothing Settled, the Relay Still Answering at Ninety-Four Seconds With a Branch of It Still Stopped and a Column of What Answered It on the Back of an Old Slate in That Shed, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-Six and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Monday the fourth day of April, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before that market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about two in the afternoon and about nine in that shed at half past five.

**A Monday rota with no wage Tuesday in it and no wipe in it. That cistern behind the market went up for the hundred and thirty-ninth time in eleven trips by two men and not one bucket of it was walked on, the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, the butt stood to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. At about two this afternoon about nine people stood at the flat of that bench stone with the page of that harbor's own answers in front of them and did not read it out and did not take a count of it, and that page carries two columns of figures and a third empty one and a fourth at one hundred and twenty-five, and the count of positions taken off it three weeks ago stands where it stands and is not being taken again. The relay answered at ninety-four seconds at about eleven this morning and a branch of it is still stopped, and the column of what answered it is on the back of an old slate in that shed and is not in any other place. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a cistern charge is not a line on that sheet and a count of positions is not a line on that sheet either. That yard was paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence at ten and paid back a hundred and sixty-eight pence at four, and on the slate under the money there were twenty-eight pence at the day's end, being two shillings and fourpence. A wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so that slate stands six hundred and twelve pence short of it, being three marks eleven shillings. The sheet this harbor has been answering for stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence of outgo, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence of income, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the whole of the difference between those two figures is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Nothing has been opened out of a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of this morning came out of that bag.**

**The ninth day of this month and the nine have gone on every one of them. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty-one days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-three. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty-one days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-seventh night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-five days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundredth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fifteenth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-second. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-three days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened. The offer of four lines has not left the upper room over that market and there is still nothing written under its fourth line and the answer to it is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket has stood since the second of this month with a paper inside it and nothing else inside it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this morning and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty-one days, one more than the day before it, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went up the ladder at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips by two men, and nothing was carried up that ladder on any other errand. The covers were back on the butt before a quarter past nine and the water stood at the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it in all that time, and that is the hundred and thirty-ninth time.

The name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Nobody has offered to put it there and nobody has asked either of those two men whether they would like it there, and that has been true for a hundred and thirty-nine charges.

---

At about two in the afternoon, with the covers still on that butt and about nine people standing at the flat of that bench stone, the man who keeps the leaf put his thumb on the corner of that page and moved his thumb about four inches down it and stopped, and then took his thumb off.

What his thumb went over was the count that was taken off that page three weeks ago off the four columns headed opened, refused, restricted and withdrawn, being three figures, two of them in the refused column and one of them in the restricted column and none in the opened and none in the withdrawn, and a figure in the opened column against the sixteenth is still struck and still readable and is not one of the three.

**He did not take that count again.** Not at about two in the afternoon in nine people and not at half past five in that shed, and about four of the nine could read the four columns and about five could not, and none of those five were told what his thumb had stopped on.

A count of positions is not a count of persons and not a count of gaps. It was taken once off a page of days, it was written down once, and it is not going to be taken again this month, and anybody in this harbor with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on the four columns of the last day of that page and come at those figures twice.

---

At about four minutes past eleven that morning the relay answered at ninety-four seconds and a branch of it did not answer at all, and the man who keeps the leaf went along the wall to the slate and wrote what answered it in a column on the back of that slate and stopped there.

That column is not in any book and it is not in a minute and it is not read out to anybody. About eleven people in this harbor could read it and about nine could not, and the number in it is not going to be said out loud tonight.

The stopped branch has been stopped since the winter. Nobody in this harbor has offered to go and look at it and nobody in this harbor has asked anybody else to.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and twenty-three words, and nobody in that shed was asked why he put his thumb on that corner and took it off.

"**A hundred and thirty-ninth charge. Eleven trips by two men before that market opened and not one bucket walked on and the covers back on before a quarter past nine and that butt up to the rim of its second hoop until about four this afternoon. And I am going to do the thing I have told this yard I would stop doing, which is to stop printing that sentence, so this is the last rota charge I am going to open a slip with.**

The figure I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. It has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month and it is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference and the man whose yard that is has said so out loud and is entitled to say it. Twenty-eight pence is lying under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**I put my thumb on that page this afternoon and moved it about four inches and took it off, and I did not say the number out loud and I am not going to say it tonight.** I took that count three weeks ago off four columns on a page of days and I wrote it down once and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could come at it twice, and a count that is taken twice is a count that has started to be a habit, and this yard is losing its edges doing that and not doing anything else.

**The fifth word on the back of that page is not a column and it is not on the sheet of positions and it carries no figure against any day and it is not counted in that count.** It has four things written against it and the four things are the same four questions that stand against every one of the fourteen at the end of that stall. Above it stand two drafts with one above the other and under those stand two lines with one under the other, and none of the four is struck and none of the four is signed and nothing anywhere says settled and nothing anywhere is going to say settled this month.

The relay answered at ninety-four seconds at four minutes past eleven this morning and a branch of it is still stopped and I wrote what answered it in a column on the back of that slate and that is the only place it is and it is not in a book and it is not going into a book.

The sheet with the fourteen things on it was not touched today. I read nothing out of it in ten days and no figure on it has moved by one and none of those fourteen has been added to and none has been taken off.

At about six this evening one entry went on to that first column and five went on to the second, and that leaf was not read out loud in this shed. Three hundred and fifty-six stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and fifty-four against the second. My third is empty and a boy's hand is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water. My fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a thumb put on a corner. That shed stood empty by about six with its door open on a slip nobody was on, and the butt behind that market was still at the rim of its second hoop when it shut.

**THAT CISTERN WENT UP ON THAT MONDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY TWO MEN AND NOTHING WALKED ON, THE COVERS BACK ON IT BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE, THE WATER TO THE RIM OF THE SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD STILL OFF IT. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF PUT HIS THUMB ON THE PAGE OF THAT HARBOR'S OWN ANSWERS AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, MOVED IT ABOUT FOUR INCHES DOWN THAT PAGE AND TOOK IT OFF, AND HE DID NOT TAKE THE COUNT OF POSITIONS AGAIN AND DID NOT SAY THE FIGURE OUT LOUD AND ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE COULD READ THE FOUR COLUMNS AND ABOUT FIVE COULD NOT AND NONE OF THOSE FIVE WERE TOLD WHAT HIS THUMB HAD STOPPED ON. THE FIFTH WORD ON THE BACK OF THAT PAGE IS NOT A COLUMN AND CARRIES NO FIGURE AGAINST ANY DAY AND IS NOT IN THAT COUNT. THE RELAY ANSWERED AT NINETY-FOUR SECONDS AT FOUR MINUTES PAST ELEVEN AND A BRANCH OF IT IS STILL STOPPED, AND WHAT ANSWERED IT STANDS IN ONE COLUMN ON THE BACK OF AN OLD SLATE IN THAT SHED AND IS NOWHERE ELSE. NO ENTRY WAS TAKEN OUT OF ANY GAP AND THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN IS UNMOVED BY ONE ON EVERY FIGURE ON IT. NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE IS THE FIGURE THAT MONEY SHEET HAS NEVER CARRIED, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS; TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS EVER GONE INTO IT, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. UNDER THAT SLATE LIES TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason I put my thumb on that corner and took it off is that three weeks ago I took a figure off that page out loud in a shed and it was the correct figure and it was on a page, and that the second time I did it I noticed that I had said it before I had looked at the page, and that a man who reads a number out of a page he has already read is a man reading his own memory, and this harbor has spent eleven weeks learning that about testimony and it turns out it is also true about a sheet of days; and that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I have written down that I am going to stop opening a slip with that cistern is that a hundred and thirty-nine sentences about eleven trips is a hundred and thirty-nine sentences this yard has not needed, and that the number of trips is not the cost of that butt and never was, and that the cost of that butt is two men's backs twice a week and I have never printed whose backs and I am not going to start this month by printing them; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason that column on that slate has not gone into a book in eleven weeks is that a book is a thing somebody else keeps, and that this yard has been wrong about minutes and books twice in two winters and both times by putting a thing in one that belonged on a slate; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason I did not tell those five people what my thumb stopped on is that they would have been told by the four who can read and there are four of those and five of the others and neither of those numbers is a figure of persons and neither of them is going on any page; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing in this column about the ninety-four seconds is that a figure somebody else measures is not mine to check and I took it down because it is in front of me twice a day, and that the branch that is stopped is not stopped by anything I know about and I am not going to ask about it, because in eleven weeks the only thing I have got out of asking anybody about a stopped thing is a stopped thing; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason nothing that happened today went on that sheet of money is that a cistern charge is not a line on it and a count of positions is not a line on it and a page a man read twice is not a line on it, and that the only lines that go on that sheet are work somebody paid for out of their own hand, and that the first line that is coming in this spring is going on it on a day I have not read and is not going on it this week.*
