# Chapter 639

*Wednesday the Twentieth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, Nine People Out of That Harbor in Three Boats at About Six in the Morning to Take a Town Off a Flat of Sand Behind a Bar Four Hours' Sail Out, Three Crews Kept Separate and Three Sheets of Their Own With a Day and an Hour on Each and No Crown Instrument Used and No Route Asked and No State Put on Any of the Nine Names, a Man of About Thirty-Two and a Woman of Thirty of No Office Arguing Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon at the Flat of That Bench Stone in About Nine People in About One Hundred and Ninety Words and About One Hundred and Twenty-Seven Words and About One Hundred and Twelve Words About What a Day Asked For by Name Is Worth, and Nobody Arbitrating and Nobody Agreeing Out Loud and Neither of Them Giving Anything Up and Nobody Thanked and the Two of Them Still Married, the First Line That Has Gone Onto That Fitting-Out Sheet in Fifty Days Written at About Four in the Afternoon With the Multiplication Printed at Every Part of It and Its Own Days on the Face of It, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Two and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Wednesday the twentieth day of April, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, a yard below it with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Wednesday with nothing standing against it: that butt covered from eight until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe, and the glass in the case at the office on the west basin up a tenth since yesterday and the sea outside that mole head still two feet above where it stood on the twelfth. At about six this morning nine people of this harbor went out in three boats for the flat of sand behind that bar at Nethermorrow, and the three of them went in three separate boats and each of those three crews wrote its own sheet with a day and an hour on it, and no Crown instrument was used to do any of it and no route was asked for anything and no state was put on any of the nine names on that sheet of days. At about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two and a woman of thirty of no office argued out loud at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people, in about one hundred and ninety words, about one hundred and twenty-seven words and about one hundred and twelve words, about what a day asked for by name is worth, and nobody arbitrated and nobody agreed out loud and neither of them gave anything up and nobody was thanked and they are still married. At about four in the afternoon the first line that has gone onto that fitting-out sheet in fifty days was written on it, with the multiplication printed at every part of it and its own days on the face of it, and it is the bill for a storm that was not a weather. That yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of it at about four that afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was on that slate at the day's end, and set against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, that is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and not one penny of that bill came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the fourth of them is a stone under a pump with nothing on it. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-third night. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-seven days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-seven days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-nine. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and sixteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-first. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-nine days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of those five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. Those four lines are still behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it, and the basket has nothing else in it. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and there were nine of them there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-seven days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about six in the morning three boats went out of that harbor with three crews in them, being three each, and they were four hours down that coast and they were alongside that flat of sand at about eleven and they were back in this harbor at about seven this evening.

Nobody in this harbor sent them with a Crown instrument and nobody asked a route for anything to do with it, and no state was put on any of the nine names on that sheet of days by any of those three crews, and each of those three crews wrote a sheet of its own with the day and the hour on it, and about four people in each place read what each of those three crews wrote.

---

At about two in the afternoon, in about nine people at the flat of that bench stone with the market trading underneath, that man of about thirty-two and that woman of thirty of no office argued out loud for about eighteen minutes and neither of them lowered their voice and neither of them gave anything up.

She went first and it was about one hundred and ninety words.

"**Nine days on that sheet is nine days of eleven people's money and you know as well as I do that not one of the eleven has seen a line of what is going on it this week. You are about to hand them a fortnight of weather at the figure in chalk for a day and a day is a day and a day is a thing a person can point at, and this one is going to be a fortnight that nobody can point at because there is nothing in it to point at.

And I am not saying the work was not done. Three boats went out at six this morning and they will be back at seven this evening and a town is off a flat of sand. I am saying what it is called, because whatever we call it is what it will be called in ten years, and in ten years somebody is going to read that line and ask what those nine days were, and the answer has to be on the face of the line and not in one of our heads.**"

He answered and it was about one hundred and twenty-seven words.

"**Then put a name on each of them and I will hand it over tomorrow. That is what the four heads on that wall are for and it is what a day is for and it is what the figure in chalk is for, and a line that says eight days asked for by name is a line anybody can check and a line that says eight days of a fortnight is a line nobody can.

And I am not going to carry a bill for eight days of a man's back on my own. If that money is going on that sheet then somebody is going to tell me what a day is worth on a day when a day is worth something, and not after.**"

She answered back and it was about one hundred and twelve words.

"**And you have just said out loud in nine people that a bill needs a man to carry it, and that is the thing we said a word about on the thirtieth of last month, and it is not settled and it is not going to be settled by a line on a piece of paper.

So write the line and I will argue with it in this yard every week for as long as I am in this yard, and that is the whole of what I am for, and I am not going to stop doing that for you, and I am not going to be thanked for it either.**"

Nobody arbitrated. Nobody said she was right and nobody said he was right, and about four people in that yard heard all three of those things and the rest of that yard was not told, and nobody thanked either of them for anything, and about five of that nine were not told that the argument had happened at all.

---

At about four in the afternoon the first line that has gone onto that fitting-out sheet in fifty days was written on it in that yard, and it is the bill for a storm that was not a weather, and every part of it carries its own multiplication and its own days on the face of it.

Air: eighteen cylinders at forty-eight pence, being eight hundred and sixty-four pence, bought on the thirteenth, the seventeenth and the twentieth.
Coal: six hundredweight at twenty-six pence, being one hundred and fifty-six pence, bought on the fourteenth and the eighteenth.
Victual: nine men at four days, being thirty-six man-days, at sixteen pence a man-day, being five hundred and seventy-six pence, from the thirteenth to the sixteenth.
Gear: two hundred and twenty yards of line at three pence the yard, being six hundred and sixty pence, bought on the fifteenth and the twentieth.
Days asked for by name: eight days at the figure in chalk, a hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, being one thousand three hundred and forty-four pence, from the thirteenth to the twentieth.

Eight hundred and sixty-four and one hundred and fifty-six and five hundred and seventy-six and six hundred and sixty and one thousand three hundred and forty-four is three thousand six hundred pence, being twenty-two marks, six shillings and eightpence. That sheet stands at one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference.

The eight days are on the flat of that bench stone as every other day's is, and the bench stone is not that sheet, and both of them can be checked by anybody with a leaf and a pencil.

---

Nobody went to the stall of that woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market at about half past four this afternoon and asked her one thing, and about nine people were standing at the flat of that bench stone, of whom about four could read what is on the wall above it.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 517 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what nine days of a fortnight are worth.

"**That sheet moved today for the first time since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month, and it moved once, and there is one line on it, and it is the bill for a storm that was not a weather, and it is the first line that has gone onto that sheet in fifty days and it may be the only one anybody ever puts on it.**

**Eight hundred and sixty-four for air and one hundred and fifty-six for coal and five hundred and seventy-six for victual and six hundred and sixty for gear and one thousand three hundred and forty-four for eight days asked for by name is three thousand six hundred pence, and every one of those five figures is a multiplication that can be done in a head, and every one of the five carries the days it was bought or asked for on the face of the line.**

That brings that sheet to one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the gap between those two is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, **and it is a bigger gap than it was this morning by three thousand six hundred pence and it is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference and it is not going to be made to balance this month.**

**Two people who are married to each other argued out loud in nine people at that stone at about two this afternoon about what a day asked for by name is worth, and I stood four paces away with a book under my arm and I have not written one word of it into a column, and nobody arbitrated and nobody agreed out loud and neither of them gave anything up, and nobody thanked either of them, and they went out of that yard together and they are still married.**

Nothing was read out of a gap today and no figure on the sheet of the fourteen went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. The count of positions was not taken again, and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and carries no figure against any day of this month.

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five on that second, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and seventy-two stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and thirty-four against the second, my third is empty and nobody is appointed to it, a boy's hand being at the head of that column, his surname being on no page and himself off the water, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nine days on a bench stone. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that fitting-out sheet was lying on the bench with one line on it that had not been there on Monday, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence did not come down any board in this harbor today and nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed.

**THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET MOVED TODAY FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF LAST MONTH AND IT MOVED ONCE, AND THERE IS ONE LINE ON IT, AND IT IS THE BILL FOR A STORM THAT WAS NOT A WEATHER, BEING EIGHTEEN CYLINDERS OF AIR AT FORTY-EIGHT PENCE BEING EIGHT HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR PENCE, SIX HUNDREDWEIGHT OF COAL AT TWENTY-SIX PENCE BEING ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX PENCE, NINE MEN AT FOUR DAYS BEING THIRTY-SIX MAN-DAYS AT SIXTEEN PENCE BEING FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY YARDS OF LINE AT THREE PENCE THE YARD BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY PENCE, AND EIGHT DAYS ASKED FOR BY NAME AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK BEING ONE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR PENCE, AND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX AND FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX AND SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY AND ONE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR IS THREE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED PENCE, BEING TWENTY-TWO MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. THAT SHEET NOW STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ARGUED OUT LOUD IN NINE PEOPLE AT THAT STONE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT 190 WORDS, ABOUT 127 WORDS AND ABOUT 112 WORDS ABOUT WHAT A DAY ASKED FOR BY NAME IS WORTH, AND NOBODY ARBITRATED AND NOBODY AGREED OUT LOUD AND NEITHER OF THEM GAVE ANYTHING UP AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND THEY ARE STILL MARRIED. AND UNDER THAT SLATE, AS ON EVERY DAY OF THIS RUN, IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about four in the afternoon the reason I wrote that one line out in five parts with the days on the face of it and the multiplication printed at every part is that a bill a stranger cannot add up is a rumour, and that this yard has spent thirteen weeks writing things down for exactly one person, which is a man who turns up in ten years with a leaf and a pencil; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the eight days are on the bench stone as well as on that sheet is that those two surfaces have been quietly doing the same job for a year and I have only just noticed it, and that the bench stone carries the days and that sheet carries the money, and that a day which is on one of them and not the other is a day a person has to guess about, and that from today they are both written the same afternoon; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason I did not go and stand at that stone at two this afternoon is that the third time this month that this yard has had to stand four paces off and put nothing in a column is a time to count the paces and not the argument, and that what is in it is his work and her record and neither of those is mine to referee; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I have written nothing in this column about three crews on the water is that three crews are not this party, that they kept three sheets of their own with a day and an hour on each, and that about four people in each place read what they wrote, and that a thing four people can read is a thing four people can be angry at, and that this yard did not put a state on the Morrow Line this morning and is not going to; and that at about six in the evening the reason I have said one word about the twenty-sixth of last month in nine people and no more is that a sheet which has not moved in thirteen weeks moves once, and that the size of the gap it moved by is not a thing to be said in a yard, and that it is printed on that sheet and it is on this page and that is the end of it; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason the private access signal on that line has not been touched in nine days is that nobody has asked to lose it, that nobody in this harbor has any business losing it, that I do not know what it is for and I have not gone and found out, and that a man who is refused a thing on Tuesday does not go and break a different thing on Wednesday.*
