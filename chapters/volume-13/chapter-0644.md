# Chapter 644

*Monday the Twenty-Fifth Day of April, a Rota Day, That Cistern Behind That Market Filled for the Hundred and Forty-Fourth Time by Two Men and a Bucket Going Up a Ladder Eleven Times a Little After Eight in the Morning, Nothing Walked on and Nothing Carried Up It on Any Other Errand, the Covers Back on It by a Quarter Past Nine and the Water Up to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, Three Sheets From Three Places Under the Door of That Shed Before That Market Opened, Each Carrying a Day and an Hour and Each a Sheet of Another Community's and Not This One's, Nobody in That Yard Asked Any of the Three Which Position It Stands In and the Count of Positions Not Taken Again, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Seven and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Monday the twenty-fifth day of April, with that cistern at the back of that market charged before that market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at half past five that afternoon.

**A Monday rota day, and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe. That butt was filled by hand for the hundred and forty-fourth time at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips and nothing walked on, nothing carried up that ladder on any other errand, the covers went back on it before a quarter past nine, and it stood at the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and nobody in that market has offered to put it there. Three sheets came under the door of that shed before that market opened, being from three places, and each of the three carries a day and an hour on it, and each of the three is a sheet of another community's and not one of this party's, and about four people in that yard can read each of them. Nobody in that yard asked any of the three which position it stands in, and the count of positions was not taken again this month, and the fifth word on the back of that other page is not a column and carries no figure against any day. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a sheet under a door is not a line on that sheet, and a cistern is not a line on that sheet. The berth fund put a hundred and sixty-eight pence into that yard at about ten this morning and drew the same hundred and sixty-eight out of it at about four, and that slate closed on twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence; four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is what a man is paid for a week, and what is left over is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That fitting-out sheet is carrying one line and is not going to be given a second one this month. What it has cost this harbor is one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings; the whole of what has ever been put into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and what it is short is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today.**

**The nine went on a day each and the fourth of them is a stone under a pump with nothing on it and nothing on it for a very long time. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-eighth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-six days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty-two days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-four. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty-two days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twenty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-sixth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-third day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-four days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole is still carrying five papers, two of them unopened by anybody here. Behind that door upstairs over that market are four lines still, with nothing under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. That boathouse holds one basket, the paper is in it, and nothing else is. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-two days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At a little after eight in the morning the two men who charge that cistern went up that ladder eleven times with a bucket between them before that market opened, and not one of those eleven buckets was walked on, and the covers were back on that butt by a quarter past nine.

It stood to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and it has been off it for longer than anybody in that market can date.

---

At about a quarter past nine that morning, before the boards filled, the man who keeps the leaf found three sheets on the floor of that shed against the door with the latch down, and each of the three had a day and an hour on it and the writing on it was not his.

He put them on the bench along the north wall and did not read any of them out and did not ask anybody for any of them, and about four people in that yard read all three before noon and about five could not read any of them and were not told what was on them.

A man of about forty-two of the second lay who fits cordage brought the third of those three down the coast road on Saturday on the top of a bus, and he said one thing out loud about it at about ten in the morning, in about 174 words, and nobody in that yard asked him a second question about it.

"**I carried that up four days on a bus and I am not going to tell you what is on it, and if you ask me what is on it I will tell you the same three words back at you in the same order, and that is not me being difficult. It is that the moment a man in a shed starts reading out somebody else's sheet, that sheet stops being that community's and starts being a thing the shed has an opinion about, and then somebody over there finds out about it, and the next one does not come.**

**And I will say the other half of it, because you have been fair with me and I have noticed. This yard has never once asked me to keep a thing for you. Not the bag, not the sheet, not the six names anybody keeps asking about. If this yard ever starts asking me to hold something, I will hand it back the same afternoon and I will not be angry about it.**"

Nobody thanked him for four days on a bus and nobody asked him what the three words are.

---

At about two in the afternoon a woman of about thirty of no office came down the boards and looked at the three sheets on that bench along the north wall for about a minute, and she did not touch any of them, and she said one thing to the man who keeps the leaf and one thing only.

"**Do not file those three. A sheet that a yard files is a sheet a yard has an opinion about, and they came here because this yard does not have opinions about other people's pages. Stand them up on that wall where anybody can reach them and let the four of you who can read them read them, and let the rest of this market know there is paper on that wall and not know what is on it.**"

The man who keeps the leaf stood them up on that wall at about half past two and about four people read them again and about five did not, and no position was put against any of the three and no community was asked anything.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 666 words, and nobody in that shed was asked which of three places a sheet came from.

"**A hundred and forty-four charges have gone past that cistern, eleven trips by two men this morning before that market opened, not one of them walked on, the covers back on it before a quarter past nine, and that butt up to the rim of its second hoop until about four this afternoon. The name at the head of that board is still off it and I am not going to say one word about that butt again this month.**

**Three sheets came under the door of this shed before that market opened, from three places, and I did not read any of them out and I did not ask anybody for any of them. They are standing up on that wall now where anybody can reach them, and about four of the people in this yard can read them and about five cannot, and this market knows there is paper on that wall and does not know what is on it, and that is the correct state of those three things this afternoon.**

**And here is what I want said out loud. Nobody in this yard asked any of those three places which position it stands in. Not one. And that count of positions was not taken again today, and I am not going to take it this month, and a page that carries a count of positions is not this yard's page to add up.**

A man of about forty-two of that second lay carried one of those three up four days on a bus and would not say what was on it, and a woman of thirty of no office said do not file those three, and both of them were right and I did not argue with either of them and neither of them has been thanked for it.

That fitting-out sheet is where the twentieth of last month left it, on one line. What that sheet has cost this harbor is one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings; the whole of what has ever gone into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and what it is still short is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. **There was no paying, advancing, cutting back, bettering, striking or closing of anything standing in this harbor today, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence came down no board.**

Those fourteen at the end of that stall were not read out of, and no figure on that sheet moved by one, and none of those fourteen went on and none came off. The fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and no day in this month has a figure against it. No count was taken today of the three columns of that sheet of days, and none is in this slip.

The slate is holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight was paid out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-two days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

One went in on that first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and neither of them was read, and they stand at three hundred and seventy-seven and one thousand seven hundred and fifty-nine, the third of those four is still a column with a boy's hand at the top of it and nobody's name anywhere, and the fourth is one hundred and twenty-five and has never taken a line."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four days on a bus. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and three sheets of three other communities were standing up on that wall with a day's slack in them and nobody's opinion on them.

**THAT CISTERN BEHIND THAT MARKET WAS FILLED BY HAND FOR THE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOURTH TIME AT A LITTLE AFTER EIGHT IN THE MORNING, TWO MEN AND A BUCKET GOING UP THAT LADDER ELEVEN TIMES BEFORE THAT MARKET OPENED, AND NOT ONE BUCKET WAS WALKED ON AND NOTHING WAS CARRIED UP IT ON ANY OTHER ERRAND, THE COVERS BACK ON IT BY A QUARTER PAST NINE AND THE WATER AT THE RIM OF ITS SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. THREE SHEETS CAME UNDER THE DOOR OF THAT SHED FROM THREE PLACES BEFORE THAT MARKET OPENED AND EACH OF THEM CARRIES A DAY AND AN HOUR AND EACH OF THEM IS A SHEET OF ANOTHER COMMUNITY'S AND NOT ONE OF THIS PARTY'S, AND THEY ARE STANDING UP ON THAT WALL WHERE ANYBODY CAN REACH THEM. NOBODY IN THAT YARD ASKED ANY OF THOSE THREE PLACES WHICH POSITION IT STANDS IN, AND THE COUNT OF POSITIONS WAS NOT TAKEN AGAIN. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THERE IS ONE LINE ON THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET AND THERE IS NO SECOND ON IT. THE FIGURE IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, THE FIGURE IT HAS TAKEN IN IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE FIGURE IT IS STILL SHORT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND WHAT THAT SLATE IS HOLDING IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-fifth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day, was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that a butt at the back of that market was filled by hand for the hundred and forty-fourth time at a little after eight in the morning, two men going up that ladder eleven times with a bucket between them before that market opened, nothing walked on and nothing carried up it on any other errand, the covers back on that butt by a quarter past nine, that butt standing to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it, and the name at the head of that rota board still off it, which nobody in that market has offered to put there; and that at about a quarter past nine that morning, before that market opened and before the boards filled, the man who keeps the leaf found three sheets on the floor of that shed against the door with the latch down, and that each of the three carried a day and an hour on it and that the writing on them was not his; and that he put them on the bench along the north wall and did not read any of them out and did not ask anybody for any of them, and that about four people in that yard read all three before noon and that about five could not read any of them and were not told what was on them; and that a man of about forty-two of the second lay who fits cordage brought the third of those three down that coast road on Saturday on the top of a bus and said one thing out loud at about ten in the morning in about 174 words, being that he is not going to tell what is on it, that if he is asked what is on it he will give the same three words back in the same order, that the moment a man in a shed starts reading out somebody else's sheet that sheet stops being that community's and starts being a thing the shed has an opinion about, and that this yard has never once asked him to keep a thing and that if it ever does he will hand it back the same afternoon and will not be angry about it; and that nobody thanked him for four days on a bus and that nobody asked him what the three words are; and that at about two in the afternoon a woman of about thirty of no office came down those boards and looked at those three sheets on that bench for about a minute and did not touch any of them and said one thing to the man who keeps the leaf and one thing only, being that he is not to file those three, that a sheet a yard files is a sheet a yard has an opinion about, that they came here because this yard does not have opinions about other people's pages, and that they are to be stood up on that wall where anybody can reach them with about four of that yard able to read them and the rest of that market knowing there is paper on that wall and not knowing what is on it; and that the man who keeps the leaf stood them up on that wall at about half past two and that about four people read them again and about five did not, and that no position was put against any of the three and that no community was asked anything; and that the fourteen standing at the end of that stall were not read out of, that not one figure on that sheet moved by a single one, and that none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off; and that no count of positions was taken again, that the fifth word is not a column and no day of this month carries a figure against it, and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted on that Monday and that count is not in that slip; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 666 words in about nine people; and that the Morrow Line is in the dormant column of that sheet of days, not struck, still one of the nine, still at Orison; and that a single entry went in on that first column at about six that evening and five on the second, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the forty-fourth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and seventy-seven and the second is one thousand seven hundred and fifty-nine; that third column took nothing and nobody was appointed to it, a boy's hand being at the head of it with his surname on no page and himself not on the water, and the fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that four hundred and fifty pence is where that board has it and has had it all winter, and that no standing thing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Monday.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I did not ask that man of forty-two what was on the third of those three sheets is that he carried it up four days on a bus to a harbor that has not asked him for a thing in a year, and that a yard which begins collecting other communities' pages is a yard that has decided it is a body, and that this yard is not a body and is not going to be one this month; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not answer the woman of thirty of no office before she had finished is that she had a better instruction than mine and that a keeper of a page who talks over a working person has already decided the page is his, and that the three sheets are standing on that wall and not in a drawer and that is her doing and not mine; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason there is no position written against any of those three places is that a community is asked which of the four it is in once and not twice, and that three of them have been asked and two have not, and that the two that have not are named on a page by the name of a place and are not going to be argued with and are not going to be asked a second time this month; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing in this column about that cistern other than that it was filled is that a butt behind a market is a thing that gets filled and that the two men who fill it are not on anybody's crew and are not paid out of that bag, and that a yard that starts putting its own opinions on a pair of arms with a bucket is a yard that has begun to think it is owed something; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on four columns and a bag and not on three sheets of paper standing on a wall is that those three are other people's and this one is mine, and that the day a sheet of another community's goes into a column of this yard's is the day this yard stops being a place where things are said and becomes a place where things are kept.*
