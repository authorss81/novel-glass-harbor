# Chapter 627

*Friday the Eighth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, a Woman of About Twenty-Six of the Third Lay Who Crews Out of Lowfen Bringing Word at About Eleven in the Morning That the Lowfen Reed Is Restricted and Not Dormant from Today and That She Is Not a Member of Anything and That Nobody Here Is to Ask Her a Second Time, the Word Dormant Left Standing Against That Reed on the Sheet of Days and the Word Restricted Put Under It, Three in the Held Column and Three in the Restricted Column and Three in the Dormant Column and Three Plus Three Plus Three Being Nine, a Man of About Thirty-Two and a Woman of Thirty of No Office Not in That Yard and Neither of Them Having Been Sent for, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Friday the eighth day of April, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, and a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Friday with nothing on it but the ordinary trade of a market: that cistern covered and untouched from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. At about eleven this morning a woman of about twenty-six of the third lay who crews out of Lowfen brought word to that yard that the Lowfen Reed is restricted and not dormant from that day, and she said it in her own mouth in about nine people and was not a member of anything and has not joined anything and is not joining anything, and nobody in this harbor is going to ask her a second time about it or about anything else. The third of the nine names on that sheet of days changed at about half past five and the word dormant that had stood against that reed since before the winter is still on that sheet and still readable under the word that has gone in under it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a word brought down a coast road by a person who is not a member of anything is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. That yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at ten in the morning and handed a hundred and sixty-eight pence back out of it at four in the afternoon, and what it kept under that slate was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. A wage of four marks comes to six hundred and forty pence and the slate is six hundred and twelve pence behind that, being three marks eleven shillings. Of that sheet, one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence is what this harbor has paid out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence is everything anybody has put in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and what the two of those leave missing is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and not one penny of this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on again this morning and the fourth of them is the one this harbor has not mentioned to a stranger in nine days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-first night, and there is nothing under it. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty-five days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty-five. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-seven. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-seven days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-nine days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and nineteenth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-sixth. Five papers stand on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened. Those four lines have not left the upper room over that market and there is still nothing written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. The basket in that boathouse is where it has been all month, with the paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole are nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty-five days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven in the morning a woman of about twenty-six of the third lay who crews out of Lowfen came down the boards from the end of that market where she had been standing with a basket, and put the basket down at the flat of that bench stone and said one thing out loud in about nine people.

She had come up that coast road on Wednesday night on the outside of a cart going the other way, and she had not been in that yard before and she had no errand in that market at all, and about nine people were standing round that stone and about four of them could read what was nailed to the wall above it.

"**The Lowfen Reed is restricted and not dormant, from today, and it is not coming back to dormant inside a week and it is not coming back at all until six of us say so in one of our own mouths.**

**And I am not here as a member of anything.** I have not joined anything, I have not signed anything, and the charter that came round in the winter has my name nowhere on it and I would like it to keep it that way. I am here because you wrote down a word about that reed in the winter and that word was wrong in the way that matters, being dormant when we were not asleep but stood there refusing, and somebody in that harbor has carried it in a column ever since and did not know it was carrying it.

**A run that is dormant is a run that is not there. A run that is restricted is a run that is there and will not take you.** We are there. We are at that reed with two boats and a punt and a boy of fifteen who is not on that number, and if you print the word restricted against us then a stranger knows there is something at that reed and if you print the word dormant then a stranger is told to go and look for a thing that is not missing. **That is the whole of what I came four days on the outside of a cart to say.**

I am going back down that road on Monday and nobody is to write to me about this and nobody is to come up that coast and ask me a second time and I am not to be asked what my six are called."

That is about three hundred words and not one person in that yard agreed with her out loud, argued with her, thanked her or told her she was wrong, and about four people heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told at all.

---

At about half past five the man who keeps the leaf put the word restricted under the word dormant against the Lowfen Reed on that sheet of days, with Friday the eighth of April and the hour of eleven against the new word, and he did not strike the word dormant out.

The word dormant is still on that sheet and it is still readable and it sits above the word that is true now, and nobody in that shed is entitled to take it off, and the only hand that could is a hand six hundred yards away in reed channels and this yard has not written to that hand and is not going to.

**The held column of that sheet now carries three names and the restricted column carries three and the dormant column carries three, and three plus three plus three is nine, and there are nine names on it and they can be read off by a stranger with a leaf and a pencil and counted.**

---

At about four in the afternoon a man of about thirty-two was at that stone and a woman of thirty of no office was not, and neither of them had been sent for, and about four people in that yard noticed one of those two facts and about five noticed neither of them.

He had his hands on the back of a boat that was not his, and he did not say anything about the reed and nobody asked him about the reed, and about an hour afterwards he went and sat down at the end of that quay and did not go in the water.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and ninety-four words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a person is allowed to carry on a page.

"**Two words went into one line of that sheet this evening and the first of them is still on it, and I am going to say why in nine people because this is the last place in this harbor where anything still gets argued about.**

What stands at the head of this slip in my own hand is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not shifted off that mark since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. What is lying under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**That woman said in about four words what this harbor has been getting wrong since the winter and she said it standing on a basket.** The word in her mouth for a run that is not there and the word in her mouth for a run that is there and will not take a boat are two different words, and this yard has been carrying the wrong one in a column since the winter. That is not her opinion and it is not a position against anybody's, and it is the plainest thing anybody has put in that yard since the fourth question on Monday a fortnight ago, and she is not a member of anything and has not joined anything and there is nothing on that sheet which can make her one.

**And I have not written her reasons on that board and I am not going to.** She gave four of them and three of them are about boats and a boy of fifteen and a road that a cart takes four days each way on, and one of them is about a stranger knowing there is something at that reed. That last one is about this harbor and about a printer and it is not about a crew, and if I put it on that sheet then that sheet is no longer carrying what nine crews say and it is carrying what a keeper of a leaf has understood about nine crews, and that is a different object and it decides something and this board decides nothing.

So she said it and I wrote one word under another word and the old word is still legible, and about four people in that nine can read those two words and about five cannot and those five were told by a man who was told by a woman in that order.

The fourteen at the end of that stall were not read out of today. No figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen has been added to and none taken off, and the count of positions is not being taken again and the fifth word on the back of the other page is still not a column.

What that leaf carried tonight was one entry on the first column and five on the second, put there at about six this evening, and I did not read them out. Three hundred and sixty stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and seventy-four against the second, my third is empty, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four words about a word. That shed stood empty by about six with its door open on a slip nobody was on, and that sheet of days stood against that wall with three in each of its three columns, and the words dormant and restricted sat one above the other on one line of it with both of them readable.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX OF THE THIRD LAY WHO CREWS OUT OF LOWFEN CAME DOWN THOSE BOARDS AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING WITH A BASKET AND PUT IT DOWN AT THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE AND SAID IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN ABOUT 300 WORDS THAT THE LOWFEN REED IS RESTRICTED AND NOT DORMANT FROM THAT DAY, AND THAT IT IS NOT COMING BACK TO DORMANT UNTIL SIX OF THEM SAY SO IN ONE OF THEIR OWN MOUTHS, AND THAT SHE IS NOT A MEMBER OF ANYTHING AND HAS JOINED NOTHING AND SIGNED NOTHING AND WANTS HER NAME NOWHERE ON ANY PAGE. SHE CAME UP THAT COAST ROAD ON WEDNESDAY NIGHT ON THE OUTSIDE OF A CART GOING THE OTHER WAY. THE WORD RESTRICTED WENT UNDER THE WORD DORMANT AGAINST THAT REED ON THAT SHEET OF DAYS AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR OF ELEVEN AGAINST IT, AND THE WORD DORMANT WAS NOT STRUCK OUT AND IS STILL ON THAT SHEET AND IS STILL READABLE. THE THREE COLUMNS OF THAT SHEET NOW CARRY THREE, THREE AND THREE, AND THREE PLUS THREE PLUS THREE IS NINE. NO REASON SHE GAVE WAS WRITTEN ON THAT BOARD. A GAP WAS NOT READ OUT OF IN TEN DAYS AND THE FIGURES ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN DID NOT SHIFT BY ONE. TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IS ALL THAT HAS EVER COME INTO THAT MONEY SHEET, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, IS WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY. THE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**
