# Chapter 603

*Tuesday the Fifteenth Day of March, the Seventy-Third Wage Tuesday, the Column Read Out on the Lid of That Step at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes by a Man of About Thirty-One of the Second Lay Who Has Read It Seventy-Three Times, Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-Third Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, a Clean Sheet With Four Columns and Four Names of Places Down the Side of It Made in That Yard in the Afternoon and Put at the End of That Stall Beside the Sheet of the Fourteen Gaps and Named Apart from It Out Loud, Two of Those Four Rows Carried Forward in the Refused Column and Two of Them Left Empty, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the fifteenth day of March, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, and about eleven of them who could not see a line of what was on the step.

**A wage Tuesday and the seventy-third of them, and no rota day and no wipe, and that cistern charged on no day of that kind, with its covers sitting on it unbroken from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon. The column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the seventy-third week stands fallen due at the close of that day, behind the seventy-second and behind the seventy-first, and the only person in that market who said a word about it was the man who read it. In any hand and in any line, the sheet that carries the money took nothing on that Tuesday and gave nothing up, and a page of positions was made in that yard in the afternoon and is not a line on it. A day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard at four o'clock and the same figure, one mark and eightpence, is in it at ten in the morning, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, when the day shut, against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, and that is three marks eleven shillings, being six hundred and twelve pence, short. That sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that gap has not been carried to that harbor's door and is not going to be. Nobody has opened a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and seventy-three wage Tuesdays have been paid for out of nobody's pocket, and a clean sheet with four columns on it has been paid for out of a counter off that market and not out of anybody's bag.**

**These are the nine that this harbor has stopped being surprised by, and they are not a list of anybody. Two objects of nobody's on that crown have now been six hundred and forty-one days without a name against them. A man of about fifty-one has not said a word to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and forty-three days. The bench under that pump went another night without the pump being under it and that is the four hundred and seventh. A bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and three days. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its hundred and ninety-fifth. Two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-five days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eightieth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twelfth day, and it has not been read out in a working place in a fortnight. Five papers are on that board at the head of the mole and two of them have never been opened, and the basket in that boathouse still has a paper in it and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole at about ten this morning. A man of about twenty-nine has a hundred and one days on the side of his head that hears out of the left and it is not a gift and it has not been improved and he is not on the water.**

---

The column was read out on that step from ten o'clock and it took four minutes, and it was read by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday and has now read it seventy-three times, and about forty people were on the boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of the lid of that step from where they were standing.

He read twenty-six lines off that lid and the last one came to one hundred and four marks, and there he stopped, and he said the figure and he said that the figure had not moved, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and that is what he says.

The seventy-third week stands fallen due at the close of that day. Nobody in that market said a word about it but the man reading it, and about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told.

---

And in the afternoon of that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf made a clean sheet in that yard and put it at the end of the end stall, and the two of them stand side by side there now, and they are not the same sheet and they are not in the same column and they do not carry the same thing.

The new one is ruled in four columns and headed OPENED, REFUSED, RESTRICTED and WITHDRAWN, and down the side of it are four names of places and no name of any person, and they are the four communities on the page of the third of last month, being this harbor, the place about a mile inside the port four days down that coast, that port itself, and the place about five miles up this coast road.

Two of those four rows are carried forward in the REFUSED column. Those two were asked on the third of last month and said no in their own mouths and were named on that page as having not agreed, and they are carried forward as a position and not as a community that has to be counted again, and nobody in that yard is going to ask either of them anything.

The other two rows are empty this afternoon. One of them is a community four days down that coast, and about a mile inside a port, which has agreed to the method and which nobody in this harbor has asked for a word, and a boat to it is a day and a night against four days each way for a cart. The other row is this harbor, and this harbor has not been asked either, and nobody was in a hurry about it.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands came up those boards at about half past three and asked whether that sheet under the stone meant that a man's working day was now fixed to a column, and a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards answered him in about eighty-five words.

"**No, and you are the one who put three separate things in the wrong order on Saturday.** It names no person. It says nothing about anybody's hours. And a word standing against a day on a piece of paper that nobody in this harbor has been asked about is a thing a stranger can be angry at, and that is better than a thing a stranger cannot find, and neither of those two is a wage column and neither of them has ever been one."

Nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her and the man of about thirty-three went back down the boards without saying anything else, and about four people heard both of those and the rest of that market was not told.

---

At half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and five words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a page of positions is worth.

"**Say the number first and say it plain: ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, which is six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** That gap is where it stood on Saturday and it is where it stands tonight, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and the cistern was not charged and the covers were on it all day, and a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of this yard at four o'clock, and it is on no line of that sheet, and it is not a Tuesday.

**The column on that step was read at about ten in four minutes and it is twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move, and there are twenty envelopes in a chest that has been shut from ten until four today and every Tuesday since the twenty-fifth of January.** The seventy-third week stands behind the seventy-second. A wage Tuesday is the day this harbor finds out what it owes and finding out is not paying, and I have said that in this shed before and I am not going to improve on it by saying it again in different words.

**A clean sheet with four columns went at the end of that stall this afternoon and it stands beside the sheet with the fourteen gaps on it, and those are two sheets and not one, and I said the name of both of them out loud twice while I was putting it there.** The two that have not agreed are carried in the refused column and are not going to be asked anything by anybody in this yard. The other two rows are empty this afternoon and an empty row is not a zero, and a zero is a figure, and an empty row is not a figure at all. Nothing on that page is anybody's business tonight except the word on it.

My third column is empty and the fourth is where it has been for a season, one hundred and twenty-five, and it has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. One line went on to the first column and five to the second at about six this evening, and I have not read that leaf out loud and am not going to on any day of this week, and that makes three days of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for seventy-three readings of a column, and by about six that shed was standing empty with the door of it open on a slip with no name written on it.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES ON THAT TUESDAY, BEING THE SEVENTY-THIRD TIME, AND IT STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND IT DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-THIRD WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A CLEAN SHEET WAS MADE IN THAT YARD IN THE AFTERNOON AND PUT AT THE END OF THAT STALL BESIDE THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN GAPS, AND IT CARRIES FOUR COLUMNS AND FOUR NAMES OF PLACES AND NO NAME OF ANY PERSON, AND TWO OF THOSE FOUR ROWS ARE CARRIED FORWARD IN THE REFUSED COLUMN AND ARE NOT TO BE ASKED AGAIN AND TWO OF THEM ARE EMPTY, AND AN EMPTY ROW IS NOT A FIGURE. THE MONEY ON THE OTHER SHEET IS UNMOVED, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND A SHORTFALL OF NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND A HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE WENT INTO THAT YARD AND A HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE CAME OUT OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the fifteenth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday and was the seventy-third of them and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the covers were on it from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday and has read it seventy-three times, and that about forty people were on those boards and about eleven of them could not see a line of the lid of that step from where they were standing; and that he read twenty-six lines off that lid and the last one came to one hundred and four marks and there he stopped, and that he said the figure and said that the figure had not moved, and that behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four; and that the seventy-third week stands fallen due at the close of the said day, behind the seventy-second and behind the seventy-first, and that the only person in that market who said a word about it was the man who read it, and that about four people heard him and the rest of that market was not told; and that in the afternoon of that day the man who keeps the leaf made a clean sheet in that yard and put it at the end of that end stall, and that it stands there side by side with the sheet carrying the fourteen gaps, and that the two of them are not the same sheet and are not in the same column and do not carry the same thing, and that he said the name of both out loud twice while he was putting it there; and that the new sheet is ruled in four columns headed opened, refused, restricted and withdrawn, and that down the side of it are four names of places and no name of any person, being this harbor, the place about a mile inside the port four days down that coast, that port itself, and the place about five miles up this coast road; and that two of those four rows are carried forward in the refused column, being the two that were asked on the third of last month and said no in their own mouths and were named on that page as having not agreed, and that they are carried forward as a position and not as a community that has to be counted again and that nobody in that yard is going to ask either of them anything; and that the other two rows are empty this afternoon, one of them being a community about a mile inside a port four days down that coast which has agreed to the method and which nobody in this harbor has asked for a word, and that a boat to it is a day and a night against four days each way for a cart, and the other row being this harbor, which has not been asked either and that nobody was in a hurry about; and that at about half past three a man of about thirty-three who hires hands came up those boards and asked whether that sheet under the stone meant a man's working day was fixed to a column, and that a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards answered in about eighty-five words that it names no person and says nothing about anybody's hours and that a word standing against a day on a paper nobody has been asked about is a thing a stranger can be angry at, and that neither of those two things is a wage column; and that nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her and about four people heard both and the rest of that market was not told; and that the berth fund put a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, into that yard at about ten in the morning and took the same figure out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that of it twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was still under the slate when that Tuesday was done with, and that a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and that six hundred and twelve pence of it, being three marks eleven shillings, is what is not there; and that that money sheet took nothing in any hand in any line on that Tuesday and gave nothing up, and that what that sheet holds of the whole of the year is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence gone out of it, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence come into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence of it, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, has never been carried to that harbor's door and is not going to be; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and thirty-six and the second at one thousand five hundred and fifty-four, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the third day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, and that the run before it was fifty days long and was counted from Saturday the twenty-second day of January, and that the figure this one starts from is the figure that one finished on.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I have written that the man reading the column said the figure and said that the figure had not moved, and did not say anything else, is that a man of about thirty-one has read that column seventy-three times and that on the sixty-first of them he said the second half of that sentence and on the seventy-third he did not, and that I have not asked him about that and I am not going to, and that the day a reader stops saying the second half of a sentence is a day a reader has decided that everybody in that market has stopped needing to hear it, and that is a Tuesday morning and not a Wednesday; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason I ruled those four columns this afternoon and not at the end of the week is that a page of positions with nothing in three of its rows is a harder thing to argue with than one with something in them, and that a man of about thirty-three came up those boards at half past three and argued with the empty version and got nowhere, and that if the page had been full on Monday he would have argued with a figure instead of a column, and that I would rather he argues with a column; and that at about half past three the reason I have not put the name of a place against a row that is empty is that the four names down the side of that page are the names of the four communities and not the names of anybody who lives in them, and that the day anybody asks me to put a man's name against a row I am going to say no and I would like it written down here that I said no before it happened and not after; and that at about half past five the reason I have not said the four figures of that leaf out loud this evening, when this is the first Tuesday of a month and about nine people are in this shed, is that saying them out loud is a thing this harbor did once in January and it cost four hundred yards of people being told a number they could not check, and that a slip at half past five is not a place to begin something; and that at about six o'clock the reason the two sheets at that stall are named apart out loud twice a week is that in eleven weeks the whole of the argument this harbor has had with itself has been about two things being confused for each other, and that every one of those confusions cost somebody a day, and that naming them is four seconds of work.*
