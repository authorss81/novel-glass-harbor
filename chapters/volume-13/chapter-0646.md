# Chapter 646

*Wednesday the Twenty-Seventh Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered from Eight Until Four and Untouched, a Parcel Wrapped in Canvas and Addressed to a Trade and Not to a Person Brought to a Chandler's Counter in That Front Street in the First Hour of the Morning and Carried Down at About Eleven to the Flat of That Bench Stone and Put Down There in the Open in Front of About Twenty People and Not Opened and Not Read Out and Not Nailed to Any Board, a Man of About Thirty-Two Out on the West Basin Until Four in the Afternoon and Reading the Face of That Paper Himself at About Half Past Four, the Name at the Foot of It on the Paper and in No Mouth, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Fitting-Out Sheet Carrying Its One Line and Taking No Second and Taking None for a Paper Either, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Nine and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Wednesday the twenty-seventh day of April, with that market trading all day under a sky that had gone back to blue about seven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Wednesday with nothing standing against it: that butt covered from eight until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. In the first hour of this morning a boy from the post cart carried a parcel into that chandler's counter in the front street, and it was wrapped in canvas and it was addressed to a trade and not to a person, and the woman of about forty-four behind that counter cut the string on it with her own knife and did not read the name at the foot of what was inside it, and she carried the whole of it down those boards at about eleven in the morning and put it down on the flat of that bench stone in the open, and about twenty people stood round that stone inside a quarter of an hour and not one of them read it and not one of them was asked to. It is a document and not a man and no man came with it and none is going to. A man of about thirty-two of this harbor was out on the west basin with a boat until about four this afternoon and read the face of that paper himself at about half past four, in about nine people, and he stopped where the paper stops, and the name at the foot of it is on the paper and is in no mouth. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a day on which a paper comes ashore is not a line on that sheet, and a document is not a cost and a refusal is not a cost. That yard was paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning for a day with a boat and two men, and it paid a hundred and sixty-eight out of itself at about four, and what it finished with under that slate was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence; a man who sets that against four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings, and he is right. That sheet of money is carrying one line and is not going to be given a second one this month. What it has cost this harbor is one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, the whole of what has ever been put into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and what it is short is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today. There is a tin in that shed and it has not been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine went on a day each and the sixth of them is a sheet of six lines standing at the back of an end stall with nothing under either end of it and nothing under it for a very long time. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twenty-third day. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-eight days. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-six days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty-four days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty-four days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-six. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and fiftieth night. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-eighth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-fifth day. Five papers stand on that board and this harbor has not opened two of them at any time this year. There are still four lines in that upper room over that market and they are still behind that door, with nothing under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and the basket is empty of everything else. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon, nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and not a word has been said in this harbor about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-four days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

In the first hour of the morning a boy of about fifteen from the post cart brought a parcel into the chandler's shop in that front street and put it on that counter, and it was wrapped in canvas and tied with a cord and the name on it was the name of a trade.

The woman of about forty-four behind that counter cut the cord with her own knife and opened it in her own shop, and she did not read the name at the foot of what was in it, and she told the boy to leave, and the boy left.

At about eleven in the morning she carried the whole of it down those boards in both arms and put it down on the flat of that bench stone in the open, and about twenty people were standing round that stone inside a quarter of an hour.

Nobody read it. Nobody in that yard read it out, nobody turned it over, and about four of those twenty could have read it and did not, and about eleven of them could not read it at all and were not told what was on it and were not told that there was a name at the foot of it.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon, with about nine people round that stone, a man of about thirty-two of this harbor came back off the west basin with his hands grey and read the face of that paper himself from end to end, twice, and stopped where the paper stops, and said one thing out loud about it in about 205 words.

"**It says what it did. I am not going to say the rest of it this afternoon because the rest of it is not mine to say and because I have not worked out yet what the limits of it are, and a man who states the limits of a paper before he has read it to nine people is doing it for himself and not for them.**

**The plain part is the only part I am going to say out loud today. It is a document and not a man, and no man came with it, and the name at the foot of it is on the paper and is not going to be read by anybody in this yard including me. That is not a courtesy. That is the rule and it is the rule that has been running all winter, and this paper is not going to be the thing that finds out what happens when it is not followed.**

I will not read it out and neither will you and neither will any of the eleven of you who cannot read it, and that is not a criticism of any of you. That is a description of what this coast is.**"

A woman of about thirty of no office stood at the other side of that stone and answered him, in about 127 words, and about four people heard both of them and the rest of that market was not told.

"**Then do not read it out, and do not stand there looking at it as if reading it out were a thing you had refused to do. You have read it twice and I have not read it once, and this yard is going to spend this afternoon deciding that the person who reads a thing out loud is the person who can live with what comes out of their own mouth.**

**And it is not going to be you, and I am not going to be the one who says why. I have said why on a bench stone three times this spring and I am not saying it a fourth time in a place where it can be written down as though it were settled.**"

Nobody said either of them was right and nobody said either of them was wrong and nobody thanked either of them and they went out of that yard together and they are still married.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net stood at her own stall at the end of that market and worked, and nobody in that harbor came to that stall and asked her a single thing, and nobody asked her whether she wanted to know what the paper on the bench stone said.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 561 words, and nobody in that shed was asked to say the name at the foot of a paper out loud.

"**A paper came ashore in the first hour of this morning addressed to a trade and not to a person, and the woman with the counter opened it in her own shop and did not read the name at the foot of it, and she brought it down here at about eleven and put it on that stone in the open in front of about twenty people, and not one of them read it and not one of them was asked to.

**It is on that stone now. It has a seal at the foot of it and a name under that seal, and that name is on the paper and is in no mouth, and it is not going to be in a mouth this month, and I want that said in nine people tonight while I still have the nerve of saying it.**

**Nobody read it out today. Nobody read it out twice. I want that in this slip in those words, because there is going to be a day when somebody says this harbor had a paper in its hands for a whole afternoon and did nothing with it, and the answer to that is that a document is not a cost and a refusal is not a cost and a paper on a stone is not a line on the money sheet, and that sheet did not move and carries one line and is not going to carry a second this month.**

Two people who are married to each other said about forty words each to each other at that stone at about half past four and neither of them gave anything up and nobody arbitrated and nobody thanked them and they are still married.

No one read out of the fourteen at that stall and no figure on that sheet went up or down by one, and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. The count of positions was not taken again this month, and the fifth word on the back of that other page is not a column. The fifth word on the back of that other page is not a column, and it is not a fifth column, and nobody has asked anybody for it. That count of the three columns was not taken today, is not in this slip, and is not going to be taken on any other day but one.

Beneath that slate this evening there are twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight was paid out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-four days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

Five went into that second column at about six this evening and one into the first, and nobody in that yard has read either of them to anybody, and the page carries three hundred and seventy-nine and one thousand seven hundred and sixty-nine, a third column empty from end to end, and one hundred and twenty-five in the fourth where it has always been."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a parcel that came to a trade and not to a person. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the paper was still lying on the flat of that bench stone where twenty people had stood round it in the morning.

**A PARCEL ADDRESSED TO A TRADE AND NOT TO A PERSON CAME INTO THAT CHANDLER'S COUNTER IN THE FRONT STREET IN THE FIRST HOUR OF THAT MORNING AND WAS CARRIED DOWN TO THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND PUT DOWN THERE IN THE OPEN, AND ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE STOOD ROUND IT INSIDE A QUARTER OF AN HOUR AND NOT ONE OF THEM READ IT AND NOT ONE OF THEM WAS ASKED TO. IT IS A DOCUMENT AND NOT A MAN AND NO MAN CAME WITH IT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO READ THE FACE OF IT HIMSELF AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND STOPPED WHERE THE PAPER STOPS, AND THE NAME AT THE FOOT OF IT IS ON THE PAPER AND IS IN NO MOUTH AND IS NOT GOING TO BE READ BY ANYBODY IN THIS YARD. NOBODY READ IT OUT TODAY AND NOBODY READ IT OUT TWICE, AND A DOCUMENT IS NOT A COST AND A REFUSAL IS NOT A COST. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT SHEET HAS CARRIED ONE LINE SINCE THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTIETH OF LAST MONTH AND IT HAS NOT TAKEN A SECOND. ON THAT ONE LINE STAND THREE FIGURES, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND THAT SLATE IS HOLDING TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not read that paper in nine people when twenty of them were standing round it is that reading a paper out loud is a job and a job has to be somebody's, and that on Tuesday a working woman of that front street said so in front of nine people and that I wrote it down, and that a keeper of a page who does a working person's job in that person's place has taken the only thing that person was going to be asked for; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason the name at the foot of that paper is in this slip and is not in anybody's mouth is that it is on the paper and that is where it is going to stay, and that a name that gets repeated in a ledger is a name that has started travelling, and that this yard has enough things travelling that it has not chosen; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I did not stand between those two people at that stone is that what was in it is his work and her record and that neither of those is mine to referee, and that this is the fourth time this spring that this yard has had to stand four paces off, and that four paces is the correct distance and that the fifth time I am going to notice it; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason there is nothing in this column about a parcel being opened in a shop is that it was opened by the person it was addressed to and that what a shopkeeper does with a parcel sent to her trade is her own business and not this harbor's, and that the harbor's business begins at about eleven o'clock on a bench stone and not before; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on four columns and a bag and not on a paper lying on a stone is that a paper on a stone is not this yard's page and that the day this yard reads another party's paper into its own columns is the day the two of them are the same page with two names on it.*
