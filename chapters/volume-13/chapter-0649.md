# Chapter 649

*Saturday the Thirtieth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered from Nine Until Four and Untouched, the Limits of That Paper Written Into the First Column of That Leaf This Morning in the Yard's Own Words and Not Read Out of It and Not Taken Out of It, about Five of the People Who Come to That Yard Not Being Able to Read a Column, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Standing at the Back of That Market at About Two in the Afternoon and Not Being Asked One Thing, a Woman of About Thirty-Nine Who Mends Net Asked Nothing at About Four, the Reading of That Leaf Not Done and Not Due Before the First of May, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Eighty-Two and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Saturday the thirtieth day of April, with that market quiet by eleven because everybody who could get away went out on the water with the tide, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Saturday with nothing standing against it: that butt covered from nine until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. The limits of that paper went into the first column of that leaf this morning in the yard's own words, and were not read out of it and not taken out of it, and the paper itself is still lying on the bench in that shed with the fold in it and the name at the foot of that seal is on it and in no mouth. The reading of that leaf was not done today and is not due before the first of May, and when it is done it will be done in this yard in the open with all four of those columns and not with three of them, and it will be done twice, because about five of the people who come to this yard cannot read a column and are not to be told what is in one. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a paper that was read out in a market yesterday is not a line on that sheet, and a day on which a thing is true is not a line on that sheet. The berth fund put a hundred and sixty-eight pence into that yard at about ten this morning and took a hundred and sixty-eight out of it at about four this afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the day's end, which against four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, leaves a man six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. One line stands on that sheet of money and there will not be a second this month, and its three figures are one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of this fortnight has come out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the second of them is the only one of the nine that is a man who has not been asked for a figure in this harbor in twelve months. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-nine. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty-seven days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty-seven days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and fifty-third night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and seventy-one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twenty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and forty-first. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-nine days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened. The fourth of those four lines has never had anything written under it, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. There is a basket in that boathouse, there is a paper in that basket, and there is nothing else in it. Nine hulls lie off that mole head at about two sea miles, they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-seven days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf took a clean sheet and copied onto it, in his own words, what a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter and a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons had said about that paper in the last two days, and he put the day and the hour against it, and he read it to nobody.

He put that sheet on the bench along the north wall of that shed with the other three sheets that came under the door on Monday, and the four of them stood side by side where anybody can reach them, and about four people in that yard can read all four and about five can read none of them.

Nobody in that yard asked him to read it out, nobody in that yard asked him to take anything off it, and nothing came off it.

---

At about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-four of the second lay stood at the back of that market where the boards meet the yard and watched about four people reading a sheet on a wall without saying a word, and he can count the marks on a line and cannot read a word, and he came to this market on the twenty-ninth of January and said in a market book that he would rather be asked than not asked, and it is written in that book in his own hand and nobody in this harbor has asked him one thing since.

Nobody asked him this afternoon. Nobody asked him what is on those four sheets and nobody asked him whether he agrees with any of it, and about four people in that yard noticed that he was standing there and none of them said anything to him about it, and that is the sixth month of that standing about.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net stood at her own stall at the end of that market and worked, and nobody in this harbor came to that stall and asked her a single thing, and nobody in this harbor has asked her for her particular since the twenty-fourth of January and nobody is going to this month.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 702 words, and nobody in that shed was asked to read that leaf out before it is due.

"**Yesterday a paper was read out once in this market and the name under the seal on it was not read, and this morning I wrote down in my own words what two working people said about it, and I read that sheet to nobody, and I put it on that wall with the other three, and I took nothing off it.**

**I am not going to say what that paper settles, because it settles nothing in this yard and everything it settles is a hundred miles from here and about thirteen years old. I am going to say what this market did with it, which is that about twenty people stood in the open and about eleven of them could not read a line of it and stayed anyway.**

**The reading of that leaf is not done and is not due before the first of May, and when it is done it will be done in this yard in the open with all four of those columns and not three of them, and it will be done twice, because about five of the people who come here cannot read a column and are not going to be told what is in one this afternoon or any other.**

And a man of about thirty-four of that second lay stood at those boards at two this afternoon watching four people read a sheet on a wall, and he cannot read a word of it and he can count the marks, and he said in a market book in the winter that he would rather be asked than not asked, and nobody in this harbor has asked him and nobody asked him this afternoon.

The money sheet did not move and it carries one line and it is not going to take a second this month, and a day on which a thing turns out to be true is not a line on that sheet, because a sheet is a list of what eleven people did with their own money. One hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence is what that sheet of money has cost this harbor, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence is the whole of what has ever been put into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence is what it is short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. **Nothing that stands in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed this Saturday, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is still the figure on that board.**

Not one of the fourteen standing at the end of that stall was read out of, no figure on that sheet went up or down, and none of them was added to and none taken off. The count of positions was not taken again, and nobody in that yard asked any of the four places which of the four it is in. The fifth word is not a column, and no day of this month has a figure standing against it. No sum of those three columns was taken today, and it is not written in this slip.

Under that slate at six o'clock on that Saturday there are twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-seven days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

A single entry went on that first column at about six and five on the second, and the reading of it is not done, and the figures are three hundred and eighty-two and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-four, and the third of those four columns is empty and the fourth is one hundred and twenty-five and has not taken a line in fifty days."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a sheet put on a wall unasked. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and four sheets of four other people stood side by side on that wall, and the man of about thirty-four had gone home and the woman of about thirty-nine was still at her stall at six.

**THE LIMITS OF THAT PAPER WERE WRITTEN INTO THE FIRST COLUMN OF THAT LEAF ON THAT SATURDAY MORNING IN THE YARD'S OWN WORDS AND WERE NOT READ OUT OF IT AND NOT TAKEN OUT OF IT, AND THAT SHEET WENT ON A WALL IN THAT SHED BESIDE THREE SHEETS FROM THREE OTHER PLACES, EACH OF THEM CARRYING A DAY AND AN HOUR, AND ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT YARD CAN READ ALL FOUR AND ABOUT FIVE CAN READ NONE OF THEM. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THE SECOND LAY, WHO CAN COUNT THE MARKS ON A LINE AND CANNOT READ A WORD AND WHO SAID IN A MARKET BOOK IN THE WINTER THAT HE WOULD RATHER BE ASKED THAN NOT ASKED, STOOD AT THOSE BOARDS AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS NOT ASKED ONE THING. THE READING OF THAT LEAF IS NOT DONE AND IS NOT DUE BEFORE THE FIRST OF MAY, AND IT WILL BE DONE IN THIS YARD IN THE OPEN WITH ALL FOUR OF THOSE COLUMNS AND TWICE. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND, AND ITS THREE FIGURES ARE ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND UNDER THAT SLATE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirtieth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was no rota day and no wage Tuesday and no wipe, and that no charge was put on that cistern upon it, the covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that that market was quiet by eleven because everybody who could get away had gone out on the water with the tide; and that at about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf took a clean sheet and copied onto it in his own words what a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter and a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons had said about that paper in the last two days, and that he put the day and the hour against it, and that he read it to nobody; and that he put that sheet on the bench along the north wall of that shed with the other three sheets that came under that door on the Monday, and that the four of them stood side by side where anybody can reach them, and that about four people in that yard can read all four and about five can read none of them, and that nobody in that yard asked him to read it out, that nobody in that yard asked him to take anything off it, and that nothing came off it; and that at about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-four of the second lay stood at the back of that market where the boards meet that yard and watched about four people reading a sheet on a wall without saying a word, and that he can count the marks on a line and cannot read a word, and that he came to that market on the twenty-ninth of January and said in that market's book that he would rather be asked than not asked, and that it is written in that book in his own hand and that nobody in this harbor has asked him one thing since; and that nobody asked him that afternoon, that nobody asked him what is on those four sheets, that nobody asked him whether he agrees with any of it, and that about four people in that yard noticed he was standing there and that none of them said anything to him about it; and that at about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing and has not been asked for her particular since the twenty-fourth of January and is not going to be this month; and that the reading of that leaf was not done that day and is not due before the first of May, and that when it is done it will be done in that yard in the open with all four of those columns and not three of them, and that it will be done twice because about five of the people who come to that yard cannot read a column and are not going to be told what is in one; and that the paper that was read out on the Friday is still lying on the bench in that shed with the fold in it and the name at the foot of that seal is on it and in no mouth; and that nothing was read out of the fourteen at the end of that stall, that not one figure on that sheet went up or down, and that not one of those fourteen was added to and not one was taken off; and that the count of positions was not taken again, that the fifth word is not a column and no figure stands against it for any day, and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted on that Saturday and that count is not in that slip and is not going to be; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 702 words in about nine people; and that the Morrow Line stands in the dormant column of that sheet of days, not struck, still one of the nine, and still at Orison; and that a single entry went in on that first column at about six that evening and five went in on the second, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the forty-ninth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and eighty-two and the second is one thousand seven hundred and eighty-four; the third of those four is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand being at the head of it with his surname on no page and himself not on the water, and the fourth of them is unmoved at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that four hundred and fifty pence stands on that board where it stood on the first of the fifty days, and that nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed this Saturday.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I wrote two working people's limits down in my own words and not in theirs is that theirs are on a market stone and on a dock and this is a leaf that gets read in ten years by somebody with a cold, and that a yard which keeps other people's exact words does not need a keeper, it needs a clerk, and this yard is not going to become a clerk; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not go across to that man of thirty-four and say the two words he wrote in that market book is that he wrote them in a book in the winter and that a book is where a man says a thing when he does not expect to be answered, and that answering it in a market would be answering it in the wrong place and would make the book a request instead of a record; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason there is nothing in this column about that woman of thirty-nine is that the last thing this yard wrote about her was a sentence about how often it mentions her, and that a second column that keeps turning into a column about one person is a column about one person; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason the money sheet is not going to take a second line this month is that a sheet is a list of what eleven people did with their own money, and that a thing turning out to be true is not something eleven people did with their own money, and that a sheet that takes a line for it is a sheet that has started keeping opinions; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on two figures and not on four is that four are due tomorrow and that a slip which gets ahead of its own page is a slip that has started hurrying, and that this yard has been in a hurry for a fortnight and this is the first day it has come out of it.*
