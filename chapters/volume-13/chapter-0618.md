# Chapter 618

*Wednesday the Thirtieth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, at About Four in the Afternoon at the Flat of That Bench Stone in About Nine People of Whom About Four Can Read, a Woman of Thirty of No Office and a Man of About Thirty-Two Arguing Out Loud in Front of All of Them About How Much Danger a Page Is Allowed to Carry and About the Fourth Thing in Two Drafts Written on Monday, in About Three Hundred and Nine Words and About Two Hundred and Twenty-Eight Words and About One Hundred and Eighty-Six Words and Nobody Arbitrating and Nobody Agreeing Out Loud, Two Lines Going on That Page at About Half Past Five With One Under the Other and Neither Struck and No Signature and No Agreement, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-One and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Wednesday the thirtieth day of March, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, and in a yard below it with a bench stone in it and about nine people in that yard at about four in the afternoon and about nine of them again at half past five.

**A Wednesday with nothing scheduled on it: that cistern covered and untouched from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. Two people who are married to each other argued out loud this afternoon at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people and neither of them gave anything up and nobody in that nine arbitrated and nobody agreed out loud, and the two of them are still married and are going to be married at the end of this month. Nothing went onto the money sheet in any hand and nothing came off it, and an argument held in the open in a market yard is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. A hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence, arrived in that yard at about ten and left it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was under that slate at the end of the day, and that is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, off a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. On the out side of that money sheet stands one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, which is six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; on the in side of it stands ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, which is sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and the space between those two sides is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, which is six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing went out of that bag today except a day's pay out of the berth fund at the figure in chalk.**

**The nine durations went on again and the fourth of them is the one this harbor has looked at most often. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and sixteen days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and eighteen. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-six. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-second night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and tenth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-seventh. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-eight days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and in that boathouse the basket stands with the paper still in it and no hand has gone into that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and sixteen days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office said a thing at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people that she had been carrying since Friday, and a man of about thirty-two answered her, and she answered him back, and neither of them lowered their voice and neither of them said anything they had not already said to themselves somewhere else first.

She went first and it was about three hundred and nine words.

"**I have taught that method out loud on those boards and I am the one who reads its page twice, and I am telling nine people in this yard that it is now a machine for handing out smooth pages at the rate of one a morning, and I did not build that and I cannot unbuild it this month.** A community stands in one of four words every day of the month, and the paper that came into this harbor wants exactly that, and between those two things a person who cannot see the edges of what they are keeping has been handed a page with no edges on it. **That is not the paper's fault. The paper says what it is and it says what it did not do and it has a seal on it and nobody has read the name under that seal out loud in twelve days.**

The fourth thing in Monday's drafts is where I come in and I am going to say it once. **To write down what would have to happen for a thing to be opened is to write a price on it.** It looks like four checkable things and it is four checkable things, and one of the four is a handle. **A record that carries a handle on the part of itself that matters is not safer for the person holding the handle. It is a page that can be opened by whoever finds a reason to open it inside four years.** I will take more danger than that in a record any day. I have spent eleven weeks making records that were dangerous and I know what the danger actually looks like and it looks like being wrong in public in a market with about eleven people in it who cannot read what you were wrong about."

He answered and it was about two hundred and twenty-eight words.

"**And I will not have a page that can be taken back on a Tuesday morning, and that is not caution, that is what a page is for.** I have to be able to put a finger on a day and an hour and a place and say that somebody said that there, and if everything in that harbor can be withdrawn the moment somebody would rather it were not there then I have got a page of moods and I cannot send a boat on a page of moods. **I will take more danger to my own comfort than I will take to the steadiness of that page, and I will take it tomorrow morning if somebody writes the condition down on it and I will not be the man who decides what a community can stand.**

And I am not going to be talked out of it by the word handle. A thing with its edges named is a thing a stranger can go and be angry at, and I have said that in this yard before and I believe it, and a handle is not a threat. **A handle is a hinge. Somebody in this harbor is going to open that page in four years anyway, and I would like them to have to go at it through a hinge somebody wrote down on purpose.**"

She answered back and it was about one hundred and eighty-six words.

"**Then you are asking this harbor to make a page that can only be opened by force, and you are the only person in this yard who has ever wanted that, and I have known you for six winters and I know exactly what you are doing when you want a thing that hard.** You are not thinking about four years. You are thinking about a boat you have not sent yet. **And I will say the rest of it because we are in a shed with nine people in it and not in a kitchen.** I settle what a record is. That is not a courtesy and it is not a favour and it is not something I do because of who I am married to, and I have said it out loud on those boards twice. **And a record that cannot be corrected by anybody standing in front of it is not a record. It is a wall with a clerk behind it, and I said that in a market on Friday and I am not taking it back because you want a hinge.**"

Nobody arbitrated. Nobody said that either of them was right and nobody said that either of them was wrong, and about four people in that yard heard all three of those things and the rest of that yard was not told, and about five of the nine could not read what was on the back of that page and were told what was on it by a man who was told by a woman, in that order, in about eleven words.

At about half past five two lines went on the back of that page with one under the other, and nobody read either of them out. About four people in that yard watched them go on and about five of them did not know there was anything at all on the back of that page. They went on under the two drafts from Monday with room left above them, and nothing was struck and nothing was rubbed out and no name went at the foot of either of them and nothing went at the head of either of them either.

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and sixty-nine words, and nobody in that shed was asked which of the two people who made that argument was the one whose job it was.

"**I am the one who keeps the page and I am not going to tell either of those two people tonight which one of them is right, because I did not hear nine people agree and I heard six onlookers and four readers, and the six are not mine to count.** Nobody in this shed is going to get a new number off me tonight, so the number is the old one: ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not shifted since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. This slate has twenty-eight pence lying on it, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**Two lines went on the back of that page at about half past five this evening with one under the other and neither of them struck, and there is no signature at the foot of either, and there is nothing at the foot of either one that says settled and nothing at the head of either one that says who wrote it.**

Here is why that is the shape of it and not a piece of cowardice. In eleven weeks this yard has tried four times to write down what two people agreed on and three of those four times one of them had said something different to somebody else by the end of the week, and the fourth time the thing was written down was not agreed on at all. **A page that says settled on it is a claim about a fortnight and not about a record, and this harbor has been given a smooth page this week already and it came in under a stone with a seal on it and said what it was.**

So they go down as two positions against each other and they are going to be read that way in a month and in April, and I am not going to write one line under them tonight that says anything at all, and that is the whole of what a keeper of a page is for.

**The fifth word goes on Friday the first day of April and it goes in with both of those positions still standing underneath it, and it is going on a page and not in a minute.** Nobody has to agree with anything and nobody is going to be thanked for agreeing with nothing.

And the thing I will say once and then leave alone. A woman of thirty-nine said no in this market on Friday in nine people and gave a ground for it, and a man with one book and one hand found on Saturday that the page of this harbor is not broken, it only has an hour on one of three rows. **A method that can be found wrong in a market and read right in a shed four hundred yards off is the best thing anybody has made on this coast in eleven weeks, and it is not going to be improved this month by anybody in it, including the two people who were arguing about it at four o'clock this afternoon.**

The sheet with the fourteen things on it was not touched and no figure on it has moved by one and none of those fourteen has been added to and none has been taken off.

One entry went on the first column of that leaf at about six this evening and five went on the second, and the leaf was not read out loud. The first of those two figures is three hundred and fifty-one and the second is one thousand six hundred and twenty-nine, my third is empty, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for three things said out loud in a market yard, and by about six there was nobody left in that shed and its door stood open on a slip with nothing on it. The two lines stayed on the back of that page until a fifth word went above them on Friday, and there is still no name at the foot of either one.

TWO LINES WENT ON THE BACK OF THAT PAGE UNDER THE TWO DRAFTS WITH ONE UNDER THE OTHER AND NEITHER STRUCK AND NO SIGNATURE AND NO AGREEMENT, AND IT IS NOT SETTLED AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE SETTLED IN THAT SHED AND NO PAGE OF THIS HARBOR CARRIES IT SETTLED. NOT A WORD OF THAT GAP SHEET WAS READ OUT AND NO FIGURE ON IT WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE, AND AN ARGUMENT ABOUT A FOURTH LINE IN INK IS NOT A PEN ON THAT SHEET. SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE IS ALL THAT ANYBODY HAS PUT INTO IT, BEING TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE; THE FIGURE IT IS SHORT BY IS SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, BEING NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE; AND WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE. IT HAS SAT THERE SINCE THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF LAST MONTH. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS UNDER THE SLATE IN THAT SHED.**
