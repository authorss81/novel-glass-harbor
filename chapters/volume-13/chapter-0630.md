# Chapter 630

*Monday the Eleventh Day of April, a Rota Day, That Cistern Behind That Market Filled for the Hundred and Forty-First Time at a Little After Eight in the Morning by Two Men Going Up a Ladder Eleven Times With a Bucket and Nothing Walked on, the Covers Back on It Before a Quarter Past Nine and the Water Up to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Sheet of Days in That Shed Carrying Nine Route Names in Three Columns and Three Figures Written at the Head of Those Three Columns on the Day of That Writing, Being Two and Four and Three, and a Man of About Fifty-Six of the Third Lay Adding Them Up With a Leaf and a Pencil in About a Minute and Getting Nine and Then Reading the Nine Names Off the Same Sheet and Getting Nine Again, and Nobody in That Shed Asked Him a Second Time and Nobody Said That Figure Out Loud and It Is Not in Any Slip, the Morrow Line Still Held and Not Struck and Still One of the Nine, the Count of Positions Not Taken Again and the Fifth Word Not a Column, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Three and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Monday the eleventh day of April, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before that market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about three in the afternoon and about nine in that shed at half past five.

**A Monday rota day, with neither a wage Tuesday nor a wipe standing in it. That butt was filled by hand for the hundred and forty-first time at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips and nothing walked on, the covers went back on it before a quarter past nine, the water stood at the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. At about three this afternoon a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish was handed that sheet of days in that shed and added the three figures at the head of its three columns up with a leaf and a pencil in about a minute, and got nine, and then read the nine names down the side of the same sheet and counted those and got nine again, and gave the sheet back and nobody asked him anything about it and nobody said anything about it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil adding three figures on a board in a shed is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. A hundred and sixty-eight pence went into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came out of it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the close of the day, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. That money sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence is the figure this harbor has never got, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Not one tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of what this afternoon cost came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and none of them is improved and none of them has moved by more than one since the day before it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty-eight days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty-eight days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-fourth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-two days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-second. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-ninth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. There are five papers on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. Those four lines have not come out of an upper room over that market, nothing has ever been written under the fourth of them, and the answer is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands holding a paper and nothing else, and it has stood there since the second of this month. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty-eight days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That ladder went up at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips by two men with a bucket, and not one bucket of it was walked on and nothing was taken up it on any other errand. By a quarter past nine the covers were back on that butt, and it stood full to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with nothing drawn out of it, and that is a hundred and forty-one times.

The name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Nobody in that market has offered to put it there and nobody has asked either of those two men about it, and that is not going to be changed this month.

---

At about three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote three figures at the head of the three columns on that sheet of days, being two, four and three, and put the day against them and the hour, and then handed the sheet to a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish and said nothing else about it.

The man of fifty-six added the three figures up with a leaf and a pencil in about a minute. He got nine. He then read the nine names down the side of the sheet, being the Morrow Line, the Saltmarch Weatherway, the Tidewatch Channel, the Ashtide Run, the Ternwatch Passage, the Lowfen Reed, the Cinder Bell, the Pale Shoals and the Orison Descent, and counted those and got nine again.

He gave the sheet back and went back to his own work. Nobody in that shed asked him a second time what he had got and nobody said out loud what he had got, and the three figures are not in any slip tonight and are not in a minute and are not on the sheet of that harbor's own answers, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on them this evening and add them and get nine and read the names and get nine again.

**The Morrow Line is still in the held column. It has not been struck and it is not out of the nine and it is on that sheet this afternoon with a day and an hour against it like every other name on it.** Nobody in this harbor has put a state on that name.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing, and about nine people in that yard stood at the flat of that bench stone and about four of them could read what was on the wall above it and about five could not, and none of those five was told what was on it.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about seven hundred and sixty-seven words, and nobody in that shed was asked which three of the nine names he had expected.

"**A hundred and forty-one charges have gone past that cistern and the name at the head of that board is still off it, and eleven trips by two men and nothing walked on and the covers back on before a quarter past nine and that butt up to the rim of its second hoop until about four this afternoon, and I am going to say nothing else about that butt this month.**

What I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is where the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month left it. It is not made to balance by asking this harbor for the difference and it is not going to be made to balance this month. Lying under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**Here is what happened to that sheet of days in the last ten days and I am going to give it to nine people without one of the three figures spoken.** Three words went into it out of three mouths on three different days and not one of those three was asked which position it was in, and every one of those three people came from the run they were talking about and none of them is a member of anything and one of them has signed nothing at all. Two words in those three were written under a word that was already there and neither of those two earlier words has been struck, and a stranger can put a finger on all three of those lines and read what was true and what is true.

And a man of fifty-six landed fish this afternoon with a leaf and a pencil and added three figures up in about a minute and read nine names off the same sheet and did it twice, and he did not ask me a single question and I did not tell him a single thing, and **that is the entire test this harbor has ever built and it has passed it in front of a man who has no stake in any of it and is going back to his own work.**

**I am not going to say those three figures out loud and they are not in this slip.** They are on a board in this shed with a day and an hour against them and a stranger can have them in about a minute. This shed has said a number out loud at half past five on ten nights running this month and every one of those numbers was a figure about a page, and there is a time for a figure about a coast to be written down instead of said and this is it, and I have known that since before the winter and I have had it in the back of a notebook for a month and this is the first time I have put it on a board.

The fourth name is still held. **It is not struck and it is not out of the nine and nobody in this harbor has put a state on it and nobody is going to.**

The count of positions was not taken again this month. The fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and carries no figure against any day. The two drafts of Monday and the two lines of Wednesday are standing on that page with nothing struck and nothing signed and nothing anywhere saying settled.

The fourteen at the end of that stall were not read out of in ten days. No figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off.

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and I did not read that leaf out aloud. Three hundred and sixty-three stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and eighty-nine against the second, my third is empty with a boy's hand at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a stranger with a leaf and a pencil. That shed was bare by about six with its door open on a slip nobody was on, and that sheet of days stood against that wall with a day and an hour at the head of its three columns and nine names down its side, and a man of about thirty-two was in that yard at about ten past six and did not go in the water and did not say one word to anybody about any of it.

---

**THREE FIGURES WERE WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF THE THREE COLUMNS ON THAT SHEET OF DAYS ON THAT MONDAY, BEING TWO AND FOUR AND THREE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX OF THE THIRD LAND ADDED THEM UP WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL IN ABOUT A MINUTE AND GOT NINE AND THEN READ THE NINE NAMES DOWN THE SIDE OF THAT SHEET AND GOT NINE AGAIN, AND NONE OF THOSE THREE FIGURES WAS SAID OUT LOUD AND NONE OF THEM IS IN ANY SLIP. THE MORROW LINE IS STILL IN THE HELD COLUMN AND IS NOT STRUCK AND IS STILL ONE OF THE NINE AND NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR HAS PUT A STATE ON IT. THREE WORDS WENT INTO THAT SHEET IN SEVEN DAYS OUT OF THREE MOUTHS AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE WAS ASKED WHICH POSITION IT WAS IN AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE IS A MEMBER OF ANYTHING. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NOT ONE FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE IN TEN DAYS. THAT MONEY SHEET STANDS AT NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS EVER GONE INTO IT, AND ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR. UNDER THAT SLATE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE.**

and that nobody in that shed asked him a second time what he had got and nobody said out loud what he had got, and that those three figures are not in any slip and are not in a minute and are not on the sheet of that harbor's own answers, and that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on them this evening and add them and get nine and read the nine names and get nine again; and that the Morrow Line is still in the held column and has not been struck and is not out of the nine and is on that sheet with a day and an hour against it like every other name on it, and that nobody in this harbor has put a state on that name; and that at about ten past six that evening a man of about thirty-two was in that yard and did not go in the water and did not say one word to anybody about any of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about three in the afternoon the reason I gave that sheet to a man who lands fish is that in eleven weeks this harbor has handed its own pages to people who work in it, and that a page handed to a man who lands fish at the end of a market afternoon is a page that has been tested by somebody with no stake in it and no reason to be kind about it, and that he got it twice, and that I did not tell him what he would get and that was on purpose; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason I wrote three figures at the head of three columns today when I have printed no figure at the head of anything since the fifteenth is that a figure about a page is a thing nine people can argue with and a figure about a coast is a thing nine people can only repeat, and that this yard has spent three weeks discovering that difference the hard way and I am not going to say the second thing out loud in a shed at half past five; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I have not written one word in this column about ten days is that ten days ago a Crown vessel went on a bar of hard sand and cost six men a season and got onto a page of this shed in one sentence, and that a sentence about a hull is not a cost and this harbor printed it once because it had to be printed once and I do not know whether it was right to print it and I am not going to find out this month; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not go and stand at the flat of that bench stone while a man of fifty-six was counting is that this yard has spent eleven weeks teaching itself that a number said out loud in a yard is a number somebody has to carry home and argue with at breakfast, and that those three figures do not need that and the nine names do not need that and the only thing they needed was a leaf and a pencil and about a minute; and that at about half past five in the evening the reason this slip ends on the fourth of those nine names and not on the three that moved is that a run which has put itself down is a thing four people can go and be angry at, and that this harbor has spent thirteen winters arguing about what a record is for and the answer this month turned out to be that it is not for the record, it is so that there is somebody to be angry at, and that the name on that row is still there and it has not been struck and nobody in this harbor has touched it.*
