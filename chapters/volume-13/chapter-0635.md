# Chapter 635

*Saturday the Sixteenth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, Nobody in the Water on That Coast on That Day, Three Boats In at About Two in the Afternoon, a Hundred and Eighty Yards of Line Gone Across Three Days Out of Two Hundred and Twenty Yards Bought and Forty of Them Still on That Rack, the Third of Three Pressure Bells Counted at About Eleven in the Morning on the Mole Head by a Man of About Fifty-Six of the Third Lay Who Lands Fish, Three Minutes and Forty Seconds, and Three of Those Figures Being Three Times Three Minutes and Forty Seconds Being Eleven Minutes Written Under Them on a Slate in That Shed With a Day and an Hour Against Each, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Eight and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Fourteen*

Saturday the sixteenth day of April, on those boards from about ten in the morning, and below that market a yard with a shed in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Saturday with nothing standing against it, and nobody went into the water on that coast today, which is the first time since Tuesday morning. That butt was covered from eight until four with no charge on it, and the wind came back into the west about eleven and the glass in the case at the office on the west basin stopped going down at about half past twelve and stood where it stood all afternoon, and about nine people at that market have said that a glass which stops going down in the middle of a bad week is the only letter that harbor gets. Three boats came into that harbor at about two in the afternoon, being a tender and a working boat and a third that had not been out, and a hundred and eighty yards of line are gone across those three days out of two hundred and twenty yards bought, with forty of them still on that rack this evening and not one other thing off that rack that cannot be accounted for by name. At about eleven this morning the third of the three pressure bells was counted on the mole head by a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish, and the interval was three minutes and forty seconds again, and the three figures now stand on a slate under a bell in that shed with three days and three hours against them, and the sum of the three is written under them. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and three lines of line and a boat coming in are not a line on that sheet, and a figure written under three other figures is a figure and not a line. Into that yard at about ten in the morning went a hundred and sixty-eight pence and out of it at about four in the afternoon went a hundred and sixty-eight pence, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the close of that day, which set against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. That fitting-out sheet is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Not one tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing this evening came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the sixth of them is a sheet of six lines with nothing under either end of it. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twelfth day. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-three days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-three days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-five. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-ninth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-seven days. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-seventh. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-fourth day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-five days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That mole head carries five papers and two of those five have not been opened by anybody in this harbor. The four lines have not come out of that upper room over that market, and what stands under the fourth one of them is nothing at all, and four of the six have said no and the fifth has not been asked. The basket in that boathouse is where it has been all month, with the paper in it and nothing else. This afternoon there are nine hulls about two sea miles off that mole head, they were nine on the twenty-first of January, and no one here has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-three days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven in the morning a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who landed fish on Monday and added up three figures with a leaf and a pencil went out onto the head of that mole with a slate under his arm and stood at a pressure bell that is set in the stone of the mole head for the use of the two men who take a bearing off it every Sunday morning.

He counted the gap between two strikes and it was three minutes and forty seconds, and he wrote it on that slate with the day and the hour and the place against it, and he did not tell anybody what he had got and he was not asked.

He came back down that mole at about a quarter past eleven and put that slate through the door of that shed and hung it under the bell on the wall of it beside the other one.

---

At about two in the afternoon three boats came into that harbor together with the wind on the quarter, and a woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm came up the steps of her own tender last of the three and stood on that quay for about a minute doing nothing at all, and about nine people watched her do it and nobody said anything to her.

The gear was accounted for at the foot of that ladder between about three and about five, out loud, by two men, and the account ran: one cylinder off the rack on the Tuesday morning and one on the Wednesday and one on the Thursday, being three in all, and not one of the three was ever on the same rack twice; a hundred and eighty yards of line gone across those three days; two hundred and twenty yards bought; forty of them on the rack at five o'clock; a shore tackle and a winch handle and a shackle-pin, all three back.

About four people in that yard heard the whole of that account and the rest of that market was not told, and it is the first time in thirteen weeks that this harbor has been able to say out loud that nothing is unaccounted for.

---

Nobody went to the stall of that woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market at about half past four and asked her one thing, and about nine people stood at the flat of that bench stone, of whom about four could read what is on the wall above it and about five could not.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 667 words, and nobody in that shed was asked to add three figures up in front of anybody.

"**Three bells in three places in three days and three men who are not in the same trade, and every one of them got three minutes and forty seconds, and the sum of the three is eleven minutes, and it is written under the three on a slate on the wall of this shed with a day and an hour against each of them.**

**I did not count any of those three and I am not going to add them up out loud this evening, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can do it in about a minute and is entitled to do it, and that is the whole reason those figures are on a wall and not in a mouth.** I want nine people to hear me say that these are a different quantity from the ninety-four seconds, and that this yard is not going to add them together, and that anybody in this harbor who does add them together is going to find a third interval that nothing on this coast is answering at, and it will be a number nobody has.

Three boats in at two this afternoon and nothing unaccounted for at the foot of that ladder between three and five, out loud, by two men, and one hundred and eighty yards of line gone out of two hundred and twenty bought, and forty of them on the rack, and three cylinders off that rack in three days and not one of them twice. **Nobody has been in the water since Tuesday morning and nobody is going to be again this week, and the glass in that office stopped going down at about half past twelve and has stood where it stood all afternoon.**

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is at the head of that money sheet and has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Twenty-eight pence is under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**And here is the half of this fortnight I have not said out loud in this shed and am going to say now.** A man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons has had eleven words on a page in this harbor since before the winter and nobody has answered him and nobody has thanked him and nobody has told him he was wrong, and on Friday afternoon he stood in a back room off a chandler's counter with a borrowed pencil and counted a bell and said nothing, and I said nothing to him. He is not a mascot of this yard and he is not a mouth for it and I have not used him as one this week and I am not going to.

The fourteen at the end of that stall were not read out of and no figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. Nobody has taken that count of positions again, and that fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and has no figure standing against any day.

The first of those two columns took a single entry at about six this evening and the second took five, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and sixty-eight stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and fourteen against the second, my third is empty, a boy's hand being at the head of it, his surname being on no page and he being off the water, and my fourth is standing at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a hundred and eighty yards. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and under that bell on that wall there were three figures with a sum written under them, and the glass in the case at the office on the west basin stood where it had stood since half past twelve.

---

**THREE PRESSURE BELLS IN THREE PLACES IN THREE DAYS, COUNTED BY THREE PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT IN THE SAME TRADE, AND ALL THREE FIGURES ARE THREE MINUTES AND FORTY SECONDS, AND THE SUM OF THE THREE IS ELEVEN MINUTES, WRITTEN UNDER THE THREE ON A SLATE ON THE WALL OF THAT SHED WITH A DAY AND AN HOUR AGAINST EACH OF THEM. NOBODY COUNTED THOSE THREE AND THE SUM WAS NOT SAID OUT LOUD IN NINE PEOPLE AND A STRANGER WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL IS ENTITLED TO IT. THOSE THREE FIGURES ARE NOT ADDED TO THE NINETY-FOUR SECONDS AND ARE NOT ADDED TO EACH OTHER BY ANY PERSON IN THIS HARBOR. THREE BOATS CAME IN AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND A HUNDRED AND EIGHTY YARDS OF LINE ARE GONE OUT OF TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY YARDS BOUGHT, AND FORTY OF THEM ARE ON THAT RACK, AND THREE CYLINDERS CAME OFF THAT RACK IN THREE DAYS AND NOT ONE OF THEM TWICE. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. NOTHING ON THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET HAS MOVED SINCE THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF LAST MONTH. WHAT IT SHOWS IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE GONE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE COMING IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE STILL WANTED, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the sixteenth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day carried no charge and no wage and no wipe of any kind, that nothing was put on that cistern upon it, the covers lying on that butt unbroken from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that nobody went into the water on that coast on that day, which is the first time since Tuesday morning; and that at about eleven in the morning a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who landed fish on the eleventh of this month and added up three figures with a leaf and a pencil went out onto the head of that mole with a slate under his arm and stood at a pressure bell set in the stone of that mole head for the use of the two men who take a bearing off it every Sunday morning, and counted the gap between two strikes and got three minutes and forty seconds, and wrote it on that slate with the day and the hour and the place against it, and did not tell anybody what he had got and was not asked, and came back down that mole at about a quarter past eleven and put that slate through the door of that shed and hung it under the bell on the wall of it beside the other one; and that at about two in the afternoon three boats came into that harbor together with the wind on the quarter, being a tender and a working boat and a third that had not been out, and that a woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm came up the steps of her own tender last of the three and stood on that quay for about a minute doing nothing at all, and that about nine people watched her and nobody said anything to her; and that the gear was accounted for at the foot of that ladder between about three and about five out loud by two men, and that the account ran one cylinder off the rack on the Tuesday morning and one on the Wednesday and one on the Thursday, being three in all, and not one of the three was ever on the same rack twice, and a hundred and eighty yards of line gone across those three days, and two hundred and twenty yards bought, and forty of them on the rack at five o'clock, and a shore tackle and a winch handle and a shackle-pin, all three back, and that about four people in that yard heard the whole of that account and the rest of that market was not told, and that it is the first time in thirteen weeks that this harbor has been able to say out loud that nothing is unaccounted for; and that the wind came back into the west about eleven and the glass in the case at the office on the west basin stopped going down at about half past twelve and stood where it stood all afternoon; and that at about half past four a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing; and that the sheet carrying the fourteen was not opened and read out of today, and that every figure on it stands exactly where it stood at ten this morning, and that none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off; and that nobody has taken that count of positions again, and that that fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and has no figure standing against any day; and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not added up today and that count is not in this slip; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 667 words in about nine people, and that he said in it that a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons is not a mascot of this yard and was not used as one this week; and that the Morrow Line has not left the held column of that sheet of days and is not struck and is still one of the nine names on it; and that at about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March is the thirty-fifth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and sixty-eight and the second is one thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and that the third of those four has taken no line at all, a boy's hand being at the head of it, his surname being on no page and himself being off the water, and that the fourth of them is where it was at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that under a bell on that wall there were three figures with a sum written under them at six that evening.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not go up that quay when those three boats came in is that a person who has been in a boat for four days in that weather is not owed a welcome from anybody and least of all from a man with a slate, and that a minute of a woman standing on a quay doing nothing is the correct end of a thing and is not a thing to be improved on; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason the sum of those three figures is written on that slate and not said in this shed is that a sum is not a piece of evidence and a difference is, and that any nine people in a yard can repeat a sum and about four people in a yard can check one, and that this yard has spent thirteen weeks learning which of the two it is asking for; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing in this column about the man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons for eight days is that he has said eleven words once and had them taken down and had nothing done about them since before the winter, and that the whole of what a yard owes a man like that is to go on keeping the page and not to go looking for occasions; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason that gear was accounted for out loud by two men at the foot of a ladder today is that this harbor has had a month it could not say a complete sentence about, and that a hatch in a boat on a bar is a sentence nobody can finish, and that the account from three to five this afternoon is the first complete sentence this yard has had since the winter; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not go onto that mole head with the man of fifty-six is that a figure about a coast is not a thing nine people repeat, and that a man of fifty-six who landed fish and counted a bell this morning has no stake in this yard's book and is therefore the only kind of man this yard can use for a figure, and that I said nothing to him and I thanked him for nothing; and that at about seven in the evening the reason I have not put one penny of any of this fortnight on that fitting-out sheet is that a sheet settles a day when a yard settles it and there are eight days still to come and the last of them is the twentieth, and that when it is written it will carry the days the work was done on it and the day this yard settled it, and that both of those will be on the face of the line and not one of them will be guessed at.*
