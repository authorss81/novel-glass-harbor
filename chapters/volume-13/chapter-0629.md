# Chapter 629

*Sunday the Tenth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Hundred and First Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Off Its Holders at About Ten Past Ten and Bare at About Half Past Ten and Up Again at About Ten to Eleven, Unrepaired and Not Whole, Eighteen Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Five Standing Bare and Eighteen to Get It Back, Being Forty-One Minutes the Whole of It from About Ten Past Ten to About Ten to Eleven, a Minute Longer Than the Third of This Month and Two Longer Than the Twentieth of Last Month and Nobody Having Said Why, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything on the Second Sunday Running and the Third, the Two Words Against the Lowfen Reed Read Out by a Woman of About Twenty-Six to About Four People at About Eleven and Not Read Out Loud to the Whole Market and Not Corrected by Anybody, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Two and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Sunday the tenth day of April, with that market trading all morning under a sky that had gone grey about eleven, and a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Sunday and the hundred and first time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and no charge on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine until four, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it. At about ten past ten two men began to take that wall off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten and it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole. Eighteen minutes to get it off, five minutes standing bare, eighteen to get it back, being forty-one minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven, which is a minute longer than the third of this month and two longer than the twentieth of last month, and nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower. At about eleven this morning a woman of about twenty-six read the two words that stand against the Lowfen Reed on that sheet of days out to about four people and stopped there, and did not read the sheet out loud to that market and nobody corrected her and nobody corrected the sheet. The woman of about thirty-nine was at her stall at about eleven and nobody asked her anything and that is the second Sunday running and the third time in thirteen days. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and two words on one line of a board is not a line on that sheet and a fourth wipe is not a line on that sheet either. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard in the morning at ten and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four in the afternoon, and the twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, under that slate at the day's end has not moved in eleven weeks. Four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is what it is being measured against, and by that measure it is six hundred and twelve pence wanting, being three marks eleven shillings. What has gone out of this harbor on that sheet is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; what has ever been put into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and the whole of the distance between those two is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. There is no tin in that shed that anybody has opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine have gone on again and the third of them is the only one of the nine that belongs to an office that is not this harbor's and has been carrying a figure for six hundred and sixty-seven days. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixty-seven days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twenty-seven. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-nine. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-third night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-nine days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-first. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirty-eighth day. Five papers stand on that board at the head of that mole, and two of them have never been opened. The four lines have not left the upper room over that market, there is still nothing written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them stands at no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with the paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole are nine hulls this morning, they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twenty-seven days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten past ten on that Sunday two men began to take the wall behind that reeve's bench down off its holders, and it was bare by about half past ten for the hundred and first time, and about five people near enough to see it happen went on with what they were doing.

They had it back up against the holders at about ten to eleven, sitting at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame was still not whole. Eighteen minutes to get it off the wall, five minutes standing with it bare, eighteen minutes to get it back, which is forty-one minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven. That is a minute longer than the third of this month and two longer than the twentieth of last month and three longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and this yard has stopped asking.

**It is the hundred and first time and the man who keeps the leaf did not say that out loud this week.** He said the hundredth time a week ago to about five people and said it would not be the last time that year, and he did not say it.

---

At about eleven, with that market trading underneath and about nine people standing at the flat of that bench stone, a woman of about twenty-six who crews out of Lowfen stood in front of that sheet of days and read two words out to about four people and stopped.

The two words are the ones that stand on one line of that sheet above each other, being dormant and then restricted, with Friday the eighth of April and the hour of eleven against the lower one. She did not read the sheet out loud to that market and she did not explain either word and she did not say anything about a boy of fifteen or about six of her own, and about four people heard her and about five of that nine stood there and did not know which two words she had read.

She went back up to her own place at the end of that market at about twenty past eleven. Nobody asked her anything about the reed and nobody asked her a second time about it or about anything, and nobody in that yard said to her that there was a word above the one she had read, and she looked at it herself before she came down.

---

At about the same hour the woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market stood at her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and worked, and nobody in this harbor went to that stall and asked her a single thing.

That is the second Sunday running and it is the third day in thirteen. She said no on the twenty-fifth in nine people and gave a ground for it once and nobody has argued with her and nobody has thanked her and nobody has asked her since, and this morning she was not asked whether she agreed with a word in a column that came down that coast road on Friday.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and eighty-one words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a person is allowed to carry on a page.

"**Forty-one minutes. Eighteen off the wall, five standing bare, eighteen back, from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven, and it is the hundred and first time and I am not going to say the hundredth time out loud twice in nine days, because a man who counts a thing for an audience has started performing it and that is the last thing that wall has needed.**

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the figure I am answerable for, and it has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Twenty-eight pence is under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**Two words sit on one line of that sheet of days above each other and both of them are readable, and this morning a woman of about twenty-six stood in front of that sheet and read them out to about four people and stopped, and did not read the rest of it and did not explain either of them.** I was nine feet away and I said nothing. About five of those nine stood there and did not know which two words she had read and none of those five asked her and that was correct of all of them.

**And she is still here.** She was going back down that coast road on Monday and it is Sunday and she is standing in that market with a basket and she is not a member of anything, and the sheet says nothing about when she leaves and I am not going to ask.

The woman of about thirty-nine was at her stall at eleven this morning and nobody went to her and nobody asked her anything, and that is three days in thirteen, and I am going to keep saying that number out loud in this shed until somebody stops it and the somebody is not going to be me.

Here is what I have not done this week and I am going to say it once. **Nobody in this harbor has put a state on any of those nine names.** Not the woman of forty-one on Wednesday, not the woman of twenty-six on Friday, and not the word that came down that coast road on Wednesday morning for the Ternwatch Passage. Three words in seven days went onto that board from three mouths and not one of them was asked a second time and not one of them is in a column because a keeper of a page decided it ought to be.

The fourteen at the end of that stall were not read out of this morning and no figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none taken off.

Onto the first column of that leaf there went one entry at about six this evening and onto the second there went five, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and sixty-two stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and eighty-four against the second, my third is empty, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for two words read to four people. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip with nothing on it, and that wall behind that reeve's bench stood at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and not whole, and the woman of about twenty-six was still in that market at six.

**THAT WALL CAME DOWN BARE ON THAT SUNDAY AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN, BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIRST TIME, AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE. IT TOOK EIGHTEEN MINUTES TO GET OFF THE WALL, FIVE STANDING BARE, AND EIGHTEEN TO GET IT BACK, BEING FORTY-ONE MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT FROM ABOUT TEN PAST TEN TO ABOUT TEN TO ELEVEN, AGAINST SEVENTEEN BOTH WAYS ON THE THIRD OF THIS MONTH AND SIXTEEN ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF LAST MONTH AND FOURTEEN ON THE THIRTEENTH, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX READ THE TWO WORDS THAT STAND AGAINST THE LOWFEN REED ON THAT SHEET OF DAYS OUT TO ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND STOPPED AND DID NOT READ THE REST OF IT AND DID NOT EXPLAIN EITHER WORD, AND ABOUT FIVE OF THAT NINE DID NOT KNOW WHICH TWO WORDS SHE HAD READ AND NONE OF THOSE FIVE ASKED HER. NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR WENT TO THE STALL OF THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE AT ELEVEN THAT MORNING AND ASKED HER ONE THING, AND THAT IS THREE DAYS IN THIRTEEN. NOTHING WAS TAKEN OUT OF A GAP AND EVERY FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN STOOD EXACTLY WHERE IT STOOD AT TEN THIS MORNING. THE FIGURE THAT MONEY SHEET NEVER CARRIED IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS; EVERYTHING THAT HAS EVER GONE INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. THE WHOLE OF THAT SLATE'S TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IS TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the tenth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the hundred and first time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that at about ten past ten two men began to take that wall down off its holders and it was bare by about half past ten with about five people near enough to see it go on with what they were doing, and that it went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, and that it took eighteen minutes to get it off the wall, five minutes standing with it bare and eighteen minutes to get it back, which is forty-one minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about ten to eleven, and that that is a minute longer than the third of this month and two longer than the twentieth of last month and three longer than the twenty-seventh of last month, and that nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and that this yard has stopped asking; and that it is the hundred and first time and that the man who keeps the leaf did not say that out loud this week, having said the hundredth time a week ago to about five people and said he would not say it again this year, and that a man who counts a thing for an audience has started performing it; and that at about eleven, with that market trading underneath and about nine people standing at the flat of that bench stone, a woman of about twenty-six who crews out of Lowfen stood in front of that sheet of days and read two words out to about four people and stopped, being dormant and then restricted with Friday the eighth of April and the hour of eleven against the lower one, and that she did not read that sheet out loud to that market and did not explain either word and did not say anything about a boy of fifteen or about six of her own, and that about four people heard her and about five of that nine stood there and did not know which two words she had read and none of those five asked her; and that she went back up to her own place at the end of that market at about twenty past eleven and that nobody asked her anything about that reed and nobody asked her a second time about it or about anything, and that nobody in that yard said to her that there was a word above the one she had read, and that she looked at it herself before she came down; and that at about the same hour the woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market stood at her own stall with her hands in the bib of her apron and worked, and that nobody in this harbor went to that stall and asked her a single thing, and that that is the second Sunday running and the third day in thirteen, and that she said no on the twenty-fifth in nine people and gave a ground for it once and nobody has argued with her and nobody has thanked her; and that that sheet of the fourteen was not read out of this morning, that no figure on it went up or down by one, and that none of those fourteen has been added to and none taken off; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and eighty-one words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March is the twenty-ninth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and sixty-two and the second is one thousand six hundred and eighty-four, and that the third of those four took nothing, and that the fourth of them is unmoved at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I stood nine feet away and said nothing while she read those two words is that the whole of what this harbor did in the winter was let people say a thing in their own mouth in front of other people, and that a keeper of a page who steps in at the moment a word is read out is a man who has decided the reading needs managing, and about five people standing there not knowing which two words they were is the correct condition of a market on a Sunday; and that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I did not say the hundred and first time out loud is that I said the hundredth time last week to five people and told them I would not say it again this year, and that a figure said to an audience gets said differently the second time, and that this yard has spent eleven weeks being careful about what gets written down when people are listening; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written down three days in thirteen about the woman of about thirty-nine is that in eleven weeks nobody in this harbor has ever counted a day on which it did not do a thing to a person, and that three is not a figure about her and is a figure about this yard and the two are not the same and this slip is the only place either of them is ever going to be written; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason I have not written one word in this column about Saturday is that two people who are married to each other argued in nine people in daylight about what a person is allowed to carry and nobody arbitrated and nobody agreed and nobody was thanked, and that the half of that argument which is mine to hold is the half about this yard and not the half about them, and that I stood four paces away with a book under my arm and put nothing in a column, and that is the correct act and it is the hardest one this shed has asked of anybody including me; and that at about six this evening the reason this slip does not close on anything that happened this week is that nine days of this fortnight were a vessel on a sand, a cistern, three words in a column, a man with a book and a woman with a basket, two people on a bench stone and a wall that is not whole, and that none of those nine days has a figure to add up and this yard has spent three weeks proving that a page does not need one.*
