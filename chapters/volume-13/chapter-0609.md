# Chapter 609

*Monday the Twenty-First Day of March, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Charge on That Cistern in Eleven Trips by Hand at About Eight in the Morning and Walked On by None of It, the Butt Behind That Market Full to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four, the Two Sheets Lying Side by Side On the Flat of That Bench Stone at About Two in the Afternoon and Both of Them Named Out Loud and Three Times, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying in About a Hundred and Ninety-Three Words What the Difference Between Them Is and the Difference Being That One Has a Word Against Every Day and No Hour Anywhere on It and the Other Has a Figure Against a Day and an Hour Against the Figure, the Sheet of the Fourteen Gaps Not Touched and Not Read Out Loud This Week and Standing Where It Stood on Tuesday, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Paid Into That Yard at Ten in the Morning and Paid Out of It at Four in the Afternoon, the Figure of the Gap Put Out Loud at Half Past Five and Not Moved, Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Under the Slate*

Monday the twenty-first day of March, with the cistern at the back of that market charged before the market opened, and about nine people in the yard below it at half past five in the afternoon and about four of them able to read what was on the bench stone that afternoon.

**A Monday rota, with no wage Tuesday and no wipe on it. That cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-fifth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and not one bucket of it was walked on, and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine and the butt stood full to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon, and the two men who carry it up that ladder have still never once been on that rota board and have never once asked to be, and the name at the head of that board is still off it. At about two in the afternoon the two sheets were lying side by side on the flat of that bench stone and both of them were named out loud, and both of them were named three times in that one afternoon. The sheet of money was not written on that Monday and not corrected on it, and two sheets on a bench stone are not a line on that sheet, and the sheet of the fourteen gaps was not touched and was not read out loud this week and stands where it stood on Tuesday. That yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at ten in the morning, one mark and eightpence, and gave out a hundred and sixty-eight pence at four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was in the bag at the close of the day, being six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. One hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence has gone out of this yard on that sheet, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever come into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the difference between the two, and neither of those two pieces of paper moves it by a penny. The last time a tin was opened in that shed was the twenty-ninth day of April past, and eleven trips up a ladder and two sheets laid on a stone have not taken a coin out of the bag under the slate, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine durations are these and none of them was looked for this week. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and seven days. The two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-seven. A bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and nine days. The bench under that pump, the four hundred and thirteenth night without it. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and first. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and eighteenth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and forty-nine days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of the mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody. The four lines have not come out of the upper room over the market: there is still nothing written under the fourth of them, four of the six they were put to have said no, and the fifth has not been put to them. The demand for the returns is in that basket in its boathouse, the paper is still in the basket, the basket is empty, and nobody has put a hand down into it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head; that is a hundred and seven days today, no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged a little after eight in the morning in eleven trips and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine and the butt behind that market stood up to the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket out of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and the name at the head of that board is off it and nobody in that market has offered to put it there or asked those two men about it.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the sheet out from under the stone at the head of those boards and put it on the flat of that bench stone beside the page of positions, and about nine people stood round that bench stone, and about four of them could read what was on both of those pieces of paper and the rest of them could not.

He said the name of each of them out loud, and then he said both of them again, and then he said them a third time, and the difference between them is what he said next in about a hundred and ninety-three words.

"**That one came into this harbor on Sunday under a stone and it has a word standing against every day of this month for two places and there is not one hour written anywhere on it, and there is nobody in it, and the name at the foot of it is under a seal and it has been there for eight days and nobody has read it out loud.** That one is ours and it has a figure against a day and an hour against the figure, and it has three figures standing on it this afternoon and one of those three has a stroke through it and is still readable. **A word against every day is a thing that can be wrong every day and there is no hour at which anybody said it out loud. A figure against a day is a thing somebody said in a place at an hour, and if it is wrong we can go and find the hour and the place and the mouth.** That is the whole of the difference and it is worth a day of anybody's time to be able to point at."

Nobody argued with that and nobody agreed with it out loud, and about four people heard all of it and the rest of that market was not told, and the sheet went back under the stone at about half past three with the stone put on it by somebody who did not say they had done it.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and forty-two words, and nobody in that shed was asked which of two papers was better.

"**I put this figure at the head of a slip because it is the figure I am answerable for, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Not a hand touched that sheet this morning and not a hand touched it this afternoon, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and that cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-fifth time before that market opened and not one bucket came out of it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and a day asked for by name is that same figure, and it is on no line of that sheet.

**Two sheets were lying side by side on the flat of that bench stone at about two this afternoon and I named both of them three times while they were on it, and the reason I did it three times is that in eleven weeks every single thing that has gone wrong in this harbor has gone wrong because two things were in one sentence.** One of those two papers has a word against every day of this month and no hour anywhere on it. Ours has a figure against a day and an hour against the figure, and it has three figures standing this evening and one of the three has a stroke through it and is still readable, and I am not going to say what that stroke is about at half past five on a Monday because a slip at half past five is said out loud to nine people and that is not the place for it.

**The sheet with the fourteen on it was not touched this week and I have not read one line of it out loud in seven days, and it is not a line on that money sheet and it is not a column on that bench stone and it is not going to be either.** The four heads on a wall in a room over the floor of that market are on no sheet of mine and the four of them are the four of them.

One line went on to the first column and five to the second at about six this evening, and I did not read that leaf out loud, and this is the ninth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth. My third column took nothing and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and I have not entered that reason in seventeen days."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for two sheets named three times, and at about six there was nobody in that shed and the door stood open on a slip with no name on it, and the cistern behind that market was still up to the rim of the second hoop.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED ON THAT MONDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIFTH TIME, IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND, WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, WITH THE COVERS BACK BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE AND THE BUTT FULL TO THE RIM OF THE SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. TWO SHEETS WERE LAID SIDE BY SIDE ON THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND BOTH WERE NAMED OUT LOUD THREE TIMES, ONE OF THEM CARRYING A WORD AGAINST EVERY DAY OF THIS MONTH WITH NO HOUR ANYWHERE ON IT AND NOBODY IN IT, AND THE OTHER CARRYING A FIGURE AGAINST A DAY AND AN HOUR AGAINST THE FIGURE WITH THREE FIGURES STANDING AND ONE OF THE THREE STRUCK AND STILL READABLE. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED. WHAT THAT SHEET CARRIES OUT OF THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND WHAT HAS EVER COMED INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT IS UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-first day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirty-fifth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and walked on by none of it, and that the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, and that the butt behind that market stood full to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket out of it, and that the two men who carry that water up that ladder have still never once been on that rota board nor ever once asked to be on it, and that the name at the head of that board is still off it, and that nobody in that market has put it there and nobody in that market has asked either of those two men whether they would like it there; and that at about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the sheet out from under the stone at the head of those boards and put it on the flat of that bench stone beside the page of positions, and that about nine people stood round that bench stone, and that about four of them could read what was on both of those pieces of paper and the rest of them could not; and that he said the name of each of them out loud and then said both of them again and then said them a third time, and that the difference between the two of them is that the one that came into that harbor on Sunday under a stone has a word standing against every day of this month for two places and has not one hour written anywhere on it and has nobody in it and a name at the foot of it under a seal that has been there for eight days and has not been read out loud, and that the other one is ours and has a figure against a day and an hour against the figure and has three figures standing on it this afternoon of which one has a stroke through it and is still readable, and that a word against every day is a thing that can be wrong every day with no hour at which anybody said it out loud, and that a figure against a day is a thing somebody said in a place at an hour, and that if it is wrong we can go and find the hour and the place and the mouth, and that that is the whole of the difference and it is worth a day of anybody's time to be able to point at; and that nobody argued with that and nobody agreed with it out loud, and that about four people heard all of it and the rest of that market was not told, and that the sheet went back under the stone at about half past three with the stone put on it by somebody who did not say they had done it; and that the sheet with the fourteen gaps on it was not touched this week and not one line of it was read out loud in seven days; and that that yard was paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten in the morning and paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence out at four in the afternoon, one mark and eightpence of it either way, and that twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was in the bag at the close of the said day, and that the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and that the bag is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind that wage; and that no hand set a figure in any line of that money sheet on that Monday and no hand took one out of it, and that two sheets lying side by side on a bench stone are not a line on it, and that it carries one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out of this yard, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and has had ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the space standing between them, and that neither of those two pieces of paper moves it by a penny; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and forty-two and the second at one thousand five hundred and eighty-four, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the ninth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason I laid the two sheets side by side instead of holding one up and talking about the other is that a thing held up is a thing being shown and two things lying flat are two things being compared, and that in eleven weeks four people in this harbor have been shown things and about nine have been able to compare them, and that the four are always the ones who get shown things; and that at about half past three the reason the sheet went back under the stone and not into a drawer in that yard is that it is not mine, and that a paper that arrives in a market under a stone is a paper this harbor has been lent for as long as anybody wants to look at it, and that the day this yard has a drawer for that paper is the day this yard has started collecting things, and that collecting things is what a harbor does before it starts owing things; and that at about ten in the morning the reason that cistern is the first thing in that slip and has been for six days running is that eleven trips a rota day is two hours of two men's backs and that this harbor has not printed a figure against that since the autumn, and that I have decided this month that the cistern is the thing I print and not the paper, because the cistern is the thing that happens whether anybody adopts a method or not; and that at about half past five the reason there is no count of anything on the page at the end of that stall in this slip is that a slip is said out loud in nine people and that a count said out loud in nine people on a Monday evening is a number nine people carry home in their heads and argue with at breakfast, and that a page that a man can point at is worth more than a figure nine people remember, and that I have got the page and I do not need the figure; and that at about six o'clock the reason I named those two sheets three times this afternoon and not twice is that the third time is the one that sticks, and that I worked that out in February with a rule read out twice in a room and got it wrong, and that a thing read out twice is read out twice.*
