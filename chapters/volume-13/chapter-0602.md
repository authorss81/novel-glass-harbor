# Chapter 602

*Monday the Fourteenth Day of March, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Thirty-Third Charge on the Cistern at the Back of That Market, Carried Up in Eleven Trips by Hand at About Eight in the Morning and Walked On by None of It, the Covers Down Again Before a Quarter Past Nine, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It and the Two Men Who Carry It Up That Ladder Never Once on It, the Sentence Printed on the Face of the Twelfth Paid in Full on This Monday and Not a Hundred and Thirty-Third Time of Anything, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Out Loud in About Two Hundred and Three Words That a Position Nobody Was Asked For Is Not a Position and That a Second Page Is Going at the End of That Stall Beside the Sheet of the Fourteen Gaps and Is Not Either of the Other Two Sheets, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Carried Into That Yard at Ten in the Morning and Carried Out Again at Four in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five and Not Moved, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the fourteenth day of March, with the market opening about six and the boards at the back of it filling by seven, and a yard below them with a shed, a bench stone, and about nine people in it at half past five in the afternoon.

**A rota day, and a cistern at the back of that market carried for the hundred and thirty-third time, and neither a wage Tuesday nor a wipe, and those last two are separate facts and a man of about thirty-three who hires hands put them in the wrong order on Saturday and was not argued with. Eleven trips by hand up that ladder, the same as every one of the hundred and thirty-two before it, and not one bucket of that water has been walked on since a Tuesday in the autumn. That Monday put no mark on the money sheet in any hand at all and took none off, and the four heads on a wall in a room over the floor of that market are on no sheet in that yard and that sheet is on no wall in that room, and neither of the two was in the same place as the other at any hour of that day. Into that yard at about ten in the morning went a hundred and sixty-eight pence, and out of it at about four in the afternoon came the same figure, that figure being one mark and eightpence, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, at four o'clock, and it is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind a wage of four marks. The sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the two do not come to the same thing and the space between them is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. A page of positions is not a line on that sheet and is not going to be one. The last tin in that shed was opened on the twenty-ninth day of April past, and a hundred and thirty-three charges have been paid for out of nobody's pocket since, and not one of those pennies came out of the bag under the slate, or out of a boat's owner, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**Nobody in that harbor can be handed the nine of these without a pencil and a wall to be slow in front of, and none of them is a person. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has been up three hundred days today. The bench under that pump has now stood bare four hundred and six nights, being one night longer than it stood bare on Friday. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and two days. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its hundred and seventy-ninth. Two lights at about a mile off that point have now been two hundred and twenty-four days with nothing set against them. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and forty days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eleventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its hundred and ninety-fourth. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing whatever to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and forty-two days, and that is the longest of the nine and the only one of the nine that has not moved by a day in a fortnight. That board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers, two of which have never been opened, and the basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nobody has looked. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning. A man of about twenty-nine whose left ear is not right can hear out of the side of it, and it is a hundred days today, and no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged a little after eight this morning, in eleven trips, and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, and the butt behind that market stood up to the top of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket out of it.

The two men who carry that water up that ladder have never once been on the rota board and have never once asked to be on it, and the name at the head of that board is still off it, and nobody has put a name on that board and nobody has put the question of it to either of those two men.

---

On Saturday the twelfth this yard printed a sentence, and the sentence was that there was a Monday and a Thursday in the month to come and both of them would be charged and neither of them would be a hundred and thirty-third time of anything.

This is that Monday. It was charged, at about eight, in eleven trips, and eleven trips is what the hundred and thirty-second was and what the hundred and thirty-first was, and the covers were back on before a quarter past nine. **And it was not the hundred and thirty-third time of anything, and the reason it was not is that the hundred and thirty-third is a figure on a run of charges and a Monday is a day, and a day is not a figure on a run.** A charge is a day somebody carried water and were paid nothing for. A figure on a run is a count of those days by a man who is not on them. Nobody asked for the figure this morning and nobody is going to be asked for it again.

---

And at about ten in the morning on those boards a woman of thirty of no office said one thing out loud, in about four hundred and sixty-five words, in about nine people, and four of those nine were told what she had said by a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay.

"**A position nobody was asked for is not a position, and that sheet on the boards has a word standing against every day of this month for two places and not one person in either of them has been asked for a word.** I am not saying the paper is wrong. I am saying that a thing this harbor learned on the third of last month is that a position is a thing a person says out loud and not a thing a person is put in. **So we are keeping our own page, and it goes at the end of that stall beside the sheet with the fourteen on it, and it is a third page and it is not that page and it is not the page that carries the money, and I am naming all three out loud so that nobody in this market can say later that two of them got mixed.** On that page there is a figure against a day and an hour against the figure, and there is one only where somebody in this harbor has said the word out loud, and where nobody has said it there is nothing, and nothing is not a figure."

The man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read that out again to the four who had not been there for the first saying, and about nine people stood on those boards while it was read and nobody agreed with it and nobody refused it, and it is a method and it binds this harbor if this harbor takes it and nobody else.

---

At about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about Four hundred and sixty-five Words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a hundred and thirty-third time of anything is.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Unmoved since the twenty-sixth of last month, and nothing went onto that sheet today and nothing came off it, and a page of positions is not a line on it, and a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of this yard at four o'clock and is not a line on it either, and those are three separate things and I have stopped letting them get into one sentence in my own head.

**That cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-third time at about eight and the sentence I printed on Saturday has been paid this morning.** It said that there would be a Monday and a Thursday in the month to come and that both of them would be charged and that neither of them would be a hundred and thirty-third time of anything, and I wrote that down on the twelfth because I could see the run of it and did not trust myself to see a day inside it. **A Monday is a day and a hundred and thirty-third is a figure on a run, and nobody asked for the figure and nobody had to.** The bag is twenty-eight pence, and it was twenty-eight pence on the Sunday and it will be twenty-eight pence on the Tuesday, and the cistern does not move it.

A woman of thirty of no office said out loud at about ten this morning, in nine people, that a position nobody was asked for is not a position, and that this harbor is keeping its own page with a figure against a day and an hour against the figure, and that there is one only where somebody has said a word out loud. **She named that page, the sheet with the fourteen on it, and the sheet that carries the money, all three, out loud, in that order, and she did it on purpose, and that is the second time in six weeks that anybody in this harbor has named two sheets out loud in the same breath and the first time it has been done by a person who is not on a crew.**

The third of my four is empty. The fourth is still at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and the reason it has taken none is not on a page of mine. At about six this evening I entered one line on the first column and five on the second, and neither of the first two figures of that leaf has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that is the second day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a hundred and thirty-three charges, and by six that shed was empty and its door stood open on a slip with nobody on it. and the butt behind that market was still up to the top of the second hoop.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED ON THAT MONDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THIRD TIME, IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND, AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING, AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND THE COVERS WERE DOWN AGAIN BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT AND THE TWO MEN WHO CARRY IT UP THAT LADDER HAVE NEVER ONCE ASKED TO BE ON IT. THE SENTENCE PRINTED ON THE FACE OF THE TWELFTH WAS PAID IN FULL ON THAT MONDAY AND NEITHER THAT MONDAY NOR THE THURSDAY AFTER IT IS A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THIRD TIME OF ANYTHING, A MONDAY BEING A DAY AND A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THIRD BEING A FIGURE ON A RUN, AND NOBODY ASKED FOR THE FIGURE. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 90 WORDS THAT A POSITION NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR IS NOT A POSITION, AND THAT A PAGE OF POSITIONS IS GOING AT THE END OF THAT STALL BESIDE THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN GAPS AND IS A THIRD PAGE AND IS NEITHER OF THE OTHER TWO, AND ON THAT PAGE THERE IS A FIGURE AGAINST A DAY AND AN HOUR AGAINST THE FIGURE AND THERE IS ONE ONLY WHERE SOMEBODY HAS SAID A WORD OUT LOUD. THE MONEY IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND NO PAGE IS A LINE ON IT. THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the fourteenth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that those are three separate facts which a man of about thirty-three who hires hands put in the wrong order on Saturday and which nobody argued with him about; and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirty-third time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and walked on by none of it, and that the covers were down on it again before a quarter past nine, and that the butt behind that market stood filled to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket out of it, and that the two men who carry that water up that ladder have never once been on the rota board nor ever once asked to be on it, and that the name at the head of that board is still off it and that nobody in that market has put it there or asked either of those two men about it; and that the sentence printed on the face of the twelfth, being that there was a Monday and a Thursday in the month to come and that both of them would be charged and that neither of them would be a hundred and thirty-third time of anything, was paid in full on that Monday, and that a charge is a day somebody carried water and were paid nothing for and that a figure on a run is a count of those days by a man who is not on them, and that nobody asked for the figure this morning and nobody is going to be asked for it again; and that at about ten in the morning a woman of thirty of no office said one thing out loud on those boards in about two hundred and three words in about nine people, and that about four of those nine were told what she had said by a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay; and that what she said is that a position nobody has been asked for is not a position, and that the sheet under the stone on those boards carries a word against every day of this month for two places and that not one person in either of them has been asked for a word, and that she did not say the paper was wrong; and that she said that this harbor is keeping its own page and that it goes at the end of that stall beside the sheet carrying the fourteen gaps, and that it is a third page and is not that page and is not the page that carries the money, and that she named all three out loud in that order on purpose so that nobody could say later that two of them had got mixed; and that on that page there is a figure against a day and an hour against that figure and there is one only where somebody in this harbor has said the word out loud, and that where nobody has said it there is nothing, and that nothing is not a figure; and that the man of about forty-six read it again to the four who had not been there for the first saying, and that about nine people were standing on those boards and that nobody agreed with it and nobody refused it, and that it is a method and it binds this harbor if this harbor takes it and nobody else; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about four hundred and sixty-five words, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence, one mark and eightpence of it, was carried in at about ten in the morning and carried out again at about four in the afternoon, and that under the slate when that Monday shut there was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and that the slate is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind that wage, and that not one figure was set down in any line of that money sheet in any hand on that Monday and not one was lifted out of it, and that a page of positions is not a line on it and a day asked for by name is not a line on it, and that what that sheet carries out of this yard stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, with ten thousand six hundred and forty pence of it ever coming in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the space standing between the two of those; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and thirty-five and the second at one thousand five hundred and forty-nine, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the second day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason I have set the cistern at the head of that slip and not the paper is that a hundred and thirty-three charges have been paid in that market in my time and that a slip which leaves it until the third paragraph is a slip that says the cistern is a fact everybody knows, and that a fact everybody knows is the reason a hundred and thirty-three of them happened without anybody counting them, and that I am the only person in this harbor who counts them and that is not a thing to be proud of in a shed with nine people in it; and that at about ten in the morning the reason I have written down that a woman of thirty of no office named three sheets out loud in that order is that in six weeks two people in this yard have put two sheets in one sentence and both times it was to make one of them sound better than it was, and that a page nobody has read out loud twice is a page that gets said wrong on purpose within a fortnight, and that naming them in an agreed order is the cheapest thing anybody has done on this coast in eleven weeks; and that at about ten o'clock the reason that sheet under the stone was not the argument I expected somebody to make is that nobody argued with it, and that I have been in this yard eleven weeks waiting for a man of about thirty-three to say that a paper with a seal on it is a paper with a person behind it, and that he said nothing, and that the man who said something was a woman with no office who was not defending it and was not attacking it and gave the two of us something to be argued with; and that at about half past five the reason I have said out loud this evening that a charge is a day and a figure on a run is a count of days and that they are not the same thing is that I printed that sentence on Saturday myself and I could not tell this morning whether I had meant it, and that a man who cannot tell on the morning whether he meant a thing he wrote on Saturday should say so at half past five in front of nine people and not carry it in a book; and that at about six o'clock the reason there is no figure in this slip about that sheet of days and no count of anything on it is that I do not know what it is yet, and that a slip is not the place where a man finds out what a thing is at half past five, and that the thing I have got is four lines she said at ten o'clock and that is a beginning and not a finding.*
