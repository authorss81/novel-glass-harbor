# Chapter 621

*Saturday the Second Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Crown Vessel of About Ninety Tons Off the Course She Was Holding at About Two in the Afternoon With No Shot Fired and Nobody Ashore, Her on Her Side on a Bar of Hard Sand at Half Past Two and Not Up Since, a Season's Work and a Hull Gone Inside One Hour and the Whole of That Cost on Six Men Before Four o'Clock and on No Sheet in This Harbor, About Nine People Standing on the Boards at the Back of That Market from Two O'Clock and Nobody Going Down to Her, Nine Hulls About Two Sea Miles Off the Head of That Mole and Not One of Them Said to Have Eased, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-Four and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Forty-Four*

Saturday the second day of April, with the boards at the back of that market standing full from about ten in the morning and a wind off the water at about four hours, and a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Saturday carrying nothing but its own work: no charge on that cistern, the covers lying on it unbroken from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. At about two in the afternoon a Crown vessel of about ninety tons, which had been standing on and off a course-mark about a mile off that point since before the middle of the month, was taken off that course. No shot was fired on her or by her. Nobody came off her and nobody in this harbor was told anything about it by anybody. She came down onto a bar of hard sand at the mouth of that channel at about half past two and lay over with her bilge in it and she has not been up since, and a season's work of gear, cordage and bottom work went into her over a year and about six men carry the whole of that loss, and not one of the six stands on any sheet in this harbor. That cost is paid inside the hour in which it happened, it is not softened anywhere in these ten days, and it does not go onto the money sheet, because a hull on sand is not a line and it is not in a minute. At about ten in the morning the day rate came into that yard at a hundred and sixty-eight pence and at about four in the afternoon the identical figure went back out of it. Lying under that slate when the day shut was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence adrift of a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, that drift being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet has cost this harbor one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and all that has ever been put into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and what is not on it at all is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of what that vessel lost this afternoon came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and not one of them is improved. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and nineteen days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty-one. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-nine days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-fifth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-three days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirteenth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and thirtieth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty-one days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole still carries five papers and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. The offer of four lines is still behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing written under its fourth line, and the answer to it stands at no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse the basket stands where it has stood since the second of this month with the paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of those nine has eased, and the vessel that went on that sand this afternoon is not one of the nine. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and nineteen days, one more than it was yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven that morning the vessel was lying on her course-mark with her bow swinging off it in about four hours of wind, and there was not one boat in this harbor close enough to her to be told anything, and about nine people on the boards at the back of that market looked out at her between one thing and another and about five did not look at all.

She had been on and off that mark for eight days. Her gear was in good order and her bottom had been looked at in February by a man from the third lay and put in a paper that nobody has ever read out. Nobody in this harbor had any business with her and nobody went down to the mole head to look at her in the morning, because there is nothing anybody here can do about a vessel on a course-mark, and that is not an opinion, it is what a course-mark is.

---

At about two in the afternoon, with the tide making, she came off the mark.

There was no gun on that coast and none was fired. There was no shout and no signal and nobody in that channel. She put her helm over of her own accord, or a man on her put it over, and in about four minutes she was down on the bar at the mouth of that channel and she took the sea on her bilge and went over until there was nothing left on her high side for a man to stand on and stayed there.

About nine people on those boards saw the whole of it from two o'clock and nobody moved for about six minutes afterwards. About four people in that market did not see it and found out at about four. Nobody in this harbor went down to her. That is not a thing anybody decided; there is no berth at that bar and no crane at it and no cart road to it, and a hull of ninety tons lying on hard sand in four hours of wind is a different object at two o'clock from the boat a man has been putting money into since the autumn.

---

A man of about thirty-two went down to the head of that mole at about half past two with one other man in the flat boat and stood off her at about two hundred yards for about twenty minutes and came back at about three.

What he could see from two hundred yards was that the sand under her was hard and that she was taking the sea on her bilge and that she was not going to be walked off in that tide, and that what was on her was a season's working: her coil, her ground tackle, the pump that had been overhauled in February, the boiler, the two bunks, and about six men whose hands had been on all of it since the autumn. He came back up the mole with his hands wet to the elbow and he said one sentence at the flat of that bench stone, and that is about thirty-seven words, and nobody argued with it and nobody thanked him for it.

"**That hull will be on that sand until the winter, and there is nothing in this yard that will lift her off it, and I would not go within twenty yards of her with what we have.**"

---

At about half past three a man of about thirty-one of the third lay, who had been on that vessel in the morning and who was in that market on that afternoon, said one thing out loud in about nine people and nobody had asked him for it.

"**I took that wheel myself at two o'clock and there was nobody else on it, because the man who keeps her course went below at one and I did not go and wake him and I did not signal anybody and I did not shout.** I want that said before anybody in this yard starts putting a reason on it. There was no order. Nobody told me to do it. I looked at her and I looked at the water and I put her round and I have been standing on this stone since.

**I am not asking anybody to agree with me. I am asking that nobody in this harbor goes down there and does something clever to her, because there was a man on the mole head at half past two who did the right thing and did not go within twenty yards, and there is a version of this afternoon where four of us go out in a punt with a block and a chain and put her further in.** She is lying where she can be got off in the autumn. She is not lying where she can be got off this week. And I have put a man's season on that sand and I will carry it and I am not going to be told about it."

That is about two hundred and twenty-two words and it went to about nine people and it was carried to the rest of that market by nobody at all, and about five people in that yard said nothing about it and about four of them did not let themselves be counted on either side.

Nobody argued with him. Nobody agreed with him. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he was wrong.

---

The cost of that afternoon was finished being finished by about four o'clock and it was not softened afterwards on any of these ten days.

Her hull was a hull and it stays on that sand until the winter and there is no figure anywhere in this country that prices a hull. Her season's work went with her: the coil, the ground tackle, the pump, the boiler, the two bunks, and every hour that six men had put into keeping that bottom in order since the autumn. Those six men are not on any sheet in this harbor and this harbor has no figure for what they lose between this afternoon and the first of June, and nobody is going to be asked about it, and a page that printed what it cost would be printing the cost of men to men. Nobody died on that sand. Nobody was named. No casualty figure was written on any page in this harbor and none is going to be.

And Calder came back up that mole at about three and put the boat away and did not go into the water again that day, and he has not gone into the water on any day since.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and sixty-three words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a bar of hard sand costs a man who keeps the helm.

"**There is a Crown vessel on the sand at the mouth of that channel and I am going to say what she cost in my own words and not one of those words will be a figure, because a Crown man is not on any sheet in this harbor and I am not going to start one.**

The figure I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Under this slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**Not one boat of this harbor put out after her.** I want that in the record in my own mouth and in nobody else's, because this is the eleventh week and the only thing this yard has ever done well is that it does not go and be clever. A man went out to two hundred yards at half past two and came back and said one sentence about it and then put the boat away and has not been in the water since. There is no page in this country that says a boat took a season of work out of this harbor, because it did not take it out of this harbor, and I am not going to put a thing on a sheet that makes it look like we paid for it.

**And I am not going to say anything about why a man of about thirty-one put a wheel over at two o'clock.** He said it himself in nine people at half past three and I heard him and about four others did and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody here is going to say he was wrong and nobody here is going to say he was right. The eleven words that are on a page in this harbor from the winter are on a page from a man with a boat of about fourteen tons and nobody has answered them either, and I am not going to start answering one man's words with another man's.

The sheet with the fourteen things on it stood at the end of that stall where it stood at ten this morning and I did not read one line of it out and no figure on it moved by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off.

At about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second and I did not read that leaf out loud. The first of those two figures is three hundred and fifty-four and the second is one thousand six hundred and forty-four. My third is empty and there is a boy's hand at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water. My fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and the reason for that is not on a page of mine."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a hull on the sand. That shed was clear of everybody by about six with its door standing open on a slip with nobody on it, and the boards at the back of that market emptied the way boards do, and the water at the mouth of that channel went on doing what it had been doing since half past two.

THE COST OF IT WAS A SEASON'S WORK AND A HULL AND IT WAS FINISHED BEING FINISHED BY ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND IT IS CARRIED BY ABOUT SIX MEN WHO ARE ON NO SHEET IN THIS HARBOR AND IT IS NOT SOFTENED ON ANY PAGE AND IT IS NOT A LINE ON THE MONEY SHEET AND IT IS NOT IN A MINUTE. NOBODY DIED ON THAT SAND AND NO BODY WAS NAMED AND NO CASUALTY FIGURE WAS WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE. THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WAS NOT READ OUT OF AND NOT ONE FIGURE ON IT WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. WHAT THAT SHEET IS SHORT BY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS; WHAT HAS EVER COME INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND WHAT HAS GONE OUT OF THIS HARBOR ON IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, LIES UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason I have written down what a man said and not what I think of it is that he said it in nine people and I heard it and about four others did and the rest of that market was not told, and that a thing said in a market about a vessel on a sand is carried to the rest of that market by nobody, and that in eleven weeks this yard has learned four different ways of carrying a thing and every one of them carries a part of it and drops the rest;
