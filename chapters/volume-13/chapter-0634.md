# Chapter 634

*Friday the Fifteenth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered and Untouched from Eight Until Four, One Man Down at About Seven in the Morning and Up at About Twenty-Three Minutes Past Seven, Being Nineteen Minutes of the Twenty-Five, and a Man of About Thirty-One Wedged in the Forepeak of That Boat With a Thwart Across Him and About Two Feet of Water in the Peak, and a Cutoff Pulled at About Seven and Twenty Minutes Without Asking Anybody and Three Minutes Taken to Come Up After It Was Pulled, in About Three and a Half Fathoms, and the Second of Three Pressure Bells Counted at About Four in the Afternoon in a Back Room off a Chandler's Counter by a Man of About Fifty-Three With a Boat of About Fourteen Tons, Three Minutes and Forty Seconds, a Slate Under a Bell in That Shed Carrying Two Figures and Room for One More, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Seven and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Nine*

Friday the fifteenth day of April, with that market trading through the morning and a yard below it, a shed in that yard, and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Friday with nothing standing against it: that butt covered from eight until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe, and the wind still in the north-west and the rain still set in and the sea outside that mole head two feet higher than it was on Monday. A man of about thirty-two went down at about seven this morning and was up at about twenty-three minutes past seven, which is nineteen minutes, and a man of about thirty-one came out of the forepeak of that boat with a thwart across him and about two feet of water in the peak. At about seven and twenty minutes the woman in the tender pulled the cutoff, which is a wire she bent into the ring of her own line at ten to five on Tuesday morning with her own hands and kept the loose end of herself, and she did not ask anybody whether she was going to, and he was on the steps of that tender about three minutes later. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a cutoff pulled without asking is not a line on that sheet and a man out of a forepeak is not a line on that sheet, and the sheet settles those days when it settles them. That yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and put the same sum out of it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, stayed on that slate. A wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and that slate is six hundred and twelve pence behind it, being three marks eleven shillings. One hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, is what that fitting-out sheet has carried out of this harbor; ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, is everything anybody has ever put into it; and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is what lies between those two figures. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the fifth of them is the only one of the nine that is a distance rather than a piece of paper. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-six days. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-two days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-two days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-four. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-eighth night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-four days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and eleventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-sixth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-third day. Five papers stand on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. Those four lines are behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them stands at no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. That boathouse still holds a basket, and there is a paper in that basket and there is nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole there lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-two days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about seven this morning Calder Marr went down the third time in four days and was on the bottom in about three and a half fathoms in the dark under the forepeak of that boat, where a man of about thirty-one had been lying with a thwart across him since Monday and about two feet of water in that peak and rising.

He got the bight of the second line under the man's arms and up over his shoulders and the man came off the thwart, and at about seven and twenty minutes the wire came out of that ring.

He was not in the water by choice. He was in the water because the woman on the surface of that boat pulled a bent wire out of a ring she had made herself, and she did not ask him whether it was time and nobody on that deck asked her whether it was time either, and he was on the steps of that tender at about twenty-three minutes past seven with nineteen minutes gone, and about two of the twenty-five were left and were not used.

Nobody argued with her about it. That is the whole of what a cutoff is and there is no other sentence about it in this yard.

---

The man of about thirty-one was put into the wheelhouse of that tender on a thwart and wrapped in a sailcloth and given hot water from the tender's own stove, and at about half past nine that morning the two Nethermorrow men went back out to that bar with the ebb and he went with them.

At about four in the afternoon, in a back room off a chandler's counter in that front street, a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons counted a pressure bell that hangs there and wrote the gap on the back of a chit with a pencil he had to borrow.

Three minutes and forty seconds is what he got between those two strikes. He put the day and the hour and the place on the chit and then stood there a while with the pencil in his hand and said nothing, and nobody in that room asked him anything about it, and nobody in that room has answered his eleven words and nobody is going to and nobody told him he was wrong.

The chit went on that wall under the bell at about a quarter past four with a nail and a strip of oiled paper over it against the damp, and it is the second of the three.

---

At about half past four a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing, and about nine people were at the flat of that bench stone, and about four of them could read what was nailed to the wall above it.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 547 words, and nobody in that shed was asked whether a cutoff ought to have gone two minutes earlier.

"**A man of about thirty-one came out of the forepeak of that boat this morning with a thwart across him and two feet of water in that peak, and the wire came out of that ring at about seven and twenty minutes and he was on the steps of that tender three minutes later, and that is nineteen minutes, and there were two left and they were not used.**

**Nobody has argued with that woman about pulling it, including me, and I want to say in nine people that a cutoff which has to be asked about is not a cutoff.** She bent that wire into that ring herself at ten to five on Tuesday morning before anybody was in the water and she has kept the loose end of it ever since, and that is the entire authority she has and it is enough.

Two figures are on a wall in this shed and a third is on a chit in a back room off a chandler's counter, and every one of them is three minutes and forty seconds, and they are not being added to anything and they are not being added to the ninety-four seconds, and I would like the second half of that sentence written down by somebody in this yard.

**That is the whole of what I am going to say about the sea.** It is still in the north-west and the glass is still down and the moorings in that outer reach are still going, and nobody has put a state on any of the nine names on that sheet of days and nobody in this harbor has asked any of those nine crews anything this month.

That fitting-out sheet is where the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month left it, being one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. That slate carries twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the berth fund put a hundred and sixty-eight pence into that yard at about ten and took a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

Nobody has read one word out of the fourteen at the end of that stall in eleven days and not one figure on that sheet has moved by one. The count of positions has not been taken again, and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column and there is no figure against it for any day.

One entry went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and sixty-seven stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and nine against the second, my third is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand being at the head of that column with his surname on no page and himself off the water, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nineteen minutes with a wire in a ring. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and under a bell on the wall of it there were two figures and room for one more.

**ONE MAN WENT DOWN AT ABOUT SEVEN ON THE FIFTEENTH OF APRIL AND CAME UP AT ABOUT TWENTY-THREE MINUTES PAST SEVEN, BEING NINETEEN MINUTES OF THE TWENTY-FIVE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE CAME OUT OF THE FOREPEAK OF THAT BOAT WITH A THWART ACROSS HIM. THE CUTOFF WAS PULLED AT ABOUT SEVEN AND TWENTY MINUTES BY A WOMAN WHO HAD BENT THE WIRE INTO THAT RING HERSELF AT TEN TO FIVE ON THE THIRTEENTH AND HAD KEPT THE LOOSE END HERSELF SINCE, AND SHE DID NOT ASK ANYBODY, AND THREE MINUTES WERE TAKEN TO COME UP AFTER IT WAS PULLED AND TWO OF THE TWENTY-FIVE WERE LEFT AND NOT USED, IN ABOUT THREE AND A HALF FATHOMS. TWO PRESSURE BELLS IN TWO PLACES HAVE NOW BEEN COUNTED BY TWO PEOPLE AND BOTH FIGURES ARE THREE MINUTES AND FORTY SECONDS, AND NEITHER IS ADDED TO THE NINETY-FOUR SECONDS AND NEITHER IS ADDED TO THE OTHER. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THAT SHEET OF MONEY CARRIES ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OF OUTGOINGS, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE OF INGOINGS, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE SHORTFALL IT STANDS AT IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about seven in the morning a man of about thirty-two went down for the third time in four days and was on the bottom in about three and a half fathoms in the dark under the forepeak of that boat, where a man of about thirty-one had been lying with a thwart across him since Monday with about two feet of water in that peak and rising; and that he got the bight of the second line under that man's arms and up over his shoulders and that the man came off that thwart, and that at about seven and twenty minutes the wire came out of that ring; and that he was not in the water by choice and that he was in the water because the woman on the surface of that boat pulled a bent wire out of a ring she had made herself and did not ask him whether it was time and nobody on that deck asked her whether it was time either, and that he was on the steps of that tender at about twenty-three minutes past seven with nineteen minutes gone and about two of the twenty-five left and not used, and that nobody argued with her about it; and that the man of about thirty-one was put into the wheelhouse of that tender on a thwart and wrapped in a sailcloth and given hot water off that tender's own stove, and that at about half past nine that morning the two Nethermorrow men went back out to that bar with the ebb and he went with them; and that at about four in the afternoon, in a back room off a chandler's counter in that front street, a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons counted a pressure bell hanging there and wrote the gap on the back of a chit with a pencil he had to borrow, and that the gap was three minutes and forty seconds, and that he put the day and the hour and the place on that chit and then stood there a while with the pencil in his hand and said nothing, and that nobody in that room asked him anything about it and nobody in that room has answered his eleven words and nobody told him he was wrong; and that the chit went on that wall under that bell at about a quarter past four with a nail and a strip of oiled paper over it against the damp, and that it is the second of the three; and that at about half past four a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 547 words in about nine people, and that he said in it that nobody has argued with the woman who pulled that cutoff and that a cutoff which has to be asked about is not a cutoff; and that the first name on that sheet of days, which is the Morrow Line, is still in the held column and is not struck and is still one of the nine; and that one entry went in on that first column at about six that evening and five went in on the second, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the thirty-fourth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and sixty-seven and the second is one thousand seven hundred and nine, and that not one line went in on the third of those four, a boy's hand standing at the head of that column with his surname on no page and himself off the water, and that the fourth of them is still standing at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that under a bell on the wall of that shed there were two figures at six that evening and room for one more.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about seven and twenty minutes in the morning the reason I have not written one word of argument about that wire in this column is that a yard that argues about a cutoff after it has been pulled has understood nothing about why the cutoff exists, and that the reason a wire was in that ring in the first place is that there was a reason for a man to be in the water at all, and the two of those are the same reason, and that anybody who wants to know why a man came up at nineteen minutes has to read the slate from twenty to five on Tuesday and not this column; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing about a man of fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons standing in a chandler's back room with a borrowed pencil is that he has eleven words on a page in this harbor that nobody has answered since before the winter, and that a man who has said a thing once and been answered with a better argument has been answered, and that a man who has said a thing once and not been answered has not been answered, and that him counting a bell this afternoon is not an answer to his own eleven words and it is not a way of making it one, and that I stood nine feet away and said nothing; and that at about ten to five in the afternoon the reason I have not put one penny of four days of diving on that money sheet is that the sheet carries a day when a yard settles it and not a day when a man is tired, and that a sheet written in the middle of a thing is a sheet nobody can check afterwards, including the man who wrote it; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have not written one word in this column about that sea since Tuesday is that I said on Tuesday in nine people that I do not know what it is, and that a man who has said he does not know does not go round this week finding out; and that at about half past five in the evening the reason this slip ends on a number and not on a man is that the three figures under those three bells are the only thing in this fortnight that a stranger can come here and check with a leaf and a pencil, and that there is one more bell to be counted and I am not going to be the one who counts it or the one who adds them up; and that at about midday the reason the fourteen on that sheet have not been read out of for eleven days is that a yard in a bad week does not go opening the hardest things it owns, and that this yard has one bad week in it this month and this is the week.*
