# Chapter 605

*Thursday the Seventeenth Day of March, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Thirty-Fourth Charge on That Cistern in Eleven Trips by Hand at About Eight in the Morning and Walked On by None of It, the Butt Behind That Market Full to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four, a Man of About Forty-Three Coming Up That Coast Road on Foot over Four Days Off the Water Since the Winter With a Thing He Took With Him Standing in About Nine People at About Nine in the Morning, that Thing Put on the Flat of the Bench Stone at About Half Past Nine and Standing There Until About Four in the Afternoon and Not Opened and Nobody Asking Him What It Is, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Out Loud in About sixty Words that She Has Not Asked and Is Not Going To, the Two Sheets at the End of That Stall Named Apart Out Loud Twice More, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the seventeenth day of March, with the market working from six, and the cistern at the back of it charged before the market opened, and about nine people in the yard below it at half past five in the afternoon.

**A rota day on a Thursday, and neither a wage Tuesday nor a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market went up for the hundred and thirty-fourth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and no water was walked on, and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, and the butt stood up to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon with no bucket out of it, and the two men who carry it up that ladder have still never once been on that rota board and have never once asked to be. A man of about forty-three came up that coast road on foot over four days and got into this harbor at first light on that Thursday, and he had a thing with him, and that thing was put on the flat of that bench stone at about half past nine and stood there until about four in the afternoon and it was not opened, and nobody in that harbor asked him what it was. No hand added a line to the money sheet on that Thursday and no hand took one off, and a thing on a bench stone is not a line on that sheet. That yard takes a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and pays a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about four in the afternoon, one mark and eightpence each way, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, at four o'clock, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of it. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and a man who walks four days up a coast road is not a penny of that. That shed has not had a tin opened in it since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and four days of a man's boots and a canvas bag have not been paid for out of the bag under the slate, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes four days of a man's boots and a night of his sleep and a canvas bag.**

**The nine below are a roster of durations and not of persons, and the only person in it is the ninth, who is not saying anything. A bellwright of this harbor has now not been asked for three hundred and five days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and three. The two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and forty-three. The bench under that pump, the four hundred and ninth night without it. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-seven days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninety-seventh. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fourteenth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and forty-five days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the demand for the returns is in a basket in that boathouse with the paper still in it and nobody has looked inside that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is a hundred and three days, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That cistern was charged a little after eight, in eleven trips, the same number it has been charged in for a year and a bit, and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it.

A man of about forty-three came into that yard at about nine with the road on his boots, and he works a stacker at a yard on that coast and he has been off the water since the winter, and he is not a soldier and not a clerk and nobody in that harbor has any other use for him. He had been carried on a paper the Crown keeps of the hands on that water, and he was carried on it in the winter, and he took himself off it in the winter, and he has said those eleven words and no more and his name is on no page in this harbor.

---

He had a thing with him and he put it on the flat of that bench stone at about half past nine, and it is a canvas bag with a strap and it sits on its side and something in it shifts when the bench stone is knocked, and it stood there until about four in the afternoon in front of about nine people who looked at it and did not touch it.

Nobody in that yard asked him what it was. Not the woman of thirty of no office, and not the man of about thirty-one of the second lay, and not the man of about thirty-three who hires hands, and not the man of about fifty-six who lands fish, and not the man who keeps the leaf. A woman of thirty of no office said one thing about it in about 60 words, in about nine people, at about eleven in the morning, and then she did not say anything else about it that day or the next.

"**I have not asked and I am not going to, and I want it said out loud in nine people that I have not asked, because a person who walks four days up a coast road with something in his hand is not required to open it on a bench stone in a market.** It is not going on that page at the end of that stall and it is not going on that sheet that carries the money and it is not going in a minute, and I have said all three of those out loud and I am naming all three so that nobody in this harbor can say afterwards that one of them was meant. **And he is not being put on anybody's crew today and he is not going to be put on one this week, and if he wants a day asked for by name there is a hundred and sixty-eight pence in it at four o'clock and he can have it or not have it and nobody is going to ask him which.**"

About four people heard that and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody argued with her, and he was on nobody's crew at about eleven that morning and he was on nobody's crew at about four that afternoon, and at about four he put the bag over his shoulder and took it away with him and it was not opened and it did not come up again that week.

---

At about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 520 words, and the flat of that stone was bare by then and had been for about half an hour, and nobody in that shed was asked what a canvas bag is.

"**Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings.** Unmoved in every hand, and I have said the word bag twice in this slip and both times it is the same twenty-eight pence under the slate, and I have written bag twice this evening and both of those are the same twenty-eight pence and not the twenty-eight pence that was on a bench stone all day.

**That cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-fourth time before that market opened and not one bucket came out of it, and the two men who carry that water up that ladder are still not on that rota board and have still not asked to be, and the name at the head of it is still off it, and I have stopped writing the same three facts about that board in three different shapes and I am going to write them in one shape from now on.**

A man of about forty-three came up that coast road on foot and got in at first light and put a thing on the flat of that bench stone at about half past nine and it stood there until about four and it was not opened and nobody asked him what it was. **A woman of thirty of no office said in nine people that she has not asked and is not going to, and she named the three pages it is not going on, out loud, in order, because in eleven weeks the only thing that has gone wrong in that market is two things being put in one sentence.** He is not on anybody's crew and he was not put on one, and his name is on no page of mine, and I did not write down the eleven words he said to me at about eleven this morning and I am not going to write them down tonight, and that is a different thing from not having heard them.

One line went in on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and I did not read that leaf out loud today and I am not going to read it out loud on any day of this week, and this is the fifth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth. The third of my four is empty. The fourth is still one hundred and twenty-five and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and that reason is not on a page of mine."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a man coming four days up a road, and that shed was empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it. and the cistern behind that market was still up to the rim of the second hoop.

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED ON THAT THURSDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FOURTH TIME, IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND, WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, WITH THE COVERS BACK BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE AND THE BUTT FULL TO THE RIM OF THE SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR, AND THE TWO MEN WHO CARRY IT UP THAT LADDER HAVE NEVER ONCE BEEN ON THAT ROTA BOARD NOR ASKED TO BE. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-THREE CAME UP THAT COAST ROAD ON FOOT OVER FOUR DAYS AND PUT A THING ON THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT HALF PAST NINE AND IT STOOD THERE UNTIL ABOUT FOUR AND IT WAS NOT OPENED, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR ASKED HIM WHAT IT WAS, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 60 WORDS THAT SHE HAS NOT ASKED AND IS NOT GOING TO AND NAMED THE THREE PAGES IT IS NOT GOING ON, AND HE IS ON NOBODY'S CREW AND WAS NOT PUT ON ONE AND HIS NAME IS ON NO PAGE. THE MONEY IS UNMOVED AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the seventeenth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirty-fourth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and walked on by none of it, and that the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, and that the butt stood full to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon with no bucket out of it, and that the two men who carry that water up that ladder have still never once been on that rota board nor ever once asked to be on it, and that the name at the head of that board is still off it; and that a man of about forty-three came into that yard at about nine in the morning with the road on his boots, and that he works a stacker at a yard on that coast, and that he has been off the water since the winter, and that he is not a soldier and not a clerk, and that he had been carried on a paper the Crown keeps of the hands on that water and was carried on it in the winter and took himself off it in the winter, and that he said eleven words to the man who keeps the leaf at about eleven in the morning and no more, and that his name is on no page in that harbor; and that he had a thing with him and put it on the flat of that bench stone at about half past nine, and that it is a canvas bag with a strap sitting on its side and that something in it shifts when that bench stone is knocked, and that it stood there until about four in the afternoon in front of about nine people who looked at it and did not touch it; and that nobody in that yard asked him what it was, and that not the woman of thirty of no office nor the man of about thirty-one of the second lay nor the man of about thirty-three who hires hands nor the man of about fifty-six who lands fish nor the man who keeps the leaf asked him; and that a woman of thirty of no office said one thing about it at about eleven in the morning in about 60 words in about nine people, being that she has not asked and is not going to, and that a person who walks four days up a coast road with something in a hand is not required to open it on a bench stone in a market, and that it is not going on the page at the end of that stall and not going on the sheet that carries the money and not going in a minute, and that she named all three out loud so that nobody could say afterwards that one of them was meant, and that he is not being put on anybody's crew and was not put on one, and that a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence and he can have it or not have it and nobody is going to ask him which; and that about four people heard that and the rest of that market was not told, and that at about four he put the bag over his shoulder and took it away with him and it was not opened and it did not come up again that week; and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was in the bag at the close of the said day, and that the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence and that is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of it; and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on the said day and nothing came off it, and that a thing on a bench stone is not a line on that sheet, and that that sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that a man who walks four days up a coast road is not a penny of that; and that a line went in on the first column at about six in the evening against the said day, and that the first column carries three hundred and thirty-eight and the second at one thousand five hundred and sixty-four, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that this is the fifth day of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past nine in the morning the reason nobody in that yard asked that man what was in the bag is that this harbor has spent eleven weeks learning to say out loud what it does not know, and that the first thing that goes wrong in a yard like this is somebody asking a question in order to be seen asking it, and that a man of about thirty-three had his mouth open twice this morning and said nothing twice and I noticed both times and I have not told him I noticed and I am not going to; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason a woman of thirty of no office said out loud that she has not asked is that an unasked question in a market of nine people gets asked again by somebody else within a day, and that the only way to stop that is to put the not-asking into nine people's ears where nine people can hear it, and that that is a thing this harbor learned about a basket in a boathouse in a different month and has not used until now; and that at about eleven o'clock the reason the eleven words that man said to me are not on any page of mine, and I have said that out loud in this shed, is that he came four days up a coast road to say them and he said them once, and that a page is a place a thing gets said again, and that I have no authority in this harbor to be the place a thing gets said again; and that at about half past five the reason I have written that I am not going to write those words down tonight, when the whole of a slip in this yard is a place things get written down, is that there is a difference between a man keeping a leaf because a stranger has to be able to count it and a man keeping a leaf because a man said something once, and that the second of those two is not a reason and it is a habit, and that habits have cost this harbor more than reasons have; and that at about six o'clock the reason there is nothing in this slip about what is in the bag is that nobody in this harbor knows what is in the bag, including me, and that a slip at half past five that said so would be the fourth thing in eleven weeks this yard has said out loud that it does not know, and that four is not many and I have stopped apologising for the number.*
