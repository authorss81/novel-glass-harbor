# Chapter 648

*Friday the Twenty-Ninth Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Covered from Eight Until Four and Untouched, About Twenty People Standing on the Boards at the Back of That Market From Ten in the Morning of Which About Eleven Cannot Read a Line of What Was on the Stone in Front of Them, a Woman of About Forty-Four With a Chandler's Counter Reading the Face of That Paper Once in About One Hundred and Fifty-Two Words and Stopping at the Seal and Not Reading the Name Under It, and the Name at the Foot of That Seal on the Paper and in No Mouth and in No Mouth for the Rest of This Run, a Man of About Fifty-Three With a Boat of About Fourteen Tons Saying One Thing Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon in About One Hundred and Ninety-Five Words About What That Paper Does Not Do, and Nobody Agreeing With Him and Nobody Arguing and Nobody Thanking Him and Nobody Telling Him He Was Wrong, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Eighty-One and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Friday the twenty-ninth day of April, with about twenty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning and a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Friday with nothing standing against it: that butt covered from eight until four with no charge on it, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. That paper was read out once this morning on those boards, in about 152 words, by a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter in that front street, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read a line of it, and she stopped where the paper stops and she did not read the name under the seal and nobody asked her to read it twice. It is a document and not a man and no man came with it and none came into that yard and none spoke. It says on its own face what it did and it says on its own face what it does not establish, and the limits of it were not stated by the paper and were stated this afternoon by a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons in about 195 words in about nine people, and nobody agreed with him and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him he was wrong. Nothing was nailed to the board at the head of that mole and nothing was put on that board, and that paper is lying on the bench in that shed with the fold still in it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a paper read out in a market is not a line on that sheet. A day's work at a berth came into that yard at a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and what the slate was holding at the end of the day was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, that is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. Not one of the three figures on that sheet moved today and not one of them is going to move this month. It carries one line, being one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it does not carry one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed today. Since the twenty-ninth day of April past no tin in that shed has been opened.**

**The nine went on a day each and the eighth of them is the only one of the nine nailed to a wall in a street with a seal on it that nobody in this harbor has ever spoken about. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifty-seventh day. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighty-six days. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and forty-six days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty-eight. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and fifty-second night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and seventy days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and twenty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fortieth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty-eight days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened, and nothing was nailed to that board today and the paper that was read out this morning is not on it. Under the fourth of those four lines there is no writing and there never has been any, and the answer to them is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse there is a basket, and there is a paper in the basket, and there is nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole there lie nine hulls this afternoon, nine were lying there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said nothing about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-six days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten in the morning the woman of about forty-four with the chandler's counter in that front street stood at the edge of those boards with that paper in her two hands and about twenty people in front of her, and about eleven of the twenty could not read a line of what she was holding.

She read it once, out loud, in about 152 words, and she stopped at the seal, and she did not read the name under the seal, and nobody in that market asked her to read it again and nobody asked her to read that name.

"**That office did not make what happened in the water three hundred and twelve years ago, did not order it and has never said it did. Thirteen years ago that office signed a transfer of evidence and the account that came with that transfer was not true in the particulars set out below this line. The particulars are the dimensions of a space the account calls empty, the hour of a line that was cut from the shore side, and the number of hands the account does not mention. This sheet does not put back into any community any record that community has lost, and it does not return to any person any voice of any person, and it does not open anything that is shut. It is a statement by that office. It is not a witness. It may not be asked a question. It stands until that office withdraws it.**"

That is the whole of what was read out in that market this morning and it was read out once.

---

At about two in the afternoon a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons stood at the back of that market with his cap in his hands and said one thing out loud in about nine people, in about 195 words, and nobody had asked him to and nobody said anything after him.

"**I have been on this coast forty-one years and I have said eleven words in a book in that shed in the winter and nobody has ever answered me, and I am not standing here to be answered and I know what I am.**

**Here is what that paper does not do, and it is the only part of this morning that belongs to me. It does not give anybody back anything. It takes nothing back from the Crown either, because it does not say what is going to be given up, and a paper that names a wrong and does not name a price is a paper about being right.**

**And it does not make that record true. It makes that record established, and there is a difference, and the difference is a name at the bottom of it. I have known men sign things they were not asked to sign and I have signed two myself.**

So I am not thanking her for reading it and I am not thanking him for not putting it on a board, and nobody in this market needs to thank anybody for a morning like this one."

Nobody in that yard agreed with him out loud, nobody argued with him, nobody thanked him, nobody told him he was wrong, and about four people in that nine heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands, who read a bearing off the head of that mole on the Friday of last week, stood at the edge of that market for the whole of that morning and said two sentences.

"**That is the cleanest thing anybody has put on this coast since the account of the fourteenth, and it is cleanest because it is not wrong about anything I can check. I am not putting my name against it and I am not taking my name off it.**"

Nobody asked him what he meant by cleanest and nobody asked him about the nine cut into the casting of the bell on that mole head.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 601 words, and nobody in that shed was asked for a second reading of anything.

"**About twenty people on those boards from ten this morning and about eleven of them could not read a line of what was in that woman's hands, and she read it once, and she stopped at the seal, and nobody asked her to read it again and nobody asked her to read that name, and the name at the foot of that seal is on the paper and is in no mouth and it is not going to be in a mouth this month or the next one.**

**I am not going to say what that paper is. I am not a man for it. I am going to say what happened in this yard this morning, because a page that records what a market did is worth more than a page that records what a piece of paper meant.**

It was read out once. It was not read out twice. Nothing was nailed to that board at the head of that mole and nothing was put on that board, and that paper is lying on the bench in this shed with the fold still in it. A man of about fifty-three said at two this afternoon that it gives nobody back anything and takes nothing back either, and that it does not make a record true, it makes it established, and nobody agreed with him and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him he was wrong.

The money sheet did not move and it carries one line. What that sheet carries out is one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings; what it carries in is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and the figure it does not carry is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. **Everything standing in this harbor is as it was at ten this morning, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence did not come down that board by one.**

The sheet at the end of that stall with the fourteen things on it was not read out of today, no figure on it went up or down by one, and none of those fourteen was added to and none taken off. The count of positions was not taken again and not on any day of this fortnight. The fifth word is not a column, and not one day of this month carries a figure against it. The three columns of that sheet of days have not been added up today and that sum is not in this slip.

Twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lies under that slate, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight was paid out of it at four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and forty-six days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.

One line went in on that first column this evening and five on the second, and no reading of it was done, and it stands at three hundred and eighty-one and one thousand seven hundred and seventy-nine, the third of them has nothing in it and the fourth is one hundred and twenty-five and takes nothing."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a morning on those boards. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the woman of about forty-four shut her shop at six and the boy of about fifteen carried her last crate out to the cart and did not ask her what was on the paper.

**THAT PAPER WAS READ OUT ONCE ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING, IN ABOUT 152 WORDS, BY A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WITH A CHANDLER'S COUNTER, IN FRONT OF ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT ELEVEN CANNOT READ A LINE OF IT, AND SHE STOPPED AT THE SEAL AND DID NOT READ THE NAME UNDER THAT SEAL, AND NOBODY ASKED HER TO READ IT AGAIN. THE NAME AT THE FOOT OF THAT SEAL IS ON THE PAPER AND IS IN NO MOUTH. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS SAID AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT 195 WORDS IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WHAT THAT PAPER DOES NOT DO, AND NOBODY AGREED WITH HIM AND NOBODY ARGUED AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NOBODY TOLD HIM HE WAS WRONG. NOTHING WAS NAILED TO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AND NOTHING WAS PUT ON THAT BOARD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. ONE LINE IS ON THAT SHEET OF MONEY AND NO SECOND ONE IS GOING ON IT THIS MONTH. THE FIGURE IT CARRIES OUT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, THE FIGURE IT CARRIES IN IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE FIGURE BETWEEN THEM IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT THAT SLATE CARRIES, BEING TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about ten in the morning the woman of about forty-four with the chandler's counter in that front street stood at the edge of those boards with that paper in her two hands and about twenty people in front of her, of whom about eleven could not read a line of what she was holding; and that she read it once out loud in about 152 words and stopped at the seal, and that she did not read the name under the seal, and that nobody in that market asked her to read it again and that nobody asked her to read that name; and that one line went in on that first column this evening and five went in on the second, at about six, and that the day of that run, counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March, is the forty-eighth, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and eighty-one and the second is one thousand seven hundred and seventy-nine; the third of those four columns has taken nothing whatever, a boy's hand being at the head of it with his surname on no page and himself not on the water, and the fourth of them is where it has always been, one hundred and twenty-five, with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that the figure of four hundred and fifty pence did not come down that board on that Friday, and that nothing standing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I am writing down that it was read once and not twice is that this yard has spent thirteen weeks keeping a count of three things, and that this is not one of the three and is not going to be a fourth, and that the only count worth keeping of a morning is whether the thing that was supposed to happen happened once; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not ask that man of fifty-three one question after he had finished is that he said himself that he was not standing there to be answered, and that a yard which follows a statement with a question is a yard that thinks a statement needs tidying; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason the limits of that paper are in his mouth and not in the yard's is that the paper states what it does not establish and the limits of a paper in a market are not the same as the limits of a paper, and that one of those is a clerk's sentence and the other one is what a boatman has to decide in the morning, and that this yard has no business putting the second one in its own mouth; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason nothing was nailed to that board at the head of that mole is that a board at the head of a mole is read by people arriving from the sea and not by people who were in this market this morning, and that pinning it there would have handed a morning market to a place none of this morning was standing; and that at about seven in the evening the reason this slip ends on four columns and a bag and not on a seal is that a seal is a mark and this yard keeps figures, and that a mark a stranger cannot add up is a mark that has to be taken on somebody's word, and that is the thing this whole run has been about.*
