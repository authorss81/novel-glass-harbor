# Chapter 633

*Thursday the Fourteenth Day of April, a Rota Day, That Cistern Behind That Market Filled for the Hundred and Forty-Second Time at a Little After Eight in the Morning by Two Men Going Up a Ladder Eleven Times With a Bucket and Nothing Walked on, the Covers Back on It Before a Quarter Past Nine and the Water Up to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Boy of Nine Off the Steps of a Tender Boat at About Six and Fifty-Three in the Morning, Being Twenty-One Minutes of the Twenty-Five, and a Wheelhouse Frame Hauled Off Its Own Bolts With a Shore Tackle Over It in About Three and a Half Fathoms, and the First of Three Pressure Bells Counted at About Eleven in the Morning by a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands, Three Minutes and Forty Seconds, a Blank Line at the Head of That Rota Board Since the Spring, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Sixty-Six and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Four*

Thursday the fourteenth day of April, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before that market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about three in the afternoon and about nine in that shed at half past five.

**A Thursday rota day, and neither a wage Tuesday nor a wipe in it. That butt was filled by hand for the hundred and forty-second time at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips by two men up a ladder with a bucket, and not one bucket of it was walked on and nothing was taken up that ladder on any other errand. The covers went back on it before a quarter past nine and the water stood at the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and has been a blank line since the spring. A boy of nine came up the steps of a tender boat at about six and fifty-three in the morning, which is twenty-one minutes and not twenty-six, and the frame of that wheelhouse came off its own bolts under a shore tackle in about three and a half fathoms to do it. At about eleven this morning the first of three pressure bells in three places was counted by a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, standing in that shed, and the interval was three minutes and forty seconds, and it is on a slate by the door of that shed with the day and the hour against it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a cistern is a Monday and a Thursday thing and a pressure bell on a wall is a thing a man of about thirty-three stood next to, and neither of those is a line on that sheet. That yard drew in a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and let the identical sum go out at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the close of the day, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. What has gone out of this harbor on that money sheet is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; what has ever gone into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is what stands between those two. Not one tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and not one penny of this morning came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the third of them is the oldest of the nine and the seventh is a paper in an upper room. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-one days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and thirty-three. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-one days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and thirty-seventh night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and fifty-five days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and tenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twenty-fifth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-second day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and seventy-three days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two have never been opened by anybody here. Those four lines have not left an upper room over that market, nothing has ever been written under the fourth of them, and the answer is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. That basket in that boathouse stands with the paper in it and nothing else in it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and not one person in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-one days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and it is not a gift and he is not on the water.**

---

That ladder went up at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips by two men, and by a quarter past nine the covers were back on that butt and it stood full to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with nothing drawn out of it, and that is a hundred and forty-two times.

---

At about half past six in the morning a shore tackle went over the wheelhouse of that boat on that bar and came down on the frame, and at about six and thirty-two minutes Calder Marr went down for the second time in three days and was on the bottom in about three and a half fathoms at about a quarter to seven.

The frame had been pinned across the boy since the tide first put her broadside. He got the bight of the second line under the boy's arms and had him off the sill and up the ladder and along the bottom to the ladder head, and the frame came away off the deck at about six and forty-nine minutes with the tackle on it and swung a foot, and nobody on that deck was hurt and the tackle was recovered at about six and fifty-one minutes and not one thing was lost off that boat except a handrail.

The boy of nine came up the steps of that tender at about six and fifty-three minutes, and nobody in that tender asked him a single thing, and about six of the people in that boat shed at the end of the west quay were still in their coats at half past seven because nobody had thought to tell them it was over.

---

At about eleven in the morning a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood in that shed and put his hand on a pressure bell that hangs on the wall by the door of it, and he waited for it to strike twice and he wrote the gap on a slate that hangs under it.

The gap was three minutes and forty seconds. He wrote the day and the hour against it and the place, being that shed, and nobody asked him to do it and nobody asked him a second time about it, and about four people in that yard watched him do it and the rest of that market was not told.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing, and about nine people stood at the flat of that bench stone, and about four of them could read what was on the wall above it and about five could not and none of those five was told.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 602 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a man in a wet jersey is holding on a bar at eleven o'clock at night.

"**A hundred and forty-two charges have gone past that cistern and the name at the head of that board has been a blank line since the spring, and eleven trips by two men this morning with a bucket and not one of them walked on, and I am not going to say one word more about that butt this month than that.**

**A boy of nine came up those steps at about six and fifty-three this morning and the frame he was under came off its own bolts with a tackle on it, and a handrail went into the water and nothing else did, and that is a hundred and forty-two times and a boy in a wet jersey and not one figure on the money sheet has moved because of any of it.**

Here is the number I have got and here is where I got it. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood at a pressure bell on the wall of this shed at about eleven this morning and counted the gap between two strikes and it was three minutes and forty seconds, and it is on a slate under that bell with the day and the hour and this shed against it. **I am not going to say that figure out loud in a shed and I am not going to add it to anything.** There is a relay on this coast that answers at ninety-four seconds and there has been for a month, and that figure and this one are two different quantities and they are not going to be added together in this yard or in any slip of mine, and I would like somebody in nine people to write that down.

That slate by that door is going to carry three figures on it and not one, because two other bells in two other places are going to be counted by two other people on two other days, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil is entitled to the sum of the three when it is written under them and not before.

Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is what stands at the head of that fitting-out sheet and it has not moved off that mark since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month. Lying under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was paid into that yard at about ten and the identical sum was paid out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

The fourteen at the end of that stall were not read out of in ten days. No figure on that sheet went up or down by one and none of those fourteen was added to and none was taken off. The count of positions was not taken again and the fifth word on the back of the other page is not a column.

That first column took one entry at about six this evening and the second took five, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and sixty-six stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and four against the second, my third took nothing into it and a boy's hand is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water, and my fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a handrail into the water. That shed was bare by about six with its door open on a slip nobody was on, and the slate under that bell had one figure on it and room for two more.

**THAT CISTERN BEHIND THAT MARKET WAS FILLED BY HAND FOR THE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SECOND TIME AT A LITTLE AFTER EIGHT IN THE MORNING, ELEVEN TRIPS BY TWO MEN WITH A BUCKET AND NOT ONE BUCKET WALKED ON, THE COVERS BACK ON IT BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE AND THE WATER AT THE RIM OF ITS SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A BOY OF NINE CAME UP THE STEPS OF A TENDER BOAT AT ABOUT SIX AND FIFTY-THREE IN THE MORNING, BEING TWENTY-ONE MINUTES OF THE TWENTY-FIVE WRITTEN ON A SLATE AT TWENTY TO FIVE ON THE THIRTEENTH, AND A WHEELHOUSE FRAME CAME OFF ITS OWN BOLTS UNDER A SHORE TACKLE IN ABOUT THREE AND A HALF FATHOMS AND A HANDRAIL WENT INTO THE WATER AND NOTHING ELSE DID. THE FIRST OF THREE PRESSURE BELLS IN THREE PLACES WAS COUNTED AT ABOUT ELEVEN BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS AND THE INTERVAL WAS THREE MINUTES AND FORTY SECONDS, AND IT IS ON A SLATE UNDER THAT BELL WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR AND THE PLACE AGAINST IT, AND IT IS NOT ADDED TO THE NINETY-FOUR SECONDS. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. THE FITTING-OUT SHEET IS UNMOVED AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the fourteenth day of April at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that butt behind that market was filled by hand for the hundred and forty-second time at a little after eight in the morning, eleven trips of a bucket by two men before that market opened, not one bucket walked on and nothing taken up that ladder on any other errand, that the covers went back on it by a quarter past nine, that it stood to the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and has been a blank line since the spring and nobody in that market has offered to put it there; and that at about half past six in the morning a shore tackle went over the wheelhouse of that boat on that bar and came down on the frame, and that at about six and thirty-two minutes a man of about thirty-two went down for the second time in three days and was on the bottom in about three and a half fathoms at about a quarter to seven; and that the frame had been pinned across a boy of nine since the tide first put that boat broadside, and that he got the bight of the second line under that boy's arms and had him off the sill and up the ladder and along the bottom to the ladder head, and that the frame came away off the deck at about six and forty-nine minutes with the tackle on it and swung a foot, and that nobody on that deck was hurt and that the tackle was recovered at about six and fifty-one minutes and not one thing was lost off that boat except a handrail; and that the boy of nine came up the steps of that tender at about six and fifty-three minutes and that nobody in that tender asked him a single thing, and that about six people in that boat shed at the end of the west quay were still in their coats at half past seven because nobody had thought to tell them it was over; and that at about eleven in the morning a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood in that shed and put his hand on a pressure bell hanging on the wall by the door of it and waited for it to strike twice and wrote the gap on a slate hanging under it, and that the gap was three minutes and forty seconds, and that he wrote the day and the hour and the place against it and that nobody asked him to do it and nobody asked him a second time about it, and that about four people in that yard watched him and the rest of that market was not told; and that at about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market was asked nothing; and that the fourteen standing at the end of that stall were not read out of in ten days, and that no figure on that sheet rose or fell by a single one, and that not one of those fourteen was added to and not one was taken off; and that nobody has taken the count of positions again, and that the fifth word standing on the back of the other page is not a column and has no figure of any kind against any day; and that the three columns of that sheet of days were not counted this afternoon and that count is not said in this slip; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about 602 words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on that first column and five went in on the second, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of March is the thirty-third, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and sixty-six and the second is one thousand seven hundred and four, and that nothing at all went in on the third of those four, that a boy's hand is at the head of that column and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water, and that the fourth of them is standing at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it; and that a slate by the door of that shed had one figure on it at six that evening and room for two more.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about six and fifty-three in the morning the reason nobody in that tender said one word to a boy of nine who had been under a wheelhouse for three days is that a child who has been in the water and has come up out of it is not a person who has been given something and is therefore not a person who owes anybody anything, and that this yard has spent eleven weeks refusing to put gratitude on a page and is not going to start this month because the tide is in; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason I have written no number out of a bell in this slip is that a figure about a page is a thing nine people can argue with and a figure about a coast is a thing nine people can only repeat, and that this slate is going to end up carrying three figures and a sum and it is going to be on a wall where anybody can put a finger on it, and that a bell on a wall in a shed is the cheapest test this harbor owns; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have not written one word in this column about the ninety-four seconds is that two numbers about intervals on this coast are not the same quantity and a man who adds them together has found a third interval that nothing on this coast is answering at, and that I have said so out loud this evening in nine people so that it is on a page in this shed's own book and cannot be lost; and that at about half past seven in the morning the reason I went up to that boat shed at twenty to five instead of being in that shed when they read that slate out is that a man who writes a reason for another man and then stands there while it is read is two men doing one job, and that a reason has to have one author and a slate has to have one voice; and that at about ten to five in the afternoon the reason I have not put a halfpenny of this morning on that money sheet is that a yard settles a day when it settles it and there are eight days of this to settle and a sheet that carries eight days written on it as they happen is a sheet that cannot be checked with a pencil by anybody, including me; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I have not written about the Morrow Line for eleven days is that it is still the first name on that sheet of days and it is still in the held column, and that a boy coming off a bar with a tackle on a frame has nothing to do with what a crew says about a run, and that nobody has asked any of those nine crews anything this week and nobody is going to.*
