# Chapter 612

*Thursday the Twenty-Fourth Day of March, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Charge on That Cistern at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Covers Back on It Before a Quarter Past Nine and the Butt Standing Full to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Page Naming Four Places Read Out Twice at About Eleven in the Morning on the Boards at the Back of That Market in About Nine People by a Man of About Forty-Three Who Came Up That Coast Road on Foot Over Four Days With a Canvas Bag and Was Not Asked One Thing, the Empty Row for the Place About a Mile Inside That Port Standing Empty and Staying Empty, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Five and the Second at One Thousand Five Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Thursday the twenty-fourth day of March, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before the market opened, and about nine people in the yard below it at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Thursday rota, and no wage Tuesday in it and no wipe in it, and that cistern charged for the hundred and thirty-sixth time in eleven trips by hand and not one bucket walked on, with the covers back before a quarter past nine and the butt full to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four. The name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the two men who carry that water up that ladder have never once been on that board and have never once asked to be on it. A page naming four places was read out twice this morning in about nine people by a man who is on nobody's crew and is not to be asked anything by anybody in this harbor. Nothing went onto the money sheet in any hand and nothing came off it, and a place-name read out in a market is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of any hand. A hundred and sixty-eight pence arrives in that yard at ten and the identical figure is handed back out of it at four, and under that slate at the close of the day there is twenty-eight pence, which is two shillings and fourpence, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, which puts that slate six hundred and twelve pence short of it, being three marks eleven shillings. What has ever come into that sheet is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; what has gone out of this harbor on it is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; and what it does not carry at all is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. The last hand to touch it was on Saturday the twenty-sixth of last month. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of this morning has come out of that bag.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had without looking, and the day has not been counted out loud by anybody. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and ten days. A bellwright of that harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twelve. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and sixteenth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-four days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and eighty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fourth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-first. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-two days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers and two of the five have never been opened; the offer of four lines is still behind a door in an upper room over that market with nothing written under its fourth line, and the answer to it is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse the basket stands with the paper in it and the basket holds nothing else, and nobody in this harbor has put a hand into it. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ten days, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went up the ladder a little after eight in the morning in eleven trips, and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine, and it stood up to the rim of the second hoop behind that market until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket drawn out of it.

The name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Nobody in that market has offered to put it there and nobody in that market has asked either of those two men whether they would like it there, and that has been true for a hundred and thirty-six charges and will be true for the one on Monday.

---

At about eleven a man of about forty-three who came up that coast road on foot over four days in the winter with a canvas bag he took off a Crown list took a page off the end of that stall and read four place names out of it, twice, in about nine people on those boards, and then handed it back.

He is a stacker at a yard on that coast and he was off the water all through the winter and he is on nobody's crew and nobody in this harbor has asked him one question about his bag or about the eleven words he said once in this yard and that nobody wrote down.

He read the four names in the order they are on that page, being two places that agreed and two places that have not, and he said the words as they are printed and did not put a face on any of them and did not read the row that is empty.

---

At about half past eleven a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question about that page and got one answer, and the question was what a place that has not agreed gets on a method that asks a word of every community that has taken it on.

"**Nothing. It gets its name on that page as having not agreed and that is the whole of what is against it.** Two rows of that page stand in the refused column this morning and they have stood there since the fifteenth and they have not been touched since and they are not going to be touched this month. **Nobody in this yard is going up that coast to ask either of those two places anything, and there is a row on that page for the place about a mile inside that port and that row is empty and it is going to stay empty until somebody standing there says a word out loud, and if nobody there ever says a word then that row is the correct state of it and not a fault in the work.**

And the man who read those four names out this morning read them because he can read and about four of the nine of us could not, and I did not ask him to and I am not going to ask him anything, and that is a page working and not a favour."

Nobody argued with that and nobody asked him for a second thing, and about four people heard the whole of that answer and the rest of that market was not told.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and ninety words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a bag on a bench stone is.

"**A cistern went up that ladder this morning for the hundred and thirty-sixth time in eleven trips by hand and not one bucket of it was walked on, and that has been the first line of this slip six days running and tonight I am putting it second.** The figure at the head of it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is exactly where the twenty-sixth of last month left it, and the bag under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**The covers were back on that butt before a quarter past nine and it stood up to the rim of the second hoop until about four this afternoon, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it.**

At about eleven this morning a page went round that market read out loud twice by a man of about forty-three who came up that coast road on foot over four days in the winter with a canvas bag he took off a Crown list. **I want nine people in this shed to hold two things at the same time.** He cannot be asked one thing by anybody in this harbor and he read four place names out of that page in front of nine people and stopped when he came to the bottom of it, and nobody thanked him and I have not asked him a question this week and I am not going to.

Against the two places on that page that have not agreed there are three lines, and one of those three says that nobody in that place is to be asked why. **Nobody in this yard is going up that coast to ask either of them anything, and the row on that page for the place about a mile inside that port is empty and is going to stay empty until somebody standing there says a word out loud.** If nobody there ever says a word then that row is the correct state of it and there is nothing to put in it.

And here is the thing I have not put in this slip and am not going to. There is a man outside this shed at that bench stone with a bag on its side beside him that shifts when you knock the stone, and there has been one thing absent from this harbor's own page for five days now, and I have not asked about it and I am not going to and I have not written down what I think it is. **A keeper of a page is one remove from a man who fills in a blank for you, and the distance between those two men this week is five days of saying nothing.**

One entry went on to the first of those four columns at about six this evening and five went on to the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud tonight. The first of those two figures is three hundred and forty-five and the second is one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine, the third of that leaf is empty, and the fourth is standing where it has stood at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four place names read out twice by a man who was not asked, and by about six the shed had nobody left in it and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it. The cistern behind that market was still up to the rim of the second hoop, and the name was still off that board.

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED ON THAT THURSDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIXTH TIME, IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND, WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, THE COVERS BACK BEFORE A QUARTER PAST NINE AND THE BUTT FULL TO THE RIM OF THE SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A PAGE NAMING FOUR PLACES WAS READ OUT TWICE AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE BY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-THREE WHO IS ON NOBODY'S CREW AND WAS NOT ASKED ONE THING, AND TWO OF THOSE FOUR PLACES ARE NAMED AS HAVING NOT AGREED AND HAVE NOT BEEN ASKED ANYTHING. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN MOVED BY ONE. FOUR PLACE NAMES READ OUT TWICE IN A MARKET ARE NOT ON THAT SHEET, AND THE SHEET IS UNMOVED. WHAT IT HAS EVER TAKEN IN IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, WHICH NOBODY HAS PUT A PEN TO SINCE THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF LAST MONTH. WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND WHAT IT IS SHORT BY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-fourth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-sixth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and walked on by none of it, and that the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine and it stood up to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket out of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and that the two men who carry that water up that ladder have never once been on that board nor ever once asked to be on it; and that at about eleven in the morning a man of about forty-three who came up that coast road on foot over four days in the winter with a canvas bag he took off a Crown list took a page off the end of that stall and read four place names out of it twice in about nine people on those boards and handed it back, and that he is a stacker at a yard on that coast and was off the water all through the winter and is on nobody's crew, and that nobody in this harbor has asked him one question about that bag or about the eleven words he said once in this yard which nobody wrote down, and that he read the four names in the order they are printed on that page being two places that have agreed and two that have not, and that he said the words as they are printed and put no face on any of them and did not read the row that is empty; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question at about half past eleven and got one answer of about a hundred and ninety-one words, and that the question was what a place that has not agreed gets on a method that asks a word of every community that has taken it on, and that the answer was nothing, and that it gets its name on that page as having not agreed and that is the whole of what is against it, and that the two rows in the refused column stand and have not been touched since the fifteenth and are not going to be touched this month, and that the row for the place about a mile inside that port is empty and is going to stay empty until somebody standing there says a word out loud, and that if nobody there ever says a word then that row is the correct state of it and not a fault in the work; and that nothing was read out of a gap and no figure on the sheet of the fourteen moved by one and the sheet was not touched at all that day; and that the sheet that came into that harbor under a stone was not opened and the stone was still on it at the close of the day; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and ninety words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and forty-five and the second is one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine, and that the third took nothing and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month is the twelfth.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not read those four place names out myself is that I can read and about four of the nine people on those boards could not, and that a page read out in a market is worth what it is to the people who cannot read it and not what it is to the person holding it, and that in eleven weeks the only reading anybody has needed this harbor was the one that had to happen in the open, and that this one did not and I am glad of it; and that at about half past eleven the reason I have written down that a man of about forty-three read those names out and was not asked to is that in this harbor a thing done for nothing gets remembered as a favour inside a week, and that the second time somebody does it it is asked for, and that I would rather this harbor had two or three things in it that nobody asks for twice than a page that only works when a man with nothing else to do is standing at the end of the stall; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have put the cistern at the front of this slip again when I told this shed last Friday that I was going to stop putting it there, is that I have broken my own rule once and I am writing down that I broke it and why, and that the why is that eleven trips a rota day is the one figure in this harbor that has not been improved by a method, and that a man reading his own slip on a Friday evening is exactly the sort of thing this yard was built to catch, and that it has caught me; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says that a keeper of a page is one remove from a man who fills in a blank in front of him is that I have had five days of watching one thing not be said and I have wanted badly to write down what it is, and that the wanting is the whole of the danger, and that a blank on a page stays a blank until somebody who is entitled to it puts something there, and that I am not entitled to put anything in this one and I have not put anything in it and I am not going to; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have written down nothing about what is in a bag on a bench stone is that a bag a man carried up a coast road for four days is not this yard's business until he opens it, and that the first thing anybody said about it in eleven weeks was that it was not going on three particular pages, and that the second thing is that nobody has asked him, and that I intend the second thing to still be true in a month.*
