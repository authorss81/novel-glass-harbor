# Chapter 616

*Monday the Twenty-Eighth Day of March, a Rota Day, the Butt at the Back of That Market Filled by Hand for the Hundred and Thirty-Seventh Time in Eleven Trips Before That Market Opened and Walked On by None of It, the Covers Back by a Quarter Past Nine and the Water to the Rim of the Second Hoop Until About Four in the Afternoon, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, at About Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People at the Flat of That Bench Stone Two Drafts of the Four Checkable Things Written Out on the Back of the Method's Own Page by a Woman of Thirty of No Office and by a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands, the Two Drafts Not Agreeing on the Fourth Thing and Both Left Standing One Under the Other and Neither Struck and Nobody Asked to Arbitrate, the Sheet That Describes a Made Thing and Is Not the Thing Not Looked At by Anybody, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Under the Slate, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Nine and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Nineteen*

Monday the twenty-eighth day of March, with that cistern at the back of the market charged before the market opened, and about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about two in the afternoon and about nine in that shed at half past five.

**A Monday rota with no wage Tuesday and no wipe in it, that cistern charged for the hundred and thirty-seventh time in eleven trips by hand and walked on by none of it, the covers back before a quarter past nine and the butt full to the rim of the second hoop until about four, and the name at the head of that rota board still off it with nobody in that market offering to put it there. Two drafts of the same four things were written out this afternoon on the back of the method's own page by two different people in about nine people and the two of them do not agree about the fourth of those four, and both drafts are standing and neither has been struck and nobody has been asked to say which of them is right. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and two drafts on the back of a page are not a line on that sheet and are not a penny out of anybody's hand. In at ten at a hundred and sixty-eight pence and out at four at a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence each way, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, is what that slate was lying on at the close of the day, being six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, on a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence. Of that sheet, one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence has gone out of this harbor, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever come into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, is the distance between those two figures and is going to be the distance between them at the end of this month. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and not one penny of this afternoon's two drafts has come out of that bag, and the two drafts took about forty minutes each and there is no figure anywhere in this country that prices forty minutes.**

**The nine durations went on again and the ninth of them this week is the one nobody in this harbor would believe if it were said out loud. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and fourteen days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and sixteen. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-four. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twentieth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-eight days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-third day, and two papers stand beside it and neither of the two is a sheet of positions. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and eighth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-fifth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-six days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. In that boathouse the basket stands where it has stood since the second of this month with the paper still in it and nothing in the basket and no hand in it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this morning. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fourteen days, one more than it was yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

A little after eight that morning the butt behind that market was filled by hand in eleven trips and a bucket, and nothing on that ladder was walked on by anybody in that market. The covers went back on before a quarter past nine and the butt stood to the rim of its second hoop until about four in the afternoon with not one bucket out of it, and that is a hundred and thirty-seven times now and the name at the head of that board is still off it.

---

At about two in the afternoon two people wrote the same four things out on the back of the method's own page at the flat of that bench stone, in about nine people, and the fourth thing in the two of them is not the same fourth thing.

The woman of thirty of no office wrote hers first and it took her about forty minutes, and she read it out when she had it.

1. *What is not said is an hour, and an hour on a night, and the hour is the only part of that night that has never been on anything in this harbor.*
2. *Who is not saying it is the person who is standing at the end of those boards, and that is not on this page as a name and is not going to be.*
3. *What they are entitled to keep is that hour, and the reason, and the right never to be asked for either of those two things.*
4. *What would have to happen for it to be opened is nothing at all, and that is the fourth thing and it is a real answer and not an empty one.*

Then a man of about thirty-three who hires hands wrote his underneath hers and it took him about forty minutes and he read that one out too.

1. *What is not said is an hour, and the same hour.*
2. *Who is not saying it is the person entitled to say it, and that row on that page is not going to be printed with a name against it.*
3. *What they are entitled to keep is the hour and the reason and the right not to be asked for either.*
4. *What would have to happen for it to be opened is that the person who has it gives it in their own mouth, unasked, on a day of their own choosing, and that nobody in this yard asks them for it, and that if it is not given then it is not given and that is the end of it.*

Nobody asked which of those two fourth things is right. Nobody argued with either of them, and about four people heard both drafts read out and the rest of that market was not told, and both are on the back of that page with one above the other and neither struck, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on both and see that they are not the same sentence.

---

A man of about thirty-two stood at the edge of that nine for the whole of both drafts and said nothing at either of them and wrote nothing down, and he did not go into the water that day, and at about half past three he was asked by nobody for a view and gave one to nobody.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and eighty-nine words, and nobody in that shed was asked which of two drafts was the better one.

"**A hundred and thirty-seven charges have gone past that cistern and I have not put a multiplier against one of them and I am not going to tonight.** It went up the ladder this morning in eleven trips and not one bucket of it was walked on and the covers were back on it before a quarter past nine and it stood up to the rim of the second hoop until about four this afternoon, and the name at the head of that board is still off it.

**Two drafts of the same four things are standing on the back of that page this evening with one above the other and neither struck, and they do not agree about the fourth thing, and I am not going to tell nine people tonight which of those two is right because I do not know.**

The figure at the head of this slip is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is where the twenty-sixth of last month left it, and the bag under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

Here is what I have learned this week and it took a woman of thirty-nine and two drafts at the flat of a stone. **On Friday a thing was said out loud in a market that could not go on a page without changing what it was.** That is not her fault and it is not anybody's fault, it is what happens when four words are asked to carry five kinds of thing, and the two drafts on the back of that page are the first honest answer anybody in this harbor has given to it in eleven weeks and neither of them is an answer I would have written.

I have an opinion about the fourth thing. I have had it since about half past two this afternoon and I am keeping it until Friday, because a keeper of a page who tells nine people at half past five on a Monday which of two sentences on a page is the right one has stopped keeping the page and started running it, and this is the eleventh week and I am the only man in this shed and I am not going to start.

**The sheet with the fourteen things on it is not being added to this week and none of those fourteen is being taken off, and I have read nothing out of it in eight days.** The four things that go against the fifth word are the four things that go against every one of those fourteen and they are not the same four lines of ink and they are the same four questions, and that is the whole of the connection and it is the only one I am going to claim.

At about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second and I did not read that leaf out loud. My first of those figures is three hundred and forty-nine and my second is one thousand six hundred and nineteen, my third is empty, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eighty minutes at the flat of a stone. That shed was empty by about six with the door standing open on a slip nobody was on. The butt behind that market was still standing at the rim of its second hoop when that shed shut and the name was still off that board.

**THE BUTT BEHIND THAT MARKET WENT UP ON THAT MONDAY FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVENTH TIME, ELEVEN TRIPS BY TWO MEN AND NOTHING WALKED ON, THE COVERS BACK BY A QUARTER PAST NINE, THE WATER TO THE RIM OF THE SECOND HOOP UNTIL ABOUT FOUR, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD STILL OFF IT. TWO DRAFTS OF THE SAME FOUR THINGS WERE WRITTEN OUT ON THE BACK OF THE METHOD'S OWN PAGE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, ONE BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE AND ONE BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS, AND THEY DO NOT AGREE ABOUT THE FOURTH THING AND BOTH ARE STANDING AND NEITHER HAS BEEN STRUCK AND NOBODY WAS ASKED WHICH OF THEM IS RIGHT. NOBODY WAS ASKED THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE ANYTHING AND NOBODY READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN MOVED BY ONE. TWO DRAFTS WRITTEN IN FORTY MINUTES EACH ARE NOT ON THAT SHEET. WHAT THAT SHEET HAS EVER TAKEN IN IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; WHAT IT IS SHORT BY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS; AND WHAT IT HAS COST IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. WHAT IS UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, entered in that yard's own mouth on the said Monday at about six in the evening and not checkable from a mole, and with no name against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason I let two people write the same four things out instead of writing one of them myself is that the fourth thing is the only one of the four that anybody can disagree about without being wrong, and that if this yard writes the fourth thing itself then it is this yard deciding what a person's hour costs, and that is a decision I am not entitled to make on behalf of anybody and I have wanted to make it for about four days; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason I have written down that a man of about thirty-two was not asked for a view and gave one to nobody is that in eleven weeks this yard has asked him three times for an opinion and got an answer twice, and that the answer on the eighteenth was eighty-one words and the answer on the nineteenth was a thing he did with his hands, and that on Monday he had nothing and a man who has nothing on a Monday is a man who is going to have something on a Wednesday or a Friday and I would rather he had it in front of nine people than at that bench stone at four in the morning; and that at about eight in the morning the reason that cistern is the first thing in this slip again is that a hundred and thirty-seven charges have gone past and this yard has never once printed what a charge costs anybody, and eleven trips a rota day is two hours of two men's backs twice a week, and that the only figure I have ever printed about it is the figure of the trips and I have printed that eleven times and I am not going to print a multiplier on it this month because I would have to say whose backs; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says that two drafts are standing on a page and neither has been struck is that in eleven weeks everything anybody in this harbor has agreed to has been written once and everything anybody has disagreed about has been written twice, and that I have never been able to find out whether that is because this harbor is honest or because this harbor is slow, and that Friday will tell me something and I do not know what; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have written nothing in this column about the fourth thing in either draft is that I have got an opinion about it and this column is the one place in this harbor where I am allowed to have one without printing it in nine people, and that I am not going to spend it before Friday, and that keeping an opinion back for three days is the only piece of discipline this yard has left and I am going to use it.*
