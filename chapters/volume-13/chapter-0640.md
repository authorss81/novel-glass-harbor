# Chapter 640

*Thursday the Twenty-First Day of April, a Rota Day With No Charge on That Cistern and the Covers Lying on It From Nine Until Four and Nobody Having Asked Why, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, the Word Dormant Going In Against the Morrow Line on the Sheet of Days in That Shed at About Three in the Afternoon With the Day and the Hour Against It and the Word Held Still Standing Above It and Still Readable, Being the First of the Nine Names to Have Gone Out of the Held Column, Said in About Nine People in About One Hundred and Nineteen Words by a Man of About Forty-Four Who Keeps the Crossings for the Boats of That Harbor and Is Not on Anybody's Crew, and a Temporary Private Access Signal on That Line Which Stopped Answering at About the Same Hour and Which Is Not Got Back, and a Sealed Record That Was Not Opened and Nothing Named in It and No Figure of It on Any Page, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Seventy-Three and the Second at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Thursday the twenty-first day of April, with that market trading all morning under a sky that had gone grey about eleven, a yard below it with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Thursday, and a rota day on the rota board at the back of that market with no charge written against today on it, and nothing put on that cistern, its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and nobody in that market has asked why a line is on that board with nothing against it, and nobody is going to be asked. The name at the head of that rota board is still off it. At about three this afternoon the word dormant went in against the first of the nine names on that sheet of days, under the word held, which is still on that sheet and is still readable above it, with the day and the hour against the new word, and it was said in about nine people in about one hundred and nineteen words by a man of about forty-four who keeps the crossings for the boats of this harbor and who is on nobody's crew, and nobody in this yard asked him anything before he said it and nobody has asked him anything since. **A temporary private access signal on that line stopped answering at about the same hour and it is not got back and it is not going to be remade this month. A sealed record was not opened, nothing inside it is named on any page in this harbor, no figure of what is inside it is on any page in this harbor, and it has not been asked one question this month.** That fitting-out sheet carries one line on it, written yesterday, and what it has carried out of this harbor is one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, and what has ever been carried into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and what it does not carry is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and nothing standing was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed in this harbor today. The berth fund paid that yard a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten in the morning and took a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, lay under that slate at the end of that day, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing this afternoon came out of that bag.**

**The nine went on a day each and the last of them is a man who has gone longer than any of the other eight without a word to anybody. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and eighty days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and thirty-eight days. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and forty. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventy-eight days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and forty-fourth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixty-two days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and seventeenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and thirty-second. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and forty-ninth day. Five papers stand on that board at the head of that mole, and there are two on it that this harbor has never opened. Behind a door in an upper room over that market there are still four lines, and under the fourth of those four there has never been anything written, and the answer to all of it is no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. There is a basket in that boathouse and the paper is in that basket and there is nothing else in it. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, they were nine on the twenty-first of January, and no man in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-eight days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about a quarter past three on that Thursday a man of about forty-four who keeps the crossings for the boats of that harbor came down the boards from the end of that market with a book under his arm that nobody in this yard has ever been allowed to read, and put it on the flat of that bench stone and said one thing out loud in about nine people.

He had not come up that coast road for it and nobody sent for him and nobody in this harbor had written to him, and about four people in that yard heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told.

"**The Morrow Line is dormant from today, and it is not coming back into held this month, and I am not asking anybody to put a word on anything. I keep the crossings for the boats of this harbor and there is nothing left at the end of that run to hold, and a run with nothing at the end of it is dormant and not held, and that is the whole of what I have come down here to say.**

**It is on your sheet and it is not struck, and you can read what was true before this afternoon on it as well as what is true now, and I would like both of those to stay.**"

That is about one hundred and nineteen words and nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him he was wrong, and about four people heard it and the rest of that market was not told.

---

At about a quarter past three the man who keeps the leaf put one word on the sheet of days in that shed against the Morrow Line, under the word held, with Thursday the twenty-first of April and the hour of a quarter past three against it, and he did not strike the word held out.

The word held is still on that row and it is still readable and it sits above the word that is true now, and nobody in that shed can take it off. **That name is still one of the nine and it has not been struck and it is still the first name down the side of that sheet, and nobody in this yard has put a state on it.** A state on a run is put on that sheet by the people who use that run, once, in their own mouths, and that is what happened at about a quarter past three this afternoon, and no storm and no loss and no Crown instrument put that word there.

---

At about half past three on that Thursday, in that shed, the private access signal on that line stopped answering, and it did not stop because anybody in this harbor touched it.

It is a bell buoy out on that line and a lamp in this shed that answers it, and between them a private question could be put to that run and a route answer got back, and it was made in the winter and there is one of them and it is not got back. The lamp in that shed did not go out. It is still there and it still works and there is a bell buoy floating about a mile and a half off that mole head that nobody can find from this quay, and the two of them have not spoken to each other since about half past three this afternoon, and nobody in this harbor is going to go and look.

---

Not one person in that yard went near the stall of that woman of about thirty-nine who mends net at the end of that market at about four this afternoon, and there were about nine people at the flat of that bench stone, and of those nine about four had the wall above it in front of them and could read it.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about 695 words, and nobody in that shed was asked what a man is allowed to carry out of a room he was never in.

"**One word went onto that sheet of days at about a quarter past three this afternoon and it was said by a man who keeps the crossings for the boats of this harbor and who is on nobody's crew, in his own mouth, in about nine people, and nobody in this yard asked him anything before he said it and nobody has asked him anything since. It is not struck. The word that was true before this afternoon is still on that row and still readable, and both of those are on that sheet for as long as that sheet exists.**

**The private access signal on that line stopped answering at about half past three and nothing in this harbor touched it. There is a bell buoy about a mile and a half off that mole head that nobody can find from this quay and a lamp in this shed that still works, and the two of them have not spoken to each other since this afternoon, and there is one of those signals on this coast and it is not got back and it is not being remade this month, and I want that written down in nine people because in a month somebody is going to ask me what happened to it and I am not going to remember.**

**And a sealed record was not opened this month and nothing inside it has been named on any page in this harbor and no figure of what is inside it is on any page in this harbor, and the claim that stands on a separate sheet behind those four heads was not exercised once, and it has not been asked one question, and it is not going to be asked one, and the man it is about did not touch it this month and I have not heard of anybody touching it.**

That sheet of money carries one line on it and it carried one yesterday and it carried none before that since the twenty-sixth of last month. It stands at one hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and ninety-nine marks and three shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the gap is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, **and that gap is three thousand six hundred pence bigger than it was a week ago and nothing standing in this harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed to make it smaller.**

The three columns of that sheet of days were not counted today and that count is not in this slip and is not going to be in one, and the nine names are on that sheet and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can read them off one at a time and add whatever he likes whenever this harbor decides to let him.

**Under this slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and it is twenty-eight pence on the fortieth day of this run as it was on the first, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and thirty-eight days, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

That first column took one entry at about six this evening and the second took five, and the reading of it was not done. Three hundred and seventy-three stands against the first of those figures and one thousand seven hundred and thirty-nine against the second, my third has nothing in it at all, a boy's hand being at the head of it, his surname being on no page and he being off the water, and my fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and holds no hundred and twenty-sixth line."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a word on a sheet. That shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that sheet of days stood against that wall with nine names down the side of it and a day and an hour against the top one and the word above it still readable, and the three columns of it were not added up.

THE TEMPORARY PRIVATE ACCESS SIGNAL ON THAT LINE STOPPED ANSWERING AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE AND NOTHING IN THIS HARBOR TOUCHED IT AND IT IS NOT GOT BACK. A SEALED RECORD WAS NOT OPENED AND NOTHING INSIDE IT IS NAMED ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR AND NO FIGURE OF WHAT IS INSIDE IT IS ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR, AND A CLAIM TO IT WAS NOT EXERCISED AND WAS NOT ASKED ONE QUESTION. THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET CARRIES ONE LINE, AND WHAT IT HAS COST THIS HARBOR IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS EVER BEEN PUT INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT CARRY IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE CAN HEAR OUT OF THE LEFT SIDE OF HIS HEAD AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT DAYS.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about one in the afternoon the reason there is a rota day on that board this week with nothing charged on it is that nobody in this market will fill it in and nobody has asked and this yard has stopped asking, and that a line with nothing against it is a fact about a harbor and not about a cistern;
