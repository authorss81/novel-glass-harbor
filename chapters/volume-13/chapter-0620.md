# Chapter 620

*Friday the First Day of April, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Counter-Plan Revised on Its Own Page at About Ten in the Morning on the Boards at the Back of That Market in About Nine People of Whom About Four Can Read, a New Position Written Against It Being KEPT, Carrying Its Own Four Checkable Things in the Same Four Questions as Every Other Gap on That Coast and Not a Fifth Question, the Two Drafts of Monday and the Two Lines of Wednesday Left Standing Above It and Nothing Struck and No Signature and No Agreement, a Line Under the New Word in a Hand That Is Not Hers Saying the Word Is This Yard's and Not Hers and That She Has Not Been Asked for It, a Man of About Thirty-Two Putting His Shoulder Under the Corner of That Bench Stone at About Ten in the Morning and Saying Nothing About It, Nobody Agreeing Out Loud and Nobody Thanked and Nobody Asked a Second Time and the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Not in That Nine and Not Asked to Be, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Fifty-Three and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Friday the first day of April, on the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning with the market trading underneath, and in a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Friday carrying nothing but its own work: no charge on that cistern, its covers lying on it from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. The counter-plan was revised this morning on its own page, in about nine people of whom about four could read it, and a new position was written against it, being kept, and it carries the same four checkable things every other gap on that coast carries and not one question more than those four. The two drafts written on Monday and the two lines spoken on the Wednesday are still standing above it and nothing was struck and nothing was rubbed out and there is no signature at the foot of any of the four things and no agreement at the foot of any of them. Nobody agreed out loud and nobody was thanked and nobody was asked a second time. The woman of about thirty-nine was not in that nine and was not asked to be in it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a word written on the back of a page is not a line on that sheet and is not a penny out of anybody's hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and the identical figure went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, is under that slate at the close of the day, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, leaves it six hundred and twelve pence short, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet's figure did not turn over once in ten days. Out of this harbor on it, one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence. Into it, ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. And the difference between those two, which is the only figure that yard is answerable for, ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. The last tin that was opened in that shed was opened on the twenty-ninth day of April past and nothing has come out of anything since, and nothing came out of it this morning.**

**The twentieth day of that run, and the nine have gone on every one of the twenty. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and eighteen days, and that is the first of the nine and it is the oldest thing this harbor is carrying. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and twenty. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-eight. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and twenty-fourth night, and there is nothing under it. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and forty-two days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-seventh day, and three papers stand at that end of that market this month and one of them was made this morning. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and twelfth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-ninth. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and sixty days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and the answer to the offer of four lines is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked, and in that boathouse the basket stands with the paper in it and no hand has gone into that basket. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighteen days, one more than yesterday, and that is not a gift, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten in the morning a man of about thirty-two got his shoulder under the corner of that bench stone and lifted it until the flat of it sat level with the flat of the yard, and then he took his shoulder out of it and stood back about four paces and said nothing about what he had done.

That stone has not been level since it was laid. A page put flat on it on Wednesday slid about half an inch toward the gutter, and four people watched it and nobody lifted it, and the man who keeps the leaf had put a finger under the low corner to stop it going and had held it there with one finger for about twenty minutes.

---

At about ten past ten the woman of thirty of no office wrote a fifth word on the back of that page in about nine people on those boards, and then read the whole of the back of that page out twice, in the same words, in the same order.

The four that were already at the head of it were OPENED, REFUSED, RESTRICTED and WITHDRAWN. Under those four she wrote, in a hand that is not the hand of the woman of about thirty-nine and not the hand of anybody who has been in this harbor on the twenty-fifth, this:

**KEPT**

And under that word she wrote the four things, being the same four questions that stand against every one of the fourteen on the sheet at the end of that stall and being those four and not one more.

1. *What is not said is an hour, on a night, and that hour is named here as belonging to somebody who is not named on this page and has not asked to be named.*
2. *Who is not saying it is the person entitled to say it, and that row carries no name and will not carry one.*
3. *What they are entitled to keep is that hour, and the ground they gave for keeping it, and the right never to be asked for either of those two things.*
4. *What would have to happen for it to be opened, in two lines with one under the other and neither struck. The first line is that it would have to be given in the mouth of the person who has it, unasked, on a day of that person's own choosing, and that if it is not given then it is not given. The second line is that nothing would have to happen, and that this yard is not going to print a handle on the thing itself. This yard is not choosing between those two lines this month and is not going to.*

And above the word kept she wrote one more line, in the same hand, being that this word is this yard's and not hers, and that she has not been asked for it and is not going to be.

---

Above all of that, still standing and still readable and still not agreed to, are the two drafts of Monday with one above the other, and under both of them the two lines of Wednesday with one under the other, and neither of the four carries a signature and none of the four says settled and the two lines of Wednesday are one position against another and are not an agreement and will not be made one this month.

Nobody agreed out loud. Nobody said that either of the two drafts was the right one. Nobody said that the two lines of Wednesday were settled. Nobody thanked anybody for anything and about four people in that nine could read the back of that page and about five could not, and those five were told what was on it in about nineteen words by a man who was told by a woman, in that order, and that is the fourth time this month that has happened and it is not going to stop happening.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and ninety-four words, and nobody in that shed was asked whose word the fifth one is.

"**Ten days. I have been standing at this stone at half past five every evening since Sunday the thirteenth day of this month and I am not going to close them tonight with a number, and there is a reason and I am giving it once.**

The figure I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it has not moved since the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of last month and it will not move before the end of this month and nothing anybody said in those markets moved it by a penny. There are twenty-eight pence in the bag under this slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and across ten days that is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence each way and twenty-eight pence left.

**There is a fifth word on the back of that page and it is KEPT, and it stands there with four things written against it, and the four things are the same four questions that stand against every one of the fourteen on that sheet at the end of that stall, and I have not added a question and I have not taken one off and no figure on that sheet has moved by one this fortnight and none of those fourteen has been added to and none has been taken off.**

The word is this yard's and not hers and there is a line under it saying so in a hand that is not hers, and she was not asked for it and is not going to be, and she was not in that yard this morning and nobody went to get her.

**And here is what I am not going to do tonight, and I have said I would not do it three times in ten days and this is the last time I am going to say it.** I am not going to say how many positions that page carries. That is a count of positions and not of persons and it is on a page where a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put a finger on every word in the four columns of the last day of it and come at it twice, and I am not going to say it in a shed at half past five and I am not going to say it in a minute and it is not going on that sheet that carries the money.

Above the new word there are still two drafts with one above the other and under both of them two lines with one under the other, and none of those four is struck and none of them is signed and the two of Wednesday are one position against another and are not an agreement and will not be made one this month. **That is the state of this yard and it is a bad state to hand to a stranger and it is the only state I have got that I would be willing to hand to one.**

One line went in on that first column at about six this evening and five went in on the second, and I did not read that leaf out loud, and that is the twentieth entry of a run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month. Three hundred and fifty-three stands against the first of those figures and one thousand six hundred and thirty-nine against the second. My third is empty and there is a boy's hand at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water. My fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and the reason for that is not on a page of mine and it is not going on one this month either."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a word written on the back of a page, and that shed stood empty by about six with the door standing open on a slip with nobody on it. and the stone outside it was level, and that page went back up on its nail on those boards at about half past four.

**THE COUNTER-PLAN WAS REVISED ON ITS OWN PAGE AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING ON THAT FRIDAY IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND A NEW POSITION WAS WRITTEN AGAINST IT, BEING KEPT, CARRYING ITS OWN FOUR CHECKABLE THINGS IN THE SAME FOUR QUESTIONS AS EVERY OTHER GAP ON THAT COAST AND NOT ONE QUESTION MORE THAN THOSE FOUR, AND THE TWO DRAFTS OF MONDAY AND THE TWO LINES OF WEDNESDAY WERE LEFT STANDING ABOVE IT WITH NOTHING STRUCK AND NOTHING RUBBED OUT AND NO SIGNATURE AND NO AGREEMENT, AND A LINE WAS WRITTEN UNDER THE NEW WORD IN A HAND THAT IS NOT HERS SAYING THAT THE WORD IS THIS YARD'S AND NOT HERS AND THAT SHE HAS NOT BEEN ASKED FOR IT AND IS NOT GOING TO BE. NOBODY AGREED OUT LOUD AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NOBODY WAS ASKED A SECOND TIME. THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WAS NOT READ OUT OF AND NO FIGURE ON IT WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE AND NONE OF THOSE FOURTEEN WAS ADDED TO AND NONE WAS TAKEN OFF. TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IS ON THE IN SIDE OF THAT SHEET, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS ON THE OUT SIDE OF IT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO SIDES. WHAT IS UNDER THE SLATE IN THAT SHED IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten past ten in the morning the reason I put that fifth word in a hand that is not hers and wrote a line under it saying so is that four people have now written on that page and one of them has only read it, and that a word on a page with four hands on it and one person not asked is a word that four people will be able to agree with, and that is the smooth page we have been talking about since Friday of last week and it does not have to arrive under a stone to arrive; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have written nothing in this column about whether the new word is right is that it is not mine and it is not hers and it belongs to whoever stands in it next, and that the only opinion I have written down in ten days is in the second column of the sixteenth and I put it there on purpose and I have not taken it out and I am not going to add another one tonight, and that a man who keeps a page is allowed exactly one opinion a month and I have spent mine.*
