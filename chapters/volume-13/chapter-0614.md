# Chapter 614

*Saturday the Twenty-Sixth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Man of About Fifty-Three With a Boat of About Fourteen Tons Standing Beside a Stall on Those Boards at About Eleven in the Morning Saying Nothing to Anybody, and Saying One Thing Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People in About Two Hundred and Forty Words About the Hour Standing Against a Figure and Against No Figure, and Nobody Agreeing With Him Out Loud and Nobody Thanking Him and Nobody Saying He Was Wrong and His Eleven Words Still on the Page and Unanswered and No Figure Set Against Them, the Two Rows Carried Forward in That Column Found to Carry No Hour and the Figure Written Yesterday Found to Carry Eleven, Nobody Asked the Woman of About Thirty-Nine Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Gap Put Out Loud at About Half Past Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Forty-Seven and the Second at One Thousand Six Hundred and Nine*

Saturday the twenty-sixth day of March, on the quay and the boards at the back of that market from about ten in the morning, and in a yard below it with a shed in it and about nine people in that shed at half past five in the afternoon.

**A Saturday with nothing on it but the ordinary work of a market: that cistern untouched and covered from eight until four, no rota day, no wage Tuesday and no wipe. Nobody in this harbor asked the woman of about thirty-nine anything yesterday or this morning, and that is the second day of a rule that is one week old. A man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came up those boards at about eleven and stood at a stall and said nothing to anybody, and at about two in the afternoon he said one thing out loud in about nine people, and nobody agreed with him and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody said that he was wrong. The money sheet was not written on and not altered and nothing went onto it in any hand, and an hour found on a page is not a line on that sheet. The berth figure went into that yard at ten in the morning, being a hundred and sixty-eight pence, and came out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a hundred and sixty-eight pence, and twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, was lying under that slate at the close of the day, which against a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twelve pence adrift, being three marks eleven shillings. What that sheet carries out of this yard is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence; what has ever come into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence; and what it does not carry is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and the difference between those two figures is that one of them was asked for and one of them was not. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**These are the nine and the sixth of them has a figure that is the roundest of the six and nobody has said so. The bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and fourteen days. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifty-two. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and twelve. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and eighteenth night. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and fifty-four days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirty-six days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and ninety-first day, and there are two papers beside it this morning and neither of them is that sheet. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and sixth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and twenty-third. Five papers are on that board and two of the five have never been opened. The offer of four lines has not left the room it is in and there is still nothing written under its fourth line, and the answer to it is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. The basket in that boathouse still has the paper in it and still has nothing else in it and no hand has gone into it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and twelve days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven on that Saturday a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came up those boards and stood beside a stall where a woman of about thirty-nine was mending net, and he stood there for about nine minutes and he said nothing at all to her or to anybody else.

She went on mending. Four people saw the two of them standing there and about five of that market did not, and nobody went over, and nobody asked either of them one question, and by about twenty past eleven he was at the bench stone in the yard below sitting on the flat of it with his hands on his knees.

---

At about two in the afternoon he stood up and said one thing out loud in about nine people, and nobody had asked him for it and nobody had asked him anything all day.

"**You are all looking at that column and half of you are reading it wrong, and I say that as a man who keeps a book with every hour in it in one hand and not because I am right.** Two of those three rows have got nothing written against them in the way the third one has. There is no hour on either of them because neither of them was ever asked for a word, so there is no hour to put down. **One of them was asked at eleven o'clock yesterday and eleven is standing there in her own hand, and I did not put it there and none of this yard could have.**

A page is not a page about two things. It is a page where one of two things has an hour on it and the other does not, and that is the whole difference between a page of ours and the piece of paper that came into this harbor under a stone a fortnight ago, which has got a word against every day of this month and not one hour written anywhere on it. **Put your finger on the two rows with nothing against them and then put your finger on the eleven, and you have not got a page that cannot be checked. You have got a page that shows you which of the three was said and which two were carried forward.**"

That is about two hundred and forty words and it went to about four people and the rest of that market was not told, and nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him for it and nobody said that he was wrong.

His eleven words have been on a page in this harbor since the winter and nobody has answered them and nobody has thanked him and no figure is set against them this morning or this afternoon.

---

The man who keeps the leaf took the page off those boards at about half past three and carried it down to that bench stone and looked at it with about four other people standing there, and he did not write anything on it, and the stone outside that yard had a scratch on it about the size of a hand and that scratch is where the scratch has been.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and forty-eight words, and nobody in that shed was asked whose book is the only one of its kind.

"**A man who has been sitting in this harbor for two days stood up in nine people this afternoon at two o'clock and read our own page better than the four of us who made it, and I have written nothing on that page since about half past three and I am not going to.** The figure I am answerable for is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and it is where the twenty-sixth of last month left it, and there are twenty-eight pence under this slate, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight.

**Two rows in the refused column of that page carry no hour against them and a figure written yesterday carries eleven, and nobody in this yard put that eleven there.** That is not a small thing and I have known it since Wednesday afternoon and I have not said it out loud until tonight, and the reason I am saying it in a shed at half past five and not in that market at two o'clock is that a thing said in a shed at half past five gets taken home and argued with at breakfast and a thing said in a market at two o'clock gets argued with in the market, and this one needs nine people to have it in the same week.

**Nobody was thanked for it and nobody agreed with him and nobody told him he was wrong and that is how it happened and not how it would have happened behind a door.** His eleven words are on a page in this harbor and nobody has answered them and nobody is going to answer them this fortnight, and I have not put a figure against them and I am not going to.

And here is the thing I want on the record in my own mouth and in nobody else's. **A page that carries two things in one column is not a broken page. It is a page where one of the two was said and one of the two was carried forward, and the difference between those two is written in a small hand against one of them and is exactly eleven o'clock.** That is the whole of what happened here on Friday and I did not see it until a man with one book and one hand said it out loud in a market.

The sheet with the fourteen things on it stands at the end of that stall where it stood. I read nothing out of it this week, no figure on it has moved by one, and none of those fourteen has been added to and none has been taken off.

At about six this evening that leaf took one entry on the first column and five on the second and it was not read out loud. Those two figures are three hundred and forty-seven and one thousand six hundred and nine, the third of the four is empty, and the fourth is unmoved at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it anywhere."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a finger put on two rows, and by six the shed had emptied and the door stood open on a slip with nothing on it at all. The cistern behind that market was covered and had not been touched, and the page went back up on that nail at about four.

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS SAID ONE THING OUT LOUD AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN ABOUT 240 WORDS, BEING THAT TWO OF THE THREE ROWS IN THAT COLUMN CARRY NO HOUR AGAINST THEM BECAUSE THEY WERE NEVER ASKED FOR A WORD, AND THE THIRD CARRIES ELEVEN AGAINST IT IN THE KEEPER'S OWN HAND, AND THAT A PAGE IS NOT A PAGE ABOUT TWO THINGS BUT A PAGE WHERE ONE OF TWO THINGS HAS AN HOUR ON IT AND THE OTHER DOES NOT. NOBODY AGREED WITH HIM OUT LOUD AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HIM AND NOBODY THANKED HIM AND NOBODY SAID HE WAS WRONG, AND HIS ELEVEN WORDS ARE STILL ON A PAGE AND HAVE NOT BEEN ANSWERED AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST THEM. NOBODY ASKED THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE ANYTHING. A FIGURE FOUND ON A PAGE BY A MAN WITH ONE BOOK IS NOT A PEN ON THAT SHEET. THE FIGURE THAT SHEET IS SHORT BY COMES FIRST ON IT AND IT IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS; WHAT IT HAS COST IS ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE; AND WHAT ANYBODY PUT INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS UNDER THAT SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-sixth day of March at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said Saturday stood on the calendar with nothing on it at all, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, the covers being on it at eight in the morning and still on it at four in the afternoon; and that nobody in that harbor asked the woman of about thirty-nine anything on that day, and that that is the second day of a rule that is one week old; and that a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came up those boards at about eleven in the morning and stood beside the stall where that woman was mending net and stood there about nine minutes and said nothing at all to her or to anybody else, and that she went on mending, and that four people saw the two of them standing there and about five of that market did not and that nobody went over and nobody asked either of them one question, and that by about twenty past eleven he was at the bench stone in the yard below sitting on the flat of it with his hands on his knees; and that at about two in the afternoon he stood up and said one thing out loud in about nine people unasked and it was about two hundred and forty words, and that what he said is that two of the three rows in that column have got nothing written against them in the way the third one has, and that there is no hour on either of them because neither of them was ever asked for a word and so there is no hour to put down, and that one of them was asked at eleven o'clock the day before and eleven is standing there in the keeper's own hand, and that he did not put it there and that none of that yard could have, and that a page is not a page about two things but a page where one of two things has an hour on it and the other does not, and that that is the whole difference between a page of ours and the piece of paper that came into that harbor under a stone with a word against every day of the month and not one hour written anywhere on it; and that nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody said that he was wrong, and that his eleven words have been on a page in that harbor since the winter and have not been answered and no figure is set against them; and that the man who keeps the leaf took the page off those boards at about half past three and carried it down to that bench stone and looked at it with about four other people standing there and did not write anything on it, and that the page went back up on that nail at about four; and that not one line of the sheet of the fourteen was read out of, that no figure on it went up or down by one, and that it was not touched; and that the sheet that came into that harbor under a stone was not opened and the stone was not moved; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and forty-eight words in about nine people; and that at about six this evening one line went in on the first column and five on the second, and that the first of those two figures is three hundred and forty-seven and the second is one thousand six hundred and nine, and that the third took nothing and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and that the day of this run counted from Sunday the thirteenth day of this month is the fourteenth.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I have written down that a man stood beside a stall for nine minutes and said nothing is that in eleven weeks this harbor has had four things said out loud that nobody asked about and not one thing found out that had been asked, and that both of those are useful and this morning was the second of them in three days and I am not sure I like the rate; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have written down what that man said and not what I thought of it is that he keeps a crossing column in one book in one hand and every hour in that book is in the hand of one man, and that when he looked at two rows with no hour on them he was not guessing and he was not agreeing with me, he was reading a page the way he reads his own, and that is the difference between a witness and a man with an opinion and this harbor has very few of the first; and that at about half past three the reason I did not write anything on that page is that the last time anything was written on it without somebody saying a word out loud in this market it was a strike through a figure one, and that strike was mine and it was right and it was also the most frightening thing I have done in eleven weeks, and that a page nobody can check and a page only this yard can check are both bad and I do not know which one I would rather have and neither does he; and that at about half past five the reason this slip says tonight that the two rows carried forward have no hour against them and that nobody put one there is that a carried-forward row with an hour on it would be a lie and a lie is cheaper to argue with than an absence, and that this harbor has spent eleven weeks learning that and it has not learned it from me; and that at about six o'clock the reason I have not written a word in this column about what a man of about fifty-three said with his mouth on those boards yesterday is that I was not there and that a thing I did not hear is not mine to write down, and that about nine people heard it and four of them could read the page it was about, and that I have only got the number of words because it was counted in the room and not by me.*
