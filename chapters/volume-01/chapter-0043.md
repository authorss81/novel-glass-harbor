# Chapter 43

*The Book Is Not the Vessel's*

The cutter's boat came in at four on Wednesday the twenty-third with a flat case against the foredeck and a man in a cutter's coat who did not come up the slip but stood at the head of it and read four sheets aloud from the step, in the road, in the cold, with a fish market standing about, which was a thing no one had done in Morrow Quay in living memory and which turned out to be the only way a paper like that could be served on a Free Keel yard without a hearing.

It was three sheets and not four, and it had the vessel's seal at the foot of the third and the captain's signature line above it, and it was headed *Answer of the commanding officer of the imperial cutter*Vigil*, on the question of a one-season provisional working charter, raised by the harbor authority on the fourteenth of this month*, and the first line of the first sheet gave the date the claim was lodged as the fourteenth of October at ten-forty in the morning, which Calder Marr had known for a month and had never once seen written down.

The second sheet asked for three things and the third sheet explained what would happen if they were not given.

**One.** That any instrument the harbor authority issues to this crew for the season *shall be expressed to be bounded by the outer marks of Gannet Shoal*, and shall grant nothing upon or within four hundred yards of them.

**Two.** That the working book of the crew be produced to the vessel on request, and that the vessel be given leave to copy it.

**Three.** That the vessel be furnished with a copy of the hearing record of the seventh, eighth and fourteenth of this month, and with a count of the printed crew copies of the public provenance record, with the places they are kept in.

"Three," Bevin Rook said, in the road, with his arm in a sling and the sheets in Nessa Pike's hand. "He has asked for the book and he has put it second, and he has put a thing we will give him either side of it, and that is a man who has thought about how to be refused."

"Two is a knife and one is a wall and three is the price of the wall," Nessa said. "And Calder wants to refuse all three and put it on the market wall in your hand, and he is going to do it in about four minutes and I am asking for the four minutes first."

The yard was in the shed with the door open and the stove going and four people in it and the second seam's chain still wet on the bench from the day's work.

"Sit down," Nessa Pike said. "All of you. I have worked this out twice on the mole today and I want to do it again with the three of you in the room, because I am going to say a thing that is against you and I want it in the air before it is in a book."

---

"Say it."

"The yard refuses the paper entire. And in my opinion the first instinct you will have about that is correct and the second instinct you will have about it is also correct, and you have got one of them for Monday and the other one for about four minutes after Monday, and we are going to spend them now while there is still a week." She put the three sheets flat on the bench. "Here is the shape of it.

"Number one, the outer marks. The charter is going to be bounded by the harbor line whatever anybody writes on it, because the harbor line is where the harbor authority's power stops and the Crown's begins, and the outer marks are a mile and a quarter off this quay and are out on a shoal that a vessel of the Crown Navy has had under salvage claim since the fourteenth of October. **We concede number one in one sentence and we concede it in the yard's own hand and we concede it for free**, because we have sworn twice in a room not to go within four hundred yards of them, and a paper that says the charter is bounded by the outer marks says nothing that is not already true and costs us nothing, and it makes the second half of that sheet look like what it is, which is an inventory."

"An inventory of what," Calder said.

"Of the ground between here and them. That is what the naming clause is. He is not asking to know where our charter reaches. He is asking the harbor authority to publish, in a municipal instrument, a list of the places on this coast where the bottom comes up." Nessa tapped the sheet. "And I am not going to let this yard's paper be the document that draws that list, not even by accident, not even by agreeing."

"That half is refused."

"That half is refused, and it is refused in a sentence that says *the yard will name the ground on which it holds an instrument and will name no ground on which it holds none*, and every word of that is checkable, because the yard will then be holding an instrument, and the instrument will be in a room in this harbor on Monday with a ground on it."

---

"Number two."

"Number two is the book and I am not going to argue about it for a minute because there is nothing to argue. The book is the only thing this yard owns. Not the iron, which is a third another crew's, and not the ten printed copies, which are a copy, and not the bench chest, which is a clerk's. The book. A hundred and forty entries of air and money and fouling and wages and a boy's hand and a man who cannot tell which bell belongs to the room, and a written reason of two hundred and forty words, and a withdrawal of ten yards, and a diver's own count given against himself by the man whose number it is. And the vessel has asked for it and asked to copy it, in a document with a seal at the foot and a captain's line above it, on the grounds of a charter that does not yet exist, on a coast where that cutter's captain signed a document thirteen years ago that this harbor has never been allowed to read the paper under."

"The yard refuses number two," Calder said, "and the yard refuses it in my words and in the words of the first term of the seventh of November, which is *the yard asked nothing and offered nothing and is content to stand there*, and the yard will say the same thing about this sheet in the same words and will put it on the market wall in Rook's hand with the date and the hour on the back, and that is the whole of the answer to the *Vigil* and I am not writing a word more than that."

"You are not writing a word more than that," Nessa Pike agreed, "and that is where I am going to say the thing that is against you."

She took the second sheet off the bench and turned it round so it was the right way up for him.

"If that is the whole of the answer, then the *Vigil*'s officer goes back into a room in Veyr with three documents. The charter question and the yard's answer, which is a refusal. The yard's three points and the yard's own withdrawal of the interval, which is a set of figures with a hole in it. And a book, never produced, which that officer knows exists, because the hearing record of the fourteenth names it, and the hearing record of the fourteenth has been read by him, and the market wall names it, and a boy with a sling in a room on the eighth asked a Registry conservator in front of a clerk what a recognized witness is and got an answer about documents."

"You are telling me that a refusal makes it worse."

"I am telling you that a *total* refusal makes it worse, and that number three is the thing that makes number two worth reading." Nessa put her hand flat on the bench. "A crew that refuses everything is a crew that is hiding. A crew that hands over a copy of a hearing record it wanted read, and a count of ten pieces of paper and where every one of them is, and a bound in one sentence, and then refuses the book in one sentence, is a crew that has decided what it will give and what it will not. And that is a crew whose refusal of the book can be believed by a man who has never met us, because he has watched us give four things first, and a man who has watched a party give four things does not think about the fifth."

Bevin Rook said, from the end of the bench, "She is right and I can check it. The officer's man counted us on the ninth of November and wrote the number and the hour on his own slate and asked for none of them. If we hand him a copy of the count with the places in it, then he has got a number that came from us and matches a number that came from them, and the two of them are the same number, and a man with two of the same number is a man who cannot say the count is a lie."

"That is not nothing," Calder said.

"It is three marks at the copying room for the hearing record and nothing at all for the count, because the count is four lines and I write them myself." Bevin took the pencil out from behind his ear with his right hand. "And I want to write one of the four lines myself and not have you write it, and I want Nessa to countersign it, and I want the vessel to have it in my hand, because it is the one thing off that wall that I put my name to and it is a number and a place and my name, and there is no reason a boy with a sling cannot be the man who hands an office a list of addresses."

---

Nessa Pike wrote her name under it herself, which was the cost, and she did it without being asked and she did not explain it afterward and nobody in the shed understood it until the sixteenth of December except the bellwright.

The count went like this. *Ten printed crew copies of the public provenance record of the fourteenth of October, printed without the conservator's name, in Morrow Quay on the twenty-second of October at twelve marks, the drawing withheld from the press.* Two on the wall of the fish market beside the original. Three on the walls of Free Keel crews, which crews being *the* *Grey Widow* and two others, entered with the harbor clerk in October and not named here. Five rolled in oilcloth in the bench chest of the Free Keel yard, pledged to the harbor authority as part of the bond of the twenty-fourth of October and released on the fourteenth of this month.

And then a sixth line, which was Bevin's and which he read out loud before he wrote it.

*Not included in this count: any paper of any other crew, including a slate of the* Low Lantern *held in that crew's own keeping, and including a third entered in the harbor clerk's book against a sale of iron.* Entered by R. Rook, twenty-fourth of November, at the tender of N. Pike, and I would like the vessel to have that line first, because if the count is going to be read by a man in a room then the yard's paper is on it and the other crews' paper is not, and the line that says so is the line that makes the count worth handing over.*

"Read it back," Nessa said.

He read it back. She countersigned it under his name and not over it, and Calder signed left-handed at the foot with the hour against it, and Nessa put the four lines and the five lines into an envelope with the yard's copy of the hearing record of the seventh, eighth and fourteenth of this month, which Mara Venn had certified as a true copy of a municipal record in a Registry hand on the Thursday for the cost of three marks out of a bag of nineteen, which is a certificate about a record and not a certificate about glass, and which was the only certificate this office had ever signed for this yard and was signed in nine seconds and gave nothing away except the fact that it had been asked for and given.

---

They nailed the answer up at six o'clock with the tide going and the light going flat on the west side of the mole, and Bevin Rook put it on the wall under the cutter's claim of the eighth of November, which was still at the top with the yard's three sheets slipped four inches below it where a man had to lift a corner.

The answer was four sentences and they read as follows.

*Answer of the Free Keel yard to the paper of the vessel of the twenty-third of November, in the yard's own hand, this date and hour on the back.*

*One is granted. Any instrument of the harbor authority issued to this crew for the season shall be expressed to be bounded by the outer marks of Gannet Shoal and shall grant nothing upon or within four hundred yards of them, and the yard has twice said in a room that it does not go within them.*

*Two is refused. The working book of this crew is not produced to the vessel and is not copied for the vessel, and the yard gives no reason, because a book is a book and a document is a document and the day a book is produced on request it is a document.*

*Three is granted. A copy of the hearing record of the seventh, eighth and fourteenth of this month is enclosed, and enclosed with it is a count of the ten printed crew copies of the public provenance record and the places they are kept in, made in the hand of the boy who reads this yard's book and countersigned by the woman who holds this yard's tender's cleat.*

*Given at the fish market wall in Morrow Quay on the twenty-third day of November, in the presence of the vessel's man, who counted the sheets as they were nailed up and wrote the number and the hour on his own slate.*

The officer's man read all of it standing up with his back to the crowd, and then he read the count, and then he read the sixth line, and then he stood there with the slate in his hand for long enough that a man at the back of the crowd said something about a shoal and was told to be quiet by four people at once.

"Mr Rook," he said.

"Sir."

"That sixth line is not in my instructions."

"No, sir," Bevin Rook said, and did not soften it and did not explain it, and Nessa Pike watched him not explain it and made a small sound in her chest that only the boy heard.

The man wrote four words on his slate and put the slate in his satchel and said that the vessel would acknowledge the answer, and got into his boat, and the boat went out past the mole and round into the road where a quarter of a mile off on the bearing of the pale run the *Vigil's* tender lay with her floats run and her oars shipped and had held that station for thirty-nine days.

---

At the top of the make, an hour later, the harbor's tide bell went three strokes out in the road, and under the third of them, a third of a second behind and very even, the note came up out of the bottom off the mole with its three marks and its flick.

Bevin Rook had the book out before the second stroke had finished and read it in the flat voice with the water state against it, and Calder did not turn round, and the officer's man was not on the quay because he had gone to the pile, and for the first time since the eighth of November there was nobody from the cutter in the road with the sound.

"Forty past six," Bevin said. "Top of the make, west-northwest two, light air, water rising two inches on the mole face, delayed note under the harbor's three. No source. Not written as a voice."

"That is six," Bevin said, when he had read it back twice. "Six logged since the fourth of November, and the eighth of November is the one a naval rating wrote at the same minute as me, and tonight is the first one in a fortnight I have not had to write *two records, same hour* under, and I do not know whether that is better or worse and I have not put a word about which."
