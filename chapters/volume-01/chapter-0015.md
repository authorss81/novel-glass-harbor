# Chapter 15

*The Free Keel*

The Free Keel met on Wednesday night in the net loft above the fish dock, where there was no chairman and no minutes, only crews sitting on coils with their books on their knees.

There were nine crews in, some with two boats, some with one. Sella Brigg of the *Low Lantern* sat with her slate showing the strop share. Harl Dace of the *Grey Widow*, a broad man with a burned ear, had the floor because he had asked for it.

"The Registry has touched the Marr yard," he said. "A seizure off their deck, a cutter at the pile, a tender setting floats on their bearing. The question is whether the rest of us stand rescue cover for a yard that brings that weight."

Calder sat against a post with his arm in a sling Nessa had made him wear so he would not use it. He had come to ask for nothing, because asking would have made it worse.

Tobias spoke first. "When the *Grey Widow* lost her tow off the bar in spring, who stood the outer line?"

Harl looked away. "Free Keel did."

"Who lent the pump when your housing went?"

"You did."

"Then the pledge holds or it does not." Tobias sat down.

Sella held up her slate. "I hold a third of their iron and I am not ashamed of it. The paper is good. But I will say this: if we cut them off from shared tools and shared rescue, we make the Registry right that glass makes a crew unfit to work. I will not do their work for them."

Harl turned to Calder. "Will you take rescue cover from crews you may not be able to pay back this winter?"

Calder stood. The bell in his ears made the loft's small sounds run double, and he waited a beat to place Harl's voice.

"I will take rescue cover if it is offered under the pledge," he said, "and I will give it the same way. What I will not do is ask another crew to stand a naval line with me. If the *Vigil*'s tender hails you off the marks, you answer them and leave me. I will write that."

He took from his coat a paper Nessa had drawn: the *Kittiwake*'s slip time through November, the only thing the yard had that other crews wanted, with two tides a week marked for other boats to use free.

"The slip is yours," he said, and laid it on the coil in the middle. "We need the cradle before the gales and we have no timber. You need a slip that does not charge by the foot. Take our tides. We will beach on the hard and take the wear."

A murmur went round. Slip time was money. To give it was to put the *Kittiwake*'s own cradle further off, through winter, with no timber in sight. It would survive into spring as a debt on the hull.

Harl picked up the paper and read it twice.

"You would give this and still owe Sella a third of iron?"

"I owe what I owe. The slip is not payment. It is what the pledge says: shared tools."

Harl folded the paper and put it in his coat. "Then the *Grey Widow* stands rescue cover inside the line. Not past the marks. Inside."

One by one the other crews said it: inside the line. Sella last.

"The *Low Lantern* stands too. And the iron stays on the stack till it fetches a fair price, Marr. I will not have it said I pressed you in a gale week."

Calder walked back to the yard with Tobias in rain.

"You gave away the slip," Tobias said.

"I gave what we had that was not money." Calder held the sling close. "The cradle goes to spring. The boat takes the wear."

"It will cost planks."

"It will. But we have rescue cover, and no crew can say we bought it."

At the yard Nessa read the note he brought and understood at once what he had spent.

"You gave them November," she said.

"I gave them November."

She wrote it in the book: *19th. Slip tides given to Free Keel for rescue cover inside line. Cradle deferred to spring. Cost survives winter.*

He signed with his left hand, and she countersigned, and the cost was on the page where it would outlast the month.
