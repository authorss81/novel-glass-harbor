# Chapter 12

*Weather Work*

The gale came in on Sunday after church bells, out of the southwest with rain in it like gravel.

By noon the basin was running white off the pile heads and the *Merrit*, a stone coaster of ninety tons, was dragging her stern mooring off the coal wharf and grinding her rudder post against a dolphin with every set. Her master stood in the reeve's office doorway with water running off his hat and asked for a diver to go down between the tides and clear a fouled screw and drive a patch over a sprung butt, or lose the boat on the wall when the tide turned.

Kest's clerk wrote it as ordinary harbor work under the Morrow Quay charter, inside the line, one tide, fifteen marks for the crew with rope and pump found by the yard. Fifteen marks would buy air and a week on the chandler's note. Calder signed before Nessa could do the sum out loud.

They rigged on the coal wharf in rain that got into everything. The doubled hose from the shoal dive was scrap, so Nessa cut the good length out of it and made a single short run with two good clamps, and wrote *short hose, one tide only* in the crew's book. The pump still had the cracked housing with the sleeve in it, and she packed it with tallow and wrote that too. Bevin took the watch with the slate round his neck in oilcloth, and Tobias held the breast line.

"You are not fit for this," Nessa said to Calder, low, while Bevin coiled the line. "You turned wrong twice yesterday. You cannot place a bell."

"I can place a hull. I have done it blind."

"That is not the work today. Today you will be under a stern in a surge with a screw turning over your head if the master loses his line." She put her hand on his helmet ring. "You go down, you clear the fouling, you come up when I say. Not when the patch is done. When I say."

He went over at half past one in eleven feet of black water with the surge running through the piles like a loom.

The work was what work always was: feel, cut, clear. The screw had a trawl wire round it, three turns, and Calder got two off with the surge lifting him and setting him down against the rudder post. He had the third half off when the *Merrit* took a set and the stern came down a foot and the wash off the dolphin threw him sideways into the hull.

His right forearm hit barnacles first, where the rope burn had closed to a pink line. The line split open again along two inches, bright blood going out into the water in a thread, and the pain came up his arm into his teeth. He kept his left hand on the toggle and his knife in his right and finished the cut because stopping would have meant going down again on the next tide for nothing.

Above him Bevin was calling the air and the depth, and through the helmet the harbor's working bell on the coal wharf came three times for the strain on the mooring, and under it, a third of a second late, the loading bell from the echo answered, and Calder turned toward the wrong sound and put his hand out for a line that was not there.

On the deck Nessa saw the line go slack-wrong and put her weight on the cutoff wire and said it once.

"Enough."

Calder had the patch half seated. He had seventy minutes of air by his own count and a job that would be lost if he came up. He put his hand on the clip and then took it off again, because the slate they had both signed in the infirmary was in his head louder than the bell, and said it back.

"Enough."

He came up the ladder with blood running down into his glove and rain running into the burn. Nessa had the dressing out before his helmet was off, and she dressed it standing in rain with her hands shaking, which he had never seen.

"You heard wrong down there," she said. "Bevin called the strain and you reached to port. The line is to starboard."

"I know."

"You do not know. That is the point." She tied the bandage too tight and then eased it. "From today you do not take the bottom alone on a surge job. Bevin goes down on the watch line as tender's mate, or we do not take the job. I will write it."

The *Merrit*'s master paid eleven marks, not fifteen, because the patch was not driven and the boat had to be warped off on the tug's line. Four marks gone for work half done, and the short hose chafed through at the clamp and fit now only for washers, and the pump sleeve wept the whole tide and would need a new housing before winter, and Calder's arm would need a week before a glove would go on it without him making a noise.

They walked back to the yard in rain with eleven marks in a wet bag and a burn reopened. Bevin carried the helmet and did not speak until the shed.

"You came up," he said. "You could have stayed and finished."

"Nessa said the word," Calder said. "Once is not a conversation."

Nessa wrote in the crew's book, in her own hand where all of them could read it: *Short dive inside line, 16th. Diver's hearing placed bell wrong. Tender called stop with work undone. Diver obeyed. Cost: hose, pump packing, four marks short, arm opened.*

Calder signed under it with his left hand, because his right would not hold the pencil.
