# Chapter 34

*The Fence Is Round My Own Ground*

The slate wall of that hearing room was blank when the yard came in on the Thursday morning, and it had been blank since eight o'clock, because the clerk takes the cloth off the top left corner where the office always starts and goes across the whole board with it every Monday whether there is a sitting or not, and there had been a sitting on the Monday, and three points, two distances, a difference of ten yards, the figure of the sixth station and a foot and a half of the yard's own phrasing had gone off that board with the cloth in the reeve's own hearing room on the morning the yard came to argue about it.

Nobody said anything about it. It was a wall.

Then Kest took the chalk, and put the three points on again in the same places and in the same words, and put the reeve's own ruling on the bottom line, and signed the corner of it the way a man signs a thing he is going to be asked about, and it took him eleven minutes and it looked like a man copying out a child's sums.

"This office's own boy took your figures off that wall with a cloth on Monday morning and did it in ten seconds and did it because he has done it every Monday for six years," he said, when he had finished, "and if that is the way your figures die then they were never much of a record. They are on the market wall and they are in the ledger and they are in four hands, and the wall is a convenience, and I have wanted to say that out loud in this room since the second of this month and it has taken a cloth to make me say it."

---

The motion came at five past ten and it came well.

The naval officer had brought his man back and the man had two sheets, and the officer did not read them himself, which Calder understood to be a courtesy of a kind that a court would not notice and that a hearing room notices very much.

"The vessel moves, under the authority of the commanding officer, that the harbor authority strike from its own record the entry made on the third of this month at the fifth seam."

Kest said, "On what ground."

"On the ground that the entry was made by the officer of this office, at the fifth seam, on ground that the same office had served closed by the notice of the thirty-first of last month, and that an observation made by an officer of this office upon ground that this office has closed by its own served notice is not the observation of this office, it is the act of a man doing what he has been told not to do, and it is the fruit of that act, and the harbor authority has no power to enter the fruit of its own breach in a book that is read in Veyr."

He said it without a single word of heat and Calder found that he would have given a great deal for one word of heat.

Kest did not answer for about a minute. He had his own book on the table in front of him and he did not open it, and when he spoke he spoke to the room and not to the officer, and Calder understood afterwards that this was the second time in three days a man had chosen the room over the man with the notebook, and that the two choices together were going to cost the reeve more than the thing he was protecting.

"That is the best argument anybody has made in this room since I came to it," Kest said, "and I am going to take it seriously, and I am going to do three things in front of you, and the first is that I am going to read the entry out, because a man who is asked to strike a document has to read it before he decides, and because four of the words in it are the reason I am about to refuse, and I would like the room to hear them before they hear me refuse.

"Mr Clerk, the third of this month. Read it."

The clerk had the book. He read it in the flat voice, in the words Kest had written standing up on the mole steps, and the words were that at the top of the tide, from the steps inside the mole wall, the officer of this office and three of the Free Keel yard saw, at the fifth seam, in eleven feet, bedded in the mole's own foundation stone, transparent material in plates, one plate being four foot by some part of four, with three keys along the upper edge and a bolted rim of a Crown-shop pattern in the lower corner, and darker glass inside the rim, and the whole of it bedded in the mound and not lying loose. Seen. Not measured. Not touched. The officer has seen it and enters it in his own book in the presence of three witnesses whose names are below, and the office is not required to send a thing it has merely seen to the circuit office, and is not sending it today, and will send it if the office is asked in writing by an office that has jurisdiction and states its reason.

Witnesses: Marr, Pike, Rook.

"Read the second half of that entry again, Mr Clerk. The bit about jurisdiction and a stated reason."

The clerk read it again.

"The second thing I do," Kest said, "is that I answer the motion, and my answer is no, and I give my reason in a form that can be checked, because a reason that cannot be checked is a temper."

He put one careful hand on the closed book.

"A reeve's served notice is an instrument of this office and it is served by this office and it is enforced by this office, and on the thirty-first I served it and read it aloud on this mole to the tide gang and to a clerk and to a man off a cutter's boat, and I added enforcement in my own words that the notice did not contain, and I have never once read that notice to myself, and I have said so on a mole in front of three witnesses and I did not say it to look well.

"The vessel's argument is that I went onto ground I had closed. That is true. The vessel's argument is that the seeing is therefore the act of a man doing what he was told not to do. That is also true. And neither of those two things has any bearing whatever on whether there is a plate of transparent material in eleven feet of water at the fifth seam, and this office is not in the business of deciding whether a plate is there. This office is in the business of writing down what it has seen, in its own hand, with three named witnesses, on the day, at the tide, with the word *not measured* in it, and that is what the book says and that is what the market wall says and that is the only thing any of us has got.

"And I will say the rest of it, because I would rather be disliked in this room than misunderstood in Veyr. I broke my own notice on purpose, on my own ground, with three witnesses standing there, and I told the yard I was doing it on the third in those words before I went down the steps, and I entered the breach in the same book as the sighting because a man who enters the second without the first has done something worse than the first. And if a court in Veyr in three years says that the harbor authority of Morrow Quay sent its officer onto ground it had closed and took his word for a plate in the bottom of it, then this office will produce this page and the three names on it and the six-clause instrument in my coat, and I would rather a court have that page than have a plate in a custody room with nobody's word on it at all."

"The plate is not in issue," said the officer.

"The plate is not in issue in this room," said Kest, "and I have had three days to learn the difference between the two, and I am going to tell the room why the vessel's motion is not refused only on the ground of good sense.

"Because if the entry goes, the chart goes."

---

He said it plainly and he said it fast, because he had it worked out and he was not going to give a man in a corner time to think of a better reason for the reeve to have said something convenient.

"On the fourth of this month this office entered on its own chart a claim over the mole's inner face from the third seam to the eleventh, pending examination. It is the only piece of paper in this harbor this month that stands between a plate of transparent material in a mole's foundation and a naval vessel's salvage claim, and between the notice of the thirty-first and a man with a line and a lamp at two in the morning. That claim is founded on one thing. It is founded on this office having seen something, on the third, at the top of the tide, and written it down.

"Strike the sighting and the claim has no subject. Strike the claim and the face is unclaimed, and unclaimed ground on a naval claim's second sheet is ground a vessel may enter upon. Strike both and the fifth seam is a piece of rubble in a harbor wall with a man's guess in a market, and the first man to go down there is a man nobody in this room can stop, because there will be no instrument in this harbor naming it at all, not mine, not the circuit office's, and not the yard's, because the yard has withdrawn everything it had by this morning and I would not let them keep it if I could."

He turned round to Calder and did not soften it.

"You may say to me that I have put a fence round my own ground and put you inside it where you cannot get at anything, and I have said so myself twice in the last four days, and it is still true. And now you may add to it that the fence is the only thing left that has a subject. So sit inside it and be quiet about it, because I have had to spend a herring fleet to keep it up and the fleet is not yours and has not been told."

---

The tide gang had been in the room since ten and had not understood one word of the legal half of it, and the foreman of the gang stood up at half past eleven and asked the reeve, in the plain voice of a man with forty years in a mole, whether the moorings on the third to the eleventh seams were going to be worked this winter.

Kest said no.

"You are the authority's own moorings on the authority's own face, and the notice of the thirty-first closes that face to all persons on any work, and I put that in the notice's own enforcement when I served it, and I said in that same paragraph that I would find another way to do them and tell the tide gang so myself, and I have not found another way and I have not told you, and I am telling you now in a hearing room in front of a naval officer rather than on a mole, because it is a thing that is going to cost somebody money and I would rather it cost me in a room."

"How long."

"Until this office lifts the notice or until the notice is lifted, and I cannot lift it, because it is not mine to lift, and there is no instrument I can write in this harbor that lifts a circuit office's notice, and I have looked for one for eleven days." He put the chalk down. "The timber fenders come out of service this month. The four sets of moorings come out of service the first of March. The two herring berths come out of service the first of March, and they are the two berths the herring fleet uses in a northeast blow, and on the first of March this harbor is going to have forty boats coming in with a north-easterly on them and nowhere to lie."

"Who pays for that."

"You do, Mr Neale, and the answer is that I do, out of the authority's dues, and there is not enough of them, and the clerk is going to write it as a loss against this office in his own book and I am going to read the figure back into this record so that it is in a hearing room and not only in a book, and then I am going to carry it up the quay myself and say it to a man whose boat it is, because I have had three days to decide whether to say it to a herring skipper before he found out or after, and I have decided before."

---

Elias Voke of the herring boat *Anna Ray* was in the road outside the quay, and he was fifty-one, and he had paid the authority's moorage dues for nineteen winters, and he had a boat's length of mooring warp out on the *Anna Ray*'s starboard quarter that he had twisted round his own fingers in a way that a person who works a line will do, and he had come up because somebody had said there was a hearing.

"The third to the eleventh," he said, when Kest had said it to him. "That's my two berths and half of my warp and the north wall where I put her in a northeaster."

"That is your two berths."

"And where am I to put her on the first of March."

"On the other side of the basin, and the other side of the basin is the fishing fleet's, and the fishing fleet is here till April, and I have written to a man at the coast office about a hard at Ashtide, and I have had no answer, and if I get no answer by the twelfth of March you will be putting her on a lee shore with a stone keel and a rudder that hangs."

"Aye," said Elias Voke. "That is what I wanted to hear in a room and not in a market."

"You will not be told in a market."

"Then you will have the dues back," Elias Voke said. "Nineteen winters of them, and I will take them in the book, and I will take them on the authority's paper and not on yours, and if that is a small way of doing it then it is the way this harbor does things and I have stopped being proud of it in about 1908."

Kest said, "Enter the claim for the dues as an entry against this office, in the amount of nineteen winters, and mark it as disputed and not as a debt, and give the man a slip of it in his own hand before he goes down the quay, and Mr Clerk, the amount is not yours to guess at, it is the officer's."

The clerk looked at the officer of the authority's accounts, who was a thin dry man who had been in the corner and had said nothing for three days, and the dry man said a number, and Kest wrote it in the hearing record with a date against it, and Calder wrote it in the crew's book too, because it was a figure and a figure that two offices held was a figure that could not go anywhere.

---

The last of the Thursday was the entry, and Kest entered it himself and read it out before it was read back, which is a thing no reeve in this harbor has ever done and which he said he was doing because he had a reason.

*10th, this office's hearing room. The officer of the cutter on the pile moved, and this office refused, that the entry of the third of this month at the fifth seam be struck from this office's record, the ground being that the entry was made by the officer of this office upon ground that this office had closed by its own served notice of the thirty-first, and that the seeing is the observation of this office and the breach is the breach of this office, and both are entered, and the entry is not withdrawn. The ground claimed by this office at the inner face between the third and eleventh seams is claimed pending examination and the claim does not depend on the entry being upheld by any other office. The cost of that claim to this office is the timber fenders, four sets of moorings, and the two herring berths, out of service from this month and the first of March, and the dues of the herring fleet are disputed and are entered as disputed, and the amount is nineteen winters. The notice of the thirty-first stands as served and is not altered and will not be re-served in narrower terms by this office.*

"Read it back," Kest said, and the clerk read it back, and it went into the book, and the entry was longer than any entry the reeve had made in nine years and Calder understood while the boy was reading it that it was not the plate Kest had been protecting. It was the plate, the claim, the cost and the date, all four in one hand, so that a man who took the entry out of the book in a year in Veyr could not take any one of them without the other three.

---

At the quay, at ten past four, on his way down to tell a man with nineteen winters of dues in a book, Kest stopped and said the other thing.

"Your pump, and your clamp stock, and your cordage, and the chest the drawing is in, come out of the clerk's hands on Monday the fourteenth, and the tin is empty, and I have not touched any of it, and I have had two officers from an office that counts things look at it twice this month without being asked to, because it is a bond and bonds are looked at.

"And I am telling you it now because the officer of the cutter does not know it, it is not his business, and if it comes out of a room in the next four days I would like it to come out of this office first." He put his hand on the quay wall. "It comes out on the fourteenth and there is no money in this harbor with a date on it after the fourteenth, and I cannot help you with that, and I would not if I could, because the moment this office puts a mark on a bond for a yard that owes the harbor's dues in another office, we are both of us inside a fence that is not about ground."

"Monday," Calder said.

"Monday. And the boy is to be in the room, because the pump comes out of a tin and the tin is not mine and the oilcloth is not mine, and a clerk does not hand a chest of drawings to a man he has not spoken to."
