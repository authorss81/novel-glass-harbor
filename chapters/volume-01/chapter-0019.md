# Chapter 19

*The Narrow Extension*

The sitting was held on Monday the 24th in the harbor authority's hearing room, with the stove going and the slate wall wiped clean from one end to the other, which the clerk did every Monday whether there was a sitting or not.

Kest sat behind the table with the clerk. In the corner sat a naval officer from the *Vigil* with a notebook, saying nothing, which was his whole office in the room. Calder sat with Nessa and Tobias and Bevin and the twelve marks in a tin on the table in front of him, and Sella Brigg's slate showing a third of unsold iron already entered to another crew, and everything else the crew owned folded into a bundle of paper tied with a piece of cord.

Mara sat apart in her Registry coat, with her book shut, as witness and not as crew.

Kest read the defense, the naval claim, and the permit's spent dates into the record.

"The Free Keel held a claim window and a work permit for two dates, both spent. The glass is in Registry custody under two numbers. The naval salvage claim covers the whole shoal past the harbor line. The question is whether a further defense is warranted, on what bond, and on what share."

The officer spoke once. "The Crown's claim is not bounded by the harbor line. Any extension should not imply access."

"It implies no access," Kest said. "It implies paper."

"Then let me put the paper to the room." The officer did not open the notebook and did not need to. "The harbor authority received labor measurements from a yard at Morrow Quay. It is not a stranger's yard. The salvage report of the *Aster Gannet*, which this office holds a copy of, was signed at Morrow Quay thirteen years ago by a Marr. The *Vigil* has that ground under claim and that report under question. I ask the room whether a harbor instrument ought to be founded on figures taken by that yard, and I ask it to be plain in the record whose figures they are."

The stove ticked. Nobody in the room moved, and Calder found that he had already known this was coming, in the way a man knows a bell is late before it arrives.

He did not stand up.

"I will not defend my father's report," he said. "Not in this room and not in any other, and the office may put that in the record in my words. The report is thirteen years old and it is under your question and I have nothing to say about it that would help anybody."

"Then say what you have," the officer said.

"The chain of work is not my family's. It is a harbor instrument." Calder put two fingers on the labor log and turned it so the clerk could see the foot of the page. "That is the countersigned labor log for the two permit dates. Kest countersigned it. The harbor's own clerk stamped it. Every hour in it was read out of a gauge by Bevin Rook and written where four men could read it the same day, and on one of the two dates the harbor's own hired tender stood the watch with us and was paid out of the harbor's book. If the Crown wants to strike measurements taken at the shoal's inner edge on a permit this office issued, then the Crown is striking the office's own record, and I would like that written down as the alternative."

Kest looked at the log for a while. Then he said, to the room and not to Calder: "I will take the labor as proved. I will not take it as a finding about what was in the hold, and no one in this room has proved what was in the hold."

"Noted," the officer said, and wrote four lines, and Calder did not hear what they were and did not ask, which was the correct thing to do in that room and cost him something to do.

"Then the sheet," Kest said. "The anchor-bearing sheet goes in as a drawing. Three bearings inside four degrees, taken by three men, and the drawing may be read to the room. It is not entered as a finding of where the *Vigil* lay, and it may not be used as one, and if either of you puts it forward as a finding I will strike it myself. Who took each bearing?"

"The men whose names are on it," Calder said. "They are on the sheet. They can be had."

"They can be had, and on a day when the paper in front of me decides whether any of your figures are ever read again, that is the difference between a record and an argument." Kest let that lie where it fell. "Go on with your bond."

The clerk had a question of his own, put in the flat voice of a man filling in a box on a form. "What is the ground worth you, as value, so the office can weigh the bond against it."

Tobias took it, because it was a question about iron. "There is a hundred and forty pound of good iron on that stack with a saw kerf in the shackle that will let half of it go for scrap, and the market will not pay scrap price for a kerf nobody can account for. Call it twenty marks at a fair sale and no more. Call it thirteen to me, because a third of it is entered to Sella Brigg and she has not been paid either."

"And the glass?" the officer said.

"The glass is in an office cupboard under two numbers," Calder said, "and it is worth nothing to me today, and that is the only honest figure in this room."

Then the bond. Calder laid out the rest. The measurements. The plate drawing with eleven keys in his hand. The three repair-seam records. The crew's book with hearing, burn, shutdown, and every cost in it, readable by anyone who cared to come and look. The ten printed copies without Mara's name, two of them on the market wall, and the two documents he had deliberately not printed.

"We can meet twelve marks now," he said, "and the iron when it sells at fair price. We ask twenty days to hold the defense while we work inside the line and pay."

He set the tin on the table and opened the lid, and the clerk counted it out loud on the table because that is the whole of what a bond is: money in a room, counted, in front of a clerk.

The clerk shook his head. "The office requires thirty."

Silence. The stove ticked twice. Tobias put his hand flat on the table.

"Then take the boat," he said. "Not the *Kittiwake*'s hull. Her winter gear. The pump, the spare clamp stock, the new cordage. Hold it against the bond."

Nessa stood. "No. The pump is how we earn."

"It is how we earn and it is what we have." Tobias did not look at her. "A bond is not a fine. It comes back to us if we hold the term, and the term is twenty days."

"Bevin takes the bottom inside the line," Nessa said. "He is twenty-two and he is good at it. The pump is what puts a man on the bottom. You take the pump, and the twenty days are spent looking for a job that needs one, and there is not one on this board."

"Twenty-two is a good age to start carrying other people's money," Tobias said, "and the board has none of it either."

The clerk wrote the value on a slip and pushed it across, and Calder read it upside down before he turned the paper.

Eighteen marks. Pump, clamp stock, cordage, the chest the drawing lived in. He had priced that gear himself in the winter and would not have put it at fifteen, and Kest said nothing whatever about the difference, which was the second time in three days the reeve had cost himself something in Calder's hearing and would not be thanked for it.

Kest wrote. "Twelve marks in hand, gear held to the value of eighteen, iron's sale pledged to the balance. Twenty days' further defense from this date, to the fourteenth of next month, and it lapses then unless a further sitting is held. Harbor's share raised by an eighth against any future raising from the disputed ground. No approach, sounding, working, or lying within four hundred yards of the outer marks. No bell entry without two clean daylight looks and a conservator present, per the crew's own book. The duplicate stays public on the market wall as I ruled. And the record of this sitting stands that the Crown has noted the connection of the yard to the report of thirteen years ago, which the harbor authority has not adopted and will not argue."

He looked up. "That is narrow, and it is what I can grant. The eighth is not negotiable. The Navy's claim stands over it, and I have written the word *noted* where the officer's question is, so that it lies in the record as his question and not as a finding of this office."

Calder felt the eighth go like a tooth. An eighth of any future raising, off the top, before wages, before air, before the chandler. It would follow them to the one-season charter if they ever earned one, and it would be there in the room when they asked for it. But the alternative was no defense, no paper at all, and the naval claim standing alone on the only ground where the compartment could be measured.

"We accept," he said. "With the duplicate staying public on the market wall, as you ruled."

"So ruled," Kest said, and wrote it. "The duplicate is a record of objects and stays."

The officer closed his notebook. Mara opened her book and wrote the terms in her nail hand as witness to method and not to truth, and signed under the reeve's clerk.

Outside, Bevin whooped once and then stopped when Nessa put a hand on his good shoulder.

Calder stood on the basin wall with the extension paper in his coat — twenty days he could barely meet, a bond of gear he could not work without, an eighth gone before it was earned, and a line in a naval notebook that would follow his father's name out of this room and into every room after it — and understood the leash entire: they had more time and less of everything time was for.
