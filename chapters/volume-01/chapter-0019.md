# Chapter 19

*The Narrow Extension*

The sitting was held on Monday the 24th in the harbor authority's hearing room, with the stove going and the slate wall wiped clean.

Kest sat behind the table with the clerk. In the corner sat a naval officer from the *Vigil* with a notebook, saying nothing, which was his whole office in the room. Calder sat with Nessa and Tobias and Bevin. Mara sat apart in her Registry coat, with her book shut, as witness and not as crew.

Kest read the defense, the naval claim, and the permit's spent dates into the record.

"The Free Keel held a claim window and a work permit for two dates, both spent. The glass is in Registry custody under two numbers. The naval salvage claim covers the whole shoal past the harbor line. The question is whether a further defense is warranted, on what bond, and on what share."

The officer spoke once. "The Crown's claim is not bounded by the harbor line. Any extension should not imply access."

"It implies no access," Kest said. "It implies paper."

Calder laid out what he had: the countersigned labor log, the measurements, the plate drawing with eleven keys in his hand, the anchor-bearing sheet with three bearings inside four degrees, the crew's book with hearing, burn, shutdown, and every cost, the ten printed copies without Mara's name, and the tin with nine marks in it and Sella Brigg's slate showing a third of unsold iron already entered to another crew.

"We can meet twelve marks now," he said, "and the iron when it sells at fair price. We ask twenty days to hold the defense while we work inside the line and pay."

The clerk shook his head. "The office requires thirty."

Silence. Tobias put his hand on the table.

"Then take the boat," he said. "Not the *Kittiwake*'s hull. Her winter gear. The good pump, the spare clamp stock, the new cordage. Hold it against the bond."

Nessa stood. "No. The pump is how we earn."

"It is how we earn and it is what we have." Tobias did not look at her. "A bond is not a fine. It comes back if we hold the term."

Kest wrote. "Twelve marks in hand, gear held to the value of eighteen, iron's sale pledged to the balance. Twenty days' further defense, lapsing on the twenty-first unless a further sitting is held. Harbor's share raised by an eighth against any future raising from the disputed ground. No approach, sounding, working, or lying within four hundred yards of the outer marks. No bell entry without two clean daylight looks and a conservator present, per the crew's own book."

He looked up. "That is narrow, and it is what I can grant. The eighth is not negotiable. The Navy's claim stands over it."

Calder felt the eighth go like a tooth. An eighth of any future raising, off the top, before wages, before air, before the chandler. It would follow them to the one-season charter if they ever earned one. But the alternative was no defense, no paper, and the naval claim alone on the ground.

"We accept," he said. "With the duplicate staying public on the market wall, as you ruled."

"So ruled," Kest said, and wrote it. "The duplicate is a record of objects and stays."

The officer closed his notebook. Mara opened her book and wrote the terms in her nail hand as witness to method, not to truth, and signed under Kest's clerk.

Outside, Bevin whooped once and then stopped when Nessa put a hand on his good shoulder.

Calder stood on the basin wall with the extension paper in his coat — twenty days he could barely meet, a bond of gear he could not work without, and an eighth gone before it was earned — and understood the leash entire: they had more time and less of everything time was for.
