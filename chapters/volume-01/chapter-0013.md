# Chapter 13

*The Name on the Paper*

Monday the 17th was cold and clear, and the hearing room in the reeve's office on the west basin smelled of stove smoke and damp paper.

Calder had come for a small thing. With the *Merrit* money short and the chandler pressing, he needed the harbor clerk to countersign a fresh ordinary job: a week of pump hire and bottom scrubbing for two coasters inside the mole, twenty-two marks, enough to carry wages and buy air. Nessa had the figures, Tobias had the recommendation from thirty years of fair work, and the Morrow Quay charter covered every fathom of it.

The clerk was a young man with ink on his cuffs who read the application twice and then turned it over.

"Marr," he said. "Calder Marr, son of Alden Marr."

"Yes."

"Then I cannot countersign this without a Registry countersign." He laid his pen down. "There is a direction. Where a harbor salvage report is under naval question, no further harbor instrument is to be issued to a man of that house without the circuit office's mark. Your father signed the Gannet salvage report. The *Vigil* has lodged over the shoal. Your name is the reason."

Calder felt the room narrow. "That report is thirteen years old. I am asking to scrub two coasters inside the mole."

"I know what you are asking. I am telling you what I am told to tell." The clerk did not meet his eyes. "You may attack the report if you wish. Make a statement that your father signed what was not true, and the office will send it to the circuit, and the circuit may lift the direction. Or you may accept the price."

"What price?"

"A higher bond. Eight marks on a twenty-two-mark job, held against the week, and the harbor's share raised by a sixteenth, for the risk." The clerk pushed the amended sheet across. "That is what a Marr pays this month."

Calder stood with his hands behind his back so the clerk would not see them. To attack the paper would be to say in a hearing room what he had never said at his father's bench: that Alden had signed a false report. It would buy work and spend the last thing between him and his father. To accept would be to pay eight marks he did not have and lose a sixteenth he could not spare.

He thought of Alden at the bench with the drawknife, coughing on a forty-minute cycle, keeping every tool in its place.

"I will not make a statement about my father's report," he said. "Not here and not for this."

"Then sign the higher bond."

"I have not got eight marks." Calder laid the *Merrit* bag on the table with eleven marks in it, wet still at the seams. "I have this, and an arm that will not take a glove, and a crew that needs the week. Hold four against the job and take the sixteenth, and I will bring the rest when the scrub is half done."

The clerk shook his head. "I cannot hold short."

From the yard that evening Calder went to his father's end of the shed. Alden was mending a tiller, and did not look up.

"I need a witness," Calder said. "Not about the report. About work. Come to the office and say I scrubbed your boats for ten years and never lost one. That is all."

Alden set the tiller down. The cough came and went.

"No," he said.

"It is not the report. It is a week's scrubbing inside the mole."

"It is my name in a room where a naval claim is on the table." Alden picked up the tiller again. "I have three repair jobs that keep this roof up. If I stand beside you in that room, the owners will take their boats to Ternwatch. I will not do it, Calder. Not this month and not in that room."

The refusal cost four marks and a week. Calder walked back up the shed with it like a weight.

Nessa was waiting at the bench. He told her plain.

"He will not stand. It is not the report he is refusing. It is being seen. And it costs us the scrubbing job, because I will not buy it by signing against him."

Nessa wrote in the book: *17th. Ordinary job refused for name. Diver refused to make statement against father. Bond short. Job lost.*

Calder signed with his left hand. "We find another way that does not ask him to spend his roof for my week."

That night he paid the chandler four marks out of the eleven and kept seven for air, and the note went to twelve weeks into six with the chandler writing the figure without comment, which was worse than comment.
