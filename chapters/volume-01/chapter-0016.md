# Chapter 16

*Honest Work*

Thursday dawned fair, and the harbor's own work came to them because nobody else would take it cheap.

The buoy master needed the outer harbor bell buoy lifted, scraped, and re-hung before the equinox tides, with a new bellwright's fitting on the clapper and a white cord spliced for a remembered route mark. It was work inside the line under the Morrow Quay charter, two days, twenty-six marks with cord and fitting found by the harbor, and it wanted a diver for half a tide to clear the shackle and a crew aloft to handle the lift.

Calder could not take the bottom. Nessa wrote it before he could argue: *Diver off bottom till burn closed. Bevin on bottom under tender.* Bevin, twenty-two, who had never taken a working dive alone, went down in nine feet off the buoy punt with Calder on the tender's cleat calling the line and Nessa on the fall.

The work went the way work goes when it is allowed to. Bevin cleared the shackle, passed the strop, came up when Calder called the time and not when he was ready, and went down again. The buoy came up streaming weed, and Tobias and Nessa scraped it on the punt while Calder spliced the white cord with his left hand doing the work his right could not, and the bellwright's fitting — a brass collar and a new tongue — went on with grease and a cotter.

At noon the buoy master came out in a skiff and watched Bevin hang the bell. When the buoy went back over, the bell rang with the swell, three clear strokes that belonged to the harbor and to nobody else, and under it Calder heard the second sound a third of a second late and did not turn, because Bevin put a hand on his shoulder and said, "Harbor bell, to starboard," the way they had agreed.

They ate on the punt: bread, cold fish, onions. Bevin ate like a boy after his first full tide below, and Tobias told a story about a buoy off Ashtide that rang for a week after it was condemned, and Nessa laughed with grease to her elbows.

The buoy master paid twenty-six marks in full on the wall, and counted out the harbor's share and the crew's share in front of them. After rope, tallow from Kest's order, and food, eighteen marks stayed in the yard's bag. Calder put seven to air, four to the chandler, and seven in the tin for the bond.

That afternoon from the fish dock they watched the harbor's lift at the mole. A crane on a lighter, a chain, a slip's crew, Kest on the wall with a book. The drifted storm anchor came up out of eight feet off the third seam streaming weed, two fathoms of old iron with Calder's lead line off it and Kest's rag float still on it, and the crew cheered when it broke water. Calder stood among them with his sling on and did not go to the wall, because he had been told not to, and watched municipal tackle go up at municipal cost while the *Vigil*'s tender watched from a quarter mile off.

"It is theirs," Bevin said beside him. "Proper."

"It is theirs," Calder said. "And it is up, which is more than it was."

They walked back with eighteen marks earned and money in the tin. Nessa wrote: *20th. Buoy work, inside line, paid in full. Diver tended, did not dive. Hearing still double. Crew compensated. Ate.*

Calder added underneath, left-handed: *Work going right is still work.*

That night he slept without waking for the first time in a week.
