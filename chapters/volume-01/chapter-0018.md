# Chapter 18

*What a Record Serves*

Saturday the 22nd Bevin came into the shed with ink on his nose and a plan.

"The print shop on Linen Street will copy the duplicate for two marks a copy," he said. "Bearing sheet and plate drawing with it. If we post them at Ashtide and the Lowfen landing, every crew on the coast will know the *Vigil* lay on our bearing. That is public use, and Kest said the duplicate stays public."

Calder looked at Nessa. Nessa looked at the duplicate's four sheets on the market wall, weather-curling at the corners, with every name on it.

"It is a record of objects," Calder said slowly. "Not of anything inside them. Kest said no office has power over that."

"Then we print fifty," Bevin said. "Mara's name is on it as witness. That will make them read it."

They took the sheets to the print shop, where the printer read them twice and named his price: two marks a copy for fifty, a hundred marks the yard did not have, or twelve marks for ten with the bearing sheet left out because the press could not hold the drawing's scale.

"We will take ten of the words," Calder said, "and keep the drawing here."

Mara found them at the press with the type already set. She read the proof, and her face went white.

"Take my name off it," she said.

Calder stared. "You witnessed it. You made it out loud."

"I witnessed measurements, hours, and hands for a harbor wall, where any hand can answer me." Her voice was low and fast. "Printed and carried to another harbor, my name becomes a Registry warrant that the record is complete and fit for strangers to act on. It is not. It has an aperture sealed and unopened, a possible handling limit, three repair histories with a ceiling not a date, and a ground band whose purpose is not stated. Strangers will read it as permission."

The printer waited with his hands on the type.

"This is the consent question," Mara said, "in its first form. What may be published about a wreck, and who does a public record serve? A record serves the living who must work the ground, not the dead who cannot answer, and not a crew that needs a crowd. If you print my name across the coast, you spend my ability to witness the next thing truly."

Calder thought of the sitting, of thirty marks and an eighth of share, of how much a printed coast would help.

"Set it without her name," he told the printer. "Crew's hands only, with the line that it is a crew's copy, witnessed in Morrow Quay, incomplete."

The printer reset the type. Ten copies for twelve marks — the last of the buoy money and five of the tin for the bond. They posted two on the market wall beside the original, gave three to Free Keel crews for their walls, and kept five tied in oilcloth.

On the way back Mara walked beside him, still angry.

"You paid twelve marks to make yourselves weaker," she said.

"We paid twelve to keep your word good," Calder said. "A record that spends its witness is not public. It is used."

She did not answer at once. Then: "Keep one copy dry. The sitting will want to see what you did not print."

Nessa wrote that night: *22nd. Ten copies printed without conservator's name, twelve marks paid. Bearing sheet and plate drawing withheld. Cost: bond tin short.*

Calder signed. The duplicate stayed public but small, and the cost of keeping it honest was on the page.
