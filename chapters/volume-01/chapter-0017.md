# Chapter 17

*Mara's Certificate*

Friday morning Mara Venn was at the bench with her book shut and a paper in her hand she did not want to show.

Calder knew the paper. It was the certificate of the public record's figures she had bought in the copying room for forty marks — the survey's ninth page and the case specification, certified as public figures at the office's expense and hers. Forty marks the yard owed her and could not pay.

"I have come about cost," she said. "Not yours. Mine."

She laid the paper down. Behind it was a second paper, a Registry memorandum in a clerk's hand: her copying expense queried, her witnessing of a crew duplicate for the same crew twice noted, her three *not stated* entries against Calder Marr underlined with a request for clarification.

"They do not call it a reprimand," she said. "They call it a query. A query is a reprimand that has not decided what it is yet. If I answer badly, it becomes a note in my file. If I answer well, it becomes a note in my file that I answered well, which is also a note."

Calder reached for the certificate. "We will pay the forty when the iron sells."

"You will. That is not what I came about." She put her hand on the book. "You asked yesterday by Bevin for two things. Say them to me now."

He said them plain. "Certify our bearing sheet for the sitting, as provenance. And write me a line to take into the custody room to look at the plate's flange again."

Mara opened the book and did not look up.

"No," she said. "To both, and I am right, and you will hear why."

"The bearing sheet has three bearings inside four degrees and your check on them. That is true."

"It is true that three men took bearings and I checked the arithmetic. It is not provenance. Provenance is chain of custody and material origin with stated limits. A bearing is a direction men took with an instrument. If I certify it as provenance, the sitting will treat it as Registry proof, and when the Navy shows it is only a direction, my word and your sheet fall together." She closed the book. "Bring the sheet. Bring the men who took each bearing to speak it. I will stand beside it as witness to method. I will not certify it as what it is not."

"And the custody room?"

"The custody room holds two objects under two numbers as seized material presumed Crown until a claimant is entered. You are not the claimant. I am not the custodian. If I write you a line, I spend the only credit that lets me witness anything for you, and we both lose the sitting." Her voice did not soften. "Ask permission in the room, through the reeve, for a supervised look with a reason. The answer will be no or heavily conditional. That is the proper refusal, and you will take it properly."

Calder was angry, and the anger was at the word she had used in the infirmary — *seam* — and at himself for needing her.

"You use words like *seam* and then tell me I cannot use them."

"I used *seam* as a possible handling limit on a loose fragment, with a written plan required. You want to use it as proof." She stood. "Mr Marr, I have three places in my book where you did not tell me something. I told you one of them will be the reason your evidence is set aside. If I give you what you ask today, I make it today."

She left the certificate on the bench and went to the door, then stopped.

"The forty marks stands as owed," she said. "I am not forgiving it to make us easier with each other. And Calder —" She used his name once, and it cost her. "The buoy bell yesterday was well hung. Bevin told me you did not turn. That is practice, not proof, and it is the only thing that will get you through a sitting."

After she had gone, Nessa picked up the certificate and put it with the labor log.

"She refused you," Nessa said.

"She refused me and she is right," Calder said. "Write it."

Nessa wrote: *21st. Conservator refused certification of bearing sheet and refused custody line. Refusal stands. Certificate debt forty marks owed.*

Calder signed left-handed. The refusal held, and the yard was poorer for it and better for it at once.
