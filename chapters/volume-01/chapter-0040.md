# Chapter 40

*The Fourteenth*

The hearing room was full by ten and the fire had been in it since half past eight, and there were people standing at the back who had not been in the room on the seventh and the eighth and who had come for a morning sitting and found a chair in front of them.

Kest let the room fill. That was new. On the twenty-fourth of October and on the seventh and the eighth of this month he had begun at ten sharp with the door shut and the clerk's pen going, and this time he began at eleven because he had sent a message down at half past eight that said the sitting would be held at eleven, and the reason, which he gave out loud before anybody asked, was that a man in a coat with a document in his apron had asked to be told something in a room and had asked to be told it by eleven, and that a reeve who cannot give a man an hour because a market is standing in the road is a reeve who has the wrong idea of what the room is for.

Mara Venn was against the wall on the same chair as the seventh, and the clerk marked her in the attendance column the same way.

The naval officer of the *Vigil* was in his corner with the notebook, and his man was beside him with a satchel, and between them and the door stood a man in a reeve's coat who had not spoken to Calder Marr since the fourth of November and had not needed to, because Ors Kest was the reeve and had been the reeve for fifteen years and the room turned to him the way water turns to a hole in a road.

Kest called the terms.

"The sitting is resumed from the eighth of this month. The reeve's rulings of the seventh are entered and stand and are not re-opened, and the yard's terms one to ten were granted, refused or received in whole or in part on the seventh and the clerk has read them back into the record twice. What is before the room this morning is four things, and I am going to name all four of them before I take any of them, because a room that knows what is coming can hear the difference between a decision and a performance.

"One. The yard's answer to the question put on the eighth, which was what a difference of ten yards means when the number it is a difference of is not better than ten yards.

"Two. The reason the yard gave on the seventh for wanting the bottom restored, entered as term eight requires, with the reeve's consent and not at the yard's asking, because a reason that a yard does not have to give is worth more to a room than a reason it is obliged to give.

"Three. A claim by the harbor's own officer of dues, entered of his own motion and without leave, which I will come to at the end because it is the only matter in this room that does not concern glass and it is going to cost this office nineteen winters of money.

"Four. The defense of the twenty-fourth of last month, which expires today."

He let the last of that sit in the room for a moment and then went to the first.

---

"It is a fair question," the naval officer said, before Calder Marr had got to his feet, "and I was going to ask it again this morning, and I am going to hold what I have in my notebook until the yard has spoken, and I would like the clerk to note that the vessel reserved the question at the sitting of the eighth and the yard has answered it first."

"That is generous," Calder said, "and I would like it in the record that it was generous, and I am going to give the officer the answer and the ground of it in one go, and I am going to do it the way the yard did it on the Tuesday, which is before anybody has to ask."

Then he read the withdrawal out of the crew's book, in his own left hand, slowly, in a hearing room, with a clerk writing.

*The difference of ten yards is withdrawn by the yard. It does not appear in the record. The two figures it was a difference between are of different kinds, being a tender's count from a boat with a hand lead on a moving bottom and the harbor authority's own run along its own course with a wire on a lead corrected by a column nobody has read for forty years, and neither figure is better than the method by which it was taken, and the crew does not know whether the three places are on a straight within anything better than the length of the first of them, which is a bedded seam in a rubble foundation running north under the mole's own stone to about two boat lengths, and the crew does not offer a figure for that either.*

"And there is a second withdrawal," Calder said, "and it is not the yard's, and I am going to make it in front of this room because it was made at my bench at half past eight this morning by the man whose number it is.

"Bevin Rook, aged twenty-two, gave the tender's count of nine feet and ten and a half feet at the mole's toe on the twenty-eighth of October, and gave his own count of the same thing, out loud, on the ladder, as seven and eleven. The diver's own count was not entered at the time because the tender's count was the one a man on the mole could check. It is entered now, by Rook, on his own statement, because the first point is his number and not mine, and because the yard has withdrawn the figure of ten yards and cannot do that and leave the only other figure about the first point inside the yard's own silence. Rook has asked that it be read into this record as given by him."

"It is given by him," said the naval officer, from the corner, in a voice that had lost some of its edge, "and I will say one thing about it, and it is not a concession, because the vessel has no interest in a hand lead on a rubble toe. That is the first time in three sittings that a party has produced the figure that damages itself, unasked, and in front of the figure it is damaging. I have made a great many notes in a great many rooms and I have never written that one."

"Then it is in your notebook," Calder said, "and I would like the yard's copy of your notebook's conclusion, which is that the vessel's question is answered and the answer is *we do not know, and here is exactly how much we do not know, and here is the one thing in it that is not ours*. Which is the sixth station of this office's own annual course, at fifteen fathoms and two corrected, one figure out of thirty-six on two miles of a flat that this office has run every spring for forty years, and which is the only one of the three points that no man can say the Free Keel yard made up."

"It is the only one of the three that is worth a damn," the officer said, "and the vessel has said so in its own voice, and the vessel will say it again in a room in Veyr if it is asked and if there is anything in the file to say it about. Which brings me to the plate drawing."

---

The officer asked for it in the terms Calder had set out in the room on the eighth: on the table, in the hearing room, in daylight, with the clerk, one at a time, no notes beyond such as the law allows on a document that is not his.

It came out of the bench chest in a hand-barrow with a rope round it, four foot two by eleven inches, with eleven keys marked in Calder Marr's own hand and a note beside them that said *continuous west, no break, no join*, and the officer's man put a rule on it and counted the keys twice, and then he turned it over and looked at the underside.

The mark was there. Nine short lines joined at a single point, cut with a tool and not a mould, standing about two inches proud in harder grey glass, clear of the fouling, on the underside of a plate that was in a custody room in the west of this city and was on a drawing in a municipal hearing room because a reeve's ruling on the seventh had let a sheet be read in daylight and had not let a man carry it out of a room.

The officer's man wrote for about four minutes. Then the officer spoke.

"I am going to ask one question about that mark and I am going to ask it of the conservator, and I would like the room to notice that I am asking a Registry officer about a Registry mark in a municipal room, and not the yard.

"Mr Venn. Is that a mark this office has a name for?"

Mara Venn said, "It is a builder's mark of a pre-Accord construction, and it is not a claim seal and not a cargo seal and not a mark of consent, and this office has four books that list every mark a Serein office is permitted to strike into glass and it is not in any of them, and the compound under it has gone to powder and the mark has not moved, which puts the mark before the plate was ever worked into a hull. I will not name a builder and I am not going to be asked to. I have asked and I have been told no and I believed them, and I have been believing them for four months and nobody has thought to ask me again in a room."

"And if the yard were to produce, at some future sitting, a mark of the same pattern on a second object?"

"Then this office would have two, and a name for nothing, and a question that has grown from one object to two, and I would write both of them down under the same four words, and the words would be *builder's mark, pre-Accord, not a seal, records no consent*, and the last three of those are not mine. They were entered in the yard's public record on the eighth of October by this office, and they are the best sentence in it, and I would like them read out again today if anybody has a moment."

The plate drawing went back in the chest and the chest went back in the barrow and the rope went round it, and the officer's man wrote the hour on the slip, and the room understood that a thing had been shown and had not left.

---

Kest took the second matter, and he took it standing up, and he did not read it, because it was Calder's two hundred and forty words and Calder had to be the one who said them.

The room was very quiet for two hundred and forty words. There was a bedded seam in a mole's foundation with three keys on its upper edge and a Crown-shop bolted rim in its lower corner and darker material inside the rim, seen and not measured on the third of this month at the top of the tide, entered in the reeve's own book with three named witnesses. There was a plate in the custody room of the Morrow circuit office entered as a transparent hull plate of undetermined origin with a bolted flange over a sealed aperture and a chain of custody from a courier and two numbers. There was an ordinary shoal anchor with a saw kerf in the crown shackle nearly through at the point of the wear, loaded again, on a yard stack. There was a fouled ground line of right-hand lay, four-strand, tarred hemp, six seasons old, seized with round stuff and three plain turns and a hitch dressed from the left, the turns closing left-handed over the standing part, which is not this harbor's make. And there was the harbor authority's own list, eight years old, naming a shoal-end coaster lying unclaimed on that ground with her ground tackle entered as light and gone in a westerly some six years back.

And then the two hundred and forty words said what the yard did not know, which was the purpose, and said what the yard was not asking, which was a comparison, because the yard is barred from the custody room by a notice of the thirty-first and has asked for nothing there and does not ask for anything there today.

Calder Marr read the last line and stopped.

"Mr Kest, the eighth term asks for a stated reason entered before the diver goes in. There is no diver and no going in, and the yard has no instrument, and I am not asking this room for one. And there is a thing in the reason that is not in it, and I would like the room to hear that from me rather than find it in a book in four years, because a man in a cutter's coat has been in three rooms in this harbor this month and has read every document this yard has, and a thing that is not in a document gets read harder than a thing that is in one.

"The yard was not asked why it wants the bottom back. Nobody in this room has had the right to ask and the yard has not been asked and has not offered and does not intend to offer. The room may enter that the yard was asked nothing and offered nothing and is content to stand there, and the yard would rather be entered that way than be entered as a crew that declined to state a reason, because a crew that declined is a crew with something, and a crew that was asked nothing is a crew with a hearing on the twenty-eighth."

The clerk read the entry back before it was made and Calder corrected one word in it and the clerk made it again.

And then Mara Venn said, from the wall, in the flat voice, in a room with fourteen people in it:

"And the fourth *not stated* against this yard is entered in this record, in this room, at this hour, at the yard's request and not at mine, and the word *not stated* in a municipal document means that a person was not asked and did not offer, and it is not a finding and it is not a judgment, and I have written it and I have three others in a book in my office and I am not going to argue about the fourth in here."

Nobody spoke for about four seconds, and then the naval officer of the *Vigil* said, "Mr Kest, the vessel notes that a person who enters a fourth such entry against a party in a public record is a person who is building a file, and the vessel does not object to it, and the vessel has a file of its own."

"I have a file of my own," Kest said, "and so does the clerk, and the boy at the counter has a book with my mooring and the tide bell in it, and I would remind this room that the office which watches a person build a file is the office most likely to have one of its own."

---

The third matter was Elias Voke's, and Kest took it last, as he had said he would, and he took it in the flattest voice he had used in a room in fifteen years.

"Mr Voke of the herring boat *Anna Ray* has a claim against this office for nineteen winters of moorage dues, on the ground that this office has claimed the mole's inner face from the third seam to the eleventh seam on its own chart, and that the two herring berths and four sets of moorings and the timber fenders on that face are out of service in consequence. It is entered of my motion, without leave, over the clerk's objection that it does not concern the matter before the room. It concerns the matter before the room. It is the only thing in this file that a person in Morrow Quay has actually lost, and no office in Veyr will ever be told about it unless somebody puts it in a record.

"Mr Voke. Say it."

Elias Voke said it in about forty words. Nineteen winters. Two berths and a warp, the north wall, a northeast blow, forty boats in on the first of March, a hard at Ashtide applied for and not answered, and he wanted the account taken out of the boat rather than the boat out of the water, and he would rather have it disputed in a hearing room than quietly forgotten in a market.

"It is entered as disputed and not as a debt," Kest said, "and the amount is on the page, and the clerk will give you a slip of it before you go down the quay, and the first of March is the date, and I will write to the coast office again this week and I will tell you in a fortnight what I have, in this room, on this day, whether it is a hard at Ashtide or nothing at all, because a man should not have to come and ask an office whether it has written a letter."

"And the notice?" Elias Voke said. "Because the notice is what shut it, and not your chart."

"The notice stands," Kest said, "and my chart is inside it, and neither of them is lifted today and neither of them will be lifted by anything said in this room this morning, and I am the man who wrote the enforcement that put this office's own moorings out of service, and I have said so in a room twice this week and I will say it again on the first of March when there is nobody in the room but the herring fleet and me."

---

Then the fourth matter, and the last, and the whole of what the morning had been for.

"The defense of the twenty-fourth of last month expires today," Kest said, "at the hour it was entered, and I am going to say what this office is going to do with it before anybody in this room makes an argument, because if I wait I will be answering an argument.

"It is discharged. Not extended. Not renewed. There is no further defense and none is offered, and I am not going to hold a term open for a yard that has discharged it. The grounds are four, and I would like them read back.

"One. The labor of the Free Keel yard upon the two dates of the permit of the twelfth of October is found proved, as it was found and countersigned on the day, and is not to be disturbed by anything said after it, in this office or in any other. That is the only finding in this file that no office in Veyr has ever had cause to touch, and I have watched four papers get built on top of it this month and not one of them has come near it.

"Two. The three points of the second of this month are received as a record of the crew's own figures and of one figure out of this office's own book, with the limits the yard has stated this morning entered over them, and the figure of ten yards withdrawn by the yard, and this office makes no finding on what the three points are, and the ruling of the second that no line may be drawn between them is not lifted.

"Three. The notice of the thirty-first is received as served and correct, and this office has no power to alter it, and the yard's statement of the seventh that it has said all it means to say about that notice in the conservator's second note, which bears the date of the twenty-second of this month and is not in this room, is entered, and the room is asked to note that date and not to wait for it.

"Four. The instrument of the third of this month is received, and the thing at the fifth seam is received as seen and not measured, and the entry of the third stands, and the reeve's breach of the notice of the thirty-first stands in this office's own book with three named witnesses, and is not withdrawn, and the ground claimed by this office on its own chart pending examination is not withdrawn, and it is not the Free Keel yard's, and the Free Keel yard is not inside it as a beneficiary of it and never was.

"Now that instrument is conditional and the condition has failed, because the circuit office returned the plan uncountersigned on the twelfth with four grounds, and the first of them is that the notice of the thirty-first was issued by that office and that this office will not countersign an instrument which would put a person inside the closure its own notice created. That is a correct ground. I told this room on the seventh that it would be refused and I said I would not ask for it, and I did not ask for it, and a man asked for it on his own paper without this office in it, and it was refused, and I would be ashamed of that office if it had not been.

"So the bottom goes back to me on tomorrow morning. The restoration of the seventh ran to the term and the term is discharged today, and from tomorrow the undertaking of the twenty-fifth of last month stands in my hand again, and no man in this room is to tell any other man in this room that the reeve gave the Free Keel yard its bottom, because I did, for seven days, and it was worth precisely nothing."

---

Bevin Rook's mother was standing at the back of the room since eleven, having come up the quay at half past ten with a folded paper in her apron, and she had not asked anybody to make a place for her and had not wanted one.

Calder Marr did not call her forward. He turned round on his heel and said it to the room, and he was in a coat because it was November and he had nothing else to stand in, and the mother came four steps into the room and stood at the side of it with the paper in her apron and let it happen.

"Twenty-four marks," Calder Marr said. "Four a week for six weeks from the twenty-ninth of October to the tenth of December, and the yard does not have it and is not going to pretend to a boy of twenty-two that it has. There is a bonesetter's mark spent and a surgeon's mark spent, and the surgeon's writing is in this woman's apron because she asked for the original, and it is hers, and the yard has a copy and has never asked for it back.

"Sixteen marks is claimed for the reading, from the fourteenth of October, and is a claim and not a gift, and the boy asked for the claim rather than the money, and the twenty-four is claimed beside it, and both are in the yard's book with the hours against them, and the reading is a trade and not a wage until the tenth of December, and after the tenth of December the wages begin at four a week and the forty marks stands.

"Calder Marr held the tender's cleat. Nessa Pike gave the call. Both are on the surgeon's paper and I am not going to read them off a paper, because the paper is not the thing. I held the cleat and I gave a boy a call of twelve minutes and I could not see him from where I was standing, and I have not been able to find one thing I would do differently that would not have cost somebody else more than it cost him, and I learned that in a month from a woman who will not write down a thing she has not checked.

"Bevin Rook goes back in the water on the tenth of December, under a tender who is not him, and nobody in this room is to suggest any other date, and the bonesetter says the same and the reeve's office is not going to be asked to say anything about it at all."

The mother took the paper out of her apron and held it up, and did not read it, and put it back.

"It is the original," she said, and went four steps back.

Kest said, "Entered in the record, at the yard's request, the claim of twenty-four marks for the hand and sixteen marks for the reading, with the hours against both, the date of the tenth of December, and the mother's statement that the original is hers." The clerk wrote it. "And the reeve will say one more thing on the same page, and it is not a finding and it is not a promise. This office has paid this crew twice in nine weeks, it has paid eighteen of twenty-six on a job that was not finished, and it refused a countersign in the middle of the middle, and it will pay on a moor of the *Anna Ray* in the first week of March if the moorings on that face are not back in service by then, and I am saying it in a hearing room because I do not expect the harbor authority to say it to itself."

---

Then he said the last thing, and he said it standing up with his hands behind his back, and Calder Marr understood four days too late that the man had been building it since the seventh.

"And there is one further matter and I have brought it on my own motion and nobody asked me, and I am aware that a reeve who brings a thing on his own motion into a discharged defense is a reeve who has put something on a table, and I am doing it in front of a naval officer so that there is a witness to me doing it.

"The harbor authority has issued a claim window, a work permit and a further defense to a crew in five weeks, and has entered a claim on its own chart, and has served a notice, and has had a Registry order served on a mole at eleven o'clock in the morning, and every one of those instruments was made against a fixed date, and the last of the fixed dates expires today, and on Monday morning this office will have a chart with a fence on it and a book with a breach in it and a wall with a notice on it and no crew.

"This office has a power it has never used and never wanted to use, which is to raise of its own motion the question of a one-season provisional working charter. I am raising it. It is a wider permission than anything this yard has held, it is granted in this room after a hearing, it must be renewed, and it is not the Second Bell and does not lead to it, and I am aware of the harbor's eighth and the harbor's share of any raising and I will not argue either of them.

"Notice goes to the Free Keel yard, to the Morrow circuit office, and to the cutter on the pile, and the *Vigil* will have a month to send a paper and I would like it to send it. The Free Keel yard will file a written purpose with this office by the twenty-first of this month in its own hand, and I will read it into the record with the door shut on the twenty-second, and the sitting is on the twenty-eighth, and I will hold it whether anybody objects or not, and anybody who objects may be heard.

"And I am not doing it as a favor. This office is being asked to pay a herring fleet out of its own dues in March, to hold a fence round a mole's face against a naval claim, and to have found a Free Keel yard's labor in a form it can be relied on, and I am doing it because in four days this office has found that the Free Keel yard is the only crew in this harbor that puts a reason in a book before it goes in the water, and because I would rather spend this office's authority on a hearing on the twenty-eighth than on a letter to a coast office about a hard at Ashtide, and I have had that preference since the twelfth of October and I have not enjoyed it."

The clerk read it back and the room was silent.

Kest sat down. "The sitting is closed," he said. "The defense of the twenty-fourth of last month is discharged. The term of the restoration of the seventh ends at midnight tonight. The purpose is due on the twenty-first. The hearing is on the twenty-eighth. And the figure of ten yards is not on any wall in this harbor, and I would like the officer's man to look at the wall before he goes, because I would like a naval record to contain the fact that a yard in this harbor took a number down."

---

They were out on the quay by twenty past twelve and the sun was out for the first time in four days, and there were eleven people outside the hearing room who had come for a morning sitting and were standing about in the cold talking to each other, which in Morrow Quay is a market that has run itself.

Alden Marr was on the top step of the quay with his hat in his hands and he had not come in and he did not say anything and Calder did not ask him anything, and that was the whole of the family beat and the whole of what it cost, and Bevin Rook's mother went down past both of them and touched the boy's sleeve as she went and did not stop.

At the top of the make, an hour later, out in the road at the end of the mole, the harbor's tide bell went three strokes, and under the third of them, a third of a second behind, the flat note came up out of the bottom off the mole with its three marks and its flick, the way a man answers a call he was not called to, and Bevin Rook had the book out before the second stroke had finished and Calder did not turn round, and the officer's man came out of the quay and wrote it on his own slate too, and the two slates had the same hour and the same state of the water and no source, and neither of the three men in the road could say what it was, and Bevin Rook read his entry back and then read the naval rating's, which he could see from where he stood, and said, "That is the same line twice in two books and neither of us put the other one there, and if a man in Veyr ever asks what it is, he has got two documents that say the same thing and neither of them says what it is, and that is more than we had in October and it is not enough, and I am going to write *two records, same hour, no source* under mine and I would like him to write that too, and then there are two records that know about each other."

At four o'clock Calder Marr took the slate off the shelf above the labor log, the one headed as the evidence heard by Calder Marr, unverified, from himself, and not corroborated by any document, witness, instrument, or second person, with the one false line in it that said the personal association had been confirmed by the diver and not disclosed, which had stood on that shelf since the fourteenth of October and gained nothing in thirty-one days.

He looked at it for a while in a shed with the stove going and four people in it and a pump standing against the wall with a cracked housing in it and nine marks in a bag.

Then he put it back on the shelf, on its own shelf, four feet from the book, and took down a blank one, and wrote a heading on it and nothing else under the heading, and the heading was the date and the words *Purpose for the sitting of the twenty-eighth, to be filed with the harbor authority by the twenty-first, in the hand of Marr, read into the record with the door shut.*

He left the rest of it blank for the night, and Bevin Rook read the heading back twice and put the pencil on top of the book, and outside the tide was making on the mole face and a quarter of a mile off the mole a naval tender held a station she had held for thirty days, and the fifth of March was a fortnight away, and the first of March was a fortnight and a half away, and the notice of the thirty-first was still on the wall, and nobody in that shed was going to be allowed to forget why.
