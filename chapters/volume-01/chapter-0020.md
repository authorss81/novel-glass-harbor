# Chapter 20

*The Leash*

Tuesday the 25th was fair and cold, and the yard opened with a bond to meet and a pump in pawn.

Calder got out the crew's book and laid it on the bench in front of all of them — Nessa, Bevin, Tobias — with Mara standing at the shed door because she had come to witness the iron's entry and would not come further.

He wrote the terms in his own hand, left-handed, slow so it would be fair: *Twenty days' defense from 24th. Bond: twelve marks paid, gear held to eighteen. Harbor's eighth off any future raising. No approach within four hundred yards of outer marks. No bell entry without two clean looks and conservator. Slip tides to Free Keel through November. Iron third to Low Lantern when sold fair. Certificate forty marks owed to Venn. Cradle to spring.*

He read it aloud. The bell in his ears gave every line a shadow a third of a second late, and Bevin said the numbers back after him so he would know he had them right, which was how they worked now.

"Air," he said. "We have a third gone and seven marks bought since. From today I do not dive the bottom till the burn takes a glove. Bevin takes bottom inside the line under Nessa. I tend. That is not for a week. That is till spring, unless the sitting lifts it."

Bevin stared. "You tend all winter?"

"I tend and I mend and I keep the books fair." Calder put his hand flat on the book. "I wanted the shoal this month. I wanted a steel on that compartment before the Navy's floats grow over. I am giving that up, because the yard eats before the bearing does. If a tide comes that would let a man measure, I will not take it on this paper, and Nessa holds the shutdown on any bell work and Bevin holds the log on any voice I claim to hear."

Nessa put her hand beside his. "Say the money."

"The *Merrit* eleven is gone to chandler and air. The buoy eighteen is gone to bond tin and print and food. The iron stays on the stack till it fetches price, and a third goes to Sella when it does. We work inside the line for day rate and pump hire, and every mark over food goes to the forty owed to Mara and the gear to get the pump back." He looked at Mara. "You are owed first after food. I am writing that."

Mara shook her head. "The office queries my expense. Do not put me first to answer them."

"I put you first because the yard owes it, not to answer them." He wrote it: *Venn forty first after food.* "If the office asks, show them this book. We owe what we owe."

Tobias picked up the whistle and put it down.

"And the cradle?" he said.

"Beach on the hard through winter. Take the wear. Planks in spring if we earn them." Calder signed the page with his left hand and pushed the pencil to Nessa, who countersigned, and Bevin after her.

Mara stepped forward once, took the iron's entry line, checked the saw kerf note against the stack, and signed as witness to method. Then she went to the door.

Calder stopped her with a question he had carried since the buoy.

"Would you have certified the sheet if I had taken your name off the print?"

"No," she said. "That is why you can ask me again."

She went. The yard was quiet. Out in the road the *Vigil* lay at the pile, and her tender ran floats on the pale bearing a mile and a half out, where no Free Keel boat could go.

Calder picked up the pencil again and added four words under the day's entry, as he had on the 14th, in front of his crew, with a bill attached and no threat received:

*Note entered by Marr. Stated to the crew.*
