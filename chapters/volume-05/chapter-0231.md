# Chapter 231

*A Night at the Inside of a Bar, Said at the Head of This Dock*

Thursday the fourth and Friday the fifth of February, at the head of this dock and in the shed.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged on the said Thursday at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the first of the six rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and the reason against the line is not a new one and is not given again.**

**And the far bench stood empty through the whole of the said Thursday and through the whole of the said Friday, and neither brother went over to it, and this party entered the two days and put nothing of what came before them against either line.**

**And the bench under that pump was bare for a fifth night running, which is the fifth night it has been bare since a sheet and a half came apart in the ship's hand on that mole on the Sunday of the thirty-first of January, and that bench is the only bare thing in that shed and its shape is the shape of that sheet.**

---

**And at about a quarter past five on the Thursday afternoon, with the market gone in and about nine people in the street, a man of about forty of the third lay said a thing at the head of this dock that this yard did not put there.**

He had a boat of his own of about thirty tons and he had been in it on the Saturday, and he said so, and he said where he had been, and he said the place two days down this coast by name, and then he said the thing.

"**On the Saturday morning a man in a house on the far side of that place asked me what a yard in Morrow Quay had said in a shed about a light on a point.**

"**And I told him. I told him because a man of the second lay told it to me at the head of this dock on the Monday the twenty-sixth of January, in about nine people's hearing, standing about where you are standing, and I did not know when he told me that it had come out of a shed. I did not know it had been read out of a book at the top of a bench. I thought he was talking about something he had seen.**

"**I have not said it wrong. I have said it the way it was said to me. And the man asked me who I was and what my trade was and whether I was of that yard, and I said I was not, and he wrote nothing down, and he said *then I have it from a boat's locker.* That is what he said.**"

About nine people stood in that street and nobody said anything, and the market boards were down behind them and the boards are the boards of traders and this yard is not a name on any board in that market.

"**Say the line back to me,**" said Calder Marr.

"**The ninth, and a hard night, and the light on the point was burning, and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light.**"

"**That is right,**" said the holder. "**That is what is on it. And I am not going to say one word more about it in this street, and here is the reason and I am giving it out loud because you have been decent and because there are nine people standing here who did not ask you for any of it.

"**I am not going to correct it because a correction is a reason, and a reason does not go into a street. That is the reason of the seventeenth of December and it is the whole of the reason and it has not got better in eleven weeks.

"**And I am not going to say what two people said in a shed to a man of the third lay on a Monday afternoon, because a man of the third lay was not in that shed and was not asked and has not been asked since, and the moment I put it into this dock I am putting it into a man's mouth in a place where he cannot check it. You are not that man and I am not going to make you one.

"**And the last of it, and this is the one I want written down: that man of the second lay told you a thing this yard had read, and he did it in about nine people's hearing, at the head of this dock, and he had been asked to bring a pump up a hill. And this yard did not tell him not to. This yard did not know he had it. And a man of about forty has now carried it two days down this coast and put it in a front room, and that is the cost and it has arrived from a direction this yard did not choose and did not know about, and there is no figure for it and I am not going to put one there.**"

"**Then what I will say is that I do not know how it got to me and I do not know what else has gone where,**" said the man of the third lay, "**and I am not sorry I said it, because if I had not said it in this street you would have had it in a worse place by the end of the week, and I would rather it was said by a man and be wrong than be said by nobody.**"

He went down the mole. Nobody stopped him.

---

**And on the Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump up that hill at about twenty to eight and brought the man with it and did not stay and did not say anything, which is not the same as not having heard what was said in that street yesterday and this party has not entered it as the same and is not going to.**

The board for the day is twelve gross. The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

A hand lead's line went over the side and back four times and the boards were written in the ship's hand at about half past one and not afterwards.

---

**And at about half past eight on the Friday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed.**

She read it. He read it after her. And then she put her hand flat on the bench, which she does, and said the thing she had been carrying up that hill since half past four the day before.

"**Then say the whole of what happened in that street, because you said eight words of it and there are four more in you.

"**A page came up this hill out of a boat's locker on Monday the eighteenth of January and it went back down it on Tuesday the twenty-sixth in about forty minutes, and this yard read it on the Monday afternoon at the top of that bench with four pages of other people's, and two people said one line of it out loud, out loud, in a shed, in about two hours. And a man of the second lay was not in that shed and has never been in that shed. And on the Wednesday of last week he told a man of about forty in about nine people's hearing, at the head of this dock, standing where a yard stands when it says something it means.

"**And the thing that is on this page now is not that he said it. The thing that is on this page is that there was a shed on this mole where two people said a line out loud, and that every word they said went out of the door into a street in the time it takes to walk from a bench to a door, and that neither of them thought about the door, because there is no door on a page.

"**That is the first thing in five volumes that has got out of this yard that we did not put out of it on purpose. And I am not going to say it is the sea or the glass or an office. It is a man of the second lay who was not asked and was not thanked and has a pump to bring up a hill and nothing else to give anybody, and he gave it away for nothing on a Monday because a man of the first lay asked him a question about it in the first place and it was in his head and it came out.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**

"**The rest of it is that we are not going to stop him and we are not going to ask him, and the reason is the fourth of October and it is not going to be given here. And the reason against the line is that a man of the second lay has a pump and a man and no wages worth the name, and the day he stops bringing that up this hill is the day this yard is one and a half instead of one, and a party of one and a half has already lost a hand since the twentieth of this month.**"

"**I have not lost a hand.**

"**You have lost four days' sleep and I have watched you not write anything down on the Wednesday and I have watched you not write anything down on the Thursday, and the fourth of October is a day you do not put in a column and I have not asked you for it and I am not going to now. I am not going to argue with you about the fourth of October. I am going to tell you what I want done and I want it done before this month is out and I want it done without one penny and without one letter and without one person sent anywhere.**"

"**Then say what it is.**

"**I want a man to be able to stand at the head of this dock on any day of a week and say that this yard does not put words into men's mouths, and for that to be true there has to be a day and an hour and a name on it, and the only place in this harbor where a day and an hour and a name go on a surface that eleven people can read is a slate over a tide-gang store, and that slate comes down on Monday morning at eight whether there is a sitting in that room or not.

"**I am not going to ask you for a room. I am going to tell you what a slate is and you can tell me whether I am right about it, and if I am not right you can say so at half past nine at night and I will take it.**"

---

*First column, entered on Friday the fifth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the first of the six rota days in the month of February that this party has entered walked on none of.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Thursday nor at any hour on the said Friday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either of the two days, and that no figure of what came before either day is put against either line.*

*And that the bench under that pump was bare for a fifth night on the said Thursday, and that it is the only bare thing in that shed, and that its shape is the shape of a sheet and a half that is not on it.*

*And that at about a quarter past five on the said Thursday afternoon, with the market gone in and about nine people in that street, a man of about forty of the third lay said at the head of that dock that on the Saturday past a man in a house on the far side of a place two days down this coast had asked him what a yard in Morrow Quay had said in a shed about a light on a point; and that the said man said that he had been told it by a man of the second lay at the head of that dock on the twenty-sixth day of January past in about nine people's hearing, and that he did not know at the time that it had come out of a shed; and that the said man said the line back when the holder asked him to and the line is the ninth, and a hard night, and the light on the point was burning, and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light.*

*And that the said holder said that he was not going to correct it and gave the reason out loud, that a correction is a reason and a reason does not go into a street, and that that is the reason of the seventeenth day of December past and has not got better; and that he said he was not going to say what two people said in a shed to a man of the third lay on a Monday afternoon, and that a man of the third lay was not in that shed and was not asked and was not going to be given that in a street where he could not check it; and that he said the cost of it is that a man of about forty has carried it two days down this coast and put it in a front room, and that that cost has arrived from a direction this party did not choose and did not know about, and that there is no figure for it and none is going on that page.*

*And that the said man of the third lay said that he does not know how it got to him and does not know what else has gone where, and that he is not sorry he said it, and that he went down that mole and nobody stopped him.*

*And that on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump up that hill at about twenty to eight and brought the man with it and did not stay; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the said woman's hand on those boards at about half past one and not afterwards.*

*And that at about half past eight on the said Friday night, with the lamp lit and the two of them and nobody else in that shed, the day's entries were read back from the top of that bench by the said woman and read back after her by the said holder.*

*And that the said woman said that a line of a page had gone out of that shed in the time it takes to walk from a bench to a door, and that neither of them had thought about the door because there is no door on a page; and that she said that that is the first thing in five volumes that has got out of that yard that that yard did not put out of it on purpose, and that the person it got out through is a man of the second lay who was not asked and was not thanked and who has a pump to bring up a hill and nothing else to give anybody.*

*And that the said woman said that this party is not going to stop that man and is not going to ask him anything, and gave the reason of the fourth day of October past and did not give it, and that the reason against that line is that a man of the second lay has a pump and a man and no wages worth the name and the day he stops bringing that up that hill is the day this yard is one and a half instead of one.*

*And that the said woman said that she wants it to be true that a man can stand at the head of that dock on any day of a week and say that this yard does not put words into men's mouths, and that for that to be true there has to be a day and an hour and a name on it, and that the only place in this harbor where a day and an hour and a name go on a surface eleven people can read is a slate over a tide-gang store, and that the said slate comes down on a Monday morning at eight whether there is a sitting in that room or not; and that she did not ask for a room and did not ask for anything and said that the holder could tell her at half past nine at night whether she was right about the slate.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the fifth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a line of a page was said out loud in that shed on Monday the twenty-sixth day of January past and went out of the door of that shed into a street inside an hour without anybody putting it there; and that the reason against that line is that a page has no door in it and this yard has spent five volumes treating a page as a thing a man can check and has not once counted the door; and that this party is entered against the fact that what left that shed on the twenty-sixth of January was not the whole of any of the four and was one line of one of them, and that the person who carried it is a man of the third lay of this town who has not been asked anything and was not in that shed.*

*And that the night of the said Thursday was lost to the body and is entered as a day this yard did not work, in this column, with the reason against the line and no name in it, and the four above the named line of the twentieth of July past are still four, are still nameless, and are not struck out, and a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing on the said Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and thirty-four marks, six shillings and three pence is in the bag, which is five thousand five hundred and fifteen pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is four thousand eight hundred and seventy-five pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and fifteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, and the second of February instant, and all fifteen are in this book in the same breath and not one after the other. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks under that figure stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and none of the three has moved by anything in this book, and this yard did not say that note out loud in the eight weeks ended on the third of February and is not going to. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past, and he is not in this book and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these twenty days and no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is on any page of this book and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office and not one mark paid and not one shilling offered, and she told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked her once. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June past stand, the head of the third column keeps that boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August past and nobody has been appointed to it and nobody is going to be appointed to it. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard has been inside its first clause on either of the two days, and no return of any kind was made out of either of them. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, owned by four people alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves and signed is in this yard's own first column and stands, and the holder did not ask to have it lifted once in these two days. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered on either of the two days and nothing was measured, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and thirty-nine days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved, and the party did not go near them on either day. The four returns are four and there is no fifth. Nothing went up on any wall in this harbor in the twenty days ended on the third of February and nothing came off, and nothing went up on either of these two days either. A working harbor of this coast is poorer on the fifth of February next than it was on the third of it, and the cistern was charged at no figure on a rota day and this party walked none of it, and nothing this yard has done in three days has made one thing better.**
