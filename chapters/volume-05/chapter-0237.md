# Chapter 237

*Two Shillings and Tenpence in Another Man's Book*

Tuesday the sixteenth and Wednesday the seventeenth of February, in the shed.

**No rota day falls on the said Tuesday and none on the said Wednesday, and there is no charge against the cistern behind the engine house on either of them, and this party entered both of those as days and entered no figure and no reason against either, because a day with nothing charged on it is not a loss and is not going to be made into one.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten until about one and was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour, and Calder Marr did not go over to him on the Tuesday and did not go over to him on the Wednesday, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past and it is not going to be given out of that shed.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and none on the said Wednesday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against the Tuesday is that it is a wage Tuesday and this yard did not work it and the reason against that half of the line is the reason already standing against the wage in that column and is not given a second time here, and the reason against the Wednesday is that the two of them were in that shed from about ten in the morning with a man's own book open on the boards and a figure out of it that was not that yard's, and a day this yard spends on another man's book is a day this yard spends and is not a day it works and is not a day it lost.**

---

**Sim Colley came up that mole on the Tuesday morning at about ten with his own book under his arm, and he had not been asked, and this party did not send for him and had not written to him, and the reason is that a man who keeps a book for twenty-two years is a man who can be asked by anybody who walks into a room with two presses in it, and that this yard had been in that room once and had asked one question and was not going back to ask a second one for the same figure.**

He put the book on the boards at the top of the bench and opened it at a page with a pencil mark in it against a line and turned it round so that the two of them could read it upside down from where they stood.

"**The ninth of August. Two sheets at a shilling and eightpence and no name.**"

"**We have that one and we have it in our own book,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I want to say before you say anything that it is entered there in my own left hand and a man in this town can come up this hill and read it, and that is the whole of the arrangement and there is nothing else about it.**

"**Now turn the page.**"

He turned the page.

"**The eleventh of January. One sheet. Two shillings and tenpence for the day. Twenty-two hours in two days and it is entered as one day because it was one day.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**

"**The rest of it is that a man came into my room on the Tuesday of the fifth of January and paid me in advance, which I do not do, and asked for it in a clean hand, which is worse, and gave me the name of a town and not the name of a man, and said *I want it in a day because I have to be at a funeral on the Thursday and I want to read it out at it.* And I said a funeral is a bad place to read a thing with thirty-eight lines on it, and he said *it is the only place I have got where everybody is listening*, and he was not joking, and I believed him, and I set it.

"**And it took me from about ten in the morning on the Tuesday until about seven in the evening on the Wednesday, because the thirty-eighth line is the one that has to be right, and I have had it in a forme twice and I got a smudge in the middle of the seventh line in the first one and had it up again, and a man who works in type does not get a day back for a smudge, he gets the same day twice.

"**And the day was a Monday. The ninth of the month. He wanted it by the Tuesday, so it had to be the Monday, and the Monday was my own day, and the Tuesday was my own day, and I was two days at that press for one sheet and I put one day against it in that book because that is the shape of my own trade and a day is a day whatever hours it takes, and I entered it at two shillings and tenpence because that is what I would have earned at the counter on a Monday in the ordinary way and not one penny more and not one penny less, and I did not charge him for the Tuesday and he did not know about the Tuesday.

"**And he is not in this book. I did not ask him his name and he did not give me one and I did not want one, and I have had that line in that book for five weeks and I have not known what to do with it, and I have been waiting since the twelfth of February for somebody to come and stand where you are standing.**"

Nobody said anything for about half a minute.

"**That is a day of your trade and it is two shillings and tenpence,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and it goes in our first column at about ten o'clock this morning, in your own figure and in your own book and not out of our money, and I am going to write it out and I am going to read it back to you and you are going to tell me if I have got it wrong.

"**And the reason it goes in the first column and not the second is that a man in this town can walk down that lane and into that room and ask you what you did on the ninth of the month and be told, and that is the whole test and it passes. It is the only thing in that book that is in the first column about a person who is not of this party, and it is there because it is checkable, and not because it is ours.

"**And the reason against the line, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it again this month: a day that this yard's money did not lose is not a figure that this yard's money lost, and the two of those are not going to be added into anything, and if anybody in four years wants to know what our page cost that day there is nothing on our page about the price, and there is a figure in a compositor's book in a lane off that mole and that is where the price is, and the price is not in our custody and is not going to be.

"**And I am not going to be the one who says that to him, and I am not going to say it to him at all, and that is what I am doing now by not saying it.**"

"**Then say it,**" said Sim Colley. "**I have come up a hill with a book under my arm to find out whether a page is worth what the man who prints it is worth, and you have told me it is not, and I have been sitting in that room for nine days not knowing what to do with a line in a book, and if you do not say it to me I will go on not knowing.**"

"**No.**

"**I am not going to say sorry to you in a shed and I am not going to say it in a market either, and here is why and it is the same reason as everything else this month. If I say sorry to you on this mole, then in about four years there is a man standing on a mole in another town who says that the yard of the writ on the west basin said sorry to a compositor for a day, and that is the whole of what that yard has left to say for itself, and it will be true, and it will be about a day that you lost, and not one word of it will be about the thirty-eighth line, which is the only thing you actually did.

"**And a compositor does not want to be the reason a yard is remembered. You would be the easiest man on this coast to make a story out of and you have been in my road for twenty-two years and you have never once asked me for a name and I have never once asked you for one, and I am not going to have that be the thing that happened between us.

"**So there is no apology. There is a figure in your book and the same figure in mine and a man in this town can go and ask you about it. That is the whole of what a yard can do for a day it did not pay for and cannot pay for, and it is not enough, and I am not going to tell you it is enough because you came up a hill for it.**"

"**It is not nothing,**" said Sim Colley. "**Twenty-two years I have kept that book and there has been one line in it with no name and now there are two, and one of them has got a yard's name against it in a shed, and I did not come up here to be paid. I came up here to find out whether anybody in this country writes down what a thing cost a man.

"**Nobody does. I have been the man who writes it down since I was nineteen and nobody has ever written one of mine down before and I am not asking you to keep it. I am asking you to leave it where it is and let the book stand open.**"

---

**And at about half past eight on the Wednesday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the night's entry read from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed, and the whole day's entry being four lines because there was one thing in it.**

"**Both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other,**" said Nessa Pike. "**A day of a man's trade is gone out of a lane off this mole on the ninth of January, and it is on his page and it is on ours, and the sheet he set that day is in a place two days down this coast and a man read it out at a funeral on the Thursday, and that is also true, and the two of them are sitting side by side on that board and the money did not buy the funeral and the funeral did not pay for the day.

"**And I am not going to be able to say which one of those is worse and I have stopped trying to be able to say it. What I can say is that we printed a line on the sixth of August that says where every copy of that sheet is, and that line is the reason a man had to pay two shillings and tenpence for a day, and that we printed it because it is the right thing to do, and we would print it again, and that is the whole of what happened and I do not want to hear either half of it on its own again.**"

"**Then the last entry of that day and I am going to say it and I am not going to be asked about it.**

"**The night of the said Wednesday is lost. It goes in the second column with a reason against the line and there is no name in it and there is not going to be one, and the reason against the line is the reason of the fourth of October past and it is not improved on and it is not going to be improved on in four years, and I am telling you it happened the night of the seventeenth and not the night of the sixteenth because it happened after I was asleep on the seventeenth and I know that much and no more, and that is the whole of the information this yard has got about it and it is going in the book in that state.

"**And the day after it is a Thursday and we are working the mole, and I am telling you that tonight, at half past nine, on the Wednesday, and not on the Thursday morning, because a day a yard knows it is going to lose is entered the night before and a day it lost is entered the morning after, and those are two lines and only one of them is in the first column.**"

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the seventeenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no rota day falls on the said Tuesday and none on the said Wednesday, and that there is no charge against the cistern behind the engine house on either of them and no figure and no reason against either of them.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten until about one and was not occupied at any hour on the said Wednesday, and that the holder did not go over to him on the said Tuesday and did not go over to him on the said Wednesday.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and none on the said Wednesday, and that the reason against the said Tuesday is that it was a wage Tuesday and this yard did not work it, and that the reason against the said Wednesday is that this party was in that shed from about ten in the morning with a compositor's own book open on the boards and a figure out of it that was not that yard's.*

*And that a compositor of forty-one came up that mole on the said Tuesday morning at about ten with his own book under his arm, and that this party did not send for him and did not write to him, and that the reason against that line is that a man who keeps a book for twenty-two years can be asked by anybody who walks into a room with two presses in it, and that this party had been in that room once and asked one question and was not going back for a second figure.*

*And that the said compositor opened that book at a page with a pencil mark against one line and that the said line is the ninth of August past, two sheets at a shilling and eightpence with no name on it; and that the said holder said that that line is in that yard's own book in his own left hand and that a man in that town can come up that hill and read it, and that that is the whole of the arrangement.*

*And that the said compositor then turned the page, and that the said line is the eleventh of January instant, one sheet, two shillings and tenpence for the day, twenty-two hours in two days entered as one day; and that a man came into that room on the Tuesday of the fifth of January instant, paid in advance, gave the name of a town and not the name of a man, and said that he wanted the said sheet in a day because he had to be at a funeral on the Thursday and wanted to read it out at it; and that the said compositor said that a funeral is a bad place to read a thing with thirty-eight lines on it and that the said man said that it is the only place he has got where everybody is listening, and that the said compositor believed him and set it.*

*And that the said compositor said that the said sheet took him from about ten in the morning on the said Tuesday until about seven in the evening on the said Wednesday, being that the thirty-eighth line is the one that has to be right, and that he had it in a forme twice and had a smudge in the middle of the seventh line in the first one and had it up again; and that the said day was a Monday, being the ninth of January instant, and that he was two days at that press for one sheet and entered one day against it at two shillings and tenpence, being what he would have earned at that counter on a Monday in the ordinary way and not one penny more and not one penny less, and that he did not charge the said man for the Tuesday and the said man did not know about the Tuesday; and that the said man is not in that book and was not asked for his name and gave none.*

*And that the said woman entered that figure in that yard's first column on the said Tuesday morning in the said man's own figure and out of the said man's own book and not out of that yard's money, and that she read it back to him, and that the reason it is in the first column and not the second is that a man in that town can go into that room and ask what was done on the ninth of the month and be told, and that it is the only thing in that book in the first column about a person who is not of that party.*

*And that the reason against that line is that a day that this yard's money did not lose is not a figure that this yard's money lost, and that the two are not going to be added into anything, and that if anybody in four years wants to know what that page cost that day there is nothing on that page about the price and there is a figure in a compositor's book in a lane off that mole, and that the price is not in that yard's custody and is not going to be.*

*And that the said holder said that he was not going to say sorry to the said compositor in that shed and was not going to say it in a market, and gave the reason, being that if he says sorry to that man on that mole then in about four years there is a man standing on a mole in another town who says that the yard of the writ on the west basin said sorry to a compositor for a day, and that it will be true and it will be about a day that the said man lost and not one word of it will be about the thirty-eighth line, which is the only thing he actually did; and that he said that a compositor does not want to be the reason a yard is remembered.*

*And that the said compositor said that it is not nothing, and that in twenty-two years there has been one line in that book with no name on it and there are now two, and that one of them has a yard's name against it in a shed; and that he said that he did not come up that hill to be paid and that he came up it to find out whether anybody in that country writes down what a thing cost a man, and that nobody does, and that he is not asking that party to keep his book and is asking that party to leave it where it is and let it stand open.*

*And that a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on the said Tuesday and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is seventeen, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant, the nineteenth of January instant, the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second of February instant, the ninth of February instant and the said sixteenth of February instant.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the seventeenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the night of the said Wednesday is lost, and that it is entered as a day this yard did not work with a reason against the line and no name in it, and that the reason against the line is the reason of the fourth of October past and has not been improved on and is not going to be improved on; and that it is entered as the night of the seventeenth and not of the sixteenth because it happened after the said holder was asleep on the seventeenth and that is the whole of the information this yard has about it.*

*And that the said woman said that a day of a man's trade is gone out of a lane off that mole on the ninth of January instant and is on his page and on that one, and that the sheet he set that day is in a place two days down that coast and was read out at a funeral on the Thursday, and that the money did not buy the funeral and the funeral did not pay for the day, and that she said that both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, and that she said that no character in that shed and no character in that book has ever been asked to say that sentence in that many places and that she is not going to remark on it and is going to bed.*

**No money came into this yard on the said Tuesday and none on the said Wednesday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and forty marks, six shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is six thousand four hundred and seventy-four pence, which is what it was on the Monday, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five thousand eight hundred and thirty-four pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and seventeen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. One figure of two shillings and tenpence entered on the said Tuesday morning out of another man's book and not out of that yard's money, and that is the only figure that yard has entered in fourteen days that did not come in or go out of it, and it is not in the bag and is not going to be and the reason is on the page above. The cistern was not charged on either of the two days and the walk is nil and stays nil. The far bench was occupied on the Tuesday and not on the Wednesday, and the holder went over on neither. The bench under that pump was bare for a seventeenth night on the Tuesday and an eighteenth on the Wednesday, and the night of the said Wednesday was lost and is entered in the second column with a reason against the line and no name in it. Four returns are four and there is no fifth. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The third column is unfilled and unappointed and the boy's hand is at the head of it. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and he is not in this book. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, unargued, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid and unoffered. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June past standing. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it has not been said out loud since the third of December past. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. A working harbor of this coast is poorer on the seventeenth of February next than it was on the fourth of it, and a compositor of forty-one is two days out of a week on a sheet this yard printed and cannot be paid and has not been asked for anything, and nothing this yard has done in fourteen days has made one thing better. Every other figure this harbor is owed is set out in the page above and is not read out again in this paragraph.**
