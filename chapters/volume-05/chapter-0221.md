# Chapter 221

*Four Ways a Page Comes Up a Hill, and a Line With No Name In It*

Friday the fifteenth and Saturday the sixteenth of January, in the shed, and at the boards.

**The book was open on the bench between them from about eight in the morning and it is this yard's own book and it is the only book in this shed with a second column in it, and they were not doing anything with it that anybody in this harbor would call work, and the day is entered as a day on which a line of that book was read out in that shed and not a day on which anything was found.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, and not on the said Saturday at any hour either, and this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.**

---

**And from about eight until about half past ten on the Friday morning the ship's hand went back through the second column from the top, in her own order and not in the order the lines were written, and the holder read them after her and did not interrupt her, and at about half past ten she put two fingers on a line near the back of a page and said: this is the one. And the other one is a page earlier, and the reason both of them are in that column and neither is in the first is on the line under them and is not going to be given out of this shed.**

The first of the two lines said that a line of places kept by more than one community, for longer than any book in that office goes back, could not have been taken out of those communities' hands without somebody having had to decide to do it. The second of the two said that a record of a chain of vessels is kept by nine communities with a date against each and a place against each, and that the said record was said out loud at a weir in this harbor in a month this party is not going to name in a book.

"**That is the whole of what this book has ever said about nine. Two lines, and neither of them has a name in it, and I have not had that second one in front of me since the second of December.**

"**What are the nine.**

"**I do not know, and I have got nothing behind it. It says nine communities and a date against each and a place against each. It does not say the places and it does not say the dates. I wrote down what a man said to me at a weir, which is a habit, and I have been telling myself it is a practice since the third of May.**

"**I have got the number nine in four different places in this book and not one of the four is a thing we could walk to.**"

---

**And about two in the afternoon they were at the boards, and a man of sixty-four of the first lay was standing there with a book under his arm, in this harbor since the Thursday before last on a job, and going back on the Monday.**

He was from a place four days up this coast. He was a joiner. He had a book of his own, in his own hand, that he began when he was seventeen years old, and he had never shown it to anybody, and he showed it to them, standing up, in about ten minutes, and the reason he gave was that he had heard in this market that there was a line in a shed on this mole about nine communities and he wanted to know whether there was anything in it for him.

"**There is not going to be anything in it for you,**" said Calder Marr, "**because it has not got a name in it, and a name is what a man finds things by.**

"**I did not say that was what I was looking for,**" said the man of sixty-four.

He turned the book round on the boards with his own hands. It is not a copy of anything. No office has ever had it and no clerk has ever seen it, and it has been in a chest in a house four days up this coast since before the man carrying it was born.

"**That is a page of ours. I am showing you one leaf of it and not the book, because you asked me for it in a way that is not the way people usually ask. If you had asked for the book I would have said no and gone home on the Thursday instead of the Monday.**

"**I am not lending it. I will bring it up that hill on the Monday and take it down on the Tuesday, and that is all I am doing and I would like it written down that that is all I am doing.**

"**And I am going to say the one line, and then shut it.**"

Then he shut it and said that the stair he had come down to set was a day of work and that he was going to go and set it. He asked this yard for nothing and this yard offered him nothing, and the cost of that day is entered on a later day and not against this one, and the reason against that is that a man is not a cost on the day he comes up a hill.

---

**And about half past nine on the Saturday morning a woman of seventy-one came up that mole on foot and stood at the door of that shed for about a minute before she said anything at all, and her house is on the north side of this town, and nobody in this harbor had sent for her and nobody in this harbor knew that she had a page.**

She did not give her name and this yard did not ask her for it, and she is in this book as a woman of seventy-one of this harbor and that is the whole of how she is in this book.

"**I have had a page in a house for forty years and I have never been asked for it. I am not answering a knock. I came up this hill because there is a man in this town putting the word nine into a conversation about a page, and he has been at it in the market since the Saturday, and I do not know who he is and it is not the man in the doorway.**

"**Here it is. I have not had it out of that house since the year my husband died, and it is not a copy and it is not a document and nobody has ever sent it anywhere. It is the page our place keeps, and I keep it because I was left it, and the two people before me kept it, and nobody ever came and said the other thing.**

"**I am not going to leave it here. It goes on this bench until the Tuesday and home on the Tuesday, and I would like it said that I did not have to be asked, and that it is not a favor. It is that nobody has ever asked.**

"**And one of you is going to ask me a question about a night and I am going to answer it. I knew at about half past seven this morning that I was going to answer it, and I did not let myself stop walking.**"

---

**And about eleven on the Saturday morning a man of about thirty-four came up the top of that mole with a barrow of lime and chalk behind him and asked whether anybody here wanted lime, and he has sold it out of a yard at the head of this dock since he was twenty-two, and there is no office in that trade and there never has been.**

He put the barrow down and wiped his hands on a rag.

"**I am not here about the lime. There was a sheet in a crate of chalk that came in on the Tuesday boat out of a place two days down this coast, and I have had it in the seat of my barrow since Wednesday, and it has a name on it at the head of it, and I have carried it up this mole on the off chance of somebody in this town knowing what to do with it.**

"**I am not giving it to you. I am leaving it on that bench, and if it is a nuisance you can put it in that drawer and I will come back for it on the Saturday. I am telling you where it came from because if it does not come back to me it is going to be asked about at four houses this week, and the first of those four is a man who bought chalk off me in the month of November and did not pay for the last load.**

"**I have carried it up a hill on nothing. I am sixty pounds short this month already and I have not put that on anybody and I am not going to.**"

And he put a folded sheet on the bench under the pump, next to the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it, and went back down the mole with his barrow.

---

**And about half past eleven on that Saturday, at the head of that dock, a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own said nine words to the shipwright and not to the holder, on his way to get the pump.**

"**There is a thing in my locker. It has been there eleven days. I do not know what it is.**"

"**Then do not tell me on a dock.**"

"**I have told you on a dock. It is a page. It is not mine and it is not the last crew's, and it is under a coil of warp in my locker, and I would like to know what a page is doing in my locker before the varnisher sees it.**

"**You can have it. I am not keeping a page in a locker. Bring it up on the Monday with the pump and I will take it down again when you are done with it, and I do not want to be asked what is in it, because I have not read it and I am not going to.**"

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the sixteenth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it; and that no day of work was booked on the said Friday and none on the said Saturday and the board was not written on either of them, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happened still happened.*

*And that from about eight until about half past ten on the said Friday morning the said woman went back through the second column of this yard's own book from the top, in her own order and not in the order the lines were written, and that the said holder read them after her and did not interrupt her, and that at about half past ten she put two fingers on a line near the back of a page and said that this was the one; and that the said line is that a line of places kept by more than one community, for longer than any book in that office goes back, cannot have been taken out of those communities' hands without somebody having had to decide to do it.*

*And that the said woman put two fingers on a second line, being a page earlier, and that the said line is that a record of a chain of vessels is kept by nine communities with a date against each and a place against each, and that the said record was said out loud at a weir in this harbor in a month this party is not going to name in a book; and that neither of the said lines has a name in it, and that both of them are in the second column and not the first, and that the reason against that is that this party has been to none of the nine and has written to none of them and has asked none of them anything.*

*And that at about two in the afternoon on the said Friday this party was at the boards, and that a man of sixty-four of the first lay, from a place four days up this coast and a joiner, who had been in this harbor since the Thursday before last on a job, was standing at the boards with a book under his arm; and that he opened a book that is his own and in his own hand and that he began when he was seventeen years old, and that it is not a copy of anything and no office has ever had it and no clerk has ever seen it and it has been in a chest in a house four days up this coast since before he was born; and that the said man said that a page of that book is a page of his place, and that he was showing one leaf of it and not the book, and that if he had been asked for the book he would have said no and gone home on the Thursday instead of the Monday; and that he said that he would bring that leaf up that hill on the Monday morning and would take it down again on the Tuesday, and that the said holder entered that, and that the reason against the line is that a man has put a bound condition on a thing and that a condition given for nothing in an open place is checkable by anybody who was standing there.*

*And that the said man read out one line of that leaf, being that a man of his place went out on a Tuesday in a bad month and did not come back, and that the said line is in a hand that is not his and is not his father's, and that the year against it is a year nobody in his house can name; and that he shut the book and put it under his arm and said that the stair he had come down to set was a day of work and that he was going to go and set it; and that he asked this yard for nothing and this yard offered him nothing, and that the cost of that day is entered on a later day and not against that day, and that the reason against that is that a man is not a cost on the day he comes up a hill.*

*And that at about half past nine on the said Saturday morning a woman of seventy-one of this harbor, whose house is on the north side of this town, came up that mole on foot and stood at the door of that shed for about a minute before she said anything; and that nobody in this harbor sent for her and nobody in this harbor knew that she had a page; and that she did not give her name and the said holder did not ask her for it.*

*And that the said woman said that she has had a page in a house for forty years and has never been asked for it, and that she came up that hill because there is a man in this town putting the word nine into a conversation about a page and that she does not know who he is; and that she said that the said page is not a copy and is not a document and has never been sent anywhere and is the page her place keeps, and that she was left it and that the two people before her kept it and that nobody ever came and said the other thing; and that she said that she is not going to leave it here and is going to leave it on that bench until the Tuesday and take it home on the Tuesday, and that she would like it said that she did not have to be asked and that it is not a favor and that it is that nobody has ever asked. And that the said woman said at about half past nine that one of the two of them was going to ask her a question about a night and that she was going to answer it, and that she had known since about half past seven that morning that she was going to answer it and had not let herself stop walking; and that the said holder did not ask her that question in that doorway and entered that he did not, and that the reason against the line is that a question asked on a doorstep is asked in a street.*


*And that at about eleven on the said Saturday morning a man of about thirty-four, who sells lime and chalk out of a yard at the head of this dock and has done since he was twenty-two, came up that mole with a barrow and asked whether anybody here wanted lime, and that there is no office in that trade; and that the said man said that he was not there about the lime, and that there was a sheet in a crate of chalk that came in on the Tuesday boat out of a place two days down this coast, and that he has had it in the seat of his barrow since Wednesday, and that it has a name on it at the head of it, and that he carried it up that mole on the off chance of somebody in this town knowing what to do with it. And that the said man said that he was not giving the sheet to this party and was leaving it on that bench and would come back for it on the Saturday, and that he was telling this party where it came from because if it does not come back to him it is going to be asked about at four houses this week, and that the first of those four is a man who bought chalk off him in the month of November past and did not pay for the last load; and that the said man said that he has carried it up a hill on nothing and that he is sixty pounds short that month already and has not put that on anybody and is not going to.* And that the said man put a folded sheet on the bench under the pump, next to the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it, and went back down that mole with his barrow and did not stay for the market.*

*And that at about half past eleven on the said Saturday morning at the head of that dock a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own said nine words to the said woman and not to the said holder, on his way to get the pump, being that there was a thing in his locker, and that it had been there eleven days, and that he did not know what it was; and that the said woman told him not to say it on a dock; and that he said that it was a page and that it was not his and was not the last crew's and that it was under a coil of warp in his locker, and that he would like to know what a page is doing in his locker before the varnisher saw it; and that the said man said that this party could have it and that he was not going to keep a page in a locker, and that he would bring it up on the Monday with the pump and take it down again when this party was done with it, and that he did not want to be asked what was in it because he had not read it and was not going to.*

*And that at the end of the said Saturday there were three pages on that bench that do not belong to this yard and a fourth under the pump, and that this party has not read one line of any of the four, and that the reason against that line is that a page is not read on the day it arrives, and that a man who has carried a page eleven days in a boat's locker and a woman who has kept one forty years in a house and a man who has kept a book of his own since he was seventeen do not get a page read at them in a doorway.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the sixteenth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said holder entered on the said day that this party went to four households on two days and asked to see a page, and that this party refused on the tenth of December past to become the party that holds a copy of a record for this coast, and that what was done on the said Friday and the said Saturday is the same act at a smaller size and is a reason not to do it again; and that the reason against that line is that a man who has not asked a thing is not the same as a man who has asked it, and that the four of them have now been asked, and that a man in this town can come up this hill and ask any one of the four of them whether this party came, and be told that it did.*

*And that the said woman entered that a record of a chain of vessels kept by nine communities with a date against each and a place against each has been a second-column line in this book since the sixth of October past and has not been looked at since the second of December instant, and that this party has been to none of the nine and has written to none of them and has asked none of them anything, and that whether the nine are places a man can walk to is not checkable from a mole and is not going to be asked of anybody this month; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**No money came into this yard on the said Friday and none on the said Saturday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and sixteen marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, and the whole of the money that has come into this yard since the sixth of December past is one thousand nine hundred and twenty pence and the whole of the money that has gone out of it in the same time is one penny. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks under that figure stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and this yard did not say that note out loud in the six weeks ended on the sixteenth of January. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past and he is not in this book. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days and no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is on any page of this book and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office and not one mark paid and not one shilling offered. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June past standing, the head of the third column keeping that boy's hand, and that column not filled since the first of August past and nobody appointed to it. The column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest, the envelopes on that bench are twenty, and twelve weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks on nobody's schedule, and the four people it belongs to hold a limit that stands. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and seventeen days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
