# Chapter 228

*Three Nines, and a Row Counted Once, and Nine Places in a Book*

Friday the twenty-ninth and Saturday the thirtieth of January, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**And the far bench was not occupied at any hour on either of the said two days, and this party entered that and put no figure against it of what came before it.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that a day this yard spends counting what is in its own book and standing at an empty bench is not a day this yard works, and that the man who sells lime and chalk came up that mole at about ten on the said morning and the two of them were where he could put his hand on the inside of his coat and say it.**

---

**And this yard worked on the said Friday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back five times, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards.**

The board for the day is twelve gross. The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

**And nobody argued about anything all day. That is entered because it happened and because a week is a week and this one was the first in a month in which two people in this shed said nothing to each other that they had not both already agreed on, and the reason against making anything of that is that a quiet week is a week the tide was on.**

Three marks is four hundred and eighty pence. The bag held four thousand and seventy-five pence and the sum of the two is four thousand five hundred and fifty-five pence, which is twenty-eight marks, six shillings and three pence.

---

**And at about ten on that Friday night the ship's hand put a pencil on the bench and said that she had been counting all day and had got four nines and that she was going to write all four of them out, and that the holder was not to stop her, and that he was not to help.**

She wrote them on the back of a sheet, not in the book, because they were not an entry yet and she wanted to see them all at once.

**One.** Nine places on a sheet of this yard's own that went on a wall in this harbor in the month of August past, six of them being six figures out of a builder's own day-book in a press in another town, and three of them being places this yard has never worked out why they are where they are.

**Two.** Nine fastenings in about seven feet of each of the six joints at the face of the second seam of this mole. That is fifty-four and nobody ever counted them that way until she did it on the seventeenth of July and they are fastenings and they are in a joint and they are what holds two ends of a thing together.

**Three.** A row of nine short lines joined at one point, cut in a plate.

**Four.** Nine communities with a date against each and a place against each, in a second-column line in this yard's own book, and nine records, of which four have been on that bench in the last nine days.

"**Four nines,**" said Nessa Pike. "**And they are four different things, and one of them is a number of fastenings, and one of them is a list of places this yard printed, and one of them is a mark cut by a man, and one of them is a set of records kept by people who have not met.**

"**And I have watched this yard treat them as one thing for a year because one of them is a nine and the other three are also nines, and a number is a number.**

"**That is the whole of the arithmetic error and it has taken four volumes to notice it.**"

---

**And the third of the four is the one she wanted to talk about, and she put the pencil down and did not pick it up again.**

"**It is a builder's mark. That is all it is and that is all I am going to say about it.**

"**It is nine short lines joined at one point and it is cut in a plate by a man who was building the thing the plate is on, and the thing is older than the calendar this coast keeps, which is a way of saying I do not know how old it is and I have not got a figure. It is not a claim seal. It is not anybody consenting to anything. It is not a seal, it is a mark, and a mark is a man telling the next man what he made, and the next man in this case was whoever cut the plates after him, and nobody since.**

"**And I counted a row of nine once. In a month I am not going to name in a book. It is on the plate and it is in my own hand in that book and it says *mark* and nothing else, and I looked at it then and I have not looked at it since, and I am not going to look at it again, and the reason I am not going to look at it again is not that it frightens me. It is that a man who counts a thing once has said what he saw, and a man who counts it twice has started a pattern, and I have not got a second sight and I am not going to invent one.**"

"**No office in this country can read it.**

"**No office in this country can read it, and I am not going to put that in the first column, and the reason I am not going to put it in the first column is that I have got it from two men and neither of them is an office and neither of them has ever been in an office.

"**The first is a man who builds stone in a town four days up this coast and who has cut stone for forty-one years and whose grandfather cut stone, and who showed me a mark cut into a stone in a yard he works in and said that his grandfather cut those and that his grandfather did not know what they were either.

"**The second is that same man's brother, who does not build and who told me that the men who come and look at that mark are men from a city with a book in their hand, and that they write it down and go, and that in his life he has never known one of them to say what it was for.**

"**That is what I have got. Two men who build stone. And the fact that no office can read it is the best thing I have ever heard about that mark and it is not worth anything, because the only things I have ever wanted in this book are the things that are hard to get and there are men on this coast who will tell you a thing they cannot read for nothing at the end of a day, and I have stopped being able to tell the difference between that and an office.**"

Nobody improved on that.

---

**And at about nine on the said Saturday morning she went back to the second-column line with the pencil and did the other half of the thing, which was the half the holder had told her on the sixteenth that was the whole of what this book had to say about nine.**

The line said nine communities with a date against each and a place against each. It did not say the places. But this yard's own book has a list of places in it, in a hand of the ship's, written in October and November, and the list has nine on it, and the list is a list of **places** and it does not say what is kept at any of them, and it never has.

She read the nine out loud at the top of that bench and they are all places:

**This harbor's own weir. The inside of the bar. The other side of the water. The point with the light. A place four days up this coast. A place two days down this coast. The next town down this coast. The hard at Ashtide. And Lowfen — a chain of reed islands and floating markets two days out where the tide does a thing no boat of ours likes and there are more people living on the water than there are on it.**

"**Nine places,**" said Nessa Pike. "**And there are four pages on this coast that came up a hill in nine days and not one of them came from a place that is not on that list, and three of the four came from the first four on that list in a different order, and this yard has not been to a single one of the nine and has not written to a single one of them and has not asked a single one of them anything, and that is the same as it was on the sixteenth and I have not moved.**

"**And the other five are this harbor's own weir, the point with the light, the next town down this coast, the hard at Ashtide and Lowfen. Five places. And this book has got no more to say about any of the five than it has got to say about the other four, and that is what a list of places is.**

"**And I am going to say the hard one, because we have had a hard week and this is the sentence that comes out of it. The point with the light is on that list as a place. And there is a page on this bench about a light on a point, and it is one of the four, and it came up this hill in a boat's locker. And the point with the light on that list and the light in that page have got nothing whatever to do with each other, and the yard has not been to either, and the two of them are not going to be joined in this book or in a street or in a shed, and if a man four years from now puts those two together then he will have made it, and not this yard, and this yard did not help him.**"

"**That is the whole of it and I am not going to say the other three sentences about it that I have got in me, because the other three are all ways of saying that one and I am not going to say a thing three ways.**"

---

**And at about ten on that Saturday morning a man of about thirty-four came up the top of that mole with an empty barrow and stood at the door of that shed, and the fourth of the four was on the bench under a weight where it had been for fourteen days, and he took it, and looked at it, and put it inside his coat.**

"**That is the sheet out of the crate.**"

"**That is the sheet out of the crate.**"

"**Has anybody written on it.**

"**Nobody has written on it. It came up this hill folded in a crate of chalk and it is going down this hill in a barrow and nobody has written on it and nothing has been added to it and nothing has been taken off it, and there is no copy of it in this harbor and there is no copy of it anywhere else that this yard knows of and it is not going to be got, and that is not a thing this yard has done. It is a thing about a piece of paper that was in a crate.**

"**And I am going to tell you the one thing I am not supposed to tell you and I am telling you because you did not ask me for it and brought it up a hill on nothing. A page that is in a place is read by the people of that place. That is the whole of the trade and it is not a rule. I have watched a page get read in a town it does not belong to and I did not see anybody stop it, and I was one of the ones reading it, and I did not stop it either.**"

He put his hand on the door frame for a moment.

"**It has got a name on it,**" he said, "**has it.**

"**It has.**"

"**Then I am glad I carried it up a hill and I am not going to say anything else about it, and I am going to carry it down the hill in my coat and not in the barrow, because the barrow has got chalk in it, and I have not got a reason for that that makes any difference.**"

And he went down the mole with his hand on the inside of his coat, and the bench under the pump was bare for the first time since the ninth of December, and the two of them looked at it.

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirtieth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour nor on the said Saturday at any hour, and that this party entered that and put no figure against it of what came before it.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the board was not written on it, and that the reason against that line is that a day this party spends counting what is in its own book and standing at an empty bench is not a day this party works, and that the said man came up that mole at about ten on the said morning and the two of them were where he could put his hand on the inside of his coat and say it.*

*And that this yard put in a day on the said Friday, from eight until about half past one, on the face of the second seam and landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account and with nobody in the water, and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back five times, and that the gross on the board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards.*

*And that at about ten on the said Friday night the said woman put a pencil on that bench and said that she had been counting all day and had got four nines and was going to write all four of them out, and that the said holder was not to stop her and was not to help; and that she wrote them on the back of a sheet and not in that book, because they were not an entry yet and she wanted to see them all at once.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the thirtieth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this party entered on the said day that a row of nine short lines joined at one point, cut in a plate, is a builder's mark and is not a claim seal and is not a mark of consent, and that no office in this country can read it; and that the said entry is in this column and not the first because the said holder has it from two men who build stone and neither of them is an office, and that the reason against that is that the only things this party has ever wanted in that book are the things that are hard to get, and that a man on this coast will tell another man a thing he cannot read for nothing at the end of a day; and that the said holder entered that he has stopped being able to tell the difference between that and an office, and that the reason against that is that the difference was never the thing this book was for.*

*And that the four nines are four different things and may not be converted into one another, and that the nine fastenings in about seven feet of each of the six joints are not the said mark, and that the nine places in six books are not the nine communities, and that the nine records of nine communities are not the nine places in six books, and that this party entered that it has counted a row of nine once and is not going to count it twice.*

*And that the said woman said that the point with the light on the list of nine places and the light in the second of the four pages have got nothing to do with each other, and that this party has not been to either, and that the two of them are not going to be joined in that book or in a street or in a shed, and that a man who joins them four years from now will have made it and not this party; and that the reason against that line is not checkable from a mole and is not going to be, and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the twenty-ninth of January and nothing on the thirtieth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and twenty-eight marks, six shillings and three pence is in the bag, which is four thousand five hundred and fifty-five pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three thousand nine hundred and fifteen pence. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, the fifth of March the oldest, the envelopes on that bench are twenty, and the weeks fallen due with nothing against them are fourteen. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and is not written out over here.**
