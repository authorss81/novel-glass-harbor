# Chapter 244

*The Living Archive and the Lead, and the Day He Did Not Take the Lead*

Tuesday the second and Wednesday the third of March, in the shed, at the foot of that mole, and on the road out of it.

**Neither of the two days of this chapter is a rota day and the cistern behind the engine house was charged on neither of them, and that is entered with a reason, and the reason is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and the second and third days of March are a Tuesday and a Wednesday.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder did not go over to him on the said Tuesday and did not go over to him on the said Wednesday, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past, and he was up a coast road and not in this harbor and the two of those are both entered and neither of them is a reason.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against the first of those two lines is that the holder of that writ went up that coast road at about ten in the morning on the said Tuesday on a cart belonging to a man of about forty-five who was going back to a place ten days up this coast, and the reason against the second of them is the same reason and is a different day, and there is no figure against either of them and there is not going to be one, because there is no figure for a day and because the only thing a day cost this yard this week was the day itself.**

**And the cost of that going was named in the first column of this book on the said Monday night at about eleven o'clock, before the day and not after it, and it stands where it was written and is not improved on.**

---

**And the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and the reason is the reason entered on the twenty-ninth of December past in the holder's own left hand in the first column and it is not improved on and it is not going to be given a second way tonight.**

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is NINETEEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and the second of March instant, and all nineteen are entered in that book in the same breath and not one after the other.**

---

**And at about a quarter to ten on the said Tuesday morning the cart was at the foot of that mole with a hand-brake on it and lime on the shafts, and the man of about forty-five was sitting on a tailboard with his hands on his knees, and the holder went down the steps and got on the cart.**

"**I am not going to ask you for anything on the way,**" the man said. "**There is nothing to ask for. I said I would be on this mole at ten and I am on it. That is all I said and I have said it.**

"**I know what you said. I wrote it down on Friday and I read it back to you and you wrote it in that hand.**

"**Then you have done more than I expected and I am not going to be asked about it. It is nine miles from my door to the bar and the bar is in water no lead of yours has been in. That is what I have got four people for and no boat.**"

"**Then I will sit on the tailboard and look at the bar.**

"**You will sit on the tailboard and look at the bar for ten days, and then you will come back, and if you have got nothing to say to me when you get here you will say so and I will not ask you twice.**"

The cart went at ten and he did not look back at the mole from the top of it, and the ship's hand stood at the head of the slip until it was round the corner, and then went up the hill and wrote the day's board at about half past eleven in her left hand, and it took her about twice as long as it takes her.

---

**And at about a quarter past four on the said Wednesday afternoon a man of about thirty-four who sells lime and chalk came up that hill on his own account and stood about four feet off the door in the rain with a bundle of ledger paper under his arm, and there was nobody in that shed to put a price on anything.**

"**My cart goes down that coast on the Friday and I am going to be four days on the road and four days coming back, and I am going now to find out whether that man of about fifty says it to a second man.**

"**I told you on the Saturday that I would go when I had been told what it is for, and I have not been told, and I have not been told because there is nobody here to tell me, and I am not going to stand on a mole for a day for a favor and I am not going to say the word *favor* again to anybody in this town.**

"**Is he here.**

"**He is not. He went up the coast road on Tuesday morning at ten o'clock in a cart with the man who keeps the book of ground, and he will be at the bar at about the eighth day and he will be back here about the twenty-second or the twenty-third, and I do not know the day because I did not ask him and I was not going to ask him.**

"**Then I am going down on the Friday, and if he comes back before the twenty-second and somebody here has a reason, I will come up the road and tell him what the man said. And if nobody has a reason, I will not, and I will not go up that coast road a second time for a thing that a man of thirty-two was told on a Saturday in a shed.**

"**That is fair.**

"**I do not know that it is fair. I know that I am a man who sells lime and that I have got a cart that goes that way on the Friday and that a cart is the same whether there is a passenger on it or not. I am not holding him up. I am not threatening anything. I am going down that coast on the Friday because I have got a load going that way and I always have. And if it turns out he is standing at the top of a slip on the sixth day asking me a question, then that is the day I will answer it, and I would rather he asked me than that I sat on a mole for a fortnight waiting to be told.**"

Nobody in that shed said one word to him, and he went down the hill in the rain, and the reason nobody said a word is entered in the first column and it is not a coldness.

"**Write this,**" said Nessa Pike to the boards, and she was writing them out in her left hand and it took her twice as long as it takes her, "**and read it back after me and do not improve it. That the price of that lead was named on the twenty-seventh of February past before the day and has not been paid and is not going to be paid. That the holder of that writ is up a coast road. That the man who brought that lead is going four days down this coast on the Friday in his own cart on his own account and is not waiting and has not been asked to wait and this party did not ask him to wait. That the lead does not come back. And that nobody in this shed is going to improve on that.**"

He read it back after her and neither of them said anything else about it, and that is entered and it is not a fault.

"**And write the other one, the one underneath, and it is the reason the whole of this happened and it is not about my brother and it never was.**

"**That a thing on the water is a thing a crew maintains, and that a crew is two people, and that on Tuesday morning one of the two people went ten days up a coast road in a cart that was going anyway, and that he is going because a man of sixty-one asked him once and has not asked him twice and is sixty-one in August, and because the going out is the whole of the craft and a man who has never been asked to go and does not go is a man who is going to be asked to go by somebody else in four years.

"**And that this is the third of three things that make a thing on the water expensive, and that this yard has not paid for any of the three and has not pretended to, and that the first of the three is that a thing on the water has to be looked at and there is no figure anybody in this country can give for how many times anybody is going to look at it, and that the second of them is that a thing on the water is a thing on a wall that moves and any man on a mole can see it and count it against you, and that the third of them is the one we are standing in, and that it is that it takes a crew, and that a crew is people, and that a party of two has no crew in it whatever is written in a book at the top of a bench on the thirtieth of October past.**"

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the third day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that neither of the said two days is a rota day and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged on neither of them, and that the reason against that line is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Tuesday and was not occupied at any hour on the said Wednesday, and that the holder did not go over to him on the said Tuesday and did not go over to him on the said Wednesday, and that the holder was up that coast road on both of the said days, and that the two of those facts are entered and that neither of them is a reason and that no reason is going to be given for either of them in this book.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and the board was not written on either of them; and that the holder of that writ went up that coast road at about ten in the morning on the said Tuesday on a cart belonging to a man of about forty-five who was returning to a place ten days up that coast; and that the reason against the line of the said Tuesday is that going, and the reason against the line of the said Wednesday is the same reason and is a different day; and that there is no figure against either of them and there is not going to be one.*

*And that the cost of that going was named in the first column of this book on the said Monday night at about eleven o'clock, before the said Tuesday and not after it, being a day of that yard at the face of the second seam which is about three marks, and a man out of that harbor and out of his own trade for ten days there and ten days back, and a cart that was going anyway and cost nothing, and that the holder of that writ is expected back at that mole about the twenty-second or the twenty-third day of that month and that the day was not asked for and is not going to be.*

*And that on the said Tuesday a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and no envelope was made; and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move; and that the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is nineteen, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and the said second of March instant, and that all nineteen are entered in that book in the same breath and not one after the other.*

*And that the said woman wrote the board of the said Tuesday at about half past eleven in the morning in her own left hand because the said woman has four stitches in that hand and cannot hold a line and cannot hold a pencil, and that it took her about twice as long as it takes her.*

*And that at about a quarter past four on the said Wednesday afternoon the man of about thirty-four who sells lime and chalk came up that hill on his own account and stood about four feet off that door and said that his cart goes four days down that coast on the said Friday on his own account, and that he is going to find out whether a man of about fifty in that place says a thing to a second man, and that he said on the twenty-seventh of February past that he would go when he had been told what it is for, and that he has not been told, and that he is not going to stand on a mole for a day for a favor, and that he is not holding anybody up and is threatening nothing and is going down that coast on the said Friday because he has got a load going that way and always has.*

*And that the said man said that the holder of that writ went up that coast road on the said Tuesday at ten o'clock in a cart, and that he will be at the bar about the eighth day and back at that mole about the twenty-second or the twenty-third, and that the said holder did not say the day and was not asked for it.*

*And that nobody in that shed said one word to the said man, and that the reason against that is that the price of that lead was named on the twenty-seventh of February past before the day and has not been paid and is not going to be paid, and that a party which has named a price and not paid it does not get to change its mind for nothing and does not get to send anybody else with the price either.*

*And that the said woman entered that the lead does not come back, and that nobody in that shed is going to improve on that.*

*And that the said woman entered, and the holder read back after her, that a thing on the water is a thing a crew maintains, and that a crew is two people, and that on the said Tuesday one of the two people went ten days up that coast road in a cart that was going anyway; and that the said holder is going because a man of sixty-one asked him once and has not asked him twice and is sixty-one in the month of August, and because the going out is the whole of the craft, and that a man who has never been asked to go and does not go is a man who is going to be asked to go by somebody else in about four years.*

*And that this is the third of three things that make a thing on the water expensive; and that the first of the three is that a thing on the water has to be looked at and that no figure in this country can be given for how many times anybody is going to look at it; and that the second of the three is that a thing on the water is a thing on a wall that moves and any man on a mole can see it and count it against the party that put it there; and that the third of the three is that it takes a crew, and a crew is people, and a party of two has no crew in it whatever is written in a book at the top of a bench on the thirtieth of October past; and that that book stands at sixteen lines and has not been extended and is not extended by this yard going up a coast road.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the third day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ chose the living archive and let the lead go, and that the choice was made on the said Tuesday morning at about ten at the foot of that mole and not on the said Wednesday and not in a room and not in a market, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a man got on a cart and cannot check what a man of thirty-two weighed at the foot of a mole at ten o'clock in the morning, and that there is no figure for that and there is not going to be one.*

*And that going after the said living archive cost a day this yard has not got and has put a man in a boat on a coast in a month when a man in a boat is a wage, and that going after the said lead would have cost nothing in this month and is worth everything in about four years, and that the day he did not take it is the third of March instant, and that nobody in that shed has improved on that and nobody is going to.*

*And that the whole of what this yard is owed is on the page above and none of it has moved, and that the forty marks to a boy of twenty-two stand whole and the forty marks first after food to a person of no office stand first after food, and that neither of them was asked about on the said Tuesday or the said Wednesday and that neither of them is going to be asked about in this month.*

*And that the four lines above the said named line are still four of them and are still nameless and are not struck out and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing came into it on the said Wednesday, and nothing went out of it on either of the two days, and fifty-eight marks, four shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is nine thousand three hundred and thirty pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is eight thousand six hundred and ninety pence, and it is the same figure it was on the Monday night and that is entered as a fact and not as a virtue. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Wednesday is three days at the face of the second seam, being one thousand four hundred and forty pence, being the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth days of February past and the first of March instant, and no two of them were worked by the same two people. The whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Wednesday is nothing at all, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and three pence in four transactions at two counters. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and nineteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and the second of March instant, and all nineteen are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and this yard did not say that note out loud in the thirteen weeks ended on the said Wednesday and is not going to. The rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
