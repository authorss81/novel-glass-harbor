**And on the said Sunday afternoon the mark that went out on the Friday was still on the water at the face of the second seam, at a quarter past two, and a person went out in a boat of about forty tons to look at it, and that is the whole of the trade, and the man of the second lay went with her because a boat of about forty tons has been a hand short since the middle of July past and is a hand short now, and he did not say that out loud and she wrote it in the first column and he read it back after her.**

At the face of the second seam the mark was where it had been set. It was fouled on the weather side with about four inches of green and it was making a small noise in a flat calm that a man on a mole could not have heard, and nobody struck it and nobody heard it on the mole and nobody is going to be told about it in this book.

**And at about a quarter past two on the said Monday afternoon, in a market in this town, with the boards up and about forty people standing about where they stand on any afternoon of that week, a herring skipper of fifty-one of this town said a thing out loud, and the boards came down at about three, and this yard is not on the boards and has never been, and that is the last true thing anybody said in this book.**

"**There is a form in a drawer on that mole with a name on it and a court in a city that has not sat, and everybody in this market has now read that form in about forty people or been told about it, and I have been in about nine rooms on this coast in nine years where a man was told what he was and was not, and I am going to say the one true thing about it and then I am going to go home.

"**A person who is not examined is not a person who has been refused. He has not been refused. He has not been examined. Nobody has found anything against him and nobody is going to, and the paper that says so is a form, not a finding, and if anybody in this market ever tells you that man has been refused, you have been told a thing that is not true and you can put it in front of forty people in about a minute.**"

Nobody said anything after that, and about forty people stood in it.
