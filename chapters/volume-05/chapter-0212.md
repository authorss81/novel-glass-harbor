# Chapter 212

*The Thirty-Fourth Monday, and a Day That Came In While Four Marks Did Not Go Out*

Monday the twenty-eighth and Tuesday the twenty-ninth of December, on the mole's steps, on the face of the second seam, and in the shed.

**On Monday the twenty-eighth of December the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-fourth Monday since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it, and there is a day and a room in his own page for the Thursday of that week and nothing has gone on the wall about it.**

**That Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, as it has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it and this party is not going to say in a street what that and this morning make between them.**

---

**And at about ten on the Monday morning, at the foot of the mole's steps, a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own came up the hill for the second time in four days and asked whether the Tuesday was on.**

He had brought nothing with him. That was the thing about him, and it had been the thing about him on the twelfth and it was the thing about him now: he came up the hill, he asked, and then he went down the hill and got the pump and the man, and the getting was his and not this yard's and it took him most of the day.

"**The Tuesday,**" said Calder Marr.

"**A day at the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on my own account, nobody in the water, and I will bring the pump and I will bring a man with it and I will not argue the figure and I will pay it at the mole the same day the way I did on the twelfth.**"

"**That is the same offer twice and I want you to hear the second half of it, because I turned you down on Friday and you came back up that hill the next morning and I have not written to you and you have not written to me and I do not know how you knew.**

"**I did not know. I had a reason on Friday and it was the wrong one. I do not want to talk about Friday because Friday was a man who had been up a hill twice in three days and was trying to be fair to himself about it.**"

That was the last of it. He went down the hill. There is nothing in this book about what he said to his partner that afternoon, because nobody asked him and nobody is going to.

"**You are going to take it,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I am going to take it. On Friday I could not say what I was doing on Saturday morning. I can say now. That is the whole of the change and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to tell you it was humility.**

"**Write it in your own hand tonight and not in mine, and put the figure of three marks against the Tuesday and not against nothing.**"

"**I will put it against the Tuesday.**"

---

**And on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of December this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back five times, which is one more time than on the twelfth of December, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, because the count and the times go on a slate and the slate goes into the book in her own writing and the holder read it back after her at half past one in the daylight and neither of them said anything about it.**

The board for the day is twelve gross. What comes off the top of a day rate comes off the top of it in the order it has come off since the third of May: the eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

**Three marks is four hundred and eighty pence, and the bag held seven hundred and seventeen pence on the Friday morning of the twenty-fifth, and the sum of the two is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence, which is seven marks, six shillings and five pence, and the twopence that were spent on a sheet of paper at a counter off the fish market on the eighteenth of December past are the difference between that figure and the figure that was in the bag this morning, and both of those figures are in this book and one of them is a shop.**

**And the day was booked by this party with a person in it and not a person short, and the cost of that is a day that goes slower than a day with three on it, and a day that goes slower is a day not earned whole and is entered as a day not earned whole and not as a day at a figure.**

---

**And at about four on the Tuesday afternoon a wage of four marks did not go in.**

It is a Tuesday and a wage week falls on a Tuesday and it has fallen on a Tuesday since the fifth of March. Six hundred and forty pence did not go out of this yard on the twenty-ninth day of December instant, and no envelope was made, and the envelope count on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move either.

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now TEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past and the twenty-ninth of December instant, and all ten are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all ten at once rather than the one that is easier to say.**

Three marks came into this yard that morning at about half past one, on the boards, out of a hirer's own hand, and four marks did not go out of it that afternoon, and both of those are in the same book in the same column on the same day, and the bag is ten marks, six shillings and four pence, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage is one thousand and thirty-six pence.

**It was five hundred and fifty-six pence this morning. Three marks came in and the figure got worse, and that is the whole of the arithmetic of it, and the arithmetic is not the reason and the reason is on the page and it was entered on the Friday night by the man whose hand it is in.**

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on the twenty-eighth instant and it came down bare, and that it was the thirty-fourth Monday since the first of May, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held on that Monday.*

*And that at about ten on the said Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil.*

*And that at about ten on the said Monday morning at the foot of that mole's steps a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own asked whether the said Tuesday was on, and that the said offer was the second of two the same, being a day at the face of the second seam landward of the third seam's mark on his own account with nobody in the water, and that he would bring the pump and a man and would not argue the figure and would pay at the mole the same day; and that the holder said yes; and that the said man said that he had had a reason on Friday for coming up that hill the next morning and that it was the wrong one, and that he did not want to talk about Friday because Friday was a man who had been up a hill twice in three days and was trying to be fair to himself about it; and that he went down the hill and got the pump and the man and that the getting was his and not this yard's, and that there is nothing in this book about what he said to his partner that afternoon because nobody asked him and nobody is going to.*

*And that the holder gave the whole of the reason in a doorway at the foot of those steps and not in a market, which is that on the said Friday he could not say what he was doing on the Saturday morning and that on the said Monday he could say, and that this is the whole of the change and that he is not going to call it humility and is not going to improve on it.*

*And that this yard worked on the said Tuesday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back five times, and that the gross on the board was twelve, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it in the order they have come off since the third of May, and that nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the bag held seven hundred and seventeen pence on the morning of the twenty-fifth instant and the sum of the two is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence, which is seven marks, six shillings and five pence, and that the twopence difference between that figure and the figure in the bag on the morning of the said Tuesday is the price of a sheet of paper bought at a counter off the fish market on the eighteenth instant and entered in this book on that day; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book, and the holder read it back after her at half past one in the daylight and neither of them said anything about it.*

*And that the holder entered that the said day was booked by this party with a person in it and not a person short, and that the cost of that is a day that went slower than a day with three on it, and that a day that goes slower is a day not earned whole and is entered as a day not earned whole and not as a day at a figure; and that the said woman entered in her own hand on the said evening that until the first of March next this yard is a party of one and a half and not two, and that every day this yard books in this month is a day it books with a man in it, and that she does not want it in her book and it is in the holder's.*

*And that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on the said Tuesday and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and that the column advances by one line and four marks on a Tuesday and only on a Tuesday and only if the wage is paid, and that the envelope count advances with the column and only with it; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is ten, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past and the twenty-ninth of December instant, and that all ten are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other.*

*And that three marks, being four hundred and eighty pence, came into this yard on the said morning at about half past one on the boards out of a hirer's own hand, and that four marks did not go out of it on the said afternoon, and that both of those are in the same book in the same column on the same day, and that the bag is ten marks, six shillings and four pence, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and that the figure the bag is short of the wage is one thousand and thirty-six pence, and that it was five hundred and fifty-six pence on the morning of the said day, and that three marks came in and the figure got worse, and that the reason against the line is in this column and not in the second and was entered by the holder in his own left hand and is not improved on and is not going to be.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder has entered the reason against the said line of the wage in the first column and not in this one, and that the whole of the reason is that a wage is a week and not a payment, and that paying one week out of ten weeks leaves a bag that pays one week and no other, and that a man who is paid a week in the last week of December and is paid nothing in the second week of January has been given a figure and not a wage, and that this yard has a page for a figure and does not want one; and that the column would stand at twenty-seven lines and one hundred and eight marks with the fifth of March still the oldest, and that a column with the fifth of March still the oldest and the twenty-ninth of December newest is a column that is nine weeks out of order, and that this yard is not going to begin it; and that whether a yard which has been silent for ten weeks is better or worse than a yard which has paid one week and gone quiet is not a question of arithmetic and is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the men the column is for, and that this yard has not asked any of them.*

*And that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the twenty-ninth of December and nothing came in on the twenty-eighth, and nothing went out of it on either day. The bag is ten marks, six shillings and four pence, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and that is four hundred and eighty more than the figure it held on Saturday last, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of it. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the yard did not say that note out loud this month and is not going to. Three marks for the eighth of August past to a man of Ashtide, and not one penny of it moved, and he is not written on any page of this book after that day and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days and no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is printed in this book and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence is owing to a woman of this harbor, and her name is on that figure in her own hand and is not rubbed out. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps that boy's hand, and nobody has been appointed to that column and that column has not been filled since the first of August past. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, and not one mark of it has been paid and not one shilling of it offered; she said not to be asked again this winter and she has not been asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, and ten weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and ninety-nine days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
