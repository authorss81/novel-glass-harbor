# Chapter 227

*A Named Man at the End of Every Line, and a Counter Off the Fish Market*

Wednesday the twenty-seventh and Thursday the twenty-eighth of January, in the shed, and in a room off the fish market.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged on that Thursday at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that is the fourth of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and stays nil.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour either, and this party entered that and carried no figure of what came before it against that line.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and none on the said Thursday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against that line is that this yard was in that shed until a quarter to ten on the said Wednesday night with a column of its own to read back and two people to read it back, and that it was in a room off the fish market at about eleven on the said Thursday morning with one question it could not carry, and that a day this yard spends asking somebody else a thing is not a day this yard works.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Wednesday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the first column of that book read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed.**

She read it the way she reads it, which is as a list of things a man did, and the holder read it after her as a list of things a man did, and that is the whole of what either of them had been hearing for four volumes.

At about a quarter to ten she stopped on a line about the sixth day of October, and read the next four, and then said:

"**There is a name at the end of that one.**"

"**There is a name at the end of all of them.**"

"**There is a name at the end of all of them but those four.**"

They went back up the page together. Every line of that column since the first of May has a day at the end of it and a name at the end of it, and the name is his, in his own left hand, and the ship's hand goes in the same line when it is her hand and not his, and where a line is the work of the two of them the two names are on it.

And four lines have a day at the end of them and nothing else. They are four days in July, they are the four lines immediately above the line of the twentieth of that month, and the twentieth of that month is the one named line, and the four above it are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out.

"**That is what it is,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Read it as a whole. It is not a column of things a yard did. It is a column of things a yard did with a man's name at the end of every one of them, and four days in July when there was no man's name to put at the end of it, and the reason there was no man's name is in the second column with no name in it and has been in there since the sixth of October.**

"**Calder. That is a line of custody. Every line of it. It is only a line of custody where a name is at the end of it, and that is why you have been able to say out loud for four volumes that a thing a man can check is the only kind worth having, and you have been saying it about a column with four gaps in it and you have not seen the four gaps, because you were standing inside the column.**"

Nobody said anything for about a minute.

"**And I have been reading that column out at the head of a dock in about nine people's hearing,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have never once been asked what is at the end of a line of it, because everybody standing there already knows what is at the end of a line of it. It is me. That is the whole of the trick. It is not clever and it is not a method. A man with a name on every line can say anything he likes in a street and it is true that he can be checked, and a man without a name on every line cannot say anything he likes in a street, and neither of those two facts is about the sea.**"

"**Then strike them out.**
"**The four. Out of the first column. I am not asking you to destroy anything and I am not asking you to say a thing that was not so. I am asking you to take four lines out of a column that you have been standing in front of in a market in about nine people's hearing, because a column with four days in it that have nobody's name at the end of them is a worse advertisement than a column with nothing in it at all.**"

"**No. A line does not come out of a column in this book because it is an awkward thing to have written in it. A line comes out of a column when it is wrong, and that is the only ground any column in this yard has ever had, and the ground is the same for the first column and the second column and the third one that the boy keeps his hand at the head of.**

"**And I am going to give you the whole of the reason, because you have earned it in nine days. I would strike them out if I could put a name at the end of them, and I cannot put a name at the end of them because on those four days I could not tell which of two sounds was the boat, and that is in the second column and has been since the sixth of October, and the reason it has no name in it is the reason of the fourth of October and I am not going to give it to you tonight and you are not going to ask me for it.**

"**So the four lines stay, and they are four days out of five months in which this yard did not know whose day it was. That is the truth about those four days and I would rather be four days short than four days smooth.**"

"**That is the whole of what I had to say and I have said it and I am going to sit down.**"

---

**And that is the whole of the discovery, and it was made in a shed at about a quarter to ten at night on a Wednesday by two people who have been inside the column for four volumes, and it is on the page, and nobody in that shed improved on it.**

And the other half of it came out of the same two hours, and it is the half that is going to cost something.

"**We have got a page on that bench that nobody in this harbor can check,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and before the seventh of December I would have said that was the worst thing that had ever happened to this yard, and I would have been wrong, and I want to say how wrong in front of you before I say anything else.**

"**It is not the worst thing. It is the thing we have got no words for. Every page that came up this hill in five months was a page somebody else made, and this yard's whole argument is about pages a man can check, and a man can check a page this yard has made because this yard's book says what it did and when and whose day it was.**

"**And this week we read four pages that are better than anything in this book and not one of them says where the person stood when it was written. And I cannot say whether that makes them better or worse. I have had three days with it and I have not been able to say it and I have got quite good at knowing when I can say a thing.**"

"**Then who can say it.**

"**I can think of one person in this town. She has a counter in a room off the fish market and she has been behind it for thirty-one years and she is the only person I know of who can put a figure on the shape of a thing without moving her lips about it. And she will not like the question, and she will answer it, and I am not asking her because I want the answer. I am asking her because we have to put the question to somebody and she is the only one.**

"**And I am not going to tell her what a column is. I want that written down. She is not to be told what a second column is, and I am not going to be the one who tells her, and the reason is that the moment you tell a woman what a column is you are selling her a thing, and she has told us to our faces that she can tell the difference between a thing a man is selling and a thing a man is showing, and she is right, and I have not got an argument that is better than hers.**"

---

**And they went down the hill at about eleven on the Thursday morning and Pernel Saye was behind her counter and there were two people in the room and one of them was waiting, and she looked at the penny Calder Marr put on the counter and said nothing about it and put it in the box, which is what happens to money at that counter.**

Nessa Pike put the sheet of paper on the counter face down and turned it over.

"**We have a page that nobody in this harbor can check,**" she said. "**I am not going to tell you what it is. I am going to ask you one question and it is the first question anybody has asked you at that counter in about four months, and I have thought about it for three days and it is a real question and it is not fair, and I am going to ask it anyway because we cannot carry it.**

"**A record a man can check, or a record nobody can check. Which is worth having.**"

The woman of fifty-eight looked at the penny in the box, and then at the two of them, and then she put both hands flat on the counter, which is a thing she does and is not a thing anybody has taught her.

"**I am not going to answer that,**" she said. "**And I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to say the nearest thing to it that I have got, and the nearest thing is about me and about this room and is not about your page, and you are not to write it down as an answer.**

"**I can price the making of a page. I can price the keeping of a page, which is nothing, and which I have told you is nothing, and I told you why on the seventh of January and I have had three weeks to sit with having told you. And I cannot price the being right about it, and neither can anybody on this coast, and anybody who tells you he can is selling you a book or a room or a good.**

"**So you have brought me something nobody has ever brought to that counter. Thirty-one years. Not one man has ever put a page in front of me and asked me what it is worth. They ask what it costs, and I can answer that, and I am good at it, and it is the whole of my trade. The making of a thing. That is my trade and I have been good at it for thirty-one years and I have never once been asked about the other half of it, and I did not know there was an other half until about three years ago and I have not said anything about it to anybody.**

"**And here is the other half, and it is not an answer to your question and I am telling you that before I say it.

"**A record a man can check is worth what the man is who checks it. A record nobody can check is worth what the person who keeps it is worth. And those are not the same figure and there is no figure for either of them and I am the only person I know on this coast who would say so out loud to a paying customer, and I have just said it to two people who are not paying customers, and that is your fault for coming in on a Thursday.**

"**I have met about nine people in thirty-one years that I would trust with a page. I have met about four hundred that I would not. And the nine are not better than the four hundred. They are just nine. And if you had brought me your page in January I would have said that, and you would have gone away and thought about it, and I would not have lost a customer or a night's sleep, because it is a thing about me and not about you.**

"**That is all. That is not an answer. Do not write it down as one.**"

"**We are not writing anything down at all. We bought a sheet of paper and that is the whole of the transaction and it is one penny.**"

"**Then put your penny in and go up that hill and do the thing you are going to do, and come back when you have done it, and if you come back I will tell you the other thing I have not looked at in thirty-one years, and I am warning you now that when I do, it is going to be a worse thing to hear than anything I have said today, and I am not going to pretend today was the worst of it.**"

---

**And at about half past eight on the Thursday night, in the shed, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed, and one penny gone out of this yard for the first time in a month and it is on the twenty-eighth day of January and it is a sheet of paper and nothing else.**

The fourth of the four was still on that bench under a weight and it was the only piece of paper in this shed that had a name on it and no date on it, and it had been there twelve days, and the man who sells lime and chalk was going to come back for it on the Saturday.

**And nobody in that shed wrote down where it goes on the Saturday, and nobody asked, and the reason against that is not going to be given tonight.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-eighth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that that is the fourth of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and that the walk is nil and stays nil; and that the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour nor on the said Thursday at any hour, and that this party entered that and carried no figure of what came before it against that line.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and none on the said Thursday and the board was not written on either of them, and that the reason against that line is that this party was in that shed until a quarter to ten on the said Wednesday night with a column of its own to read back and two people to read it back, and that it was in a room off the fish market at about eleven on the said Thursday morning with one question it could not carry, and that a day this party spends asking somebody else a thing is not a day this party works.*

*And that at about half past eight on the said Wednesday night, with the lamp lit and the two of them and nobody else in that shed, the first column of that book was read back from the top of that bench by the said woman and read back after her by the said holder; and that the said woman stopped at about a quarter to ten on a line about the sixth day of October past and read the next four and said that there was a name at the end of that one; and that there is a name at the end of every line of that column since the first of May past, and that four lines have a day at the end of them and nothing else, and that those four lines are the four lines immediately above the line of the twentieth day of the month of July past.*

*And that the said woman said that, read as a whole, that column is not a column of things a yard did and is a column of things a yard did with a man's name at the end of every one of them, and four days in the said month when there was no man's name to put at the end of it, and that the reason there was no man's name is in the second column with no name in it and has been in there since the sixth of October past; and that the said woman said that that is a line of custody, and that it is only a line of custody where a name is at the end of a line, and that that is why the holder has been able to say out loud for four volumes that a thing a man can check is the only kind worth having, and that he has been saying it about a column with four gaps in it and has not seen the four gaps because he was standing inside the column.*

*And that the said holder said that a man with a name on every line can say anything he likes in a street and that it is true that he can be checked, and that a man without a name on every line cannot, and that neither of those two facts is about the sea.*

*And that the said woman asked the said holder to strike the four lines out of the first column, and gave the reason herself, that a column with four days in it that have nobody's name at the end of them is a worse advertisement than a column with nothing in it at all; and that the said holder refused, and said that a line does not come out of a column in that book because it is an awkward thing to have been written in it, and that a line comes out of a column when it is wrong, and that that is the only ground any column in that yard has ever had, and that the ground is the same for the first column and the second column and the third one that the boy keeps his hand at the head of; and that he gave the whole of the reason, that he would strike them out if he could put a name at the end of them, and that he cannot, and that the reason he cannot is in the second column and has been since the sixth of October past.*

*And that this party entered that the four lines stay, and that they are four days out of five months in which this yard did not know whose day it was, and that the said holder said that he would rather be four days short than four days smooth; and that the said woman said that that was the whole of what she had to say and that she was going to sit down.*

*And that the said woman said on the said Wednesday that this yard has got a page on that bench that nobody in this harbor can check, and that before the seventh of December past she would have said that that was the worst thing that had ever happened to this yard and would have been wrong, and that it is not the worst thing and is the thing this yard has got no words for, and that every page that came up that hill in five months was a page somebody else made, and that this yard's whole argument is about pages a man can check, and that a man can check a page this yard has made because that book says what it did and when and whose day it was; and that in the week then ended this party read four pages that are better than anything in that book, and that not one of them says where the person stood when it was written, and that the said woman said that she cannot say whether that makes them better or worse, and that she had had three days with it and had not been able to say it.*

*And that at about eleven on the said Thursday morning this party went down that hill into a room off the fish market, and that a copyist of fifty-eight was behind that counter and that there were two people in that room and one of them was waiting, and that she looked at the penny the said holder put on that counter and put it in the box and said nothing about it; and that the said woman put a sheet of paper on that counter face down and turned it over and asked one question, being whether a record a man can check or a record nobody can check is worth having, and said that the question was not fair and that she was asking it anyway because they could not carry it.*

*And that the said copyist said that she was not going to answer that, and that she can price the making of a page and can price the keeping of a page, which is nothing, and cannot price the being right about it, and that nobody on this coast can price the being right about it, and that anybody who tells a man he can is selling him a book or a room or a good; and that she said that the said party had brought her something nobody has ever brought to that counter in thirty-one years, and that the making of a thing is her whole trade and that she has never once been asked about the other half of it; and that she said that a record a man can check is worth what the man is who checks it and a record nobody can check is worth what the person who keeps it is worth, and that those are not the same figure and there is no figure for either of them; and that she said that she has met about nine people in thirty-one years that she would trust with a page and about four hundred that she would not, and that the nine are not better than the four hundred and are just nine; and that she said that that is not an answer and was not to be written down as one.*

*And that the said holder said that nothing was being written down at all and that the whole of the transaction was one penny; and that the said copyist told the said party to put the penny in and go up that hill and do the thing they were going to do, and to come back when they have done it, and said that when they come back she will tell them the other thing she has not looked at in thirty-one years, and said that when she does it is going to be a worse thing to hear than anything she has said that day, and that she is not going to pretend that day was the worst of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the twenty-eighth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said holder entered on the said day that a party which has spent four volumes insisting that a record a man can check is the only kind worth having has arrived, in one week, at a record that nobody in this harbor can check, and cannot say whether that is better or worse, and that the reason against that is that a yard which has been insisting on one of the two for four volumes is not a witness to which of the two it wants; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And that the said woman entered that she is not going to tell the said copyist what a second column is and is not going to be the one who tells her, and that the reason is that the moment a man tells a woman what a column is he is selling her a thing, and that the said copyist has told this party to their faces that she can tell the difference between a thing a man is selling and a thing a man is showing, and that the said woman has not got an argument that is better than that one; and that this party did not tell her, and did not ask her anything about it, and she did not ask, and the reason against that is that a thing a man has not offered a woman is not a thing the woman has been given.*

*And that the said copyist said that a record a man can check is worth what the man is who checks it and a record nobody can check is worth what the person who keeps it is worth, and that the said figure of nine and the said figure of four hundred are a figure about the said copyist and are not checkable from a mole and are not a figure about any page, and that the reason against that is that the whole of a copyist's trade is the making of a thing and not the being right about it; and that this party has asked one question at that counter in four months and it was answered with a refusal and a figure about the person who refused, and that this party entered that it got what it asked for.*

*And that the fourth of the four was on that bench on the said Thursday night under a weight and was the only piece of paper in that shed with a name on it and no date on it, and that it had been there twelve days; and that nobody in that shed wrote down where it goes on the Saturday and nobody asked, and that the reason against that is not given tonight.*

**One penny went out of this yard on the twenty-eighth of January, being a sheet of paper bought at a counter off the fish market, and that is the first money this yard has spent since the eighteenth of December past and the whole of the money that has gone out of it since the sixth of December is two pence. Nothing came in on the twenty-seventh and nothing came in on the twenty-eighth, and twenty-five marks, six shillings and three pence is in the bag, which is four thousand and seventy-five pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three thousand four hundred and thirty-five pence, and that is one penny less than it was on the Wednesday and the penny is the sheet of paper. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and fourteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and none of it is set out again here.**
