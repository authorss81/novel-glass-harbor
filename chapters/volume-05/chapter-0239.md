# Chapter 239

*Four Places, Four Readings, and a False Thing Said About a Man in a Kitchen*

Saturday the twentieth and Sunday the twenty-first of February, at the head of this dock, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**No rota day falls on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday, and the cistern behind the engine house was not charged on either of them, and this party entered both of those as days and entered no figure and no reason against either of them.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday and was not occupied on the said Sunday, and this party entered the two of them and carried nothing forward to either line.**

---

**And a man of fifty-five came up that mole at about a quarter to eight on the said Saturday morning and stood at the shed door in the dark with his hat in his hand and said that he was called Amos Reedy and that he read a sheet out in a room off the fish market for a penny a sheet, and that he had not been asked to come and that this yard did not send for him and that nobody sent for him, and that he had come because a man of about fifty-five is not a man anybody sends for and that is the only recommendation he has got.**

He had a folded sheet in his coat and he took it out and put it on the boards and did not open it.

"**I have read that sheet out eleven times since the eighth of August past and I am not going to read it out again, and I am not going to be the man who reads out a thing twice for the same penny. What I have come up here for is to say four things that four people have said to me about that sheet, and I am not going to say them to you as one thing, because I have tried it on the walk up here and it does not go together and I have not been able to make it go together, and I have been trying to make it go together since the Tuesday.

"**The first is a kitchen in a place two days down this coast. The man there is a man of about thirty and he cannot read and he has four men in his trade who can, and he came to me and paid me a penny and I read it and he said: *that is the yard, then. That is the yard's days. Then I can go up there.* And he said it as though I had given him a map. And I did read the thirty-eighth line at the bottom, and he did not hear that part properly because he was writing a figure down at the time, and I did not read it again.

"**The second is a room in this town. A man of this town, sixty-eight, cannot read, and he came to me in September, and I read it, and he said: *that is the only page on this coast that tells me where to go and who to ask.* And he was right. He is right and he is the only one of the four of them who is right about what the paper is, and he meant it exactly as he said it.

"**The third is at a funeral. The eleventh of January. A man I do not know, and he paid me in advance, and he wanted it read out on a Thursday and he said it was the only place he had where everybody was listening. And it went off all wrong, because there were about sixty people at that funeral and a man of about seventy stood up in the middle of it and said out loud that he was not going to be asked anything by anybody about any day of his own, and he said it to the room and not to me, and the man who had paid me was his brother and did not know he was going to say it. And I read the whole thing out and I charged the penny and I did not stop, because you stop a reading when somebody is ill and nobody was ill.

"**And the fourth is a house in this town that a man of fifty-eight who sells timber goes into, and he read it himself in November, and he wrote you a letter, and I have read that letter.

"**Those are the four. A map, a directory, a thing to say out loud at a funeral, and a set of days that ought to be asked about. Four readings of one sheet. And every one of the four men is right. And I have read that sheet eleven times and I have never once been able to see what they were all looking at, and I have not told any of them about any of the other three, and I am not going to, and I am telling you all four because you printed it and because I am the one who read it and there is nobody else on this coast who has read it to all four.**

"**And I am not going to say the same thing four times so that it comes out even, and I want it written down that I could have and did not.**"

"**Then say the part you have not said and go down that mole, because it is a quarter to nine and the tide is on.**

"**The part I have not said is mine and it is not about the four of them.

"**In the kitchen in that place two days down the coast there are four men and one of them is the youngest and he is the one who said the thing about a place being allowed to decide about itself, and he said it to me at a table on a Sunday because I am a stranger and strangers are safe. And on the Wednesday last week his uncle came to the house I board at when I am in that place and asked me, in front of the other three, whether I worked for the yard.

"**And I said no.

"**And I said no because I do not work for the yard, and because I have not worked for the yard, and because I read a sheet out for a penny a sheet and live two days up the road, and those are the two true things and the one of them is the one that gets said. And he went away and he told the other two, and the other two have told the house I board at that the man who read the sheet is a man of the yard, and they told it the way you tell a thing that is not a scandal, and I have been in that place nine times since the August and I have not been asked to read anything since the Wednesday.

"**I have let it stand. I want to be exact about that, because you are a yard that writes things down and I do not want anybody to think I was brave about it. I let it stand because the true answer is that I am a man who reads a sheet out at a table in a kitchen on a Sunday for a penny and lives two days away, and if I say that in that room the room has it, and the room is forty people, and there are four men in that kitchen and three of them are the one who is asking.

"**And I am fifty-five and I have been reading sheets out for thirty years and this is the first time anybody has said I worked for anybody, and I am not a man who gets thanked for my trade and I am not a man anybody is angry with either, and I have got a daughter at that place who is nineteen and who goes to that kitchen.

"**So there it is. Four readings, and a fifth thing, and the fifth thing is the price of my not being able to correct a sentence in a room without handing the room a worse one, and I would not have had that five weeks ago, and four weeks ago I would have stood up and said it, and I have not stood up, and the reason I have not stood up is a sheet of paper with thirty-eight lines on it and I have read the whole thing and I have not understood one word of it, and I am not going to be a man who does not understand a word of a thing and still says no to a question about it.

"**That is the whole of it. You can write that I came up at a quarter to eight and I am going down now and I have not been asked anything and I am not going to be.**"

He went down the mole before the light came up.

---

**And this yard worked on the said Saturday from eight until about two, and at about ten past two the ship's hand put her right hand into a pump handle that had a split in it and came out of it with the two middle fingers opened across the knuckles, and the man of the second lay got the water out of it with a bucket and a clean cloth and did not say anything about it for about nine minutes and then said one sentence, which was that it was a bad handle and that he had been telling this yard about that handle since the eighteenth of January.**

At about six in the evening a bonesetter came up that mole and put four stitches in it and charged two shillings, which was the figure, and the two shillings went out of that bag on the said Saturday and it is the whole of the money this yard has spent in a month and it is the second and last penny's worth of anything this yard has bought since the eighteenth of December past.

**And no day of work was booked on the said Sunday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that the said woman has four stitches in her right hand and cannot hold a line and cannot hold a pencil, and that a day a yard loses to a hand is a day the yard lost and not a day the yard did not know about, and the figure for it is the two shillings that went out on the Saturday and that is the only figure there is.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Sunday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the lamp turned round so that the light was not on the page because that was the arrangement on the Saturday night and it was the arrangement on this one, and the day's entry read from the top of that bench by the holder and read back after him by the woman, and nobody else in that shed.**

It is the first time in five volumes that the day's entry has been read from the top of the bench by the holder and read back after him by the woman, and it is entered that it is, and the reason against the line is not given and is that there was nothing else in that day to read.

"**Four places,**" said Nessa Pike, in the end, and her right hand was flat on the bench inside a cloth and she was writing with her left and it took her about twice as long. "**A map, a directory, a thing to be said out loud at a funeral, and a set of days that ought to be asked about.

"**And all four of them are right. And I have spent the whole of today trying to find the sentence that makes them one and there is not one and I am not going to look for it again, and the reason is that they are four different questions and we printed a page that does not ask a question, and a man in a kitchen answered the question he had, and a man of sixty-eight answered the question he had, and a man at a funeral answered the question he had, and a man of fifty-eight answered the question he had.

"**And Calder: we are not going to make them say the same thing. There is no version of the next three weeks in which four places on this coast read that sheet the same way, and if there were one it would mean that one of us had written it, and we did not write it and we are not going to.

"**And the cost of not doing that is on that bench and it is not ours. It is in a room in a place two days down this coast where a man of about thirty is not a man anybody sends for, and he has been called a man of this yard, and he has let it stand, and he has a daughter of nineteen who goes to the kitchen where the sheet was read, and the reason he let it stand is that the true sentence about him is worse than the false one.

"**And I am going to say the thing I have to say and I am going to say it with my left hand and I am not going to apologise for my left hand. That man is owed something by this yard and this yard is not going to pay it and is not going to write to him and is not going to send anybody, and the reason is the reason of the fourth of October past and is not going to be given, and the reason against the line is that the only thing this yard has ever been able to give anybody in a place is a page, and we have just worked out what a page does to a place when it is a list of names.

"**So he gets nothing from us, and he gets it from the four men in that kitchen or he does not get it, and I am going to write that down and I am going to go to bed, and the first of March is in eight days and there is a man's book of hours with sixteen lines in it that I have not opened since the thirtieth of October and I am not going to open it tonight either.**"

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-first day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no rota day falls on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday, and that the cistern behind the engine house was not charged on either of them and no figure and no reason is put against either of them.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Saturday nor at any hour on the said Sunday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either of the two days.*

*And that a man of fifty-five came up that mole on the said Saturday morning at about a quarter to eight and said that he is called Amos Reedy and that he reads a sheet out in a room off the fish market for a penny a sheet, and that this party did not send for him and nobody sent for him; and that he said that he had read that sheet out eleven times since the eighth of August past and was not going to read it out again and was not going to be the man who reads a thing out twice for the same penny.*

*And that the said man put a folded sheet on the boards and did not open it, and gave four accounts of what four men said to him about that sheet, being: in a kitchen in a place two days down that coast, a man of about thirty who cannot read, with four men in his trade who can, who said that the said sheet showed him where the yard was and that he could go up there, and that the said man heard the thirty-eighth line and did not hear it properly and was writing a figure at the time; in a room in that town, a man of sixty-eight who cannot read, who said in September past that the said sheet is the only page on that coast that tells him where to go and who to ask; at a funeral on the eleventh of January instant, where about sixty people were present and a man of about seventy stood up in the middle of it and said out loud that he was not going to be asked anything by anybody about any day of his own, and that the said reading was not stopped; and in a house in that town that a man of fifty-eight who sells timber goes into, who read it himself in the month of November past and wrote this party a letter.*

*And that the said man said that those are the four readings of one sheet and that every one of the four men is right, and that he has never been able to see what they were all looking at, and that he has not told any one of them about any of the other three and is not going to, and that he told this party all four because this party printed the said sheet and because he is the one who read it and there is nobody else on that coast who has read it to all four; and that he said that he was not going to say the same thing four times so that it came out even, and that he could have and did not.*

*And that the said man said that in the said kitchen there are four men and that one of them is the youngest and is the one who said that a place is allowed to decide things about itself, and that he said it at a table on a Sunday because the said man is a stranger; and that on the Wednesday last week the said man's uncle asked the said man in front of the other three whether he worked for the yard; and that the said man said no, and gave the reason, being that he does not work for that yard and has not worked for that yard, and that he reads a sheet out for a penny a sheet and lives two days up the road, and that those are two true things and that the one that gets said is the second one; and that he has been in that place nine times since the month of August past and has not been asked to read anything since the said Wednesday.*

*And that the said man said that he has let it stand, and gave the reason, being that the true answer is that he is a man who reads a sheet out at a table in a kitchen on a Sunday for a penny and lives two days away, and that if he says it in that room the room has it and the room is about forty people and there are four men in that kitchen and three of them are the one asking; and that he is fifty-five and has been reading sheets out for thirty years and this is the first time anybody has said he worked for anybody, and that he has a daughter at that place who is nineteen and who goes to that kitchen.*

*And that the said man said that the four readings and a fifth thing are the whole of it, and that the fifth thing is the price of his not being able to correct a sentence in a room without handing the room a worse one, and that four weeks earlier he would have stood up and said it and he did not stand up, and that the reason he did not stand up is a sheet of paper with thirty-eight lines on it that he has read whole and has not understood one word of.*

*And that the said man went down that mole before the light came up, and that this party entered that he came up at a quarter to eight and that he has not been asked anything and is not going to be.*

*And that this yard worked on the said Saturday from eight until about two on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that at about ten past two the said woman put her right hand into a pump handle that had a split in it and came out of it with the two middle fingers opened across the knuckles, and that the man of the second lay got the water out of it with a bucket and a clean cloth and said nothing about it for about nine minutes and then said that it was a bad handle and that he had been telling that yard about that handle since the eighteenth of January past.*

*And that a bonesetter came up that mole at about six in the evening on the said Saturday and put four stitches in that hand and charged two shillings, which went out of that yard on the said Saturday, and that two shillings is the whole of the money that yard has spent in a month.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Sunday and the board was not written on it, and that the reason against that line is that the said woman has four stitches in her right hand and cannot hold a line and cannot hold a pencil, and that a day a yard loses to a hand is a day the yard lost and not a day the yard did not know about.*

*And that at about half past eight on the said Sunday night, with the lamp lit and the lamp turned round so that the light was not on the page, and the two of them and nobody else in that shed, the day's entry was read from the top of that bench by the said holder and read back after him by the said woman, and that it is the first time in five volumes that the day's entry has been read that way, and that the reason against the line is not given and is that there was nothing else in that day to read.*

*And that the said woman said that the four places have read that sheet as a map, as a directory, as a thing to be said out loud at a funeral, and as a set of days that ought to be asked about; and that all four of them are right; and that she said that this party is not going to make them say the same thing, and that there is no version of the next three weeks in which four places on that coast read that sheet the same way, and that if there were one it would mean that one of this party had written it, and that this party did not write it and is not going to.*

*And that the said woman said that the cost of not doing that is not that yard's and is in a room in a place two days down that coast, where a man of about thirty is not a man anybody sends for and has been called a man of that yard and has let it stand, and that he has a daughter of nineteen who goes to the kitchen where that sheet was read, and that the reason he let it stand is that the true sentence about him is worse than the false one.*

*And that the said woman said that that man is owed something by this party and that this party is not going to pay it and is not going to write to him and is not going to send anybody, and gave the reason of the fourth of October past and did not give it, and that the reason against that line is that the only thing this party has ever been able to give anybody in a place is a page, and that this party has just worked out what a page does to a place when it is a list of names.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the twenty-first day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that four places on that coast have read one printed sheet four different ways and that all four readings are correct, and that this party is not going to make them agree and has no standing to make them agree and did not write the said sheet, and that the reason against that line is that a page is a record of where the person was standing when it was written, and that four people standing in four rooms are four different places, and that this yard has been asking for the page and has never once asked who was going to be standing in front of it.*

*And that a man of fifty-five who is not of this party and who is not a friend of it is carrying the fifth thing of that day, being a false sentence said about him in a room of about forty people, and that this party is entered against itself on that point and has entered no name against it, and that the reason against that line is that a party which has written a page that tells a man who he may ask has not bought the right to be introduced in four places, and did not know until the nineteenth of February that it had bought anything at all.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing on the said Sunday, and two shillings went out of it on the said Saturday and nothing else went out on either day, and forty-six marks, four shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is seven thousand four hundred and ten pence, which is three marks less the bonesetter out of what it was on the Friday, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six thousand seven hundred and seventy pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and seventeen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The cistern was not charged on either of the two days and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the one rota day left in this month is the twenty-second and this party is not going to walk it. The far bench was not occupied on either of the two days. The bench under that pump was bare for a twenty-second night on the Saturday and a twenty-third on the Sunday. Four returns are four and there is no fifth, and no finding of worked glass was made on either of the two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, unargued, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure, and that is a different two shillings from the bonesetter's and is in no sentence with it. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid and unoffered. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June past standing. The book of hours of the thirtieth and thirty-first of October past stands at sixteen lines and was not opened on either of the two days and is not going to be opened in this batch, and no buoy went into any water and no keep-light was extended, and that yard is still short a hand and has been since the said Saturday. A working harbor of this coast is poorer on the twenty-first of February next than it was on the fourth of it, and a woman on that mole has four stitches in her writing hand, and nothing this yard has done in eighteen days has made one thing better, and a man of about thirty two days down that coast is now a man of that yard in about forty people's hearing and is not one.
