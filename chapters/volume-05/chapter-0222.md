# Chapter 222

*A Page That Is Not a Copy of Anything, and the Thirty-Seventh Monday*

Sunday the seventeenth and Monday the eighteenth of January, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**At about ten on that Monday morning four men this yard does not know put the cistern behind the engine house full at no figure, and this party walked it, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the first of five rota days this month that this party has entered walked on none of.**

**And on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-seventh Monday since the first of May, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour, and this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.**

---

**And on the Sunday night the ship's hand did not come down to that shed at half past eight and did not come down at nine, and she was in that shed on the Monday morning before seven and she had been up since about four, and the holder did not ask her and she did not say anything about it, and it went in the second column on the Monday night with a reason against the line and no name in it, and the reason is that a day this yard did not work is entered as a day this yard did not work and is not entered twice.**

---

**And this yard worked on the said Monday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and the pump was his and the man was his and the day was his own account.**

The board for the day is twelve gross, and what comes off the top of a day rate came off the top of it in the order it has come off since the third of May: the eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

A hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, which is one fewer time than on the eighth and two fewer than on the twenty-ninth of December, and the count and the times went onto a slate in her hand and the slate went into the book.

**And he brought the page up with him, and he brought it in a piece of oilcloth, and he put it on the bench without a word and went and got his pump, and he said one thing on his way down, which was that he had not looked inside the oilcloth and that he did not want to.**

Three marks is four hundred and eighty pence. The bag held two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence on the Friday and on the Saturday and has held it every day since, and the sum of the two is three thousand one hundred and sixteen pence, which is nineteen marks, six shillings and four pence.

---

**And at about two in the afternoon the oilcloth came off, and the thing inside it was one sheet of ruled paper, about the size of a board, folded once across, and it was not a copy of anything.**

There is no office stamp on it and no number at the head of it and no name at the foot of it and no date in any hand this yard can read. It is a leaf of a thing that was never bound, because the fold at the left edge is a tear and not a stitch, and it has been torn out of something and put in a locker and carried four hundred miles and it has been in this shed for about fourteen hours.

It is kept in a place at the inside of a bar, this party does not know which, and the first two hands on it are about thirty years apart by the iron in the ink, and the third hand is the hand of the man of the second lay's own bosun and it is four years old, and he wrote his name at the foot of it because somebody asked him to and he wrote it the way a bosun writes.

Nessa Pike read it. She did not read it out of a book and she did not read it out of a copy, and she read it off the page with her own hand on the bench, and the holder did not read it and did not look at it for the first hour and a quarter.

She read the parts that were a person wrote. She stopped on the first page for a long time on a line about a boat's bilge and a man bailing all night, and she read the rest of that line and did not comment on it, and she turned the leaf over and came back to it at the end.

Then she came to the line and she read that out loud, and this is the whole of it:

"**The ninth, and a hard night, and the light on the point was burning and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light.**"

She put her hand flat on the bench.

"**That is a page a place has. It is not a copy and it is not a document and nobody sent it anywhere. It is the page of a place at the inside of a bar, and it is about a night, and there is nothing on it about why anybody did anything, and the man at the foot of it wrote his name because a bosun writes his name.**

"**And it does not say the year. I looked. There is a year against one line out of about ninety and the year is in the oldest hand and I would not read it out of a hand like that and neither would you.**"

The holder said, after a while:

"**You read that line twice. The second time you stopped in the middle of it and came back to the beginning of it.**"

"**I did.**

"**Say why.**"

"**I am not going to, and I want it written down that I was not going to, and the reason it is in the second column is that I do not know it and that if I did know it I would not be able to say it in a way you could check either.**"

He said that was the first honest thing either of them had said about a page in three weeks, and she said it was the first one she had said in eleven months, and neither of them improved on it, and the second column took it as it was given.

**And then the holder read it, and it took him about fifty minutes, and he read it the way a man reads a thing he is going to be asked about.**

There is a line in the third hand, four years old, and it is not about the ninth. It is about a Tuesday in a month in the same year as the oldest hand, and it says that a boat of about sixty tons came in on the tide and was not asked where she was from and that somebody on the wall said a thing about that which is not written down because whoever wrote it did not want to write it.

"**That is a page about a place,**" said Calder Marr. "**It is about a night, and it is about a boat, and it is about somebody who said a thing on a wall. Three of the things that have happened to this country and none of them is a document and all of them are true, and the only reason I know the last one is true is that a man wrote it down four years ago and did not want to.**

"**And what I cannot do is check one line of it. Not one. There is no second of it anywhere in this harbor, and there is no man I can go and ask, and this yard has not been to any of the places on that page because this yard does not know where any of them are.**

"**And that is the whole of it, and I have been sitting here for an hour and a quarter waiting to find out that I did not like it, and I have not found out that.**"

---

**And at about half past eight on the Monday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back off the top of that bench in the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed.**

The oilcloth went back on. The man of the second lay is going to take it down the hill on the Tuesday and this yard is going to have it for four days and is going to lay out three more pages beside it on the Monday afternoon, and every one of the three has a man at the end of it who has told this yard in his own words what he will do with it and when.

Nessa Pike did not say anything about that and neither did the holder.

"**Write the line down,**" she said. "**The one about the light. I do not want it in my hand at the top of the book where anybody reads it. Put it in the second column and not the first, because I cannot check it and neither can you and that is the whole of the reason.**

"**And put the reason against the line and do not put a name in it, because the moment there is a name in it somebody is going to be able to tell me that a woman of seventy-one was in my shed on a Saturday morning and that is the first time in forty years that anyone has been in that house and it is going to be the last thing anybody in this town ever says about that house.**"

---

*First column, entered on Monday the eighteenth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about ten on the said Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the first of five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of; and that on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and came down bare, and that it was the thirty-seventh Monday since the first of May, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour nor on the said Monday at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it; and that no day of work was booked on the said Sunday and the board was not written on it, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happened still happened.*

*And that this yard put in a day on the said Monday, from eight until about half past one, on the face of the second seam and landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account and with nobody in the water, and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man and that the pump was his and the man was his and the day was his own account; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and that the gross on the board was twelve, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it in the order they have come off since the third of May, and that nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book.*

*And that the said man brought a sheet up that hill with him on the said Monday morning, in a piece of oilcloth, and put it on that bench without a word and went and got his pump, and that he said on his way down that he had not looked inside the oilcloth and did not want to.*

*And that the said sheet was opened at about two in the afternoon on the said Monday and that it is one sheet of ruled paper about the size of a board, folded once across, and that it is not a copy of anything; and that there is no office stamp on it and no number at the head of it and no name at the foot of it and no date in any hand this yard can read, and that the fold at the left edge is a tear and not a stitch, and that it has been torn out of a thing that was never bound and put in a locker and carried four hundred miles; and that it is the page of a place at the inside of a bar and that this party does not know which place, and that the first two hands on it are about thirty years apart by the iron in the ink, and that the third hand is four years old and is the hand of the said man's own bosun and is signed with a name at the foot because a bosun writes his name.*

*And that the said woman read that sheet at the top of that bench and that the said holder did not read it and did not look at it for the first hour and a quarter, and that she read the parts of it that a person wrote and stopped on a line about a boat's bilge and a man bailing all night and came back to that line at the end; and that the said line she read out loud is the ninth, and a hard night, and the light on the point was burning and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light; and that there is no year against that line and that there is a year against one line out of about ninety and that the year is in the oldest hand and was not read out.*

*And that the said holder read that sheet after her and it took him about fifty minutes; and that there is a line in the third hand which is four years old and is not about the ninth, being that a boat of about sixty tons came in on the tide and was not asked where she was from and that somebody on a wall said a thing about that which is not written down because whoever wrote it did not want to write it.*

*And that at about half past eight on the said Monday night, with the lamp lit and the two of them and nobody else in that shed, the day's entries were read back off the top of that bench in the said woman's writing and read back after her by the said holder; and that the oilcloth went back on, and that the said man of the second lay said he would take that page down that hill when this party was done with it, and that he did not say a day for it and that neither of them asked him for one; and that this party is going to have that page for as long as the said man will leave it, and is going to lay three more pages beside it, and that every one of the three has a man at the end of it who has told this yard in his own words what he will do with it and when.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the eighteenth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said woman did not come down to that shed on the said Sunday at half past eight and did not come down at nine, and that she was in that shed on the said Monday morning before seven and had been up since about four, and that the said holder did not ask her and she did not say anything about it, and that this party entered that and entered no name against it, and that the reason against the line is that a day this yard did not work is entered as a day this yard did not work and is not entered a second time.*

*And that the said woman read the line about the light twice and that the second time she stopped in the middle of it and came back to the beginning of it, and that the said holder asked her why and that she said she was not going to and wanted it written down that she was not going to; and that the reason against that line is that she does not know it, and that if she did know it she would not be able to say it in a way the said holder could check either.*

*And that the said woman asked that the line about the light be put in the second column and not the first, and gave the reason herself, that neither of them can check it, and that she said that a name must not go against it, and that the moment there is a name in it somebody will be able to tell her that a woman of seventy-one was in that shed on a Saturday morning, and that that is the first time in forty years that anybody has been in that house and is going to be the last thing anybody in this town ever says about that house.*

*And that the four lines above the said named line are still four and are still nameless and have not been struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the eighteenth of January and nothing on the seventeenth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and nineteen marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is three thousand one hundred and sixteen pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is two thousand four hundred and seventy-six pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and twelve weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. Nothing has gone into a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and is not read out again here.**
