# Chapter 243

*Midnight on the First of March, and Who Is Going to Be Told Something*

Sunday the twenty-eighth of February and Monday the first of March, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**At eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was the forty-third Monday since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any day of this book.**

**And on the said Monday the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the second of the six rota days that this party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the twenty-fifth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder did not go over to him on either day and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past and is not going to be given out of that shed in this book.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Sunday and the reason against that line is that a Sunday is the day the two of them have had since the twenty-first of February past. And on the said Monday this yard worked from eight until about one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Monday night, with the lamp lit and the lamp turned round, the two of them, the day's entries read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and then the holder turned the lamp about a foot to the left and opened the drawer and did not take anything out of it.**

"**Say the Wednesday of the twenty-fourth out of the first column,**" he said, "**because I want to hear it in this room with both of us in it before I write anything else tonight, and then say the day.**

"**The clerk of this harbor's own authority said, and said it as it stands and was not asked to improve on it, that the writ of the first of May past is a municipal instrument of that harbor, is inside that harbor line, is bounded by the outer marks, carries four names and no fifth, is not the Second Bell license, does not lead to one, renews nothing, and runs to midnight on the first of March; and that the office which issued it has said on the face of it in its own words that it is not the office that can renew it.**

"**Go on.**

"**And that at midnight on the first of March that paper stops being a thing the holder holds and nothing takes its place, and nobody is charged with anything, and no court sits, and nobody is fined, and no clerk comes up that hill, and nobody is told that the holder has not got it, and that that is not a thing there is a penalty for not having.**

"**That is the whole of it and it is a fact and not a forecast and I wrote it down in a clerk's words and I have read it back twice and I am not going to improve on it. The day is the first of March, and it is a Monday, and it is in about three hours and a half, and there is no part of this shed I would have to get up in the night to do anything about.**"

"**Then the second column,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**No. The second column is after midnight. I am not going to write a thing down at half past eight that I am going to have to correct at ten minutes past twelve, and neither are you, and the book does not get a line at half past eight that is dated the first of March and written on the twenty-eighth, because in about four years a man will read that page and he will want to know what time it was written and I am not going to be a man whose page cannot tell him.**"

---

**And at ten minutes past midnight the holder wrote in the first column of that book, in his own left hand, and the ship's hand was not used, and the reason against that is entered on the same line.**

"**And that at ten minutes past midnight on the said first of March the writ of the first of May past, being a municipal instrument of this harbor, inside that harbor line, bounded by the outer marks, carrying four names and no fifth, not the Second Bell license, leading to no license, renewing nothing, ceased to be a thing this party holds; and that no court sat on that day in that town or in any other town, and no clerk came up that hill, and nobody was charged with anything, and nobody was fined, and nobody was refused anything, and nobody was examined; and that the holder of that writ is a man of thirty-two in this town with a one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past, a bare bench, a drawer with four pieces of paper in it and a lamp, and a bag of fifty-eight marks, four shillings and two pence, being nine thousand three hundred and thirty pence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is eight thousand six hundred and ninety pence; and that what is gone is an instrument and not a man, and that a party which is not doing a thing on a day is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on.**"

She read that back and he read it after her and neither of them said anything for about two minutes.

"**Now tell me the thing you have been not saying since the Friday,**" she said.

"**It is that it is not a disaster.**

"**No. It is not, and I want to say why out loud before it gets written down, because in about four years a man is going to read this page and he is going to think that something happened to this yard on the first of March, and nothing happened. A man went down to a counter on the twenty-fourth and asked a clerk what he would be on the first of March and the clerk told him, and the clerk was right, and the clerk told him because a man ought to be told. That is the whole of it. That is four days of my life and about four hundred words of yours and a day at the seam on the Friday and a day at the seam today, and it bought us the knowledge that we are not going to be asked for anything, and that is the cheapest thing this yard has ever bought and it is the only thing in the last five months I would go back for.**"

"**And you are not writing a second paragraph about it, because the paragraph you want to write is a paragraph about how a man feels at midnight, and that is the second column, and I want to write it and I want you to stop me.**"

"**Write it in the second column,**" he said, "**and put the reason against the line, and do not put the reason in the first, and do not say the day of the week in it. A man on a mole can check what happened on this coast at midnight. A man on a mole cannot check what a man of thirty-two was, and there is no figure for that, and there has not been one since the sixth of December and there is not going to be one tonight.**"

---

*First column, entered on Monday the first day of March next at about twelve minutes past midnight in this shed: that at eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and came down bare, and that that was the forty-third Monday since the first of May past, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any other day of this book.*

*And that on the said Monday the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and that party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the second of the six rota days that that party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and that that is the twenty-fifth of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Sunday and was not occupied at any hour on the said Monday, and that the holder did not go over to him on the said Sunday and did not go over to him on the said Monday.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Sunday and the reason against that line is that a Sunday is the day the two of them have had since the twenty-first of February past; and that on the said Monday this yard worked from eight until about one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence.*

*And that at about half past eight in the evening on the said Monday the day's entries were read back from the top of that bench by the said woman and read back after her by the holder, and that the finding of the twenty-fourth day of February past was read back out of that first column in the yard's own words and not in a clerk's.*

*And that the said holder said that he was not going to write a line dated the first of March at half past eight in the evening, and that the reason against that is that in about four years a man will read that page and will want to know what time it was written, and that this party is not going to be a party whose page cannot tell him.*

*And that at ten minutes past midnight on the said first of March the holder entered in the first column in his own left hand, and that the said woman's hand was not used for it, and that the writ of the first of May past, being a municipal instrument of that harbor, inside that harbor line, bounded by the outer marks, carrying four names and no fifth, not the Second Bell license, leading to no license, and renewing nothing, ceased to be a thing this party holds; and that no court sat on that day in that town or in any other town, and no clerk came up that hill, and nobody was charged with anything, and nobody was fined, and nobody was refused anything, and nobody was examined; and that what is gone is an instrument and not a man; and that a party which is not doing a thing on a day is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on.*

*And that the said holder said out loud in that shed, before that line was written, that the said day is not a disaster, and that the reason is that a man went down to a counter on the twenty-fourth and asked a clerk what he would be on the first of March and the clerk told him and was right to tell him; and that that is the cheapest thing this yard has ever bought and is the only thing in the last five months the said holder would go back for.*

*And that on the said first of March, being the day that instrument ceased to be a thing this party holds, the four communities to which this yard wrote in the month of February past and this harbor and the other places on that coast that keep records of their own and that this yard has not been to are being told something by somebody, and that that somebody is not this yard; and that the one of those things this yard knows about came on Friday the nineteenth of February past into a room with two presses in a lane off that mole, from a man who paid in advance and gave a town and not a man, and was set in a day and a half at four shillings, and that the man in that room has entered it in his own book of twenty-two years, and that this yard has written to none of them, has sent nothing to any of them, and has sent nobody to any of them, and is not going to.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the first day of March next at about twenty minutes past midnight in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of the said writ is a man of thirty-two in this town with a charter, a bare bench, a drawer and a bag, and that a paper a man holds is not a thing he is, and that what is checkable about the said first of March is the four names, the outer marks, the hour and the absence of a court, and that what is not checkable is what a man of thirty-two was at ten minutes past midnight, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can go and look at a harbor line and cannot go and look at a man, and that there is no figure for a man and there is not going to be one.*

*And that the said day was not a disaster and that saying so is not a way of putting a good face on it, and that the reason against that line is that there was no paper to be asked for on that day and no office to ask and no clerk to send, and that a party which is not doing a thing on a day is not fined because a court does not fine a poor man.*

*And that on the said first of March five volumes' worth of a habit of treating an instrument as though it were a man came to an end at ten minutes past midnight on a mole in this town and nobody in this shed has improved on it and nobody is going to.*

*And that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four, are still nameless, are not struck out, and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that a line does not come out of a column because it has been printed and cannot be got back.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing on the said Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either of the two days, and fifty-eight marks, four shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is nine thousand three hundred and thirty pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is eight thousand six hundred and ninety pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Monday is three days at the face of the second seam, being one thousand four hundred and forty pence, being the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth days of February past and the first of March instant, all on hirers' own accounts, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man on every one of the three. The whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Monday is nothing at all, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and three pence in four transactions at two counters, being a penny on the eighteenth of December past, a penny on the twenty-eighth of January instant, a penny on the thirteenth of February instant, and two shillings to a bonesetter on the twentieth of that month. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and eighteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and all eighteen are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past runs to the last day of March next and has not been touched, and the first of March is not a date on it and is not a day anything happens to it. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. The rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
