# Chapter 233

*Ten Words in a Market, and a Slate That Came Down Bare*

Monday the eighth and Tuesday the ninth of February, at the head of this dock, and in the shed.

**At eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down, and it came down bare, and that was the fortieth Monday since the first of May, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on that day or on any day of that week that anybody has entered.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged on that Monday at no figure and this party walked none of it, as it has walked none of them since the third of August past, and that is the second of the six rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and it stays nil and it is not going to be improved on before the end of the month.**

**And the far bench stood empty on the said Monday and on the said Tuesday from first light to the going in of the market, and this party entered both of those and set nothing of what came before them against either.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Monday and none on the said Tuesday, and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against the Monday is that a slate came down at eight and this yard went and looked at it because a slate coming down bare is a thing that is in a book every Monday whether anybody looks at it or not, and the reason against the Tuesday is that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and this yard did not work it, and that the reason against that half of the line is the reason already standing against the wage in that column and is not given a second time here.**

---

**And on the Tuesday morning a woman of twenty-seven took the half sheet of a court in a city out of a drawer nine feet from where she was standing, and folded it once, and put it inside her coat, and went down that hill on her own, and said nothing to anybody about it, and the holder of the writ of the first of May did not go with her and stayed up that hill and did not ask her to, and the reason against that is on the page and the reason is that a form is not a thing two people answer.**

The market was on that Tuesday and it was a full one for the time of year, and there were about forty people in it and in the street at the head of the dock, and the boards were down behind them, and the boards are the boards of traders and this yard is not a name on any board in that market and has never been and is not going to be.

She stood where she could be seen, which was at the head of the dock and not on the boards, and she waited until the noise had gone down the way it goes down, and then she said ten words.

"**It is here. It was not sent on the day.**"

About forty people in that market heard all ten of them and nobody said anything, and the market went on being a market inside about four seconds, and a woman of about sixty who sells eels said to the man beside her that the man with the pump had lost something and the man beside her said that a market was not the place for it.

Then she put her hand up flat for about a second, which she does, and she said one more sentence, and it was not part of the ten and she said it afterwards and she said it to nobody in particular and it was heard by whoever was nearest.

"**That is all of it. Do not make a speech of it and do not write it on anything. There is nothing to sign.**"

And she turned round and went up the mole, and the holder of the writ of the first of May was at the top of it, and he did not speak in that street that morning, and this yard is not on the boards and did not speak and had nothing on the wall that day and put nothing on it and took nothing off it.

---

**And the price of that was named at the top of that bench at about two in the afternoon and it is not a small one and it is not going to get smaller.**

"**You said it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I said it.**

"**Then you have told about forty people in this market that this yard holds a copy of a record in a matter that stands before a court in a city, and that it was not produced on the fourth of January, and that it is in a drawer in this town, and that is the whole of what I said and every word of it is true and none of it can be taken back.

"**And Calder — a yard that has spent five volumes saying that a thing nobody can check is worth nothing has this morning, in about forty people's hearing, told this market where the thing it cannot check is.

"**That is the cost. I am not going to make it smaller than that by calling it a good answer. It is the best answer there was and it is the answer I would give again in the same words in the same street, and it has cost us the one thing this yard has spent four volumes saying it was not going to lose, which is the fact that nobody knows where this yard's papers are.

"**And I want it in the first column in that order. The answer first, at ten words, at about eleven in the morning, in about forty people's hearing, in the street, with the boards down. And the price second. And not one word of the price in a market and not one word of the price in a room.**"

"**Say the last part of it. You have not said it.**

"**I have. I am not going to say a second thing about the price and if I say a second thing I will improve on the first one, and a thing that has been improved on in a week is a thing that was not a price.

"**And there is one line in that half sheet in a drawer in this room that a market would have taken in about four seconds this morning and I did not say, and I am not going to say it, and I am not going to tell you what it is, and the reason is that a line on a piece of paper that came up a hill in the ordinary post is the paper's line and not this yard's, and this yard is not going to start putting a court's words into a market in its own mouth in about forty people's hearing. When that line is said in a market it will be said by whoever says it, and it will be said about a person and not about a yard, and this yard has no claim on it and is not going to take one.**"

"**Then the form.**

"**The form is answered and it is in the drawer with the other three and it is going to stay in the drawer, and there is nothing written on it and there is no name at the foot of it and there was no name at the foot of it when it came up this hill on the fourth of December.

"**And here is the whole of the last thing on that page and it is a fact and not a plan. That half sheet came up this mole in the ordinary post with a court's name at the head of it and the same court's name at the foot of it and no address on it anywhere on it. Whether it goes back in a week or whether it does not go back at all is not a thing anybody in this harbor can check, and this yard is not going to check it, and the reason is the reason of the fourth of October past and is not going to be given.

"**A man in this town can walk down to the post counter and ask whether a half sheet went out of this town on such a day, and if it did, where it went, and the man at that counter will not know and will have to go and find out, and a clerk in a city will be written to in a fortnight. And nobody in this shed is going to ask that man at that counter, and if this form does go back to that court in a week this yard will not know it until somebody in that city opens a book, and if it does not go back this yard will not know that either.

"**Both of those are true and neither of them is a resolution and I am not going to write one of them down as though it were.**"

---

**And a wage of four marks did not go in on the said Tuesday, and no envelope was made, and the column did not move, and the envelope count did not move, and the reason against both of those is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December past and is not improved on.**

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now SIXTEEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant, the nineteenth of January instant, the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second of February instant and the ninth of February instant, and all sixteen are entered in that book in the same breath and not one after the other.**

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the ninth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and came down bare, and that that was the fortieth Monday since the first of May past, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held.*

*And that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Monday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the second of the six rota days in the month of February that this party has entered walked on none of.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Monday nor at any hour on the said Tuesday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either of them.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Monday and none on the said Tuesday and the board was not written on either of them, and that the reason against the Monday is that a slate came down at eight and this party went and looked at it, and that the reason against the Tuesday is that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday.*

*And that on the said Tuesday morning the said woman took the half sheet of a court in a city out of a drawer nine feet from where she was standing and folded it once and put it inside her coat and went down that hill on her own and said nothing to anybody about it, and that the holder did not go with her and did not ask her to, and that the reason against that line is that a form is not a thing two people answer.*

*And that the market was on on the said Tuesday and that there were about forty people in it and in the street at the head of that dock, and that the boards were down behind them and that this yard is not a name on any board in that market; and that the said woman stood at the head of that dock and not on the boards, and that she said ten words, being *it is here, and it was not sent on the day*, and that about forty people heard all ten of them and nobody said anything, and that she then said one sentence that was not part of the ten, being that that was all of it and that there was nothing to sign; and that she went up that mole and the holder was at the top of it and did not speak in that street that morning, and that this yard did not speak and nothing went up on any wall in that market that day and nothing came off it.*

*And that the said woman said at about two in the afternoon on the said Tuesday that about forty people in that market now know that this yard holds a copy of a record in a matter standing before a court in a city and that it was not produced on the fourth of January past and that it is in a drawer in this town; and that she said that a yard which has spent five volumes saying that a thing nobody can check is worth nothing has told that market where the thing it cannot check is; and that she said that it is the best answer that was available and that she would give it again in the same words in the same street, and that it has cost this party the fact that nobody knows where its papers are.*

*And that the said woman said that there is a line in that half sheet in that drawer which a market would have taken in about four seconds and that she did not say, and that she is not going to say it and is not going to say what it is, and that the reason is that a line on a piece of paper that came up that hill in the ordinary post is the paper's line and not this yard's.*

*And that the said half sheet is answered and is in that drawer with the other three and has nothing written on it and no name at the foot of it and had no name at the foot of it when it came up that hill on the fourth of December past; and that whether it goes back in a week or does not go back at all is not a thing anybody in that harbor can check, and that this party is not going to check it, and gave the reason of the fourth of October past and did not give it.*

*And that a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on the said Tuesday and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is sixteen, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second of February instant and the said ninth of February instant.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the ninth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this party has answered a form of a court in a city in a market in about forty people's hearing, and that the answer was ten words and not a speech and not a defense and did not put the writ of the first of May anywhere near it, and that the reason against that line is that the only room this yard has ever had for saying a thing out loud with strangers in it is a street, and that this yard has not been to the counter of that circuit office since the twenty-fourth of November past and has asked no office for anything and has sent nothing to a city in aid of the fourth of January past and is not doing that now, and that a form which came up a hill in the ordinary post has been answered in a market and is going back into a drawer.*

*And that this party is entered against itself on the same page, being that about forty people now know where a copy of a record is, and that the reason against that line is the whole of the movement and is not going to be improved on and is not going to be answered in a market or in a room.*

**No money came into this yard on the said Monday and none on the said Tuesday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and thirty-four marks, six shillings and three pence is in the bag, which is five thousand five hundred and fifteen pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is four thousand eight hundred and seventy-five pence and it has not moved in six days. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and sixteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The slate wall came down bare on the fortieth Monday since the first of May past and no sitting was held and the clerk kept his own page. The cistern was charged at no figure on a rota day and this party walked none of it, and the walk is nil. The far bench was not occupied on either of the two days. The bench under that pump was bare for a tenth night on the Monday and an eleventh on the Tuesday. Nothing went up on any wall in this harbor in the twenty-three days ended on the said Tuesday and nothing came off, and the one paper on that wall is this yard's own fourth return under a sheet and is still the only paper on it that any office in any city is obliged to look at. Four returns are four and there is no fifth, and no finding of worked glass was made on either of the two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The third column is unfilled and unappointed and the boy's hand is at the head of it. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it has not been said out loud since the third of December past. A working harbor of this coast is poorer on the ninth of February next than it was on the fourth of it, and the tide gang is still four men short. Every other figure this harbor is owed is set out in the page above and is not read out again in this paragraph.**
