# Chapter 240

*The Forty-Second Wipe, Eighteen Weeks, and a Line in a Lane*

Monday the twenty-second and Tuesday the twenty-third of February, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**At eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was the forty-second Monday since the first of May, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and the five lines that were chalked on that wall on the eleventh of February came down with it and were not copied into the file by anybody, and this party has entered that and is not going to enter it again in this book.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged on that Monday at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the sixth and last of the six rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the twenty-third of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past, and the next rota day is a Monday, and this party has entered nothing about it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour, and Calder Marr did not go over to him on the Monday and did not go over to him on the Tuesday, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past and it is not going to be given out of that shed this month.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Monday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that the said woman has four stitches in her right hand and that the said Monday was a Monday, and that a yard of two does not work a Monday when one of the two cannot hold a line, and that is entered as a day the yard lost and not as a day the yard did not know about, and there is no figure against it because there is no figure for a day and there is not going to be one.**

**And at about eleven on the said Monday morning the holder of the writ went down that mole and into a lane off it and into a room with two presses in it, on his own, without being asked and without asking, and there was a man of forty-one pulling a stone and there was nothing on the stone but a funeral card and a bill of lading.**

"**I have come about a figure,**" said Calder Marr. "**One sheet. What is one sheet.**

"**A penny and fourpence, and I will not take less for setting a thing that has to be right and a man who wants it in a day. The thirty-eighth line is the one that costs. It is the same thirty-eighth line and I have had it up four times and every time it is the one that goes wrong, because it is the only line on the sheet that is asking a man to do a thing and men do not set lines that ask them to do things the way they set lines that are receipts. That is not a complaint. That is a compositor telling you what your paper costs.**

"**And I am telling you something else and I have been putting it off since the seventeenth and I am not going to put it off past the end of this month.

"**On the Friday of last week I took another order for that sheet from a man in a fifth place. He came in at about ten, paid me in advance again, which I do not do, and gave me a town and not a man, and asked for it in a clean hand, and I set it in a day and a half and I have charged him four shillings for it and that is my own figure at my own trade and not one penny more.

"**And I have set that sheet five times now. Once for you in August, twice for men in this town who cannot read, once in January for a man at a funeral, and once on Friday for a place I had never heard of. And I have got the price of a sheet of paper right, and the price of a day of my trade, and every figure in that book is a figure I put there myself.

"**And I have got no way whatever of writing down what your thirty-eighth line has done to my book, and I have looked for a line for it since the Tuesday, and there is not one, and I have been keeping that book for twenty-two years and I have never before wanted a line in it that I could not put a figure against.**"

"**Then we will not put one there,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I want that on the face of your book and I will not pay you for it and I am not going to send anybody to read it to you, and the reason is that a figure for that is not a figure you are owed and is not a figure I have got, and any man who can put a figure on it is selling something.

"**And I am going to say the rest of it here and not in a market, because you are the only person on this coast who has asked me the question, and you asked it with a stone and not with a book, and that is the better way.

"**The page is going to be in five places. You have set it five times and there are four of the sixty boats that have it and a man of fifty-five has read it out for a penny in four towns, and a fifth place came to you on Friday on its own, and none of them came to me. That is the whole of it. That is the discovery that this yard made on Friday night at the head of this dock from a man of sixty who makes sails, and I have not got a better version of it and I am not going to go looking for one.

"**A list of names is a list of who can be asked what. We printed it as evidence. It is an instruction. And five places are now acting on the instruction and this yard has not been asked about any of them and is not going to be, and there is no office on this coast I can go and ask whether that is allowed, and I have not got a second page to put that on.**"

**And the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and the wage of four marks did not go in, and no envelope was made, and neither the column nor the envelope count moved, and the reason is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December past and is not improved on.**

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now EIGHTEEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant, the nineteenth of January instant, the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second of February instant, the ninth of February instant, the sixteenth of February instant and the said twenty-third of February instant, and all eighteen are entered in that book in the same breath and not one after the other.**

**And on the said Tuesday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man and stayed, and the gross on that board was fourteen and eleven of the fourteen was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the day's book was written in the ship's hand on those boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and it took her about twice as long as it takes her.**

**And at about half past eight on the said Tuesday night, with the lamp lit, and the lamp turned round, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed, and the bench under that pump bare for a twenty-fourth night.**

She read it and he read it after her and neither of them read anything else for about four minutes, and the reason against that is the reason of the Friday of the nineteenth, which is that there is nothing left in that book to read out that is not one of the things they have been saying for twenty days.

"**Then say the one we have not said,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and it is a question and it is not a good one and I am going to say it anyway because it has been standing in the middle of that bench since Friday and nobody has picked it up and we are five days out of a month and the first of March is a Tuesday.

"**What does a yard owe for a line it printed in good faith and cannot get back.

"**Not what a line costs to print. There is a figure for that and a man in a lane has it and it is a penny and fourpence and it is a day and a half for the one that asks a man to do a thing, and that is a price and it is a real one and it is the only clean figure in this whole business.

"**What it owes. A man of sixty-two down that coast is a man of this yard in about forty people's hearing and is not one. A man of thirty is standing at a kitchen table in that same place and has a daughter of nineteen who goes to that kitchen and he has not been asked to read anything since the Wednesday. A woman of fifty-eight in a room off this market has had a page in her own back room for nine years that she made for a man who did not come back and has never looked at, and she told us about it on the Saturday because we asked her a question nobody had asked her in four months. A man of fifty-eight who sells timber has our days in three houses in a place four days up this coast and has put his name to a piece of paper about it, and we have not written to him and we are not going to.

"**Four people. And a man of forty-one in a lane off that mole has been looking for a line in his own book for a week that he cannot put a figure against, and he is the only man on this coast who has ever asked this yard that question, and he asked it with a stone in his hands.

"**And I am going to say the answer the way this book says answers, which is with a second-column fact and a reason against the line, and I am not going to say the reason.

"**The fact is: that yard printed thirty-eight lines on the sixth of August past because a clerk in a city in November said a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and that the only answer to that is a page a man can check, and that the thirty-eighth line is the only line in that sheet that is honest, and that the thirty-three names under it are the price of putting it there, and that the four gaps in the middle of July are the only instruction on the whole sheet and they were printed by a man who did not know what they were.

"**The reason against the line is the reason of the fourth of October past and it is not going to be given in this shed or in a market or in a room or in four years.

"**And that is the answer. I have got a fact and I have got no reason and I have got eighteen weeks of wage on that board and three marks that came into this bag this morning and forty-nine marks, four shillings and two pence in it, and a plate in eleven feet with a note against it that stands open and is not going to be closed by any of this, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench that neither brother has gone over to on any one of the twenty days, and five days between tonight and the first of March next.**"

"**Say the five days and then I am going to say the last line and the last line is not mine.**

"**There are five days between the twenty-third of February and the first of March. And the writ of the first of May runs to midnight on the first of March, is inside the harbor line, is bounded by the outer marks, carries four names and no fifth, is not the Second Bell license, does not lead to one, and renews nothing. And this yard is a party of one and a half until the first of March next, and since the Saturday the half of it has had a hand on it that cannot hold a line, and I am not putting that in a column as a figure because there is no figure for it and there is not going to be.

"**And I am not going to improve on the last line. You have had twenty days of it and I have had twenty days of it and neither of us has said it out loud since the third of February.

"**Here it is, and it is a fact, and a man can check it on any day of a week, and it is not a resolution and it is not a good place to stop.

"**The bench under that pump is bare. It has been bare for twenty-four nights, since a sheet and a half came apart in the ship's hand on that mole on the Sunday of the thirty-first of January, and it is the only bare thing in that shed, and the shape of that bench is the shape of a sheet and a half that came up this hill on foot on the ninth of December and came apart in your hand on the thirty-first and cannot be put back and cannot be bought.

"**The copy of a record of the twenty-fourth of November is nine feet away in a drawer in that room. The acknowledgment of the twenty-ninth of November is in the same drawer. The half sheet of a court in a city, which was answered in ten words in a market in about forty people's hearing on the ninth of February, is in the same drawer. The printer's letter of the twentieth of December is on that bench with a question in it that has not been answered and that this yard has the answer to and is not going to send.

"**And there are five places on this coast that have a copy of a page this yard printed in good faith, and this yard has not been to one of them, and has written to none of the nine, and has sent nobody anywhere, and has a bare bench and a drawer and a bag of forty-nine marks, four shillings and two pence and eighteen weeks of wage standing against it with no envelope in it.

"**That is the whole of what this yard is on the twenty-third of February. Nobody in this shed has improved on it and I am not going to be the first.**"
