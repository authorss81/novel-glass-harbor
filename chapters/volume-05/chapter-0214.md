# Chapter 214

*The Berth Nobody in That Crew Has Gone to See, and a Share With a Figure Missing Off It*

Friday the first and Saturday the second of January, in the shed, at the boards, and at the foot of the mole's steps.

**No day of work was booked on the Friday and none on the Saturday, and the board was not written on either of them, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened; and that the Friday is a day on which a day of work was offered to this yard and was not taken, and that is two things and the second of them is a cost.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.**

---

**And at about ten on the Friday morning at the foot of the mole's steps the man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own came up the hill a third time in a week and asked about the Saturday.**

Calder Marr got up those steps twice and sat down on the third of them, and the second time he was at the third step for about a minute and a half, and there is a reason against that in the second column of this yard's book and there is no name in it and there is not going to be one.

"**I cannot take the Saturday,**" he said.

"**You took Tuesday.**

"**I took Tuesday.**"

The man of the second lay stood on the bottom step and looked at him for a while, and then he said: "**Right. When.**"

"**I do not know. Come up on the Monday and ask.**"

"**I will ask on the Monday and I will not ask on the Monday if you look like that, because I have a boat and I have to lay it tomorrow whatever a yard on a hill does.**" He went down the hill. He did not say anything about it at the boards that the shipwright heard.

**The figure of three marks is entered against the said Saturday in the first column, in the holder's own hand, and against no day at all, and it was not earned, and it is not going to be entered twice, and the man of the second lay was not told that a figure was put against it because a man who is told a figure was put against a day he offered takes it personally and this yard is not going to be a thing a man takes personally.**

---

**And at about nine on the Friday morning at the boards the ship's hand was told a thing by a man of the third lay, and he told her and not the holder, and he said at the start that he was telling her because she writes things down and not because he wanted anything out of it. That is the second time a man has said that sentence to her at that board and she wrote it down both times.**

He was about forty and he had a boat of about forty tons of his own and he was one of three part owners in it, and the three of them had been part owners in it since the year before last.

"**My boat is short a hand since the fifteenth of May,**" he said. "**Not a day. Since the fifteenth of May. It has been short a hand for eight months and I have not said it out loud to anybody in this harbor because there is nothing to say out loud about it in a street.**

"**It was short a hand because your boy was on my boat in June.**"

Nessa Pike did not look up from the slate.

"**He was a hand on my boat in the last week of June,**" the man said, "**and he was a good hand and I paid him the same as the man he took, which was the same as anybody, and I did not know he was your boy until the middle of July when a man on the boards said so, and I did not stop him being on my boat and I am not going to go and say anything to him now either, and you can have the whole of that for nothing.**

"**He is not on my boat now. He has not been since the middle of July. My boat has been one hand short since the middle of July and it has been two nights short twice, and the three of us have been making it up out of the shares, and I am telling you because you are going to hear the figure off somebody else's mouth in about a month and I would rather you had it off mine.**"

"**What is the figure.**"

"**Since the middle of July I have taken out of my own share, out of the three shares, four marks, one shilling and eightpence, and that is the pay of a hand and his victuals and nothing else, and I have not had it back and I am not going to have it back, and the other two have paid their halves and neither of them has said anything about it either.**

"**Four marks, one shilling and eightpence is six hundred and sixty pence,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I am going to write it down and I am going to write down that it came out of a share and not out of a day's work, and those are not the same thing and I am not going to let them be the same thing in a book.**"

"**That is the whole of it. I have not come up here to ask you for it and I am not going to come up here to ask you for it in a month either.**"

"**Nobody is going to ask you for it. Do you know that? Nobody in this yard is going to ask you for a penny of it and I want that said in front of the man who is not here.**"

The man of the third lay looked at the boards for a while.

"**There is a thing you are not going to ask me,**" he said, "**and I will tell you what it is because you will get it off somebody else. There is a boy who was in that boat in June and there are four men in that crew now and not one of them has gone to see him. Not one. And I am one of the four and I have not gone, and I am telling you that I have not gone, because you are going to hear it said that a crew went to look after its own, and nobody went.**"

"**This yard has not gone either,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I did not say you had. I said not one of us.**"

---

**And at about half past eight on the Saturday night in the shed the two of them were there and the lamp was lit and neither of them read anything back, and neither of them said why, and the reason is in the book and is that a thing that four men in a boat have not done is not a thing two people in a shed are going to do for them, and it does not want reading back.**

The three nots of the eighth of June past stand. Forty marks stands whole to a boy of twenty-two and has stood whole since the fourteenth day of October past, and has not been reduced by a penny and has not been paid by a penny and is not going to be paid out of anything that comes into this yard. The head of the third column keeps that boy's hand and that column has not been filled since the first of August past and nobody has been appointed to it, and nobody is going to be appointed to it in this batch.

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the second day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on the said Friday or on the said Saturday and that the board was not written on either day, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened, and that the said Friday is a day on which a day of work was offered to this yard and was not taken, and that those are two things and that the second of them is a cost.*

*And that at about ten on the said Friday morning at the foot of that mole's steps a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own came up that hill a third time in a week and asked about the said Saturday, and that the holder got up those steps twice and sat down on the third of them and was on the third step the second time for about a minute and a half, and that the reason against that is in the second column of this book and that there is no name in it and that there is not going to be one; and that the holder said that he could not take the said Saturday, and that the said man said right, and asked when, and that the holder said he did not know and told him to come up on the Monday and ask, and that the said man said he would ask on the Monday and would not ask on the Monday if the holder looked like that, because he had a boat to lay; and that the said man went down the hill and said nothing at the boards that the ship's hand heard.*

*And that the figure of three marks is entered against the said Saturday, in the holder's own hand, in this column, and against no day at all, and that it was not earned, and that it is not entered a second time, and that the said man of the second lay was not told that a figure had been put against the day he offered, and that the reason against that is that a man who is told a figure was put against a day he offered takes it personally, and that this yard is not going to be a thing a man takes personally.*

*And that at about nine on the said Friday morning at the boards the ship's hand was told a thing by a man of the third lay, about forty years old, with a boat of about forty tons of his own, one of three part owners in it, and that he told her and not the holder and said at the start that he was telling her because she writes things down and not because he wanted anything out of it, and that this is the second time a man has said that sentence to her at that board and that she wrote it down both times.*

*And that the said man said that his boat is short a hand since the fifteenth of May past and not since a day, and that it has been short a hand for eight months, and that he has not said it out loud to anybody in this harbor because there is nothing to say out loud about it in a street; and that the said hand was short because a boy was on that boat in June; and that the boy was a hand on that boat in the last week of June and that he was paid the same as the man he took, which was the same as anybody, and that the said man did not know he was anybody's boy until the middle of July past, when a man on the boards said so, and that he did not stop him being on his boat and is not going to go and say anything to him now, and that the said woman said the whole of that could be had for nothing.*

*And that the boy is not on that boat and has not been since the middle of July past, and that the said boat has been one hand short since the middle of July past and two nights short twice, and that the three part owners have been making it up out of the shares, and that the said man said that he was telling the ship's hand because she was going to hear the figure off somebody else's mouth in about a month and he would rather she had it off his.*

*And that the figure is four marks, one shilling and eightpence, being six hundred and sixty pence, and that it came out of a share and not out of a day's work, and that it is the pay of a hand and his victuals and nothing else, and that it has not been had back and is not going to be had back, and that the other two part owners have paid their halves and neither of them has said anything about it; and that the said woman entered that the figure is entered in this column and is entered as coming out of a share and not out of a day's work, and that those are not the same thing and are not going to be the same thing in this book; and that the said man said that he had not come up the hill to ask for it and was not going to come up the hill to ask for it in a month either, and that the said woman said that nobody is going to ask him for it, and that she said that in front of the man who was not in the shed.*

*And that the said man of the third lay then said that there is a thing the ship's hand is not going to ask him, and said what it was: that there is a boy who was in that boat in June, and that there are four men in that crew now, and that not one of them has gone to see him, and that he is one of the four and has not gone, and that he said it because the yard was going to hear it said that a crew went to look after its own and that nobody went; and that the said woman said that this yard has not gone either; and that the said man said that he did not say she had and that he said not one of them.*

*And that the holder entered on the said day, in his own left hand and with the date and the hour on it, that this party did not go, and did not write, and did not send anybody, and did not ask anybody who has seen him what he said; and that the reason against that line is the reason of the fourth day of October past and is not a new one and has not been invented again; and that the said reason in this yard's own words is that a yard which goes and looks at a thing is a yard which has begun asking, and that a yard which asks is a yard whose questions are in a book, and that this yard entered on the fourth day of October past that it was not going to invent a second reason, and that a second reason is not invented here and will not be invented in a month either; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that any man in this town can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask the two of them whether they went, and be told they did not, and that the two of them went down to the boatyard in the next town down this coast in September past and are the only two people in this harbor who have never asked a man in it a question about himself.*

*And that the read-back was not done on the said Saturday night, and that the reason against that line is that a thing which four men in a boat have not done is not a thing two people in a shed are going to do for them, and that it does not want reading back.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the second day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder got up the steps of that mole twice on the said Friday morning and sat down on the third of them the second time for about a minute and a half, and that the reason against that line is not checkable from a mole and has no name in it and is not going to have one, and that the cost of it is a day of about three marks that was offered and not taken, and that the figure is in the first column and not in this one, and that a man can check the figure and cannot check the reason; and that the three nots of the eighth day of June past stand and are entered again here because a chapter is a day and a day has to carry what was on it, and are not struck out, and the forty marks to that boy stands whole and is not reduced and is not paid; and that what a harbor loses when a boy of twenty-two is off the stone since the fifteenth of May past and is not replaced is not a figure anybody can add up and is a hand, and a hand is a person, and this party is not going to price a person; and that the four people of this coast who could say what that costs are not going to be asked; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**No money came into this yard on the first of January and none on the second, and nothing went out of it on either day, and ten marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and that figure has not moved since the twenty-ninth of December past. A day of about three marks was offered on the first of January instant and was not taken, and the figure of it is on the page and against no day at all, and four marks and one shilling and eightpence came out of a man's share in a boat of about forty tons on the fifteenth of May past and is not coming back to this yard and is not being asked for by anybody. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks on it stand where they have stood for months at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. That shed has had nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth day of April, and it has none this week. A man of Ashtide is owed three marks for the eighth of August past and has been owed it since; he is not named in this book, nobody has been sent to him, and nobody is going. Seven pence is owing to this yard's own shipwright and has not been paid and is not going to be paid out of a sheet of paper at a penny. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is printed here, and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. This harbor owes eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of it, at her own asking, and her name is on that figure and stays on it. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps that boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August past and nobody has been appointed to it and nobody is going to be appointed to it in this batch. Forty marks, first after food, to a person of no office, owing since the fifteenth of October past and untouched since; she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked and is not going to. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks stand in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, and ten weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The far bench was not occupied on either of these two days at any hour and the line carries no figure of what came before it. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and three days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
