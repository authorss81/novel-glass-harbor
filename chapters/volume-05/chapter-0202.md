# Chapter 202

*A Form With No Date on It, and a Man Who Is Not the Man Who Directs It*

Tuesday the eighth and Wednesday the ninth of December, in the shed, and on the mole.

**On Tuesday the eighth of December the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now SEVEN, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth day of August past, the week of Tuesday the fifth day of September past, the week of Tuesday the sixth day of October past, the week of Tuesday the third day of November past, the week of Tuesday the seventeenth day of November past, the week of Tuesday the first day of December past, and the week of Tuesday the eighth day of December instant, and all seven are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all seven at once rather than the one that is easier.**

Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and the column does not move on a Tuesday on which nothing is paid. Twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and the envelope count advances with the column and only with it. Four marks and six shillings and five pence in the bag, which is seven hundred and seventeen pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of those, and the figure the bag was short of the wage is seventy-seven pence.

---

**And at about eleven in the morning of Wednesday the ninth, a man came up the mole on foot, in a coat that had been made for weather and had been in weather, with nothing in his hands until he was inside the doorway of the shed, and he did not give his name.**

He gave the name of the office at the foot of the sheet he was carrying, and then he said why he was not going to give his own, and the reason was the reason a clerk of that circuit office gave at this bench on the twenty-third of November and the yard has it in the first column in two hands.

"**A man's name at the foot of a thing is a thing a man in a city can hold for four years,**" he said. "**I am not the man who directs this and you are not going to be told who is, and I have walked two days to put a sheet on a bench and I am not going to spend the walk being a name on it.**"

"**Then sit down,**" said Nessa Pike, "**because I have got about six things to say and I am not going to say any of them standing up in a door.**"

He put the sheet on the bench.

It is a sheet and a half, and there is no signature anywhere on it, and there is no date on it, and at the foot of it, where a signature would be if this were the sort of document that had one, there is the name of an office. **The name of that office is not on any page of this yard's book and it is not printed here, and the reason is on the page and is not a defect: the sheet is the form of an office in a city of the same kind as the form that put its name at the foot of the acknowledgment of the twenty-ninth of November and signed nothing, and this yard has entered that it does not know what office it is and is not going to guess in a book that a man in a city is going to read.**

> *To the holder of a provisional writ of salvage working granted at Morrow Quay on the first of May instant, and to any person in this place who is keeping a copy of a record, —*
>
> *It is proposed that one copy of a record be held in this place for the use of this coast, and that the holder of it be named in the entry of it as the person who holds it, and that the entry be made now and not on any later day.*
>
> *The office which makes this proposal has the keeping of records of this kind in a city and has had the keeping of them for three hundred years, and it is not able to make a copy safe in a place where the copy cannot be checked, and it does not say that a copy is unsafe anywhere else. It says it does not know about anywhere else.*
>
> *The party to whom this is addressed is not asked to be examined, to be licensed, to be enrolled, to be paid, or to find anything. It is asked one question. Will you hold a copy of a record for the coast. And it is asked to have the answer entered in the form of an entry, and the entry will carry the name of the party, the place, and the date of the answer, and the date of the answer is the only date on this sheet.*
>
> *This office has not asked this party for anything before and is not asking it now.*
>
> *This office says on the face of this sheet that a party which holds a copy of a record becomes the address of that copy; that a copy which has been made cannot be unmade; and that an address, once it is an address, can be found.*
>
> *This sheet does not say what follows an answer of no. This sheet does not say what an address is used for. This sheet does not say what an office in a city is going to do with the name of a party in a working harbor, and this office is not going to say it in a form, and if a man asks it in a room the answer will be that the office does not decide that and does not direct it either.*
>
> *There is no date at the head of this sheet. There is no date in the body of it. There is no date at the foot of it where a signature would be. There is the name of an office.*

Calder Marr read it twice, and the second time he read it out loud in a flat voice, and he did not read the man's name because there was not one on it.

"**Now say the one sentence,**" said the man from the door, "**because I have said everything else on the paper and there is one thing on the paper that I have to say with my mouth or I have not said it.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**Testimony is safe in one place and nowhere else, and this harbor is not that one, and that is not an opinion, it is what three hundred years of an office is.**"

Nobody in that shed said it was a lie.

Nobody in that shed said the man was anything at all, which is the thing about eleven people say about a sentence they cannot get past and have not been asked about.

"**And the rest of it is the sheet,**" said the man, "**and I am going to say the two things I am able to say and then I am going to go down that mole, because I have got a road to walk and the tide is on the other side of it.**

"**I am not the man who directs this and I have told you that.**

"**And the second thing is this. That is a form and there is no date on it, and I have put it on your bench, and I do not know what picking up a sheet off a bench is, and the office that made the sheet does not say either, and I have asked and I was told that the office does not decide that.**"

"**Then you have left a piece of paper on a bench,**" said Nessa Pike, "**in a shed, in a working harbor, with a pump under it, and you have walked two days to do it, and neither of us has said one word about whether we are holding anything.**"

"**I have not left a thing in your keeping,**" said the man. "**I have left a sheet of paper. If that is a custody then your office is going to be very busy and I would rather it were not mine.**"

He went down the mole and nobody followed him, which is a thing this yard does not do with a man who has come up a hill.

**And at about half past eight on the Wednesday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the sheet squared to the edge of the pump the way she puts a sheet down, they read it out from the top of the bench one more time, and the holder read it back after her, and nobody else was in the room.**

"**Right,**" said Nessa Pike. "**I have been in this business nine years and I have read a great many forms and I know what a form is for, and I am going to tell you what this one is for, and then you are going to tell me I am wrong.**

"**This one is for a name. That is the whole of it. There is no money on it, there is no term on it, there is no instrument on it, there is not a single line on it that says what a person gets for it. It is a form with a hole cut in it the shape of a party's name, and everything else on the sheet is there to make the hole look like a courtesy.**

"**And the reason they want the name is on the fourth paragraph in their own words and they have been very careful to put it in their own words, and it is that a party which holds a copy of a record becomes the address of that copy, and a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and an address can be found.**

"**And that is true, and I have been sitting here since Wednesday morning trying to make it not true, and I cannot.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**The rest of it is that a room is a building and an address is a name,**" she said. "**We have spent four volumes being careful about rooms. Every single thing we have done since the third of May has been about a room — what goes in a room, what does not go in a room, who is in a room, who is not, who is standing at the back of a room, and what a reason does when it gets out of a room. And we have got very good at it. We wrote eleven lines on a Sunday in November of the things that are not going into a room and a clerk read the eleven lines back twice and did not ask about the eleventh of them.

"**And there is no list in this yard that says what does not go into a name.**"

She squared the sheet again, which it did not need.

"**And I am not going to say the thing I want to say, because I have said it in this shed once already and I am not going to say it twice in one month. Write down what I have said and the reason and stop there.**"

*First column, entered on Wednesday the ninth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on Tuesday the eighth instant was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the bag held four marks and six shillings and five pence in it on the morning of that day, which is seven hundred and seventeen pence, and that a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and that the figure the bag was short of the wage is seventy-seven pence; and that the column of this book stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest, and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is twenty, and that an envelope that is not made is not a wage and this column is not the wage; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is seven, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past and the eighth of December instant, and that the seven figures are seven different figures and none of them is either of the others; and that the reason against the line is that this yard entered on the fourth instant that no item on this harbor's list of what a yard owes is to be paid, advanced, reduced or improved in this month, and that the wage is on that list.*

*And that on Wednesday the ninth instant at about eleven in the morning a man came up the mole on foot in a coat that had been in weather, with nothing in his hands until he was in the doorway of this shed, and that he did not give his name and gave the name of the office at the foot of a sheet he was carrying, and that he gave the reason he was not giving his own, which is that a man's name at the foot of a thing is a thing a man in a city can hold for four years, and that this yard has that reason in its first column already in two hands and entered it again; and that the said sheet is a sheet and a half and carries no signature anywhere and no date anywhere, and that at the foot of it, where a signature would be if it were the sort of document that had one, there is the name of an office; and that the name of that office is not on any page of this yard's book, and that the sheet is the form of an office in a city of the same kind as the form that put its name at the foot of the acknowledgment of the twenty-ninth day of November past and signed nothing, and that this yard has entered that it does not know what office it is and is not going to guess it in a book that a man in a city is going to read.*

*And that the said sheet states that it is proposed that one copy of a record be held in this place for the use of this coast and that the holder of it be named in the entry of it as the person who holds it, and that the entry be made now and not on any later day; and that the office which makes the proposal has the keeping of records of that kind in a city and has had the keeping of them for three hundred years, and that it is not able to make a copy safe in a place where the copy cannot be checked, and that it does not say a copy is unsafe anywhere else and says it does not know about anywhere else; and that the party to whom it is addressed is not asked to be examined, to be licensed, to be enrolled, to be paid or to find anything, and that it is asked one question, which is whether it will hold a copy of a record for the coast, and that the answer is to be entered in the form of an entry carrying the name of the party, the place and the date of the answer, and that the date of the answer is the only date on the said sheet; and that the said sheet states that that office has not asked this party for anything before and is not asking it now; and that the said sheet states on its own face that a party which holds a copy of a record becomes the address of that copy, that a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and that an address, once it is an address, can be found; and that the said sheet does not say what follows an answer of no, does not say what an address is used for, and does not say what an office in a city is going to do with the name of a party in a working harbor, and that that office is not going to say it in a form, and that if a man asks it in a room the answer will be that the office does not decide that and does not direct it either; and that there is no date at the head of the said sheet, none in the body of it and none at the foot of it, and that there is the name of an office.*

*And that the said man said, with his mouth and not on the said sheet, that testimony is safe in one place and nowhere else and that this harbor is not that one, and that that is not an opinion and is what three hundred years of an office is; and that the said man said he was not the man who directs it; and that the said man said he did not know what picking up a sheet off a bench is and that the office that made the sheet does not say either, and that he had asked and had been told that the office does not decide that; and that nobody in that shed said the said sentence was a lie and nobody said anything at all about the said man; and that the said man went down the mole at about half past eleven in the morning and that nobody followed him, which is a thing this party does not do with a man who has come up a hill.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the ninth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand said in this shed on the evening of the said day that the whole of the said sheet is for a name, and that there is no money on it and no term on it and no instrument on it and not one line on it that says what a person gets for it, and that it is a form with a hole cut in it the shape of a party's name and that everything else on it is there to make the hole look like a courtesy; and that she said that a party which holds a copy of a record becomes the address of that copy, and that this is true, and that she had been in the shed since the morning trying to make it not true and could not; and that she said that a room is a building and an address is a name, and that this yard has spent four volumes being careful about rooms, and that there is no list in this yard that says what does not go into a name; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill and look at the said sheet and ask the man who carried it where he came from, and that what is going to be done with a name in a city is not checkable from a mole, and that the whole of what a party is in four years to the four people of this coast it did not tell about is not checkable from a mole either.*
