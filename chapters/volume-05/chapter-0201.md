# Chapter 201

*The First Morning, and a Counter at Thirty-One*

Sunday the sixth and Monday the seventh of December, in the shed, and at the mole's steps.

**There was no day of work booked on the Sunday and the board was not written, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened, and which is the first day of a month in which this yard is going to have to be able to be checked on any day of a week by anybody who can get off a mole.**

**And on the Sunday morning there were three sheets of paper on the bench in that shed where the pump used to be, and none of them was glass and none of them was a tool, and they had all been there for longer than a day and none of them had been put away.**

One sheet, one page and a half, six heads, a clerk's hand, dated the twenty-fourth day of November, which is the date of a counter and not the date of a record. One half sheet with no signature anywhere and the name of an office at the foot of it, dated the twenty-ninth. And one half sheet with the name of a court in a city at the head of it and the name of that same court at the foot of it, dated the fourth of December, which is Friday last.

"**Three sheets,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and about two and a half of them are about a thing that has already happened, and the third one is about a day in January, and there is not one of the three that this yard can check.**

"**Say the day,**" she said, before he had answered anything.

"**The fourth of January.**" Calder Marr did not look up from the bench. "**A copy of a record is to be produced to a court in a city on the fourth of January, and a party which does not produce it is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on. That is the sixth sentence of a half sheet and it is the sentence the whole thing turns on and I have read it about nine times since Friday and I have not got better at it.**"

"**How many days.**"

"**Twenty-nine.**"

"**And how many of those are Tuesdays.**"

"**Four.**" He put his thumb on the page and did not move it. "**The eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth. A wage is on a Tuesday and it has not been paid on any Tuesday since the seventeenth of November and it is not going to be paid on any of those four, and I entered on the fourth of December that no item on this harbor's list of what a yard owes is going to be paid in this month, and the wage is on that list, and the four of those Tuesdays are going to go by with a figure written against them and no envelope made, and that is a fact about a Tuesday and not a failure, and I have written it down four times in four months and I am not going to write it a fifth time in a better way.**"

"**Then what are we going to do about the fourth of January.**"

"**Nothing,**" he said, "**and I am going to say the whole of why, and then I am going to stop, because in about four years there is going to be a page with a paragraph on it and I would rather the paragraph is the same one I am saying now.**

"**This party has not asked any office for anything in aid of that day. This party has not sent to a city. This party has not written to a man in a coat whose name is on no page of this book, and this party has not been to the counter of that circuit office since the twenty-fourth of November, and the reason for all four of those is one reason and not four: a thing you do in order to be ready is a thing that arrives with a date on it. Every office that has ever got a letter out of this yard has got it because this yard asked for something, and if this yard writes to anybody this month about the fourth of January then a clerk in a city has a piece of paper with a date on it and this yard's name at the top of it, and the piece of paper will be in a file whatever happens to the yard.**

"**So we do nothing. And doing nothing is not a trick and it is not clever, and it is the only thing in this shed I am sure of, and I will put it in the first column in my own hand tonight with the date and the hour on it, and you may read it back to me after I have written it and not before.**"

Nessa Pike put her right hand flat on the edge of the bench, which is the thing she does with a hand when she is going to leave it where it is.

"**And the first of March.**"

"**Goes in the same paragraph, and for the same reason, and it is a different paragraph.**" He did not move the thumb. "**The writ of the first of May runs to midnight on the first of March. Four names and no fifth. It is not the Second Bell license, it does not lead to one, it renews nothing, and the only office that may issue it or renew it is the harbor authority of this place, and that office is not the office before that court in a city and has said in its own instrument that it is not. The second season is a named absence. A sheet came up this hill on Friday last that says in writing that nothing in it touches it, grants it, refuses it or deals with it.**

"**So I have two dates in front of me and neither of them is mine. The fourth of January is a date in a form of a court in a city. The first of March is a date in this yard's own instrument and it is the only one of the two that a man in this harbor can put his finger on. And this yard is going to carry both of them and is not going to join them, and I entered that on Friday night in the first column in the sentence I have not improved on since and I am not going to improve on it now.**"

"**Then you have nothing to do for twenty-nine days,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and the two of us are a party of one and a half until the first of March, and there is a day coming on the twelfth at about three marks, and you are going to have to be able to say what this yard does with a day.**"

"**I am going to book it,**" he said.

"**You are going to book it with a man in it,**" she said, "**because that is the rule you entered in my hand on the twenty-first of November and neither of us has improved on it. And on the twenty-ninth of this month there is a Tuesday, and on the fifteenth there is a Tuesday, and on the eighth there is a Tuesday, and on every one of those four there is a figure going into this book with a word against it that says the wage was not paid. And I have been up since five thinking about a form.**

"**And I have got to this and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it again this month.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**The question a man of the first lay asked in this market on the second of December is standing.**" She said it flatly, the way she says a figure. "**It is not going away because nobody in this harbor has asked it a second time. It is in a second column of this book with a reason against the line and no name in it, and the reason is that this yard does not know, and the yard said the same in this market on the thirteenth of August, and the yard is not going to say it a second time about the same thing, and the yard is not going to say it in a book either, and a market and a book are two different rooms and the refusal is the same refusal in both and I am not going to be the one who improves on it.**

"**And I have not asked it. I am not going to ask it. And if somebody asks it in a market in January the answer is going to be the answer that is already on this page and it is not going to be a better one, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it.**"

Nobody said anything about that for a while, and out past the door the tide was making somewhere on the far side of the mole and nobody in that shed could hear it.

**On Monday the seventh of December the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-first Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it.**

**And at about ten on that Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and has been since June, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the Monday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and the said man was not at that bench on the Sunday at any hour.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Monday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in that shed, the whole of the two days went into the first column and one line of it went into the second, and the one had a reason against it.**

*First column, entered on Monday the seventh day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on Sunday the sixth instant or on Monday the seventh instant and that the board was not written on either day, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and that the said day is the first of a month in which this party is to be able to be checked on any day of a week by any man who can get off a mole; and that on the Sunday morning there were three sheets of paper on the bench in this shed, being a provisional public copy record of the twenty-fourth day of November past in six heads and about a page and a half, a sheet of an office of the twenty-ninth day of November past with no signature anywhere and the name of an office at the foot of it, and a sheet of a court in a city of the fourth day of December instant with the name of that court at the head of it and at the foot of it, and that the said three sheets have been on that bench since before the said day and none of them has been put away, and that none of the three can be checked by any man in this harbor; and that the holder entered on the said day, in his own left hand and with the date and the hour on it, that a copy of the said record is to be produced to that court on the fourth of January next and that this party will do nothing in aid of that day, and gave the whole of the reason, which is that this party has not asked any office for anything in aid of it, has not sent to a city, has not written to a man in a coat whose name is on no page of this book, and has not been to the counter of the copying room of that circuit office since the twenty-fourth day of November past, and that the reason for all four of those is one reason and not four, which is that a thing done in order to be ready is a thing that arrives with a date on it, and that in about four years a clerk in a city would have a piece of paper with a date on it and this party's name at the head of it, and that the piece of paper would be in a file whatever happened to the yard; and that the said sheet is entered in this column and not in the second because a man in this harbor can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask to see it and can go to the post and ask on what day a letter came up this road.*

*And that the holder entered on the said day that the writ of the first of May instant runs to midnight on the first of March next, carries four names and no fifth, is not the Second Bell license, does not lead to one and renews nothing, and that the only office which may issue it or suspend it after an inspection for cause or renew it is the harbor authority of this place, and that that office has said in its own instrument that it is not the office before that court in a city, and that the second season of that writ is a named absence and that the sheet of the fourth instant says in writing that nothing in it touches, grants, refuses or deals with it; and that the holder entered that this yard is carrying two dates and is not going to join them, and that the fourth of January is a date in a form of a court in a city and the first of March is a date in this yard's own instrument and is the only one of the two a man in this harbor can put his finger on.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the seventh instant, being the thirty-first Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Monday, and this party walked it, and the walk is nil; and that the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that the holder did not go over to him, and that the said man was not at that bench on the Sunday at any hour.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the seventh day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this party is to carry twenty-nine days between this day and the fourth of January next without acting on the day itself, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a party of one and a half that is not paying a wage cannot be idle for twenty-nine days without something happening to it that it did not choose, and that what happens to it in those twenty-nine days is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the two of them and the four people of the Sill, who wrote a line in the month of August and who have not been asked and are not going to be.*

**No money came into this yard on the Sunday and none on the Monday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and four marks and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is seven hundred and seventeen pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of those, and the figure the bag is short of the wage on the Tuesday of this week is seventy-seven pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven, and the yard is not going to say that note out loud a fourth time and did not say it in these two days. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, six weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and a seventh will fall due on the eighth instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and seventy-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter was written about it in these two days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at.**
