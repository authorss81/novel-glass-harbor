# Chapter 241

*What the Holder of the Writ Is on the First of March*

Wednesday the twenty-fourth and Thursday the twenty-fifth of February, in the shed, on the face of the second seam, and at the counter of the harbor authority on the west basin.

**At about ten on the said Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the first of the six rota days that this party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the twenty-fourth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder did not go over to him on either day and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past and is not going to be given out of that shed this month.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that the said woman has four stitches in her right hand and cannot hold a line and cannot hold a pencil, and that a Wednesday is not a day a yard of two takes off when there is nothing to take off, and that that is a day this yard lost and not a day this yard did not know about, and there is no figure against it because there is no figure for a day.**

---

**And on the Wednesday afternoon the two of them went down that mole and along the front and up onto the west basin to the counter of this harbor's own authority, at about half past two, and asked one question, and the clerk of this harbor's own authority answered it, and this party did not go to the counter of the circuit office and has not been to it since the twenty-fourth of November past and did not go to it on the said day.**

The clerk was a man of about fifty with a cold and a book open on the counter, and this office owes him two days, which this party knows about and has not been asked about and did not raise.

"**You are the holder of the writ. I will read you the two you are asking about, and I am going to read the second one and you are going to want me to stop after the first, and I am not going to, because if I stop after the first one you will go away up that hill with a thing in your head that is wrong, and I have enough in this office that is wrong without adding a clerk's memory of a conversation.**

"**The writ of the first of May past is a municipal instrument of this harbor. It is inside the harbor line. It is bounded by the outer marks. It carries four names and there is no fifth name on it and there is not going to be one. It is not the Second Bell license. It does not lead to one. It renews nothing. It runs to midnight on the first of March next. And this office is the office that issued it, and this office said on the face of it, in its own words, that it is not the office that can renew it. So at midnight on the first of March that paper stops being a thing you hold, and nothing takes its place, and nobody is charged with anything, and no court sits, and nobody is fined, and no clerk comes up that hill. You will not be asked for it. Nobody will be told that you have not got it. It is not a thing there is a penalty for not having.**

"**That is the whole of that one and I have said it as it is and I am not going to improve on it for you.**

"**The other one is the charter, and you did not ask me about the charter and I am going to read it anyway.**"

"**We asked about both.**

"**You asked about one and you touched the other, and there is a difference and you have lived inside it for five months.**"

"**The one-season provisional working charter is dated the twenty-eighth of November past, was granted in the room over the tide-gang store, is on harbor ground inside the line, is bounded by the outer marks and by four hundred yards off them. The freed shoal iron is its bond at a clerk's thirteen and has not been sold and cannot be sold without my office's hand on a page. The eighth and the harbor's share follow you in anything you raise and the crew's share is yours. It may be suspended by this office at any time after an inspection and for cause, and this office has no cause and has not looked for one. It runs to the last day of March.**

"**And it is a different paper from the writ, and it is not the writ, and it is not the Second Bell license, and it does not lead to one, and I am putting those four things in one another's hearing on purpose, because a man who has one of them and not the other one is a man who will tell a room that he has been refused, and he has not.**"

"**We have not said that to anybody.**

"**I know you have not. I am telling you what the paper is and not what you have said. And the fourth thing on that sheet, and I will read it because it is the reason I have said the other three, is that a party which does not produce a thing on a day is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on. That sentence is on the form and it is not mine, and I have never had an office that told me to say it and I would say it if there was not one.**"

He shut the book and did not shut it loudly, and told them the cistern rota was a Monday and a Thursday, and said he would rather they had that from the counter than find it out.

---

**And on the said Thursday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump up that hill and brought the man with him and stayed, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the day's book was written in the ship's hand on those boards at about half past one and not afterwards and it took her about twice as long as it takes her, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence.**

**And at about half past eight on the said Thursday night, with the lamp lit and the lamp turned round, the day's entries were read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed, and the finding of the Wednesday was read back out of the first column in the yard's own words and not in a clerk's.**

"**And you will tell me where it is wrong,**" said the holder, "**because it is going in a book and it is going to be read in about four years by somebody who was not in that room, and I have never once in five volumes read back a thing I could not put a figure against.**

"**There are three places and one of them is in the counter and not in the book,**" said Nessa Pike. "**You have written that the clerk told you the rota. He did not tell you the rota. He told you he would rather you had it from the counter than find it out. Those are not the same thing, and one of them is a man doing you a kindness and the other is a man doing his duty, and in about four years the second one is the one worth having in that book.**

"**And the third place is the one you cannot hear, because you have been turning it over since Wednesday night. You have put *is not a thing that has happened to anybody in this town* into the second column, and that is your sentence and not the counter's, and there is a line in this yard's own book that has been standing since the fourth of December with a number on it that neither of us has read out loud, and you have just walked up to it in the dark. Put mine on the page and not yours. My sentence is that on the first of March at midnight the holder of the said writ is a man of thirty-two in this town with a charter and a bare bench and a drawer, and then the first column, and then the second column, and the second column is where it goes because a man on a mole can check the four names and the outer marks and the day and cannot check what a man of thirty-two is.**"

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-fifth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and that party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the first of the six rota days that that party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and that that is the twenty-fourth of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Wednesday and was not occupied at any hour on the said Thursday, and that the holder did not go over to him on the said Wednesday and did not go over to him on the said Thursday.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and the board was not written on it, and that the reason against that line is that the said woman has four stitches in her right hand and cannot hold a line and cannot hold a pencil, and that a Wednesday is not a day a yard of two takes off when there is nothing to take off, and that that is a day that yard lost and not a day that yard did not know about, and that there is no figure against it.*

*And that on the said Wednesday afternoon the holder and the said woman went to the counter of this harbor's own authority on the west basin at about half past two and asked what a holder of that writ is on the said first of March, and that the clerk of that authority said, and said it as it stands and was not asked to improve on it, that the writ of the first of May past is a municipal instrument of that harbor, is inside that harbor line, is bounded by the outer marks, carries four names and no fifth, is not the Second Bell license, does not lead to one, renews nothing, and runs to midnight on the said first of March; and that the office which issued it has said on the face of it in its own words that it is not the office that can renew it.*

*And that the said clerk said that at midnight on the said first of March the said paper stops being a thing the holder holds and nothing takes its place, and that nobody is charged with anything, and that no court sits, and that nobody is fined, and that no clerk comes up that hill, and that nobody is told that the said holder has not got it, and that that is not a thing there is a penalty for not having.*

*And that the said clerk read the one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past without being asked, and said that it is a different paper from the said writ, is not the said writ, is not the Second Bell license, does not lead to one, is on that harbor's own ground inside that harbor line, is bounded by the outer marks and by four hundred yards off them, has the freed shoal iron as its bond at a clerk's thirteen which has not been sold, carries the eighth and that harbor's share of any raising and leaves the crew's share with the holder; and that it may be suspended by that office after an inspection and for cause, of which that office has none and has not looked; and that it runs to the last day of March next.*

*And that the said clerk said, out of the form and not out of an office, that a party which does not produce a thing on a day is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on, and that he said that sentence because it is on the form and is not his; and that he told the holder that the cistern rota is a Monday and a Thursday, and that he said he would rather the holder had that from the counter than find it out, and that those two things are not the same thing, and that in about four years the second of them is the one worth having in that book.*

*And that on the said Thursday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump up that hill and brought the man with him and stayed; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the said woman's hand on those boards at about half past one and not afterwards.*

*And that at about half past eight on the said Thursday night, with the lamp lit and the lamp turned round and nobody else in that shed, the day's entries were read back from the top of that bench by the said woman and read back after her by the holder, and that the finding of the said Wednesday was read back out of that first column in the yard's own words and not in a clerk's; and that the said woman said that there were three places in it and one of them was in the counter and not in the book, and that the said holder put the said woman's sentence against his own in the second column and not his own.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the twenty-fifth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a paper a man holds is not a thing he is, and that what is going to be true of this yard at midnight on the said first of March is that the holder of the said writ is a man of thirty-two in this town with a charter and a bare bench and a drawer, and that this party is entered against itself for the ninth time in five volumes for having gone to a counter and got a fact and wanting it to be a smaller one; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check the four names, the outer marks, the day, the hour and the words of a clerk at a counter, and cannot check what a man of thirty-two is on the morning of a day when a thing he was holding stops existing, and that there is no figure for that and there is not going to be one.*

*And that the two instruments this party has had for five months are two instruments, and that they are now kept apart on the page in a clerk's mouth and not only in the holder's head, and that they are the writ of the first of May past and the one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past, and that neither of them is the Second Bell license and neither of them leads to one and neither of them renews anything, and that the two of them do not stand in one another's place, and that the loss of the first is not the loss of the second and the running of the second is not the renewal of the first.*

*And that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are the four lines of the printed sheet, and are still four, are still nameless, are not struck out, and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that a line does not come out of a column because it has been printed and cannot be got back.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing on the said Wednesday, and nothing went out of it on either of the two days, and fifty-two marks, four shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is eight thousand three hundred and seventy pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is seven thousand seven hundred and thirty pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Thursday is one day at the face of the second seam, being four hundred and eighty pence, being the twenty-fifth of that month, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water. The whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Thursday is nothing at all, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and three pence in four transactions at two counters, being a penny on the eighteenth of December past, a penny on the twenty-eighth of January instant, a penny on the thirteenth of February instant, and two shillings to a bonesetter on the twentieth of that month. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and eighteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and all eighteen are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks standing under that figure are at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and none of the three has been moved by anything in that book, and this yard did not say that note out loud in the twelve weeks ended on the said Thursday and is not going to, and it was said out loud at the head of that dock for the third time in seven months on the third of December past and not a fourth time. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past, unmoved, and he is not in that book and nobody was sent for him and nobody in that book said a word about him. Seven pence owing to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these twenty days and no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is on any page of that book and the chest was not opened and was not brought down that hill. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure, and that is in no sentence with the bonesetter's two shillings. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, not one mark paid and not one shilling offered, and she told that yard not to ask her again this winter and that yard has not asked her once in the twenty days ended on the said Thursday. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June past stand, the head of the third column keeps that boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August past and nobody has been appointed to it and nobody is going to be appointed to it. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of that yard has been inside its first clause on any of these two days, and no return of any kind was made out of either of them. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in that mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter out of that shed. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, owned by four people alive in that town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves and signed is in that yard's own first column and stands, and the holder did not ask to have it lifted once in these twenty days. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered on either of these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in that harbor has ever said why and that party is not going to be the first. The lead plug is in a chest in that shed with a number in no book in that harbor and was not asked about and was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and fifty-nine days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved, and that party did not go near them on either of these two days. The four returns are four and there is no fifth. Nothing went up on any wall in that harbor in the twenty days ended on the said Thursday and nothing came off, and the two naval notices are not on that wall and were not taken down by that party, and the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it. A working harbor of that coast is poorer on the twenty-fifth of February next than it was on the twenty-third of that month, one herring berth of two is out, the north berth is out, the mole-head light is not on because a light costs oil, the fire party's hose is dead until the spring, the tide gang is four men short, the hard at Ashtide has been unanswered since the ninth of November past, a boat of about forty tons is a hand short since the middle of July past, and five marks of compressor's gland are unpaid and useless and the thing after the gland is the hose and that harbor has no hose and no figure has been put on the hose, and nothing this yard has done in two days has made one thing better, and no figure in that harbor is the price of any of it.**
