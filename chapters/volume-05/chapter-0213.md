# Chapter 213

*The List of What a Thing on the Water Is Made Of, and a Year Nobody Noticed*

Wednesday the thirtieth and Thursday the thirty-first of December, in the shed, and on the crown of the mole.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on that Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, as it has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and stays nil.**

**And on the Wednesday the far bench was not occupied at any hour and on the Thursday it was not occupied at any hour, and this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.**

---

**And on the Wednesday morning at about eight, before the two of them went anywhere, the holder wrote the cost of that day against that day, in his own left hand, in the first column, at the top of the page where the day is.**

The cost of the day was two of them and a pump this yard does not own and a boat this harbor does not have, and the figure of a day at a seam is about three marks, and neither of those figures is a price for anything because nothing was bought.

"**Say what that line is,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**It is the day. It is here before the day is, and the day is going to be spent whether we agree to a thing at the end of it or not, and if we had spent it and agreed to nothing then it is still a day, and if we had agreed to a thing and not spent it that would be a lie with a date on it.**"

"**Then go and look at the water.**"

They went up the crown of the mole with the wind coming round behind them and stopped at the two objects of nobody's and did not touch either of them, and stood there about four minutes looking at the mouth of this harbor, where the water goes out past the third seam's mark and off to the outer marks four hundred yards away, and where there is a bank about two hundred yards off the south end that every boat in this harbor knows about and that is on no chart in this town.

Nessa Pike had a piece of paper in her coat. She had had it in her coat since the Friday.

"**Give me twenty minutes and do not talk to me.**"

She wrote on the boards, sitting down on the third step of the inner stair with her back to the wind, and she wrote the way she writes a board, which is fast and in a hand that leans.

---

**And at about half past eleven on the Wednesday she read it out in the shed, once, and it is nineteen lines and it is in her own hand and nothing has been added to it and nothing has been taken off it.**

"**One. A body of iron that will float and take a chain and take a weight and not go under when a sea comes across the mole.** This yard has none. This harbor's gear has none. The only iron in this harbor that is spoken for is four hundred yards out and is somebody else's.

"**Two. A chain. Not rope. A chain, because a rope chafes and a chain does not, and there is chain in this harbor in about four hundred feet of it and I am not writing down whose and where it is, because that is on a list in this yard already and item two of a list that was written on the twenty-second of November is not going to be improved on by a list written on the thirtieth of December.**

"**Three. A weight, and I do not know what a weight is, and I have left it as a weight.**

"**Four. Something to be seen from a boat in the dark.** I can price a lamp. I can price a lamp and I cannot price the oil and there is a mole-head light in this harbor that is not on because a light costs oil, and that light is this harbor's and not ours, and if we hang anything off that mole we are asking the harbor authority a question we have not asked it.

"**Five. A mark. Not a name, a mark. Somebody has to be able to say from four hundred yards which thing is ours.**

"**Six. A place to put it. Not the water. A place on the mole where it is put down when it comes in, that is not a berth and not a board and not somebody's room.**

"**Seven. A hand that goes out and looks at it.**"

She stopped there and put the pencil down.

"**That is the list, and that is seven of the nineteen, and the other twelve are what a thing on the water is made of out of a chandler's shop and I am not going to read twelve lines of a chandler's shop out loud in a shed, and there is nothing on that page that anybody in this town can buy this week.**

"**And the seventh line is the one that stops me and I wrote it last and I wrote it last on purpose.**

"**The only other name this yard has is on no list of mine, and he is twenty-two, and he is off the stone since the fifteenth of May, and there are forty marks standing whole against him and three nots of the eighth of June in a book, and if his name went on a thing in the water then it would go on a thing that owes him more than any other thing in this harbor, and there is no man alive who is going to put a boy's name on a buoy to make a list look finished.**"

"**So the list is not a plan.**

"**The list is a list. A plan is what you do when somebody is going to pay for it.**"

---

**And the cost of the Wednesday was entered before the day, and the day was spent, and the thing at the end of the day was a list, and the holder entered that no agreement was made, and gave the reason.**

"**Nineteen lines and not one of them is a thing this yard can pay for and not one of them is gear this yard owns, and one of them is a hand and that is a person and a person is not a figure and it is not on this page, and I have not got a person and I am not going to write down that I have not got a person in a book that goes in two columns, because the second column is for things I cannot check and that one I can check every morning of my life by counting how many of us there are.**

"**And I am not going to build it and I am not going to say I am not going to build it. What I am going to say is that a day was spent and the day is on the page and the day cost what a day costs, and what came of the day is nineteen lines and a pencil and no agreement.**"

He entered in the first column, in his own hand and with the date and the hour on it, that a day was spent on that Wednesday by two of them, and that nothing was bought on it, and that no money came into this yard on it and none went out of it, and that those three things are in this column because a man on a mole can ask all three of anybody in this town and be told the same answer by any of them. **And the figure of three marks, which is what a day at a seam is worth and not what that day cost this yard, went in the second column with a reason against the line, because a day nobody here worked is not a figure this yard has paid out and this column is a record of what this yard's own money did and not of what a day is worth to somebody else.**

---

**And on the Thursday afternoon, at about four, at the head of this dock, with the market gone in and about nine people in the street, a man said a thing about the year.**

He said it to nobody in particular, which is what men do at the head of a dock in the last week of a year, and what he said was that this was the last day of the year and that a man ought to be able to say what he had done in it.

Neither of them answered him. Neither of them said the day of the month. Neither of them said anything at all, and about nine people in a street heard a man say a thing about the end of a year and two of them did not take it up, and that is the whole of what happened, and it is entered because a day on which a thing is said in a street is a day on which a thing was said in a street.

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the thirty-first day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil; and that the far bench was not occupied on the Wednesday instant at any hour nor on the said Thursday at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.*

*And that the holder wrote the cost of the said Wednesday against the said Wednesday, in his own left hand, in this column, at the top of the page where the day is, before the day was spent; and that the cost entered was two of them and a pump this yard does not own and a boat this harbor does not have, and that the figure of a day at a seam is about three marks, and that neither figure is a price for anything because nothing was bought; and that the three marks themselves are in the second column and not in this one, and that the reason is against the line there; and that the holder said in that shed that the day was entered before the day was spent because the day is going to be spent whether the two of them agree to a thing at the end of it or not, and that a day spent and agreed to nothing is still a day, and that a day agreed to and not spent would be a lie with a date on it.*

*And that the two of them went up the crown of that mole on the said Wednesday morning, and stood at the two objects of nobody's and touched neither of them, and stood about four minutes looking at the mouth of this harbor and at a bank about two hundred yards off the south end which is on no chart in this town; and that the two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and one days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and that neither of them has moved; and that the said woman had a piece of paper in her coat since the Friday past, and that she wrote nineteen lines on the boards in her own hand with her back to the wind, and that she read them out once in that shed and that nothing has been added to that page and nothing has been taken off it.*

*And that the said nineteen lines are, in her order: a body of iron that will float and take a chain and take a weight and not go under when a sea comes across the mole, and this yard has none and this harbor's gear has none; a chain and not rope, and there is chain in this harbor in about four hundred feet of it and this party did not write down whose it is or where it is, and gave the reason, which is that that is on a list in this yard already and item two of that list is not going to be improved on by a list written on the said Wednesday; a weight, which the said woman did not know and entered as a weight; something to be seen from a boat in the dark, which she can price and cannot price the oil for, and the mole-head light in this harbor is not on because a light costs oil and is this harbor's and not this yard's; a mark and not a name, so that somebody can say from four hundred yards which thing is this yard's; a place to put it on the mole that is not a berth and not a board and not somebody's room; and a hand that goes out and looks at it.*

*And that the said woman stopped at the seventh line and said that that is the one that stops her and that she wrote it last and wrote it last on purpose; and that the only other name this yard has is on no list of hers, and that he is twenty-two and off the stone since the fifteenth of May past, and that forty marks stand whole against him and the three nots of the eighth of June past are in a book, and that if that name went on a thing in the water it would go on a thing that owes him more than any other thing in this harbor, and that no man alive is going to put a boy's name on a buoy to make a list look finished; and that the said list is not a plan, and that a plan is what you do when somebody is going to pay for it.*

*And that the holder entered that no agreement was made, and gave the reason, which is that not one of the nineteen lines is a thing this yard can pay for and not one of them is gear this yard owns, and that the seventh is a hand and a person is not a figure and it is not on that page, and that this yard has not got a person and this party is not going to write down in a book that goes in two columns that it has not got a person, because the second column is for things that cannot be checked and that one can be checked every morning by counting; and that the day was spent and what came of it is nineteen lines and a pencil and no agreement, and that a day was spent on it by two of them and that nothing was bought on it and that no money came into this yard on it and none went out of it, and that those three things are in this column because a man on a mole can ask all three of anybody in this town; and that the figure of three marks went in the second column with a reason against the line and not in this one, and that a day nobody here worked is not a figure this yard has paid out, and that this column is a record of what this yard's own money did and not of what a day is worth to somebody else.*

*And that on the said Thursday afternoon at about four at the head of this dock, with the market gone in and about nine people in the street, a man said out loud that this was the last day of the year and that a man ought to be able to say what he had done in it; and that neither of them answered him, and that neither of them said the day of the month, and that about nine people heard a man say a thing about the end of a year and that two of them did not take it up; and that this party entered it because a day on which a thing is said in a street is a day on which a thing was said in a street, and that this yard did not say anything about the end of a year because this yard did not notice it.*

*And that this party entered on the said day, in this column, that item eleven of the list of the twenty-second day of November past, which is the two figures for the bar of gravel and the Kettle, which was not looked at, is a thing this yard has said out loud in its own book that it cannot put in front of anybody; and that the reason that line is in this column and not the second is that it is checkable by any man in this town, who can go down to a counter off the fish market and ask a copyist of fifty-eight what she wrote on the thirtieth of November past, and can ask a man of the first lay whether a page of his coast road has been copied anywhere, and can be told; and that item eleven is not off that list and is not going to be.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the thirty-first day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the figure of three marks is entered against the said Wednesday as what a day at a seam is worth and not as a price this yard paid, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that no money came into this yard on that day and none went out of it and a man on a mole cannot check what a day is worth to a man who was not here, and that a day nobody here worked is not a figure this yard's own money did; and that a thing that floats is a thing somebody has to look after, and that looking after it is a person and a calendar, and that the sixteen lines this yard wrote of pumps and boats in and boats out on the nights of the thirtieth and thirty-first of October past are in a book in this shed and are not extended by anything said on the said Wednesday, and that sixteen lines is a thing one man did on three nights because he could not sleep; and that whether a party of one and a half until the first of March can be a crew is not checkable from a mole and is not going to be answered by a page with nineteen lines on it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can read a list of nineteen lines and count them, and cannot count what it costs to keep a thing afloat in a winter; and that the four people of this coast who could say whether any of it is worth having have not been asked and are not going to be; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And that what is not checkable from a mole about the said item eleven is who was right about the two figures in the first place, and that this party has not asked and is not going to ask, and that the four people of this coast who could say have not been asked about it either; and that the coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and that this yard does not decide which of them is right, which is in the first column above and is not given twice; and that the Kettle was not looked at and is not going to be looked at by this yard.*

**No money came into this yard on the thirtieth of December and none on the thirty-first, and nothing went out of it on either day, and ten marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and that figure is four hundred and eighty more than the one this yard held on the twenty-fifth day of December past. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and ten weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the yard did not say that note out loud in the three weeks ended on the twenty-fifth and has not said it in the four days since. Nothing has gone into a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past and nothing is in one now. The three marks to a man of Ashtide, the seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, the eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, the eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor, the forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two and the forty marks first after food to a person of no office are all where they were on the twenty-ninth of November and none of them moved, and the whole of them is on the page above and is not read out again here. The three nots of the eighth of June past stand and nobody has been appointed to that column, and the forty marks that stand first after food have stood since the fifteenth of October past without a mark paid or a shilling offered, and this yard has not written to her, because she said it was not to be asked again this winter. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four people it belongs to hold a limit, and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and did not ask this month. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and one days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
