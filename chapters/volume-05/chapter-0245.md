# Chapter 245

*Thirty-One, and a Form With a Name on It*

Thursday the fourth and Friday the fifth of March, in the shed, at the head of that dock, and at the counter of the harbor authority on the west basin.

**At about ten on the said Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the third of the six rota days that this party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the twenty-sixth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten until about four and was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder of that writ is up a coast road and did not go over to him on either of the said two days and is not going to, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Thursday and the reason against that line is that the said Thursday is a rota day and that four men this yard does not know charged the cistern at about ten on it, and that a party of one does not work a day it has spent standing at a bench watching a piece of paper arrive.**

**And on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and the day's book was written in the ship's hand in her own left hand at about half past one and not afterwards and it took her about twice as long as it takes her.**

---

**And at about seven on the said Thursday morning, at the head of that dock, with the boards not up and about eleven people standing about where they stand on any morning, the ship's hand said a number out loud, and it was the fourth time in seven months that number has been said out loud at the head of that dock, and it had not been said once in the thirteen weeks before it.**

"**Thirty-one,**" she said, standing where she could be seen and not on the boards. "**On the chandler's note, not re-written. The three counts of weeks under that figure are at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and none of the three has been moved by anything in our book, and I am going to say them in one breath and not one after the other: twenty-two, thirteen, eleven. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. The refusal of the thirty-first of March stands unreversed. And this yard is not going to say it a fifth time, and I am saying that in the same breath as the number so that nobody in about eleven people can go home and tell anybody that this yard said thirty-one and did not mean it.**

"**That is the fourth time,**" said a man of the first lay who had been there for the third one. "**You are not improving on it.**

"**I am not improving on it. That is the fourth time and the last one, and I have said that and you heard it, and I am going to go back up that hill and there is nothing else I am going to say about it.**"

Nobody at the head of that dock said anything after that, and about eleven people stood in it, and the boards went up at about eight, and this yard is not on the boards and has never been.

---

**And at about half past nine on the said Thursday a boat lay off the north side with nothing in the way of a cutter's paint on her, and at about ten a sealed envelope came up that mole in a hand, and nobody came up the hill with it, and the envelope had a name on the outside of it.**

The form is a single sheet folded twice. It is a Crown form. At the head of it, in the office's own type, is a name, and the name is **Halven Rook**, and the office he holds is written out under it, and that name is in this yard's own first column on the Thursday in full because a name on a piece of paper is a thing a man can check, and it is on no page of this shed and it is not going to be read out in a market. Beneath the name, in the same type and no larger:

> **That a party holding a crew's charter in a port of this coast is required to produce that charter at the vessel of a patrol of this Crown lying in that port, within one day of the day this form is delivered.**
>
> **That the charter so produced is entered on the schedule of that patrol and is not returned, and that the party producing it may enter in writing, once, any particular of it that the party wishes to be recorded.**
>
> **That this form is not a writ, and is not a licence, and is not a finding, and does not touch any matter before any court, and that nothing in this form is a suspension and nothing in it is a penalty.**
>
> **That a party which does not produce the thing on the day is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on.**

Beneath all of that, in the same hand as the name, a line that is not the office's type: **That the party may not be required to produce any book of its own in any column, and that nothing in this form requires a party to say who altered anything or at whose instance or with what intent, and that the party is not asked to say it and is not going to be asked to say it.**

Nessa Pike read it twice and put it down flat on the boards and put her hand flat on it.

"**He is not here,**" she said, to nobody.

"**I know he is not here.**

"**Then we do not get an answer out of the west basin on this. It is not the same question and it is not the same man and I am not going to put a clerk who is owed two days by his own office in the middle of this to keep a thing apart that I cannot keep apart myself, because I cannot.**"

She went down at about half past eleven and was back at about twenty past one, and the clerk of this harbor's own authority gave her four sentences and the last of them is the one that is in the book.

"**I will say the first three and you can check them at the counter on Friday. That the writ of the first of May past is a municipal instrument of this harbor and it is not in this office any longer than midnight on the first of March, and that it is not a charter and is not the Second Bell license and leads to neither. That the one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past was granted in the room over the tide-gang store by a reeve of this harbor and it is on that harbor's ground inside that harbor line, and that it is a different paper from the writ. And that a party which does not produce a thing on a day is not fined because a court does not fine a poor man and the matter simply goes on, and that sentence is on their form as well as on mine, and I did not give it to them.

"**And the fourth. This office is not the office that can give that charter back. It never was. It granted it and it can suspend it after an inspection for cause and I have no cause and have not looked for one, and I could not give it back this afternoon if the man it was granted to were standing at this counter with both hands on it. A thing with a name on the outside of it came into this harbor this morning and took a paper off a bench in a shed, and there is no counter in this office and there is no officer of this authority in that shed, and I have not been asked to do anything about it and I have not done anything about it.**"

---

**And the charter came out of the drawer at about two on the said Thursday afternoon and went down that mole in a hand, and the hand is the whole of what this book has to say about who took it, and the sheet with the name on it went into the drawer in its place, and the four pieces of paper that were in that drawer are in it now, and the drawer is the drawer and not a new one.**

"**Write this,**" said Nessa Pike, in her left hand, on the boards, at about half past five. "**And do not improve it and do not make it bigger than what happened.

"**That a Crown patrol lay off the north side on the said Thursday morning and that a form came up that mole at about ten in a sealed envelope with a name on the outside of it and nobody came up the hill with it; and that the said form is a single sheet folded twice and is not a writ and is not a licence and is not a finding and does not touch any matter before any court, and says that a party which does not produce the thing on the day is not fined because a court does not fine a poor man and the matter simply goes on; and that the said form says that the party producing the charter may enter in writing once any particular of it that the party wishes to be recorded.

"**And that the holder of that writ is up a coast road and was not in that harbor and could not be, and that the said form does not say that the holder did anything and does not say that he did not, and that nobody in that shed has any idea what the said form says about him and is not going to find out and is not going to be asked.

"**And that the one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past went out of that drawer at about two in the afternoon of the said Thursday and down that mole in a hand, and that the clerk of this harbor's own authority said out loud, and has it in a book, that this office is not the office that can give that charter back and never was and could not this afternoon if the man it was granted to were standing at that counter with both hands on it, and that a thing with a name on the outside of it came into that harbor and took a paper off a bench in a shed, and that there is no counter in that office and no officer of that authority in that shed, and that he was not asked to do anything about it and did not do anything about it.

"**And now the last of it, and it is the one that is going to be true on the last page of this book and I am writing it tonight while I still have a bad temper about it. That a yard which has spent five volumes refusing to be an instrument of anybody's has got, since ten o'clock this morning, nothing at all to stand on outside its own harbor line. Not a writ, which stopped existing at midnight on Monday. Not a charter, which went down that mole at two this afternoon. Not a licence, which this holder has not got and cannot be examined for and has not applied for and is not going to. And that is the cost of a form that says nothing wrong while it does it, and it is not the form, and I am not going to let anybody say it was the form.**"

*First column, entered on Friday the fifth day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and that party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the third of the six rota days that party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and that that is the twenty-sixth of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten until about four and was not occupied at any hour on the said Friday, and that the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on the said Thursday and did not go over to him on the said Friday and is not going to.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Thursday and the reason against that line is that the said Thursday is a rota day on which four men that yard does not know charged the cistern at about ten, and that a party of one does not work a day it has spent standing at a bench watching a piece of paper arrive; and that on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the said woman's own left hand at about half past one and not afterwards.*

*And that at about seven in the morning on the said Thursday, at the head of that dock, with the boards not up and about eleven people standing, the said woman said out loud the figure of thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks under that figure at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven in one breath and not one after the other, and that the refusal of the thirty-first day of March stands unreversed, and that nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April; and that she said in the same breath that that yard is not going to say it a fifth time; and that that is the fourth time in seven months that figure has been said out loud at the head of that dock, and that it had not been said once in the thirteen weeks before it; and that nobody at the head of that dock said anything after it and about eleven people stood in it; and that the boards went up at about eight and that yard is not on the boards and never has been.*

*And that a boat lay off the north side of that harbor on the said Thursday morning with nothing in the way of a cutter's paint on her, and that at about ten a sealed envelope came up that mole in a hand and nobody came up the hill with it, and that the envelope had a name on the outside of it and the said name was a man's name and is entered in that book and is not read out in that shed.*

*And that the said form is a single sheet folded twice, is a Crown form, has at the head of it the name of a man and the office he holds, which name is entered in that book in full, and that he is in no room and on no mole and has said no word, and that the said form is not a writ, and is not a licence, and is not a finding, and does not touch any matter before any court, and that nothing in it is a suspension and nothing in it is a penalty, and that a party which does not produce the thing on the day is not fined because a court does not fine a poor man and the matter simply goes on; and that it requires a party holding a crew's charter in a port of that coast to produce that charter at the vessel of a patrol of that Crown lying in that port within one day of the day of its delivery, and that the charter so produced is entered on the schedule of that patrol and is not returned, and that a party producing it may enter in writing once any particular of it that the party wishes to be recorded; and that it says that the party may not be required to produce any book of its own in any column, and that nothing in it requires a party to say who altered anything or at whose instance or with what intent, and that the party is not asked to say it and is not going to be asked to say it.*

*And that the holder of that writ is up that coast road and was not in that harbor and could not be, and that the said form does not say that the holder did anything and does not say that he did not, and that nobody in that shed knows what the said form says about him and is not going to find out and is not going to be asked.*

*And that the one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past came out of that drawer at about two in the afternoon of the said Thursday and went down that mole in a hand, and that the hand is the whole of what that book says about who took it; and that the said sheet with the name on it went into that drawer in its place, and that the four pieces of paper that were in that drawer are in it now, and that the drawer is the drawer and not a new one.*

*And that the clerk of this harbor's own authority said out loud, at the counter of that authority on the said Friday, and has it in a book, four things: that the writ of the first of May past is a municipal instrument of that harbor and is not in that office any longer than midnight on the said first of March, and is not a charter and is not the Second Bell license and leads to neither; and that the one-season provisional working charter of the twenty-eighth of November past was granted in the room over the tide-gang store by a reeve of that harbor, is on that harbor's ground inside that harbor line, and is a different paper from the said writ; and that a party which does not produce a thing on a day is not fined because a court does not fine a poor man and the matter simply goes on, and that that sentence is on the Crown's form as well as on that authority's and that the clerk did not give it to them; and that that office is not the office that can give that charter back and never was, and could not have given it back on the said Thursday afternoon if the man it was granted to had been standing at that counter with both hands on it, and that a thing with a name on the outside of it came into that harbor and took a paper off a bench in a shed, and that there is no counter in that office and no officer of that authority in that shed, and that the clerk was not asked to do anything about it and did not do anything about it.*

*And that the said woman entered that a yard which has spent five volumes refusing to be an instrument of anybody's has, since ten o'clock in the morning of the said Thursday, nothing at all to stand on outside that harbor's own line: not a writ, which stopped existing at midnight on the said Monday; not a charter, which went down that mole at two on the said Thursday; and not a licence, which the holder has not got and cannot be examined for and has not applied for and is not going to; and that the cost of that is not the form, and that nobody in that shed is going to let anybody say that it was the form.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the fifth day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a paper with a name on the outside of it can take another paper off a bench in about nine minutes, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a boat was lying off the north side and that an envelope came up and that a charter went down, and cannot check what is written on a schedule in about four hundred miles, and that in about four years the whole of what that harbor knows about itself will be the fact that a paper went into a drawer and came out of it.**

*And that the two instruments of this harbor are now neither of them in this yard's hands and that they were never going to be for longer than they were, and that the yard's whole five volumes' argument is that a party which can be found is findable, and that on the said Thursday a party of one in that town was found by a boat lying off the north side, and that the reason against that line is the reason of the fourteenth of January past and is not going to be given.*

*And that the four lines above the said named line stand where they stood, and are still four, and are still nameless, and have not been struck out, and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing on the said Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either of the two days, and sixty-one marks, four shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is nine thousand eight hundred and ten pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is nine thousand one hundred and seventy pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Friday is four days at the face of the second seam, being one thousand nine hundred and twenty pence, being the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth days of February past and the first and the fifth of March instant, and no two of the four were worked by the same two people. The whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Friday is nothing at all, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and three pence in four transactions at two counters, being a penny on the eighteenth of December past, a penny on the twenty-eighth of January instant, a penny on the thirteenth of February instant, and two shillings to a bonesetter on the twentieth of February. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and nineteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and the second of March instant, and all nineteen are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and none of the three has been moved, and it was said out loud at the head of that dock for the fourth time in seven months on the said Thursday at about seven in the morning in about eleven people's hearing, and this yard said in the same breath that it will not say it a fifth time. The rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
