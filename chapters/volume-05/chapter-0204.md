# Chapter 204

*Three Marks on a Board, and the Thing After the Gland Is the Hose*

Saturday the twelfth and Sunday the thirteenth of December, on the face of the second seam, and in the shed.

**On Saturday the twelfth of December this yard worked about four hours and a quarter on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, because the count and the times go on a slate and the slate goes into the book in her own writing.**

The board for the day is twelve gross, and what comes off the top of a day rate comes off the top of it in the order it has come off since the third of May: the eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three, and the three went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people and no figure was argued.

**Three marks is four hundred and eighty pence, and the bag held seven hundred and seventeen pence on the Friday morning, and the sum of the two is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence, and that is seven marks, six shillings and five pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of those.**

The man who hired the day was of the second lay and had a boat of about seventy tons of his own, and he brought the pump and he brought a man, and he said one thing at about ten o'clock, which was that he had asked for a yard.

"**You have got a yard,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and the figure is on the board and the figure is what it is and I am not going to argue it with you at the mole. What I am going to say is that the day is going slower than a day with three on it and it is going slower because there are two of us and not three, and the reason for that is in the book in his hand tonight and not in mine, and you are not going to be told about it now because you would only have an opinion.**"

"**I have not got one,**" said the man of the second lay, and worked the pump until half past one and did not say anything else that is in this book.

They hauled from eight until about half past one. A hand lead's line went over the side and back four times and the count and the times went onto a slate in the ship's hand and the slate went into the book on the board in her own writing and the holder read it back after her and neither of them said anything about it. The day went at the speed of a day with a man on the pump and a woman on the line, which is not the speed of a day with three on it and has not been since the twenty-third day of October, and the yard is a party of one and a half until the first of March and not two, and every day it books in this month is a day it books with a man in it.

---

**And on Sunday the thirteenth of December there was no day of work booked and the board was not written, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened, and which is the second Sunday in three weeks on which a sheet of paper has been read in that shed and no work has been done in it.**

**And there was nothing in a tin in that shed on either day, and nothing in one since the twenty-eighth of April, and five marks of compressor's gland has been standing on that page since the nineteenth of May, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose, and this harbor has no hose.**

"**Say the rest of that,**" said Calder Marr, "**because you have stopped in the middle of a sentence and you did that on purpose.**

"**I stopped because the rest of it is a thing I have said once and it is in this book and I am not going to say it a second time in a December,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Write it down in your hand and let it stand where it is.**"

He wrote it down in his hand and let it stand where it was.

**And the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it was on the bench in that shed on the Sunday, where it has been since the Wednesday, and neither of them has answered it and neither of them has put it in the stove, and the pump was under it and the lamp was not lit because it was not dark yet.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirteenth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on Saturday the twelfth instant this yard worked about four hours and a quarter on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on the board was twelve, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it, and that nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the bag held seven hundred and seventeen pence on the Friday morning and the sum of the two is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence, which is seven marks, six shillings and five pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book, and the holder read it back after her and neither of them said anything about it; and that the man who hired the day was of the second lay and had a boat of about seventy tons of his own and brought the pump and a man, and that he said at about ten o'clock that he had asked for a yard; and that the said woman said that the day was going slower than a day with three on it and that the reason for that is in the book in the holder's hand and not in hers and that the said man was not going to be told about it at the mole because he would only have an opinion, and that the said man said he had not got one, and worked the pump until half past one and said nothing else that is in this book.*

*And that the holder entered on the said evening that the day was booked by this party with a person in it and not a person short, and that the cost of that is a day that went slower than a day with three on it, and that a day that goes slower is a day not earned whole and is entered as a day not earned whole and not as a day at a figure; and that the said woman entered in her own hand on the said evening that until the first of March next this yard is a party of one and a half and not two, and that every day this yard books in this month is a day it books with a man in it, and that she does not want it in her book and it is in the holder's.*

*And that no day of work was booked on Sunday the thirteenth instant and that the board was not written, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and that the said day is the second Sunday in three weeks on which a sheet of paper has been read in that shed and no work has been done in it; and that the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it was on the bench in that shed on the said Sunday, where it has been since the Wednesday, and that neither of them has answered it and neither of them has put it in the stove, and that the pump was under it and the lamp was not lit because it was not dark yet.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the thirteenth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that there has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past and that five marks of compressor's gland has been standing on that page since the nineteenth day of May past, unpaid and useless, and that the thing after the gland is the hose and that this harbor has no hose; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can read a page and see a figure of five marks and a date of the nineteenth of May and can ask what it is for, and that the answer is a length of hose this harbor has not got, and that what a hose is worth in a harbor that has not got one is not a figure anybody can get at the bottom of, and that the yard is not going to price it and is not going to buy one out of a day at a seam, and that the said woman said the rest of that sentence once and it is in this yard's book already and she is not going to say it a second time in a December.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the twelfth of December and nothing came in on the thirteenth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and seven marks, six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, seven weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and an eighth will fall due on the fifteenth instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and eighty-three days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter was written about it in these two days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. And a working day was worked on the Saturday and a form with no date on it went on being read on the Sunday, and one of those is on a board and one of those is in a book, and this yard entered both on the same night and did not join them.**
