# Chapter 242

*Two Things That Came Up the Coast, and the Price of Each Before the Day*

Friday the twenty-sixth and Saturday the twenty-seventh of February, in the shed, and at the head of that dock.

**Neither of the two days of this chapter is a rota day and the cistern behind the engine house was charged on none of them, and that is entered with a reason, and the reason is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh days of that month are a Friday and a Saturday.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten until about one and was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and the holder did not go over to him on the said Friday and did not go over to him on the said Saturday, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past and is not going to be given out of that shed this month.**

**And this yard worked on the said Friday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and did not stay, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times. And no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the reason against that line is that a Saturday is the day the two of them have had since the twentieth of February past, and that it is entered as a day the yard did not take and not as a day the yard did not know about.**

---

**And at about a quarter to two on the said Friday a man of about forty-five came up that mole and left his handcart at the bottom of it, and he had come ten days up this coast road and was going back on the said Monday at about ten.**

He had lime on a coat that was not his own. He gave his name and it was entered, and it is not on any page of this yard's book as a man this yard has spoken to twice.

"**I have got a thing and I have been up and down this road for a year and a bit and I did not know until the day before yesterday that there was a yard on this mole that keeps a book. I want to say first that I have not come to ask you to keep it. I have come because I have got nobody on this coast to say it to and you are the first place I have come to that has ever had a thing come up it.**

"**It is a book of ground. It is mine and my father's before me and his before him, and I will not tell you how far back because I have got a figure for it and the figure is my own and I would rather you had the thing than the figure.**

"**It is about a stretch of water about nine miles along the coast from you, in water no lead of yours has ever been in and no boat of yours has ever been over. It is about when boats could and could not pass a bar at the mouth of a river, and about a light that four houses on that coast keep burning and take turns at, and about a night in a year nobody on that coast will name, of which the book has two accounts and the two accounts are not the same.**

"**Four people alive keep it. I am one. Two of the other three are women and one of them is seventy-one. It has never been in an office and it is not a document anybody sent anywhere.**

"**And I am not going to say a word about it if you do not want to hear it, and I am not going to leave it, because it does not come out of the place it is about.**"

"**Why not.**

"**Because that is the oldest line in it and my father had it before me and it is the reason the four of us can sleep at night. A page about a place is the place's. If it leaves, it is a thing anybody can be asked about, and then it stops being the place's and starts being evidence, and there is no putting it back. I have watched a man on that coast lose a thing exactly that way and I am not going to be the second one.**"

He said that in about nine seconds and did not stop in the middle of it, and he did not say it twice, and the two of them did not ask him one question about the book.

"**I have got a cart and lime on it and I am going back on the Monday. If you come you come. And if you do not come nobody will ever hear about it from me again, because I am sixty-one next August.**"

---

**And at about ten on the said Saturday morning a man of about thirty-four who sells lime and chalk came up that hill on his own account and stood about four feet off the door with a bundle of ledger paper under his arm, which is the way he stands, and he was let in this time because it was raining.**

"**I have got a thing and I want it in your book and I want it in your book before I say it, because if I say it and you do not put it in, it is a thing I said in a shed.**

"**There is a place four days down this coast and there is a man there of about fifty who says he went out in a boat of about thirty tons with your brother in the month of October two years ago and was out two days and came back on the second night. He says your brother's name. He says the name of the boat. He says the two days.**

"**I did not ask him one question. I want that in your book first. I did not tell him where I was going.**"

"**You could go back and ask him.**

"**I could. I have a cart that goes that way on the Wednesday and it is four days each way and I am not going for nothing and I am not going for a favor either. I came up here because it is a Saturday and you are on the mole on a Saturday.**"

The ship's hand wrote the date and the hour and the words on the boards and read them back and the holder read them back after her, and then he said one thing before the entry was closed.

"**It goes in the first column and the price goes in against it before the day and not after it, and I am naming the price now and it is not a mark. The passage down that coast and four days there and four days back is two men off the water, eight days of a boat, a man's board and a bed in a place where this yard knows one man of about fifty and does not know the name of his street. A day at the face of the second seam is about three marks. Eight days is about twenty-four marks. This yard has fifty-five marks, four shillings and two pence in a bag and eighteen weeks of wage standing against it with no envelope in it, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is eight thousand two hundred and ten pence and it is the same on Saturday night as it was on Thursday night, because we worked on Friday and nothing went out of this yard on either day.

"**There is the price of that lead, named before the day, and it is not paid and it is not going to be paid, and nobody is going to ask that man of about fifty anything about my brother. Write down that I have not asked the man who brought it one question beyond the one he came here to ask.

"**And write this beside it. That on the twenty-third of February past this yard was told that a man of about thirty who cannot read, in a place two days down this coast, is a man of this yard in about forty people's hearing and is not one, and that this yard owes him something and is not going to pay it and is not going to write to him and is not going to send anybody, and that the cost of not making four places read one page the same way is falling on him and on a daughter of nineteen who goes to that kitchen. That is entered and is not going to be improved on by anybody.

"**And I have got a man at this door who has come ten days up a coast road with a book he is not going to be able to put down anywhere on this coast, and I have not asked him one question about it and I have not asked him to leave it and I have not told him what a column is, and he did not ask.

"**Two things came up this coast inside two days. Neither came from an office. Neither is going anywhere. One costs eight days and about twenty-four marks. The other costs four days and two men and a thing a man of sixty-one is not going to be able to put down. I have one yard and one and a half of a crew and it is the twenty-seventh of February, and I am not going to pretend I can do both, and I am not going to do the second one first and call that fairness.**"

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-seventh day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that neither of the said two days is a rota day and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged on none of them, and that the reason against that line is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten until about one and was not occupied at any hour on the said Saturday, and that the holder did not go over to him on the said Friday and did not go over to him on the said Saturday.*

*And that on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and did not stay; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the reason against that line is that the Saturday is the day the two of them have had since the twentieth of February past.*

*And that at about a quarter to two on the said Friday a man of about forty-five who sells lime and chalk came up that mole on his own account and left his handcart at the bottom of it, and said that he had come ten days up that coast road and was going back on the said Monday at about ten; and that he said he had a book of ground about a stretch of water about nine miles along that coast from that harbor, in water no lead and no boat of that yard has been in; and that the said book is about when boats could and could not pass a bar at the mouth of a river, and about a light that four houses on that coast keep burning and take turns at, and about a night in a year nobody on that coast will name, of which the said book has two accounts that are not the same.*

*And that the said man said that the said book is his own and his father's and his grandfather's, has never been in an office, is not a document anybody sent anywhere, and is kept by four people alive of whom he is one and of whom two are women and one is seventy-one; and that the said man said that the said book does not come out of the place it is about, and that a page about a place is the place's, and that if it leaves it then it is a thing anybody can be asked about, and then it stops being the place's and starts being evidence and there is no putting it back; and that the said man said he has watched a man on that coast lose a thing exactly that way and is not going to be the second one; and that he said that in about nine seconds and did not stop in the middle of it and did not say it twice.*

*And that this party asked the said man no question about the said book, and did not ask him to leave it, and did not tell him what a column is, and that he did not ask.*

*And that at about ten on the said Saturday morning a man of about thirty-four who sells lime and chalk came up that hill on his own account and stood about four feet off that door and was let in because of the rain, and said that at a place four days down that coast on the said Tuesday there is a man of about fifty who says he went out in a boat of about thirty tons with the said holder's brother in the month of October two years ago and was out two days and came back on the second night, and gave the name of that brother, the name of the boat and the two days; and that the said man said he asked that man nothing and is not going to, and did not say where he was going, and would go back and ask when he has been told what it is for.*

*And that the said holder entered that lead in the first column, and that the price of it was named before the day and not after it, being a passage down that coast and four days there and four days back, being two men off the water and eight days of a boat and a man's board and a bed in a place where that yard knows one man of about fifty and does not know the name of his street; and that eight days at the face of the second seam is about twenty-four marks at about three marks a day; and that the bag stands at fifty-five marks, four shillings and two pence, being eight thousand eight hundred and fifty pence, and that the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is eight thousand two hundred and ten pence, and that it was the same on the said Thursday as it is on the said Saturday, because that yard worked on the said Friday and nothing went out of it on either of the two days.*

*And that the said holder said that the price is not paid and is not going to be paid, and that nobody is going to ask that man of about fifty anything about the said holder's brother; and that the said holder asked the said man who brought the lead one question and no more; and that the said man was entered as having said the word *no* where the said holder said what it costs and that he is not going to pay it, and that those are two different sentences; and that the said man wrote it in those words.*

*And that beside that line the said holder entered that on the twenty-third day of February past this yard was told that a man of about thirty who cannot read, in a place two days down that coast, is a man of that yard in about forty people's hearing and is not one, and has a daughter of nineteen who goes to a kitchen, and that that yard owes him something and is not going to pay it and is not going to write to him and is not going to send anybody; and that the cost of not making four places read one page the same way is falling on him; and that that is entered and is not going to be improved on by anybody.*

*And that the said holder said that two things came up that coast inside two days and neither of them came from an office and neither of them is going anywhere, and that one of them costs eight days and about twenty-four marks and the other costs four days and two men and a thing a man of sixty-one is not going to be able to put down, and that this yard is one yard and one and a half of a crew on the twenty-seventh of February next, and that he is not going to pretend he can do both, and that he is not going to do the second one first and call that fairness.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the twenty-seventh day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a book of ground about nine miles along that coast from that harbor is in water that no lead and no boat of that yard has been in, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check the name of a man, the day, the hour and the words of a man standing at a door, and cannot check whether four people alive nine miles away have a book or have not got one, and that those four have not been asked by this party and are not going to be, and that a book which does not come out of the place it is about is a book this yard cannot be given, and that a man of sixty-one is not going to be able to put it down.*

*And that a lead about the said holder's brother came up that hill on the said Saturday from a man who was not sent for, and that it is real, and that it is checkable, and that the price of checking it has been named and has not been paid; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a man stood at a door and said four things and that the four things are in that book in that hand, and cannot check that a man of about fifty in a place four days down that coast ever said anything at all, and that nobody is going to ask him.*

*And that the two things arrived inside two days and neither of them is resolved on the said Saturday, and that this yard is entered against itself a tenth time in five volumes for wanting two things at once on a coast where one of them takes a day it has not got.*

*And that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four, are still nameless, are not struck out, and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing on the said Saturday, and nothing went out of it on either of the two days, and fifty-five marks, four shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is eight thousand eight hundred and fifty pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is eight thousand two hundred and ten pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Saturday is two days at the face of the second seam, being nine hundred and sixty pence, being the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth of that month, both on hirers' own accounts, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man on every one of the two. The whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Saturday is nothing at all, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and three pence in four transactions at two counters, being a penny on the eighteenth of December past, a penny on the twenty-eighth of January instant, a penny on the thirteenth of February instant, and two shillings to a bonesetter on the twentieth of that month. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and eighteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and all eighteen are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and this yard did not say that note out loud in the twelve weeks ended on the said Saturday and is not going to. The whole of the rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
