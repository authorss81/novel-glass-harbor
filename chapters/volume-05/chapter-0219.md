# Chapter 219

*The Thirty-Sixth Monday, and a Name Taken Off a List With a Pen*

Monday the eleventh and Tuesday the twelfth of January, on the mole, at the boards, and in the shed.

**On Monday the eleventh of January the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-sixth Monday since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held on that Monday.**

**That Monday the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, as it has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and stays nil.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Sunday at any hour.**

---

**And on the said Monday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back six times, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book.**

The board is twelve gross. The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

**And at about two in the afternoon of the same Monday, at the boards, the man of the second lay told the ship's hand a thing, and he told her and not the holder, and he did not say why, and she did not ask him, and he had a great deal of it and got through it in about four minutes.**

"**I have a man in that crew whose name is Davy, and he is thirty-one and he has a mother in the next town down this coast, and I hired him on Thursday last for today and I told him on Thursday last.**

"**And on Thursday last, at about four in the afternoon, before I had spoken to him, he had put his own name down at Harker's yard in the next town. He went up there on the Wednesday and put it down, because Harker's had a week of work in her and wanted two men for the Monday and the Tuesday, and he wanted the Monday and the Tuesday, and that is two days and about six marks, and he wanted it, and I have not got a complaint against him for that and I am not going to have one.**

"**And on Friday afternoon he took his name off that list. He went up and he said the Monday was not going, and a girl in Harker's office wrote his name off the list in a book with a pen in about four seconds, and he said thank you, and he came down the road and worked for me on Friday and he worked for me today.**

"**That is the whole of it and I have told you and not him because you write things down.**

"**What do you want me to do with it.**

"**Nothing. I want you to do exactly nothing with it and I want it written down that I did not ask you to do anything with it, because if it is written down that a man of the second lay brought a thing up a hill and said a name, then in four years there is a page in this harbor with a name on it that was not asked for, and that name came off a list and went on a page, and it was not on a page before Friday and now it is.**

"**So it goes in the book and the name does not.**

"**I am not asking you to leave his name out of a book. I have got a book. I am asking you not to put him in yours. There is a difference and you know the difference better than I do.**

"**Then it goes in without him.**

"**And the figure is about six marks and he will not get it back and he does not expect to and he told me on the hill on Friday that he had told them at Harker's on the Thursday before he told me, which I have thought about for two days and I have not worked out whether that was honest or clever.**

"**It was probably neither. It was a man with two days in front of him telling the second one first. I am not going to sit up here and decide which and neither are you.**"

She wrote it in the second column that night, and the name is not in this book, and the reason for that is entered against the line, and it is that a yard that puts a name in its own book on the day the name comes off another man's book has turned a man's list into a page, and that is the whole of what happened and the whole of what a page is for.

---

**And on Tuesday the twelfth day of January a wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is TWELVE, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant and the twelfth of January instant, and all twelve are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other.**

The reason against the said line is the one the holder entered in this column with his own left hand on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of December past, and it stands as it was written, and it is not being added to this month and it is not being said again.

**And the bag is sixteen marks, six shillings and four pence, which is two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of that, and the figure the bag is short of the wage is one thousand nine hundred and ninety-six pence, and it was one thousand and thirty-six pence a week ago, and three marks came in on the Monday and the figure got worse, and that is the whole of the arithmetic of it and the reason is on the page and is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December and is not being improved on because it is right.**

The column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest. The count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty.

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twelfth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on the said Monday and it came down bare, and that it was the thirty-sixth Monday since the first of May, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Monday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil; and that the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that the holder did not go over to him, and that he was not at that bench on the Sunday instant at any hour.*

*And that this yard worked on the said Monday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back six times; and that the gross on the board was twelve, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it, and that nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book; and that the pump was brought up that hill by a man of the second lay and the man was brought up that hill by the same man, as they have been brought up every time this yard has worked since the twelfth day of December past.*

*And that at about two in the afternoon of the said Monday at the boards a man of the second lay told the ship's hand a thing and told her and not the holder and did not say why and she did not ask him; and that the said man said that he has a man in that crew who is thirty-one and has a mother in the next town down this coast, and that he hired him on Thursday the seventh instant for the said Monday and told him on the said Thursday; and that on the said Thursday at about four in the afternoon, before the said man of the second lay had spoken to him, that man of thirty-one had put his own name down at a yard in the next town down this coast, having gone up there on the Wednesday, because that yard had a week of work in her and wanted two men for the Monday and the Tuesday, and that he wanted the Monday and the Tuesday, and that the figure of two days at a good trade is about six marks, and that the said man of the second lay said he has got no complaint against him for that and is not going to have one.*

*And that on Friday the eighth instant that man of thirty-one took his own name off that list, and that a woman in the office of that yard wrote his name off a list in a book with a pen in about four seconds, and that he said thank you and came down the road and worked on the Friday and worked on the said Monday; and that the figure of it is about six marks and that he will not get it back and does not expect to, and that he told the said man of the second lay on the hill on the Friday that he had told that yard on the Thursday before he told the said man, and that the said man has thought about that for two days and has not worked out whether it was honest or clever, and that the said woman said it was probably neither, and that it was a man with two days in front of him telling the second one first, and that neither of them is going to sit and decide which.*

*And that the said man of the second lay said that he wanted the ship's hand to do exactly nothing with it, and that if it were written down that a man of the second lay brought a thing up a hill and said a name, then in about four years there would be a page in this harbor with a name on it that was not asked for, and that the said name came off a list and went on a page and was not on a page before the Friday; and that the said woman said that it goes in the book and the name does not; and that the said man said that he was not asking her to leave the name out of a book, that she has got a book, and that he was asking her not to put him in hers, and that there is a difference and she knows the difference better than he does; and that the holder said nothing about any of that and was not in that conversation.*

*And that the said woman entered on the said evening that the said name is not in this book, and gave the reason against that line, which is that a yard that puts a name in its own book on the day the name comes off another man's book has turned a man's list into a page, and that that is the whole of what happened on the Friday instant and the whole of what a page is for; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill on any day of a week and look at this book and see that there is no name in it, and can go down to the next town down this coast and ask a yard there whether a man took a name off their list on the eighth of January instant and be told that he did.*

*And that on the said Tuesday the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes stands at twenty and did not move, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is twelve, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant and the twelfth of January instant, and that all twelve are entered in this book in the same breath; and that the reason against the said line is the reason entered in this column on the twenty-ninth day of December past in the holder's own left hand and is not improved on and is not shortened and is not going to be said again; and that the bag is sixteen marks, six shillings and four pence, which is two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, and that the figure the bag is short of the wage is one thousand nine hundred and ninety-six pence, and that it was one thousand and thirty-six pence a week before, and that three marks came in on the said Monday and the figure got worse.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the twelfth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of thirty-one took his name off a list in a book in the next town down this coast on Friday the eighth instant, and that a woman wrote it off with a pen in about four seconds, and that a man is not asked at that place again, and that this is not a day and it is not a wage and it cannot be put back by anybody, and that it is about six marks and that the figure is the least of it; and that a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills, and that the somebody else in this case was a woman in an office in another town with a pen and a book and about four seconds, and that she did not know what she was writing off and would not have cared and could not be asked about it if she were asked; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in this town can go up that road and ask a yard there and be told that a name came off a list on a day, and cannot ask what a list in a book does to a man in the next town in four years, and that what a list does to a man is not anybody's business except the man and the four people of this coast, and this party has not asked the four people of this coast and is not going to; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the eleventh of January and nothing came in on the twelfth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and sixteen marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, and that is the whole of the money that has come into this yard in the three weeks ended on the twelfth of January instant, and the whole of it is three days at the face of the second seam on hirers' own accounts and one penny has gone out of this yard since the sixth of December past and that penny was for a sheet of paper. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the yard did not say that note out loud in the five weeks ended on the twelfth of January and is not going to. A tin in that shed has not been opened since the twenty-eighth of April past and was not opened this week. A man of Ashtide is owed three marks for the eighth of August past and nobody has collected it and nobody has been asked about it and he is not in this book. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August past, and it has not been paid in these twenty days and was not paid in the twenty days before them. The eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past stand exactly where they stood, and he was not asked anything in these two days, and the chest was not opened and did not come down that hill. To a woman of this harbor, eight shillings and tenpence, with her name on the figure at her own asking and unpaid. The forty marks to a boy of twenty-two are whole and are not reduced and are not paid, the three nots of the eighth of June past stand, and the head of the third column is that boy's hand and nobody has been appointed to the column since the first of August past. The forty marks first after food to a person of no office are untouched and have been since the fifteenth of October past, and the woman they are owed to has told this yard not to ask her again this winter, and this yard has not asked her for anything and is not going to. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and twelve weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The far bench was occupied on the said Monday and not on the Sunday, and the holder did not go over to him. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and thirteen days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
