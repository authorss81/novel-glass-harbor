# Chapter 230

*Fifteen Weeks, a Letter Not Answered, and a Bench Under a Pump That Is Bare*

Tuesday the second and Wednesday the third of February, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**And on neither of those two days was the cistern behind the engine house charged, and this party walked no rota day in that time, and that is not a fault and is not a thing anybody has to account for: the rota runs out of days before this book does, and the last rota day in it is the first of this month, and the next one is the fourth of February next and is past the end of this book.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour either, and this party entered that and entered no figure of what came before it against that line.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and this yard did not work it, and that the reason against that half of the line is the reason already standing against the wage in that column and is not given a second time here, and that a day a yard loses on a Tuesday it has already given the reason for is entered as that day and not as a day it did not know about.**

---

**And on the said Tuesday a wage of four marks did not go in, and no envelope was made, and the column did not move, and the envelope count did not move, and the reason is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December past and is not improved on.**

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now FIFTEEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant, the nineteenth of January instant, the twenty-sixth of January instant and the second of February instant, and all fifteen are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other.**

---

**And on the said Wednesday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards.**

The board for the day is twelve gross. The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

**And that is the last day of work this yard books in this book, and it is booked, and it is in the book, and the cost of it is that it is three marks and it is a day and it is the whole of the money this yard makes, and it does not put a single one of the fifteen weeks on that Tuesday into an envelope.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Wednesday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed.**

She read it. He read it after her. And then neither of them read anything else for about two minutes, and the reason against that is that there is nothing left in that book to read out that is not one of the five things they have been saying for nine days.

"**Then say the one we have not said.**"

"**The one we have not said is a question and it is not a good one and I am going to say it anyway because it has been standing in the middle of that bench for nine days and nobody has picked it up.

"**A copy is not a page and has none of a page's standing. That is a line on a piece of paper that came in a crate of chalk out of a place two days down this coast, and it went down this coast on the Saturday in the inside of a man's coat, and it is the only sentence anybody has said to this yard in five volumes that this yard cannot argue with, because you cannot argue with a page about what a copy is when you have got a copy in your own drawer and you have never once been asked about it.

"**So the question is: what is a copy. Not what is it worth. What is it. There is a copy of a record of the twenty-fourth of November in that drawer and there is a copy of that copy, because a man on the twenty-second of July wrote out a first column on the boards and this yard has had it in type in this harbor and in about sixty boats since the fifteenth of August. And nobody in four volumes, in any room, in any market, in any shed, has ever said out loud what a copy is.

"**And a man of fifty-eight with a counter in a room off this market said on the twenty-eighth of January that a record a man can check is worth what the man is who checks it and a record nobody can check is worth what the person who keeps it is worth, and that there is no figure for either of them. That is a third thing. We have got a rule from a page, a price from a copyist, and no definition from anybody, and that is what this yard has got.

"**I am not going to answer it. Nobody in this shed is going to answer it tonight, and I am not going to answer it in a market, and I am not going to answer it in a room, and I am not going to answer it in this book, and the reason is that the answer to it is either nothing or it is the whole of the next two things that happen to this coast, and this yard is one and a half and there are twenty-five days to the first of March next.**"

"**You have had four pages in this shed that you cannot check, and every one of them has gone home, and you have a copy in a drawer that you cannot define, and you have been treating those two as one question for nine days.**

"**They are not the same question. One of them was a page that belongs to a place, and this yard had it for nine days, and it has gone down that coast in the inside of a man's coat. The other one is a piece of paper this yard bought at a counter and paid a penny for and has had for three months, and there are about sixty boats on this coast with a copy of it in them. Those are not the same and I am not going to sit here at half past nine at night and join them up because it is tidy.**

"**And I am going to leave the question standing where you have put it, and it is in the second column with a reason against the line, and the reason is that a thing this yard has not got a definition of is not a thing this yard is going to put a definition of into a book that a man in a city can read in four years.**"

---

**And the cost of that night is the printer, and it is paid and it is not going to be paid twice.**

There is a printer in a town of about two thousand up this coast who sent a letter on the twentieth of December with one question in it, and the question is where the copy of the record is, and he is not going to write again until this party writes to him, and this party has not written, and it has had a name and a question in the same drawer for six weeks and has used neither of them.

Calder Marr took the letter off the shelf and put it on the bench.

"**I want to write to him.**

"**And I have wanted to write to him since the twenty-second of December and I have not written, and the reason I have not written is on the page and I have not improved on it, and the reason is that a printer in that town keeps a list of everything that comes into his shop that anybody means to keep, and that is a fact about his trade and not a fault of him, and if this party writes to him then this party's name is in a book in a town of about two thousand people, and we would be a line in it, and a man with a book is a man we would then have to be able to lie to in four years.

"**And the reason I want to write is that we have got the answer to his question. We have had it since the twenty-fourth of November. I have had it under my hand since the twenty-ninth of November and I have not sent it and I am not going to, and the whole of what is left of that is the answer in a drawer and no letter.

"**So the cost is that the answer to a printer's question is in this yard's drawer and this yard is not going to send it, and that is the whole of it, and it goes in the first column and not the second, because a man in this town can get on the coach and go to that printer and ask him whether this yard has written to him and be told that it has not.**"

"**You are not going to write.**

"**I am not going to write this month. I have said the same thing on the twenty-second of December and I am not going to say it a second way tonight.**

"**I know. I have known since the twenty-second of December. I am the one who said it.**"

She put her hand flat on the bench, which she does.

"**And I want it entered that the holder wants to write and has wanted to for six weeks, and that the reason he has not is not the money and is not the writ and is that a man with a book is a man we would have to be able to lie to in four years. And that a man with a book is also a man who could be asked a question about a page by a person who is not you, and that this yard has spent four volumes telling this harbor that a man who is asked a question about a page is a man whose page is not his own any more.**"

"**That is exactly right and I have not improved on it.**
"**And you are not going to improve on it either, and we are not going to argue about it tonight, and I would like a drink of water before I say the last thing, because the last thing is a fact and it is not a good last thing and I would like to be able to say it straight.**"

---

**And the last thing is this, and it is on the page, and nobody in that shed improved on it.**

The writ of the first of May runs to midnight on the first of March next, is inside the harbor line, is bounded by the outer marks, carries four names and no fifth, is not the Second Bell license, does not lead to one, and renews nothing. **This yard is a party of one and a half until the first of March next, and there are twenty-five days between the third of February and the first of March next, and this yard has not worked out what the two of them are going to do with twenty-five days and is not going to work it out tonight, and that figure is a figure and not a plan.**

And the sheet and a half is not on that bench and cannot be put back. The copy of the record of the twenty-fourth of November is nine feet away in a drawer in that room, and the acknowledgment of the twenty-ninth of November is in the same drawer, and the half sheet of a court in a city is in the same drawer, and the printer's letter of the twentieth of December is on the bench with a question in it that has not been answered, and the sheet the shipwright bought at a counter off the fish market on the twenty-eighth of January has six titles on it and is going into the book and not out of it.

**And the bench under the pump is bare, and it is the only bare thing in that shed, and the shape of that bench is the shape of a sheet and a half that is not on it, and the copy of a record of the twenty-fourth of November is nine feet away in a drawer in that room, and that is a fact a man can check on any day of a week, and nobody in that shed improved on it.**
