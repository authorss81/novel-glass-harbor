# Chapter 210

*Three Weeks Read Out at the Head of This Dock, and a Day Refused*

Thursday the twenty-fourth and Friday the twenty-fifth of December, at the head of this dock, and in the shed.

**And on the Thursday morning at about ten the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and was not occupied on the Friday at any hour, and this party entered that and did not go and look at it twice.**

---

**And at about half past four on the Thursday afternoon, at the head of this dock, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, the two of them read the three weeks out, and it took about nine minutes, and nobody was asked to be quiet, and the yard's own second column was not read out in it.**

"**Seven marks, six shillings and four pence,**" said Nessa Pike. "**One thousand one hundred and ninety-six pence. On the sixth of December it was one mark and five pence more than that, and the whole of the difference is a day at the second seam at about three marks and a sheet of paper at a penny, and there is no figure in this harbor that is the price of any of it.

"**And on the Friday there was a day refused at about three marks and that figure is not in the bag and it is not going to be in it and it is the first day in four volumes that this yard has turned money down.

"**The column is twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the oldest line in it is the fifth of March. Twenty envelopes on a bench. Nine weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November, the seventeenth of November, the first of December, the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second. Two of those were not paid because there was a sheet of paper and a bone, and seven of them were not paid because there was nothing.

"**Three marks to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, and he was not asked anything this month, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor, with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand, and nobody has been appointed to that column. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and on the twenty-seventh a woman of no office wrote this yard four paragraphs and a price and the answer was no and there is not a shilling of it here.

"**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on the seventh, the fourteenth and the twenty-first, being the thirty-first, thirty-second and thirty-third Mondays since the first of May, and no sheet has gone up on any wall in this harbor in seven weeks and none came off, and that is entered.

"**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged on the seventh, tenth, fourteenth, seventeenth, twenty-first and twenty-four days of this month at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of them, and the charge of the ninth day of November stands entered with no figure against it, and this yard is not going to stand in a street and tell anybody what those six and that one make between them. The walk is nil and it stays nil.

"**The far bench was occupied on the seventh, the eighteenth and the twenty-first of this month, and on no other day of the three weeks, and neither brother went over to the other on any of the three.

"**And two men of the first lay are a morning down on a lighter, and two marks and a victual of it were paid on the Saturday evening for the Monday, and this party entered the price on the Sunday and did not ask for it and has not gone and looked for the man.

"**And a day of a man's own time, in nine places, is what a page costs that anybody in nine places could check, and a sheet and a shed and no money at all is what it costs that nobody could, and both of those figures came out of a woman's mouth at a counter on Friday last and this yard did not pay her a penny for them except a penny for a sheet of paper.

"**And there is a thing none of us can check, which is a page and a half with six heads on it, a form with an office's name at the foot of it and no man's name anywhere on it, a half sheet of a court in a city with the fourth of January on it, a sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it that wants a name, and a printer's letter with one question in it that this yard has put in a drawer and is not going to answer.

"**That is the three weeks. It is in my hand and his and there is not one line of the second column in it, and if anybody asks me in a room in a city in four years I am going to point at a page in our own book that says why.**"

Nobody said anything about the day refused, which is what about eleven people in a street say about a man who has turned down a day of work in the last week of December.

---

**And at about nine on the Friday morning a man of the second lay came up this hill about a day at the face of the second seam on the Saturday, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, at about three marks, and the holder said no.**

The man said it was the same man who came on the Friday the eleventh and Calder Marr said it was, and the man said a day is a day.

"**It is a day,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am turning it down, and I am going to say why in a doorway and not in a market, and the reason is a figure and not a principle.

"**A day at a seam is a figure that is earned. This yard is a party of one and a half with nine weeks of no envelope against it and a bag with seven marks and six shillings and four pence in it, and I am not turning down three marks in the last week of December because a man came up a hill with a sheet of paper. In January I will take every day that comes and I will not be saying that to anybody, and he will hear it in February when he asks me why I was busy in December.

"**And the second half of it is that I do not know what I am doing on Saturday morning. There is a form on that bench and there is a letter in a drawer and I have not answered either of them, and a man who books a day he does not know about books a day with a man in it twice, and this yard is already a day short.**"

The man of the second lay said that was the first time anybody had turned him down since the spring and that he would go down the hill. He did go down the hill.

"**Write it in your own hand and not in mine,**" said Nessa Pike, "**with the day of the twenty-fifth on the same line as the cost of it, and put the figure of three marks against it and not against nothing, because a day that is turned down is a day with a figure missing off it and this book is going to have that in it or it is going to be a book of days that all went the same way.**

"**And I am not going to be thanked for that line and neither of you is going to thank me.**"

---

**And at about half past eight on the Friday night in that shed, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in the room, the whole of the three weeks went into the first column and two lines of it went into the second, and the two of them had reasons against them.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-fifth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and with the date and the hour on it: that this yard entered on the said day, in its own left hand, that a yard which has spent four volumes saying that a record a man can check is the only kind worth having has found out in the three weeks ended on the said day that being checkable is being findable; and that the instrument it built to make itself credible is the instrument that is going to make it locatable; and that this yard built that instrument on purpose on the seventh day of July last, in a market, on the ground that a record a man can check is the only kind worth having, and that nobody made it do it and no office asked it to and no man in a coat ever said a word about it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill on any day of a week and check it, and that a man can go down to a counter off the fish market and ask a woman of fifty-eight what she copied on the thirtieth day of November past and for how much and on whose account, and that she will tell him because her trade is the only trade in this harbor that gets asked about a thing and answers it; and that a man can go and look at the wall in this market and count the sheets on it; and that a man can find out whether this yard has said anything in a market this month, and that it has said one thing, on the Wednesday the sixteenth instant, and that it did not take it back and is not going to.*

*And that this yard entered on the said day, in its own left hand, that a question was asked in a market in this harbor on the second day of December instant, in about forty people's hearing, by a man of the first lay who is fifty-one and is master of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons of his own, and that the question was what an office in this country that has kept records for three hundred years thought it was guarding against at the point at which it decided what it was allowed to lose; and that this yard did not answer it; and that the refusal is in this book's second column with the reason against the line, and that the reason is that this yard does not know, and that this yard said the same out loud in this market on the thirteenth day of August last in about forty people's hearing and is not going to say it a second time about the same thing; and that the whole of what this yard has on the question is a bare fact in the second column with a reason against it and no name in it, and that this yard is not going to call a fact an answer in a market and is not going to answer it in a book either, and that the refusal is the same refusal in a market and in a book and has not been improved on and is not going to be; and that no document in this harbor or in any other that this yard has seen would answer it, and that this yard has not been shown one and does not know whether one exists.*

*And that on Friday the twenty-fifth instant at about nine in the morning a man of the second lay came up this hill about a day at the face of the second seam on the Saturday instant, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, at about three marks, being the same man who came on the eleventh instant, and that the holder said no; and that the reason against the line, which the holder gave in the doorway and not in a market, is that a day at a seam is a figure that is earned, and that this yard is a party of one and a half with nine weeks of no envelope standing against it and a bag with seven marks, six shillings and four pence in it, and that it is not turning down three marks in the last week of December because a man came up a hill with a sheet of paper; and that the second half of the reason is that the holder does not know what he is doing on the Saturday morning, and that a man who books a day he does not know about books a day with a man in it twice, and that this yard is already a day short; and that the figure of three marks is entered against that day and not against nothing, and that a day that is turned down is a day with a figure missing off it, and that the said woman said she wanted it in the holder's hand and not in hers and that she is not going to be thanked for it; and that the said man said it was the first time anybody had turned him down since the month of March past and that he went down the hill.*

*And that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on Thursday the twenty-fourth instant and this party walked it, and the walk is nil; and that the far bench was occupied on the seventh, the eighteenth and the twenty-first days of December instant and on no other day of the three weeks, and that neither brother went over to the other on any of the three, and that it was not occupied on the said Thursday or the said Friday at any hour; and that this party went past both objects of nobody's on the crown of that mole on the Friday morning and touched neither, and the figure against them is in the closing position below and is not given twice.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the twenty-fifth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this yard has found out in three weeks that being checkable is being findable, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check where a page of this yard's has been in nine days and cannot check where it will be wanted in four years, and that what a record is worth in four years to the four people of this coast it did not tell about is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the four of them, who wrote a line in the month of August and who have not been asked and are not going to be; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And that this party entered on the said day that a day of about three marks was refused by this party on the twenty-fifth instant, and that the reason against that line is not checkable from a mole, and that whether a yard which is one and a half with nine weeks of no envelope standing against it should turn down a day in the last week of December is not a question of arithmetic, and that the arithmetic is that the day is three marks and the bag is seven marks and six shillings and four pence and that both of those are figures anybody can check, and that the reason is not a figure, and that the four people of this coast who could say whether the reason is a good one are not going to be asked about it.*

**No money came into this yard on the Thursday and none on the Friday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and three marks was refused on the Friday and is not in the bag, and seven marks, six shillings and four pence is in it, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-six pence, and the bag held four marks and six shillings and five pence on the sixth day of December and the whole of the difference between those two figures is a day at the second seam at about three marks and a sheet of paper at a penny, and no figure in this harbor is the price of any of it. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the answer that came up this hill on the twenty-seventh of November was no. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, nine weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is not on the schedule of this harbor authority, nor of the circuit office, nor of anybody's, and the four people it belongs to hold a limit and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and did not ask this month. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and ninety-five days up the crown of this mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on any day of the three weeks. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office, and no better letter was written about it in three weeks. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. Neave Tarrant, nine winters, not found, one line, and this yard has not gone. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these three weeks and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years, and the four of them were told that in a street on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these three weeks. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. And this yard has been checkable on purpose since the seventh of July last and a sheet with an office's name at the foot of it and no signature on it went nine days from a market in this harbor to a line in a printer's book in a town of about two thousand, and this yard is not going to answer the one question in the letter that caused it, and a day of about three marks was refused on the Friday with the figure of three marks entered against it and the reason against the line, and that is the whole of the three weeks and every part of it is on this page and anybody in this town can come up this hill on any day of a week and look at it.**
