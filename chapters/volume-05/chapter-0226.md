# Chapter 226

*Two Hours at the Top of a Bench, and a Fifth Thing None of the Four Says*

Monday the twenty-fifth and Tuesday the twenty-sixth of January, in the shed.

**On that Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-eighth Monday since the first of May, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held.**

**And at about ten on that Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure and this party walked it, as it has walked none of them since the third of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the third of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour, and this party entered that and put no figure against it of what came before it.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Monday and none on the said Tuesday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against that line is that the four of them were on that bench from about two until about four on the said Monday afternoon and that a day this yard spends reading four pages that are not its own is not a day this yard works, and that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and that the reason against that half of the line is the reason already standing against the wage in that column and is not given a second time here.**

---

**And a man of sixty-four came up that hill on the said Monday at about eight with a book under his arm and took the fourth page of the ninth leaf out of it himself and put it on the bench and shut the book and took the book away again, and he said that he would be back for it on the Tuesday at about four, and he said that he would come at four and not at eight, and that the reason was that he did not want to be in the room.**

"**You are not going to be in the room on the Wednesday either, and the reason is going in the first column and I am telling you the whole of it now while you can still ask me a different question.

"**A page read in a room with the person who keeps it in it is a page read to a person. And a thing that is read to a person is a thing that person has agreed with by being quiet, and I have been in a room in this town where a man was quiet for an hour and a half and it was put in a book afterwards as though he had been asked.**"

"**Then we will do it without you.**
"**And I am going to say the other thing, because you have been decent about this and you are owed the other thing. The second page on that bench is yours, and you are seventy-one, and the reading is going to be of a page you keep in a house. And I am not going to be in the room for it, and I am not going to be told about it afterwards, and I do not want it copied.**

"**If you copy it you will have a copy, and there is a piece of paper on that bench at this moment that says a copy is not a page, and I have not read that paper and I am not going to, because that paper is about a place that is not this harbor and I have never been asked anything about it and I am not going to start.**

"**So there is no copy. You will get yours back on the Tuesday and you will never know what was said about it, and that is the price of not being in the room and I am paying it and I would like it noticed.**"

---

**And they read the four from about two until about four on the Monday afternoon, at the top of that bench, in that shed, in about two hours, by two people, and there was no clerk in that room and no man with a seal in that room and no market anywhere near it, and nobody else came up that hill in those two hours except a man with a barrow who did not stop.**

The first page is a night. There is a hard night and there is a light on a point and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light.

The second page is the same night from about two hundred yards the other way, and there was no light on the point, and the man that keeps it had the lamp out the week before and the oil was not carried, and the two boats that came in were in before it was light and were not in on the light. The same page also carries a sale of the right to take what came ashore, written out in words and not in figures, and the words do not add up to the figures.

The third page is a man. He went out on a Tuesday in a bad month in a boat of about sixty tons and did not come back, and there was a lantern in his pocket and a boot knife and two shillings and sixpence, and the boat came back and he did not.

The fourth page is not about anything that happened. It is four lines about a page, and a name at the head of it, and one line at the foot in a later hand.

**And the four are incompatible, and all four of them are true, and this party entered that on the Monday night and did not improve on it and is not going to.**

They put the light and the no-light side by side first, and that is the one that cannot be got round, and the reason it cannot be got round is that neither of the two pages says where the person was standing when it was written, and one of them is about a mile and a half from the point and one of them is about two hundred yards from it, and both of them were written at night.

"**We could write a sentence that settles it,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have written it four times in my head this afternoon and I have not put it on the page, and I am going to tell you what it would say and why I am not writing it. It would say that the two pages cannot both be of the same night. And that is true. And it does not say that one of them is a lie. It does not even say that one of them is about a different night. It says that if they are both about the same night then something is wrong, and if they are not then we have two nights and a page that is wrong, and either way the thing that is wrong is in this shed and not at either of the two places, and I am not going to write a sentence with that in it.**"

Then they put the third page beside the first, because a boat of about sixty tons is in both of them, and the man of sixty-four has already said the thing about that, and the holder said the same thing in his own words and got the same answer from the second page: **a boat of about sixty tons came in on the tide and was not asked where she was from**, and **a boat of about sixty tons went out on a Tuesday and came back**, and those are two boats, and this yard is not going to make them into one, because that would be a thing this yard did.

Then they put the fourth page beside the other three, and it is where it does the most and where it says the least.

"**A copy is not a page and has none of a page's standing,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and that sentence is written on a copy. I am not going to say that makes it nothing. I am going to say that a man wrote a thing down, and then a person copied it out at some point, and the person is not of the place it belongs to and is not alive as far as we know, and the copy is what a man selling lime finds in a crate.**

"**And a page is not to be carried out of the place it is about, and there is a man going to collect a page from this bench on Tuesday at four and put it back in a book and carry it four days up this coast, and I have not asked him whether he is one of the nine and neither has he and neither are we going to.

"**And at the foot of that page in a later hand of that same place it says that a page was carried out of that place in a year nobody wrote down and was brought back in another year and has been kept ever since in a house that is not the house it was kept in before.**

"**So the fourth says the carrying is not to happen, and the fourth's own foot says it happened, and the fourth's own foot does not say it was wrong, and that line is in a hand of that place and it is later, and I do not know who wrote it and I have not got a way of finding out and I am not going to look.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

---

**And at about ten past four on the Monday they stopped, and that is about two hours and it is the whole of the reading, and there is a second column line about it and it is in the reason that the reading was in a shed and not in a room.**

"**A third person in this room would have made it checkable,**" said the holder. "**And a third person in this room is a person who can be asked about it afterwards by a man with a seal, and there is no version of this in which that man asks him and gets the wrong answer out of him. There is a version in which he asks him the right one and writes his name at the top of the page in a city and we never hear of it. That is the whole of what a third person buys and I am not buying it and I have not got the three marks for it and would not spend them.**

"**So there are two of us and one other person and that is the whole of the finding and the whole of who knows it.**"

Nessa Pike wrote that in the first column and then made herself write it again in the second one, because the reason against the second is that whether a finding made in a shed is worth less than a finding made in a room is not checkable from a mole.

---

**And on Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of January a wage of four marks did not go in, and no envelope was made, and the column did not move, and the envelope count did not move, and the reason is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December past and is not improved on.**

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now FOURTEEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant, the nineteenth of January instant and the twenty-sixth of January instant, and all fourteen are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other.**

**And at about four on that Tuesday afternoon a woman of seventy-one came up that hill and took her page off that bench and put it inside her coat, and did not ask a single question about the reading, and had told this yard on the Saturday that she did not want to be told about it afterwards, and a man of sixty-four came up at about ten past four and took the fourth page of the ninth leaf off the top of that bench, where this yard had left it, and put it into his own book and shut the book, and the man of the second lay came up at about half past four and took the oilcloth off the bench and did not ask what had been said about anything, and went down the hill.**

**And the fourth of the four is still on that bench, and the man who sells lime and chalk said he would come back for it on the Saturday, and this yard has not got his word for a day after that and has not asked him for one.**

---

**And the fifth thing came out of the two hours and it is the only thing this party is going to say about all four of them, and it is not what anybody went into that shed for.**

Nessa Pike found it at about ten past four and she did not find it by reading. She found it by going back to the first column of this yard's own book with a pencil in her hand and counting the dates, and then she went back to the bench and counted something else.

"**There is not one date on any of the four.**

"**The first has no year against the ninth. The second has none against the ninth and none against the sale, and the sale says that the words do not add up to the figures. The third says a bad month and no year. The fourth has no date on it at all, and whoever copied it did not put the date of the copying on it either, and that is the hand of a person who thought the date of the thing was the date.

"**Four places. Four things. Four pages. And not one date between the four of them, and not one of them can be put into this yard's first column as it stands, and the reason is that a date is a thing you have to get from somewhere and none of the four of them got it from anywhere.**

"**And I am not going to make four dates up. I have got a pencil out and I have been sitting here for ten minutes with it and I have not put anything on the page, and I want it entered that I did not put anything on the page, because I know exactly what the four dates would look like if I invented them and they would look right.**"

"**Then that is the fifth thing and that is all it is,**" said Calder Marr. "**It is a thing about the shape of the four. It is not what any of the four says. It is not the answer to anything and it is not a question anybody has asked. Nobody in this shed is going to say what it means, and I am not going to say what it means, and if the four pages are read out in a room in four years by somebody who has never seen this bench, then what they will take from them is what is on them, and what is on them is four things that happened at four places and no dates at all.**"

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and came down bare, and that it was the thirty-eighth Monday since the first of May, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held; and that at about ten on the said Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the third of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of; and that the far bench was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour nor on the said Tuesday at any hour, and that this party entered that and put no figure against it of what came before it.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Monday and none on the said Tuesday and the board was not written on either of them, and that the reason against that line is that the four of them were on that bench from about two until about four on the said Monday afternoon and that a day this yard spends reading four pages that are not its own is not a day this yard works, and that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and that the reason against that half of the line is the reason already standing against the wage in that column and is not given a second time here.*

*And that a man of sixty-four came up that hill on the said Monday at about eight with a book under his arm and took the fourth page of the ninth leaf out of it himself and put it on that bench and shut the book and took the book away again, and that he said he would be back for it on the said Tuesday at about four and would come at four and not at eight, and gave the reason, that he did not want to be in the room, and that the said reason is that a page read in a room with the person who keeps it in it is a page read to a person, and that a thing read to a person is a thing that person has agreed with by being quiet, and that the said holder has been in a room in this town where a man was quiet for an hour and a half and it was put in a book afterwards as though he had been asked.*

*And that a woman of seventy-one sent word on the said Saturday, by a boy of about eleven who was paid a penny out of a box at the top of that mole and not out of this yard's money, and that she would not be in the room on the said Monday, and would come up that hill on the said Tuesday at about four for her page and would not stop, and would not be told about the reading afterwards, and did not want a copy of it, and gave the reason, that there is a piece of paper on that bench that says a copy is not a page, and that she has not read that paper and is not going to, because that paper is about a place that is not this harbor and she has never been asked anything about it; and that the said holder entered that the whole of that is to his credit and not hers, and that the reason against that is that a woman who is owed a thing and is not told about it has not been given a thing.*

*And that the four of them read the four pages from about two until about four on the said Monday afternoon, at the top of that bench, in that shed, in about two hours, by two people, and that there was no clerk in that room and no man with a seal in that room and no market anywhere near it, and that nobody else came up that hill in those two hours except a man with a barrow who did not stop.*

*And that the first page is a night, being a hard night and a light on a point and two boats that came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light; and that the second page is the same night from about two hundred yards the other way, being no light on the point and the man that keeps it having the lamp out the week before and the oil not carried, and the two boats that came in being in before it was light and not in on the light; and that the said second page also carries a sale of the right to take what came ashore, written out in words and not in figures, and that the words do not add up to the figures; and that the third page is a man who went out on a Tuesday in a bad month in a boat of about sixty tons and did not come back, with a lantern in his pocket and a boot knife and two shillings and sixpence, and that the boat came back and he did not; and that the fourth page is four lines about a page, and a name at the head of it, and one line at the foot in a later hand.*

*And that the four are incompatible and that all four of them are true, and that this party entered that and did not improve on it; and that the said party put the line about the light beside the line about no light first, and that the reason that cannot be got round is that neither of the two pages says where the person was standing when it was written, and that one of them is about a mile and a half from the point and one of them is about two hundred yards from it.*

*And that the said holder said that a sentence could be written that settles it and that he had written it four times in his head and had not put it on the page, and that the said sentence would be that the two pages cannot both be of the same night, and that that is true, and that it does not say that one of them is a lie, and that it does not say that one of them is about a different night, and that it says that if they are both about the same night then something is wrong, and if they are not then there are two nights and a page that is wrong, and that either way the thing that is wrong is in that shed and not at either of the two places.*

*And that the said party put the third page beside the first, and that a boat of about sixty tons is in both of them, and that this yard is not going to make them into one, and that the reason against that is that that would be a thing this yard did.*

*And that the said woman said, of the fourth page, that a copy is not a page and has none of a page's standing, and that the said sentence is written on a copy, and that she was not going to say that makes it nothing, and that a man wrote a thing down and then a person copied it out at some point, and that the copy is what a man selling lime found in a crate; and that she said that a page is not to be carried out of the place it is about, and that a man was going to collect a page from that bench on the said Tuesday and carry it four days up this coast, and that this party has not asked him whether he is one of the nine and neither has he; and that at the foot of the said fourth page in a later hand of that same place it says that a page was carried out of that place in a year nobody wrote down and was brought back in another year and has been kept ever since in a house that is not the house it was kept in before, and that the said fourth says the carrying is not to happen and its own foot says it happened, and that the said foot does not say it was wrong.*

*And that at about ten past four on the said Monday they stopped, and that that is about two hours and is the whole of the reading, and that the said holder said that a third person in that room would have made it checkable, and that a third person in that room is a person who can be asked about it afterwards by a man with a seal, and that there is no version of this in which that man asks him and gets the wrong answer out of him, and that there is a version in which he asks him the right one and writes his name at the top of the page in a city and this party never hears of it; and that the said woman wrote that in the first column and then made herself write it again in the second, and that the reason against the second is that whether a finding made in a shed is worth less than a finding made in a room is not checkable from a mole.*

*And that on the said Tuesday a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is fourteen, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant, the nineteenth of January instant and the twenty-sixth of January instant, and that all fourteen are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other.*

*And that at about four on the said Tuesday afternoon a woman of seventy-one came up that hill and took her page off that bench and put it inside her coat and did not ask a single question about the reading; and that a man of sixty-four came up at about ten past four and took the fourth page of the ninth leaf off the top of that bench, where this party had left it, and put it into his own book and shut the book; and that the man of the second lay came up at about half past four and took the oilcloth off that bench and did not ask what had been said about anything and went down that hill; and that the fourth of the four is still on that bench and that the man who sells lime and chalk said he would come back for it on the Saturday, and that this party has not got his word for a day after that and has not asked him for one; and that the whole of the money this yard has made off anybody for any of the four of these pages is nothing, and that the whole of the money this yard has spent on any of the four of them is one penny, and that the penny was not spent on them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the four of the four pages are incompatible and all four of them are true, and that this party has entered that and has not resolved the incompatibility and is not going to resolve it, and that the reason against that is that the disagreement is between two pages about a thing neither of them was in a position to see whole, and that this party is not going to put a party in a position it was not in.*

*And that the said woman entered on the said day that there is not one date on any of the four of the pages, and that the reason she found that out is that she went back to the first column of this yard's own book with a pencil in her hand and counted the dates, and then went back to that bench and counted what was on it; and that she sat down with a pencil for about ten minutes and put nothing on the page, and that she said that she knows exactly what the four dates would look like if she invented them and that they would look right, and that she was not going to; and that this party entered that she did not, and that the fifth thing is a thing about the shape of the four and is not what any of the four says and is not a conclusion and is not an answer to a question anybody has asked.*

*And that the said woman said that if this party copies the said page it will have a copy, and that a copy of a page would have to be got from a place two hundred yards off the water by a woman who is seventy-one, or bought from a woman of fifty-eight in a room off the fish market, and that the reason it is not going to happen is not the money and is the fourth of the four; and that this party entered that it would give a year to have a copy of a page and is not going to have one, and that the reason against that is that a sheet in a crate is the only evidence this party has about whether a copy of a page is worth anything, and that evidence is going back down this coast in a barrow on the Saturday.*

*And that the four nameless lines above the said named line are still four and are not struck out, and that the request to strike a line out of a column is refused in one set of words and the same set for any column.*

**No money came into this yard on the twenty-fifth of January and none on the twenty-sixth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and twenty-five marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is four thousand and seventy-six pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three thousand four hundred and thirty-six pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and fourteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and a fifteenth falls due on Tuesday the second of February next. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and this page does not give it a second time.**
