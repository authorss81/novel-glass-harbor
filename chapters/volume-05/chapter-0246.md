# Chapter 246

*One Page, One Drawer, One Town, and One Hand*

Saturday the sixth and Sunday the seventh of March, in the shed, and at the post in that town.

**Neither of the two days of this chapter is a rota day and the cistern behind the engine house was charged on neither of them, and that is entered with a reason, and the reason is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and the sixth and seventh days of March are a Saturday and a Sunday.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten until about two and was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on either of the said two days, and a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything on either of the said two days, and asking nothing on either of the said two days, and no fragment and no report talk and no second story, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October past.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the reason against that line is that a Saturday is the day the two of them have had since the twentieth of February past and that the holder of that writ is not in that harbor. And on the said Sunday this yard worked from eight until about one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and the day's book was written in the ship's hand in her own left hand at about half past one and not afterwards and it took her about twice as long as it takes her.**

---

**And on the said Saturday morning at about nine the ship's hand put a sheet of paper on the boards with her left hand and wrote on it, and at about two that afternoon she folded it and put it in the drawer on those benches, and the drawer is the drawer and not a new one, and the four pieces of paper that were in it are in it now and were in it before she came down the hill.**

The page is not long. It is a page about that harbor, made in that harbor, and it says so on its face, in the first line and in the last, that it is not a document and not a record and not evidence and not a return and not a copy of anything, and that it is about a place and the place is the place it is about.

What is on it is what anybody in that harbor could stand on that mole and say is true. The mole, and the second seam's own face, and the ninth seam's mooring with an end of it entered for the first of September past and found in service on that day. The weir at the south end of that mole, called the Sill, with its stone under four feet of stone and a foot and four inches above the third of six joints. The point with the light, and the fact that the light was burning on a night in one household's account and was not lit in another household's account, and that both are that household's page and neither is a lie. A cistern behind the engine house charged on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday at no figure by four men that party does not know. A fire party's hose dead until the spring. A mole-head light not on because a light costs oil. One herring berth of two out and the north berth out. A tide gang four men short. A hard at Ashtide unanswered. Four days in the middle of July past in about sixty boats on that coast that cannot be unprinted, and the thirty-third of them have a man's name at the end of them and four do not, and the four do not go out of a column because they have been printed.

**And at the head of the page, in the said woman's own hand, is a line that is the whole of it and is not a heading: *A page about this place, in this place's hand, on this place's paper. It is not to be carried out of this place. Any person this page is about may come to the shed on that mole and read it, or not, and the person who keeps it may say yes or no to any one of them at any time and cannot be made to say yes. Nobody may be asked for it. Nobody may be told it exists. There is no office to ask and no form to fill in and no penalty on anybody for not producing it, and that is not an accident and it is the whole of the design.***

---

**And at about half past four on the said Saturday the said woman walked up the hill from the post with four sheets of paper in four envelopes in her coat and the receipt in her left hand, and the fourpence is entered and is a going-out and not a going, and she said out loud on the steps of that shed the thing that is the finding of the whole of this chapter and she said it to nobody because there was nobody there.**

"**Four sheets, four envelopes, four places and not one name, and the addresses are the addresses of places and not the addresses of people, and one of them is a chain of reed islands and floating markets and I have never been on it and I have never sent anybody to it and I have written its name on an envelope with a street in it that a woman there gave me in February and that I put in this book and have not looked at since.**

"**And the price of a going was named before the going and is not a going. Four days there and four days back is two men and a passage and about twenty-four marks, and I have not got twenty-four marks and I have not got the four days and I am not going to pretend that a sheet in a post is a man in a room.

"**And this is what the other four can do about this one, and it is the whole of what anybody can do about any of them: they can come to that shed on that mole and ask. That is it. There is no counter they can go to. There is no form. There is nobody in any city they can write to, because there is no office that has heard of this and this yard has not told one. And if a man of a hundred in a room four hundred miles away asks them whether the page is in the drawer, they can say no, and they can say no for forty years, and nobody can make them say anything else.**

"**And the four sheets in the post are gone. I put them in at about half past four on the Saturday and the man took the money and gave me a receipt and the receipt is for four pence and it does not say what is in them. If one of those four does not go, it does not go, and there is no return and no record of a return and no way of knowing in four years, and that is what it costs to make a thing where five towns keep a page each and nobody can be made to show it, and I have paid fourpence for the whole of it.**

"**And the one that is in that drawer went in in my hand at about two o'clock this afternoon and I am the person who keeps it, and the holder of that writ is up a coast road and has not been asked and is not going to be, and that is entered, and a person who keeps a page has to be somebody and not an office, and if it has to be somebody then it is me.**"

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the seventh day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that neither of the said two days is a rota day and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged on neither of them, and that the reason against that line is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten until about two and was not occupied at any hour on the said Sunday; and that the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on the said Saturday and did not go over to him on the said Sunday; and that a man of sixty-one was not asked for anything on either of the said two days and asked nothing on either of them; and that there was no fragment and no report talk and no second story.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the reason against that line is that a Saturday is the day the two of them have had since the twentieth of February past and that the holder of that writ is not in that harbor; and that on the said Sunday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the said woman's own left hand at about half past one and not afterwards.*

*And that on the said Saturday morning at about nine the said woman put a sheet of paper on those boards and wrote on it in her own left hand, and that at about two in the afternoon of the said Saturday she folded it and put it in the drawer on those benches, and that the said drawer is the drawer and not a new one, and that the four pieces of paper that were in that drawer are in it now and were in it before she came down that hill.*

*And that the said page is not long and is about that harbor and is made in that harbor, and says on its face in the first line and in the last that it is not a document and not a record and not evidence and not a return and not a copy of anything, and that it is about a place and the place is the place it is about; and that on it are that mole, the face of the second seam, the ninth seam's mooring with an end of it entered for the first of September past and found in service on that day, the weir at the south end of that mole called the Sill with its stone under four feet of stone and a foot and four inches above the third of six joints, the point with the light and the two households' accounts of one night, a cistern charged on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday at no figure by four men that party does not know, a fire party's hose dead until the spring, a mole-head light not on because a light costs oil, one herring berth of two out and the north berth out, a tide gang four men short, a hard at Ashtide unanswered, and the four days in the middle of July past in about sixty boats on that coast with the thirty-third of them carrying a man's name and four carrying nothing and the four not coming out of a column because they have been printed.*

*And that at the head of the said page, in the said woman's own hand, is a line that is not a heading and is the whole of the said page, being that a page about that place is in that place's hand on that place's paper, and is not to be carried out of that place; and that any person the said page is about may come to that shed on that mole and read it or not; and that the person who keeps it may say yes or no to any one of them at any time and cannot be made to say yes; and that nobody may be asked for it and nobody may be told it exists; and that there is no office to ask and no form to fill in and no penalty on anybody for not producing it, and that that is not an accident and is the whole of the design.*

*And that at about half past four on the said Saturday the said woman went up that hill from the post with four sheets in four envelopes in her coat and a receipt in her left hand; and that the fourpence is entered and is a going-out and not a going; and that the four envelopes are addressed to four places and not one name, and that one of the four places is a chain of reed islands and floating markets which that party has never been on and has sent nobody to, and that the address of that place is one a woman there gave in the month of February past and that it is in that book and has not been looked at since; and that the receipt is for fourpence and does not say what is in the four envelopes.*

*And that the price of a going was named before the going, being four days there and four days back, two men and a passage and about twenty-four marks, and that the said party has not got twenty-four marks and has not got the four days and has sent nobody and is not going to; and that a sheet in a post is not a man in a room and is not going to be improved on by anybody.*

*And that the said woman entered what the other four can do about the page in that drawer, being that they can come to that shed on that mole and ask, and that there is no counter they can go to and no form and nobody in any city they can write to, because there is no office that has heard of this and that yard has not told one; and that if a man in a room four hundred miles away asks them whether the page is in the drawer they can say no, and can say no for forty years, and that nobody can make them say anything else.*

*And that the four sheets in the post are gone, and that if one of the four does not go it does not go, and that there is no return and no record of a return and no way of knowing in about four years, and that that is what it costs to make a thing where five towns keep a page each and nobody can be made to show it, and that fourpence is what that yard paid for the whole of it.*

*And that the page in that drawer went in in the said woman's hand at about two in the afternoon of the said Saturday, and that the said woman is the person who keeps it, and that the holder of that writ has not been asked and is not going to be, and that a person who keeps a page has to be somebody and not an office, and that if it has to be somebody then it is the said woman.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the seventh day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this is the only instrument that yard has ever made that that yard cannot produce, and that it was made on the sixth of March instant and is not dated any earlier and cannot be dated any earlier by anybody, and that there is no entry about it in that book before the said day and there is not going to be one after it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a sheet went into a drawer and cannot check that a sheet is in a drawer in a town nine days away, and that in about four years the whole of what is known about this thing is four words on a receipt.*

*And that a page in a drawer in five towns is a page no man in this harbor can look at, and that this yard has spent five volumes insisting that a page a man cannot check is worth less than a page a man can look at, and that the yard knows that, and has entered it, and is not going to get out of it by a better sentence.*

*And that the four lines above the said named line are four days in the month of July past with a day at the end of them and nothing else, and are still four, and are still nameless, and are not struck out, and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that a line does not come out of a column because it has been printed and cannot be got back.*

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and three marks came into it on the said Sunday, and four pence went out of it on the said Saturday for the postage of four envelopes at the post in that town, and nothing went out of it on the said Sunday, and sixty-four marks, three shillings and ten pence is in the bag, which is ten thousand two hundred and eighty-six pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is nine thousand six hundred and forty-six pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Sunday is five days at the face of the second seam, being two thousand four hundred pence, being the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth days of February past and the first, the fifth and the seventh days of March instant, and no two of the five were worked by the same two people. The whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Sunday is fourpence, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and seven pence in five transactions at three counters, being a penny on the eighteenth of December past, a penny on the twenty-eighth of January instant, a penny on the thirteenth of February instant, two shillings to a bonesetter on the twentieth of February, and fourpence at the post on the sixth of March. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and nineteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, and the second of March instant, and all nineteen are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. The rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
