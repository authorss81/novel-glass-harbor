# Chapter 238

*A List of Names Is a List of Who Can Be Asked What*

Thursday the eighteenth and Friday the nineteenth of February, in the shed, on the face of the second seam, and at the head of this dock.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged on the said Thursday at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifth of the six rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and one of the six is left and this party is not going to walk that one either and has entered that it is not going to on the eighteenth of February and will not improve on it before the twenty-second.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line.**

**And on the said Thursday this yard worked from eight until about half past twelve on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and did not stay, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times. The day went faster than a day goes with three on it and neither of them said why and the reason is in the second column and is not the tide.**

---

**And at about six on the Friday evening, with the market gone in and about eleven people standing at the head of that dock, a man of about sixty of the second lay said something to this yard that this yard has not been able to put down since, and he was not asked to say it and this party did not ask him anything and has not asked him anything before or since, and he is not a friend of this party and has not said that he is and would not know what to do with it if he had, and he is not on a list of anything and there is no list in this harbor he is on.**

His name is Hiram Voss and he is a sailmaker and he has a boy of seventeen in one of the sixty boats.

"**I have got your sheet in my kitchen.**"

"**I know you have. There are sixty of them.**

"**Then you have not got one. You have got four, and my boy brought one and a man of the first lay brought one and a man of the third lay brought one and a man I do not know brought one, and the four of them are not the same sheet, and that is not because somebody wrote them different. Two of them are different because they are different weeks of a thing that is still going.**"

Nobody at the head of that dock said anything and about eleven people stood in it.

"**I am sixty years old and I make sails, and I have never in my life wanted to know what a day of anybody's was worth. I want you to hear that before I say the rest of it, because what I am going to say is going to sound like I have come here to be clever and I have not.

"**On the Sunday in January my boy came home with a sheet out of a boat's locker and I could not read it. I am sixty and I can read. I have never learned to read a thing like that, and that is not your fault and it is not anybody's, and there is a man in a room off the fish market who reads a sheet out for a penny and I went to him and he read it to me and I asked him to read it to me again and he said he would not read the same sheet twice for the same penny.

"**And I sat down at that table with it, and here is what it is. It is not a list of days. I have had a list of days out of that man, my own days, in that room, and a list of days is a list of days and you do not need anybody to tell you what it is.

"**It is a list of names.

"**Thirty-eight lines. Thirty-seven of them are a day, and thirty-three of them have a man's name at the end of them and four do not, and my boy's name is on the twelfth of August, and the man who set it in type is not on it and neither are you, and there is a line at the foot of it that says go and read it at the head of that dock.

"**And I have been sitting in my kitchen for a month with that sheet on the table in front of me, and I am not a clever man, and it took me until Tuesday of this week to say the thing out loud, and I am going to say it now and then I am going to go home.

"**A list of names is a list of who can be asked what.

"**That is what your sheet is. It is not a record. A record is a thing you can go and check against a man who was there. That sheet is a thing that says: this man, on this day, did this thing, and here is where to find me about it. Thirty-seven times. In order. With a man's name at the end of thirty-three of them and nothing at the end of four.

"**And you did not print it as that. You printed it because a clerk in a city in November said a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and you decided that the only answer to that is a page you can check, and you were right, and I have read it four times and I would give you the boat back tomorrow.

"**And a page that says who can be asked what is not a record. It is an instruction. It is the only instruction in that sheet. Everything above the last line is a receipt and the last line is an order, and you have printed thirty-seven receipts and one order and the order is the one anybody uses.

"**And the four lines with nothing at the end of them. The four in the middle of July. That is the part of your sheet that is an instruction to anybody, and it is the only part, and it is the only part of that sheet that says a man is not to be asked. I have been in this harbor forty years and I have never seen a piece of paper tell a man not to ask a question before, and your four days in July do it, and you did not know you had printed them, and I found them in October and I have not known what to do with them since.**"

He put his hat on.

"**I am not asking you for anything. I have not asked you for anything and I am not going to. I came down here to say that and I have said it and I am going up that mole.

"**And I am going to say the last of it and it is not clever either, and it is the only reason I stood here for four minutes instead of two. I have a boy of seventeen in one of those boats and I did not know until the Sunday in January what a man does on the eighth of August. And I did not know that there was a place I could go and ask. And now there is, and it is written on a piece of paper in my kitchen with his name on it, and I have not used it and I am not going to, and I would have liked to have it written down that I did not use it and that I was not going to, and I am telling a man on a mole instead because there is nowhere to write that down.

"**That is what your sheet did. It is not a record about a yard. It is a place where a man goes to find out who he can ask about his own boy. And you printed it in August and I found it in January and that is five months and it is about four weeks longer than it needed to be and neither of those is anybody's fault and one of them is not going to happen again.**"

He went up the mole and nobody stopped him and about eleven people in that street said nothing at all, and it is entered that they said nothing and that it was not asked of them.

---

**And at about half past eight on the Friday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed, and the two of them not saying anything for about three minutes before either of them started.**

"**Four months,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Four months. It went up on a wall in this town on the fifteenth of August and it has been in sixty boats since and it took a man of sixty who has a boy of seventeen to work out what it was for, and he worked it out at his own kitchen table on a Tuesday, and he has known since October about the four days and he has told nobody until tonight.

"**And Calder, that is not a reading. I want to say that on the page, because in six weeks this yard read four pages of other people's at the top of that bench and I said the fifth thing that came out of them was about the shape of them and not about what they said, and I said a page is a record of where the person was standing when it was written.

"**That was true and it was about four pages in a shed. This is not that. This is not a reading of the face of anything. That man did not look at one word of the writing to find that out. He looked at where the names were and he worked out what a page that says a name at the end of a line is for, and the page had been doing that in about sixty kitchens for six months and nobody had said it out loud.

"**So: a document this yard has been calling evidence is, in the hands of the people it came from, a set of instructions about who may be asked what.

"**And the four days in the middle of July are the only instruction on the whole sheet, and they are the four we would not strike out, and the reason we would not strike them out was that a line comes out of a column when it is wrong. It is not wrong. It is the only line on that sheet that a man is not to ask about, and we printed it in July without knowing what it was, and a man of sixty found it in October and sat on it for four months.

"**And I am not going to unprint it and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to add a line at the foot of it saying that no man is to be asked anything. If we add a line saying that, then the thirty-ninth line is an order too, and we will have printed two orders instead of one, and the second one will be an order about asking and there is nobody in this country we can send that to.

"**So the sheet stands as it is, with thirty-three names and four gaps and one line at the foot that says go and read it, and this yard is not going to be the party that says on a page that a man may not be asked what.

"**And the last part. The last part is the part that is going to be hard, and I am going to say it and then I am not going to say it again until after the first of March.

"**Four of the nine communities on this coast have had a page of this yard's read to them. In a kitchen in a place down this coast, in a room off a fish market, at a funeral in a place down this coast, and in a house of the harbor's own that a man of fifty-eight sells timber into. And that was not a decision. That was a man of about fifty-five with a penny a sheet and a rule about who cannot read, and this yard printed a sheet that says where every copy of it is and the man of fifty-five did the only thing anybody could do with the instruction at the foot of it.

"**And every one of those four places has taken it as a licence to send a man to this mole. Not as a fact. As a licence.

"**And that is the turn and I am not going to be tidier about it than that. This yard printed, in good faith, and in a shed, a document that is an instruction to go and find out who you can ask, and the coast has taken it up, and this yard cannot unprint it and does not want to, and four places on this coast are now going to read this yard's copy and each of them is going to read it as a different thing, and there is not one office in this country we can go and ask whether that is allowed.**"

"**Say the last sentence of it and then we will do the day.**

"**The last sentence of it is: the thing we printed so that a man could check us has told four places that they have permission, and I have not got one idea what to do about that, and I have not had one since about six o'clock this evening, and I am not going to get one tonight, and I would rather write that down than go to bed and pretend I have.

"**And it is not the thirty-eighth line. I want to be exact, because we will be asked in four years and I want the page to be right. The thirty-eighth line is not the problem. The thirty-eighth line says where the copies are, and every place that has one of those copies found it in a boat or in a room, and none of them came here first. The thirty-eighth line is the only honest line in the sheet and I have said so and I am not taking it back.

"**The problem is the thirty-three names, and they were the evidence, and I signed every one of them, and a man of sixty stood in a street tonight and told me that a page of names is a page about men, and he is a sailmaker and he is right, and I have been calling that page evidence since the fifteenth of August and it is not evidence and it never was.**"

*First column, entered on Friday the nineteenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the fifth of the six rota days in the month of February that this party has entered walked on none of, and that one of the six is left.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Thursday nor at any hour on the said Friday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either of the two days.*

*And that on the said Thursday this yard worked from eight until about half past twelve on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and did not stay; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times.*

*And that at about six in the evening on the said Friday, with the market gone in and about eleven people standing at the head of that dock, a man of about sixty of the second lay said that he has got four of the said party's sheet in his own kitchen, one brought by a boy of seventeen out of a boat's locker and three brought by men of that town, and that two of the four are different because they are different weeks of a thing that is still going.*

*And that the said man is a sailmaker of sixty and can read and had never learned to read a thing like that, and that on the Sunday in January instant his boy came home with a sheet out of a boat's locker and he went to a man in a room off the fish market and had it read to him for a penny and had it read to him again and was refused the second reading for the same penny; and that he said that he has been sitting in his own kitchen for a month with that sheet and that it took him until the Tuesday of the said week to say the thing he said.*

*And that the said man said that the said sheet is not a list of days and is a list of names, and that it is thirty-eight lines and that thirty-seven of them are a day and that thirty-three of them have a man's name at the end of them and four do not, and that the boy of seventeen is on the line of the twelfth of August instant, and that the man who set it in type is not on it and neither is the holder of the writ, and that a line at the foot of it says to go and read it at the head of that dock; and that the said man said that a list of names is a list of who can be asked what, and that the said sheet is not a record but an instruction, and that everything above the last line is a receipt and the last line is an order and the order is the one anybody uses.*

*And that the said man said that the four lines with nothing at the end of them in the middle of the month of July past are the only part of that sheet that is an instruction to anybody and are the only part of it that says a man is not to be asked, and that he found them in the month of October past and has not known what to do with them since and has told nobody until the said Friday.*

*And that the said man said that he is not asking this party for anything and has not asked it for anything and is not going to, and that he said that he has a boy of seventeen in one of the said boats and that he did not know until the Sunday in January instant what a man does on the eighth of August, and that he did not know there was a place he could go and ask, and that now there is, and that he has not used it and is not going to, and that he would have liked to have it written down that he did not use it and was not going to and is telling a man on a mole instead because there is nowhere to write that down; and that he said that it took five months from the fifteenth of August past and that it is about four weeks longer than it needed to be and that neither is anybody's fault and that one of them is not going to happen again.*

*And that the said man went up that mole and nobody stopped him and that about eleven people in that street said nothing and that it is entered that they said nothing and that it was not asked of them.*

*And that at about half past eight on the said Friday night, with the lamp lit and the two of them and nobody else in that shed, the day's entries were read back from the top of that bench by the said woman and read back after her by the said holder, and that neither of them said anything for about three minutes first.*

*And that the said woman said that the said sheet went up on a wall in that town on the fifteenth of August past and has been in about sixty boats since, and that it took a man of sixty who has a boy of seventeen to work out what it was for, and that he worked it out at his own kitchen table on the said Tuesday and had known since the month of October past about the four lines and had told nobody until the said Friday.*

*And that the said woman said that the said finding is not a reading of the face of a page, and that the said man did not look at one word of the writing to find it and looked at where the names were; and that she said that a document this party has been calling evidence is, in the hands of the people it came from, a set of instructions about who may be asked what.*

*And that the said woman said that the four lines in the middle of the said month of July are the only instruction on the whole of that sheet and are the only part of it that says a man is not to be asked, and that this party would not strike them out and gave the reason that a line comes out of a column when it is wrong, and that they are not wrong; and that she said that this party is not going to unprint that sheet and is not going to improve it and is not going to add a line at the foot of it saying that no man is to be asked anything, and gave the reason, being that a thirty-ninth line saying so would be an order as well and there is nobody in this country to send it to.*

*And that the said woman said that four of the nine communities on that coast have had a page of that yard's read to them, being in a kitchen in a place down that coast, in a room off the fish market, at a funeral in a place down that coast, and in a house of that town that a man of fifty-eight who sells timber goes into; and that that was not a decision and was a man of about fifty-five with a penny a sheet and a rule about who cannot read, and that this party printed a sheet that says where every copy of it is and the said man of fifty-five did the only thing anybody could do with the instruction at the foot of it.*

*And that the said woman said that every one of those four places has taken it as a licence to send a man to that mole and not as a fact; and that that is the turn; and that the said sheet stands as it is with thirty-three names and four gaps and one line at the foot of it, and that this party is not going to be the party that says on a page that a man may not be asked what.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the nineteenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a printed sheet that has been in about sixty boats on that coast since the fifteenth of August past has been read as a set of instructions about who may be asked what, and that the finding was made by a man of about sixty of the second lay at the head of that dock on the said Friday, and that this party did not ask him anything and had not asked him anything before and is not going to ask him anything after, and that the reason against that line is that he is not a friend of this party and is not on a list and has not been asked a question by anybody in this yard in four volumes.*

*And that four places on that coast have read a page of this yard's and each of them has taken it as a licence, and that the reason against that line is that this party printed the said sheet to be checkable and a checkable page is a page anybody can act on, and that this party has never once had to choose between the two and is not going to choose between them now, and that the four lines in the month of July past are the only instruction on the said sheet and are the only part of it that says a man is not to be asked.*

*And that the said holder said that the thirty-eighth line is not the problem and that the said line says where the copies are and that every place that has one found it in a boat or in a room and none of them came here first, and that the problem is the thirty-three names, and that they were the evidence, and that he signed every one of them, and that a man of sixty told him on the said Friday evening that a page of names is a page about men, and that he said that he has been calling that page evidence since the fifteenth of August past and that it is not evidence and never was.*
