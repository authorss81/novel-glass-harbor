# Chapter 223

*A Wage Tuesday, and a Line of a Page That Says the Light Was Out*

Tuesday the nineteenth and Wednesday the twentieth of January, in the shed, and on the north side of this town.

**On Tuesday the nineteenth day of January a wage of four marks did not go in and no envelope was made, and the column did not move, and the envelope count on that bench in that chest did not move, and the reason the wage is not paid is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December past and it is not improved on and it is not going to be.**

**And the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now THIRTEEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant and the nineteenth of January instant, and all thirteen are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all thirteen at once rather than the one that is easier to say.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour, and this party entered that and carried no figure of what came before it against that line.**

---

**And no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that this yard has had two Tuesdays in a row where it did not work and there is nothing to say about that except that it happened, and that a day on which nothing happened still happened.**

The two of them were in that shed from about nine until about four with a sheet of paper on the bench between them that does not belong to this yard, and neither of them read a line of it all day, and there is no more to be said about that than there is.

At about half past two the sheet of seventy-one said this:

"**You are going to ask me about the ninth.**
"**I am not going to help you find it and I am not going to have it found for me. I know the line and I have known it since I was about nine years old and I have not had to look it up once in sixty-two years, and if you ask me for it I will say it, and you may write it down, and that is the whole of what I have.**"

---

**And they went down the hill at about ten on the Wednesday morning and the woman of seventy-one opened the door before they got to it, which she said was not a courtesy, it was that she had been standing in the front room since about eight.**

The house is on the north side of this town and there are three other houses within thirty yards of it and all three of them have a front door that faces this one. That is on the page and the reason against it is that the reason is the reason of the day it is in and not a general reason.

She had the page out on a table. It is one sheet, folded once, and the fold is a crease and not a tear, and there are about forty lines on it in three hands, and the top of it has been folded over and folded back so many hundreds of times that the fold is a channel.

Nessa Pike did not sit down. Calder Marr sat down.

"**I am not going to pretend I have not got a page on my bench up that hill that says a light was burning on the ninth,**" he said. "**I am going to say what it says and I am going to tell you the whole of it and then I am going to ask you one question, and it is the only question I am going to ask you today, and I am not going to ask you where you got it or who has seen it.**

"**My page says: the ninth, and a hard night, and the light on the point was burning, and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light.**"

He put his hand flat on the table the way she does, and did not know he was doing it.

"**What does yours say.**"

She said it without looking at it.

"**The ninth, and a hard night, and there was no light on the point, and the man that keeps it had the lamp out the week before and the oil was not carried, and the two boats that came in were in before it was light and were not in on the light.**"

Nobody said anything for about six seconds. A man crossed the street outside with a barrow.

"**I was born in this house,**" she said. "**That line is my grandmother's and it is the oldest hand on the sheet. And she was not on the point. She was at the window of this front room, and this house is on the wrong side of the point for a light, and it is about two hundred yards off the water and the point is a mile and a half from the other place, and I have never once been able to make that line say anything else, and I stopped trying to make it say anything else at about nine years old.**

"**So one of us is wrong. And I am not going to say which one, and neither are you, and I have known since I was nine.**"

"**I am not going to say which one either. I want to be exact about that, because I can see what you are going to ask me next and I have an answer ready and it is no.**

"**But I am going to say this, and I want it written down in the second column and not the first, because neither of us can check a word of it and you cannot check me any more than I can check your page.**

"**There are two pages. There is a night. There is a point with a light on it, or there is not. And the two of you are about a mile and a half and two hundred yards from it respectively, and both of you wrote it down at night, and neither of you wrote down where you were standing when you wrote it.**

"**That is not a thing about a light. That is a thing about what a page is. And I have had this page since I was nine and I have never once seen a person write down where they were standing.**"

"**Then that is the whole of what we have got.**

"**That is the whole of what you have got, and you have had it since Saturday and you did not know you had it.**"

---

**And at about eleven she said, without being asked, that there was a line on that sheet about a thing that had nothing to do with a night, and that she would read it because she wanted it read in a house and not in a shed.**

"**It is in my mother's hand and it is about the year my grandfather was alive and it says that the right to take what came ashore was sold out of this place, to a man from the town two days down the coast, for a sum which is written out in words and not in figures, and that the words do not add up to the figures, and that the sale was witnessed by four men and three of them were of this place.**

"**And the sale is the whole of what that page has ever been asked for by anybody. Twice, in my lifetime, both times by men from a city, both times for a piece of work they did not do here. And the page went out both times and came back both times and I have it.**

"**And a thing that came out of a house and went back is not the same thing as a thing that was never in the street, and I have never worked out why that is, and I have been thinking about it since Saturday.**"

"**You have been thinking about it since Saturday.**
"**I have had it in my head since I was standing in your shed. I am seventy-one and I do not waste a thing. You had three pages on a bench and you had not read one of them and you were about to read four, and I thought: there is a woman on this coast who has been putting pages in and out of houses for forty years and has never once had a conversation about it, and there is nobody to have it with.**"

---

**And this yard worked on the said Wednesday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and nothing was read back on that day, and the reason against that line is that a thing said in a front room on the north side of this town is read back by the four people who heard it through a wall and by nobody else.**

Three marks is four hundred and eighty pence. The bag held three thousand one hundred and sixteen pence on the Monday and the sum of the two is three thousand five hundred and ninety-six pence, which is twenty-two marks, six shillings and four pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage is two thousand nine hundred and fifty-six pence.

---

**And the cost of that Wednesday, which is not a figure and is not a day, is that this party went into a house on the north side of this town and asked a woman of seventy-one one question, and that is the first time in forty years anybody has asked that house anything, and that the question is going to be in three other front rooms by Friday, and that this yard cannot unask it and is not going to try, and that the reason it is not going to try is that a woman who has kept a page in a house for forty years has just told this yard what a page is and a page is not a thing this yard is going to go out and tell this town it knows how to keep.**

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twentieth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on the said Tuesday a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and that the column advances by one line and four marks on a Tuesday and only on a Tuesday and only if the wage is paid, and that the envelope count advances with the column and only with it.*

*And that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is thirteen, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth of January instant, the twelfth of January instant and the nineteenth of January instant, and that all thirteen are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other; and that the reason the wage is not paid is the reason entered in the first column on the twenty-ninth day of December past in the holder's own left hand, and that it is not improved on and is not going to be.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour nor on the said Wednesday at any hour, and that this party entered that and carried no figure of what came before it against that line; and that no day of work was booked on the said Tuesday and the board was not written on it, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happened still happened.*

*And that the two of them were in that shed from about nine until about four on the said Tuesday with a sheet of paper on that bench that does not belong to this yard, and that neither of them read a line of it on that day, and that there is no more to be said about that than there is.*

*And that at about half past two on the said Tuesday the said sheet said that the two of them were going to be asked about the ninth, and that she was not going to help them find it and was not going to have it found for her, and that she had known the line since she was about nine years old and had not had to look it up once in sixty-two years, and that if they asked her for it she would say it.*

*And that at about ten on the said Wednesday morning this party went down that hill to a house on the north side of this town, and that the said woman opened the door before they got to it and said that that was not a courtesy and that she had been standing in the front room since about eight; and that the said house is one of four houses within thirty yards of each other and all three of the others have a front door that faces that one, and that the said woman did not sit down and the said holder did.*

*And that the said holder said, before he asked, that he was going to read out the whole of a line from a page that is on a bench in that shed and was going to ask one question and that it was the only question and that he was not going to ask her where she got it or who had seen it; and that the said line is the ninth, and a hard night, and the light on the point was burning, and two boats came in on it about two in the morning and lay there till it was light.*

*And that the said line on the said woman's page is the ninth, and a hard night, and there was no light on the point, and the man that keeps it had the lamp out the week before and the oil was not carried, and the two boats that came in were in before it was light and were not in on the light; and that the said woman said the said line without looking at it, and that she said that the said line is her grandmother's and is the oldest hand on that sheet, and that her grandmother was at the window of that front room and was not on the point, and that the said house is on the wrong side of the point for a light and is about two hundred yards off the water and the point is a mile and a half from the other place, and that she had never been able to make that line say anything else and stopped trying at about nine years old.*

*And that the said woman said that one of them is wrong, and that she is not going to say which one and that neither of them is going to say which one, and that she has known since she was nine; and that the said holder said that he is not going to say which one either.*

*And that at about eleven on the said Wednesday morning the said woman said, without being asked, that there is a line on that sheet about a thing that has nothing to do with a night, and that she read it, and that the said line is in her mother's hand and says that the right to take what came ashore was sold out of that place to a man from the town two days down this coast for a sum written out in words and not in figures, and that the words do not add up to the figures, and that the sale was witnessed by four men and three of them were of that place; and that the said woman said that the sale is the whole of what that page has ever been asked for by anybody, twice in her lifetime, both times by men from a city, both times for a piece of work they did not do in that place, and that the page went out both times and came back both times and that she has it.*

*And that this yard worked on the said Wednesday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man; and that the gross on the board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and that nothing was read back on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the twentieth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said holder entered on the said day that there are two pages about the ninth of a month in a year nobody on either of them has written down, and that one of them says the light on the point was burning and that two boats came in on it, and that the other says there was no light on the point and that the two boats that came in were in before it was light and were not in on the light, and that neither of the two is a copy of anything and neither of the two was sent by anybody; and that the said party entered that the two of them are incompatible and that both of them are true, and that the said party does not know which of them is right and is not going to know this month, and that the disagreement is not going to be put to any of the four people at the top of that bench and is not going to be put in a market.*

*And that the said holder entered that neither of the two pages says where the person was standing when it was written, and that one of them is about a mile and a half from the point and one of them is about two hundred yards from it, and that the said woman of seventy-one said that that is not a thing about a light and is a thing about what a page is, and that she has had that page since she was nine and has never once seen a person write down where they were standing; and that the reason against that line is that this party cannot go and stand on either of those two pieces of ground and does not know whether it would be allowed to.*

*And that the said woman said that the said party had three pages on a bench and had read none of them and was about to read four, and that she had thought in a front room on the north side of this town that there is a woman on this coast who has been putting pages in and out of houses for forty years and has never once had a conversation about it and that there is nobody to have it with.*

*And that the cost of the said Wednesday is that this party went into a house on the north side of this town and asked the said woman one question, and that that is the first time in forty years anybody has asked that house anything, and that the question is going to be in three other front rooms by Friday, and that this party cannot unask it and is not going to try, and that the reason against that is that a woman who has kept a page in a house for forty years has told this party what a page is and a page is not a thing this party is going out into a street and tell this town it knows how to keep.*

*And that the said four lines are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that the words in which a request to strike a line out of a column is refused are the same words for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the twentieth of January and nothing on the nineteenth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and twenty-two marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is three thousand five hundred and ninety-six pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and thirteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and a fourteenth falls due on Tuesday the twenty-sixth instant. The reason the wage is not paid is in the first column and is the reason of the twenty-ninth of December and is not improved on. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and is not read out a second time here.**
