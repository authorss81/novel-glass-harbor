# Chapter 234

*A Slate That Is Wiped on Monday, and a Price Named Before the Yes*

Wednesday the tenth and Thursday the eleventh of February, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and in the shed.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged on the said Thursday at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the third of the six rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and no reason is given against that line in this chapter because there is no reason and there has not been one since the third of August and putting one there would be inventing one.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and this party entered the two of them and carried no figure of what came before them forward to either.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Wednesday and none on the said Thursday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against that line is that this yard was in a room over a tide-gang store from ten until about twenty past eleven on the said Wednesday with about eleven people in it, and that a room is not a day of work and is not a day this yard loses, it is a day this yard spends, and the two of those are on different lines of that book and are not the same line and are not going to be made the same line.**

---

**The store under that room was shut from ten in the morning of the Wednesday and the men who count the catch in it were in another building, and one of them was a morning down, and that morning is not named in that room because he is not in that business, and this party does not know his name and has not asked, and that is the second morning of that kind this harbor has lost to that floor and the first one was on the twenty-sixth of November past, and this party did not ask for the room and does not get the morning back and is not going to ask anybody for it.**

**And at ten on the Wednesday morning there were about eleven people in that room and the stove against the far wall had not been lit since the middle of September and was not lit on that morning, and the windows were high and green with salt, and the light came in from behind the people rather than over them, and the clerk of this harbor's own authority took the chair at the end of the table and wrote the date and the hour and the place on a sheet before anybody said anything, which is a practice and has been one since April, and the sheet went into a file and the file went onto a shelf and the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it.**

The reeve of Morrow Quay did not take the chair at the end of the table. He took the one against the wall, which is his own, and he had a book in front of him that was not the file.

"**Two things and then the slate.**

"**One. This office is not sitting to decide whether a man of the third lay was told a thing at the head of that dock. That happened or it did not and it is not in dispute and nobody in this room is going to argue about it, and I have not opened this room to find out. I have opened it because a party has asked this office to put a day and an hour and a name on a surface eleven people can read, and I am going to say no to that in a moment and I am going to say the price of the part I am going to say yes to first, in front of all of you, which is a thing I have not done in this room in the whole time I have had it.

"**Two. On the tenth of December I put one more thing in a book in this office that I did not have to put in, at this office's own cost, and I said out loud on the mole's steps that I was not going to do that twice inside a month without saying so out loud first. It is the tenth of February and that is inside a month twice over, and I am saying it out loud first, and here is why, and it is not that you asked me to.

"**This office's ledger is spent and has been since the fifth of December. Its repair account has been empty since the middle of February last. There is nothing in it to seat anything with. What is left is a reeve's own hand and a man's day out of a week, and that is the whole of the stock of this office on this coast, and I have been spending it since the third of May on other people's questions and I have got about three things left in it.

"**This is the bigger of them, and it is bigger than the tenth of December because the tenth of December was a thing about a man and a notice. This one is about what a piece of paper is, and there is no office on this coast that has an answer to that, and the reason there is no office is that the answer would be a figure and no figure in this country is worth what it costs to defend in four years.**"

"**Then name the price,**" said Nessa Pike. "**You have not named it and you cannot seat anything until you name it, and we did not ask you to do this and you did not agree to do it before you came in, and both of those are correct and neither of them means you have said the price.**"

"**Then that is the price and it is the whole of it and I am not going to say it in a smaller voice because there are eleven people in here.

"**I am going to write six words on a slate in this room on the Thursday, and I am going to write the date and the hour on them, and I am going to have the man who sits at that end of the table copy them into the file in his own hand so that the file and the slate say the same thing.

"**And on Monday morning at eight that slate comes down, and the office wipes it whether I am in this room or not, and it has been doing that since before I came to it, and there is a man in a city who put that rule there and I have never asked him why and I am not going to.

"**So the six words last one night. The only other copy in the world is the clerk's own page, which he keeps, and which he has not mentioned to me once in the four years I have had this room, and I do not know what is on it and I have not asked and I am not going to.

"**And the third copy is the party's own book, in the party's own ink, which does not come down on any Monday, and I am going to say the thing I have to say and I am going to say it once: a record that survives is the one the party keeps, and a record that is on a slate in this room is a record that a man in a city can wipe off the face of the only place in this harbor where anybody has ever been made to be in a room and listen to a thing.

"**The price is that I am spending a reeve's hand and a clerk's day on a thing that is in a drawer nine feet from where you sleep, and I am not being paid for it out of anything, because the repair account is empty and there is nothing to pay me out of, and the day the clerk loses is the second day that office owes him for a room over his own store and it is mine and not the harbor's and not yours.

"**And I am not a friend of this party. I have said that out loud on these steps twice and I am not going to unsay it to get a thing seated in my own room, and I am not going to be paid for this and I am not going to call it a favour, and if you tell anybody in a market in this town that the reeve did this for you then I will put on that slate that the reeve did this for nobody and that a man of fifty-eight with a fair hand is the one who did it for you.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds.

"**That is the price and it is named and I am not going to improve on it and it is not going to be a second thing.**"

"**Then the yes is given,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to say what I am not going to do in this room, and I am going to say it in front of about eleven people so that it is in eleven mouths and not one.

"**I am not going to read anything out of that book. Not a page, not a column, not a line, and not the day. There is a page and a half in a room in a city that this yard has never read out in a room, and there was a reading in this room in November and it was the right decision and it is not going to happen twice in the same year for a slate that comes down on Monday.

"**And the six words are not going to be the six words I would choose if I were a reeve with a spent ledger. The six words are going to be what is true. And the man at the head of that dock who said the thing on the Thursday is not in this room and is not going to be asked anything and his name is not going on that slate, because the six words are about what this yard did and not about what a man of the third lay did.**"

---

**And on the Thursday morning at about ten, Ors Kest wrote on the slate in that room, in chalk, in a hand that had gone smaller than it usually goes, and it is on the face of that slate and it is not in this book because this book is not a slate.**

He wrote the day, and he wrote the hour, and then he wrote this.

> *A man of the third lay was told a thing on the fourth day of February at the head of that dock, in about nine people's hearing, by a man of the third lay. This yard did not put it there. This yard did not correct it. This yard has not written to any of the nine.*

"**Read it back,**" said the clerk of this harbor's own authority, and the holder of the writ read it back after him, and the clerk wrote what was read back into the file, and the file went onto the shelf.

"**Now go and look at it,**" said Ors Kest, "**because in about two hours it is still going to be on that wall, and on Monday morning at eight it is going to be down, and that is the entire life of it, and I would like two people in this room to have stood in front of it and seen how little time it has got.**"

About eleven people looked at it. Two of them were of the tide gang and one of those two was the man who had lost a morning to that floor on the Wednesday and had not been in the room and had been named on the mole's steps and was standing at the back of it and did not say anything.

---

**And at about half past eight on the Thursday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of that bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed.**

She read it. He read it after her.

"**You were asked a question in that room that you have been asked four times in five volumes and you have never once been asked it in those words.**

"**The holder of that writ is the best reader of joins in this harbor. Every man in that room knows it and about six of them have watched him do it, and this morning a reeve with a spent ledger asked him to look at a thing that is not a join, in a room, in front of eleven people who did not need him to be right.

"**He wanted six words. He did not want the man to be right. He wanted the date and the hour, and he wanted the words to be true, and he did not care in the least whether this yard was right about anything, and that is the first time in five volumes that anybody has wanted something out of that book without wanting that book to be right, and I want you to know that it is a bigger thing than the slate and I am not going to be able to say why today.**

"**And the other thing. He asked you at the end whether there was anything else you wanted that room for, and you said no, and then you said the sentence.**

"**Say it again. Not because I want to improve on it. Because there are eleven people in a room and a slate on a wall and a book in a drawer and a man at the end of a mole, and a sentence is only a sentence until somebody else has to keep it.**"

"**A plate in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone is not reached by a better letter out of this shed,**" said Calder Marr. "**It is seen and not measured and not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it, including me, is a change of politics in an office. It has not changed. It is not going to change in a month because a reeve spent a slate on a Monday, and I am not going to write a better letter about it, and I am not going to ask that man for anything when he has already told us what he has got left in him and it is three things.

"**And the sentence is the whole of what I have got for that plate and it was true in November and it is true tonight and it will be true in a year, and I would rather it were a sentence than a letter, because a letter can be improved on and a sentence cannot, and I have watched us try to improve on a sentence three times in five months and I have never once been able to do it.**"

"**Then it stands,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I am going to shut this book, and I am going to say one fact and no more, and the fact is that a reeve with an empty repair account and a clerk owed two days wrote six words on a board that comes down on Monday, and that in four years the only place in this country where those six words are is a drawer in this shed, and that is what he has bought and he is not a friend of us and he did not do it for us, and I am not going to let either of those two facts get used to make us feel better about it.**"
