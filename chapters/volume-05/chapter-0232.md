# Chapter 232

*A Letter, and a Man on the Step Who Did Not Come In*

Saturday the sixth and Sunday the seventh of February, in the shed, and at the head of this dock.

**No rota day falls on either of those two days and this party entered that and entered it as a fact and not as a fault, and there is no charge against the cistern behind the engine house on either of them and there is no figure and there is no reason, because a day with nothing charged on it is a day and is entered as a day.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and this party put nothing at all against either of those two lines, not a figure and not a tail.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against that line is that this yard spent two days with a letter in it and a man on a step, and a day a yard spends on a thing that came to it from outside that yard is not a day that yard works, and the pump did not come up that hill on either of them and the man with it did not come and the reason for that is the last thing said in that shed on Friday night and is not improved on.**

---

**The letter came up the mole on the Saturday in the ordinary post and it came in an envelope with a postmark on it from a place four days up this coast, and it was addressed in a hand to the yard that keeps the book on the mole at Morrow Quay, which is a thing a man can write if he has seen the mole, and the hand on the outside was not the hand inside it, and the yard has noticed that twice this winter and has not put a figure on it.**

The ship's hand read it at about eleven on the Saturday morning at the top of the bench and read it out for the holder, and there were four lines written on one side and nothing on the other, and the four lines were written close together and in a fair hand and there was no name at the foot of them.

"**It says this: that a sheet of a yard's own days came into this place in the second week of January and is in three houses in this place now, and that it was read out in a kitchen in one of them on a Sunday to about nine people, and that the man who read it is not a fool and is not drunk, and that he read it because a man asked him to and because the man asked him to pay for the sheet.

"**And it says: that a page of a place is the place's, and that the sheet in the three houses is not a page of a place and is a page of a yard, and that a yard's page read out in a kitchen in this place is a thing this place did not ask for and cannot unask, and that the man who read it did not know that and the man who asked him to did not know that either.

"**And it says: that the writer is not asking this yard to take the sheet back and is not asking for it to be recalled and is not asking for anything, and that the writer is a man of fifty-eight who sells timber and that he has put his name to it because a page about a place with no name on it is a page about a place with no man on it, and that is the whole of what he has.**

"**And it says the fourth thing and it is a single line and it is the only line on that sheet that sounds like a man writing to a yard and not to a person: *and a place that has a yard's days in its houses ought to be asked which of them it wants, and not told.**"

"**He is right,**" said Nessa Pike. "**He is right and I have been trying to say that since the twenty-eighth of January in a different way and I have not managed it in a month.**

"**And he is also the only man who has ever written to this yard about anything and he has done it in five lines and he has got it in one line and this yard has been at it for five volumes.

"**And I am not going to write back to him. I am going to say that out loud and let it be in the book in my own words: this yard has not written to one of the nine and is not going to write to this one, and the reason is the reason of the fourth of October past and I am not giving it, and the reason against the line is that a man of fifty-eight who sells timber has put his name to a sheet in a place four days up this coast and the day this yard writes to him is the day he is a man this yard has spoken to, and then he is a man a party can be asked about, and this yard has spent four volumes telling this harbor that a man who is asked a question about a page is a man whose page is not his own any more.

"**So he gets nothing. Not a letter, not a card, not a word carried up by a man on a mole. And I want that in the first column with the date on it and I want the date on it to be the sixth of February and not a general thing.**"

---

**And the man came up that hill on the Sunday morning at about eleven, and he did not come in.**

He was about fifty and he had the look of a man who had come a long way on purpose and had thought better of it at the bottom of the mole. He stood about four feet off the shed door with his hat in his hand and he said one thing and he did not say it twice, and he did not come under the roof, and this yard has entered the reason for that on the page and the reason is not a suspicion and is not a slight and is that a man who has come up a hill to say one thing does not want to be asked a second one under a roof, and because everything this yard has learned in five volumes is that the moment a man is inside a shed and has not gone out again he is a man who has agreed to something by being warm.

"**I am not asking you for anything,**" he said. "**I have come from a place two days down this coast and I have come on a Sunday because I could not come on a week day, and I am not going to say my name because my name is on a page and you have had that page and you gave it back and I am not here about the page.

"**I am here about the four men. The man who sells lime and chalk has told you he was going to be asked about a sheet by four people and he told you on the sixteenth of January and it is now the seventh of February and it has happened and it happened on the Wednesday, and I was one of the four.

"**And what I am here to say is this and I am saying it on your step and not inside your shed: they have read it. All four of them have read it and they have not liked it, and two of them think it is a lie and one of them thinks it is a coward's piece of paper and one of them — and this one is the youngest and he is the one I am frightened for — says it is the first thing anybody has shown him that says a place is allowed to decide things about itself, and he cannot say that out loud in that place because the two who think it is a lie are his uncle and his master's brother, and he has said it in my kitchen and not theirs.

"**So a page is in four hands in a place two days down this coast, and none of the four is going to put it down, and one of the four thinks it is the best thing he has ever had in his hands, and I have walked up a mole on a Sunday to tell a yard that there is nothing to be done and that I have done it anyway because my wife said a man ought to.**

"**That is the whole of it. I am not asking you to do anything about it. If you do anything about it you will be doing something about it in a place where four people have already decided, and they decided it before you knew it was going on, and that is not a thing you can be let into now.**"

"**Then I am not going to do anything about it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I know you are not. I came up to hear you say it and I have heard it and I am going down that mole now, and I would like it in whatever you write down that I asked you for nothing and that you gave me nothing and that those are the same thing tonight and not the same thing in a fortnight.**"

He went down the mole. He did not knock and he did not go in and he did not look at the boards through the door, and the ship's hand stood in the doorway until he was off the top of the steps and then she went back to the bench.

---

**And the two of them do not agree, and this yard is not going to make them.**

"**The man of fifty-eight says a place ought to be asked which of a yard's days it wants and not told,**" said Nessa Pike. "**And the man on the step says four people in a place have already decided and that we are not to be let in. And they do not agree and both of them are talking about the same thing, which is a page that is not in the place it is about, and that is the only thing either of them has said that this yard had not already got written down in its own book nine days before the first of them opened his mouth.

"**And a man of fifty-eight with a fair hand and his name on it is right that a place ought to be asked. And a man of fifty who walked up a mole on a Sunday to say that four people have decided without being asked is also right, and he is right in a way that is worse for us, because the four people decided without being asked, and we put the page where they could decide, and the page was in a crate of chalk before it was in anybody's hand, and this yard was not in the crate and did not put it in the crate and cannot get it out of the four hands it is in.

"**So here is the whole of the disagreement and it is one sentence and I am going to put it in the book and I am not going to improve on it: one of them wants the asking done before, and the other one says it has already been done after, and this yard is in the middle of it with a board and a pump and no copy of anything.

"**And we are not writing to either of them. Not this week and not after the first of March. And I want it entered that we are not writing, and I want the reason against the line to be that a man of fifty-eight who sells timber and a man of about fifty who walked up a mole on a Sunday are two people this yard has not sent a man to, and there is nobody else in this harbor we could send.**"

*First column, entered on Sunday the seventh day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no rota day falls on the said Saturday or the said Sunday, and that there is no charge against the cistern behind the engine house on either of them and no figure against either of them, and that a day with nothing charged on it is entered as a day.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Saturday nor at any hour on the said Sunday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either day, and that no figure and no tail is put against either line.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday and the board was not written on either of them, and that the reason against that line is that this party spent two days with a letter in it and a man on a step, and that the pump did not come up that hill on either of them and the man with it did not come.*

*And that a sheet came up that mole on the said Saturday in the ordinary post in an envelope postmarked from a place four days up this coast, and that it was addressed in a hand to the yard that keeps the book on the mole at Morrow Quay, and that the hand on the outside of it was not the hand inside it; and that the said sheet had four lines written on one side of it and nothing on the other side and no name at the foot of it.*

*And that the said four lines say that a sheet of a yard's own days came into that place in the second week of January and is in three houses in it, and that it was read out in a kitchen in one of them on a Sunday to about nine people, and that the man who read it is not a fool and is not drunk and read it because a man asked him to and because the man asked him to pay for the sheet; and that a page of a place is the place's and that the said sheet is not a page of a place and is a page of a yard, and that a yard's page read out in a kitchen in that place is a thing that place did not ask for and cannot unask; and that the said man of fifty-eight who sells timber has put his name to that sheet because a page about a place with no name on it is a page about a place with no man on it; and that the fourth of those lines is that a place that has a yard's days in its houses ought to be asked which of them it wants and not told.*

*And that the said woman said that the said man of fifty-eight is right, and that he is the only man who has written to this yard about anything, and that this party is not going to write back to him, and gave the reason of the fourth day of October past and did not give it, and that the reason against that line is that the day this yard writes to him is the day he is a man this yard has spoken to and then a man this party can be asked about.*

*And that a man of about fifty came up that hill on the said Sunday at about eleven and did not come under the roof of that shed, and that this party entered the reason for that on the page, and that the reason is not a suspicion and is not a slight and is that a man who has come up a hill to say one thing does not want to be asked a second one under a roof.*

*And that the said man said that he was not asking for anything and was not saying his name and that his name is on a page and that he was not here about the page; and that he said that the man who sells lime and chalk told this yard on the sixteenth day of January past that four of his customers were going to be asked about a sheet, and that it has happened and it happened on the Wednesday, and that the said man was one of the four; and that he said that all four of them have read it, and that two of them think it is a lie and one of them thinks it is a coward's piece of paper and one of them says it is the first thing anybody has shown him that says a place is allowed to decide things about itself, and that the said one is the youngest and cannot say it out loud in that place and has said it in the said man's kitchen.*

*And that the said man said that there is nothing to be done and that he had walked up a mole on a Sunday to say so; and that the said holder said that this party is not going to do anything about it; and that the said man said that he came up to hear that and is going down that mole, and that he would like it in whatever this yard writes down that he asked for nothing and was given nothing and that those are the same thing that night and are not the same thing in a fortnight.*

*And that the two of them do not agree, and that the said woman entered that in one sentence, being that one of them wants the asking done before and the other says it has already been done after; and that this party is not writing to either of them this week and not after the first of March next.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the seventh day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a place four days up this coast has this yard's own days in three houses in it and this yard does not know in which three and has not asked; and that the reason against that line is that this yard has written to none of the nine and has sent nobody to any of them and is not going to start on the seventh day of February because a man who sells timber has put his name on a sheet.*

*And that four people in a place two days down this coast have a page in their hands that this yard had on a bench for fourteen days, and that one of the four says a place is allowed to decide things about itself and has said it in a kitchen and not in the place, and that the reason against that line is that the said page is not about that place and is about a place two hundred yards off the water, and that this yard has not asked that page's keeper about it and is not going to, and that a man came up a hill on a Sunday and lost a day of his own trade coming up it and no figure is put on that day and the reason against that line is that the day is not checkable from that shed and is not a figure this yard's money lost, and that the same figure is not going to be taken off a different man in a different week to make a bigger one.*

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing on the said Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and thirty-four marks, six shillings and three pence is in the bag, which is five thousand five hundred and fifteen pence, and that is what it was on the Friday, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is four thousand eight hundred and seventy-five pence and it has not moved in five days. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and fifteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and that figure stands still until a Tuesday and there was no Tuesday in these two days. The cistern was not charged on either of them and the walk is nil and stays nil. The far bench was not occupied on either of them. The bench under that pump was bare for an eighth night on the Saturday and a ninth on the Sunday. No day of work was booked on either of them and the board was not written on either. Four returns are four and there is no fifth, and no finding of worked glass was made on either of the two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The third column is unfilled and unappointed and the boy's hand is at the head of it. The bellwright has not been asked anything and the chest was not opened. This yard has not been to the counter of that circuit office since the twenty-fourth of November past and has written to no office at all in the three days ended on the said Sunday, and no office has sent anything up that hill. Every other figure this harbor is owed is set out in the page above and is not read out again in this paragraph.**
