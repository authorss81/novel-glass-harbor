# Chapter 236

*The Thirty-Eighth Line, and a Man Who Counted It in July*

Sunday the fourteenth and Monday the fifteenth of February, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**At eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was the forty-first Monday since the first of May, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and nothing was on that wall on the Saturday and nothing came off it on the Monday and there is no paper in this harbor on any wall that this party put there.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged on the said Monday at no figure and this party walked none of it, and that is the fourth of the six rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and it is still nil, and the twenty-third charge this party has entered walked on none of is going to fall in the next six days and this party is not going to walk that one either.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Sunday and was not occupied on the said Monday, and this party entered both of them with nothing whatever set against either line.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Sunday, and the reason against that line is that the two of them spent the whole of that day at the top of that bench counting a printed sheet line by line with a rule against it, and that a day spent counting a thing this yard printed is not a day this yard works, and that the said Sunday is entered as a day this yard spent and not as a day this yard lost, and those are two lines and they are not going to be run into one.**

**And on the said Monday this yard worked from eight until about one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man, and the gross on that board was thirteen and ten of the thirteen was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times.**

---

**The sheet came off the shelf at about ten on the Sunday morning and it is a folded broadside of about the size of a board, and it has been in that drawer since the eighth of August, and it went up on the outside of the market wall in this town on the fifteenth of August past and it came off on the sixteenth, and it has been printed in this harbor ever since and it is the only thing this yard has ever had in type.**

Thirty-eight lines.

Thirty-seven of them are days. They are the days this yard worked and the days it did not, from the sixteenth of June to the twenty-second of July, and every one of them is in this yard's own left hand except the four, and the four are the four.

The thirty-eighth line is at the foot of it, and it is not a day, and it has never been a day, and it is the only line in the whole of that sheet that this yard did not write for itself.

> *There is a copy of this sheet in about sixty boats on this coast. If you have one and this one has changed, say so to the yard that printed it, and the yard that printed it is at the head of this dock, and any man may go in there and read the thirty-eighth line and see where the other ones are.*

"**That is the whole of it,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Thirty-eight lines and I have read all thirty-eight twice and I have put a rule against them twice, and I have made him read all thirty-eight twice, and the two of us have got the same count and the count is thirty-eight, and I am going to say the thing I have had in my mouth since about eleven o'clock and it is not a discovery anybody could have made in a hurry.

"**A yard in this country put a line in a printed sheet that says where every other copy of that sheet is.

"**And that line has been in about sixty boats since the fifteenth of August past. And this yard has spent six months being afraid of it.

"**And in a room over a tide-gang store on Thursday the eleventh a man with a spent ledger asked this yard for six words on a board that comes down on Monday, and this yard gave him six words, and I sat on that mole on the Friday night and said out loud that a sentence cannot be improved on, and the reason I said it is that a sentence is finished. And that man in that room said the same thing about his own six words in front of eleven people, and he was not talking about the sentence.

"**He was talking about the thirty-eighth line. He has got an empty repair account and a clerk owed two days and a slate that comes down on Monday at eight, and he wrote five lines on it on Thursday morning and told about eleven people to go and look at it because in about two hours it would still be on that wall and on Monday it would be gone. And that is the thirty-eighth line. That is exactly the thirty-eighth line and he did it in chalk on a Tuesday's week for five lines.

"**He built this line in July and he said so in a room and he was right, and it has taken this yard from the twenty-second of July to the fourteenth of February to count it, and the reason it has taken that long is that this yard has been reading the sheet to see what is wrong with it, and there is nothing wrong with it, and there was never anything wrong with it.**"

"**Say the part that is the cost, because the part that is the finding is easy and the part that is the cost is not.**

"**I will say it, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it a second way.

"**This yard has spent four volumes refusing to hold a record for this coast, and has said so out loud in about eleven people's hearing, and has been told to its face by two men of the first lay that a refusal to hold is read as an intention to keep. And the only way to be read correctly is to have somewhere to put a page that is not a hold, and this yard has not got one, and knows what one would cost, and is not going to pretend it knows the cost of a thing it has not built.

"**And the whole of that is a hole in a street and we said so in a shed in the middle of January. And the thirty-eighth line is the answer to it and this yard printed it six months ago and has not noticed until a woman put a rule against it on a Sunday.

"**Because a line that says where every copy of a page is, is not a hold. It is the opposite of a hold. A hold is a place a page goes and does not come out of. A line that says where every copy of a page is says that anybody may go and get one, and that there is no place a page goes and does not come out of, and that a man who wants to check this yard has only to walk into a boat.

"**So the thing this yard has been afraid of and the thing this yard has been proud of are one line, and it has been one line since the fifteenth of August, and there is no version of it that is the first without the second, and there is no way to be findable and be quiet about it, and I have known that since the eighteenth of May and I have been printing the answer anyway.**

"**And the fourth of the four that came up this hill in a crate of chalk says that a copy is not a page and has none of a page's standing. And this yard has had that sheet in a drawer since the sixth of December and has had a copy of a record in a drawer since the twenty-fourth of November and has never once put the two side by side, and they are the same size and they are the same shape and one of them is a page about a place and the other one is a page about a yard, and this yard has been holding one of them and calling it a hold and the other one and calling it a copy.

"**And I am going to say the last part of it and then I am going to go and boil a kettle. The thirty-eighth line does not put a page in anybody's hands. It says where the copies are. Those are two different sentences, and a party which has spent four volumes saying the second one is the first one is a party that has been wrong since the third of May, and I would rather be wrong on the page than right in my head, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

"**Then what I want to say is the finding and not the cost, and I am going to say it flat and I am not going to put anything round it.

"**Four days are in print.

"**The four days in the middle of July, the ones with a day at the end of them and no name at the end of that. They are in that sheet. They have been in that sheet since the fifteenth of August, in about sixty boats, on this coast, for six months, and this yard has read that column out at the head of that dock in about nine people's hearing four times since the seventh of July and has never once said out loud that four of the lines in the middle of it have got nobody's name at the end of them.

"**You asked for them to be struck out on the twenty-seventh of January and I would not do it and I said the only ground any column in that book has ever had is that a line comes out when it is wrong. That has not changed and I am not changing it tonight.

"**What has changed is that they are in print. And I am not going to be able to unprint them, and neither are you, and I am not going to pretend to you in a shed on a Sunday that this is a small thing, because it is the first thing in five volumes that this yard has printed and not been able to take back, and there is a printer in a town of about two thousand up this coast who keeps a list of everything that comes into his shop that anybody means to keep, and this yard has not written to him and is not going to this month, and there are four of those days in sixty boats instead of none.

"**And the reason against the line is not that the four are wrong. They are not wrong. They are four days out of five months in which this yard did not know whose day it was, and the reason it did not know is in the second column with no name in it and has been there since the sixth of October, and the four lines stand where they stand, and a line does not come out of a column in this book because it has been printed and cannot be got back.**"

---

**And on the Sunday afternoon they went down the mole to a lane off it and into a room with two presses in it, because the sheet this yard printed in this harbor was printed in this harbor, and because the ship's hand wanted to ask one question of a man who sets type and had not been told she was going to ask it.**

Sim Colley was forty-one and had been a compositor for twenty-two years and had two sheets of the broadside pulled on a stone on the sixth of August last, and he was pulling a third thing on the Friday and did not stop for it.

"**We are not here about a job,**" said Nessa Pike. "**We are here about the thirty-eighth line.**

"**I know it is the thirty-eighth line. Everybody in this harbor knows it is the thirty-eighth line. Half of them think it is a boast and the other half think it is a confession and the ones who think it is a boast do not say so out loud.**"

"**Then here is the question and it is one sentence. Who asked you for the thirty-eighth line.**

"**Nobody asked me for it. Your man came in on the fourth of August with thirty-seven lines written out for me on a board and I said, and I said it out loud and I remember it because I thought it was unusual: *that is a page that cannot be checked unless somebody can find the other one.* And your man said, *that is the point, and I am going to have it printed at the bottom where the man at the top cannot take it off.* And I pulled the sheets and he took them up the hill and that is the whole of the conversation I have had with any of you in six months.**

"**And I have set it four times since the eighth of August,**" said Sim Colley, "**which is the part I want to say and which I have been wanting to say to somebody since the Tuesday. Once for a man in a place down this coast who came into this room on a Tuesday and paid me in advance and asked for it in a clean hand, which I do not do. Once for a woman in a place up this coast who asked for thirty-seven lines and did not ask for the thirty-eighth and I put the thirty-eighth on anyway and charged her the same and told her why. And twice for men in this town who could not read and who wanted it read to them by somebody, and a man of about fifty-five read it out loud in a room off the fish market for a penny a sheet and took the penny out of what he was given.

"**And I have a book. Every job goes in it, the day, the sheet, the figure and the time, and it is my own book and it is in that drawer. And in it, on the eighth day of August last, there is one line against two sheets at a shilling and eightpence, and it is the only line in that book in twenty-two years with no name on it, and the only line with no name on it is the one you put in yourself, and I did not ask you for a name then and I am not asking you for one now.**"

He pulled the stone and wiped his hands and did not look up again.

"**That is a man who has kept a book for twenty-two years,**" said Calder Marr, as they came out into the lane, "**and the only line in it with no name on it is ours. That is four months and it is the fourth thing in five volumes that has come back to us off our own page.

"**And he has set it four more times since and one of those four was for a man who could not read and who was read to in a room off this fish market by a man of about fifty-five for a penny a sheet, and I want that entered, and I want it entered in the first column and not the second, because a man in this town can walk into that room and ask that man what he read and when and for whom, and be told.**"

---

*First column, entered on Monday the fifteenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at eight in the morning on the said Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and came down bare, and that that was the forty-first Monday since the first of May past, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that there was no paper in this harbor on any wall that this party put there.*

*And that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Monday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the fourth of the six rota days in the month of February that this party has entered walked on none of.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Sunday nor at any hour on the said Monday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either of the two days.*

*And that no day of work was booked on the said Sunday, and that the reason against that line is that the two of them spent the whole of that day at the top of that bench counting a printed sheet line by line, and that a day spent counting a thing this yard printed is a day this yard spent and is not a day this yard lost.*

*And that the said sheet is a folded broadside of about the size of a board with thirty-eight lines on it, of which thirty-seven are days, being the days from the sixteenth of June to the twenty-second of July past, and that the said thirty-eighth line is at the foot of it, is not a day, was never a day, and is the only line in that sheet that this party did not write for itself; and that the said line says that there is a copy of that sheet in about sixty boats on this coast, and that if a man has one and that one has changed he is to say so to the yard that printed it, and that the yard that printed it is at the head of that dock, and that any man may go in there and read the said line and see where the other ones are.*

*And that the said sheet went up on the outside of the market wall in that town on the fifteenth of August past and came off on the sixteenth, and that it has been printed in that harbor ever since, and that it is the only thing this yard has ever had in type.*

*And that four days in the middle of the month of July past are in that printed sheet, being four days with a day at the end of them and no name at the end of that, and that they are the four lines immediately above the line of the twentieth day of the said month, and that they have been in about sixty boats on that coast since the fifteenth of August past, and that this party has not said out loud in any street or room that they are in that sheet; and that the said four lines stand and are not struck out, and that a line does not come out of a column in that book because it has been printed and cannot be got back.*

*And that on the said Sunday afternoon this party went down that mole into a lane off it and into a room with two presses in it, and that a compositor of forty-one had pulled two sheets of the said broadside on a stone on the sixth of August past, and that the said compositor said that nobody asked for the thirty-eighth line, and that the holder of the said writ said it on the fourth of August past that a page that cannot be checked unless somebody can find the other one is the point of the sheet, and that it was to be printed at the bottom where the man at the top cannot take it off.*

*And that the said compositor said that he has set that sheet four times since the eighth of August past, once for a man in a place down this coast who paid in advance and asked for it in a clean hand, once for a woman in a place up this coast who asked for thirty-seven lines and did not ask for the thirty-eighth and was given the thirty-eighth and charged the same, and twice for men in that town who could not read and had it read to them by a man of about fifty-five in a room off the fish market for a penny a sheet; and that the said compositor keeps a book of every job with the day and the sheet and the figure and the time in it, and that in that book there is one line against two sheets at a shilling and eightpence on the eighth of August past with no name on it, and that it is the only line in twenty-two years with no name on it, and that he did not ask for a name then and is not asking for one now.*

*And that on the said Monday this yard worked from eight until about one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man; and that the gross on that board was thirteen and ten of the thirteen was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the fifteenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a line in a printed sheet that says where every copy of that sheet is, and that says that any man may go and read it, has been in about sixty boats on that coast since the fifteenth of August past, and that this party has been afraid of it and proud of it in the same breath, and that the reason against that line is that a party which has refused to hold a record for a coast and has said so in the open in about eleven people's hearing is read as a party which means to keep it, and that a line which puts every copy within reach of any man who can get into a boat is the same act as a hold and is the opposite of a hold, and that this party printed the one and has been describing it as the other.*

*And that the thirty-eighth line does not put a page in anybody's hands and only says where the copies are, and that this party has spent four volumes treating the second sentence as the first one, and that the said difference is entered on that page and is not going to be argued.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing on the said Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and forty marks, six shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is six thousand four hundred and seventy-four pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five thousand eight hundred and thirty-four pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and sixteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The slate wall came down bare on the forty-first Monday since the first of May past, and the five lines that were on that wall on the eleventh of February came down on that Monday at eight and this party entered that and is not going to enter it again. The cistern was charged at no figure on a rota day and this party walked none of it, and the walk is nil and stays nil. The far bench was not occupied on either of the two days. The bench under that pump was bare for a fifteenth night on the Sunday and a sixteenth on the Monday. No money went out of this yard in these two days. Four returns are four and there is no fifth, and no finding of worked glass was made on either of the two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The third column is unfilled and unappointed and the boy's hand is at the head of it, and the four lines of July are not it and may never be converted into it. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved and unasked about. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, unargued, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid and unoffered, and forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, neither reduced and neither paid. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it has not been said out loud since the third of December past. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. A working harbor of this coast is poorer on the fifteenth of February next than it was on the fourth of it, and a page of that yard's is in about sixty boats on it, and nothing this yard has done in twelve days has made one thing better. Every other figure this harbor is owed is set out in the page above and is not read out again in this paragraph.**
