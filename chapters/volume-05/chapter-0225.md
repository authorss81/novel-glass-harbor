# Chapter 225

*A Crate of Chalk, and a Name at the Head of a Sheet*

Saturday the twenty-third and Sunday the twenty-fourth of January, in the shed.

**And no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday and the board was not written on either of them, and the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happened still happened, and that these two days were the third and the fourth of six days this fortnight that this yard spent on somebody else's page, and that this yard has entered that count and is not going to improve on it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and this party entered that and entered nothing against it of what came before it.**

**And on neither of those two days was anything read out in a room, and nothing was read out in a market, and there was no clerk in this shed and there was no man with a seal in this shed and there is no office in this shed of any kind, and the reason that is on the page is that this party has been saying since the seventh of December that a thing read out in a street is checkable by anybody who was in the street, and that a thing read out in a room is checkable by a clerk, and that a thing read at the top of a bench in a shed is checkable by one other person, and that one other person is what this yard has got.**

---

**And on the Saturday morning they took the folded sheet out from under the pump, and it came out in two pieces, because the fold had gone through in one place, and it was put back together with a weight on the corner of it and the two pieces were not joined and are not joined now.**

It is one sheet, folded twice, and the fold is a fold and not a tear, and it is the copy of a thing and not the thing, and this party knows it is a copy because the writing on it is even all the way down the page, which is one person writing all of it, and because the ink on the upper two thirds is a different make from the ink on the lower third, and because a copy of a thing that was made by a person has a person in it and the original does not.

Nessa Pike did not read it first.

"**You take it,**" she said. "**I have not read a page in twenty years that I had to write out afterwards and I am not going to start on one that somebody else put a name to.**

"**And I want it read out loud, and I want it read out twice, and I want you to read it the same way both times, including the parts you do not understand, and if you skip a part the second time I am going to know.**"

So he read it out loud, twice, and read the parts he did not understand the same way both times.

At the head of it, in a hand bigger than the rest and with a space under it, is the name. **THE NINE HARBORS.**

Under that, in the same hand and smaller: *an agreement made at a place, between nine, concerning a page.*

There is no date on the sheet. There is no number on it. There is no seal and no stamp and no signature and no office named anywhere on it, and this is the only one of the four pages on that bench with a name at the head of it.

Four of the lines are legible and the other two are not, and what there is says:

"**that a page about a place is the place's,**
"**and that it is not to be carried out of the place it is about,**
"**and that a copy is not a page and has none of a page's standing,**
"**and that no one of the nine is to be asked what another of the nine keeps.**"

And at the foot, in the second ink, and in a hand of that same place, and later, and not one of the four lines: **that a page was carried out of that place in a year nobody wrote down, and was brought back in another year, and has been kept ever since in a house that is not the house it was kept in before.**

Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half.

"**Say the last line again.**"

He said it again.

"**That is not one of them,**" said Nessa Pike. "**That is a hand of that place, and it is later than the four lines, and the ink is different, and it is somebody at that place writing down that a page was carried out of that place. And it does not say the carrying was wrong. It does not say it was right. It says it happened, and it says where it went afterwards, and that is a thing you write when you have decided to stop arguing about it.**

"**And there is a man in this harbor who has had a page in his book for nine years and who is carrying it home in the same book on Tuesday morning, and I do not know that he is one of the nine and neither does he, and I have not asked him and I am not going to.**"

"**I am not going to ask him either.**

"**And I am not going to say that the four lines at the top held. Nobody has asked me and I have got nothing to say about it. I am going to say three things and I am going to write them down and I am not going to improve on any of them.**

"**One. That is a name of an agreement. It is not a name of a body, and there is no body in it, and nothing on that sheet says that nine places ever made one thing of themselves or ever did anything again after the day it was written.

"**Two. That sheet is a copy. Whoever made it made it out of something. Whoever made it is a person and not a place, and a person made a copy and put the copy where a man selling lime would find it in a crate, and I do not know that person and I am not going to try.

"**Three. The name is on the page and I am not going to write it in this book.**"

"**You read it out twice.**
"**I read it out twice to two people on a Saturday in a shed and there is nobody else in this shed and there is not going to be a copy of it going anywhere, and a word that has been said out loud in a shed to one other person is a word that exists in two people and not in a page, and that is the whole of where it exists. It goes in this book as the name at the head of the fourth of the four and not as the name, and if you want to know what it is you will have to ask me and I will tell you, and I will tell you the same way twice.**"

---

**And at about four on the Sunday afternoon the man of sixty-four came up the hill for nothing, on his way to the coach, and put his head in the door of that shed for about a minute, and looked at the four pages on the bench, and did not come in.**

"**That is four,**" he said.

"**That is four.**

"**I have got one of them in a book nine yards from here and I am going to be asked about it at my own house tonight by a woman of sixty who is my aunt, and she is going to ask me why I gave it to two people I met on a mole, and I am going to have to say something, and I do not know what.**

"**If you were me you would say the truth and I have never in my life said the truth to my aunt about anything.**"

He went down the hill.

Nessa Pike said, to the holder, and not to the street:

"**He will say the truth, because he has the face of a man who has never once lied to anybody and does not know how. And what he does not know is that he has just been made the reason there are four pages on a bench in this harbor, and that is not a thing you can put in a book and it is not a thing you can pay him back for, and it is the first cost anybody has told us about face to face this month.**

"**Write it in the second column. Do not write his name. He did not give it and we did not ask him and it is in the second column and it has no name in it, and the reason against that is that a man who has been made a reason without knowing it does not need a page.**"

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday and the board was not written on either of them, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happened still happened, and that these two days were two of the six days this fortnight that this party spent on somebody else's page, and that this party entered that count and is not going to improve on it; and that the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour nor on the said Sunday at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered nothing against it of what came before it; and that on neither of the said days was anything read out in a room, and nothing was read out in a market, and there was no clerk in that shed and no man with a seal in that shed and no office in that shed of any kind.*

*And that on the said Saturday morning the said holder and the said woman took the folded sheet out from under the pump, and that it came out in two pieces because the fold had gone through in one place, and that it was put back together with a weight on the corner of it and that the two pieces were not joined and are not joined.*

*And that the said sheet is one sheet folded twice, and that the fold is a fold and not a tear, and that it is the copy of a thing and not the thing, and that this party knows it is a copy because the writing on it is even all the way down the page, which is one person writing all of it, and because the ink on the upper two thirds is a different make from the ink on the lower third; and that there is no date on the said sheet and no number on it and no seal and no stamp and no signature and no office named anywhere on it, and that it is the only one of the four pages on that bench with a name at the head of it.*

*And that the said woman said that the said holder was to read the said sheet, and gave the reason, that she has not read a page in twenty years that she had to write out afterwards and is not going to start on one that somebody else put a name to; and that she said that she wanted it read out loud and read out twice and read the same way both times, including the parts that are not understood, and that if a part was skipped the second time she would know.*

*And that the said holder read the said sheet out loud twice, and that at the head of it in a hand bigger than the rest is a name, and that under that name in the same hand and smaller is a line saying that it is an agreement made at a place, between nine, concerning a page; and that four of the lines on the said sheet are legible and two of them are not, and that what there is says that a page about a place is the place's, and that it is not to be carried out of the place it is about, and that a copy is not a page and has none of a page's standing, and that no one of the nine is to be asked what another of the nine keeps.*

*And that at the foot of the said sheet, in the second ink, and in a hand of that same place, and later than the four lines, and not one of the four lines, it says that a page was carried out of that place in a year nobody wrote down and was brought back in another year and has been kept ever since in a house that is not the house it was kept in before.*

*And that the said woman said that the said line at the foot is a hand of that place and is later than the four lines and the ink is different, and that it is somebody at that place writing down that a page was carried out of that place, and that it does not say the carrying was wrong and does not say it was right, and that it says it happened and says where it went afterwards, and that that is a thing you write when you have decided to stop arguing about it.*

*And that the said woman said that there is a man in this harbor who has had a page in his book for nine years and who is carrying it home in the same book on the said Tuesday, and that she does not know that he is one of the nine and neither does he, and that she has not asked him and is not going to; and that the said holder said that he is not going to ask him either.*

*And that the said holder said three things and entered them and is not going to improve on any of them. That the name at the head of the said sheet is the name of an agreement and is not the name of a body, and that there is no body in it, and that nothing on the said sheet says that nine places ever made one thing of themselves or ever did anything again after the day it was written. And that the said sheet is a copy, and that whoever made it made it out of something, and that whoever made it is a person and not a place, and that a person made a copy and put the copy where a man selling lime would find it in a crate, and that this party does not know that person and is not going to try. And that the said name is on the said page and is not going to be written in this book.*

*And that the said holder said that he read the said name out twice to two people on a Saturday in a shed and that there is nobody else in that shed, and that a word that has been said out loud in a shed to one other person is a word that exists in two people and not in a page, and that it goes in this book as the name at the head of the fourth of the four and not as the name, and that if anybody wants to know what it is they will have to ask him and he will tell them, and he will tell them the same way twice.*

*And that at about four on the said Sunday afternoon a man of sixty-four came up that hill for nothing on his way to the coach and put his head in the door of that shed for about a minute and looked at the four pages on that bench and did not come in; and that he said that it was four, and that he has got one of them in a book nine yards from that shed and was going to be asked about it at his own house that night by a woman of sixty who is his aunt, and that she was going to ask him why he gave it to two people he met on a mole and that he was going to have to say something and did not know what; and that he said that if this yard were him he would say the truth and that he had never in his life said the truth to his aunt about anything; and that he went down that hill.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said woman entered on the said day that a man of sixty-four was going to be asked at his own house that night by a woman of sixty who is his aunt, why he had given a page to two people he met on a mole, and that he was going to have to say something and did not know what; and that the said woman entered that he will say the truth because he has the face of a man who has never once lied to anybody and does not know how; and that the said woman entered that he has been made the reason there are four pages on a bench in this harbor and does not know it, and that that is not a thing this party can put in a book and is not a thing this party can pay him back for, and that it is the first cost anybody has told this party about face to face in a month; and that the reason against that line is that a man who has been made a reason without knowing it does not need a page and did not give his name and was not asked for it.*

*And that this party entered that the fourth of the four pages is a copy of an agreement between nine about a page, and that the name at the head of it is a name of an agreement and is not the name of a body, and that this party is not going to say that the agreement held, and that this party does not know whether it held, and that the fourth of the four does not say; and that the reason against that is that a thing a copy says about what happened after it was made is a thing a person wrote down and not a thing anybody agreed to, and that the four lines are what was agreed and the line at the foot is what one person saw afterwards, and that this party has put them side by side on a bench and has not decided whether that is one record or two.*

*And that the said four lines stand, and are nameless, and are not out of that column, and that the ground on which a line comes out of a column in that yard is one ground and it is the same one in every column.*

**No money came into this yard on the said Saturday and none on the said Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and twenty-five marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is four thousand and seventy-six pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three thousand four hundred and thirty-six pence. The wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest, the envelopes on that bench are twenty, and the weeks fallen due with no envelope against them are thirteen. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and is not read out again on this page.**
