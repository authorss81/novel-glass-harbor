# Chapter 217

*A Hand at a Counter, and What a Yard of One and a Half Can Actually Build*

Thursday the seventh and Friday the eighth of January, in a room off the fish market, on the face of the second seam, and in the shed.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on that Thursday morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, as it has walked none of them since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

---

**And at about eleven on the Thursday morning this party went down the hill into that room off the fish market for the second time in three weeks, and the counter is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, and where a sheet is a penny and forty sheets a shilling, and the copyist of fifty-eight was behind it and there were four people in the room and two of them were waiting.**

Nessa Pike put nineteen lines on the counter face down and then turned them over.

"**I have been in that room thirty-one years,**" said Pernel Saye, "**and nobody has ever put a list on my counter that was not a list of things to write. I am going to read this one and I am going to tell you which half of it I can price.**

"**I can price one, four and six.** One is a page. Four is a mark. Six is a mark and a half.

"**And I am not going to price two, three, five or seven, and I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to ask you three questions and I have never been asked three questions at that counter in a year and I do not know how this is going to go.**

"**What I can price is what you would pay a person to do for a day. And a man's time in a working port in the last week of December is about three marks, and I said that at this counter three weeks ago and I have said it twice since and I am not going to improve on it. That is the only unit I have and it is the only honest one, because a thing I cannot express in it I do not know the price of.**

"**What I cannot price is iron, and chain, and a weight, and a lamp, and a man going out in a boat in February. And I am not going to guess at any of them, and the reason is that a guess in a list is worse than a blank, because a blank can be filled in by a man who knows and a guess cannot be got out.**"

"**Then what can you tell us.**"

"**I will tell you three things and two of them are in my trade and one of them is not, and I will say which is which.**

"**The first, and it is in my trade. You have asked me what a yard of one and a half with about three marks in it can build. Three marks is a day at a good trade with the eighth and the share off the top. And in thirty-one years at this counter I have never once had a man come in here with three marks and build anything at all, and that is not a thing about you, it is a thing about three marks. Three marks buys about forty sheets of writing, or a man's victuals and his busk back to his own house, or nothing.**

"**The second, and it is in my trade, and I would like you to have it. A thing on the water is not a building. A building is made once. A thing on the water is a bill — and it is a bill every single time anybody looks at it, and nobody can produce the number of times anybody is going to look at it, and if a man tells you that number he has made it up.**

"**The third is not in my trade and you may do what you like with it. I have had a book behind that counter for thirty-one years and I could not tell you what is in nine tenths of it, and I would not trust it to keep a page of anything, and I have never thought about that until this morning, so you are not the first man to be surprised by it and you are not going to be the last.**"

She put her hands flat on the counter.

"**Now the three questions.**"

"**One. What has a man got to know to keep a thing on the water.**"

"**In my trade? The last time it was looked at, and by whom, and what it was found to be. That is three things and all three of them go in a book, and I will tell you what a book costs to keep: nothing. It is the cheapest thing in this country. And that is exactly why it is not worth anything, and I have just told you the reason and I did not know it until I said it.**

"**Two. Is there anybody in this harbor you would go to for that.**"

"**I do not know a man of fifty-eight who does not already have three men he goes to and I do not know one man in this harbor I would send a boat out in February with.**

"**Three. And this is the one I will take home with me, and I am telling you I am taking it home with me.**" She looked at the nineteen lines. "**A thing that has to be looked at is not made, it is kept, and the difference between the two is about four years and a bucket, and I have never in my life seen anybody budget for the keeping of a thing. They budget for the making of it. Every man who ever came to this counter with a list wanted a list written, and not one of them in thirty-one years asked me what it would cost to be the thing afterwards. Not one.**"

Nobody said anything for a while. There were two people waiting at the end of the room and one of them got up and came back in ten minutes.

"**You are not buying a sheet today,**" she said.

"**We are not.**"

"**Then I have made two sheets of nothing and I am going to charge you nothing for them, and that is the first time in a year and I am not going to get used to it.**"

Calder Marr put two fingers on the counter.

"**You did not ask me what the second column is.**"

"**No.**

"**Why not.**"

"**Because you have not offered it to me,**" said Pernel Saye, "**and because a woman of fifty-eight who has kept a counter for thirty-one years can tell the difference between a thing a man is selling and a thing a man is showing, and you have been showing me things for about an hour and you have not sold me one, and I have not asked about a thing I have not been given.**"

Neither of them said anything about that on the walk back up the hill.

---

**And on Friday the eighth day of January this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and the pump was his and the man was his, and the day was his account, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book, and nobody read anything back on that day because there had been four people in a room off the fish market on the Thursday and a thing said in a room off the fish market is read back by whoever was standing in that room and two of them were waiting for a counter.**

The board for the day is twelve gross. The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of twelve paid at the mole and the yard's being three.

**And the compressor in the engine house was not used on that day, and could not have been, and the gland is on the page at five marks since the nineteenth day of May past and it holds and it is a good piece of work and nothing came out of the other end of it, because there is no hose in this harbor that is not the fire party's, and the fire party's is dead until the spring. So the day came on a pump a man brought up a hill, and the three marks is for the hands and the line and the tide, and not one penny of it is for a machine, and that is what a day at a seam in this harbor is and it is going to be that until somebody in this town buys forty feet of hose.**

Three marks is four hundred and eighty pence. The bag held one thousand one hundred and ninety-six pence on the Saturday the twenty-sixth of December past, and one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence has been in it every day since the twenty-ninth of that month, and another four hundred and eighty pence came in on this Friday morning on the boards out of a hirer's own hand, which makes two thousand one hundred and fifty-six pence, which is thirteen marks, six shillings and four pence.

*First column, entered on Friday the eighth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on Thursday the seventh instant by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil.*

*And that at about eleven on the said Thursday morning this party went down this hill into a room off the fish market for the second time in three weeks, being a counter open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, where a sheet is a penny and forty sheets a shilling, and that there were four people in that room and two of them were waiting; and that the said woman put nineteen lines on that counter face down and then turned them over, being the list of the thirtieth day of December past in her own hand, and that the said copyist said that in thirty-one years nobody had ever put a list on her counter that was not a list of things to write.*

*And that the said copyist said that she can price one, four and six of those lines and will not price two, three, five or seven, and that the thing she can price is what a person is paid to do for a day, and that a man's time in a working port in the last week of December is about three marks, and that she said that at that counter three weeks ago and has said it twice since and is not going to improve on it, and that that is the only unit she has; and that the reason against the lines she will not price is that a guess in a list is worse than a blank, because a blank can be filled in by a man who knows and a guess cannot be got out.*

*And that the said copyist said three things and said that two of them are in her trade and one is not. That the first is that a yard of one and a half with about three marks in it cannot build anything, and that in thirty-one years at that counter no man has ever come in with three marks and built anything, and that that is a thing about three marks and not a thing about anybody, and that three marks buys about forty sheets of writing or a man's victuals and his busk back to his own house or nothing. That the second is that a thing on the water is not a building, and that a building is made once and a thing on the water is a bill, and that it is a bill every time anybody looks at it, and that nobody can produce the number of times anybody is going to look at it, and that a man who gives you that number has made it up. And that the third is that in thirty-one years no man who ever came to that counter with a list has asked her what it will cost to be the thing afterwards, and that they all budgeted for the making of it.*

*And that the said copyist then took three questions. That the first was what a man has got to know to keep a thing on the water, and that she answered it in her trade, and that what he has got to know is when it was last looked at, by whom, and what it was found to be, and that all three of those go in a book, and that a book costs nothing to keep, and that that is exactly why it is not worth anything, and that she said she had not known that until she said it. That the second was whether there is anybody in this harbor the holder would go to for that, and that she said she did not know a man of fifty-eight who does not already have three men he goes to, and that she did not know one man in this harbor she would send a boat out in in February. And that the third was not asked by the holder and was asked by the said woman, being that a thing that has to be looked at is not made, it is kept, and that the difference between the two is about four years and a bucket.*

*And that the said copyist said that this party was not buying a sheet on the said day, and that she said that she had made two sheets of nothing and was going to charge nothing for them and that that was the first time in a year; and that nothing was bought at that counter on the said day and no money of this yard went into that room; and that the said holder put two fingers on the counter and said that the said copyist had not asked him what the second column is, and that she said no, and that he asked why not, and that she said that because he had not offered it to her, and that a woman of fifty-eight who has kept a counter for thirty-one years can tell the difference between a thing a man is selling and a thing a man is showing, and that he had been showing her things for about an hour and had not sold her one, and that she had not asked about a thing she had not been given; and that neither of them said anything about that on the walk back up that hill.*

*And that on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man and that the pump was his and the man was his and the day was his own account; and that the gross on the board was twelve, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it, and that nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards and the count and the times went onto a slate in her own writing and the slate went into the book, and that nothing was read back on that day and that the reason against that line is that a thing said in a room off the fish market on the said Thursday is read back by whoever was standing in that room, and that two of them were waiting for a counter.*

*And that the compressor in that engine house was not used on the said day and could not have been, and that the gland is on the page at five marks since the nineteenth day of May past and it holds and it is a good piece of work and nothing came out of the other end of it, because there is no hose in this harbor that is not the fire party's and the fire party's is dead until the spring; and that the said day therefore came on a pump a man brought up that hill, and that the three marks is for the hands and the line and the tide and not one penny of it is for a machine, and that that is what a day at a seam in this harbor is and it is going to be that until somebody in this town buys forty feet of hose.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the eighth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this yard went to a trade in this harbor on the said Thursday and asked to be priced, and that a copyist of fifty-eight has been keeping a counter in that room for thirty-one years, and that no man on this coast had ever asked that trade what it costs to be a thing rather than to make it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill and ask the said copyist on any day of a week what she said to this party on the seventh of January instant and be told, and cannot check what any of the four people in that room will do with the third of the three things she said; and that whether it is a good trade to ask a trade is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the four people of this coast, who have not been asked and are not going to be; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the eighth of January and nothing came in on the seventh, and nothing went out of it on either day, and thirteen marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is two thousand one hundred and fifty-six pence. Nothing was bought at any counter this week and the only money that has gone out of this yard since the sixth of December past is one penny for a sheet of paper. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, and it has not been re-written, and the three counts of weeks under the figure are twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and are not moved by anybody. Nothing has been put in a tin in that shed for eight months and nothing was put in one this week, and the day is the twenty-eighth of April. To a man of Ashtide, three marks for the eighth of August past, and not one shilling of it has moved and not one man has been sent for it. This yard owes its own shipwright seven pence and has owed it since the month of August past. This harbor's own bellwright has had eight marks and a day of a boat owing to him since the twenty-third of May past, and nobody has argued it, and he was not asked anything this week. Eight shillings and tenpence, to a woman of this harbor, and it is on the page under her own name because she would have it that way. Forty marks, standing whole to a boy of twenty-two; the three nots of the eighth of June past; the head of the third column is his hand and nobody has been put in that column since the first of August past. The forty marks first after food to a person of no office, owing since the fifteenth of October past, is a figure this yard has not touched and is not going to touch, and the woman it is owed to has not been asked again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and eleven weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and a twelfth will fall due on Tuesday the twelfth of January instant. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and nine days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
