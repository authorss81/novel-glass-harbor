# Chapter 205

*Eleven Lines Beside a Form, and a Reason That Cannot Be Written Down*

Monday the fourteenth and Tuesday the fifteenth of December, in the shed.

**On Monday the fourteenth of December the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-second Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it, and there is a day and a room in his own page for the Thursday of this week and nothing has gone on the wall about it.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it and this party is not going to say in a street what that and this morning make between them.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Monday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, the ship's hand went to the shelf over the bench where this yard's own paper is kept and took down a sheet of it, folded once, and unfolded it, and it is the sheet of the twenty-second day of November past and it has eleven lines on it in her hand and there is nothing else on it and nothing has been added to it and nothing has been taken off it.**

"**Sit down,**" she said. "**I am going to put this beside that thing, and then I am going to go down eleven lines, and I have been thinking about it since Saturday and I have got it down to two and the two are not the same size.**

"**One. The place of the case of five bells. Two. The four people it belongs to. Three. The limit they wrote themselves. Four. The plate at the fifth seam. Five. The two objects on the crown of this mole. Six. The boy. Seven. The forty marks standing whole to him. Eight. The forty marks first after food to a person of no office. Nine. The delay. Ten. The letter that was coming up this hill on the Saturday. Eleven. The two figures for the bar of gravel and the Kettle, which was not looked at.**"

"**Ten has happened,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Ten has happened,**" she said, "**and it came back no, and it is the best thing that has come up that hill in four months and it does not go in anywhere, and that is item ten now and item ten was never going to go in anywhere. Read out the rest and do not stop me.**"

"**Three,**" she said, and she put her finger on it. "**The limit they wrote themselves. And here is the whole of it, because you have it in the first column of the nineteenth of October and so do I, and it is four sentences and I am going to read them in this shed once tonight and not again: the account is not to be read out, is not to go into a book that leaves this harbor, is not to be said to a man with a seal, and not one name to any office in any city.**"

Neither of them said anything for about a minute.

"**Now read the fourth paragraph of that form again,**" she said, "**the one that says the answer is to be entered with the name of the party, the place and the date.**"

"**I have it.**

"**Then say it back to me as a sentence and not as a paragraph.**"

"**A party which agrees to hold a copy of a record is a party that has put its name in a record.**"

"**And the limit says not one name to any office in any city.**"

"**The limit says not one name to any office in any city.**"

"**So we cannot put the limit in the answer,**" said Nessa Pike, "**because the answer has our name at the foot of it, and the limit is the reason, and a reason does not go into a record, and we have known that since the thirtieth of October and it has not improved.**

"**And we cannot put the limit in the answer without our name on it, and we cannot put our name on it without the limit in it, because that is what an answer is. So the only thing this yard has that would let it say no to that form is a thing that cannot be written down in a form, and that is not a figure and it is not a fault and it is the whole of what has happened in this shed since Wednesday.**"

She squared the eleven lines on the bench and then squared the form, and put them side by side with about four inches of bare wood between them.

"**That is the disagreement, and it is the whole of it, and I have been wrong about the shape of it since Wednesday morning and I am going to say how I was wrong, because I said in this shed on Wednesday that there is no list in this yard that says what does not go into a name, and that was a good sentence and it was a stupid one.**

"**There is a list. It is on this sheet. It is a list of what does not go into a room, and a copy of a record kept in a place IS a room. A shed with a pump under the bench is a room. This is the yard's own instrument for the one job it has just been asked to do, and it is eleven lines long, and it was written by a shipwright on a Sunday morning in twenty-eight minutes because she did not want to go into a room in Morrow Quay on a Thursday with the reasons off.**

"**And it works. That is the horror of it. Every one of those eleven lines is a thing this yard would be right to keep out of a room, and a place a record is kept in is a room, and so the eleven lines are the answer to that form, and the answer to that form is eleven lines this yard cannot put in an answer.**"

"**So what is the answer going to be.**"

"**A sentence with no reason in it,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I am not going to write that sentence this month, and here is why and it goes in the first column in your hand and not in mine. The day we put a no in a form with a name at the foot of it, that name is on a page in a city, and the reason we did it is a paragraph about four people alive in this town with no boat and about nine marks between them, and that reason does not travel, and so in about four years a man in a city is going to read a bare no from a yard of Morrow Quay and he is going to read it as a man who had somebody to be careful about, which is exactly the reading we have spent four volumes trying to put out of the world, and we would be putting it there ourselves with our own hand.**

"**So: no sentence this month. The sheet stays on the bench. And when the answer goes it is going to be a sentence that says we are not going to hold it, and it is not going to say why, and both of you are going to be alive when it is read and you are going to have to sit there and take it.**"

"**That is not a plan,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No,**" she said. "**It is a refusal to be helped, and you have had four of them this month.**"

**And on Tuesday the fifteenth of December the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now EIGHT, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December instant, and the fifteenth of December instant, and all eight are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all eight at once rather than the one that is easier.**

The column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, the fifth of March the oldest, and it does not move on a Tuesday on which nothing is paid. There are twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest. The bag holds seven marks, six shillings and five pence, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence; a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of those, and the figure the bag is short of the wage is five hundred and fifty-seven pence.

**And the eleven lines and the form were still on the bench in that shed on the Tuesday morning with about four inches of bare wood between them, and neither of them had touched either, and the eleventh line is the two figures for the bar of gravel and the Kettle, which was not looked at, and the reason against the eleventh line is the reason of the twentieth of October past and the twenty-second of November past, which is that a reason does not travel, and this yard has not moved that line off that list in three weeks and is not going to move it off it in a December.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Tuesday night the two of them read the day's entries back from the top of the bench, the ship's hand first and the holder after her, and nobody else was in that shed.**

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the fifteenth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on the said day was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the bag held seven marks, six shillings and five pence in it on the morning of that day, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence, and that a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and that the figure the bag was short of the wage is five hundred and fifty-seven pence; and that the column of this book stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest; and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is twenty; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is eight, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December instant and the fifteenth of December instant, and that the eight figures are eight different figures and none of them is either of the others; and that the reason against the line is the reason of the fourth instant, which is that no item on this harbor's list of what a yard owes is to be paid, advanced, reduced or improved in this month, and that the wage is on that list.*

*And that on the Monday the fourteenth instant at about half past eight in the evening, in this shed, the ship's hand took down from the shelf over the bench a sheet of this yard's own paper folded once, being the sheet of the twenty-second day of November past, and unfolded it, and that the said sheet carries eleven lines in her hand and nothing else, and that nothing has been added to it and nothing has been taken off it since the said day; and that she read the eleven out, being the place of a case of five bells, the four people it belongs to, the limit they wrote themselves, the plate at the fifth seam, the two objects on the crown of this mole, the boy, the forty marks standing whole to him, the forty marks first after food to a person of no office, the delay, a letter that was to come up this hill in the ordinary post on the Saturday, and the two figures for the bar of gravel together with the Kettle, which was not looked at; and that the holder said that the tenth has happened and that it came back no, and that the said woman said that the tenth is now a thing that has happened and was never going to go in anywhere.*

*And that the said woman read the limit of the four of the Sill out in this shed on the said evening, once, and that it is that the account is not to be read out, is not to go into a book that leaves this harbor, is not to be said to a man with a seal, and not one name to any office in any city; and that the holder read the fourth paragraph of the said form again and said it back as a sentence, which is that a party which agrees to hold a copy of a record is a party that has put its name in a record; and that the said woman entered on the said evening that this yard cannot put the said limit in an answer to the said form because the answer carries the name of the party, the place and the date, and that a reason does not go into a record; and that this yard cannot put its name to an answer without the limit in it, because that is what an answer is; and that the only thing this yard has that would let it say no to that form is a thing that cannot be written down in a form, and that this is not a figure and is not a fault.*

*And that the said woman said in this shed on the said evening, and it is entered here in the holder's own left hand at her asking, that a place a copy of a record is kept in is a room, and that the eleven lines on the said sheet are a list of what does not go into a room, and that every one of them is a thing this yard would be right to keep out of a room, and that the eleven lines are therefore the answer to that form and the answer to that form is eleven lines this yard cannot put in an answer; and that the day this party puts a no in a form with a name at the foot of it then that name is on a page in a city, and the reason for the no is a paragraph about four people alive in this town with no boat and about nine marks between them, and that the said reason does not travel, and that in about four years a man in a city is going to read a bare no from a yard of Morrow Quay and read it as a man who had somebody to be careful about, which is the reading this yard has spent four volumes trying to put out of the world and would be putting there itself with its own hand; and that it is entered here because a man can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask whether the sheet of the twenty-second of November past is on the shelf, and it is.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the fourteenth instant, being the thirty-second Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Monday and this party walked it, and the walk is nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the fifteenth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand said in this shed on the Monday that there was a no sentence available to this yard and that she is not going to write it this month, and that she gave the reason, which is the reason of the sixteenth day of November last about a part payment and the reason of the twenty-second day of November last about eleven lines, and that a bare sentence in a form with a name at the foot of it is going to be read in about four years as a man who had somebody to be careful about; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether a man is comfortable being read that way in four years is not a thing anybody can check from a mole and is not anybody's business except the four people of this coast who wrote a line in the month of August, who are alive in this town with no boat and about nine marks between them, and who have not been asked and are not going to be.*

**No money came into this yard on the Monday and none on the Tuesday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and seven marks, six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, eight weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and eighty-five days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter was written about it in these two days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted and did not put it near a paper about a form in a city. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. And a form with no date on it is on the bench in that shed and has not been answered, and the answer to it is on a sheet of this yard's own paper folded once on the shelf over the bench, and the two of them have been eleven inches apart on a bench for three days.**
