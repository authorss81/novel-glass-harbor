# Chapter 220

*A Corner Gone Soft on a Bench, and There Is No Place in This Harbor That Is Not a Hold*

Wednesday the thirteenth and Thursday the fourteenth of January, on the crown of the mole, and in the shed.

**At about ten on that Thursday morning four men this yard does not know charged the cistern behind the engine house at no figure, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the sixth rota day since the twenty-eighth of December past, and it is the twelfth of them this party has entered walked on none of.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.**

---

**And on the Wednesday morning at about nine they went up the crown of the mole and went past the two objects of nobody's and did not touch either of them, and they stood there about two minutes and looked at the mouth of the harbor, and they came down.**

**And on the Thursday morning, at about half past eight, the ship's hand picked the sheet and a half up off the bench, which is the sheet with no signature and no date on it and the name of an office at the foot of it, and which has been on that bench since the ninth day of December past under a pump, and the corner of it that was folded under is gone soft, and it is soft about the width of two fingers and it is not wet.**

"**That is a month,**" she said, "**and I have known since the fourteenth day of December last that I was not going to write that sentence this month, and I have written nothing since, and I have not once lifted it off that bench, and the reason I have not lifted it off that bench is that there is nowhere in this harbor for me to lift it on to.**

"**Do not want to. That is the whole of the reason and it is about half a second long,**" said Calder Marr.

"**You have had thirty-six days to find a place for it and there is not one in this harbor and I am not going to say that in a street,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**And the sheet is going. That is the thing I have not said out loud since the ninth of December. It came up this hill on foot and it is going soft on a bench under a pump, and it is a sheet and a half and it is worth a penny and there is not a figure of a penny against it and a man cannot buy it anywhere on this coast,**" said Calder Marr.

"**And the cost of that is a sheet of paper. And the cost of the whole of the three weeks before it is a day of about three marks refused on the twenty-fifth of December, and a day of about three marks not taken on the first of January, and four marks, one shilling and eightpence out of a man's share in a boat of about forty tons, and this yard's own method said out loud in a street in about nine people's hearing, and a name off a list in a book in the next town down this coast, and about six marks that a man of thirty-one will not get back. And that is the whole of it and every one of those is on the page.**"

"**Say the next one, because I know there is a next one and you have had it in your face since Saturday,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**The next one is that the only way this yard is going to be read correctly about that sheet is to have somewhere to put the copy that is not a hold. And this yard has not got one, and knows what one would cost, and is not going to pretend it knows the cost of a thing it has not built.**"

"**That is what I am going to write tonight. And the reason it goes in the first column and not the second is that a man in this town can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask the two of us whether we have a place to put a page in, and be told we have not, and can go down to a chandler's on the north side and ask a man of sixty-eight whether he has offered us his shelf and been told that he has.**"

---

**And at about half past eight on the Thursday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in that shed, the two lines went in, and then the third went in after them, and the third one is the one anybody is going to remember this winter for and it is nine lines long.**

Nobody in that shed argued with the third one and nobody in that shed improved on it.

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the fourteenth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the sixth rota day since the twenty-eighth day of December past, and that it is the twelfth of them this party has entered walked on none of, and that the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it; and that the far bench was not occupied on the Wednesday instant at any hour nor on the said Thursday at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.*

*And that on the Wednesday instant at about nine the two of them went up the crown of that mole, went past the two objects of nobody's, touched neither of them, stood about two minutes looking at the mouth of this harbor, and came down; and that the figure against those two objects is in the closing position below and is not given twice.*

*And that on the said Thursday morning at about half past eight the said woman picked up off that bench the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it and the name of an office at the foot of it, which has been on that bench since the ninth day of December past under a pump, and that the corner of it that was folded under is gone soft about the width of two fingers and is not wet; and that the said woman said that that is a month, and that she has known since the fourteenth day of December past that she was not going to write that sentence this month and has written nothing since and has not once lifted it off that bench, and that the reason she has not lifted it off that bench is that there is nowhere in this harbor for her to lift it on to; and that the said holder said that that is about half a second long and is the whole of the reason.*

*And that the said woman said that the sheet is going, and that it came up this hill on foot and is going soft on a bench under a pump, and that it is a sheet and a half and is worth a penny, and that there is not a figure of a penny against it and a man cannot buy it anywhere on this coast; and that the cost of that is a sheet of paper; and that the cost of the whole of the three weeks before it is a day of about three marks refused on the twenty-fifth day of December past by this party, a day of about three marks offered on the first day of January instant and not taken, four marks one shilling and eightpence out of a man's share in a boat of about forty tons since the middle of July past, that boat being a hand short since the fifteenth of May past, this yard's own method said out loud in a street on the sixth of January instant in about nine people's hearing, a name off a list in a book in the next town down this coast on the eighth of January instant, and about six marks that a man of thirty-one will not get back; and that every one of those is in this book and every one of those is checkable by a man who can get off a mole.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the fourteenth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a refusal to hold is read as an intention to keep, and that this yard has found that out in the street at the head of this dock in about eleven people's hearing on the ninth of January instant and has entered it, and that the reason it cannot be corrected is that there is no sentence of it that is both true and safe, and that the reason against that is that this party does not have a reason that does not cost somebody, and that it looked for one for two days and did not find one, and that the four people of this coast are not going to be asked; and that whether a yard of one and a half is a place anybody can be trusted with a record is not checkable from a mole and is not going to be answered by anybody in this harbor; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And then the third entry, and it is in the first column and it is in the holder's own left hand and the date and the hour are on it: that this party entered on the said day that a party which has refused to hold a copy of a record for the coast, and has said so in the open in about eleven people's hearing, is read as a party which means to keep it; and that the only way to be read correctly is to have somewhere to put it that is not a hold; and that this yard has not got one, and knows what one would cost, and is not going to pretend it knows the cost of a thing it has not built; and that this yard has been a party of one and a half since the twenty-third day of October past and is one and a half until the first of March next, and that there are forty-five days between the said day and the first of March next, and that the second pair of hands this yard needs is not bought with money but found, and that the only way this yard knows to find one is to work, and that a day at the face of the second seam is about three marks, and that the three marks are the whole of the money this yard makes; and that a question is standing after that entry which this party is not answering this month, which is what a place that is not a hold is, and who would keep it, and what it costs, and where the money for it comes from in a harbor where the mole-head light is not on because a light costs oil; and that this party entered that it is not going to answer that question in a market, is not going to answer it in a room, and is not going to answer it in this book, and that the reason is that a yard which has an answer to that question and gets it read out of its own book in four years is a yard that has answered it in a street this month.*

**No money came into this yard on the thirteenth of January and none on the fourteenth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and sixteen marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, and the whole of the money that has come into this yard since the sixth day of December past is one thousand nine hundred and twenty pence, being a day at the face of the second seam on the twelfth of December past and three days at about three marks on the twenty-ninth of December past, the eighth and the eleventh of January instant, and the whole of the money that has gone out of it in the same time is one penny, for a sheet of paper bought at a counter off the fish market on the eighteenth of December past. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the yard did not say that note out loud in the five weeks ended on the fourteenth of January and is not going to. There is nothing in a tin in that shed, and there has not been since the twenty-eighth of April past, and there was not anything in one in any of these twenty days. Three marks to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past and every day of the twenty has passed without a word about it, and he is not in this book. Seven pence is owed to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter, and it is the oldest small debt on this page that is not being argued, and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these twenty days, and no digit of anything he said on the second of July past is printed in this book, and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence is owing to a woman of this harbor, and it is written under her own name because that is what she asked for and nothing has ever been done about it. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, owing since the fifteenth of October past, and not one mark paid and not one shilling offered, and she told this yard not to ask her again this winter, and this yard has not asked her once in the twenty days ended on the fourteenth of January instant. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June past stand, and the head of the third column keeps that boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August past and nobody has been appointed to it and nobody is going to be appointed to it. The column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and its oldest line is still the fifth of March, the envelopes on that bench are twenty, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against any of them is twelve. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard has been inside its first clause on any of the twenty days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The case of five bells stands at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on the schedule of nobody at all, and the four people it belongs to hold a limit, and the holder has not asked to have it lifted. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and fifteen days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the fourteenth day of January instant and this party walked it. The walk is nil and it stays nil. Sixteen marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is two thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, and there is a corner of a sheet and a half gone soft on a bench in that shed. There is no place in this harbor that is not a hold, and that is in the first column with the date and the hour on it, and nobody in that shed improved on it.**
