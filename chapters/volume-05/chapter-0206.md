# Chapter 206

*A Sentence in a Street, and a Reason That Does Not Travel*

Wednesday the sixteenth and Thursday the seventeenth of December, at the head of this dock, and in the shed.

**Neither of these two days carried a day of work and the board was not written on either of them, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened; and a day on which two things happened and neither of them was work is a day that has to be entered twice and the second time it says so.**

---

**And at about half past eleven on the Wednesday morning, at the head of this dock, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, a man of the third lay asked this yard one question, and he asked it standing, and he said at the start that he was not asking it for anybody.**

Denzil Hare, of the third lay, about fifty, with a boat of his own and no crew of his own since the spring, who asked in a market on the second of December whether a case of five bells was coming up, and was told that it is on nobody's schedule.

"**There is a thing on that bench up that hill,**" he said. "**I have had it from three people since Friday and none of them has been up there, and I am not asking you what is on it. I am asking what you are going to do about it.**

"**And I will tell you why I am asking and it is not to be difficult. I have got a boat of my own and no crew of my own since the spring, and every week since the spring somebody has come to me and said a yard up your hill is going to be doing this, and a yard up your hill is going to be doing that, and I have stopped repeating it back because I have nothing to repeat.**"

"**Then I will give you the honest shape of it,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am giving it to you in a street on purpose, because you are standing in a street and a thing said in a street is checkable by anybody who was in it, and a thing said in my shed is checkable by one other person and that person is the ship's hand of this party.**

"**There is a sheet and a half on the bench in that shed. It has no signature on it and no date on it and the name of an office at the foot of it, and that office is not in this yard's book and I do not know what office it is. It proposes that this yard hold a copy of a record for the coast and be named in an entry as the person who holds it. And it says on its own face that a party which holds a copy becomes the address of that copy, and that a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and that an address can be found.

"**And here is the part that is going to be the answer when the answer comes, so you may as well have it in December. The answer will be in my own hand and it will be this yard's answer and not an office's, and it will not be signed by a clerk, and there is no day on that sheet for it to be late.

"**And I am not going to be able to tell you today whether it is going to be a yes or a no.**"

"**That is not an answer,**" said Denzil Hare.

"**No.**

"**And I am not going to be in a position to tell you in three weeks either,**" said Calder Marr, "**and that is the honest shape of it, and I would rather you had it from me in a street than stood in this street working it out of my face for a month.**"

Somebody further up the dock said, to nobody in particular, *Well.* It is not recorded here that anybody said anything, because nothing was said.

---

**And on Thursday the seventeenth of December, at the head of this dock, with about nine people in the street, two men of the first lay were saying that this yard had not said no.**

They were saying it to each other and not to anybody, and this party did not go up and correct either of them, and one of them said the word *hold* twice.

**And at about ten on the Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the Thursday at any hour, and the last time before these two days that it was empty for two days together was in the month of September past, and this party entered that and did not go and look at it twice.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Thursday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, the ship's hand did not take the pencil off the bench and neither of them read the day back, because a thing that is said in a street is read back by whoever was standing in the street and there were about eleven of them and not one of them is in this shed.**

"**You have done it,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I have done what.**

"**You have said in a street that you cannot tell a man in a month, and four hundred people have heard you, and a man who cannot tell you in a month is a man who is thinking about it.**" She was not sitting down. "**That is what I would have said about it. That is exactly what I would have said about it, and I would have said it in the same words, and I would not have said it on purpose, and I have said things to men at that dock about that yard before and I have never once thought about what they carried out of the street with them.**

"**And you cannot put it right.**

"**You cannot put it right, and I am going to tell you why and then I am going to stop, and you are going to write down the why and not the what.**

"**To say no in that street you would have to say the whole of it. You would have to stand there and say that a form came up a hill and that a limit was written by four people of this coast who are alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and that the limit says not one name to any office in any city, and that the limit is the reason and the reason does not go into a record and does not go into a street either. And the second you say that, three men in this town know a thing about four people who did not know it before Friday, and one of them will repeat it, and he will repeat it in a market, and in about four years it will be a thing a man in a city can look up and find out four people on a weir who said no to something in a market in Morrow Quay in December.**

"**So you cannot say no in the street, and you cannot say yes in the street, and you cannot say *I have not decided* twice, because the first time it was true and the second time it is a thing a man says when he has decided.**"

"**Then what is left.**"

"**Nothing is left in that street,**" said Nessa Pike. "**That is the whole of it. There is nothing left in that street and there never was, and every word any of us says in it from here on is a man using his own book on other people, and I have got four volumes of a book about a reason that does not travel and I have just watched you travel one in ninety seconds in eleven people's hearing.

"**Write it down. Do not fix it. And do not go up to those two men tomorrow, because the going up is the only thing that would make it a real thing instead of a thing somebody said in a street.**"

*First column, entered on Thursday the seventeenth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on Wednesday the sixteenth instant or on Thursday the seventeenth instant and that the board was not written on either day, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and that the said day is a day on which two things happened and neither of them was work; and that at about half past eleven on the said Wednesday, at the head of this dock, in about eleven people's hearing, a man of the third lay, who gave his name as Denzil Hare and who is about fifty and has a boat of his own and no crew of his own since the spring, asked this party one question, which was what this party is going to do about a thing on a bench up the hill, and said at the start that he was not asking it for anybody and gave the reason, which is that he has had it from three people since Friday, none of whom has been up the hill, and that he has stopped repeating it back because he has nothing to repeat.*

*And that the holder answered in the street on purpose and gave the reason he answered in a street, which is that a thing said in a street is checkable by anybody who was in it and a thing said in this shed is checkable by one other person, and that he gave the whole of the shape of the said sheet, being that it is a sheet and a half with no signature and no date and the name of an office at the foot of it, that the office is not in this yard's book and this yard does not know what office it is, that it proposes that this party hold a copy of a record for the coast and be named in an entry as the person who holds it, and that it says on its own face that a party which holds a copy becomes the address of that copy, and that a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and that an address can be found; and that he said in the street that the answer will be in his own hand and will be this yard's answer and not an office's, that it will not be signed by a clerk, and that there is no day on the said sheet for it to be late; and that he said in the street that he was not going to be able to say on that day whether it was going to be a yes or a no.*

*And that the said man said that that is not an answer, and that the holder then said in the street that he was not going to be in a position to say in three weeks either, and that that is the honest shape of it; and that the said man said he would rather have it from him in a street than stand in the street working it out of his face for a month; and that nothing further was said in that street that is in this book.*

*And that on the said Thursday at about ten in the morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and the walk is nil; and that two men of the first lay were saying at the head of this dock on the said Thursday, in about nine people's hearing and to each other and not to anybody, that this yard had not said no, and that one of them said the word hold twice, and that this party did not go up and correct either of them, and that the reason this yard cannot put the said sentence right is entered in the second column above and the reason the said sentence is entered in this column and not in the second is that a man in this harbor can come to this dock on any day of a week and stand where about eleven people stood on the Wednesday morning and ask any one of them what was said there, and can do that because about nine of them are still here and are still trading.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and that the last time before these two days that it was empty for two days together was in the month of September past, and that this party entered that and did not go and look at it twice.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the seventeenth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand told the holder in this shed on the said evening that he cannot put the sentence of the Wednesday right, and gave the whole of the reason, which is that to say no in that street he would have to say the whole of it, including that a form came up a hill and that a limit was written by four people of this coast alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, that the limit says not one name to any office in any city, that the limit is the reason, and that the reason does not go into a record and does not go into a street either; and that the second he says that, three men in this town know a thing about four people who did not know it before Friday, and one of them will repeat it, and he will repeat it in a market, and in about four years it will be a thing a man in a city can look up and find out four people on a weir who said no to something in a market in Morrow Quay in December; and that she said that he cannot say no in the street and cannot say yes in the street and cannot say he has not decided twice, because the first time it was true and the second time it is a thing a man says when he has decided; and that she said that there is nothing left in that street and there never was, and that every word either of them says in it from here on is a man using his own book on other people; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether a refusal made in a street makes a person safer or only makes a person harder to reach is not checkable from a mole, and that the four people of this coast who wrote a line in the month of August are the only people in this harbor who could answer it, and they are not going to be asked.*

*And that this party entered on the said day that the gap between the two columns above has not been closed and is not going to be closed in December, and that a reason is in the second column and a fact is in the first, and that in about four years a man in a city is going to have both of them and neither one of them in the other's language, and that this party has known that since the thirtieth of October past and has entered it four times since and is not going to improve on it in a December.*

**No money came into this yard on the Wednesday and none on the Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and seven marks, six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-seven pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the yard did not say that note out loud in these two days and is not going to say it a fourth time. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, eight weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and a ninth will fall due on the twenty-second instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and eighty-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter was written about it in these two days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted and did not say one word of it in a street. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. And a form with no date on it is on the bench in that shed and has not been answered, and a sentence said at the head of this dock on Wednesday last has not been taken back, and the two of those are in this book and are not in one another's line.**
