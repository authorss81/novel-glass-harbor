# Chapter 224

*A Stair in a House Four Days Up This Coast, and a Day of a Trade*

Thursday the twenty-first and Friday the twenty-second of January, in the shed, and on the face of the second seam.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged on that Thursday at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and this party has now walked none of them since the third day of August past, and that is the second of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Friday at any hour, and this party entered that and set no figure against it of what came before it.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Thursday and the board was not written on it, and the reason against that line is that the said man of sixty-four was at the top of that bench with his book from about eight in the morning and that this yard does not put in a day with a man sitting at it who has told this yard he does not want to be asked anything.**

---

**And a man of sixty-four came up that hill on the Monday morning with a book under his arm and stood about forty feet off the mole and waited until this yard was in the water, and then he went back down, and he came back on the said Thursday at about eight, and the reason he gave for not coming up on the Monday was that he was not going to stand on a mole at eight in the morning holding a book while a day was being worked.**

He put the book on the bench and opened it himself and turned it round and did not take his hand off it.

"**It is the fourth page of the ninth leaf and it is going to be the fourth page of the ninth leaf when it goes back in. I have had it in there nine years and I am going to put it back in the same place tonight and I am going to leave it on your bench until the Tuesday because the two of you said you would read it and I have decided to let you.**

"**And I am going to read it. I am not going to have you read it. I have read it about forty times and I could do it with the book shut and I want to hear somebody else in the room while I do it.**"

He read it. It is six lines and it is in a hand that is not his and is not his father's and his great-grandfather is the earliest hand anybody in his house can put a name to, which is not the same as the earliest hand on it.

"**It says: on a Tuesday in a bad month, Thomas Arrick of this place, aged thirty-one, went out in the *Mary Hask* of about sixty tons, and the tide was making at about six in the evening, and he did not come back, and there was a lantern in his pocket and a boot knife and two shillings and sixpence, and his mother held his things and his brother held the boat, and the boat came back and he did not.**"

"**That is a page about a person.**
"**That is a page about a man and a boat and a lantern and two shillings and sixpence, and it is the only page of the four on this bench that is about one man, and I want you to notice that, and I have been noticing it since Friday.**

"**And I want you to notice the other thing. My page says a man went out. Your page from the locker says a boat came in. Those are not the same boat and nobody in this shed is going to make them the same boat, because that would be a thing two men did so that two pages could be one page, and I have watched that happen to a page in this harbor and I am not doing it to yours.**"

---

"**I will ask you one thing and then I will go and set a stair,**" said the man of sixty-four. "**I have never showed that book to a living soul. Not my sister. Not the man I did the fire upstairs for. I have had it nine years and I have carried it up that coast road and back about thirty times, and every single time I have done it I have gone over the same four yards of road and looked at the same thing, and the thing I have looked at is not whether I am allowed.**

"**I have looked at whether anybody has noticed.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**That is a stupid thing to have said out loud to two strangers and I am not going to unsay it. You tell me whether I have been carrying it right. And I know what that question is worth and I am asking it anyway, and there is no figure in this shed that I can put against the asking of it.**"

Calder Marr took about nine seconds over it, which is a long time in a shed.

"**I am not going to tell you that you have been carrying it right. I could not do that if I wanted to and I have been in a room in this harbor where I have watched a man refuse to say a thing he did not know, and the whole of what this yard is is not saying it.**

"**I am going to tell you what I know, and then I am going to tell you what I do not know, and I am not going to put the second of them in the same breath as the first one as though they weighed the same, because they do not.**

"**What I know is that a page about a man who went out and did not come back, with a lantern and a boot knife and two shillings and sixpence in it, is a thing about a man and a boat and a lantern and two shillings and sixpence. That is worth having. I would give a day for that line and I have not got a day to give and I am not going to pretend I have.**

"**What I do not know is whether a page of a place has ever been carried out of that place, and I have never met anybody who knows, and I have not got the name of anything in this country that the answer would be in.**"

The man of sixty-four put his hand flat on the closed book.

"**Then I will go and set a stair, and I will put the page back where it was tonight, and I will go over the same four yards of road looking at the same thing.**"

"**You are going to keep doing it.**
"**I am going to keep doing it. I am sixty-four and it is the only thing I have that is not in a house where it does not belong.**"

---

**And the stair is a two-day job in a house on a street behind the fish market and it was booked to be done on the Thursday and it was done on the Friday and the Saturday, and a day of a joiner's trade in this town in this month is about three marks, and the said man put that figure in his own book on the Thursday night against the Thursday and not against the Friday, and this yard entered the figure because a man in this town can go and ask him what it cost him, and entered also that no money of this yard is against it and that it is not in this yard's money and is not going to be, and that the reason against that is that a day this yard's money did not lose is not a figure this yard's money lost.**

**And on the Friday morning this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man, and the board was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards, and nothing was read back on that night, and the reason against that line is that a thing four people had said in the front room of a house on the north side of this town on the Wednesday is read back by the four people who heard it through a wall and by nobody else, and that a man's own book is the only thing in this harbor that is read back to him by another person, and that the said man of sixty-four had not been asked anything on the Thursday and was going to be asked something at his own house that night and had not been told it in advance.**

---

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-second day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that this party has walked none of them since the third day of August past, and that that is the second of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of; and that the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour nor on the said Friday at any hour, and that this party entered that and set no figure against it of what came before it; and that no day of work was booked on the said Thursday and the board was not written on it, and that the reason against that line is that the said man of sixty-four was at the top of that bench with his book from about eight in the morning and that this yard does not put in a day with a man sitting at it who has told this yard he does not want to be asked anything.*

*And that a man of sixty-four came up that hill on the said Monday morning with a book under his arm and stood about forty feet off that mole and waited until this party was in the water and then went back down, and that he came back on the said Thursday at about eight, and that the reason he gave for not coming up on the said Monday was that he was not going to stand on a mole at eight in the morning holding a book while a day was being worked.*

*And that the said man put that book on that bench and opened it himself and turned it round and did not take his hand off it, and that he said that it is the fourth page of the ninth leaf and that it would be the fourth page of the ninth leaf when it went back in, and that he has had it in there nine years, and that he read it himself and would not have it read, and that he gave the reason, that he has read it about forty times and could do it with the book shut and wanted to hear somebody else in the room while he did it.*

*And that the said line is that on a Tuesday in a bad month a man of that place, aged thirty-one, went out in a boat of about sixty tons, and that the tide was making at about six in the evening, and that he did not come back, and that there was a lantern in his pocket and a boot knife and two shillings and sixpence, and that his mother held his things and his brother held the boat, and that the boat came back and he did not; and that the said line is in a hand that is not his and is not his father's, and that his great-grandfather is the earliest hand anybody in his house can put a name to, which is not the same as the earliest hand on that page.*

*And that the said man said that that is a page about a person, and that it is the only one of the four pages then on that bench that is about one man, and that he had been noticing that since the Friday before last; and that he said also that his page says a man went out and that the said page out of the locker says a boat came in, and that those are not the same boat, and that nobody in that shed was going to make them the same boat, because that would be a thing two men did so that two pages could be one page, and that he had watched that happen to a page in this harbor and was not doing it to theirs.*

*And that the said man said that he had never shown that book to a living soul, and that he had had it nine years, and that he had carried it up that coast road and back about thirty times, and that every time he had done it he had gone over the same four yards of road and looked at the same thing, and that the thing he had looked at was not whether he was allowed and was whether anybody had noticed; and that the said holder said that he was not going to tell him that he had been carrying it right, and gave the reason, that he could not do it if he wanted to and that he has been in a room in this harbor where he watched a man refuse to say a thing he did not know and that the whole of what this yard is is not saying it; and that the said holder said that what he knows is that a page about a man who went out and did not come back, with a lantern and a boot knife and two shillings and sixpence in it, is a thing about a man and a boat and a lantern and two shillings and sixpence, and that he would give a day for that line and has not got a day to give and is not going to pretend he has; and that the said holder said that what he does not know is whether a page of a place has ever been carried out of that place, and that he has never met anybody who knows and has not got the name of anything in this country that the answer would be in.*

*And that the said man said that he would go and set a stair and would put the page back where it was that night and would go over the same four yards of road looking at the same thing, and that the said holder said that he was going to keep doing it, and that the said man said that he was sixty-four and that it is the only thing he has that is not in a house where it does not belong.*

*And that the said stair is a two-day job in a house on a street behind the fish market, and that it was booked to be done on the said Thursday and was done on the said Friday and the said Saturday, and that a day of a joiner's trade in this town in this month is about three marks, and that the said man put that figure in his own book on the said Thursday night against the said Thursday and not against the said Friday; and that this party entered that figure because a man in this town can go and ask the said man what it cost him, and entered also that no money of this yard is against it and that it is not in this yard's money and is not going to be, and that the reason against that is that a day this yard's money did not lose is not a figure this yard's money lost.*

*And that this yard worked on the said Friday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and brought the man; and that the gross on the board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the ship's hand on the boards at about half past one and not afterwards.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the twenty-second day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said man said that in nine years of carrying a page out of a place and back he had never once stopped to ask himself whether he was allowed to, and that what he had looked at instead was whether anybody had noticed, and that the said holder could not tell him, and gave the reason that a man in a room in this harbor has watched a man refuse to say a thing he did not know and that the whole of what this yard is is not saying it; and that the reason against that line is that a thing this yard does not know is not a thing this yard is going to put in a page that goes into a book, and that this party has had four pages on that bench this week and has not been able to check one line of any of them.*

*And that this party entered that it has a page on that bench which is the fourth page of the ninth leaf of a book of two hundred and six leaves that has been in a chest in a house four days up this coast, and that the book is a man of sixty-four's own book and not a page of a place, and that the yard is holding it for two days at a man's word and at nobody else's, and that the reason against that is not entered in this column and is not going to be.*

*And that the four above the named line of the twentieth of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a line is not struck out of a column in that book on the ground that it is an awkward thing to have been written in it.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the twenty-second of January and nothing on the twenty-first, and nothing went out of it on either day, and twenty-five marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is four thousand and seventy-six pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three thousand four hundred and thirty-six pence, and the whole of the money that has gone out of this yard since the sixth of December past is one penny. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and thirteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. Every other figure this harbor is owed is on the page above and is not read over again here.**
