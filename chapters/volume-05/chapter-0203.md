# Chapter 203

*The Harbor Authority Agrees, and Says What It Cannot Do, and Names the Price of the Rest*

Thursday the tenth and Friday the eleventh of December, on the mole's steps, and in the shed.

**There was no day of work booked on either of these two days and the board was not written, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened, and that a day on which a sheet is read twice in one shed is a day on which something happened and it was not work.**

**And at about ten on the Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third of August, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

---

**And at about eleven on the Thursday morning, in the open air, at the foot of the mole's steps, with the market coming in behind them and about eleven people in the street, the holder of the writ of the first of May asked the officer of the harbor authority of this place one question, and he asked it in the open, and he did not go into that office to ask it.**

"**I am going to say what I am not asking for first, because in about four years somebody is going to read that a yard went up a hill twice in a week and I would rather the record had the order right.**

"**On Tuesday of last week I was in this street and I was refused a document about a Wednesday, and I said out loud that the third lay of this harbor had lost four days in June for the want of a room and that it would not happen again. And on the nineteenth of November I asked you for a room and a day and not for an instrument, and you named the price out loud before you said yes, and the price was a morning of a man of the tide gang, and I have not asked for one thing since and this morning is not that either.**

"**So: I am not asking your office for an instrument, I am not asking it to seat anybody, and I am not asking it to hold anything.**"

"**Then what are you asking,**" said Ors Kest.

"**I am asking you what your office thinks of a thing, and I am asking you out here, in the street, in about eleven people's hearing, because you told me in the street in November that you would rather say a thing out loud than have it found out in a market in March.**"

"**Say the thing.**"

"**A sheet came up this hill on Wednesday morning. It is a sheet and a half, no signature, no date, and the name of an office at the foot of it, and the name of that office is not in this yard's book. And it proposes that this yard hold a copy of a record for the coast and be named in an entry as the person who holds it. And it says on its own face that a party which holds a copy becomes the address of that copy, and that a copy which has been made cannot be unmade, and that an address can be found.**

"**And it does not say one word about what happens if this yard says no, and there is not a date on it anywhere, and the man who carried it up said with his own mouth that testimony is safe in one place and nowhere else and that this harbor is not that one.**"

Ors Kest took his hands out of his pockets, which he does not do at that end of the steps.

"**I am going to tell you what my office's view is, and then I am going to tell you what my office cannot do, and then I am going to name the price of the one thing I will do, and I am going to name it before I do it, and if you do not like it you can go back up that hill and put the sheet in the stove.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**My office's view is that a thing kept in Morrow Quay is a thing kept in Morrow Quay.**"

"**That is not an opinion about the sheet.**"

"**No.**" The reeve did not raise his voice, because that is not how he talks. "**It is a statement about the ground. And I am going to say the rest of it, and the rest of it is the reason I would rather you burned the thing than answered it.**

"**There is no office on this coast that can get at a copy of a record kept in this place. None. Not the one whose name is on the foot of that sheet, and not mine, and not the one in a city with the court on it. A record in a city can be asked for and can be sent for and can be got at by a man with a carriage. A record in Morrow Quay can be got at by about four people, and two of them are the men standing on this mole, and if a man in a city wants it in a fortnight he will not send a carriage. He will send a letter up a coast road and he will be very correct about it, and he will write to this harbor authority first, because that is the name a man writes first when he does not know any other, and my office's name is on the head of a chart.**

"**And my office will answer him. And my office cannot tell him a thing about a record, and it will tell him so, and he will then write to whoever else he can find. And that is how a man in a city finds out where a thing is.**"

Somebody up the steps said something to somebody else and neither of them said anything about it.

"**Now the part my office cannot do,**" said Ors Kest. "**It cannot certify a copy of a record. It cannot hold a record for this coast. It cannot enter this in my ledger, and the reason for that is not caution: that ledger is spent, there are eight acknowledgements in it, the ninth went in on the twenty-eighth of August at your party's asking, it is spent, and a tenth may not be entered and I will not enter one, and if you have come up here to ask me for a tenth then you have come up here for nothing and I would rather you knew that at the foot of the steps than at the end of a morning.**

"**It cannot renew the writ of the first of May, and it is the only office that can, and there is nothing in that line of mine that changes it. It cannot overrule a seizure. It cannot certify a glass witness. And I am not going to do the rest of it and pretend afterwards that it was part of it.**"

"**Then what is left of you in this,**" said Calder Marr.

"**A sentence in a street and a line on a page of my own,**" said the reeve. "**And I am going to name the price of both, and it is not a morning of the tide gang and it is not a day of the third lay and it is not anybody's body, and it is worse than either of those.

"**If I say this out loud and put it on a page of my own, then this office is a name in this business. Not the office that made the sheet. Mine. And in about four years a man in a city is going to be able to write down that the harbor authority of Morrow Quay stated in open air and in writing that a copy of a record in Morrow Quay is a copy of a record in Morrow Quay. And that is not against the sheet. It is not a refusal and it is not a warning and it does not say a word about any of the twelve men who have come up this hill about a document since the fourteenth of July.

"**And after I have said it once, every party who comes up this hill about anything at all is going to be told at the foot of these steps that this office has an opinion about a copy of a record. Every one of them. For years. Including the ones who have nothing to do with you and never will.

"**That is the price and I am naming it before I say yes, and I have been made to do this in front of people about five times this year and every time I have said yes first it has cost somebody who was not standing here.**"

"**Say yes or do not,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Yes.**" The reeve took a book out from under his arm and it was not the ledger and he did not open it. "**And I am putting it on this and not in that, and I am going to tell you why, and the reason is the same reason the clerk of the circuit office gave you on the twenty-third of November for a different document: a page of my own is not a record and cannot be produced on demand by anybody, and if this office is asked in a year whether it said a thing it will be able to say that a man wrote it on a page at his own table and not entered it anywhere, and that is a true answer and it is the best one there is.**"

"**And you are not a friend of this party,**" said Calder Marr, "**and you have said so out loud on a mole before and I have entered that this party agrees with you and is not going to improve on it.**"

"**I am not a friend of this party,**" said Ors Kest, "**and I have never said I was, and I am not going to begin on a Thursday in December either. And I am still not going to enter a tenth thing and I am not going to be asked twice, and if you want an instrument out of this office you have come to the wrong hill and I would rather say so now than in March.**"

---

**And on Friday the eleventh the far bench was occupied from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Thursday at any hour.**

**And at about four on the Friday afternoon a man came up the hill about a day at the second seam's own face on the Saturday, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, at about three marks and the eighth and the share off the top of it, and the yard took it, and neither brother said why they had taken it, and it is in the first column with the date on it.**

---

*First column, entered on Friday the eleventh day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on Thursday the tenth instant or on Friday the eleventh instant and that the board was not written on either day, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and that a day on which a sheet is read twice in one shed is a day on which something happened and it was not work; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Thursday and this party walked it, and the walk is nil.*

*And that at about eleven in the morning of the said Thursday, in the open air, at the foot of the mole's steps, in about eleven people's hearing, the holder of the writ of the first of May asked the officer of the harbor authority of this place one question, and that he asked it in the open and did not go into that office to ask it, and that he said first that he was not asking that office for an instrument, that he was not asking it to seat anybody, and that he was not asking it to hold anything, and that he gave the reason, which is that on Tuesday of last week he was refused a document about a Wednesday and said out loud that the third lay of this harbor had lost four days in June for the want of a room and that it would not happen again, and that on the nineteenth day of November past he asked that office for a room and a day and not for an instrument, that the officer named the price out loud before he said yes, and that the price was a morning of a man of the tide gang; and that this party has not asked for one thing since and the said morning was not that either.*

*And that the said officer said that the view of that office is that a thing kept in Morrow Quay is a thing kept in Morrow Quay, and gave the whole of the reason in the street, which is that no office on this coast can get at a copy of a record kept in this place, that a record in a city can be asked for and sent for and got at by a man with a carriage, and that a record in Morrow Quay can be got at by about four people, two of whom are the men on that mole, and that a man in a city who wants a thing in a fortnight will not send a carriage and will write to this harbor authority first, because that is the name a man writes first when he does not know any other, and that that office will answer him and cannot tell him a thing about a record and will tell him so, and that this is how a man in a city finds out where a thing is.*

*And that the said officer said in the street what that office cannot do, and that it cannot certify a copy of a record, cannot hold a record for this coast, cannot enter the matter in his ledger, and that the reason for that is not caution, which is that the ledger is spent, that there are eight acknowledgements in it, that the ninth was entered on the twenty-eighth day of August past at this party's asking and is spent, and that a tenth may not be entered and will not be entered; and that it cannot renew the writ of the first of May and is the only office that can; and that it cannot overrule a seizure, and cannot certify a glass witness, and that the said officer said he was not going to do the rest of it and pretend afterwards that it was part of it.*

*And that the said officer said that what was left of that office in this matter was a sentence in a street and a line on a page of his own, and that he named the price of both before he said yes, and that the price is not a morning of a man of the tide gang and not a day of the third lay and not anybody's body, and is that if he says it out loud and puts it on a page of his own then that office is a name in this business, not the office that made the sheet but his, and that in about four years a man in a city will be able to write down that the harbor authority of Morrow Quay stated in open air and in writing that a copy of a record in Morrow Quay is a copy of a record in Morrow Quay, and that that is not against the sheet and is not a refusal and is not a warning and says nothing about any of the parties who have come up that hill about a document since the fourteenth of July; and that after he has said it once, every party who comes up that hill about anything at all is going to be told at the foot of those steps that that office has an opinion about a copy of a record, every one of them, for years, including the ones who have nothing to do with this party and never will.*

*And that the said officer then said yes, and that he put it on a page of his own and not in the ledger, and gave the reason, which is that a page of his own is not a record and cannot be produced on demand by anybody, and that if that office is asked in a year whether it said a thing it will be able to say that a man wrote it on a page at his own table and not entered it anywhere, and that that is a true answer and is the best one there is; and that the said officer said again that he is not a friend of this party, that he has never said he was and is not going to begin on a Thursday in December, and that he is not going to enter a tenth thing and is not going to be asked twice; and that the holder answered that this party agrees with him and is not going to improve on that entry.*

*And that on Friday the eleventh instant the far bench at the end of that shed was occupied from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and the holder did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Thursday at any hour; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill on any day of a week and stand in that shed at the top of the mole and look down its length and see whether that bench is occupied or not, and that includes a Sunday, and that this party entered the Friday and did not enter the days of that week it was not on the hill for.*

*And that on Friday the eleventh instant at about four in the afternoon a man came up this hill about a day at the face of the second seam on the Saturday instant, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, at about three marks with the eighth and the share off the top of it, and that this party took it, and that neither brother said why they had taken it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the eleventh day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the officer of the harbor authority of this place said in the street that a man in a city who wants a thing in a fortnight will not send a carriage and will write to that office first, and that what that office is going to be asked about in four years is not the sheet on this bench and is this yard; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in this harbor can go and stand at the foot of those steps and hear what was said there, and cannot go and find out what is going to be written in a city in a month because somebody in a street said a thing out loud; and that whether naming an office in an open street makes that office safer or only makes that office easier to write to is not checkable from a mole, and that this yard put him in the street and knows what it did.*

**No money came into this yard on the Thursday and none on the Friday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and four marks and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is seven hundred and seventeen pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, seven weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and eighty-one days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter was written about it in these two days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short, and is four men short on a day when no man of it lost anything, which is the first Monday and the first Thursday in three weeks that this yard can say that about. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence, and that office has said in the street that it is the only office which may renew it and has not renewed it. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. And a sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it is on the bench in that shed and has not been answered, and a man in a coat has been up and down this mole once in six days and has not been asked to come again.**
