# Chapter 211

*A Board With Nothing in It For Fourteen Days, and the Thing After the Gland*

Saturday the twenty-sixth and Sunday the twenty-seventh of December, in the shed, and in the engine house.

**No day of work was booked on the Saturday and none on the Sunday, and the board was not written on either of them, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and this party entered that and put no figure against it of what came before it, because there are days of this month that this party has not entered and is not going up that hill again to enter, and a man who comes up and looks at that bench and asks the two of them what it was on the day before will be told that they do not know.**

---

**And at about nine on the Saturday morning the two of them went across the yard to the engine house and the holder put his hand on the compressor, and it was cold, and it has been cold since the middle of October, and neither of them had gone in there since the twentieth day of October and they went in now because a man who has written a thing down about a thing ought to look at the thing once in a while.**

It is an old machine and it belongs to the harbor and not to this yard, and it stands in the engine house because the engine house is where the harbor keeps the things it owns and cannot move.

"**Say what is on that page,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Say it in order, and I have read it and you have read it and neither of us has said it in a room.**

"**Five marks. Compressor's gland. The nineteenth day of May. Unpaid and useless.**"

"**Now say what useless means, and I have never heard you say it and I have wanted to hear it since the month of June.**"

"**It means the part is on the machine and it holds.**" He took his hand off it. "**The pressure goes into that end and it does not come out anywhere, and that is what five marks bought, and it is a good piece of work and the man who fitted it did it in a morning and did not overcharge me and I have not paid him either.**

"**And then the pressure is supposed to go somewhere, and where it goes is down a length of hose about forty feet long to a nozzle, and there is no hose. There is no hose in this engine house, there is no hose in that shed, and there is no hose in this harbor belonging to anybody who would lend one, and the fire party has one and it is the fire party's and it has been dead since before the frost and it is dead until the spring and the spring is four months off.**"

"**Then write down what a foot of it costs.**"

"**No.**

"**Why not. It is a pipe and a man can price a pipe.**

"**Because I do not know, and because I am not going to find out by asking a man in a chandler's shop what a foot of hose is worth, and then have a figure against it in this book that came out of a shop. And because the answer is not a figure anyway. The answer is that a compressor in a harbor with no hose is a thing that makes noise, and a day at a seam in this harbor is a day with somebody else's pump on it, and there is no way to write either of those down in marks and shillings and have anybody be able to check it.**"

She wrote nothing down. She wrote, in her own hand, in the first column, under the date, the word *engine house* and the word *looked at*, and no figure against either.

---

**And after that they went down to the boards and the board was clean.**

It is a board about five feet long and it is the same board it has been since the third of May and it has been scrubbed so often in eight months that the grain has come up. It is ruled in three columns in chalk, and the columns are the day, the gross, and what is left, and the last line on it is the sixteenth day of December and the day before that is the twelfth, and there is nothing on it for the thirteen, the fourteenth, the fifteenth, the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth, the twentieth, the twenty-first, the twenty-second, the twenty-third, the twenty-fourth, the twenty-fifth, and now the twenty-sixth.

"**Fourteen days,**" said Nessa Pike. "**And a man on a mole can count them, and that is the trouble with it.**

"**It is not a trouble,**" said Calder Marr. "**It is a figure, and I have spent four volumes saying that a figure is the only kind of thing worth having. I am not going to come in here in a fortnight and say that a figure is not the only kind of thing worth having because one of them is fourteen.**

"**Then say what you are going to do about it, and do not stand at that board.**"

"**I am going to take the next day that comes up this hill.**

"**And if the next day that comes up this hill is on a Tuesday and the wage is four marks?**

"**Then I will take the day and the wage will not go in, and both of those will be on the same line of the same page, and that is not a contradiction, it is a week.**"

"**Write that down.**"

"**I am going to write it down tonight with the date and the hour on it and in my own hand, and you are not going to thank me for it, and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

---

**And the working question was not answered in that shed on the Saturday, and it was answered on the Sunday, and it was answered by the two of them sitting at the bench with the lamp not lit, and it is entered here because a question a yard asks itself is a question and has to be on the page like anything else.**

The question was who is going to hire it.

A day at a seam is not a thing a yard goes and gets. A day at a seam is a pump, a hand lead's line, a man on the pump, and about five hours of a tide with a slack water in it at some hour or other, and a man on the mole who wants that. This yard has the line and this yard has the two of them and this yard has a writ that lets it go inside the outer marks and landward of the third seam's mark on anybody's own account. This yard has not got a pump. This harbor's gear has no boat of its own and that has been in this yard's book since before the first of May.

"**That is the whole of it,**" said Calder Marr. "**Every day I have taken in three weeks came up this hill because a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own brought a pump up it. I have not gone down the hill and asked anybody for a day. There is nothing to go and ask with. A day is not a thing you are owed. A day is a thing somebody needs and brings a pump for.**

"**Then do not go and ask,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and do not stand at that board either, and take the next one that comes.**

"**That is what I said ten minutes ago and you did not hear it because you were looking at the board.**

"**I heard it. I want it in your hand and not mine.**"

---

**The lamp was lit on the Sunday night at about half past eight and there were two of them in that shed and nobody else, and the day's entries went from the top of the bench in the ship's hand, and the holder read it back after her in the ordinary way, and then the two lines went in.**

The two lines were a day with nothing booked on it, with the reason against it, and a decision, with the reason against that.

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on the twenty-sixth instant or on the twenty-seventh instant and that the board was not written on either day, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened; and that on the said Saturday morning at about nine the holder and the ship's hand went into the engine house and looked at the compressor, and that the compressor is the harbor's and not this yard's and it stands in that house because that is where the harbor keeps the things it owns and cannot move, and that neither of them had been in there since the twentieth day of October past.*

*And that the said gland is on the page at five marks since the nineteenth day of May past, unpaid and useless, and that the said woman asked the holder in that engine house to say what useless means, and that he said that the part is on the machine and it holds, and that the pressure goes into that end and does not come out anywhere, and that that is what five marks bought, and that a day at a seam in this harbor is a day with somebody else's pump on it, and that there is no hose in that engine house and no hose in that shed and no hose in this harbor belonging to anybody who would lend one, and that the fire party has one and it is the fire party's and it is dead until the spring; and that the said woman asked him to write down what a foot of it costs and that he said no, and gave the reason, which is that he does not know what a foot of it is worth and is not going to get the figure out of a shop and put a shop's figure in this book, and that the answer is not a figure and is that a compressor in a harbor with no hose is a thing that makes noise; and that she wrote nothing down against the said hose and wrote in her own hand in this column the word engine house and the word looked at and no figure against either.*

*And that the board in that shed is clean from the sixteenth day of December past to the twenty-seventh instant, being fourteen days, and that the last day of work written on it is the twenty-second day of December past, and that a man on a mole can count fourteen days off that board, and that the said holder said that is not a trouble and that it is a figure, and that this yard has spent four volumes saying that a figure is the only kind of thing worth having and is not going to say in a fortnight that a figure is not the only kind of thing worth having because one of them is fourteen; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that any man in this town can come up this hill and look at that board and count what is on it.*

*And that the holder entered on the said evening, in his own left hand and with the date and the hour on it, that this party will take the next day of work that comes up this hill, and gave the reason against the line whole, which is that a day at a seam is not a thing a yard goes and gets, and that a day is a pump and a hand lead's line and a man on the pump and about five hours of a tide with a slack water in it, and that this yard has the line and two of them and a writ and has not got a pump, and that the harbor's gear has no boat of its own and that is in this book before the first of May; and that every day this yard has taken in three weeks came up this hill because a man of the second lay with a boat of about seventy tons of his own brought a pump up it, and that this party has not gone down that hill and asked anybody for a day and is not going to; and that the said woman said that a day is not a thing a yard is owed, it is a thing somebody needs and brings a pump for, and that the said holder said he wanted the whole of that in his hand and not hers, and that she said he was going to be thanked for it by nobody including her.*

*And that the said far bench was not occupied on the twenty-sixth instant at any hour and was not occupied on the twenty-seventh instant at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it, and that there are days of this month which this party has not entered and is not going up that hill again to enter, and that a man who comes up and looks at that bench and asks the two of them what it was on the day before will be told that they do not know.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a hose about forty feet long between a compressor and a nozzle is a thing nobody in this harbor can price, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can read a page and see a figure of five marks and a date of the nineteenth of May past and can ask what it is for, and can be told it is a part that holds, and can go and look at the machine and put his own hand on it, and that what it is worth without the thing after it is not a figure and is not checkable and is not going to be invented; and that the four people of this coast who could say whether a thing on the water in this harbor is worth having are not going to be asked about it, and that this yard has not asked and is not going to; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And that the holder entered on the said day that he cannot go up that mole's steps on a morning in this batch and that the reason against that line is not checkable from a mole, and that the day it costs is not entered against a day it was earned on.*

**No money came into this yard on the twenty-sixth and none on the twenty-seventh, and nothing went out of it on either day, and seven marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-six pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of that, and the figure the bag is short of the wage is five hundred and fifty-six pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. There is nothing in a tin in that shed and there has been nothing in one since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August past, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright, and it is not paid, and a sheet of paper at a penny is not how it is going to be paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor, with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June past stand, and the head of the third column keeps that boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August past and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid since the fifteenth of October past, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, and it does not move on a Tuesday on which nothing is paid. Twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and the count of them advances with the column and only with it. Nine weeks have now fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, and the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second of December instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and ninety-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
