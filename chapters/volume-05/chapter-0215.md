# Chapter 215

*The Thirty-Fifth Monday, and a Date on a Half Sheet That Arrived and Nothing Happened*

Sunday the third and Monday the fourth of January, in the shed, and on the mole.

**On Monday the fourth of January the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-fifth Monday since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held on that Monday and none is held on any Monday that the wall comes down on.**

**And at about ten on the said Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, and this party has walked none of them since the third day of August past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the Sunday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour, and this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.**

---

**And on the Sunday morning the ship's hand said the only thing anybody in that shed said about the month, and it was that the month is out.**

"**The month's out,**" she said.

"**So it is.**"

"**That is all I have got. I did not get past that and I am not going to get past that in a shed at nine in the morning on a Sunday.**

"**You did not get past it because it is not the sort of thing you get past. It is a month. There is a new one and this one is a different length from the last and that is the whole of the news in this room this morning.**

"**I know. I am not asking you for more than that. I want it written down that I said it, because a thing a woman says in a shed on a Sunday and nobody answers is a thing that is gone in a month, and there will not be one page in this book in a year that says any of us noticed anything.**"

He wrote it in the first column in his own hand, in the ordinary way, with the day against it, and what he wrote was that the ship's hand said in that shed on the Sunday morning that the month was out, and that the holder said that the month being out was the whole of the news in that room, and that neither of them said anything else about it, and that neither of them noticed anything else, and that this party entered it because a thing said in a shed is a thing that happened.

Then they went and did what they do on a Sunday, which is nothing that earns anything.

---

**And at about half past nine on the Sunday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in that shed, the day's two lines went in and one of them was a month.**

---

**And on Monday the fourth day of January the date on the half sheet arrived, and nothing happened.**

That is the whole of it and there is no more to it than that, and a yard which has spent four volumes writing down everything that happens to it is going to write down that, in those words, with the day on it, and is not going to improve on it.

The half sheet is a half sheet. It came up this hill in the ordinary post on the fourth day of December last, at about half past two in the afternoon while the tide was making, and it has the name of a court in a city at the head of it where a name would be if the thing had one, and the name of that same court at the foot of it, and no signature anywhere, and it says six paragraphs and the sixth of them says that a person who is not examined is not a person who has been refused.

It is on the bench in that shed. It has been on the bench in that shed since the fourth day of December last. The pump is under the other sheet, which is the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it, and the two of them have not answered either and have not put either in the stove.

**On Monday the fourth of January instant, a copy of a said record was to be produced to that court. This yard has not produced it. This yard did not ask any office for anything in aid of producing it, did not send anything to a city, did not write to a man in a coat whose name is on no page of this book, and did not go to the counter of that circuit office, and has not been to the counter of that circuit office since the twenty-fourth day of November past, which is a figure and a date and can be checked at a counter in about nine seconds by a man with the receipt.**

**Nothing else happened on that day in this harbor in connection with that court, and that is entered, because the yard of this book does not say that nothing happened unless the nothing has been looked for.**

There was no court in this harbor and none sat. No clerk of that court came up this hill. Nobody was charged with anything. Nobody was fined, and the reason nobody was fined is on the face of a half sheet that has been on that bench for a month, and the reason is that a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on. Nobody was refused anything, because that court finds against nobody. Nobody was examined, because that court is not examining anybody. Nobody in this town did anything about the fourth of January except two people in a shed who had a thing in a drawer and left it there.

"**Say it,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**Say what.**

"**Say the reason. Out loud, in this room, once, and then it goes in the book and it is not said out loud again in a January.**"

"**A thing done in order to be ready is a thing that arrives with a date on it. That is the reason, and I gave it in the first column on the seventh day of December last with the hour on it, and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to shorten it and I am not going to say it a second way.**

"**And the other half of the reason, which is the half I would not have said in a market and I am saying in a shed because it is the fourth of January and there is nobody in this shed but you: I could produce that copy tomorrow. It is a page and a half in six heads and it is in a drawer nine feet from where we are standing, and I know a man in a city who would take it in a hand, and if I wrote to him tonight it would be up that road in four days and in his hand in nine, and it would be in a court in ten, and nothing whatever would happen to anybody. And in about four years a clerk in that court would have a piece of paper with this yard's name at the head of it and a date on it, and this yard would have done a thing in order to be ready, and a thing done in order to be ready is a thing that arrives with a date on it.**"

"**So it is not going.**"

"**It is not going. And the reason is on the page and the page is a page, and the day it was written on is the seventh of December, and the day it was said out loud in a room is the fourth of January, and both of those are in this book and a man can find either of them.**"

---

*First column, entered on Monday the fourth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on the said Monday and it came down bare, and that it was the thirty-fifth Monday since the first of May, and that the counter did not restart, and that the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure on the said Monday by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil; and that the far bench was not occupied on the Sunday instant at any hour nor on the said Monday at any hour, and that this party entered that and entered no figure against it of what came before it.*

*And that on the Sunday instant the said woman said in that shed that the month was out, and that the holder said that the month being out was the whole of the news in that room that morning, and that neither of them said anything else about it and neither of them noticed anything else; and that the holder entered it because a thing said in a shed is a thing that happened and because a woman who says a thing in a shed on a Sunday and gets no answer has a thing in a month; and that this party entered it and did not improve on it.*

*And that on Monday the fourth day of January instant a copy of a said record was to be produced to a court in a city, and that this yard did not produce it, and that this yard did not ask any office for anything in aid of producing it, did not send anything to a city, did not write to a man in a coat whose name is on no page of this book, and did not go to the counter of that circuit office; and that this yard has not been to the counter of that circuit office since the twenty-fourth day of November past, which is a figure and a date and can be checked at a counter in about nine seconds by a man with the receipt; and that the reason against the said line is the reason entered in this column on the seventh day of December last with the date and the hour on it, and that the said reason is that a thing done in order to be ready is a thing that arrives with a date on it, and that it was said out loud in that shed on the said Monday evening once and is not going to be said a second way; and that this party entered that nothing else happened in this harbor in connection with that court on the said day, and that this yard of this book does not say that nothing happened unless the nothing has been looked for.*

*And that this yard entered on the said day, in the holder's own left hand, that he could produce the said copy in a day, and that it is a page and a half in six heads in a drawer nine feet from where he was standing, and that he knows a man in a city who would take it in a hand, and that if he had written to that man on the Sunday the letter would be up that road in four days and in that man's hand in nine and in a court in ten, and that nothing whatever would happen to anybody, and that in about four years a clerk in that court would have a piece of paper with this yard's name at the head of it and a date on it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can go to the counter of that circuit office and ask what this yard has done and been told it, and can go to the post and ask on what day a letter did not come up this road.*

*And that the holder entered on the said evening that the writ of the first of May last runs to midnight on the first day of March next, and that it carries four names and no fifth, and that it is not the Second Bell license and does not lead to one and renews nothing; and that this yard is a party of one and a half until the first of March next and has not been two since the twenty-third day of October past; and that there are fifty-five days between the said day and the first of March next, and that a day at a seam in that time is about three marks, and that a wage in that time is four marks, and that neither of those figures is being put against the other one and that the yard is not going to do it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the fourth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a copy of a said record is to be produced to a court in a city on the said day and was not produced, and that a party which does not produce it is not fined because a court does not fine a poor man, and that the matter simply goes on, and that this court finds against nobody, and that a person who is not examined is not a person who has been refused, and that all four of those are on the face of a half sheet on a bench in that shed and not one of them was said out loud in this harbor on the said day by anybody; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that no court sat, that no clerk came up the hill, that nobody was fined and that nobody was charged with anything, and cannot check what is in a file in a city in about four years, and that what is going to be in that file in about four years is this yard's name with nothing after it, and that a name in a file with nothing after it is a different shape of thing from a name in a file with a page after it, and that which of the two is better is not a question this yard can answer and is not going to try to answer; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**No money came into this yard on the third of January and none on the fourth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and ten marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and that figure is four hundred and eighty more than the one this yard held on the twenty-fifth day of December past and is nine hundred and fifty-nine pence more than it held on the sixth day of that month. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and ten weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and an eleventh will fall due on Tuesday the fifth of January instant and is entered before it happens and not after. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the yard did not say that note out loud in the four weeks ended on the fourth of January and is not going to. A tin in that shed has been empty since the twenty-eighth of April, and it is empty on these two days. The three marks to a man of Ashtide, the seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, the eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, the eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor, the forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two and the forty marks first after food to a person of no office are all where they were on the twenty-ninth of December and none of them moved, and the whole of them is on the page above and is not read out again here. The three nots of the eighth of June past stand and nobody has been appointed to that column. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and did not ask this month. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and five days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
