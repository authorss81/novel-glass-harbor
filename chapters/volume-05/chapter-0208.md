# Chapter 208

*Two Marks Paid on Saturday and a Man Who Did Not Come on Monday*

Sunday the twentieth and Monday the twenty-first of December, at the boards, in the shed, and on the mole.

**No day of work was booked on the Sunday and the board was not written, and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened; and the Sunday was a day on which this yard was told a price it had not asked for and entered it before anybody asked it for anything.**

---

**And at about nine on the Sunday morning at the boards, a man of the second lay told the ship's hand a thing, and he told her and not the holder, and he said at the start that he was telling her because she writes things down and not because he wanted anything out of it.**

"**I lightered alongside your mole on the sixth of November,**" he said, "**and you will not remember me and I do not expect you to. I have a lighter and I hire a man when I want a lighter worked in a day, and I hired one on Saturday evening for the Monday, and I paid him then, two marks and his victuals, in my own hand, out of my own pocket, because that is how it is done and it has been done that way in this harbor for thirty years.

"**And he did not come at eight on Monday morning. And I am not going to say that is his fault, because I do not know that it is his fault, and I am a man of fifty-five and I have been stood up by better men than him.

"**And I am going to tell you why I think I know why, and you can do what you like with it, because it is not my business and it is yours.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**On Saturday evening at the head of that dock there were two men of the first lay saying a yard up your hill is going to hold a record for the coast, and saying it like a thing that has been settled, and my man was at that dock, because he is twenty-six and he stands at the head of that dock the way a man of twenty-six stands at the head of a dock.**" The lighterman shifted his weight. "**And he came back to my quay at about six and he said one thing to me and I am going to give it to you in his words because I do not improve on other people either.

"**He said: *If everything a yard says in this town goes into a book in a city, then a day's work of mine goes into one too, and I have got a girl at a school in a town four days up this coast and I am not having her name read out in a town I have never been to.**

"**And I said that is a great deal of fuss about a day's work. And he said it is my day's work, and that is the end of it, and that is the end of it.**"

"**What is his name.**"

"**He is not going to tell you and neither am I, and if you were to go down to that quay tonight I would be the man who told you, and I would rather not be that man in the year I am in.**"

---

**And this party entered that Saturday evening at the head of that dock as the second of two days on which the same two men of the first lay were saying a thing about this yard, the first of the two being Thursday the seventeenth, when they were saying that this yard had not said no and one of them said the word *hold* twice, and this party did not go up on the Thursday and did not go up on the Saturday, and the reason against both of the lines is the reason of the seventeenth and is that a correction is the reason and the reason does not go into a street, and neither of the two men has been asked anything by anybody in this shed, and this party has not got the name of either of them and is not going up this hill to get it.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Sunday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in that shed, she said the thing she had been holding since the morning.**

"**Write this line tonight and put it in your hand, and I want it on the page before he comes, and he is not going to come and ask, and if he does I am going to tell him no.

"**And the reason I want it written before he asks is that if it is written after he asks then it is a favor with a date on it, and I have seen a favor with a date on it go into a book in a city and come out of it in about four years as somebody being owed something by a yard that publishes itself. And this yard has one page and a half in a city in it already and I am not having a second one because a man of fifty-five lost two marks on a lighter.

"**And there is a second reason and it is the real one and I am not going to soften it. Two marks and a victual is a day a man bought on Saturday evening with his own money. If this yard offers him the two marks then this yard has bought a street, and this yard has seven marks and six shillings and four pence in a bag and a wage of four marks it is not paying, and I would not have the two marks if they were sitting on that bench in front of me, and buying a street is a worse trade than being misunderstood in one.

"**So: no. In your hand. Tonight. With the date on it and the reason against the line, and the reason is that a yard which pays a man's day for a thing it said in a street has bought the street, and the yard does not want it and cannot afford it and the first of those three is the one that is going to be on the page.**"

"**In my hand,**" said Calder Marr, "**tonight, and with the day of the twentieth on the same line, because the day of the twentieth is the same argument in a different place and it has been since the twenty-first of November.**"

---

**And on Monday the twenty-first of December the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-third Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked it, and the walk is nil and it stays nil.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the Monday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Sunday at any hour.**

**And at about eight on the Monday morning the two of them went down the hill past the west quay, and the lighter was lying there with one man on it, and the second man was not there, and neither brother said anything about it and neither of them stopped, and it is entered.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-first day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on Sunday the twentieth instant and that the board was not written, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and that the said day is a day on which this party was told a price it had not asked for and entered it before anybody asked it for anything; and that at about nine in the morning of the said Sunday, at the boards, a man of the second lay told the ship's hand a thing and told her and not the holder, and said at the start that he was telling her because she writes things down and not because he wanted anything out of it.*

*And that the said man said that he lightered alongside that mole on the sixth day of November past, that he has a lighter and hires a man when he wants a lighter worked in a day, that he hired one on Saturday evening for the Monday, and that he paid him then, two marks and his victuals, in his own hand and out of his own pocket, and that the said man did not come at eight on the Monday morning; and that the said man said he was not going to say that it was the hired man's fault because he did not know that it was, and that he is a man of fifty-five and has been stood up by better men than him; and that the said man gave his reason in his own words, which are that on the Saturday evening at the head of that dock two men of the first lay were saying that a yard up that hill is going to hold a record for the coast and were saying it like a thing that had been settled, and that the hired man was at that dock, and that he came back to the said man's quay at about six and said *if everything a yard says in this town goes into a book in a city then a day's work of mine goes into one too, and I have got a girl at a school in a town four days up this coast and I am not having her name read out in a town I have never been to*, and that the said man said that was a great deal of fuss about a day's work, and the said hired man said it is my day's work and that is the end of it; and that the said man said the hired man is not going to give his name and he is not going to give it either, and that if this party went down to that quay on the night he would be the man who told it, and that he would rather not be that man in the year he is in; and that this party entered that said Saturday evening as the second of two days on which the same two men of the first lay were saying a thing about this yard, and that on the first of the two, being Thursday the seventeenth instant, they were saying at the head of that dock that this yard had not said no and one of them said the word hold twice, in about nine people's hearing; and that this party did not go up on the first of those days and did not go up on the second, and that the reason against both of the lines is the reason of the seventeenth, and it is that a correction is the reason and the reason does not go into a street, and that neither of the two men has been asked anything by anybody in that shed, and that the second of the two days is in this column out of what a man of the second lay said at the boards on the Sunday morning and not out of this party's own eyes, and that this party has entered that as well and is not going up that hill to ask.*

*And that the said woman said in this shed on the evening of the said day, and it is entered here in the holder's own left hand at her asking, that the line is to be written on the page before anybody asks for anything and not after, and that the reason is that if it is written after he asks then it is a favor with a date on it, and that a favor with a date on it goes into a book in a city and comes out of it in about four years as somebody being owed something by a yard that publishes itself, and that this yard has one page and a half in a city in it already and is not having a second one because a man of fifty-five lost two marks on a lighter; and that she gave the second reason and did not soften it, which is that two marks and a victual is a day a man bought on the Saturday evening with his own money, and that if this yard offers him the two marks then this yard has bought a street, and that this yard holds seven marks, six shillings and four pence in a bag and is not paying a wage of four marks, and that she would not have the two marks if they were on that bench, and that buying a street is a worse trade than being misunderstood in one; and that the entry is that this party will not pay it, that the reason is that a yard which pays a man's day for a thing it said in a street has bought the street, and that the yard does not want it and cannot afford it, and that the first of those three is the one that is on the page; and that the said woman said that the said man is not going to come and ask, and that if he does she is going to tell him no.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the twenty-first instant, being the thirty-third Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Monday and this party walked it, and the walk is nil; and that the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and the holder did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Sunday at any hour; and that the two of them went down the hill past the west quay at about eight in the morning of the said Monday, and that the lighter was lying there with one man on it and the second man was not there, and that neither brother said anything about it and neither of them stopped.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the twenty-first day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of twenty-six stood at the head of this dock on the Saturday evening and heard a thing said in a street by two men who were saying it like a thing that had been settled, and gave up a day of his own work that he had been paid for in advance out of another man's pocket, and gave it up on the ground that a day's work of his might be read out in a town he has never been to, and he has a girl at a school four days up this coast; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in this harbor can go to the west quay on any day of a week and look at a lighter and count the men on it, and cannot find out what was said at the head of a dock on a Saturday evening or what a man of twenty-six said to a man of fifty-five on a quay, and that the whole of what this yard is going to be judged by in four years is not the men on a lighter and is what gets said in front of them, and that this party entered the price of it on the Sunday before it was asked for and did not ask for it and has not gone and looked for the man.*

**No money came into this yard on the Sunday and none on the Monday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and seven marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is one thousand one hundred and ninety-six pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything in these two days, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered, and she has told this yard not to ask her again this winter and this yard has not asked, and two marks and a victual of a light is not a payment of any part of it. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, eight weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, and a ninth will fall due on the twenty-second instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and ninety-one days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter was written about it in these two days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. And a day of a man's own work has been lost on a lighter on this harbor's own ground as a direct result of a sentence this yard said at the head of this dock on the Wednesday, and the price was entered on the Sunday and was not asked for, and it is not the third lay's four days and it is not a man of the first lay's Monday and it is not a morning of the tide gang.**
