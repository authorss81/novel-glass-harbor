# Chapter 229

*A Word That Is Not on Any of the Five, and a Corner That Came Off in the Hand*

Sunday the thirty-first of January and Monday the first of February, in the shed.

**And on that Monday the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirty-ninth Monday since the first of May, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held.**

**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged on that Monday at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked it, and that is the fifth of the five rota days in this month that this party has entered walked on none of, and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the next rota day after that one is the fourth of February next and it is past the end of this book and this party has not walked any of them.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not occupied on the said Sunday at any hour, and the holder did not go over to him on either of the two days, and the reason against that line is the reason that has been against it every day since the sixth of October and it is not going to be given out of this shed tonight.**

---

**And on the Sunday morning the two of them laid out the five pieces of paper.**

A copy of a record of the twenty-fourth of November in six heads and about a page and a half, dated the date of a counter and not the date of a record. The acknowledgment of half a sheet of the twenty-ninth of November, with an office's name at the foot of it and no signature. A half sheet of a court in a city, dated the fourth of December, with a date on it that came and went. A printer's letter of the twentieth of December with one question in it that has not been answered. And the sheet and a half with no signature and no date on it and the name of an office at the foot of it, which came up that hill on foot on the ninth of December and has been on that bench ever since, and a corner of it about the width of two fingers has been gone soft since the fourteenth of January.

They did it on the boards at about ten with the stone off the slate, because the boards are flat and there is nowhere else in that shed that is.

"**We are looking for a word,**" said Nessa Pike. "**That is all this is. Six of us, a name, and a word at the head of it, and we are going to look at every piece of paper this yard has got and find out whether the word is on any of them.**"

"**And if it is not.**

"**Then we will have looked.**"

It took about an hour. It took longer than an hour, because the acknowledgment is half a sheet and there is nothing on it but a sentence, and they read the sentence four times, and Calder Marr read the copy of the record out of the middle of it twice in case he had missed a name in one of the six heads.

It is not on any of the five. It is not on the acknowledgment, and it is not on the form of the court, and it is not on the printer's letter, and it is not in the six heads of the copy of the record, and it is not on the sheet and a half.

And then the ship's hand took the sheet and a half up off the boards to put it back under the pump, and it came apart in her hand, along the fold that had gone soft, from the corner inward, about three inches.

It went into the palm of her left hand and it came away from itself, and the part that came away is the part that had been soft, and the part that came away is about the width of two fingers and three inches long and it is not wet and it is not torn and it is simply not a sheet any more.

She put both hands flat on the boards and looked at them for about nine seconds.

"**There. That is done and it cannot be put back and I am not going to pretend it was going to happen to somebody else next month.**"

"**Say the whole of it.**"

"**A man came up that hill on foot on the ninth of December and left that on that bench under a pump and I have had it there for fifty-three days and I told you on the fourteenth of January that I was not going to lift it off because there was nowhere in this harbor for me to lift it on to, and that was the reason and I gave you the reason and I gave it to you in a good conscience.

"**And the reason was true and it was not the reason. The reason was that lifting it meant deciding what to do with it. And as long as it was on that bench under a pump it was a thing that had not been dealt with, and this morning we decided to deal with it, and it came off in my hand, and that is the whole of what happened and there is no more to it than that.**"

Nobody in that shed said anything for about half a minute.

"**What was it worth.**

"**It was a sheet and a half. It was worth a penny. There is no figure of a penny against it in this book and there is nowhere on this coast a man can buy a sheet of paper with an office's name at the foot of it that nobody sent here.

"**And it was worth four weeks of somebody looking at it and deciding not to, and I do not know what that is worth and I am not going to put a figure on it, and that is the first thing in this book I have not put a figure on and I have not slept a night since the Saturday and I am not going to.**"

---

**And the cost of the nine days is that this yard spent six days of its own time on four pages that are not its own, and one day of a joiner's trade that is in another man's book, and one sheet and a half, and the question that a woman of seventy-one is now going to have at three doors on the north side of this town, and a man of sixty-four who has been made the reason there were four pages on that bench and does not know it, and one sheet that went down this coast on the Saturday in the inside of a coat with a name at the head of it.**

**And the shape of the cost is not a person who has read a page and cannot unread it. It is a page that has been in a shed in this town for fourteen days and is going to have to go home, and it has gone home, down that coast in the inside of a man's coat to a place two days down it, and four people are going to be asked about it this week, and not one of them by this yard. And not one of the four of them is going to be told what two people said out loud on a Monday afternoon about a light on a point, and that is the cost, and it is not anybody's fault, and it is not going to be put back.**

**And this party is not going to write to those four people, and it is not going to send anybody, and it is not going to put a word into the ear of a man who is going to ask them, and the reason is the reason of the fourth of October past and it is not a new one, and the reason against the line is that this is the same ground as the four nameless lines in the second column and it has a cost already, and this yard does not pay a cost twice to feel the same thing twice.**

---

**And at about half past eight on the Monday night, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back off that bench in the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder, and nobody else in that shed, and the bench under the pump bare for the second night running.**

Nessa Pike read the six titles of the five pieces of paper off the sheet she had bought a penny for on the Thursday, and then she did not read anything else, and the holder read the six titles back after her, and then he sat with his hands on his knees for about two minutes before he said the thing he had come in to say.

"**We have been asking people to write things down since the third of May.

"**A man came up a hill and I could not say what I was doing on the Saturday morning, so I asked a man to come on the Monday. That is a man being asked to keep a day.

"**A day at a seam, and the day's cost entered against that day before the day was spent, and a man of the second lay told me on the boards that about three marks came in and four marks did not go out, and I wrote both of them in the same column on the same day. That is a man being asked to keep a column.

"**A boat one hand short since the middle of July and four marks, one shilling and eightpence out of one part owner's own share, and the man told the ship's hand and not me because she writes things down. That is a man being asked to keep a book.

"**And a party which has spent four volumes insisting that a record a man can check is the only kind worth having.**

"**And a page came up that hill out of a crate of chalk on Saturday and it has got four lines on it and a name at the head of it and the four lines say that a page about a place is the place's, and that it is not to be carried out of the place it is about, and that a copy is not a page, and that no one of the nine is to be asked what another of the nine keeps.**

"**That is a record a man can check. I have never seen a better one and I have not got one and I am not going to get one. And it is not a new thing. It is not a thing anybody in this country thought of. It is somebody's ordinary practice, and the ordinary of it is that nobody had to be asked, and a man of the first lay of this town said in a market in the month of December that it is six centuries old, and this party has carried that figure for a month and has never had a line of it on this yard's own page.

"**The practice had a name. The name is at the head of the fourth of the four and it is on one sheet in this country that this yard has ever been shown, and this yard laid that sheet out on the boards this morning with four others and read every word of every one of them, and the name is not on any of the five.

"**And I am not going to say what that means, and I am not going to say it in a market, and I am not going to say it to Jonas Peake in a town of two thousand up this coast, and I am not going to write it down in a book that is going to be read in a shed in four years. I have said it out loud twice in this shed to one other person and that is the whole of where it is, and that is the most I have got.**

"**And I am not going to say that the name being on one sheet and not on five is a fact about the world. It is a fact about this yard's drawer.**"

"**It is a fact about the drawer and the drawer is the only thing I have got.**

"**Then say it that way, in the second column, and say that we have looked in one drawer in one town and that the name is not in it, and that what a drawer in one town proves about any other drawer on this coast is nothing at all, and that this is the first time in four volumes I have entered a line saying that what I have in my hand is not evidence.**

"**I will enter that. And I want it entered in the same breath with the other thing: this country has had an office keeping records for three hundred years. Six centuries is the age of a practice and three hundred years is the age of an office, and they are two different figures and neither of them is going into the other's sentence, and the yard has got both of them now and is not going to put them together to make a bigger number.**"
