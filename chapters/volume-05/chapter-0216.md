# Chapter 216

*A Page Off a Press in a City, and a Man's Own Figure Refused*

Tuesday the fifth and Wednesday the sixth of January, in the shed, and at the head of this dock.

**On Tuesday the fifth of January the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now ELEVEN, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past and the fifth of January instant, and all eleven are entered in this book in the same breath and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all eleven at once rather than the one that is easier to say.**

The reason against that line is the reason entered in the first column on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of December past in the holder's own left hand, and it is not improved on and it is not shortened and it is not going to be said again in a January. The column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move. The count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move.

**And the bag is ten marks, six shillings and four pence, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of that, and the figure the bag is short of the wage is one thousand and thirty-six pence, and that is the same figure it was on the twenty-ninth of December past and that is because nothing has come in and nothing has gone out since the day at the second seam.**

---

**And on the Wednesday morning a page came up this hill that did not come up this hill, because it was already in this harbor and had been since the Saturday before last, and nobody sent it and nobody brought it and nobody thought about it.**

It was lying on the boards at about half past eight in the morning with a stone on it, and the stone was on it to keep a slate flat, and it was a page off a printed sheet, and it was folded twice and it had been folded twice a long time, and there was a thumb's width of dirt in the second fold.

There were about nine people at the boards. The man whose stone it was was not there and had not been there since the Saturday before last and was in a boat.

It is a printed page about this coast, out of a press in a city, and it is not dated on the page this yard has, and it is set in type the way a thing is set in type when a man wants it to be found and not when he wants it to be read. On that page, in a column of five figures about what a boat of a given tonnage costs to be moved, there are two figures about a bar of gravel off this harbor's mouth, side by side and in the same weight of type, and the first of them is **two and three-quarters** and the second of them is **two and a half**, and the second one is a pilot's figure and the first one is not a pilot's figure and nobody in this yard wrote either of them and nobody in this yard has ever seen this page before the Wednesday morning.

**Neither is withdrawn. Both are in type. The yard's two and three-quarters has been in a book in this shed since the month of August and the pilot's two and a half has been in a book in this shed for longer than that, and this yard does not decide which of them is right and is not going to decide it in a book and is not going to decide it in a street either. The Kettle was not looked at and is not going to be looked at by this yard.**

---

**And a man of fifty-eight came up this hill at about ten on the Wednesday morning, and he is of the first lay and he has a boat of about two hundred and thirty tons of his own, and he has taken that printed sheet out of a city every three weeks for nineteen years and has never once written to anybody about it, and he had it on the boards on the Saturday before last with a stone on it.**

"**I did not know it was yours,**" he said. "**I have had it nineteen years and I did not know that one of those two figures came from a yard on a hill until somebody at those boards this morning said so, and I would not have come up here at all if it had not been for that. I am not asking you for anything. I want you to know that I put a stone on it and did not read it and did not keep it and did not send it anywhere, and I want that in your book before anything else, because I know what a page does.**

"**It is in the book. Go on.**

"**I have been over that bar of gravel forty-one times in thirty years and I have a figure of my own and it is a mark and a half, and I have had it in my own book since I was thirty-four, and I am bringing it up here because there are two figures about that bar in a printed sheet in a city and mine is not one of them and there is a man on this hill who would keep a book.**"

The man of the first lay put a folded sheet on the boards. There was a figure on it in his own hand and there was a year-and-a-month beside the figure and there was the name of a man who had been in his boat in one of the two years.

Nessa Pike did not touch it.

"**I am going to refuse it,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to do it here and not in the shed, because you came up a hill with it in your hand and you are owed a refusal with a room in it.**

"**Refuse it, then.**

"**Item eleven of a list this yard wrote in its own hand on the twenty-second of November last is the two figures for the bar of gravel and the Kettle, which was not looked at. And that list is a list of what does not go into a room. And on Thursday last I said out loud in our own book that this yard cannot put those two figures in front of anybody, and I am not going to un-say that in a street, because a thing said out loud in a book has two people in it and a thing said out loud in a street has about eleven, and I would rather the two were right than the eleven heard it.**

"**And here is the half of it that is about you, and I want you to hear it properly. You have offered me a figure of your own for that bar and I am not going to write it in that book. I am not going to carry it in my head either. I am not asking you for it. I am refusing one.**

"**And the reason is that a book that has this yard's figures in it is a book that goes somewhere. The day that book has your figure in it beside ours is the day it is a book of opinions about a bar, and a page of that is already on that list and it has been on the list since the twenty-second of November and it is not coming off, and if it came off that list it would come off in a book, in our own hand, on a Monday, and that is the only instrument this yard has and it is not going to be used to take a line off.**"

"**That is a straight answer,**" said the man of the first lay, "**and I have had a great many of them and most of them were not straight.**

"**I have been over that bar forty-one times,**" he said, "**and I have had a figure in my own book since I was thirty-four, and there is not one man on this coast who has ever wanted it. Not one. I have told it to two people in thirty years and both of them wrote it down and neither of them used it and one of them is dead.**

"**Then you have still got it.**

"**I have still got it. And you are right not to take it, and I want it said that I think you are right, and I am going down that hill now and I am not going to come up it again about a figure.**"

He took his own sheet off the boards and put it inside his coat and went down. He did not say anything else and nobody stopped him.

*First column, entered on Wednesday the sixth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on Tuesday the fifth instant the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and did not move, and that the count of envelopes stands at twenty and did not move, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is eleven, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth of December past, the fifteenth of December past, the twenty-second of December past, the twenty-ninth of December past and the fifth of January instant, and that all eleven are entered in this book in the same breath; and that the reason against the said line is the reason entered in this column on the twenty-ninth day of December past in the holder's own left hand and is not improved on and is not shortened and is not going to be said again in a January.*

*And that on the Wednesday morning at about half past eight there was a page lying on the boards in this harbor with a stone on it, and that the stone was on it to keep a slate flat, and that it is a page off a printed sheet out of a press in a city, folded twice, with a thumb's width of dirt in the second fold; and that it had been on those boards since the Saturday before last and that the man whose stone it was was not there and had not been there since that Saturday and was in a boat; and that about nine people were at those boards when it was seen; and that this party took it up and carried it up this hill in a coat.*

*And that on the said page, in a column of five figures about what a boat of a given tonnage costs to be moved, there are two figures about a bar of gravel off the mouth of this harbor set side by side in the same weight of type, and that the first of them is two and three-quarters and the second is two and a half, and that the second is a pilot's figure and the first is not a pilot's figure, and that nobody in this yard wrote either of them and that nobody in this yard had ever seen that page before the said morning; and that neither is withdrawn and both are in type, and that this yard does not decide which of them is right and is not going to decide it, and that the Kettle was not looked at and is not going to be looked at by this yard; and that the refused licence stands unreversed, and that the thirty marks were not paid, are not owed to anybody, and cannot be applied to anything.*

*And that a man of fifty-eight of the first lay, with a boat of about two hundred and thirty tons of his own, came up this hill at about ten on the said morning, and that he has taken that printed sheet out of a city every three weeks for nineteen years and has never once written to anybody about it, and that he had it on those boards on the Saturday before last with a stone on it; and that he said at the start that he did not know the figure was this yard's, and that he would not have come up at all if he had not heard it said at the boards, and that he is not asking this yard for anything; and that he said that he put a stone on it and did not read it and did not keep it and did not send it anywhere, and that the said holder entered that at the front of the said line and the man of fifty-eight asked for that.*

*And that the said man said that he has been over that bar forty-one times in thirty years and has a figure of his own, being a mark and a half, in his own book since he was thirty-four, and that he brought it up this hill because there are two figures about that bar in a printed sheet in a city and his is not one of them and there is a man on that hill who keeps a book; and that he put a folded sheet on those boards with a figure on it in his own hand and a year and a month beside it and the name of a man who had been in his boat in one of those years; and that the said woman did not touch it.*

*And that the holder refused it in the street and not in the shed, and gave the reason against the line, which is that item eleven of the list of the twenty-second day of November past is the two figures for the bar of gravel and the Kettle, which was not looked at, and that the said list is a list of what does not go into a room, and that on the thirty-first day of December past the holder said out loud in that book that this yard cannot put those two figures in front of anybody, and that he is not going to un-say that in a street, and that a thing said out loud in a book has two people in it and a thing said out loud in a street has about eleven, and that he would rather the two were right than the eleven heard it.*

*And that the holder said the rest of it in the street, which is that a book that has this yard's figures in it is a book that goes somewhere, and that the day that book has the figure of the man of fifty-eight in it beside this yard's is the day it is a book of opinions about a bar, and that a page of that is already on that list and has been on the list since the twenty-second of November past and is not coming off, and that if it came off that list it would come off in a book, in this yard's own hand, on a Monday, and that that is the only instrument this yard has and it is not going to be used to take a line off it; and that the holder said that he was not asking the said man for a figure and that he was refusing one; and that the said man said that that was a straight answer and that he had had a great many and most of them were not straight.*

*And that the said man then said that he has been over that bar forty-one times and has had a figure in his own book since he was thirty-four, and that there is not one man on this coast who has ever wanted it, and that he has told it to two people in thirty years and both of them wrote it down and neither of them used it and one of them is dead; and that the holder said that he had still got it; and that the said man said that the holder is right not to take it, said that he thinks the holder is right, and said that he was going down that hill and was not going to come up it again about a figure; and that he took his own sheet off those boards and put it inside his coat and went down, and that nobody stopped him and nothing else was said in the street about it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the sixth day of January instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this yard's own method was said out loud in a street in this harbor on the said day for the first time in four volumes, in about nine people's hearing, and that what was said out loud was not a figure and was not a fact about a bar of gravel but the method itself, which is that a list of what does not go into a room is the instrument this yard has and that no line of it comes off; and that a method said out loud in a street is a method that anybody in about nine people can use, and that a method nobody can say is a method that two people can check; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask the man of fifty-eight what was said to him at the head of that dock on the sixth of January and be told, and that what cannot be checked is what any of the nine people who were standing there will do with it; and that the four people of this coast are the only people in this harbor who could say whether that trade is a good one, and that they have not been asked and are not going to be; and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**No money came into this yard on the fifth of January and none on the sixth, and nothing went out of it on either day, and ten marks, six shillings and four pence is in the bag, which is one thousand six hundred and seventy-six pence. A man's own figure for the bar of gravel was offered at the head of this dock on the sixth of January instant and was refused, and the figure is not in this book and was not asked for and will not be asked for, and the two figures that are in type in a city are in type in a city and neither of them is withdrawn. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest, and eleven weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. Thirty-one on the chandler's note and not re-written, with the three counts of weeks still on it at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Since the twenty-eighth of April past there has been nothing in a tin in that shed, and there is none this month either. The three marks to a man of Ashtide, the seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, the eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, the eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor, the forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two and the forty marks first after food to a person of no office are all where they were on Tuesday and none of them moved, and the whole of them is on the page above and is not read out again here. The three nots of the eighth of June past stand and the third column has the boy's hand at the head of it and nobody's after it. The case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks and is on nobody's schedule, and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted. Those two objects of nobody's are about two hundred and seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved.**
