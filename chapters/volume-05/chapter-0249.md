# Chapter 249

*Five Drawers, Five Hands, and a Thing on the Water That Has to Be Looked At*

Friday the twelfth and Saturday the thirteenth of March, in the shed, on the face of the second seam, and in a boat of about forty tons.

**Neither of the two days of this chapter is a rota day and the cistern behind the engine house was charged on neither of them, and that is entered with a reason, and the reason is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and the twelfth and thirteenth days of March are a Friday and a Saturday.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten until about one and was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on either of the said two days.**

**And this yard worked on the said Friday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence. And no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the reason against that line is that a Saturday is the day the two of them have had since the twentieth of February past and that the holder of that writ is not in that harbor.**

**And the case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, owned by four people alive in that town with no boat, no wages and about nine marks between them, and the holder did not ask to have it lifted once in these twenty days and is not going to, and the limit those four wrote themselves and signed is in that yard's own first column and stands: that they will not say what a bell-archive is to a man with a seal; that a page about a place is the place's and is not to be carried out of the place it is about; that they will not give their names to any office in any city in any year; and that is the whole of the limit and they wrote it themselves and are not going to be asked to take it off.**

---

**And four lines came up to that shed in four days, one of them on the Friday and three of them before it, and every one of them is a different thing done to a page in a drawer, and not one of the four is a thing this yard asked for, and not one of the four is a thing this yard could have stopped if it had known.**

*From a place two days down that coast, in a hand that is not the yard's: the page is in a drawer and was read out in a kitchen on a Sunday to about nine people and put back in the drawer, and is not going to be read again.*

*From a place four days up that coast, in a hand that is not the yard's: the page is in a drawer and has been read by the person who keeps it and by nobody else, and has been put back in the drawer, and I have told nobody what is on it.*

*From a chain of reed islands and floating markets, in a hand that is not the yard's: the page is in a drawer and has not been opened since it came, and I am writing that down so that whoever reads this knows it was not opened and not that it was not looked at, because I have not looked at it.*

*From the next town down that coast, in a hand that is not the yard's: the page is in a drawer and I have copied it out in my own hand once and hung the copy in a room people come into, and the page is in the drawer.*

"**Four answers and this yard asked none of them for an answer and sent nobody to any of those four places and has not been to any of them and cannot go to any of them, and that is the whole of the difference between the four answers and the four of them are all right, and none of them is the answer this yard would have given and none of them is wrong.**"

"**Then say what you are going to do with them.**

"**Nothing. That is what I am going to do with them, and I am going to write them in the first column because four men can be asked whether they said what they said, and I am going to write in the second column that we cannot go and look in any of those four drawers and that in about four years we will not know whether three of the four pages are in three drawers or on three fires, and that is the price and it is not going to be improved on. And the second thing I am going to do with them is that one of those four towns has a room that people come into, and that is the only one of the four that is doing the thing we have spent five volumes saying a page is for, and I am not going to write to that town and tell them so, because a page that a town puts on a wall because a yard told it to is a page the yard is holding in a drawer in that town.**"

---

**And at about a quarter past two on the said Friday afternoon a boat of about forty tons went out from the head of that dock with the man of the second lay in her and a mark on a line over her side, and a man short of a crew went out in her, and about eleven people on that mole saw her go and one of them asked whose it was.**

The mark is a thing a yard makes when it wants to know where a thing is without going near it. It is a bell, a frame, a line and a shackle, and it goes out on a boat and is set and is let go, and it is on the water because that is what it is for, and a thing on the water is a thing on a wall that moves.

"**Two marks for a fortnight and one mark and eightpence for the boat with a man in her for the day, and a penny of line,**" said the man of the second lay, when she asked him what it was, "**and that is my figure at my trade and I am not going to put a mark on a thing for you. The season is nine marks. Nine marks is a season of it. I have not got nine marks either and I would not take it off you if you had.**

"**A thing on the water has to be looked at,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Somebody has to go out and see whether it is there. That is the whole of the trade and it is the first thing about it and it is not the trade. And I cannot tell you how many days a year that is, and you cannot tell me, and nobody on this coast can tell anybody, and there is a woman in a room off the fish market who told us in January that a thing on the water is a bill and a building is made once and that is the whole of the difference, and I have not improved on that.

"**And both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other. The mark is worth something to a man in a boat and it is worth something else to a man on a mole, and the mark is a thing anybody standing on that mole can see and can count against the yard that put it there, and that is a cost and I am paying it now, and I am not going to pretend it is a thing I have got free.

"**And the third thing is the one that is going to be on the last page of this book. This is a trade. It takes a crew. I am a party of one and a half, and the half has been in a chair with four stitches in her hand since the twentieth of February, and the other half is up a coast road in a cart, and I have paid two men out of a bag of sixty-four marks to do a day's work that two of us would do between us on a day when there are two of us. And the season is nine marks and I am not going to pay nine marks and I am not going to ask anybody for nine marks and there is nobody in this country to ask.**"

She said the shape of it once and neither of them said anything about how many times anybody had said it.

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirteenth day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that neither of the said two days is a rota day and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged on neither of them, and that the reason against that line is that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday.*

*And that the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten until about one and was not occupied at any hour on the said Saturday, and that the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on the said Friday and did not go over to him on the said Saturday.*

*And that on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that no day of work was booked on the said Saturday and the reason against that line is that a Saturday is the day the two of them have had since the twentieth of February past and that the holder of that writ is not in that harbor.*

*And that the case of five bells is at about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards off the outer marks, on nobody's schedule, owned by four people alive in that town with no boat, no wages and about nine marks between them, and that the holder did not ask to have it lifted once in these twenty days and is not going to; and that the limit those four wrote themselves and signed is in that first column and stands, being that they will not say what a bell-archive is to a man with a seal, that a page about a place is the place's and is not to be carried out of the place it is about, that they will not give their names to any office in any city in any year, and that that is the whole of the limit and they wrote it themselves and are not going to be asked to take it off.*

*And that four lines came up to that shed in the said four days, being on the said Friday one of them and before it three of them, and that every one of them is a different thing done to a page in a drawer, and that not one of the four is a thing that party asked for, and that not one of the four is a thing that party could have stopped if it had known; and that the said four are: a page in a drawer in a place two days down that coast read out in a kitchen on a Sunday to about nine people and put back in the drawer and not going to be read again; a page in a drawer in a place four days up that coast read by the person who keeps it and by nobody else and put back in the drawer, and the keeper of it has told nobody what is on it; a page in a drawer in a chain of reed islands and floating markets not opened since it came, the keeper of it writing that down so that whoever reads it knows it was not opened and not that it was not looked at; and a page in a drawer in the next town down that coast copied out once in the keeper's own hand and hung in a room people come into, with the page itself in the drawer.*

*And that that party asked none of those four for an answer and sent nobody to any of those four places and has not been to any of them and cannot go to any of them, and that all four of the said answers are right, and that none of them is the answer that party would have given, and that none of them is wrong.*

*And that at about a quarter past two on the said Friday afternoon a boat of about forty tons went out from the head of that dock with the man of the second lay in her and a mark on a line over her side, and a man short of a crew went out in her, and about eleven people on that mole saw her go and one of them asked whose it was, and that that is the whole of the public part of it and there is not going to be any more of it.*

*And that the said mark is a bell, a frame, a line and a shackle, and it went out on a boat and was set and let go, and is on the water because that is what it is for, and a thing on the water is a thing on a wall that moves.*

*And that the said man of the second lay gave his own figure at his own trade, being two marks for a fortnight for the mark, one mark and eightpence for the boat with a man in her for the day, and a penny of line, being three marks and nine pence in all, being four hundred and eighty-nine pence, and that the season of the said mark is nine marks, and that the said man has not got nine marks either, and that that party has not got nine marks and is not going to pay nine marks and is not going to ask anybody for nine marks and that there is nobody in that country to ask.*

*And that a thing on the water has to be looked at and that somebody has to go out and see whether it is there, and that the said woman said that she cannot tell how many days a year that is and that the said man cannot tell her and that nobody on that coast can tell anybody, and that a woman in a room off that fish market said in the month of January that a thing on the water is a bill and a building is made once, and that that party has not improved on that.*

*And that the said woman said, and said it once, that the said mark is worth something to a man in a boat and is worth something else to a man on a mole, and that the said mark is a thing anybody standing on that mole can see and can count against the yard that put it there, and that that is a cost and she is paying it and is not going to pretend it is a thing she has got free.*

*And that this is a trade and it takes a crew, and that the said party is a party of one and a half of which the half has been in a chair with four stitches in that hand since the twentieth of February past and the other half is up that coast road in a cart; and that two men were paid out of that bag for a day's work that two of that party would do between them on a day when there are two of them; and that the season of the said mark is nine marks and that yard is not going to pay nine marks and is not going to ask anybody for nine marks.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the thirteenth day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that five towns are keeping five pages and no two of them are the same page, and that this yard cannot go and look in four of the five drawers and has not tried to, and that in about four years it will not know whether three of the four pages are in three drawers or on three fires, and that that is the price of the whole thing and it is not going to be improved on.*

*And that the one of those four towns that has hung a copy in a room people come into is the only one of the four doing the thing this yard has spent five volumes saying a page is for, and that that yard is not going to write to that town and tell them so, because a page that a town puts on a wall because a yard told it to is a page the yard is holding in a drawer in that town; and that that is entered as a decision and not as a fault.*

*And that the three costs of the thing on the water are all paid on the page in this chapter and none of them is waived, being that a thing on the water has to be looked at and that there is no figure for it in that country, being that a thing on the water is a thing on a wall that moves and about eleven people saw this one go out on the said Friday, and being that it takes a crew and a crew is people and that the crew in that harbor is a party of one and a half and the season of the thing is nine marks and the nine marks are not going to be paid.*

*And that the book of hours of the thirtieth and thirty-first of October past stands at sixteen lines and has not been extended by anything on the said Friday and is not going to be, and that the sixteen lines are nine pumps, four boats out and three boats in, with the hour against each and no reason against any of them, and that they are the first thing about that trade and are not the trade.*

*And that the four lines above the said named line are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that a line does not come out of a column because it has been printed and cannot be got back.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing on the said Saturday, and three marks and nine pence went out of it on the said Friday, being two marks for the hire of a mark for a fortnight, one mark and eightpence for a boat with a man in her for the day, and a penny of line, being four hundred and eighty-nine pence, and nothing went out of it on the said Saturday, and sixty-seven marks, three shillings and one penny is in the bag, which is ten thousand seven hundred and fifty-seven pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is ten thousand one hundred and seventeen pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Saturday is seven days at the face of the second seam, being three thousand three hundred and sixty pence, and the whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Saturday is three marks, one shilling and one penny, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is three marks, three shillings and four pence in six transactions at four counters, being a penny on the eighteenth of December past, a penny on the twenty-eighth of January instant, a penny on the thirteenth of February instant, two shillings to a bonesetter on the twentieth of February, fourpence at the post on the sixth of March, and three marks and nine pence to the man of the second lay on the twelfth of March. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and twenty weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, the second and the ninth of March instant, and all twenty are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. The rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
