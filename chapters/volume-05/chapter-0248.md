# Chapter 248

*The Same Question, the Second Time, and Still Not Answered*

Wednesday the tenth and Thursday the eleventh of March, in the shed, and on the boards in a market in that town.

**At about ten on the said Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifth of the six rota days that this party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and the walk is nil and it stays nil, and that is the twenty-eighth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour and was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and this party entered both of them and set nothing forward to either line, and the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on either of the said two days.**

**And this yard worked on the said Wednesday from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and the day's book was written in the ship's hand in her own left hand at about half past one and not afterwards.**

**And no day of work was booked on the said Thursday and the reason against that line is that the said Thursday is a rota day and that four men this yard does not know charged the cistern at about ten on it, and that a party of one does not work a day it has spent at a market.**

**And no sheet went up on any wall in that town in this chapter and nothing came off any wall in that town. The hearing room over the tide-gang store was not used on either of the said two days and no sitting was held in it. The market wall of that town carries that yard's own fourth return under a sheet and it is still the only paper on it that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and the two naval notices are not on that wall and were not taken down by that party, and the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it.**

---

**And at about a quarter past eleven on the said Thursday morning, on the boards in a market in that town, with the market up and about forty people standing about where they stand on any morning of that week, a man of about sixty of the first lay of this town, who has been on that water forty-one years, said a question out loud, and he had said the same question in the same market on the second of December past and had been told *this yard does not know*, with a reason against the line, and he had been told it by the holder of that writ, and the holder of that writ is up a coast road.**

"**I asked it in December and he said he did not know, and he gave me a reason for not knowing, which is more than most of you do, and I want it asked again with him up the coast and I am not going to wait for him.**

"**What was that office guarding against, at the point where it decided what it was allowed to lose. Not what it was for. What it was stopping. There was a practice on this coast before there was an office that kept records, and the practice was six hundred years old by anybody's count who has looked, and the office came along inside one lifetime and put a hand on the only copy and called it keeping. So: what did it think it was stopping.**"

Nobody at those boards said anything, and about forty people stood in it.

"**I am not going to let a yard of two answer that with a feeling,**" he said. "**I want a fact or I want nothing and I will take nothing the same way I took nothing in December.**"

Nessa Pike came down off the mole at about half past twelve, in her left hand, and stood where she could be seen and not on the boards, and she had a sheet of paper with four things written on it and she did not read it out, and it went in the second column and not the first, and the reason it went in the second column is that the reason a yard does not answer is not checkable from a mole and is not going to be improved on.

"**I have three things and I am going to say all three in one breath and I am going to tell you now that none of them is an answer, and I am not going to stand here and call a fact an answer, and in December a man in this market asked me the same question and I did not say it and the man who said it to him is up a coast road and would not have said it either, and I am not improving on him.

"**The first. Nine communities on this coast kept a record of their own place, one in each, and there always were nine, and that is in our book as a second-column fact with no name in it since the sixth of October past and it is not new and it is not ours. The second. That practice is six centuries old, and a man of the first lay said so in a market in that town in December past and nobody has asked him for a figure and he has not given one. The third. The office that answers that question has kept records for three hundred years. That is a different number and it is not the same number and neither of them is going into the other's sentence, and I am saying it in that order because if I say them in the wrong order a man in about forty people will think three hundred years is how long the communities have been at it, and it is not, and a figure that is right and in the wrong place is a lie.

"**Those are three figures and they are all true and none of them says what that office was guarding against, and I am not going to improve them and I am not going to hand them to a man as though that made them an answer, because in about four years somebody will write down that a yard in Morrow Quay was asked what an office was stopping and said three numbers, and then the three numbers will be the answer, and they are not.

"**And the reason this yard is not going to say more than that is not a figure and I am not going to give it. The question was put by a man of no office. There is no office in this country that could be put the same question, and if there were, an office that answered it would be an office's answer and not an answer, and that is the whole of the reason and I am not going to give it and I am not going to give it to you either.**"

"**That is nothing,**" said the man of about sixty. "**That is three numbers and a refusal.**

"**It is a fact and a reason and I told you at the top that it is not an answer. I would rather say that in about forty people than say a thing I have not got.

"**And now the two other things, because you will say them and because they are true, and then I am going back up that hill.**"

"**Say them.**

"**The first. A Crown form came up that mole on Thursday last with a name on the outside of it and it said on its own face that nobody in this harbor is to be asked to produce any book of its own in any column and that nothing in it requires anybody to say who altered anything or at whose instance or with what intent, and we did not say it and nobody asked us to and nobody is going to ask us to. So no, they could not make this yard say it, and I am not going to stand here and take a victory for that, because a thing they did not ask for is not a thing we won.

"**And the second. There are four communities on this coast that have read a page of ours and each of them has read it as a different thing and all four of them are right, and we are not going to make them agree, and the cost of not making them agree is already sitting on a man of about thirty two days down this coast who has a daughter of nineteen, and we owe him something and we are not going to pay it and we are not going to write to him and we are not going to send anybody. And I am not going to stand here and take a victory for that either, because we cannot make any one of those four communities trust us and we have not tried to and we are not going to.

"**Both of those are true. Neither of them is a victory. A man who tells forty people on a Thursday morning that he cannot make anybody trust him is a man who has spent five volumes finding out what a page does to a place, and it is not a good morning's work, and I would rather be in a boat.**"

Nobody said anything after that, and about forty people stood in it, and the boards came down at about two, and this yard is not on the boards and has never been, and no office in any city answered the question and no office was asked.

*First column, entered on Thursday the eleventh day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and that party walked none of it, and that the walk is nil and stays nil, and that that is the fifth of the six rota days that party has entered walked on none of since the twenty-fourth day of February past, and that that is the twenty-eighth of the charges that party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Wednesday and was not occupied at any hour on the said Thursday, and that the holder of that writ is up that coast road and did not go over to him on the said Wednesday and did not go over to him on the said Thursday.*

*And that on the said Wednesday this yard worked from eight until about half past one on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence; and that the day's book was written in the said woman's own left hand at about half past one and not afterwards; and that no day of work was booked on the said Thursday and the reason against that line is that the said Thursday is a rota day and that a party of one does not work a day it has spent at a market.*

*And that no sheet went up on any wall in that town in the said two days and nothing came off any wall in that town, and that the hearing room over the tide-gang store was not used on either of them and no sitting was held in it; and that the market wall of that town carries that yard's own fourth return under a sheet and is still the only paper on it that any office in any city is obliged to look at; and that the two naval notices are not on that wall and were not taken down by that party, and that the clerk kept his own page and has not mentioned it.*

*And that at about a quarter past eleven on the said Thursday morning, on the boards in a market in that town, with about forty people standing, a man of about sixty of the first lay, who has been on that water forty-one years, said out loud what an office that kept records for three hundred years thought it was guarding against at the point at which it decided what it was allowed to lose; and that the said man said that he had asked the same question in the same market on the second of December past and had been told that that yard does not know, with a reason against the line, by the holder of that writ, and that the holder of that writ is up that coast road and that the said man is not going to wait for him.*

*And that the said woman came down off that mole at about half past twelve on the said Thursday, stood where she could be seen and not on the boards, and gave three facts and said that none of them is an answer, being that nine communities on that coast kept a record of their own place, one in each, and there always were nine, which is in that book as a second-column fact with no name in it since the sixth of October past; that the said practice is six centuries old, which a man of the first lay said in a market in that town in the month of December past and which has not been asked for a figure by anybody; and that the office that answers that question has kept records for three hundred years, which is a different number and not the same number and is not going into the other's sentence; and that the said three figures were given in that order on purpose, because a figure that is right and in the wrong place is a lie, and that none of them says what that office was guarding against, and that the said woman said so before she said them and not after.*

*And that the said man said that that is three numbers and a refusal, and that the said woman said that it is a fact and a reason and that she said at the top that it is not an answer, and that she would rather say that in about forty people than say a thing she has not got.*

*And that the said woman said, in the same market, that a Crown form came up that mole on the said Thursday last with a name on the outside of it, and that it said on its own face that nobody in that harbor is to be asked to produce any book of its own in any column, and that nothing in it requires anybody to say who altered anything or at whose instance or with what intent, and that that yard did not say it and nobody asked it to and nobody is going to ask it to; and that the said woman said that a thing they did not ask for is not a thing that yard won, and that she was not going to stand in that market and take a victory for it.*

*And that the said woman said that four communities on that coast have read a page of that yard's and each of them has read it as a different thing and all four of them are right, and that that yard is not going to make them agree, and that the cost of not making them agree is already sitting on a man of about thirty two days down that coast who has a daughter of nineteen, and that that yard owes him something and is not going to pay it and is not going to write to him and is not going to send anybody; and that the said woman said that that yard cannot make any one of those four communities trust it and has not tried to and is not going to, and that she was not going to stand in that market and take a victory for that either.*

*And that nobody said anything after that and about forty people stood in it, and that the boards came down at about two in the afternoon, and that that yard is not on the boards and never has been, and that no office in any city answered the said question and no office was asked.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the eleventh day of March instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the question what an office was guarding against at the point at which it decided what it was allowed to lose was asked a second time in a market in that town on the said Thursday, and that it is the same question, and that it is not answered; and that the three facts and the reason went in this column and not the first, and that the said woman said out loud in about forty people that none of them is an answer and that the yard is not going to call a fact an answer.

*And that the reason the yard is not going to say more than that is that the question was put by a man of no office, and that there is no office in that country which could be put the same question, and that an office which answered it would be an office's answer and not an answer; and that the said reason is a person of no office and not an office, and that it is not a figure, and that it is not going to be given in a shed or in a market or in a room or in about four years.*

*And that the three figures of the said day are three and not two, being nine communities and the records one in each, six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and that none of the three may be swapped for another, and that the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities and is not going to be.*

*And that the four lines above the said named line stand where they stood, and are still four, and are still nameless, and have not been struck out, and are in about sixty boats on that coast; and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that a line does not come out of a column because it has been printed and cannot be got back.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing on the said Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either of the two days, and sixty-seven marks, three shillings and ten pence is in the bag, which is ten thousand seven hundred and sixty-six pence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is ten thousand one hundred and twenty-six pence. The whole of what came into that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Thursday is six days at the face of the second seam, being two thousand eight hundred and eighty pence, and the whole of what went out of that yard in the twenty days ended on the said Thursday is fourpence at the post on the sixth of March, and the whole of what has gone out of that yard since the sixth of December past is two shillings and seven pence in five transactions at three counters. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and twenty weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth day of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past, the first of December past, the eighth, the fifteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of December past, the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth of January instant, the second, the ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of February instant, the second and the ninth of March instant, and all twenty are in that book in the same breath and not one after the other. The chandler's note stands at thirty-one and is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and the said note was said out loud at the head of that dock for the fourth time in seven months on the fourth of March instant in about eleven people's hearing and that yard said in the same breath that it will not say it a fifth time. The rest of that standing position, and every figure in it, is on the page above, and none of it has moved.**
