# Chapter 235

*The Half She Has Not Looked At in Thirty-One Years, and a Penny*

Friday the twelfth and Saturday the thirteenth of February, on the face of the second seam, and in a room off the fish market.

**No rota day falls on the said Friday and none on the said Saturday, and there is no charge against the cistern behind the engine house on either of them, and this party entered that as two days and entered nothing else against either of them and there is no reason because there is nothing to have a reason about.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Friday and was not occupied on the said Saturday, and neither brother went over to it, and this party carried no figure of anything forward to either of those two lines.**

---

**And on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past two on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed for the whole of it, which he has not done since the eighteenth of January, and he did not stay for any reason this yard knows of and did not say he was staying.**

The gross on the board was twelve, and the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it, and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back five times and not four, and the ship's hand wrote the fifth one down and the man of the second lay looked at it and did not say anything about the five.

It was a good day for weather and a poor one for the tide and the day went the way a day goes with three on it and a man who has decided to stay.

---

**And they went down that hill on the Saturday morning at about a quarter to nine, before the market was off the boards, because a counter in a room off the fish market is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards and on a Saturday the market is off the boards at about ten, and this yard had never once worked that out before the sixth of December past and had found it out on the seventh.**

Pernel Saye was behind the counter and there was one man in the room buying a sheet for a will and she was copying a deed for a woman of about seventy and she did not stop either of them.

Calder Marr put a penny on the counter. She looked at it and put it in the box, which is what happens to money at that counter, and said nothing about it.

"**We are not here to buy anything,**" said Nessa Pike. "**We bought a sheet of paper on the twenty-eighth of January and we have not been back since and we have thought about it every day, and we have come back inside a month because you told us to come back, and I want to say that we came back because you told us to and not because we wanted to be told something.

"**And I am not going to tell you what a column is. Nobody is going to tell you what a column is in this room today.

"**And I am going to say the other thing first, before you say yours, and it is that we have bought a sheet of paper off you and paid a penny for it and that a sheet is a penny and forty sheets are a shilling and that is the whole of your trade and we have been trying to get at the other half of it for a month and we have been rude about it.

"**So: what is the other half. And I am asking it as a person and not as a party and I am not going to write it down.**"

"**You told me you would not write it down on the twenty-eighth of January,**" said Pernel Saye, "**and you have come back inside a month to be told a thing by a woman of fifty-eight who has not looked at it in thirty-one years, and you are going to ask me whether it is worth something.

"**I am going to tell you and I am going to tell you first that it is about me and not about a page, and I am telling you because you asked me the other question first, and if you had not asked me the other question first I would have taken this to my grave and my grave is a better place for it than a counter in a room off a fish market.

"**And it is worse. I told you on the twenty-eighth that when I came to tell you it would be a worse thing to hear. It is worse and it is not worse for you and it is worse for me, and that is the whole of the difference and I have never had a customer in here on a Saturday morning in the eleven years you have not been coming.

"**Here it is, and it is nine years old and it has not been a day of my life since.

"**In the twenty-second year of this counter a man came in at about eleven on a Thursday with a page under his arm, and it was a page about a place, and he wanted a copy of it made, and he paid me a penny and four pence for it in the coin of the day and he left the paper with me and went out and said he would come back for it in a week, because that is what people say, and I have heard about nine hundred people say that in thirty-one years and about four of them meant it.

"**He did not come back. Not in a week and not in nine years. And I have the copy. It is in my own back room in a bundle with some other paper of my own making and it is in my own hand and it is a copy of a page about a place and it is the only thing I have ever made that I cannot account for to anybody.

"**And here is the part that is worse and that I have not said out loud to anybody. It is not that I have got a page I have no business having. It is that in nine years I have never once looked at it. Nine years. I do not know what is on it. I know what a page of that shape looks like because I have copied four hundred of them and I do not know what is on that one, and I copied it and I have not read it, and there is a woman of about seventy in this town who could not read a page of her own for forty years and I have thought about her exactly once in nine years and I thought about her last week.

"**And the other half of my trade is not the making. I have known that since about three years ago and I have not said it. The other half is that somebody has to be able to say where a thing came from and who has had it, and there is nobody in this trade to say that, and I have been a good woman at a counter for thirty-one years and I have never once written a line of it down anywhere, and neither has anybody else in this town who makes things out of other people's words.

"**So when you came in on the twenty-eighth of January and asked me which of two records was worth having, I did not refuse it because I had no answer. I refused it because I have got a bundle in my own back room with a page in it that I made for a man who did not come back, and I have never told one living person, and a person who has that in her house and asks a stranger which record is worth having is a person who is asking a stranger to sign for something.

"**And I am telling you two strangers on a Saturday morning and not one of you is anybody, and that is why I am telling you, and if either of you had been anybody I would not have told you and you would have got the other thing again and the other thing was true and it was not the worst of it.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for a while, and the woman of about seventy finished her deed and put it in her bag and went out.

"**Then say the part about the penny,**" said Nessa Pike, "**because we owe you a figure and we have owed you a figure since the seventh of January and we have not asked.**

"**No. You do not owe me a figure. I cannot price the being right about a page and I have told you that and it was true on the twenty-eighth and it is true now. What you owe me is a sheet of paper and you have paid it and it went in the box and it is the second penny this yard has spent since the eighteenth of December and there is not going to be a third one for about a month.

"**And I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to go back to my deed, and it is the only thing in this conversation that is about you and not about me: the reason I told you to come back in a month was not that I wanted to be asked again. It was that I wanted to find out whether you would come back, and I want you to understand what a small thing that is. A woman behind a counter can tell in about four seconds whether a thing she says has been taken away and used. In nine years, one.**"

"**Then here is what we are going to do with it,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to say all three parts and then I am going to stop.

"**We are not going to ask to see it. We are not going to ask where it is. And we are not going to offer to take it off you.

"**And the third one is the one I want written down, and the reason for it is not a favour and is not a kindness. That thing in your back room is a page about a place, and we have spent six weeks learning what happens to a page about a place when it is in a shed on a mole four hundred miles from the place it is about, and we did it to four people and we cannot do it back to one woman who has done nothing to anybody. If we offered to hold it you would have to say yes or no, and if you said yes this yard would have a page it cannot check in a town it cannot get to, and if you said no you would have spent a night deciding, and neither of those is a thing we are entitled to put on a woman of fifty-eight before her market is off the boards.

"**So it is yours and it stays yours and it is not a thing this yard knows about beyond what you have told us in this room this morning, and I am not going to write it in that book, and if you ever want to know what this yard is doing with a page in this country you can come here and read that book off the shelf and take it away and that is a better thing than anything I can offer you.**"

"**You are the first party in four volumes that has offered me a book to take away,**" said Pernel Saye, "**and I am not going to take it, and not because of anything you have said. Because in about four years, when somebody comes into this room with a question and a clean sleeve, I am going to have to be able to say that I have not read it.

"**And I have read yours. I read the first four lines of it at about half past seven this morning and then I put it back, and that was a decision and I made it at half past seven, and you had better have a figure for that or you had better have nothing at all.**"

"**We have nothing at all,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I am going to say the only figure I have got and it is a bad one. Three days ago a man set a copy of a page of ours for a man in a place down this coast and was paid two shillings and tenpence for the day of his trade that it took him, and that figure is in his own book and it is in ours and it is not our money, and it is the first time in four volumes this yard has entered a figure of another man's lost day, and I do not know what that is worth either, and I have not slept properly since the Saturday and I am not going to pretend to you that I have.**"

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirteenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no rota day falls on the said Friday and none on the said Saturday, and that there is no charge against the cistern behind the engine house on either of them and no figure against either of them.*

*And that the far bench was not occupied at any hour on the said Friday nor at any hour on the said Saturday, and that the holder did not go over to him on either of the two days.*

*And that on the said Friday this yard worked from eight until about half past two on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water; and that the man of the second lay brought the pump and the man and stayed for the whole of the day for the first time since the eighteenth day of January past and gave no reason for it and said none; and that a hand lead's line went over the side and back five times and not four, and that the said man looked at the fifth turn being written down and said nothing about it; and that the gross on that board was twelve and nine of the twelve was paid at the mole and the yard's was three, being four hundred and eighty pence.*

*And that this party went down that hill on the said Saturday morning at about a quarter to nine, before the market was off the boards, and that a counter in a room off the fish market is open from eight until the market is off the boards, and that on a Saturday the market is off the boards at about ten, and that this yard had not worked that out before the sixth of December past and worked it out on the seventh.*

*And that one penny went out of this yard on the said Saturday, being a sheet of paper bought at that counter, and that it went into the box and was not spoken about, and that it is the second money this yard has spent since the eighteenth of December past and the whole of the money that has gone out of it since the sixth of December past is three pence, and that there is not going to be a third one for about a month.*

*And that the said copyist said that she would tell the said party the half of her trade that she has not looked at in thirty-one years and that it is worse than anything she said on the twenty-eighth of January past, and that she said beforehand that it is about her and not about a page, and that she is telling them because they asked the other question first, and that if they had not asked the other question first she would have taken it to her grave.*

*And that the said copyist said that in the twenty-second year of that counter a man came in at about eleven on a Thursday with a page about a place and had a copy of it made for a penny and four pence, and that he said he would come back for it in a week and did not come back in a week and did not come back in nine years, and that the copy is in her own back room in a bundle with some other paper of her own making and is the only thing she has ever made that she cannot account for to anybody.*

*And that the said copyist said that in nine years she has never once looked at that copy, and that she does not know what is on it, and that the other half of her trade is not the making of a thing but that somebody has to be able to say where a thing came from and who has had it, and that there is nobody in that trade to say it, and that she has never written a line of it down anywhere and neither has anybody else in that town who makes things out of other people's words.*

*And that the said copyist said that she refused the question of the twenty-eighth of January past not because she had no answer but because she has a bundle in her own back room with a page in it that she made for a man who did not come back and has never told one living person, and that a person who has that in her house and asks a stranger which record is worth having is a person who is asking a stranger to sign for something, and that she told it to two strangers on a Saturday morning and would not have told one of them.*

*And that the said holder said that this party is not going to ask to see that paper and is not going to ask where it is and is not going to offer to take it off her, and gave the reason, being that that thing is a page about a place and this party has spent six weeks learning what happens to a page about a place when it is in a shed on this mole, and that if the said party offered to hold it she would have had to say yes or no, and that neither of those is a thing this party is entitled to put on a woman of fifty-eight before her market is off the boards; and that he said that it is hers and it stays hers and that this party is not going to write it in that book.*

*And that the said copyist said that the said party is the first in four volumes to have offered her a book to take away, and that she is not going to take it, and not because of anything that was said, and that in about four years when somebody comes into that room with a question and a clean sleeve she is going to have to be able to say that she has not read it, and that she read the first four lines of that book at about half past seven on the said morning and then put it back.*

*And that the said woman said that the first figure she has got is that a man set a copy of a page of that yard's for a man in a place down this coast three days ago and was paid two shillings and tenpence for the day of his trade that it took him, and that the said figure is in his own book and in that one and is not that yard's money, and that it is the first time in four volumes this party has entered a figure of another man's lost day, and that she said she does not know what that is worth.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the thirteenth day of February next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a woman of fifty-eight has had a copy in her own back room for nine years that she made for a man who did not come back and has never looked at, and that this party has been told the fact of it and has not been told what is on it and has not asked and is not going to ask, and that the reason against that line is that a person who keeps a thing she cannot account for is not owed an inspection by anybody, least of all by a party that has spent a month reading other people's pages in a shed.*

*And that the days of the said Friday and the said Saturday were not read back out of that book, and that the reason against that is that the said Friday was a day of work and the said Saturday was a morning in a room off a fish market, and that the two of them did not sit down in that shed until nine o'clock and that when they did there was nothing in that day of that party's to read out that was not about somebody else, and that a day this yard spends on a thing that is not its own is entered as a day this yard spent and is not entered as a day the yard lost.*

**Three marks came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing on the said Saturday, and one penny went out of it on the said Saturday and nothing else went out on either day, and thirty-seven marks, six shillings and two pence is in the bag, which is five thousand nine hundred and ninety-four pence, and that is one penny less than it was on the Thursday, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five thousand three hundred and fifty-four pence. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on that bench, and sixteen weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The cistern was not charged on either of the two days and the walk is nil and stays nil. The far bench was not occupied on either of them. The bench under that pump was bare for a thirteenth night on the Friday and a fourteenth on the Saturday. Four returns are four and there is no fifth, and no finding of worked glass was made on either of the two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and is not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and the note against it stands open. The third column is unfilled and unappointed and the boy's hand is at the head of it. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and he is not in this book. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright, unargued, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, unpaid and unoffered. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June past standing. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and it has not been said out loud since the third of December past. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. The whole of what this yard has spent since the sixth of December past is three pence in two sheets of paper at one counter off this fish market, and there is no other counter on this coast this yard has ever gone into. A working harbor of this coast is poorer on the thirteenth of February next than it was on the fourth of it, and nothing this yard has done in ten days has made one thing better. Every other figure this harbor is owed is set out in the page above and is not read out again in this paragraph.**
