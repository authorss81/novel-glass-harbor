# Chapter 559

*Sunday the Thirtieth Day of January, a Wipe, the Ninety-First Time, the Wall behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Not Whole, Six People Walking Past It and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket under It, the Two Men on That Cistern Raising the Covers and Looking Down and Putting the Covers Back and Not Putting a Pail in It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, a Man of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets on the Third Lay Refusing a Second Time in About a Year on a Different Ground From the First, Being That He Said No on the Twenty-Sixth of December Past to a Name Going on a Page and Says No Today to a Hand Going Under the Six Lines of That Account, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Asking Him Once and a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking Him Why Once and Nobody Asking Him Why Again, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the thirtieth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and at a bench on the third lay where a man of about forty-six mends nets and has mended them there for nineteen years.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare for the ninety-first time at about half past ten in the morning and went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and it did not go up whole, and six people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-ninth days. The four names and five marks went up on four tacks on the boards of the floor of that market on the twenty-sixth of December past and they are still on those boards and the name of no man of that harbor is on them. The wage column in that harbor is read out again on the first of February and the last time it was read out it did not move and a week stands fallen due behind it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-seven days and nobody has set a figure against it and he has not been to the road with the pump on it.**

---

There was no pail anywhere near that wall at half past ten on that Sunday and there was none at the middle of the day. It came down bare, as it has come down bare ninety times now, and it went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and it did not go up whole, and the six people who came past it went past it. The two men who do that cistern were there at about eleven and lifted the covers and looked straight down into it and put the covers back on and went off, and the covers are on it now and the butt behind that market is full and none of it came out of anywhere that anybody had to carry it to.

---

And the woman of thirty of no office came to that bench on the third lay at about eleven in the morning on her own and not with anybody and put a sheet down on the bench beside his twine, and the sheet had a line on it and a space for a hand and a date, and it had come round that harbor on the Saturday and it had been brought back to her on the Saturday evening by about nine people who had carried it as far as the end of the third lay and no further, and she asked him one question and he said no in one word, and about six people heard both of the two things.

"Do you want your hand under that."

"No."

She did not ask him why and she put the sheet back inside her own coat and she stood at that bench for about a quarter of an hour and said nothing else about it, and she has not asked him about it since, and she said out loud on the way up the lay that a sheet with a line and a space for a hand is neither an account nor an answer and is a third thing, and that a third thing that has come round nine houses and been carried back to the same bench is going to come round again.

---

And the man of about thirty-three who hires hands came up the third lay at about half past one in the afternoon and asked him why, once, and he was answered in about a hundred and sixty-seven words and nobody in that harbor has asked him why again and nobody is going to, and he said out loud at the end of it that if anybody else wanted to ask him why they were welcome to come and stand on that lay themselves.

"I said no on the twenty-sixth of December to a name going on that board, and my reason then was that a name goes on a page by the person writing it there and that a man in that market cannot put a name on it from that market. **That is a reason about belonging and it is finished and it has been finished for a month.** Today's is not that one. Today's is that this is the first correct account anybody has handed this harbor since before I was crewing, and every line of it is one I could stand in front of and say is right, and I am not going to be the first name under it. A man who is the first name under a thing makes it his, and I have got nineteen years of a bench on this lay and I am not going to start the next nineteen with a piece of paper about a night I was not on."

He put his hand flat on the bench and he said one more thing and he said it once.

"And I want it on the record that I do not think it is a lie, because I have read all six of those lines twice and there is not one of them I could argue with, and a man who says no to a thing that is all true is in a different place from a man who says no to a thing that is not, and I know which of those two I am and I do not want anybody in this harbor to be on my side about it."

---

A man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud at about two o'clock in the afternoon that the two of those were the same thing and that a man who knows the difference is a man who has understood something, and the man of about forty-six said that it was not the same thing and that the difference was the whole of the argument, and neither of them said it twice and neither of them was thanked and about six people heard both of those things on a Sunday afternoon on a third lay in about twenty minutes.

He had a slate at home with a list on it that nobody in that harbor knew he kept, and it was not a page of hours and had no hours in it and carried nothing a stranger could check. It was a list of things done in that yard since the autumn that had not been thanked for, and it had eleven lines on it in the autumn and had nineteen that night, and the nineteenth was a man on a bench on a third lay saying no to a sheet that had come round nine houses and been carried back to the same bench by nine pairs of hands. He did not put it on the leaf. He had told himself in November that a list of what a man is not thanked for is a list of what he thinks he is owed, and that a man who thinks he is owed has stopped working, and he had gone three weeks without adding a line to it on purpose and he was not going to start again because of a Sunday.

Nobody answered the question a woman of thirty of no office asked in that room over that market on the twenty-fourth of this month, and nobody has answered it since, and one man saying no to a line is not that harbor saying no, and that harbor has not said anything.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and said in about a hundred and one words what a second no costs and what it does not, and nobody in that harbor was asked whether it was worth it.

"Ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence. Unmoved, being the second wipe this month, and the wall came down and the covers went back on. **A man said no twice in a year on two different grounds and the second one costs him a page and the first one cost him nothing at all, and there is no figure for that anywhere in this harbor and I am not going to make one up, and the man who said it was not thanked and did not ask to be.**"

Nobody was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Sunday, and nobody was thanked for a second refusal and nobody was thanked for carrying a sheet back to a bench and nobody was thanked for the hundred and sixty-seven words at half past one.

---

**THE WALL CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE NINETY-FIRST TIME AND WENT UP AGAIN UNREPAIRED AND NOT WHOLE AND SIX PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. TWO MEN RAISED THE COVERS OF THAT CISTERN AND LOOKED DOWN AND PUT THE COVERS BACK AND DID NOT PUT A PAIL IN IT. A SHEET WITH A LINE ON IT AND A SPACE FOR A HAND CAME ROUND THAT HARBOR ON THE SATURDAY AND WAS CARRIED BACK TO THE SAME BENCH ON THE THIRD LAY BY NINE PEOPLE WHO CARRIED IT NO FURTHER, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX SAID NO IN ONE WORD, AND THE REASON HE GAVE WAS ABOUT BEING THE FIRST NAME UNDER A CORRECT ACCOUNT AND NOT ABOUT BELONGING, AND HE SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE DOES NOT THINK IT IS A LIE. HE WAS ASKED WHY ONCE BY ONE MAN AND NOT AGAIN BY ANYBODY. THAT HARBOR HAS ANSWED NOTHING. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirtieth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, being the ninety-first wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the two men on that cistern raised the covers and looked down into it and put the covers back on and did not put a pail in it; and that the wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare for the ninety-first time at about half past ten in the morning and went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and did not go up whole, and that six people of that harbor walked past it and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that a sheet with a line on it and a space for a hand and a date came round that harbor on the twenty-ninth of this month and was brought back on that same evening by about nine people who carried it as far as the end of the third lay and no further, and that a woman of thirty of no office put it down on the bench of a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay at about eleven in the morning on this day and asked him whether he wanted his hand under that, and that he said no in one word, and that about six people heard both of the two things; and that she did not ask him why and put the sheet back inside her own coat and said out loud on her way up the lay that a sheet with a line and a space for a hand is neither an account nor an answer and is a third thing; and that the man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked him why at about half past one in the afternoon, once, and was answered in about a hundred and sixty-seven words, being that his reason of the twenty-sixth of December past was about a name going on a page by the person writing it there and that that reason is finished and has been finished for a month, and that the reason of this day is that this is the first correct account that harbor has been handed and that he is not going to be the first name under it, and that a man who is the first name under a thing makes it his; and that he said out loud that he does not think it is a lie, that he has read all six of those lines twice and that there is not one of them he could argue with, and that a man who says no to a thing that is all true is in a different place from a man who says no to a thing that is not; and that he said out loud that if anybody else wants to ask him why they are welcome to stand on that lay themselves; and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said at about two o'clock in the afternoon that those two are the same thing and was told that the difference is the whole of the argument, and that neither said it twice and neither was thanked; and that nobody asked that man why a second time and nobody has asked him since; and that nobody in that harbor has answered the question asked in the room over that market on the twenty-fourth of this month, and that one man saying no to a line is not that harbor saying no and that that harbor has not said anything; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a day of the berth fund was paid on the ninety-first wipe is his own and is not the reason either of the two wipes before it carried, and is that a sheet came round nine houses on the Saturday and was carried back to a bench by nine pairs of hands, and that nine pairs of hands is a day's work and that a man in this yard who had been asked to price it would have priced it and would not have got a penny, and that a berth fund pays a day for a day and has paid three this week; and that the reason the man of about forty-six said out loud that he does not think it is a lie is his own, and is that on the twenty-sixth of December past he had a reason that was about who he belonged to and that a man who has got a reason like that can be moved by a better fact, and that he has now got a reason that is about what he is prepared to be first at, and that a man with a reason like that cannot be moved by a better fact and can only be waited out, and that he would rather be a man who cannot be moved than a man who can; and that the reason nobody asked him why a second time is his own, and is that a man of about forty-six who has said no twice has given that harbor about a hundred and sixty-seven words on a Sunday and that the whole of what he has left is the part he did not say, and that a second why would take the part he did not say out of him in front of six people, and that there are six things in this harbor that only he can say and that none of them are on that slip.*
