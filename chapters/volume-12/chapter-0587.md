# Chapter 587

*Sunday the Twenty-Seventh Day of February, a Wipe, the Ninety-Fifth Time, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Wall behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Again at About Ten to Eleven at the Angle It Has Been at Since the Twelfth and Not Going Up Whole, It Having Taken About Eleven Minutes Both Ways, a Room over a Joiner's Shop at the Top of Fish Street Let Out From About Eleven in the Morning Until About Four in the Afternoon and Not Paid For, a Civil Ceremony Gone Through in It at About One in the Afternoon Before About Nine People of Whom About Four Cannot Read, Four Lines on a Form of Which the Second and the Third Are That the Ceremony Does Not Make Her Responsible for His Diving and Does Not Make Him Responsible for Her Record and That Neither of Them May Put the Other in a Sentence About the Other's Work, the Two Names Said Once Each in Their Own Mouths and Not Written on This Page, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying in About One Hundred and Ninety-Three Words That the Room Was Not Paid For and That a Room of That Kind Is the Only Thing a Sheet of Money Cannot Have, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying in About One Hundred and Sixty-Seven Words That Nothing About That Afternoon Was Arranged Before That Afternoon, Nobody Asked and Nobody Thanked and Not One Person Put on a Step, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Four Hundred and Thirty-Four Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the twenty-seventh day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street with about nine people standing in it at one in the afternoon.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was the ninety-fifth wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing, and the two men who do that cistern raised the covers at about eleven, looked straight down into it, and put the covers back on. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten in the morning and went up again at about ten to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole, and it took about eleven minutes both ways this time, which is longer than the ninth minutes of the twentieth and longer than the four minutes of the thirteenth, and neither of the two men who do it said a word about that and neither has said a word about it since. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the room that was let out at the top of Fish Street was not paid for and is not on that sheet, and the day that was asked for by name on that day was paid at the figure in chalk and is not on that sheet either. That yard took one hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence out at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there were twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and that bag is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, under the wage of four marks. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninety-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-seven days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the offer of four lines is behind a door in the room over that market and is not on that board and nothing is written under its fourth line. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-five days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

That wall came down bare at about half past ten and went up again at about ten to eleven, and it took about eleven minutes both ways, and the boards at the join are showing weather on the face of them and there are two of them that are not the colour they were. The light at the head of that mole was out at about half past seven this morning as it is on every clear night and there is no figure for it on any board in that harbor, and about eleven people went past that wall between half past ten and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it, and one of the eleven was a man of about nineteen with a handcart who stood and looked at the angle of it for about a minute and then went on up the street.

About eleven o'clock the two men who do that cistern came to it and took the covers off, and both of them stood looking straight down the shaft with a pail in neither hand, and they put the covers back on where they had been, and the one of the two who does the Monday and the Thursday said out loud to the other and to nobody else that a cistern is a Monday and a Thursday thing and that on a Sunday the most a man can do is look at one.

---

The front room over that joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street was let out from about eleven in the morning until about four in the afternoon, and it was not paid for.

Nobody paid for it. The woman of thirty of no office asked for it on Friday and told the man who owns the room that nobody was going to pay him for it, and he said that it did not matter to him and that it did not matter to him because it is a room with a press in it on a Sunday. The day of the twenty-seventh was asked for by name at the figure in chalk, which is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and it was paid whether anybody was on the water or not, and it is not on that sheet and is not on any sheet. An hour of a man of about twenty-seven went into writing the four lines out in a large hand, and he was not paid for that hour either and did not ask to be.

The ceremony was gone through at about one in the afternoon. Nothing about that afternoon was arranged before that afternoon. The form was not written out until about ten past one, with about nine people in that room, and about four of those nine could not read it.

Here are the four lines and there are four of them.

1. *Two people have gone through a civil ceremony in this room on the twenty-seventh day of this month at about one in the afternoon, and no other person has been asked anything about it, and no other person is named on this page.*
2. *This does not make the woman of thirty of no office responsible for the diving of the man of about thirty-two, and it does not make him responsible for her record, and neither of them may put the other one in a sentence about the other one's work, in a room or on a wall or in a page.*
3. *Neither of them may use this page as a reason for anything that either of them does, and neither of them has said that out loud and neither of them was asked to.*
4. *This is a small civil ceremony. It is not on any sheet in this harbor. The two names on the form are the two names of the two people standing in this room and each of them has been said once, in their own mouth, in this room, and neither name is written on this page and neither one is said twice.*

The man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read those four lines out loud in that room with the door open, and read the second and the third of them a second time at the asking of a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, and got nothing wrong on either reading.

Then the man of about thirty-two said his own name once, and the woman of thirty of no office said hers once, and that is where the names are. Neither name is on this page, neither name is in that room twice, and about four people in that room could not read the four lines and were told what was on them by the man of about forty-six.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, in about one hundred and ninety-three words, what that room was and what it was not, and a man of about thirty-four of the second lay who cannot read was standing at the back of it the whole time and was not asked to do anything.

"**That room cost nothing and it was got for nothing and the man who owns it is not on any sheet in this harbor and is not going to be. I have been in this harbor thirty years and I have seen men ruin themselves over a room and a table and a bottle of something, and the whole of the ruin is always that somebody paid for it and could not say no afterwards.** There are about four people standing in that room who cannot read what is on that page and every one of the four of them has just had all four lines read out to them twice, and none of them was asked to sign it, and one man at the back of that room who cannot read a word of it has not been asked to do a single thing this afternoon. That is what a room is for. A sheet of money cannot do that, and a room can, and the price of a room is nothing and the price of a sheet is that everything on it is a thing somebody can be held to afterwards."

A woman of thirty of no office said out loud, in about one hundred and sixty-seven words, that nothing about that afternoon had been arranged before that afternoon, and that a man of about thirty-four was at the back of that room and had not been asked to read anything and was not going to be asked to read anything.

"**I asked for that room on Friday and I have been in this harbor eleven weeks and I have watched every single thing that has been done in this harbor arranged by somebody the day before, and the two of us decided this on Friday and the only thing decided on Friday was that I would ask.** The second of those four lines is the one I would have written if I had been allowed to write any of them, and the second of those four lines is in a hand that is not mine and not his, and that is on purpose as well. And the man at the back of that room is not on a step and he is not going to be put on one this afternoon, and he is not being asked to read this and he is not being asked to hold this and nobody in this room is going to turn round at some point today and look at him."

Nobody answered her, nobody thanked her, and the form went into the inside of a coat at about twenty past two and neither of those two coats has been opened by anybody else, and the room was given back at about four o'clock with a press in it and nothing else in it that was not in it on Friday.

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about four hundred and thirty-four words on a room that was not paid for, and nobody in that slip was asked what two people had done in a room at the top of Fish Street.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved since four o'clock yesterday afternoon, and a day was asked for by name this morning and paid at the figure in chalk and it is not on that sheet, and the room was not paid for at all and there is no figure for it anywhere in this harbor, including on a bench stone in this shed.

**A man of about thirty-two and a woman of thirty of no office went through a civil ceremony this afternoon in a room that was got for nothing, and the whole of the price of it is a day at one hundred and sixty-eight pence and an hour of a man of about twenty-seven and a room with a press in it, and none of the three of those is on that sheet and one of the three is not on anything.** A day is a day and I have got it in my own hand and I am not going to write it in there.

The second of the four lines on that page is that it does not make her responsible for his diving and does not make him responsible for her record, and it is printed on the face of the page and it was read out twice in that room, and I have had that line on the back of that slate for four days in my own hand as a thing I was thinking about and there is now a page in a coat at the top of Fish Street that says it in letters bigger than mine, and that is a strange thing to happen to a man who keeps a leaf. I am not going to be one of the two men who worked this out. Somebody else worked it out, in a room, on a Sunday, and wrote it down, and I am going to write the number of the day I ran in this leaf and nothing else about it.

The wall came down bare this morning for the ninety-fifth time and went up not whole and took about eleven minutes, and nobody has said why it is slower than the last two times. The third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing, and one hundred and twenty-five is what that fourth has stood at for eleven weeks with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and the reason for that is not on any page in this harbor and is not going onto one this evening either."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for the afternoon, and the wedge was still on that bench stone where it had been put on Thursday, and nobody looked at it.

NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT HARBOR WAS PUT ON A STEP.
