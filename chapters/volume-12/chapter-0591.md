# Chapter 591

*Thursday the Third Day of March, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Thirtieth Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, a Page of Two Columns and Three Lines at the Head of It Going onto That Bench Stone at About Eleven in the Morning in About Nine People, a Man of About Thirty-One Who Keeps the Crossing Column of the Port Four Days Down That Coast in One Book in One Hand Being Asked First and Saying No in About two hundred and forty-one Words and Being Named on That Page as Having Not Agreed and Not Argued With and Not Asked a Second Time, a Woman of About Thirty-Nine Who Mends Net on the Boards Being Asked Second and Saying Yes in About two hundred and sixty-four Words and Putting Ten o'Clock in the Morning of the Tenth of This Month onto That Page and Her Own Name at the Foot of It, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands and That Woman Arguing in About ninety-six and About one hundred and seven Words About What a Yes Is and What a Name at the Foot of a Page Is For, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About four hundred and nineteen Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the third day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at eleven o'clock in the morning.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirtieth time a little after eight in the morning, in eleven trips by hand and no more, and the covers were down on it again before a quarter past nine, and the butt filled to the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon, and not a bucket came out of it all day, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the page that went onto that bench stone at about eleven in the morning is on no sheet in this harbor and is in no minute. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninety-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and thirteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninetieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty-one days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is standing in a boathouse with the paper still in it and nobody in that harbor has looked inside it. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-nine days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That cistern behind that market was charged for the hundred and thirtieth time a little after eight this morning, in eleven trips by hand and no more, and the covers were back down on it before a quarter past nine. The butt stood filled to the rim of the second hoop and stayed that way until about four in the afternoon and not one bucket came out of it. The two men who carry it up that ladder are still not on that rota board and have still not asked to be, and the name at the head of that board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern this morning was not used for anything at any hour of this day.

---

And at about eleven in the morning a page went onto that bench stone and about nine people were in that shed and the door stood open, and the page was a page of two columns with three lines at the head of it, and the three of them were read out first.

1. *Two communities are asked, one at a time and out loud, in front of anybody who wants to stand there, whether they will put up a permitted account at the same hour on the same morning as any other community that has said yes. Nobody is to be asked a second time and nobody is to be argued with.*
2. *The hour is ten o'clock in the morning and the morning is the tenth of this month, and that is on the page so that anybody who wants to say it is a different hour can say it before the tenth and not on it.*
3. *A community that says no is named on this page as having not agreed. That name is the name of the place and not the name of a person, and nobody in that place is to be asked why.*

The man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read those three out twice, and got nothing wrong on either reading, and about four of the nine people in that shed were told what was on them by him.

---

The port four days down that coast was asked first, and the man who answered for it is a man of about thirty-one who came up that coast in a boat on Saturday, a boat takes a day and a night against four days each way for the cart, and he keeps the crossing column of that port in one book in one hand and has kept it eleven years. He said no, in his own mouth, in about 241 words, and then he stopped and sat down on the bench at the back of that shed.

"**I will answer for that place because I keep the book and there is nobody else there who can, and I am going to say no, and I am not going to be asked about it a second time.** You have asked whether we will put an account up at the same hour as everybody else, and the answer is that we are not going to, and it is not a trick and it is not a complaint and it is not a mood. That book is eleven years old and one man has put every hour in it and one man is the only name on it, and that is the whole reason a stranger can go and read it and cannot get a word wrong out of it. A permitted account is a different animal. It is a thing printed with four things set against every place a community has not said something, and I have read as far as that and I am not going to tell you what the four of them are this morning. We have not got a second hand to put next to ours. We have not got a second hand at all. So the page is going to say in its own words that that port has not agreed, with the name of the port on it, and that name can sit there and be disagreed with for as long as anybody likes."

Nobody argued with him. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked him one question and got one answer, and it was about whether that port would be asked again before the tenth, and he said no, and nobody put it to him a second time.

The name went onto the page in the place set against the first column, and the three lines against it are that the port four days down that coast has not agreed, that it is named as having not agreed, and that nobody is to be asked about it again this month.

---

The community that has permitted everything was asked second, and the woman who answered for it is a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on the boards at the back of that market and has mended net there for nineteen years. She said yes, in her own mouth, in about 264 words, and she put the hour on the page herself with the chalk out of her own pocket.

"**Yes, and I am saying it for the people on these boards, and if there are eleven of us and ten of them disagree with me at four o'clock this afternoon then my yes comes off that page before it goes up and I will take it off it myself with this same piece of chalk.** I have mended net on these boards for nineteen years and nobody has ever once asked me what I keep, and there is a page on that bench stone with a figure no person has changed in eleven weeks in one of its columns and three hours of the same night with three different figures in it in the other, and both of those are real and the second of them is the whole of what this harbor has done since the eleventh of this month. We have permitted everything. That has cost us the one hour any of us could stand behind afterwards, and I have stopped saying it was worth it, because a person who cannot say what a thing cost is not permitted anything, he is only unanswered. Ten o'clock in the morning, the tenth of this month. That is the hour and it is on the page now. And a community that has sealed everything is going to be standing in the same column on the same morning as one that has permitted nothing at all, and the one that says no is going to be named as having not agreed, and I would rather that than a page with one voice in it."

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands was not satisfied with that, and he was not satisfied with it for a reason of his own and not for hers, and he said so in about 96 words.

"You have said yes for about eleven people in about ten seconds and I do not know ten of the eleven. I am not saying that you are the wrong person to say it to. I am saying that I do not know what a yes is until I know whose it is, and a yes in a market on a Thursday is a thing four people will be right about in four kitchens by supper, and that is how we got a column on a wall that nobody could argue with in the first place."

She answered him in about 107 words and she did not raise her voice to do it.

"My name goes at the foot of it. I am not leaving it off, and that is the whole of the difference, and you are going to tell me that a name at the foot of a page turns it into a party, and it does not, because a party is a thing that sends a person somewhere and a page with a name under it sends nobody anywhere. Write it down and check me on Saturday, and if I have said yes for somebody who did not, take it off in your own hand and put your own name under it while you are about it."

Her name went at the foot of the second column in her own hand, and the man of about thirty-three read that name off the page out loud in front of the nine of them and then said that he had read it and not agreed with it, and both of those are on the page and neither of them is a signature.

Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for any part of it. The page went in behind that press at about four in the afternoon with two columns on it and three lines at the head of it and one name at the foot of one of the two, and it is not on that sheet that carries the money and it is in no minute, and the two men who carry that cistern up that ladder walked past that shed at about five o'clock without going in.

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 419 words on a question that was asked twice in one morning and answered twice in one morning, and nobody in that slip was asked what a community has to be asked before it can say no.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved since Saturday afternoon at four o'clock, and nothing went onto that sheet this morning and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and that cistern was charged for the hundred and thirtieth time before the market opened and not one bucket came out of it.

**A community four days down that coast was asked this morning in a shed with the door open whether it would put up an account at the same hour as everybody else, and one man said no in about nine people and he was not argued with and he was not asked again, and a community in this harbor was asked the same question and one woman said yes with her name at the foot of it.** That is two communities and two answers and I have got both of them on a page behind a press and I have got neither of them on a sheet, and the reason neither of them is on a sheet is that that sheet is a list of what people did with their own money and a thing that was said in a room is not that.

Ten o'clock in the morning of the tenth is on that page in chalk and in two hands. If three more places say yes before Thursday then there are three of us standing at the same hour on the same morning, and the ones that do not say yes are going to be named on it as having not agreed and are not going to be argued with. That is the whole of what a gap is for and I am not going to explain it in this slip because I have not got it down yet.

The third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and the reason it has no line in it is not on any page of mine and is not going on one this month. I entered one line on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening and I have not read either of the first two figures of that leaf out loud to anybody this month, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that first column by counting days and cannot be stopped from doing it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for the two answers, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip and nobody was on it, and the page with two columns on it was behind the press and the wedge was on the corner of the bench stone.

THE PORT FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST WAS ASKED FIRST AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO KEEPS THAT CROSSING COLUMN IN ONE BOOK IN ONE HAND SAID NO IN ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-ONE WORDS AND WAS NOT ARGUIED WITH AND WAS NOT ASKED A SECOND TIME. THE COMMUNITY THAT HAS PERMITTED EVERYTHING WAS ASKED SECOND AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE WHO MENDS NET ON THE BOARDS SAID YES IN ABOUT 264 WORDS AND PUT HER OWN NAME AT THE FOOT OF THAT COLUMN. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE OBJECTED IN ABOUT 96 WORDS AND WAS ANSWERED IN ABOUT 107 WORDS, AND HE READ HER NAME OFF THE PAGE OUT LOUD AND THEN SAID HE HAD READ IT AND NOT AGREED WITH IT, AND BOTH OF THOSE ARE ON THAT PAGE. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**
