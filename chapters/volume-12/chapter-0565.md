# Chapter 565

*Saturday the Fifth Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Asking a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay at About One o'Clock in the Afternoon, Out Loud, in the Open, in Front of the Same Nine People, Whether He Will Hold One of Three Channels, Whether He Says Yes or No, and a Man of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets Writing the Asking and the Answer Out on a Page in a Hand That Is Not His and That Page Not Being a Page of Hours and Not Going on That Bench Stone Beside the Other Two, the Answer Being No, in One Word, and Nobody Arguing Him Out of It, One Line Going Onto That Sheet at About Four in the Afternoon at One Hundred and Sixty-Nine Pence, Being One Mark and Ninepence, Carrying Two Days on Its Face, the Fifth of February and the Twenty-Ninth of January Past, the Second of Those Two Being the Day a Man Asked in the Open to Be Asked and Was Not Asked, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the fifth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line before about four in the afternoon of that day, and one line went onto it at about four in the afternoon at one hundred and sixty-nine pence, being one mark and ninepence, being one sheet bought blank at a penny and one day asked for by name at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one and one hundred and sixty-eight is one hundred and sixty-nine, and that line carries two days on its face, being the fifth of this month settled on the fifth of this month and the twenty-ninth of January past, and the second of those two days is the day a man of about thirty-four asked in the open to be asked and was not asked, and the person whose yard it is said both of those days out loud in that yard before a figure was written against either of them. Nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and five days. The two pages of hours in that shed are the two they have always been, being one with two names on it and one with three, and the page that was written out on this afternoon is neither of them and does not carry an hour against anything and is not on that bench stone. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-three days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

And at about one o'clock in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two asked the man of about thirty-four of the second lay, out loud, in the open, at the end of that slip, in front of the same nine people, whether he would hold one of three channels, and whether he said yes or no, and he said both of those two things and he did not add anything to them.

He said on the twenty-ninth of January that he would not do it that day, and that when he did it would be in the open in front of the same nine people and on a page in a hand that was not the man's own, and that he would not ask twice if the first time was a no. All three of those things were as he had said they would be and he said them again in the same order.

The man of about thirty-four of the second lay said one word.

"No."

Nobody argued him out of it. The man of about thirty-two said nothing back, and about nine people stood on that slip and about two of them were about to say something and were not, and the man of about thirty-two said out loud and once that he was not going to ask him again, and that the asking had been asked and the answer had been given and both of them were standing up in the open on a Saturday afternoon with about nine people round them.

---

A man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay wrote the asking and the answer out at about half past one on a page in that shed, in a hand that is not the hand of the man of about thirty-four, and it is not a page of hours, it carries no hour against anything, it does not go on that bench stone beside the other two, and the only three things on it are the asking, the answer and the hour at the top of it.

The man of about thirty-four asked for it to be read out to him before the man of about forty-six put it down, and it was read out to him, and he said it was right, and the man of about forty-six did not read it out to anybody else and did not offer it to anybody.

---

And then the man of about thirty-four of the second lay said, out loud, to about nine people, unasked, that he was not going to be asked why and so he was going to say it once, and he said it in about two hundred and twenty-six words, and nobody asked him why before it or after it.

"I have said no and I want it on that page and it is on that page. Here is the rest of it and I am saying it once.

**A channel is a name on a page.** The rule about the three of you is on a page and it is a good rule and the man who wrote it out did not write my name on it and that is the only reason I have ever been able to read any of it. If I say yes this afternoon then my name is at the top of a page, and from that day there is a thing about me on a page that I cannot read, and in a year of this coast not one person has ever come to me and told me what is written about me on a page. I want to be asked in words, in a room, where people can hear the answer, and that is all I want and it is not a small thing to want.

The man of about thirty-three said on that Friday that he would put the asking and the answer on a page in a hand that is not mine, and he kept that. He did not have to. Put the no on there, that is right. Do not put a yes on there."

A man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that a man who says no and then says why in a market is doing two things and that most men only do one, and the man of about thirty-four said that he had had a week to think about which of the two he was going to do and that was not a week of thinking, it was a Saturday.

---

And at about four in the afternoon the person whose yard it is put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before a figure was written against it, in about nine people, and the two days on that line were said before the figure and the figure was said after them.

"One sheet bought blank at a penny, being one penny. And one day asked for by name at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and the day was asked for of the man of about forty-six who wrote the page out. That is one and one hundred and sixty-eight is one hundred and sixty-nine pence, being one mark and ninepence. The fifth of February is settled on the fifth of February and I am printing that day once because it is one day.

**And there is a second day on that line and it is the twenty-ninth of January past, and it is not the day the work was done, and I am going to say out loud why it is on there, because this yard has not done this before and I am not going to do it quietly.** The twenty-ninth of January is the day a man of about thirty-four stood at the end of that slip and asked out loud to be asked whether he would hold one of three channels, and was not asked, and the whole of that slip knew he had not been asked. That is a day something was refused, and this yard prices a day something was refused. It is the first line in this sheet that carries a day which is not the day the work was done, and it will not be the last, and every time there is one I am going to say out loud what the day is before I say the figure, and if a man in this yard ever puts a day on a line that is not the day the work was done and does not say what that day is, the line is off the sheet."

Nobody asked her a second question about it and nobody thanked her and the line went onto that sheet in about nine people and it is the hundred and sixty-ninth penny to go onto it since the twenty-ninth day of December of the year before last and there have been two lines on that sheet in this year and one of them is that one.

---

And when the light had gone off that water at about half past five, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and got out in about a hundred and fifty-eight words what a sheet that moved means, and nobody agreed with him and nobody argued him.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. It moved this afternoon by one hundred and sixty-nine pence, being one mark and ninepence, and what it moved by is a sheet and a day of a man who mends nets, and not one person is in it. **A man said no out loud in the open in front of nine people and then said why out loud in the open in front of the same nine people, and the no went onto a page in a hand that was not his, and the page is not on that bench stone and does not carry an hour. Two things were put on that paper today and one of them was a figure and one of them was a date that is not the day the work was done, and I would rather have that sheet than a clean one.**"

Nothing further was asked of anybody on that Saturday and nothing standing in that harbor was touched, and not one voice in that shed or on that slip managed the words for a thing handed over, and the page stayed in the hand of the man of about forty-six and went home with him.

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO ASKED A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR, OUT LOUD, IN THE OPEN, IN FRONT OF THE SAME NINE PEOPLE, WHETHER HE WOULD HOLD ONE OF THREE CHANNELS AND WHETHER HE SAID YES OR NO, AS HE SAID ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JANUARY THAT HE WOULD, AND HE DID. THE ANSWER WAS NO, IN ONE WORD. NOBODY ARGUED HIM OUT OF IT AND THE MAN WHO ASKED SAID NOTHING BACK AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO ASK AGAIN. THE ASKING AND THE ANSWER WERE WRITTEN OUT ON A PAGE IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE MAN'S, AND THAT PAGE IS NOT A PAGE OF HOURS AND IS NOT ON THAT BENCH STONE AND CARRIES NO HOUR. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR SAID UNASKED, IN ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIX WORDS, WHY, AND WAS NOT ASKED WHY EITHER BEFORE OR AFTER. ONE LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-NINE PENCE, BEING ONE MARK AND NINEPENCE, CARRYING TWO DAYS ON ITS FACE, THE FIFTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JANUARY PAST, THE SECOND BEING THE DAY SOMETHING WAS REFUSED, AND THE PERSON WHOSE YARD IT IS SAID BOTH DAYS OUT LOUD BEFORE THE FIGURE. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the fifth day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the man of about thirty-two asked the man of about thirty-four of the second lay at about one o'clock in the afternoon, out loud, in the open, at the end of that slip, in front of the same nine people, whether he would hold one of three channels and whether he said yes or no, and that he said both of those things and added nothing to them, and that he had said on the twenty-ninth of January that he would not do it that day and that when he did it would be in the open in front of the same nine people and on a page in a hand that was not the man's own and that he would not ask twice if the first time was a no, and that all three of those were as he had said they would be; and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay said no in one word and that nobody argued him out of it and that the man of about thirty-two said nothing back and said out loud and once that he was not going to ask him again; and that a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay wrote the asking and the answer out at about half past one on a page in that shed in a hand that is not the hand of the man of about thirty-four, and that it is not a page of hours, that it carries no hour against anything, that it does not go on that bench stone beside the other two, and that the only three things on it are the asking, the answer and the hour at the top of it, and that the man of about thirty-four asked for it to be read out to him before it was put down and it was read out to him and he said it was right and the man of about forty-six did not read it out to anybody else and did not offer it to anybody; and that the man of about thirty-four said out loud, unasked, in about two hundred and twenty-six words, that a channel is a name on a page, and that if he says yes then his name is at the top of a page he cannot read, and that he wants to be asked in words in a room where people can hear the answer, and that the no goes on the page and a yes does not; and that nobody asked him why and nobody thanked him; and that one line went onto that sheet at about four in the afternoon at one hundred and sixty-nine pence, being one mark and ninepence, being one sheet bought blank at a penny, being one penny, and one day asked for by name at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, for the man of about forty-six who wrote the page out, and that one and one hundred and sixty-eight is one hundred and sixty-nine, and that that line is dated the fifth of this month and settled on the fifth of this month and carries a second day, being the twenty-ninth of January past, being the day a man of about thirty-four asked out loud to be asked and was not asked, and that the person whose yard it is said both days out loud in that yard before a figure was written against either of them and said out loud that this is the first line in that sheet carrying a day which is not the day the work was done and that every time there is one both days will be said out loud before the figure; and that the fourteen days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and eight and one thousand four hundred and fourteen and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those fourteen days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day except that one line.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the twenty-ninth of January is on that line is the person whose yard it is's and not his, and is that a yard which prices what it costs has two kinds of day and has been quietly using one of them for a year, being the day the work was done, and that the twenty-ninth of January is the day the asking of a man of about thirty-four was refused by nobody at all, and that a refusal by nobody is the cheapest thing that has happened in that harbor this month and it is going to be the most expensive thing anybody in it ever pays for; and that the reason the page is not on that bench stone is the man of about forty-six's, and is that a bench stone is a public thing and a man asked to be asked in front of nine people did not ask to become a public thing, and that a man of about thirty-two wrote thirteen days of rules about who may stop a thing and was wrong about all thirteen of them in the same way, and that the only instrument that has worked this month in that harbor is a person asking another person a question out loud; and that the reason he said the sheet is worth more dirty is his own, and is that a sheet with two days on one of its lines is a sheet that admits out loud what kind of book it is, and that a sheet where every line is the day the work was done is a sheet that cannot carry a refusal at all, and that a thing that cannot carry a refusal is going to be given to somebody one day as a way of not carrying one, and that this yard has eleven of those refusals on it already and no figures at all; and that the reason nobody said anything about two hundred and six words of chalk on a wall in that harbor on Wednesday is his own, and is that the man of about twenty-seven wrote them for nothing and asked for nothing and that the only entry for that is in the second column of Wednesday and not in this one, and that the first column takes the day and the day was a nail and a page and a basket.*
