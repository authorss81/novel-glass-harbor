# Chapter 589

*Tuesday the First Day of March, the Seventy-First Wage Tuesday, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Wage Column on the Step at the Back of That Market Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes Before About Forty People and Not Moving at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-First Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of the Day, a Man of About Forty-Seven Who Makes Barrels at the Top of Fish Street Saying Out Loud at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon in That Shed in About Nine People What a Failure Is in a Harbor, in About Two Hundred and Seventy-Nine Words and Twice Through Because a Man of About Thirty-Three Asked for the Second Part Again, and Not a Speech, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Answering in About One Hundred and Sixty-Six Words a Question Nobody Asked, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Three Hundred and Fifty-Three Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the first day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at two o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Tuesday was the seventy-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing. The wage column in that harbor was read out at about ten in the morning and did not move, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at the close of the said day, being the seventy-first. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence has not come down that board since the first of December and nothing standing in that harbor was paid or advanced or cut back or bettered or struck or closed. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and the bag at the close of the said day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, more than that bag holds. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninety-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and eleven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-nine days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is a day and a night of a boat from that coast and comes up it on the morning after, and nobody in that harbor has said out loud on any day this month that the returns of that port have never been asked for. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-seven days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The column was read out on that step from ten o'clock in the morning and it took four minutes, and the reading was done by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday and has read it seventy-one times, and about forty people were on the boards and about eleven of them could not see a line.

He read twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks off the lid of that step, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and when he got to the end of the twenty-sixth line he stopped and said the figure and said that the figure had not moved, and that is what he says.

The seventy-first week stands fallen due at the close of that day. It stands behind the seventieth, which stands behind the sixty-ninth, and the only person in that market who said a word about it was the man reading, who says it every Tuesday because it is on his list.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a man of about forty-seven who makes barrels at the top of Fish Street stood up in that shed with about nine people in it and was asked nothing at all, and he said what a failure is in a harbor, in about two hundred and seventy-nine words, and he was interrupted four times and he went back over the second part of it twice because a man of about thirty-three asked for it twice, and it was not a speech and there was no speech in that room that day.

"**A failure is not the thing that went wrong. Anybody can tell another man the thing that went wrong and anybody in this harbor can be argued into agreeing about the thing that went wrong inside a morning.** A failure is a thing that went wrong and comes round again next month with the same face on it and nobody in this harbor able to say whose it is. That is the whole of it and I have made barrels for twenty-six years and that is the only failure I have ever had to sell anybody a replacement for.

On the eleventh of this month there was a number on a slate in that shed with three lines under it that had been counted in three places and three of them were the same. On Thursday a man put a pin out of a clapper arm in four fathoms and the number on that slate is a different one, and a man with a leaf and a pencil can take both off and see that it went up, and I cannot tell anybody a single thing about whose it is and neither can the man who wrote it. **That is what a failure is. Not a bad afternoon. A number that is still there next month and that no man in the room can account for.**

And a failure is not a lesson, and I will say that to the four people in this room who have been saying it is one all month. It is not a lesson because nobody can teach it. There is no apprentice in this harbor who is going to learn anything from Thursday."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked, once, mid-way through that, and then again a minute later, for the second part of it again, and the man of about forty-seven gave it both times in about the same words.

"**There is no man in that room who can say whose it is and there is no man in that room who is going to be able to say on the eleventh of next month, and if there is, he will have had to work it out in thirty days without a single person in this harbor helping him, and that is not a failure, that is Tuesday.**"

Nobody agreed with him out loud, and nobody said that he was wrong, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a light at the top of a point about nine miles up that coast was not in that room and has not been in that room since the winter of the eleventh.

Then a woman of thirty of no office said one thing, and nobody had asked her anything, and it was in about one hundred and sixty-six words.

"**Nobody has asked whether any of it is being paid for. That is a different question from the one that has just been asked four times in that shed and it is the one I came in here with, and I am not going to be here for the rest of the afternoon so I will say it now.** Every one of those three things is free. The slate is free and the wall is free and the form in a coat is free and the pin in a jar is free, and a man of about forty-seven has just told nine people that none of it belongs to anybody, and that is exactly right and it is also the reason it is still there in three weeks when the coal and the victual are somebody's problem. Free is not the same as paid. A harbor full of free things that are all in the same few hands is not a harbor that has learned anything."

Nobody took her up, and the man of about forty-seven sat down, and about four people left that shed at about half past two and about nine people stayed and nobody said anything else about it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about three hundred and fifty-three words on a man of about forty-seven, and nobody in that slip was asked who that failure belonged to.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved since Saturday afternoon, and the column was read out on that step at ten and did not move, and the seventy-first week stands fallen due behind it, and that has happened seventy-one times.

**A man who makes barrels stood up in that shed this afternoon and said out loud the thing that this harbor has been doing for eleven weeks without putting it on a page, and he said it in four sentences and was interrupted four times and it is not on that sheet and it is not on any sheet in this harbor, and there is no figure anywhere in this country for what a failure is.** I have had a page of two communities on that bench stone since the sixteenth with a figure on it about how long an answer takes, and a slate with five lines on it since the eleventh, and a bill on a sheet since Saturday with six figures and a sum, and this afternoon a man who has never been on the water in his life put the whole of those four things into one sentence in about nine people and nobody wrote it down and nobody will repeat it accurately and I am not going to try.

The four hundred and fifty pence has not come down that board. The seventy-first week stands behind the seventieth. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office is not paid and not advanced and not reduced. The third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and there is no date against it anywhere in this harbor and I am not going to put one on it, and there is a pin in a jar on that shelf and a wedge on that bench stone and eight lines of chalk on a wall across the lane and none of the four of them is a record and none of the four of them is on that sheet."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for the four sentences, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip and nobody was on it.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE STEP AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES AND STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-FIRST WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SEVEN WHO MAKES BARRELS AT THE TOP OF FISH STREET STOOD UP IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON UNASKED, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-NINE WORDS WHAT A FAILURE IS IN A HARBOR, BEING THAT A FAILURE IS NOT THE THING THAT WENT WRONG BUT A THING THAT WENT WRONG AND COMES ROUND AGAIN NEXT MONTH WITH THE SAME FACE ON IT AND THAT NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR CAN ACCOUNT FOR, AND THAT A FAILURE IS NOT A LESSON BECAUSE NOBODY CAN TEACH IT AND THERE IS NO APPRENTICE IN THAT HARBOR WHO IS GOING TO LEARN ANYTHING FROM THURSDAY, AND HE WAS INTERRUPTED FOUR TIMES AND HE GAVE THE SECOND PART OF IT TWICE BECAUSE A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE ASKED FOR IT TWICE. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE THEN ANSWERED, UNASKED, IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIX WORDS, A QUESTION NOBODY HAD PUT, BEING WHETHER ANY OF IT IS BEING PAID FOR, AND SAID THAT FREE IS NOT THE SAME AS PAID. NOBODY AGREED WITH HIM OUT LOUD AND NOBODY SAID THAT HE WAS WRONG AND NOBODY WAS THANKED FOR THE FOUR SENTENCES. NOTHING WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY AND THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE DID NOT COME DOWN ANY BOARD. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS.**

and that the wage column was read out on the step at the back of that market at about ten in the morning in four minutes before about forty people, of whom about eleven could not see a line, by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who has read it seventy-one times, and that it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and that nobody was paid a penny out of it, and that the seventy-first week stands fallen due at the close of the said day behind the seventieth, and that the only person in that market who said a word about it was the man reading; and that at about two o'clock in the afternoon a man of about forty-seven who makes barrels at the top of Fish Street stood up in that shed with about nine people in it and was asked nothing at all and said what a failure is in a harbor, in about two hundred and seventy-nine words, and was interrupted four times and went back over the second part of it twice at the asking of a man of about thirty-three, and that it was not a speech and there was no speech in that room that day; and that what he said is that a failure is not the thing that went wrong and that anybody in that harbor can be argued into agreeing about the thing that went wrong inside a morning, and that a failure is a thing that went wrong and comes round again next month with the same face on it and nobody in that harbor able to say whose it is, and that in twenty-six years of making barrels that is the only failure he has ever had to sell anybody a replacement for, and that on the eleventh of this month there was a number on a slate in that shed with three lines under it that had been counted in three places and three of them were the same, and that on Thursday a man put a pin out of a clapper arm in four fathoms and the number on that slate is a different one, and that a man with a leaf and a pencil can take both off and see that it went up, and that he cannot tell anybody a single thing about whose it is and neither can the man who wrote it, and that that is what a failure is and not a bad afternoon but a number that is still there next month and that no man in the room can account for, and that a failure is not a lesson and that he said that to the four people who have been saying it is one all month, and that it is not a lesson because nobody can teach it and there is no apprentice in that harbor who is going to learn anything from Thursday; and that a woman of thirty of no office then said one thing that nobody had asked her, in about one hundred and sixty-six words, being that nobody has asked whether any of it is being paid for and that that is a different question from the one asked four times in that shed and is the one she came in with, and that every one of those three things is free, and that the slate is free and the wall is free and the form in a coat is free and the pin in a jar is free, and that a man of about forty-seven has just told nine people that none of it belongs to anybody and that that is exactly right and is also the reason it is still there in three weeks when the coal and the victual are somebody's problem, and that free is not the same as paid, and that a harbor full of free things that are all in the same few hands is not a harbor that has learned anything; and that nobody took her up and that the man of about forty-seven sat down and that about four people left that shed at about half past two and about nine people stayed and nobody said anything else about it; and that the thirty-nine days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and twenty-two and one thousand four hundred and eighty-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those thirty-nine days.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I put the seventy-first week in as a week and not as a figure is mine and is that a figure of weeks is a figure that goes up by itself every Tuesday without anybody deciding anything, and that a man who writes it as a number every week is a man who has started to be the author of it, and that a week is a thing that happens to that column and not a thing anybody in this harbor has done; and that I have put the number of it in the first column twice in two months because the second of those two is the day somebody will want to argue with, and that is the only reason a number is ever worth the ink; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I did not write down one word of what the man of about forty-seven said is mine and is that he said it out loud in a room and I have been writing down things said out loud in rooms for twenty days and have printed a hundred and fifty of them, and that this is the first one this month I could not put on a page without making it smaller than it was, and that a man of about forty-seven said four sentences and was interrupted four times, and that the four interruptions are in it, and that if I had written it down I would have had to choose which four interruptions and that is a thing this harbor has no business doing to a working man's afternoon; and that at about ten to three the reason a woman of thirty of no office said the thing nobody asked is hers and is that she has been in eleven rooms this month where a figure was read out and every one of those rooms was full of people who own a thing that cost money, and that she is the one person in this harbor whose work this month has not cost anybody anything, and that a man who makes barrels has just told nine people that a thing nobody owns is the definition of a failure, and that the two of those are the same fact said from opposite ends, and that she is not going to argue about it in a shed at half past two on a Tuesday; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have not written the free things onto that sheet is not pride and is arithmetic, and is that if a thing that costs nobody anything goes onto a sheet, then a person who is asked to pay for the next thing has a precedent on that sheet, and that in six weeks a man in this harbor will be able to point at the slate and the wall and the jar and say that the last four things this party did cost nothing, and that what it actually says is that they cost nothing to the people who were not there; and that at about ten past six the reason there is no answer on that bench stone tonight to what that failure belongs to is that there is not one, and that the reason there is not one is on the face of the fifth line of that slate and in the second of those six lines on that wall across the lane, and that I have had both of those on paper in front of me for a week and this month and that a man of about forty-seven is the first person in that harbor who has said the two of them out loud without being asked and that I am not going to improve on him in a slip at half past five.*
