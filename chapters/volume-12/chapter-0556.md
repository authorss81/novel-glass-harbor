# Chapter 556

*Thursday the Twenty-Seventh Day of January, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, One Man over the Rail off the First Mark at About Ten Past Two in the Afternoon and on the Floor at About a Quarter Past Two and His Helmet Off at About Thirty-Three Minutes Past Two and In at About Thirty-Five Minutes Past Two, the Air on That Bottle at Two Hundred and Eighty Pence a Man, a Man of About Twenty-Eight of the Third Lay Named as the Tender on the Surface With His Own Hand on the Chain Weight and a Cutoff He Can Pull Without Asking Anybody's Leave, a Reason on a Slate in His Own Words With the Hour Against It, a Line Going Onto That Sheet at About Four in the Afternoon at Four Hundred and Forty-Eight Pence, Being Two Marks, Ten Shillings and Eightpence, With the Twenty-Seventh Against It and Settled on the Twenty-Seventh, a Thing That Can Be Stopped by Any One of Three People Printed on the Face of the Page of Hours and the Other Half of It Printed Beside It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the twenty-seventh day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in about four fathoms off the first mark on the same water.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twentieth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern at about eight in the morning was not used for anything at any hour of that day. One line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about four in the afternoon at four hundred and forty-eight pence, being two marks, ten shillings and eightpence, with the twenty-seventh of this month against it and settled on the twenty-seventh. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixtieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-six days. The page of hours that went out on that water this afternoon carries two names on it and no more than two, and the two names are the only names on it, and the rule about who may stop it is printed on the face of it in the same hand as the hours and is not smaller than the hours and names three people that page does not carry, and a second page went on that bench stone beside the first one on the Friday and carried three names and the third rule copied out from the first. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-four days and he did not see the boat go out and did not hear about it until the Wednesday and nobody told him on purpose.**

---

There was no water in the butt behind that market at eight o'clock on that Thursday and there was no water in it at four in the afternoon either, and the two men who carried the eleven trips up that ladder had been at it since a quarter to eight and were finished by about a quarter past nine. Neither of those two has ever been on that rota board, which has had a name at the head of it left off since the spring, and neither of them has ever asked to be put on it.

---

And the page of hours went on the bench stone at about one o'clock in the afternoon in a hand that is not the man of about thirty-two's, being a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay writing out what a man of about thirty-eight said to him, and the man of about thirty-eight who said it is not on that water and is not in that slip and has not been asked about it.

The hours on that page are one descent, and the hours run from about ten past two in the afternoon to about thirty-five minutes past two, and against each of the six lines there is an hour. There is the name of the man who goes over the rail and there is the name of the man who stays in the boat, and they are two men and no one of them is a member of anything. The third rule on that page names three people and that page does not carry their names, and it did not carry them when it went out on the water, and a page of hours that names three people it does not carry has said out loud on its own face that nobody is going to be able to keep it running that night, and the man who wrote it out said so out loud on the quay and was not asked about it.

The reason is on a slate in the words of the man who is going down, and it is four lines, and the hour is at the foot of it.

"One shackle and one link of chain on the stone at the first mark. The stone is under about four fathoms and has been there since before any of us. I am going to look at the ring on the chain and take a shackle off it and put a new one on and come back up. Nobody is to touch that mark tonight and the chain is not to be pulled."

Three rules are printed at the head of that page and the third of them is the one that matters and it is the one that is hardest.

1. *One person in the water. Not two at any hour and not the same man twice in one day.*
2. *An independent line and a chain weight on the bottom, and a cutoff the man in the boat can pull without asking anybody's leave and without saying a word first.*
3. *Any one of three named people may stop this. A thing that can be stopped by any one of three people is also a thing that cannot be kept running by anybody if those three are not in the room. Neither half of that is fixed and both halves are printed here on the same day.*

Nobody in that yard could tell the man of about thirty-two which of the two halves of the third rule was the true one and nobody asked him, and the three names the rule asks for went onto the second page on the Friday and not onto this one, and this page has not been taken down and does not carry them and is not going to.

---

And the man of about twenty-eight of the third lay took that boat out at about ten past one in the afternoon and stood off the first mark in about four fathoms and put his own hand on the chain weight and kept it there, and the top of the flood that afternoon was about half past one and the man of about thirty-two went over the rail at about ten past two, which is after it, and he was on the floor at about a quarter past two and his helmet was off at about thirty-three minutes past two and he was in at about thirty-five minutes past two, and that is five going down and eighteen on the floor and two coming up, and five and eighteen and two is twenty-five and it is twenty-five of twenty-five, and he did not ask for his ascent and nobody argued him out of anything.

He came up with a new shackle on a chain that has been in that water longer than anybody in that boat, and he said one thing about it on the quay at about half past three in the afternoon in about forty-six words and nobody thanked him and nobody set a figure against the hour he spent in that water.

"**There is no second man down there. That is the whole of what today was, and I went down alone and I came up alone and a thing that is done by one man is a thing that one man can be told to stop doing.**"

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud and once that three bells had moved nine boats in this harbor in the middle of January and that one man with a shackle had done less than a gull. He was answered in about thirteen words and was not answered again and was not thanked for asking.

"Then do it with three and see what a gull thinks of it."

---

And at about four in the afternoon the person whose yard it is put one line onto that sheet and said the whole of it out loud before she wrote a figure against it, in about nine people, and one of the two figures on that line is air.

"One man, one descent, one bottle. Two hundred and eighty pence a man, being one times two hundred and eighty is two hundred and eighty, being one mark and ten shillings, and that is the figure that bottle came in at on the thirtieth of December and it has not come down since. A shackle and a link of chain and three fathoms of line out of the store at the back of this yard, one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence. **That is four hundred and forty-eight pence, being two marks, ten shillings and eightpence, and the twenty-seventh is against that line and it is settled on the twenty-seventh and there are two days on it because they are the same day and I am printing them both.**"

Somebody asked her, once, whether the day the man of about thirty-two spent in that water was going on that sheet, and she said no and said why in about eighty-six words and did not say it again.

"His hours are not on that sheet because that sheet is a record of what a yard costs and not of what a man is worth, and because he is not mine and does not work for me. **If I put a man's hours on that sheet then a man who works for nothing would have to be on it, and then somebody would ask me for a figure for his hours, and I have not got one and I am not going to invent one.**"

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and the sheet had moved for the first time in ten days and he said what it moved by.

"Ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence. It moved this afternoon by four hundred and forty-eight pence, being a bottle of air and a shackle and a link of chain and three fathoms of line, and not one of those four is a person. One hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four out and ten thousand six hundred and forty in, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree. **And nothing on that sheet settles the question on that mole, and nothing on that sheet prices what three people holding one thing each is worth, and the page of hours for today is in that shed with two names on it and both halves of its own rule printed on it and the three names the rule asks for not on it at all, and neither half of that rule has been tested by anybody.**"

Nobody was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Thursday, and nobody was thanked for the descent and nobody was thanked for the twenty-five minutes of air and nobody was thanked for the forty-six words on the quay at half past three.

---

**ONE MAN WENT INTO ABOUT FOUR FATHOMS OFF THE FIRST MARK AT ABOUT TEN PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND CAME UP AT ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE MINUTES PAST TWO, WITH A NAMED TENDER ON THE SURFACE WITH HIS OWN HAND ON THE CHAIN WEIGHT, AN INDEPENDENT LINE, A CUTOFF HE COULD PULL WITHOUT ASKING, A REASON ON A SLATE IN HIS OWN WORDS WITH AN HOUR AGAINST IT, AND NOBODY IN THAT WATER TWICE AND NOBODY IN IT AT THE TOP OF A FLOOD. THE PAGE OF HOURS CARRIED TWO NAMES AND A RULE PRINTED ON THE FACE OF IT THAT NAMES THREE PEOPLE IT DOES NOT CARRY, BEING THAT ANY ONE OF THREE MAY STOP IT AND THAT A THING LIKE THAT CANNOT BE KEPT RUNNING BY ANYBODY IF THOSE THREE ARE NOT IN THE ROOM, AND BOTH HALVES OF IT ARE PRINTED AND NEITHER IS FIXED. ONE LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AT FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO MARKS, TEN SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND NOT ONE PERSON IS IN IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-seventh day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twentieth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and that the water that went into that cistern was not used for anything at any hour of that day; and that one line went onto that sheet at about four in the afternoon at four hundred and forty-eight pence, being two marks, ten shillings and eightpence, being air for one man for one descent at two hundred and eighty pence a man, being one times two hundred and eighty is two hundred and eighty, being one mark and ten shillings, and a shackle and a link of chain and three fathoms of line out of the store at the back of that yard at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and that the twenty-seventh of this month is against that line and that it was settled on the twenty-seventh and that both days are on the face of the one line; and that the hours of that descent were from about ten past two in the afternoon to about thirty-five minutes past two, being five going down, eighteen on the floor and two coming up, being five and eighteen and two is twenty-five of twenty-five, and that a man of about twenty-eight of the third lay was the tender on the surface with his own hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave, and that the top of that flood was about half past one and that nobody went in at the top of it, and that there was an independent line and a reason on a slate in the words of the man going down with an hour at the foot of it, and that one person was in that water and the same man was not in it twice; and that a page of hours went on that bench stone at about one o'clock in the afternoon carrying two names and no more than two and a rule printed on the face of it in the same hand as the hours and not smaller than the hours, being that any one of three named people may stop it, and that page not carrying the three the rule asks for and a second page going on beside it on the Friday to carry them and that a thing that can be stopped by any one of three is also a thing that cannot be kept running by anybody if those three are not in the room, and that both halves of that rule are on the same page on the same day and that neither half of it is fixed; and that the man of about thirty-two said out loud at about half past three in the afternoon on that quay in about forty-six words that there was no second man in that water and that a thing done by one man is a thing one man can be told to stop doing, and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said once that three bells had moved nine boats in that harbor in the middle of January and was answered in about thirteen words and was not answered again; and that the person whose yard it is said out loud in about nine people that the hours of the man of about thirty-two are not on that sheet and gave the reason in about eighty-six words, and that no figure was set against the hour any person spent in that water; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the third rule on that page of hours was written out in full before anybody went over the rail is the man of about thirty-eight's, and is that a rule which can be stopped by one of three people is a rule that three people have to be in the room for, and that any page of hours that says so is a page that is telling every reader of it that it will not run tonight, and that a page which tells its own reader that it will not run is the only kind of page this coast has ever put a rule on; and that the reason the hours of the man of about thirty-two are not on that sheet is hers, and is that a sheet with money on it cannot carry what a man is, and that the moment a man's hours go on it, a man is a line, and that a line can be added to; and that the reason the man of about twenty-eight pulled nothing this afternoon is his own, and is that he had his hand on that weight for about an hour and a quarter and there was nothing to pull and that a man who has never pulled a cutoff in a boat does not know whether he could have, and that the only way to find out is a day when there is something, and that he does not want that day this month and did not say so to anybody.*
