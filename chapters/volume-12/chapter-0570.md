# Chapter 570

*Thursday the Tenth Day of February, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Twenty-Fourth Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Clerk of That Harbor Authority Answering in the Room over That Market at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon, Out Loud, Once, in Front of About Nine People, the Question a Woman of Thirty of No Office Asked in That Room on the Twenty-Fourth of January, and Saying What the Answer Costs Before He Says the Answer, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Out Loud in a Second Room at the Top of Fish Street at About Half Past Four in the Afternoon, in Front of About Nine People, in About Thirty Words, What the Price of That Answer Is Paid By, and Nobody Answering Her, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the tenth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in two rooms four hundred yards apart with about nine people in each of them.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-fourth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern at about eight in the morning was not used for anything at any hour of that day. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and ten days. There are five papers on the board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened and the demand for the returns went into a boat on the fourth of this month and a boat takes a day and a night and it is now the sixth day of that sailing. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-eight days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and the two men who carried it up that ladder were at it from a quarter to eight and had finished by about a quarter past nine. The butt behind that market is full to the top of the second hoop and the hose that goes in at the top of it has been dead since the spring.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon the room over the floor of that market was let out and about nine people went up those stairs, and the six lines were on the wall of that room in a large hand with a ring of black chalk at the foot of them, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room.

A clerk of that harbor authority, who is the man who read the first sealed sheet on the step on the twenty-fourth of January and who put his own name and the hour at the foot of that reading, said what he wanted before he said anything else, and what he wanted was for the five papers on the board at the head of that mole to be gone through in the open, one at a time, in a room, with a person reading each of them out, and that nobody had asked him to do that and that he could not do it on his own because he is the clerk and not the harbor.

Then he answered the question a woman of thirty of no office asked in that room on the twenty-fourth of January, out loud, once, in two hundred and twenty-six words, in front of about nine people, and he said what the answer costs before he said the answer, and he named no name and read nothing out of any paper and stopped at nothing.

"I read that paper on the step on the twenty-fourth and I have been in this room for every reading of it since, so I am the person that question went to. **This harbor accepts that paper as its account of the fourteenth.**

And here is what that costs, and I am putting it before the answer and not after it. It costs the wall at the top of Fish Street. Four lines of correction and one line of a man's own refusal are on the end of a joiner's yard and they are the only thing on this coast a man can stand under and read. The moment that paper is the account, a correction is a thing a person puts against an account, and there is no account left to put it against, and that man's refusal stops being his own business and becomes an argument about his crossings and his daughter's name. And the sixth of those six lines, which is the only one of the six with anybody in it, stops being an offer, because a particular is only a particular if there is something for it to differ from.

That is the price. It is not money and I have not got a figure for it, and the column does not move on Tuesday, and six lines do not pay anybody."

Nobody answered him. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands was at the back of that room and said nothing at all, and a man of about fifty-three was not in that room and nobody went to tell him anything. The woman of thirty of no office stood under the wall her own question had been asked under seventeen days before and said nothing, and she did not say whether she thought it was the right answer, and she was not thanked, and she did not thank him.

---

And at about half past four in the afternoon she went up to the top of Fish Street and into the room over the joiner's shop, and about nine people were in it, and the man of about thirty-five of the first lay was in it with his own hand at the foot of the fifth of the five lines of chalk on the wall of the passage underneath.

She said one thing, and it was thirty words, and it was not said to the man of about thirty-five and it was not said to anybody in particular, and about nine people heard it, and four of them cannot read the wall underneath them.

"**The price of that is paid by people who are not in the room, and they cannot be told what they are paying, and it goes on all the same.**"

Nobody answered it. Nobody argued with it. The man of about thirty-five of the first lay stood in that room for about a quarter of an hour afterwards and did not say a word about it, and he has not said a word about it since, and nobody in that harbor has asked him about it. A man of about forty-six who mends nets said one thing afterwards and it was that he was not going to be the man who answered that, and he was not thanked for saying it and nobody asked him why not.

Nothing in that room was resolved on that afternoon, and the six lines stayed on the wall of the room over that market, and the five lines stayed on the wall of the passage at the top of Fish Street, and the two of them were four hundred yards apart and neither of them had changed.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday, four hundred yards short of the room at the top of Fish Street, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and took about a hundred and eighty-five words to say what this month's ten days cost, and he did not answer either room and nobody in either room asked him to.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and it is the last day of ten and a further week stands fallen due at the close of it. **A question asked in a room on the twenty-fourth of January was answered out loud in that room this afternoon by a man who was in it, and a woman said out loud in a second room what the answer is paid for, and neither of those two things was answered by anybody and neither of them was thanked, and there is no line on any sheet in this harbor for either of them and there is not going to be one.** The first of those goes in the first column because it is a day with an hour on it. The second one goes in the second column and it stays there, and it is the only sentence anybody has said out loud in ten days that I cannot put a number on and cannot put a wall on and am not going to try."

The ten days of this month from Tuesday the first of February to this day are closed, and no standing item in that harbor was paid or advanced or cut back or bettered or struck or closed in any of them, and neither room nor that shed heard the word for a gift from anybody, and the bag was twenty-eight pence at the close of it.

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOURTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A CLERK OF THAT HARBOR AUTHORITY, WHO WAS IN THE ROOM OVER THAT MARKET ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF JANUARY, ANSWERED THE QUESTION A WOMAN OF THIRTY ASKED IN THAT ROOM, OUT LOUD, ONCE, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, THAT THIS HARBOR ACCEPTS THAT PAPER AS ITS ACCOUNT OF THE FOURTEENTH, AND SAID WHAT THAT COSTS BEFORE HE SAID THE ANSWER, BEING THE WALL AT THE TOP OF FISH STREET AND THE SIXTH OF THOSE SIX LINES. A WOMAN OF THIRTY THEN SAID, IN A SECOND ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY, IN ABOUT THIRTY WORDS, WHAT THE PRICE OF THAT ANSWER IS PAID BY. NOBODY ANSWED HER. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE OF THE FIRST LAY STOOD IN THAT ROOM FOR A QUARTER OF AN HOUR AFTERWARDS AND SAID NOTHING AND HAS SAID NOTHING SINCE. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SEALED SHEETS ARE BOTH STILL ON THAT BOARD AND NEITHER HAS BEEN TAKEN DOWN. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the thirty words of that woman in the room at the top of Fish Street are in here and are not on any page, and that the reason is his own, and is that the first column takes one entry a day and takes the day, and that a thing a woman said in a room about a price is not a day, and that if it went in the first column it would be a day in a column a stranger can count by counting days, and that the whole of what she said is that the price is not paid by the people who can be counted in a room; and that the reason nobody answered her is theirs and not his, and is that about nine people were in that room and about four of them cannot read the wall underneath them and that the sentence she said names the four of them without naming them, and that a room in which somebody has just been described as a person who pays and cannot be told what he pays is not a room anybody is going to argue in, and that the quiet there was not a failure and was the correct answer and he is not going to write anything in this column that makes it sound like a failure; and that the reason the clerk's answer is on the first column and hers is not is his own, and is that a clerk reading a paper on a step with his own name and an hour at the foot of it is a thing with an hour on it and a name against it, and that a thing with an hour and a name can be looked up, and that a woman in a room saying what a price is paid by is a thing that can only be looked at, and that the two of them are the two halves of this month and he is not going to put them in the same column; and that the reason the ten days of this month are closed without a figure on either of them is his own, and is that a day of the berth fund went in and a day went out on every one of the ten and that twenty-eight pence is in the bag and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence and that one thousand six hundred and eighty pence is ten marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that none of those three figures has moved, and that a man said out loud in a room on Wednesday that one book would have put two boats on the bottom, and that the one thing this yard has proved in ten days is that about eleven men who will not agree with each other can be put on a leaf and counted, and that the other thing has not been proved yet and may not be provable at this price, and that he is not going to say that out loud on a slip, and that it is in here and it is not going on a wall.*
