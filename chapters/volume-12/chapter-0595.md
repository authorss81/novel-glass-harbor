# Chapter 595

*Monday the Seventh Day of March, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Thirty-First Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, Seven Lines of a Second Form of a Thing People Can Disagree With in Public Going into a Room Over the Floor of That Market at About Eleven in the Morning and Being Read Out Twice and Signed by Nobody and Agreed to by Nobody, a Woman of About Fifty-Two Who Keeps a Chandler's Counter and Is One of About Thirty People at a Place About Five Miles up That Coast Road Saying Out Loud in About three hundred and seventeen Words That Her Community Wants to Be Asked, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying in About two hundred and forty Words That Wanting to Be Asked and a Person Asking to Be Asked Are Two Different Sentences and That the Second of Those Is on a Sheet in That Room and Is Not Going to Be Asked Again, Three More Gaps Entered at the End Stall in the Afternoon and Nobody Asked to Agree to Any of Them, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About five hundred and forty Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the seventh day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at half past five in the afternoon.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirty-first time a few minutes past eight this morning, in eleven trips by hand and no more, and the covers were back down on it before a quarter past nine. The butt behind that market stood filled to the rim of the second hoop until about four in the afternoon and not one bucket came out of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the seven lines that went into that room at about eleven in the morning are on no sheet in this harbor and are in no minute and carry no signature. At about ten in the morning that yard took in a hundred and sixty-eight pence and at about four in the afternoon it gave a hundred and sixty-eight pence back out, a hundred and sixty-eight pence being one mark and eightpence, and the bag held twenty-eight pence at the close of it, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, behind the wage of four marks. The sheet carries one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and stands ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is in that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and nobody answered it. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no part of the cost of that day was paid out of that bag, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes the whole of a day a woman of about fifty-two spent sleeping in a back room off a chandler's counter because her community has not been asked anything this month.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and ninety-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and thirty-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninety-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and seventeen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventy-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty-five days. Five papers are on the board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is still in that boathouse with the paper in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon are nine hulls. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been ninety-three days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and thirty-first time this morning, in eleven trips by hand, and the butt behind that market stood filled to the rim of the second hoop from about a quarter past nine until about four in the afternoon and not one bucket came out of it, and the hose at the top of that butt is still dead since the spring and there is still no figure anywhere in this harbor for a new length of it.

And at about eleven in the morning seven lines went up on the wall of the room over the floor of that market, under the four heads, in a large hand in chalk, and the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read them out twice in front of about nine people and got nothing wrong on either reading. Nobody asked him to write them and nobody was asked to agree to them.

1. *Any person in this harbor may disagree in public with any thing anybody in this harbor has said out loud, and a disagreement is not a thing anybody has to answer.*
2. *A person may write a disagreement in their own words and in their own hand, and may leave their name off it, and that is not a fault in what they wrote.*
3. *A disagreement may be made on any public wall, on any sheet, in any room that is let out, or on the foot of any page, and none of those places is a record and none of them is in any book.*
4. *Nobody in this harbor loses a berth, a place, a day of work or a market for writing a disagreement or for refusing to answer one, and if any person in this harbor can do that to any other person, then this is not the second form of this, and the person who can do it is the one who has to say so out loud and not this page.*
5. *The four lines of correction and the one line of refusal at the top of Fish Street stay where they are and are not copied onto this wall and not copied onto any other wall.*
6. *The six lines of chalk across the lane from that market are the first form of this and are not superseded by this, and nothing written under that wall has been agreed to by anybody.*
7. *Nobody has agreed to this, and a turn may not be given to anybody who has not asked for one, and this binds the person who writes a disagreement and nobody else.*

Nobody signed it. Nobody thanked the man of about twenty-seven, who wrote the six lines across the lane on the twenty-eighth of last month, because he was not the man who wrote these seven and was not in the room when they went up, and about eleven of the people of that market could not read that wall and were told what was on it.

And a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a chandler's counter and is one of about thirty people at a place about five miles up that coast road, and who came in on Saturday on the cart and is sleeping in a back room off her own counter tonight, stood up at about half past eleven in that room and said out loud, unasked, in about 317 words, that her community wants to be asked.

"**Nobody has asked us anything this month and we are not complaining about it, and I have come five miles on a cart on Saturday to say one sentence and then I will go back on Wednesday.** We are about thirty people and we keep our own hours and we have never once had a page of ours read out in a working place, and two of us cannot read. A community that has sealed everything was asked on Thursday and said no in its own mouth. A community that has permitted everything was asked on Thursday and said yes with a woman's name at the foot of it. **We have not been asked, and we are not asking anybody to feel bad about it, and I am not going to stand here and tell this room that not being asked is the same as being refused.** It is not the same. A refusal is a thing a person says. Not being asked is a thing that happens to about thirty people who are five miles up a coast road while nine people stand in a shed, and I would like somebody to come and ask us, and I would like it to be somebody who is not me, because if I ask us then I have asked us and there is no point in anybody in this room having done anything at all.

I am not going to say we will say yes. I do not know what we will say. I have not asked them, and I am not going to go back up that road and ask them and come back up it again, and if somebody in this room comes up that road with the question, then we can talk about it with all of us in one room instead of me standing here with one sentence and a cart booked for Wednesday."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said one thing about that, out loud, in about 240 words, and he said it to her and not to the rest of that room.

"**Wanting to be asked and a person asking to be asked are two different sentences and I have got both halves of that in my own head this morning and only one of them in my mouth.** You have said the first one and it is yours to say and nobody is entitled to it off you. The second one is on a sheet in this room from the twenty-ninth of January, written out in a hand that is not a clerk's, and a man asked in the open for to be asked and was not asked, and he is not going to be asked by me and he is not going to be asked by anybody in this room, and that is not a punishment, it is a fact about a sheet, and the sheet is over there and it has not moved since the fifth.

You have five miles and a cart and Wednesday. I have two men on that quay who will take a boat up to the top of that point for nothing tomorrow morning and a boat takes a day and a night against four days each way for the cart, and I am not offering that as a fix, I am offering it because a boat exists and a question does not, and that is the whole of the difference between the two sentences and I have been sitting on it for two days."

She looked at him for a while and said that she would take the boat and that she would not take the question with it, because a question and a lift in the same boat is a question people say yes to, and she said that out loud, and nobody argued with her, and about four people in that room said nothing at all and about nine went back down those stairs.

And at about four in the afternoon three more gaps went onto the sheet at the end stall at the back of that market, in the same four lines and the same four headings as Saturday, and one of the three was entered by a man of about twenty-six of the third lay, and one of the three was entered by a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards, and the third of them was entered by a man of about fifty-two who carries a basket down that coast and back and who had not said a word to anybody since Friday, and nobody asked him about the other basket and nobody asked him anything at all.

Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for any part of it. The man of about thirty-three went up that quay at about five o'clock and told the man of about forty-one of the first lay about the boat, and the man of about forty-one said he would take the other side of it and not be in the same boat, and both of those are on no page.

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 540 words on a second form and on a woman five miles up a coast road, and nobody in that slip was asked whether the first form had failed.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet this morning and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and that cistern was charged for the hundred and thirty-first time before the market opened and not one bucket came out of it, and a woman of about fifty-two is sleeping in a back room off a chandler's counter tonight because nobody asked her community anything this month.

**Seven lines went up on the wall of the room over the floor of that market at about eleven this morning and they are the second form of a thing people can be disagreed on in public, and nobody signed them and nobody agreed to them and the fourth of them says that if any person in this harbor can take a day off another person for writing a disagreement down, then this is not the second form and the person who can do it has to say so out loud and not on a page.** The sixth of them says the six lines of chalk across the lane are the first form and are not superseded by anything, and the seventh says that nobody has agreed to it and that it binds the person who writes a disagreement and nobody else. That is a better set of lines than anything this harbor has had on a wall and it cost nothing and about eleven people of that market cannot read either set.

A woman of about fifty-two stood up in that room at about half past eleven and said out loud that her community wants to be asked, and she said it in about 317 words and she was not asked for it, and a man of about thirty-three said out loud in about 240 words that wanting to be asked and a person asking to be asked are two different sentences, and that the second of those is on a sheet in that room from the twenty-ninth of January past and is not going to be asked again by anybody in that room. **A boat is going up to the top of that point in the morning, two men in her, no question in her, and that is not a fix and it is not on a page and it is not on any sheet in this harbor.**

Three more gaps went onto that sheet at the end stall at four o'clock and the last of those three was entered by a man who has said nothing to anybody since Friday and who was not asked about the other basket and was not asked anything at all. Nothing was entered on the third column today and nothing on the fourth, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and I entered no reason for that on any column. I entered one line on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and neither of the first two figures of that leaf has been read out loud in any room this month, and that is the forty-fifth day of it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for seven lines, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the seven lines were on the wall under the four heads with nothing under them.

THE FOURTH OF THOSE SEVEN LINES SAYS THAT NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR LOSES A BERTH, A PLACE, A DAY OF WORK OR A MARKET FOR WRITING A DISAGREEMENT OR FOR REFUSING TO ANSWER ONE, AND THAT IF ANY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR CAN DO THAT TO ANY OTHER PERSON THEN THIS IS NOT THE SECOND FORM OF THIS AND THE PERSON WHO CAN DO IT HAS TO SAY SO OUT LOUD AND NOT ON THAT PAGE. THE SEVENTH SAYS THAT NOBODY HAS AGREED TO IT AND THAT A TURN MAY NOT BE GIVEN TO ANYBODY WHO HAS NOT ASKED FOR ONE. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**
