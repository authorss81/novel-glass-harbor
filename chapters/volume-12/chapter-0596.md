# Chapter 596

*Tuesday the Eighth Day of March, the Seventy-Second Wage Tuesday, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Wage Column on the Step at the Back of That Market Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes Before About Forty People and Not Moving at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventy-Second Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of the Day, the Person Whose Yard It Is Pricing What One Permitted Account Costs on a Page at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon in About three hundred and fifty-five Words, in Hours and Material With the Multiplication Printed at Every Figure, Being Twenty-Four Minutes, Twelve Minutes, Four Hours and Twenty-Two Minutes and Two Hundred and Ninety-Eight Minutes in All, Being Four Hours and Fifty-Eight Minutes, and Two Sheets, One Stick of Chalk and One Slate, and No Figure of Money Against Any of It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About five hundred and thirty-four Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the eighth day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at two o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Tuesday was the seventy-second wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing, and the covers were on it all day. The wage column in that harbor was read out at about ten in the morning and did not move, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at the close of the said day, being the seventy-second. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the page that went onto that bench stone at about two in the afternoon is on no sheet in this harbor and is in no minute and is not a line on anything. A hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, came into that yard in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in the afternoon, and at the close of the said day the bag held twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so the bag is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of it. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is written in that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and no person answered it. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and nothing in the cost of that day was paid out of that bag, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes the whole of four hours and fifty-eight minutes of one man's hand and one woman's.**

**Nine figures in that harbor can be had off a wall by anybody who goes and looks for them, and these are the nine of them, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and ninety-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and thirty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the four hundredth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and eighteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventy-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty-six days. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and neither of the two that are sealed has been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is in that boathouse with the paper in it. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been ninety-four days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The column was read out on that step from ten o'clock in the morning and it took four minutes, and the reading was done by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday and has read it seventy-two times, and about forty people were on the boards and about eleven of them could not see a line.

He read twenty-six lines off the lid of that step and the last one came to one hundred and four marks, and there he stopped, and he said the figure and he said that the figure had not moved, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and that is what he says.

The seventy-second week stands fallen due at the close of that day. It stands behind the seventy-first, which stands behind the seventieth, and the only person in that market who said a word about it was the man reading.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon the person whose yard it is put a page on that bench stone and priced one permitted account in it, out loud, in about 355 words, with the multiplication printed at every figure, in hours and in material, and not one figure of money against any part of it.

The four lines and the multiplication printed at each of them are these.

1. *Four gaps, each with four things against it, six minutes a gap to write in a hand a stranger can read at arm's length. Four times six is twenty-four minutes.*
2. *The same four read out twice in a room of about nine people, six minutes a reading. Two times six is twelve minutes.*
3. *One hour a week for four weeks answering any question anybody in that community asks about it. Four times one hour is four hours, being two hundred and forty minutes.*
4. *The walk to that end stall and back, twice, eleven minutes a way. Two times eleven is twenty-two minutes.*

Twenty-four and twelve and twenty-two is fifty-eight minutes, and fifty-eight and two hundred and forty is two hundred and ninety-eight minutes, and two hundred and ninety-eight is four hours and fifty-eight minutes. The material is two sheets bought blank, being two times one sheet, being two sheets, and one stick of chalk, being one, and one slate, being one, and that slate is already on that bench stone and was washed on Saturday.

"**That is what one permitted account costs one community for the first month, and it is in hours and in material, and there is no figure of money against any part of it, and none of it goes onto that sheet.** A day asked for by name is a day somebody sold and it is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of this yard at four o'clock, and it is not on that sheet and it is not on this page, and the two of those are separate facts and I am not going to hold them together for anybody's convenience. If this harbor ever wants a permitted account for itself, then somebody in this harbor is going to lose four hours and fifty-eight minutes of a hand and a walk, and I am telling the nine people in this shed that now and not in six weeks.

**And here is the part I have not said out loud in eleven weeks and I am saying it on a Tuesday because a Tuesday is the day this harbor finds out what it owes.** A resolution that cost somebody something is on no line of that sheet, and it is on no line of it because that sheet is a list of what people did with their own money. A resolution was not bought by anybody. On the fourteenth of last month a man refused an instrument for his own mark and it cost him three days and seven hours and twelve minutes of a season, and three marks and two shillings of that can be sold and the rest of it cannot be sold by anybody at any price in this country, and it is not on that sheet and it is not going on that sheet, and if I put it on that sheet then the six people who keep that mark look like six people who paid for a result, and the result looks like a thing this yard can sell, and I have already sold a shipyard once in my life and I am not doing it again with somebody else's season."

Nobody argued with her. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question, which was whether the four hours and fifty-eight minutes came out of the man who kept the crossing column, and she said that it did, and that it was the same man, and that he had been told about it on Sunday, and that nobody had asked him whether he would do it.

Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for the pricing of it. That page went behind that press at about half past three with the multiplication on it, and the four figures and the sum are the only figures on it, and none of the four is a figure of money.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 534 words on a page of hours that had no money on it, and nobody in that slip was asked what a permitted account is worth.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet today and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and the column was read out on that step at ten and stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the seventy-second week stands behind the seventy-first, and there are twenty envelopes in a chest that has been shut from ten until four today and every Tuesday since the twenty-fifth of January.

**The person whose yard it is priced one permitted account this afternoon at about 355 words, in hours and in material, and put it on that bench stone with the multiplication printed at every figure: four times six minutes is twenty-four, two times six is twelve, four times one hour is two hundred and forty minutes, and two times eleven is twenty-two, and twenty-four and twelve and twenty-two is fifty-eight, and fifty-eight and two hundred and forty is two hundred and ninety-eight minutes, being four hours and fifty-eight minutes of one man's hand and one man's walk.** Two sheets and one stick of chalk and one slate. There is not one figure of money against any part of it and not one penny of it is going onto that sheet, and a day asked for by name is still a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard at four o'clock and still is not a line on that sheet.

She said the other thing out loud at about twenty past two, and she said it in front of nine people, and it is the first time in eleven weeks that anybody in this yard has said why a resolution that cost somebody something is on no line of that sheet, and the reason is that that sheet is a list of what people did with their own money and a resolution was not bought by anybody, and that putting one on it would make six people who keep a mark look like six people who paid for a result. **A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud on the twenty-first of last month that a page with no name on it can be argued with and held to by nobody, and this is a page with a figure on it that can be argued with, and neither of those two pages is on that sheet, and I have not entered the reason on either column today because the reason is a thing about a sheet and not a thing about a day.**

The third of those four columns took nothing today and the fourth took nothing and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and I have not entered that reason in eleven days and am not going to enter it in the twelfth. I entered one line on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and neither of the first two figures of that leaf has been read out loud anywhere this month, and that is the forty-sixth day of it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four hours and fifty-eight minutes, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the wedge was still on the corner of that bench stone.

**THE COLUMN ON THE STEP AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES AND STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTY-SECOND WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. THE PERSON WHOSE YARD IT IS PRICED ONE PERMITTED ACCOUNT ON A PAGE AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT 355 WORDS, IN HOURS AND IN MATERIAL, WITH THE MULTIPLICATION PRINTED AT EVERY FIGURE, BEING FOUR TIMES SIX MINUTES FOR FOUR GAPS, TWO TIMES SIX MINUTES FOR TWO READINGS, FOUR TIMES ONE HOUR FOR FOUR WEEKS OF ANSWERING QUESTIONS, AND TWO TIMES ELEVEN MINUTES FOR TWO WALKS, AND TWENTY-FOUR AND TWELVE AND TWENTY-TWO IS FIFTY-EIGHT MINUTES, AND FIFTY-EIGHT AND TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT MINUTES, BEING FOUR HOURS AND FIFTY-EIGHT MINUTES, AND TWO SHEETS AND ONE STICK OF CHALK AND ONE SLATE, AND THERE IS NO FIGURE OF MONEY AGAINST ANY PART OF IT AND NOT ONE PENNY OF IT GOES ONTO THAT SHEET. SHE ALSO SAID OUT LOUD, FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS, THAT A RESOLUTION THAT COST SOMEBODY SOMETHING IS ON NO LINE OF THAT SHEET BECAUSE THAT SHEET IS A LIST OF WHAT PEOPLE DID WITH THEIR OWN MONEY AND A RESOLUTION WAS NOT BOUGHT BY ANYBODY. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

and that the column was read out on that step at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who has read it seventy-two times, in front of about forty people of whom about eleven could not see a line, and that he read twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks off the lid of that step and said the figure and said that the figure had not moved, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four and that nobody was paid a penny out of it, and that the seventy-second week stands fallen due at the close of the said day behind the seventy-first and the only person in that market who said a word about it was the man reading; and that nobody argued with her, and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question, being whether the four hours and fifty-eight minutes came out of the man who kept the crossing column, and that she said that it did and that it was the same man and that he had been told about it on Sunday and that nobody had asked him whether he would do it; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the seventy-second week is entered as a week and not as a figure for the second time this month, and the first of those two was the seventy-first, is that this is the last wage Tuesday this harbor gets before the tenth of next month and that a man who writes a hundred of those as a number is a man who has started to be the author of the column, and that I entered the seventy-first as a week in the second column on the first of this month and I am not going to enter two numbers in a row in a column that is not a count of anything anybody can check; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason those four lines are in that order, being the writing and then the reading and then the answering and then the walking, is hers and I have not asked her about it and am not going to, and that the writing comes first because a thing that is not written cannot be read out to about eleven people who cannot read it, and that the walking comes last because a person who has been walking for twenty-two minutes is not able to hold a number in his head, and that I noticed that on Saturday when about four people were walked through a page by somebody else and I have been the somebody else twice this week; and that at about twenty past two the reason I have not entered the three days and seven hours and twelve minutes again in this column is that it was priced once, in this shed, on the fifteenth of last month, with the multiplication printed, and that a thing priced once in a room in front of nine people does not need a second column, and that the second column is not a place for things that have been said out loud and are on a page somewhere else, and that I have put this reason in here and not on that page because that page is about hours and this is about why a page is not a line; and that at about half past three the reason that page went behind a press and not onto that bench stone is mine and is that a page with a multiplication on it is a page that anybody can pick up and check, and that anybody picking it up and checking it is the entire of what it is for, and that four hours and fifty-eight minutes is a figure that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take off that page and be angry about, and that a figure a stranger can be angry about is worth four times what the wall across the lane is worth and about a hundred times what the slate with five lines on it is worth; and that at about five o'clock the reason I have put a Tuesday slip about a woman not being thanked and a man not being thanked in the same slip is that a Tuesday is the day a harbor finds out what it owes and that four hours and fifty-eight minutes of one man's hand is a debt this harbor has taken on this month and is not going to pay and has not pretended it is going to pay, and that a debt that is named in hours is a debt that somebody can refuse, and that is the whole difference between the two.*
