# Chapter 553

*Monday the Twenty-Fourth Day of January, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Sealed Sheet With No Figure on It Coming Ashore in a Bag and Going Onto the Board at the Head of That Mole Beside Two Papers That Have Not Been Opened, Six Lines on It With a Limit on the Fifth and a Name Under a Seal That Is Not Read Out, Read Out Word for Word Once on the Step at the Back of That Market at About Ten o'Clock in the Morning in Front of About Forty People of Whom About Twenty Cannot Read It and Again in a Room over That Floor at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty Up Those Stairs, Every Particular of It Being One a Stranger Can Check, and Then the Question Asked Out Loud in That Room, in About Sixteen Words, in the Mouth of a Woman of Thirty of No Office, Being Whether This Harbor Will Accept This Paper as Its Account of the Fourteenth, and No Answer Being Given to It by Anybody, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the twenty-fourth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market with about twenty people in it.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and nineteenth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and is going to still be off it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirtieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-three days. The paper that came ashore this morning is the third thing on that board and it is the first of the three that has ever been opened by anybody, and the other two have not been touched. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole today and the mole head is dark. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-one days and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and the two men who carried it are still not on that rota board, and the name at the head of that board is still off it, and the hose that goes in at the top of that cistern has been dead since the spring.

---

And the paper came ashore at about seven in the morning in a canvas bag with a seal on the bag and not a Crown hand on the slip at all, only a man in a boat's boat with a rope round the bollard, and it was carried up to the front street and nailed to the board at the head of that mole beside a paper saying that the sea is under martial custody and beside a paper with a seal on it and no figure on it from the third of December, and neither of those two was looked at while it was being nailed up.

The sheet has a seal at the foot of it and a name under the seal and no figure of money anywhere on it. It has a head, and the head names the night it is about, being the night of Friday the fourteenth of this month, and it says on its own face that it is an account of that night and that it is offered as a complete account of that night.

These are the six lines, and there are six of them.

1. *Nine boats of that port went inside a line of boats on the night of Friday the fourteenth and seven of them came back inside that line before six o'clock in the morning.*
2. *Two did not come back and were found on the ground off the outer water on the morning of Saturday the fifteenth, about forty yards apart, with their bows pointing the same way, and with a hole under the forefoot of each of them.*
3. *The gear of each of the two was in the bottom of that boat and no gear was on the deck of either of them, and there was nobody standing on either of them at any hour between about half past four in the morning and the time they were found.*
4. *No person is recorded as lost, and no person has been charged, and no person is named on this sheet.*
5. *This account is made from the returns of that port and from the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line, and it stops at what those returns contain. It does not state the cause of the loss of either boat and does not attribute it to anything.*
6. *Any person in that harbor who holds a particular that differs from any line of this account may set it out in his own words and in his own hand anywhere on a public wall in that harbor, and his name does not have to be on it.*

A clerk of that harbor authority read all six out loud on the step at the back of that market at about ten o'clock in the morning with his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading, and about forty people were on the boards in front of that step and about twenty of them cannot read the sheet itself, and the clerk stopped at the seal and did not read the name under it and nobody on those boards asked him to.

"It stops at what those returns contain. **That is on the front of the paper in the fifth of those six lines and there is no trick in it anywhere, and it is the first correct account any of us has ever been handed.**"

Nobody on that step contradicted him and nobody on that step agreed with him either and nobody thanked him for reading it.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, once, that she had refused to read a figure of his in that room on the sixteenth and that she was now reading six lines off a paper, and she said out loud, once, why the two of those were not the same act.

"**A figure of his comes out of his mouth and a figure on that sheet comes out of a hand that a stranger can take it off and check, and I will not stand in a room and be the reason a number in it cannot be checked, and I will stand in a room and be the reason six numbers in it can.** That is about thirty words and she did not say them twice and nobody in that room thanked her for them and the man of about thirty-three said that he had not said they were the same."

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over the floor of that market was let out and about twenty people went up those stairs, and about eleven of them cannot read what is put in front of them, and a woman of thirty of no office, who is not on this party's crew and is not its mouth and is not restored and is not forgiven, read the same six lines out a second time, word for word, with the same clerk standing at the end of that long table, and then she asked the room one question and nobody answered it.

"The sixth line says that any person holding a particular may set it out on a public wall. **Does this harbor accept this paper as its account of the fourteenth.**"

About twenty people were in that room and about eleven of them cannot read what was on that table, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and is not in it, and the four heads on that wall are the four heads and no fifth head went on it, and the two of those things were named out loud separately and neither was named in the same breath as the other.

Nobody answered. A man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons was at the back of that room and did not open his mouth, and a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay asked out loud and once whether the first four lines had been checked against anything or were simply what the Crown had to say about it, and he was answered by the woman of thirty of no office in about twenty-five words, and neither of them was thanked.

"No. **Somebody handed us half of it. I want to know who, and that paper is not going to tell me, and I have asked it nothing because it is a sheet of paper and a sheet of paper cannot be asked anything.**"

She did not say that the paper had refused her. She said it out loud once and it went into a minute of her own in her own hand and she did not put a figure on it.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and put the two pieces of paper side by side in about ten words without lifting either of them off the boards.

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. Unmoved, and nothing on it today, and a cistern is not a cost. **There is a sheet nailed to a board at the head of that mole with a seal on it and no figure on it and every line of it is one a man with a leaf and a pencil can check, and there is a sheet in that shed with a figure on it and a stranger can check that in a minute, and the two of them are not in the same world and the whole of this harbor's trouble this month is that both of them are true.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Monday, and nobody was thanked for the six lines and nobody was thanked for the second reading of them and nobody was thanked for the question and nobody was thanked for the answer that the paper could not give.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH TIME AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A SHEET WITH A SEAL ON IT AND NO FIGURE OF MONEY ON IT CAME ASHORE IN A BAGGED CANVAS SLIP, WENT ONTO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE BESIDE TWO PAPERS THAT WERE NOT TOUCHED, AND WAS READ OUT TWICE, ONCE ON THE STEP AT ABOUT TEN AND ONCE IN THAT ROOM AT ABOUT TWO, AND EVERY ONE OF ITS SIX LINES IS A PARTICULAR THAT A STRANGER CAN CHECK. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ASKED THAT ROOM IN SIXTEEN WORDS WHETHER THAT HARBOR WILL ACCEPT THAT PAPER AS ITS ACCOUNT OF THE FOURTEENTH, AND ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE WERE IN THAT ROOM AND NOBODY ANSWED. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT AND THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ON NO WALL IN THAT ROOM. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-fourth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and nineteenth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that a sheet with a seal at the foot of it and a name under the seal and no figure of money on it came ashore at about seven in the morning in a canvas bag with a seal on the bag and was carried up to the board at the head of that mole and was nailed there beside a paper saying that the sea is under martial custody and beside a paper with a seal on it and no figure on it from the third of December, and that neither of those two was looked at and neither was taken down and both remain on that board; and that that sheet carries six lines, being that nine boats of that port went inside a line of boats on the night of Friday the fourteenth and seven came back before six o'clock in the morning, being that two did not come back and were found on the ground off the outer water on the morning of Saturday the fifteenth about forty yards apart with their bows pointing the same way and a hole under the forefoot of each, being that the gear of each was in the bottom of that boat and no gear was on the deck of either and nobody was standing on either at any hour between about half past four in the morning and the time they were found, being that no person is recorded as lost and no person is named on that sheet, being that the account is made from the returns of that port and from the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line and stops at what those returns contain and does not state the cause and does not attribute it, and being that any person holding a differing particular may set it out in his own words and in his own hand on a public wall in that harbor without his name on it; and that a clerk of that harbor authority read all six aloud on the step at the back of that market at about ten o'clock in the morning with his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading in front of about forty people of whom about twenty cannot read the sheet, and that he stopped at the seal and did not read the name under it and nobody asked him to; and that a room over that market was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about twenty people up those stairs and about eleven of them who cannot read what is put in front of them, and that a woman of thirty of no office read the same six lines out a second time word for word, and then asked that room in sixteen words whether that harbor will accept that paper as its account of the fourteenth, and that nobody answered it, and that a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay asked once whether the first four lines had been checked against anything, and was answered in about twenty-five words that somebody handed us half of it and that the paper is not going to say who and that a sheet of paper cannot be asked anything; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the sixth line of that sheet is the one that is going to cost this harbor the most is his own, and is that the first five lines are all things a man can stand in front of and say is wrong, and that the sixth line is not a thing a man can be wrong about because it is an offer, and that an offer made by somebody who has a boat two days sail away costs the person it is made to nothing to refuse and costs the person who refuses a season; and that the reason the man who keeps the leaf did not answer her question in that room is his own, and is that she is the person who decides what gets recorded in that room and that a man who answers first in front of twenty people takes the answer out of her hands, and that a sheet with a seal on it and a name under it is not going to be settled in a slip by the man who keeps a leaf with twenty-eight pence in it; and that the reason a clerk of that harbor authority said the first correct account any of us has ever been handed out loud on a step in the open on a Monday morning is his own, and is that he reads things out on that step and that a thing read out on that step in front of forty people is a thing that cannot afterwards be called a rumour, and that he has been on the west basin long enough to know that the next time a paper comes ashore it will be easier to be believed the second time than the first; and that the reason nobody on those forty boards agreed with him is his own, and is that about twenty of them could not read it and the other twenty had a figure of forty-seven seconds on the back of two hands and a wall of a reeve's bench that has come down bare ninety times, none of which that paper mentions, and that a paper which is correct about what it covers and silent about the rest is the hardest kind of paper there is to argue with in the open.*
