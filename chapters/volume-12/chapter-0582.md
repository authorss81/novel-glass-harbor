# Chapter 582

*Tuesday the Twenty-Second Day of February, the Seventieth Wage Tuesday, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Wage Column on the Step at the Back of That Market Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes Before About Forty People and Not Moving at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Shut from Ten Until Four, the Seventieth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of the Day, a Sheet Made in That Shed at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon Pricing a Night's Victual and Coal and Air in Hours and Material With the Multiplication Printed at Every Figure and Five Hundred and Eight Pence on the Face of It and Not One Penny of It Going Onto That Sheet, a Man of About Thirty-Three Saying in About One Hundred and Thirty-Nine Words That He Could Pay Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence of It and Cannot Check a Minute of the Rest, a Woman of About Thirty-Nine Who Mends Net Saying in About One Hundred and Twenty-Eight Words That Sixteen Hours and a Quarter of a Man Is a Thing a Figure Does Not Cover, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Three Hundred and Three Words Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the twenty-second day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at two o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Tuesday was the seventieth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing. The wage column in that harbor was read out at about ten in the morning and did not move, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at the close of the said day, being the seventieth. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the sheet of hours and material that was made in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon is a page and is not on that sheet and is not a line on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-two days. The figure of four hundred and fifty pence has not come down that board since the first of December and nothing standing in that harbor was paid or advanced or cut back or bettered or struck or closed on that day. The demand for the returns goes back down that coast in a basket on Saturday, a boat taking a day and a night and a cart taking four days each way. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The column was read out on that step from ten o'clock in the morning and it took four minutes, and the reading was done by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads it every Tuesday and has read it seventy times, and about forty people were on the boards and about eleven of them could not see a line.

He read twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks off the lid of that step, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and when he came to the end of the twenty-sixth line he stopped and said the figure and said that the figure had not moved, and that is what he says.

The seventieth week stands fallen due at the close of that day. It stands behind the sixty-ninth, which stands behind the sixty-eighth, and nobody in that market said a word about any of them except the man reading, who says it every Tuesday because it is on his list.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon the person whose yard it is put a sheet on that bench stone, and about nine people were in that shed, and she prices hours and material and nobody in that room had asked her for it, and she said in about twenty-one words before she started that she was not pricing a night.

"**I am not pricing a night. A night is not a thing anybody can be charged for until it is over.**"

Here is the whole of the sheet.

1. *Air. One bottle, one man, one descent, at two hundred and eighty pence a man, being the figure that bottle came in at on the thirtieth of December and has not come down since. One times two hundred and eighty is two hundred and eighty pence, being one mark and ten shillings.*
2. *Coal. Four hundredweight for the pump at forty-five pence a hundredweight, being a figure that has gone up twice since the autumn and that is on no board in that harbor. Four times forty-five is one hundred and eighty pence, being one mark, one shilling and eightpence.*
3. *Victual. Six men at eight pence a head, being supper for six men in that shed from about ten at night until about four in the morning. Six times eight is forty-eight pence, being four shillings.*

Two hundred and eighty and one hundred and eighty and forty-eight is five hundred and eight pence, being three marks, two shillings and fourpence.

Then she wrote the hours underneath it, and the hours do not join the money and she said so on the face of it.

The hours under those three are ten hours of a man at the pump, being two men for five hours, and six hours of six men at a bench, being six men for one night at one hour apiece, and a quarter of an hour of a man counting a bottle and reading its neck. **Sixteen hours and a quarter of a man all told, and not one of those hours is on that sheet and no figure in this country prices a night watch, and I am not going to invent one and put it under a man's name.**

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, in about one hundred and thirty-nine words, what he could do with the top half of that sheet and what he could not do with the bottom half of it.

"**Three hundred and thirty-six of that five hundred and eight is the coal and the air and I would pay every penny of it tomorrow and I could not say whether it was right, which is the best kind of paying there is. I have been trying for three days to check a quarter of an hour of a man reading the neck of a bottle and I cannot check it, and I have had a fire in my own kitchen I could not check either.** What is on that sheet can be taken off with a pencil. What is under that sheet is a man sitting up in the dark at two in the morning, and nobody in this harbor has a figure for that and I am not going to be the first one to invent it."

A woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on the boards and is on nobody's crew said out loud, in about one hundred and twenty-eight words, that the two halves of that sheet are the whole of what a yard is.

"**I mend net for a living and I have done it for twenty-two years and I can say exactly what my sixteen hours are worth and I cannot say what they are worth, and those are two different things that most people in this market run together.** One of them is a figure. The other one is a woman on a board at two in the morning sewing a hole in a net that a boat will need at four. Both of those are real and only one of them can go in a book, and the one that cannot is the one that keeps the boats coming in, and that is not a thing I am going to be argued out of in a shed this afternoon."

Nobody argued with either of them, and the sheet went onto that bench stone at about a quarter to three and stayed on it until the middle of the next morning, and about four of those nine people could not read the three numbered lines and were told what was on them by the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay, and nobody copied it out and nobody put a name on it, and it is not on that sheet and it is not in any minute.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about three hundred and three words on what five hundred and eight pence is, and nobody in that slip was asked whether that figure was the bill.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and the column was read out on that step at ten and did not move, and the seventieth week stands fallen due behind it, and that has happened seventy times.

**Five hundred and eight pence is on a bench stone in that shed with a multiplication printed at every figure in it and the multiplication is right and it checks, and not one penny of it has gone onto that sheet and none of it is going to.** I have had a page of hours on that stone twice in three days and a bill that has not been written yet, and I am not going to be the man who puts a bill on that sheet before the thing it is a bill for has happened.

The two parts of that page do not join and the person who wrote it said so on the face of it before she said anything else, and that is the second time this month that somebody in this harbor has put the two halves of a thing side by side and refused to add them. The sixteen hours and a quarter of a man is not priced. The five hundred and eight pence is not charged. The bag is twenty-eight pence and will be twenty-eight pence on Wednesday, and the third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed with a boy of twenty-two's hand at the head of it, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck, and the reason it has taken no line is not on any page in this harbor and is not going on one tonight."

Nobody thanked the arithmetic and nobody asked for the rest of it, and that cistern was not charged today and the covers were on it and the rope through the ring of it had not been pulled since Thursday morning.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE STEP AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES BEFORE ABOUT FORTY PEOPLE AND STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE SEVENTIETH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A SHEET OF THREE NUMBERED LINES WENT ONTO THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON PRICING ONE BOTTLE OF AIR AT TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE, FOUR HUNDREDWEIGHT OF COAL AT FORTY-FIVE PENCE, AND SIX MEN AT EIGHT PENCE A HEAD, BEING ONE TIMES TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY AND FOUR TIMES FORTY-FIVE AND SIX TIMES EIGHT AND FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHT PENCE ON THE FACE OF IT, BEING THREE MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH SIXTEEN HOURS AND A QUARTER OF A MAN UNDER IT AND NO FIGURE ANYWHERE IN THAT COUNTRY FOR THOSE HOURS. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINE WORDS THAT HE WOULD PAY EVERY PENNY OF THE TOP HALF OF IT AND COULD NOT CHECK A QUARTER OF AN HOUR OF A MAN READING THE NECK OF A BOTTLE. A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-NINE SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT WORDS THAT WHAT A FIGURE IS AND WHAT A MAN SITTING UP AT TWO IN THE MORNING IS ARE TWO DIFFERENT THINGS THAT MOST PEOPLE IN THAT MARKET RUN TOGETHER. NOT ONE PENNY OF THAT SHEET WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED FOR THE COLUMN OR FOR THE PAGE. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-second day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the seventieth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing, and that the covers were on it and the rope through the ring of it had not been pulled since Thursday morning; and that the wage column was read out on the step at the back of that market at about ten in the morning in four minutes before about forty people, of whom about eleven could not see a line, by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who has read it seventy times, and that it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and that nobody was paid a penny out of it, and that the seventieth week stands fallen due at the close of the said day behind the sixty-ninth; and that at about two o'clock in the afternoon the person whose yard it is put a sheet on that bench stone with three numbered lines on it, and that she said in about twenty-one words before she started that she was not pricing a night and that a night is not a thing anybody can be charged for until it is over; and that the first of those three lines is one bottle of air for one man for one descent at two hundred and eighty pence a man being the figure that bottle came in at on the thirtieth of December and has not come down since, and one times two hundred and eighty is two hundred and eighty pence being one mark and ten shillings; and that the second of them is four hundredweight of coal for the pump at forty-five pence a hundredweight being a figure that has gone up twice since the autumn and that is on no board in that harbor, and four times forty-five is one hundred and eighty pence being one mark, one shilling and eightpence; and that the third of them is six men at eight pence a head for supper in that shed from about ten at night until about four in the morning, and six times eight is forty-eight pence being four shillings; and that two hundred and eighty and one hundred and eighty and forty-eight is five hundred and eight pence being three marks, two shillings and fourpence; and that under those three she wrote the hours, being ten hours of a man at the pump for two men for five hours, and six hours of six men at a bench, and a quarter of an hour of a man counting a bottle and reading its neck, being sixteen hours and a quarter of a man all told, and that the hours do not join the money and she said so on the face of the sheet, and that no figure in that country prices a night watch and that she is not going to invent one and put it under a man's name; and that a man of about thirty-three said out loud in about one hundred and thirty-nine words that he would pay every penny of the top half of it and could not check a quarter of an hour of a man reading the neck of a bottle, and that a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on the boards said out loud in about one hundred and twenty-eight words that what a figure is and what a man sitting up at two in the morning sewing a hole in a net are two different things that most people in that market run together; and that nobody argued with either of them, and that the sheet went onto that bench stone at about a quarter to three and stayed on it until the middle of the next morning, and that about four of those nine people could not read the three numbered lines and were told what was on them by a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay, and that nobody copied it out and nobody put a name on it, and that it is not on that sheet and it is not in any minute; and that the thirty-two days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and fifteen and one thousand four hundred and forty-nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those thirty-two days.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the man reading that column says the figure has not moved every Tuesday is his own, and is that in eleven weeks the figure has not moved and that he said it the first week to be helpful and now says it because there is nothing else on his list to say, and that a man who reads out a figure that has not moved seventy times stops hearing the figure and starts hearing himself, and that on the third week of this year he read the whole of that column out loud in his own head before he got to the end of the first line; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the person whose yard it is wrote the hours underneath the money and not on the same line is her own, and is that the money is what a yard owes and the hours are what a person gave, and that in eleven weeks she has never once put the two in one column and that the reason is that the two of them disagree about what a day is, a day being eight hours in that yard and a day being a day at a pump, and that if she put them in one column somebody would eventually add them and the sum would be a figure that is not true of anything; and that at about ten to three the reason a woman of about thirty-nine said that about what a yard is is her own, and is that in the winter of the twelfth a yard two streets up her sold for a figure that turned out to be the figure of her net mending for nine years including a week in which she did not mend, and that the man who took the figure did not care and that she cared for eleven years afterwards and that she has never since put a thing on a bench stone where the two of those could be added together; and that at about half past five the reason he said he was not going to put a bill on that sheet before the thing it was a bill for had happened is his own, and is that a yard puts a line on that sheet when the work is done and not when the work is spoken of, and that on the twenty-fourth of January somebody in that harbor put a day on that sheet for a man who was asked to be asked and not asked, and that the figure on that line was right and the day on it was not the day the work was done, and that he would rather have a sheet that is slow and right than a sheet that is quick and has to be argued about; and that at about six the reason that page was left lying on that bench stone overnight on purpose is his own, and is that a page read out in a room in the afternoon is a page that four people will repeat in four kitchens by supper, and that this one has three figures on it that anybody can check and a row of hours that nobody can, and that he wants those two facts to get into this harbor separately and not as one thing, because as one thing the first one will be used to carry the second one.*
