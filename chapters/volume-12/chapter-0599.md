# Chapter 599

*Friday the Eleventh Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, Nine Days Counted Off a Sheet in That Shed at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With the Door Standing Open and Nobody on the Slip, Four of Those Nine Days Carrying a Figure and the Other Five Carrying None, the Figure Not Said Out Loud in a Room and Not Said in the Half-Past-Five Slip and Standing in a Column on That Sheet and on No Wall, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking in About one hundred and ninety-six Words Whether Anybody Is Going to Keep a List of Everybody Who Put Nothing In and a Woman of Thirty of No Office Answering in About three hundred and sixty-three Words That There Is Not Going to Be One and That This Harbor Has Already Got One and Did Not Build It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About five hundred and twenty-one Words and Not Carrying That Figure, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the eleventh day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at half past five in the afternoon.

**The said Friday carried no rota and was no wage Tuesday and was no wipe, and nothing was put on that cistern on it, the covers being on it at eight in the morning and on it at four in the afternoon. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the sheet of nine days that was counted off in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon is on no sheet in this harbor and is in no minute and is on no wall, and the figure that was taken off it is not a count of persons and is not a roster of anybody and is not kept by anybody. A hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, was carried into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence was carried out of it at about four in the afternoon, and the bag was left with twenty-eight pence in it, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is three marks eleven shillings, being six hundred and twelve pence, behind the wage of four marks. That sheet carries one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and stands ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is in that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and nobody answered it. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of that day came out of that bag, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes an hour of a man counting nine days and a quarter of an hour of a man writing down that he had counted them.**

**These are the nine that a stranger in that harbor could have taken off any wall, in the order a stranger would find them, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and ninety-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and thirty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventy-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninety-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty-nine days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is where it has been since the second of this month, with the paper in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole there are nine hulls this afternoon and there have been nine there since the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been ninety-seven days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That market was working and the boards were dry for once, and about nine people were in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and the door stood open on the slip and the slate lay face up on that bench stone with the wedge on the corner of it.

---

At about two o'clock in the afternoon a man counted nine days off a sheet.

The sheet is a sheet of days and there are nine of them on it, being the third, the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, the seventh, the eighth, the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh of this month, and against each of those nine days there is either a figure or nothing at all, and a figure means that one or more gaps were entered that day against the four lines on the sheet at the end stall.

Four of the nine days carry a figure. Five of the nine carry nothing. He counted them twice, once down the page and once across the bottom, and the two counts are the same, and the addition at the foot of that sheet is four and three and two and five, being fourteen.

The figure was not said out loud in that room. It was not said in the half-past-five slip that afternoon either. It is in a column on that sheet and on no wall in this harbor, and it is not a count of persons, and nobody in that harbor is keeping a list of anybody.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question about that sheet in about 196 words, out loud, in front of the nine of them, and he asked it the way he asks things, which is once and then waits.

"**You have got nine days on that sheet and a figure against four of them and a sum at the foot, and I have been standing here for a quarter of an hour working out what a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could do with that, and there is one thing he could do with it and I want it stopped before Friday is out.** He could turn it round. He could take those five days that carry nothing and go and find out who it was that put nothing in on those five days, and there are about ninety people who could have put something in on any one of them, and he could have ninety names off a page in a week if anybody stood over him while he did it. **Is anybody in this harbor going to keep a list of everybody who put nothing in.** That is the whole question and I have asked it in a shed in front of nine people and I would like it answered in a shed in front of nine people and not written down afterwards in a better way than it was said."

The woman of thirty of no office answered him in about 363 words and she did not look at the sheet while she said it.

"**No, and there is not going to be one, and I have been waiting nine days for somebody to ask me that and you have done it in a shed in front of nine people exactly as I wanted and I am not going to thank him for it.** A list of everybody who put nothing in is a thing this harbor has already got one of and did not build it. It is the wage column and it is the board behind that reeve's bench and it is a page of six names in a drawer that nobody in this room has been told about and that I have not been in. Every one of those three is a list of people and not one of them has ever once told anybody what to do. **A gap is not a person. What is counted on that sheet is a set of things that people and communities are entitled to keep and have not given up, and there are more of them than there are days on that page, because one day will carry four of them and another day will carry none at all, and every one of them is a different thing that a different person is holding, and not one of those people is on that sheet and not one of them can be got at through it.**
If a stranger takes that figure off that page and turns it round, he will find five days with nothing against them and no name anywhere in this harbor he can put against those five days, and that is not a gap in the work. That is the sheet working. A person who cannot see a gap is a person who has been given a smooth page, and the second thing that follows from that, which nobody has said out loud yet, is that a person who cannot see a blank is in a worse way still, and a blank is not a fault and it is not anybody's doing, and about five of those nine days got nothing against them because about nine people were doing something else that mattered more."

Nobody argued with him. Nobody asked her for a second thing. Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for the counting of nine days or for the answer to a question that was asked once.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 521 words on nine days and a blank column, and nobody in that slip was asked for the figure, because the figure was not in it.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet today and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and no charge went on that cistern and the covers were on it at eight and at four, and a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard at four o'clock and is not a line on that sheet.

**Nine days were counted off a sheet in that shed this afternoon with the door open and nobody on the slip, and four of those nine days carry a figure against them and five of them carry nothing, and the count was taken twice, once down the page and once across the bottom, and the two counts are the same.** I am not going to say the figure in this slip and I want it written down here that I did not say it, because a figure said in a slip is a figure four people carry home and this one is going to sit on a sheet where a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can find it and be angry at it in about three minutes, and that is the whole of what it is for. It is in a column and it is not in a room and it is not on a wall, and it is not in this slip, and I will not put it in a minute.

A man of about thirty-three asked, once, in about 196 words, in front of nine people, whether anybody in this harbor is going to keep a list of everybody who put nothing in, and he said he would like it answered in a shed and not written down afterwards in a better way than it was said, and a woman of thirty of no office answered him in about 363 words that there is not going to be one, and that this harbor has already got one and did not build it, and that a gap is not a person, and that what is counted on that sheet is a set of things that people and communities are entitled to keep and have not given up, and that about five of those nine days got nothing against them because about nine people were doing something else that mattered more.

Nothing went onto the third column today and nothing onto the fourth, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and what is behind that is not on a page of mine. I entered a line on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and that is the forty-ninth day of it, and tomorrow is the fiftieth, and I have not read those two figures back to anybody in this harbor since the twenty-second day of January and I am not going to read them back in a slip, and I will say where they are read when they are read."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nine days, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the sheet with nine days on it was face up on that bench stone with a figure against four of them and nothing against five.

**NINE DAYS WERE COUNTED OFF A SHEET IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON WITH THE DOOR STANDING OPEN AND NOBODY ON THE SLIP, AND FOUR OF THOSE NINE DAYS CARRY A FIGURE AGAINST THEM AND FIVE OF THEM CARRY NOTHING, AND THE COUNT WAS TAKEN TWICE, ONCE DOWN THE PAGE AND ONCE ACROSS THE BOTTOM, AND THE TWO COUNTS ARE THE SAME, AND THE ADDITION AT THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET IS FOUR AND THREE AND TWO AND FIVE, BEING FOURTEEN. THAT FIGURE WAS NOT SAID OUT LOUD IN A ROOM AND WAS NOT SAID IN THE HALF-PAST-FIVE SLIP AND IS IN A COLUMN ON THAT SHEET AND ON NO WALL IN THAT HARBOR, AND IT IS NOT A COUNT OF PERSONS. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE ASKED IN ABOUT 196 WORDS WHETHER ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO KEEP A LIST OF EVERYBODY WHO PUT NOTHING IN, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ANSWERED IN ABOUT 363 WORDS THAT THERE IS NOT GOING TO BE ONE AND THAT THIS HARBOR HAS ALREADY GOT ONE AND DID NOT BUILD IT, AND THAT A GAP IS NOT A PERSON. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. A STRANGER CAN TAKE THREE FIGURES OFF THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY AND FIND THAT THEY AGREE, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT.**

and that that market was working and that those boards were dry for once and that about nine people were in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon and that the door stood open on the slip and that the slate lay face up on that bench stone with the wedge on the corner of it; and that at about two o'clock in the afternoon a man counted nine days off a sheet, and that the sheet is a sheet of days and that there are nine of them on it, being the third, the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, the seventh, the eighth, the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh of this month, and that against each of those nine days there is either a figure or nothing at all, and that a figure means that one or more gaps were entered that day against the four lines on the sheet at the end stall, and that four of the nine days carry a figure and five of the nine carry nothing, and that he counted them twice, once down the page and once across the bottom, and that the two counts are the same, and that the addition at the foot of that sheet is four and three and two and five, being fourteen; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked one question about that sheet in about 196 words, out loud, in front of the nine of them, being whether anybody in that harbor is going to keep a list of everybody who put nothing in, and that he said he wanted it stopped before Friday was out, and that he said a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could turn that figure round and take the four days that carry nothing and go and find out who it was that put nothing in on those four days, and that there are about ninety people who could have put something in on any one of them, and that he could have ninety names off a page in a week if anybody stood over him while he did it, and that he would like it answered in a shed in front of nine people and not written down afterwards in a better way than it was said; and that nobody argued with him, and that nobody asked her for a second thing, and that nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for the counting of nine days or for the answer to a question that was asked once; and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence found its way into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence found its way out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight pence was in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, of a wage of four marks, and that not one penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past; and that the entry against that day went onto the first column at about six in the evening, and that the first column is at three hundred and thirty-two and the second at one thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and that the third takes none and the fourth takes none and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither of the first two of those figures has been read out loud in any room or on any slip this month and the run of days is the forty-ninth counted from Saturday the twenty-second day of January, and that tomorrow is the fiftieth day of that leaf since Saturday the twenty-second day of January.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason I counted that sheet twice, once down and once across, is mine and is that a count taken once off a page of days is a count somebody can be right about in a kitchen by supper, and that the two of those counts being the same is the only argument I have got for it, and that it is not a very good argument, and that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil will do both in about three minutes anyway and will not need me to have done it first; and that at about half past two the reason I did not say the figure in that room and have not said it in this slip is that it is the third counted thing this month that has a number in it that four people would be right about, after the interval and the count of what answered, and that both of those two are in a column on a slate or a sheet for exactly this reason, and that the day somebody says a number like that out loud in a room in this harbor is the day this harbor stops being able to be argued with about three things at once; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason the second of that question has never been answered before this month is that nobody has asked it, and that a man of about thirty-three has spent the whole of this month looking for the thing that is going wrong and he has found it four times, and that this is the first time he has found a thing that is going to go wrong after this month is over and it is a thing that has not happened yet, and that is what a man looks like when he is about to stop being useful, and I have watched about four men in this harbor get there over two winters and I have never once been able to say out loud which part of it did it; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the five days that carry nothing are not a fault is hers and I have not asked her about it, and that five of nine days in the first week of a thing anybody has ever done on this coast is not a bad figure and that if somebody puts a figure against those five days in a month I would want to be asked about it before it went on a page, and that I am saying that here and not in that room because I was not asked it in that room; and that at about five o'clock the reason I have written on that sheet that the count was taken twice and the two counts are the same, and have not written on it whose hand it was in, is that the second column is the ledger's own and the first column is a count of days, and that the man who keeps the leaf is not the man who keeps that sheet, and that two of us keeping two things in the same shed is a fact about this shed in March and not a fact about anybody.*
