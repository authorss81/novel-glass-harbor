# Chapter 597

*Wednesday the Ninth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Boards at the Back of That Market Working at About Eleven in the Morning in About Nine People, a Permitted Account of a Community That Has Permitted Everything Being Read Out in the Open for the First Time With a Wrong Hour in It, a Man of About Fifty-Eight of the Third Lay Who Pulls Kelp Saying Out Loud in About one hundred and sixty-eight Words That the Hour Is an Hour Wrong, and the Wrong Figure Being Left on the Page Under the Right One in the Holder's Own Hand and With No Name Against It, and the Account Still Being Offered at Four o'clock, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying in About two hundred and one Words What a Thing That Can Be Found Out in the Open Cannot Do, Two More Gaps Entered at the End Stall in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About four hundred and fifty-nine Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the ninth day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at half past five in the afternoon.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the covers were on it at eight in the morning and were on it at four in the afternoon. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the page that was read out in the open on those boards at about eleven in the morning is on no sheet in this harbor and is in no minute, and the correction that went into it in the open at about half past eleven carries no name and is not in any book. That yard was given a hundred and sixty-eight pence at about ten in the morning and gave a hundred and sixty-eight pence back at about four in the afternoon, a hundred and sixty-eight pence being one mark and eightpence, and the bag ended the said day with twenty-eight pence in it, being two shillings and fourpence, that being six hundred and twelve pence, or three marks eleven shillings, of a wage of four marks. The money that has gone out of that yard is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and the money that has come in is at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the difference between them is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that difference is on that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and nobody answered it. No tin in that shed has held anything since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and nothing in the cost of that morning was paid out of that bag, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes a morning of a page read out loud on boards and a wrong hour found in it and corrected in the open, and not one of those three things can be sold to anybody at any price in this country.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and ninety-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and thirty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and nineteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventy-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty-seven days. There are five papers on the board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened by anybody, and the basket with the second asking in it stands in that boathouse with the paper still in it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon, the same nine as on the second of this month. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been ninety-five days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That market was working and those boards were wet from about nine, and the end stall was taking the first boat of the day off the beam at about half past ten, and the man of about fifty-six who lands fish had the slate face up on the lid of that stall with the interval on it and nothing else on it.

---

At about eleven in the morning a permitted account of a community that has permitted everything was read out in the open, on those boards, in about nine people, for the first time, and it is four pages and it is the account that was offered on Thursday the third of this month with a woman's name at the foot of one column, and the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read all four pages out loud and about eleven people of that market were told what was on them by him.

A man of about fifty-eight of the third lay, who pulls kelp and is on nobody's crew, stood at the back of that crowd for about nine minutes and then said out loud, in about 168 words, that the hour on the second page was an hour wrong, and the crowd did not move and about four people turned round.

"**That boat of ours went out at ten on the second of this month and that page says eleven, and I know it is an hour wrong because I was on the beam at ten and the light at the head of that mole is out and I have been out at that hour for nineteen springs.** I am not going to put my name on it and I would be surprised if anybody could make me. I have told you what I know and I have told you how I know it and that is the whole of what I have got, and if two of you were standing on that quay at ten o'clock in the morning on the second of this month then one of you knows whether I am right, and if neither of you was standing there then I am a man pulling kelp telling you about a boat and I am no more than that and you can leave the page alone."

The woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on those boards, who holds that account, said the figure on the page was right, and she said so in about nine words, and about four people in that crowd agreed with her before anybody had checked anything.

And then a man of about twenty-six of the third lay, who is one of the six who keep the third mark and who crews two of that lay, said that he had been on that quay at ten o'clock on the second of this month and that the man of about fifty-eight was right, and that the page was an hour wrong, and the woman of about thirty-nine kept her hand off the figure and did not take it off the page, and the man of about thirty-six said nothing at all for about a minute and then said the page was an hour wrong.

That correction went into that page in the open at about half past eleven, in the hand of the woman of about thirty-nine, and the wrong figure was not rubbed out and is still on the page under the right one, and against it is a line in the same hand that says what the page said and what it says now and who said the first one and that no name is against either.

Then that page was read out again from the top, the whole four pages, with the correction in it, and that account was still being offered at about four o'clock in the afternoon, and nobody was asked whether he wanted to go on offering it after that.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said one thing about what had just happened, out loud, in about 201 words, on those boards, with the crowd still standing there.

"**That is the thing about it, and I have watched it happen twice this month and I have not been able to say it both times. There are two sheets on a board at the head of this mole and one of them went round nine houses in February, and I could not put a wrong hour in either of them if I had a year and a knife, and not one person in this harbor would have believed me if I had.** About ten minutes ago a man who pulls kelp said a figure was wrong in a page on these boards and got the last of it out of him in the open with the reason attached to the reason, and the page still went out at four o'clock this afternoon with the mistake in it and the correction under it. One of those two things cannot be found out and the other one can be found out at half past eleven on a Wednesday morning in front of nine people, and I know which is which, and I have not been able to work out how to make that sound like an argument and I have stopped trying."

Nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him out loud. Nobody was asked for the rest of it and nobody was thanked for the demonstration, and about four people who had come up those steps to hear something went back down them, and the man of about fifty-eight who pulls kelp went back to his kelp and was not asked about it again that day.

---

And at about four in the afternoon two more gaps went onto the sheet at the end stall, in the same four lines and the same four headings, and one of the two was entered by the man of about fifty-six who lands fish and the other of them by the man of about forty-one of the first lay who took the other side of a boat up to the top of that point on Tuesday morning and came back on Wednesday night, and neither of them was asked where the boat had got to and neither of them was asked anything.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 459 words on a wrong hour found in the open on a working morning, and nobody in that slip was asked for the name of the man who found it.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet this morning and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and no charge went on that cistern and the covers were on it at eight and at four.

**Four pages of a permitted account were read out in the open on those boards at about eleven this morning in about nine people for the first time, and about ten minutes later a man who pulls kelp said one of the hours on the second page was an hour wrong, and he said how he knew it, and he said he would not put his name on it, and a man who was on that quay at that hour said he was right, and the page was read out again from the top with the mistake in it and the correction under it, and that account was still being offered at about four this afternoon.** The wrong figure is still on that page. Nobody rubbed it out. That is the whole of what happened this morning and it took about half an hour and it cost nothing and there is no figure anywhere in this country for what it cost, and that is the first time in eleven weeks that I have been able to write down a thing that this harbor did and that nobody has to be asked about twice.

A man of about thirty-three said out loud, in about 201 words, that two sheets on a board at the head of that mole could not be found out at by anybody, and that about ten minutes earlier a page on these boards could be, and that he has watched it happen twice this month and has not been able to make it sound like an argument and has stopped trying. I am not going to make it sound like an argument either. It is on those boards in about nine people's ears and it is not on that page and it is not in any book and there is no name in it anywhere, and the man who did it has gone back to his kelp.

The third column took nothing and the fourth took nothing today and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and that reason is on no page of mine and is going on none. I entered one line on the first column and five on the second, at about six this evening, and the first two of those figures have not been read out loud in a room this month, and that is the forty-seventh day of it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a wrong hour, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the page with the mistake in it and the correction under it was on those boards with somebody's hand flat on the corner of it.

**FOUR PAGES OF A PERMITTED ACCOUNT WERE READ OUT IN THE OPEN ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE FOR THE FIRST TIME, AND ABOUT TEN MINUTES LATER A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT OF THE THIRD LAY WHO PULLS KELP SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 168 WORDS THAT THE HOUR ON THE SECOND PAGE WAS AN HOUR WRONG AND SAID HOW HE KNEW IT AND SAID HE WOULD NOT PUT HIS NAME ON IT, AND A MAN WHO WAS ON THAT QUAY AT THAT HOUR SAID HE WAS RIGHT, AND THE PAGE WAS READ OUT AGAIN FROM THE TOP WITH THE MISTAKE STILL IN IT AND THE CORRECTION UNDER IT, AND THAT ACCOUNT WAS STILL BEING OFFERED AT ABOUT FOUR O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON. NO NAME IS AGAINST THE CORRECTION AND NONE IS IN ANY BOOK. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT 201 WORDS THAT TWO SHEETS ON A BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE COULD NOT BE FOUND OUT AT BY ANYBODY AND THAT A PAGE ON THOSE BOARDS COULD BE, AT HALF PAST ELEVEN ON A WEDNESDAY MORNING, AND THAT HE HAS WATCHED IT HAPPEN TWICE THIS MONTH AND HAS STOPPED TRYING TO MAKE IT SOUND LIKE AN ARGUMENT. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK STANDS AT NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, ON THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY, AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about half past eleven the reason the wrong figure is still on that page is the woman of about thirty-nine's and she said so in one sentence and I have not asked her about it, and that a page with the mistake rubbed out is a page that has been right, and that a page that has been right cannot be found out at again, and that the whole of the four lines on that page at the end stall depends on a thing being visible that somebody could have got wrong; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason two gaps went onto that sheet at four o'clock and not at eleven is that about eleven this morning was the only hour this month at which a person in this harbor was willing to stand in the open and be wrong in front of nine people, and that two people did it in about half an hour and both of them are in the same gap business now, and that the hour after that is the hour people go and do something, and that is the entire of the difference between the two hours; and that at about five o'clock the reason I have not entered the number of gaps on that sheet is that I wrote it in this column on Saturday and I am not going to write it twice, and that the sheet has four and three and two on it now and a man who keeps a leaf of days is not the man who keeps that sheet, and that there is no figure for the whole of it in either place and that whoever wants one is going to have to go and count, and that I have decided that I am not going to do it for them.*
