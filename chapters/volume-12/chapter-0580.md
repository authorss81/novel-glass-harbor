# Chapter 580

*Sunday the Twentieth Day of February, a Wipe, the Ninety-Fourth Time, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Again at the Angle It Has Been at Since the Twelfth and Not Going Up Whole, It Having Taken About Nine Minutes and Nobody Saying Why, a Berth-Fund Day of One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In and the Same Out, a Shed at About Three o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Nine People In It and a Page of Days Open on That Bench Stone With Thirty Days On It, Six Things Those Days Cost and None of Them Carrying a Figure and Four Things They Proved and Every One of Those Four Carrying a Figure, the Fitting-Out Sheet Unmoved and No Line Going Onto It on Any of the Ten Days, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the Leaf at Three Hundred and Thirteen and One Thousand Four Hundred and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, and Neither of Those Two Figures Read Back Aloud*

Sunday the twentieth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at three in the afternoon.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was the ninety-fourth wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that cistern was not charged on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten in the morning and went up again at about a quarter to eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and did not go up whole, and it took about nine minutes both ways this time and nobody has said why it is slower than the Sunday before it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on any of the ten days from Friday the eleventh of this month to this day, and nothing came off it in any hand on any of them. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of those ten days came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence came into that yard on those ten days and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence went out of it, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eighteen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty days. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and is neither closed nor struck and there is no date against it anywhere. The demand for the returns went down that coast in a basket on the fourth, came back in the same basket on the thirteenth with the basket empty, and goes down again on the Saturday. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-eight days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That wall came down bare at about half past ten and went up again at about a quarter to eleven, and it took about nine minutes both ways, which is about five minutes longer than the Sunday before it, and neither of the two men who do it said a word about that and neither has said a word about it since.

The boards at the join are showing weather on the face of them and there is one that is not the colour it was. The light at the head of that mole was out at about half past seven this morning as it is on every clear night and is out now, and there is no figure for it on any board in that harbor and there never has been, and about four people went past that wall between half past ten and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it.

---

At about three o'clock in the afternoon about nine people were in that shed and the man who keeps the leaf had a page of days open on that bench stone, and it is not that leaf, and he said so out loud before anybody looked at it.

He did not make a speech. He read two lists off a page with days against the lines, and the first list is what those ten days cost and there are six of them and not one of them carries a figure, and the second is what those ten days proved and there are four of them and every one of the four carries a figure that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take off a page of days.

**What they cost.** A paper of four lines refused out loud in the open at about half past two on Monday by a man whose crossings those are, on four out of six, with the paper behind a door and nothing written under its fourth line. A basket down that coast and back and going down again, a day and a night each way, and nothing in it either time and no line on any sheet for any of it. Three days and seven hours and twelve minutes of one man's season, of which three marks and two shillings can be sold and the rest cannot. A wall that came down bare this morning for the ninety-fourth time and went up unrepaired and not whole. A man who gave up the only thing he could be caught out in, and said it out loud once, and put it in a coat. And a boy's hand at the head of an empty column with nobody appointed to it and nobody asking him about it.

**What they proved.** Three figures of ninety-four seconds in three places, the same figure, and the sum of the three is two hundred and eighty-two seconds, being four minutes and forty-two seconds. Nine vessels of that port answering one signal on two nights, counted off the crossings and not off an estimate, being eighteen lines, and the same nine that two sealed sheets put inside that line at ten o'clock at night and at six in the morning. A wrong hour in a crossing column in a market, argued with and corrected in about four minutes in front of about nine people, at no cost, by two men who were standing on that quay at that hour. And a man who cannot read, saying what a column is for, in one sentence, better than anything anybody in that harbor had said about it in eleven days.

And he said the last thing about the two lists before anybody said anything, and it was in about fifty-six words, and it was the only sentence of the afternoon that was his own and it was said to a page and not to the room.

"**Six things that cost and not one of them has a figure on it, and four things that were proved and every one of them is a figure. I have been trying for ten days to make those two be the same kind of thing and they are not, and I have not found the join.**"

Nobody answered him. Nobody made a speech and nobody asked him a second question about it, and about four of the nine people in that shed could not read either list and were told what was on them by a man of about forty-six who mends nets, and he read both lists out twice without getting a word wrong, and nobody said a word about that either.

The page of days went back inside a coat at about ten to four. It is not that leaf and it was not read back out of that leaf and it is not on that bench stone tonight.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and eighty-nine words on what ten days of this month came to, and nobody in that slip was asked to agree with any of the two lists.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved on every one of the ten days, and no line went onto that sheet on any of them, and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence came in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence went out, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way, and the bag is twenty-eight pence.

**Ten days from Friday the eleventh of this month to this day, and the ten of them cost six things with no figures on them and proved four things with figures on them, and the two lists do not join and I have not found the join, and that is the whole of what I have.** Nothing improved this month. The figure of four hundred and fifty pence did not come down that board. The sixty-ninth week stands fallen due and will stand behind the seventieth on Tuesday. A wall came down bare twice and went up unrepaired and not whole twice, and this morning it took about nine minutes.

Three hundred and thirteen and one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine, none, and one hundred and twenty-five, are the figures on that leaf at the close of this day, being three hundred and three and one thousand three hundred and eighty-nine at the close of Thursday the tenth of this month plus ten entries and fifty entries, and thirty days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January. I have not read them back out loud to anybody this month and I am not going to tonight, and the third column took nothing and the fourth took nothing and there is no date against either of them anywhere."

Nobody thanked him for the arithmetic and nobody asked him what happens next, and the door of that shed stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the wall behind a reeve's bench stood up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and not whole.

**THE WALL CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE NINETY-FOURTH TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK ABOUT NINE MINUTES BOTH WAYS AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. NOTHING WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY ON ANY OF THE TEN DAYS FROM THE ELEVENTH OF THIS MONTH TO THIS DAY, AND ONE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE CAME INTO THAT YARD AND ONE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE WENT OUT OF IT. AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, TWO LISTS WERE READ OFF A PAGE OF DAYS IN THAT SHED, BEING SIX THINGS THOSE TEN DAYS COST AND NONE OF THEM CARRYING A FIGURE, AND FOUR THINGS THOSE TEN DAYS PROVED AND EVERY ONE OF THEM CARRYING A FIGURE. A MAN WHO CANNOT READ SAID WHAT A COLUMN IS FOR AND IT WAS BETTER THAN ANYTHING ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAD SAID ABOUT IT IN ELEVEN DAYS. NOBODY ANSWERED AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE LEAF STANDS AT THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN AND ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINE AND NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE, AND NEITHER OF THE FIRST TWO FIGURES HAS BEEN READ BACK ALOUD IN A ROOM ON ANY DAY OF THIS MONTH, AND THE THIRD COLUMN TOOK NOTHING AND THE FOURTH TOOK NOTHING AND THE REASON THE FOURTH TOOK NOTHING IS NOT ON ANY PAGE. THE FITTING-OUT SHEET STANDS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HAS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN IT AND HAS HAD IT ON ALL TEN DAYS.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten the reason the nine minutes is on that page is his own, and is that a wipe is a thing two men do on a Sunday and that the only figure anybody has ever had for it is that it happens, and that a thing which is slower this week than it was the week before is either a fact about a wall or a fact about two men, and that he is not going to guess which and print the guess; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason he took a page of days out and not that leaf is his own, and is that the leaf takes a day and a day is a counter, and that the two lists in that shed at about three o'clock are about ten days and are not about any one day, and that the leaf has no way of holding ten days of anything except by not holding it, and that the only instrument in that harbor that could hold ten days is a piece of paper that is not the leaf, and that a yard which has two instruments ends up with two of them on the same bench stone at six in the evening and everybody reading the wrong one; and that at about ten to four the reason that page went into a coat is his own, and is that a page of ten days read out in front of about nine people in a shed is a page that nine people will be asked about by people who were not there, and that a man who read a list of six costs and four proofs out loud in a room on a Sunday afternoon is a man who has made a speech whether he meant one or not, and that the only defence against that is to make it a list with days on it and put it in a coat before anybody can turn it into a speech in a bar; and that at about half past five the reason he said that the two lists do not join is his own, and is that he has spent twenty days trying to put a cost and a proof on the same page so that a stranger could take one off it and hand it to somebody, and that in twenty days every cost in that harbor has come out of a room and every proof has come out of a column, and that he does not know how to make one of them the other kind and is not going to pretend on a Sunday that he does, and that the not-knowing is going in this column and the two lists are going in the first one because the two lists are the day; and that at about ten past six the reason nothing this month improved is his own, and is that he was not asked to improve anything and would have said so out loud in a slip if he had been, and that the one thing he wanted out of ten days was a figure that could be set against a person and there is not one on any page in that harbor, and that a yard with a hundred and ninety-six thousand pounds of nothing on it and no figure against anybody is the only kind of yard that can be checked by a stranger with a leaf and a pencil in a year, and that he is not going to be the man who improves the sheet by finding a person to put a figure next to.*
