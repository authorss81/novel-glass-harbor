# Chapter 555

*Wednesday the Twenty-Sixth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Flat End of the Joiner's Yard at the Top of Fish Street Chalked in a Large Hand With Four Lines of Correction and One Line of Refusal in the Same Hand Off the Same Piece of Chalk and the Second of the Five Not Smaller Than the First, Read Out Loud Twice in the Passage Underneath by a Woman of Thirty of No Office, a Man of About Thirty-Five of the First Lay Saying Out Loud in Front of About Eleven People That He Is Not Going to Be Asked Again and Being Argued With by Nobody, the Paper of Four Lines in That Street at the Top of the Same Wall on Its Hundred and Forty-Seventh Day, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the twenty-sixth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and at the top of Fish Street where that street is steep and there is one flat face on it.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-five days. The six lines on that sealed sheet are still on the board at the head of that mole and the two papers that were on that board before it are still on that board and neither has been opened. The lamp oil board at the back of that end stall is at four hundred and fifty pence with the first of June written above it and that figure has not come down that board since the twenty-ninth day of August. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-three days and nobody has walked him up the road with the pump on it and nobody has sent for him.**

---

And the flat end of the joiner's yard at the top of Fish Street was chalked at about eleven in the morning, in a large hand, by a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay who could write and who has not been asked to do it by anybody and who said afterwards that he would rather not have done it and had done it anyway. The paper of four lines has been on the same wall at the top of it for a hundred and forty-seven days and the new chalk went in under it and did not cover any of it.

These are the five lines that went onto that wall, and the fifth is not in a lighter hand and is not smaller than the other four.

1. *That the account on the board at the head of the mole says nine boats went inside that line and seven came back, and does not say that there was no Crown hull inside that line at any hour of that night, and that nobody on that coast had to agree to anything to say so.*
2. *That it names no person, and does not say that nobody was hurt, and that those are two different silences and only one of them is a favour.*
3. *That it was made from returns, and that the returns themselves are not on the paper and are not on any wall in this harbor and have not been read out in any room, and that a person cannot be shown to be wrong by a piece of paper that will not show where its own figures came from.*
4. *That this harbor is one body among the bodies on that coast, and that a sheet with a seal on it does not turn it into the body that decides anything.*
5. *Refusal. I will not have my crossings put into that account or into any other one. I do not want my name on it and I do not want my daughter's name on it. I am not going to be asked about it again. A man of the first lay.*

---

And the man of about thirty-five of the first lay, whose own hand is the fifth of those five, stood in the passage underneath that wall from about eleven until about half past one and did not go up the street, and about eleven people stood in that passage with him at one time or another, and nobody argued with any part of the fifth line and nobody thanked him for any part of it and nobody thanked the man of about twenty-seven who wrote it.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said the only thing anybody said about the fifth line in three days, and he said it once, in the passage, at about half past one.

"That is the only one of the five that is about a person and it is the only one of the five that cannot be taken back."

The man of about thirty-five said that it could be taken back by him any time he liked and that that was not the point, and that the point was that a refusal with his own name at the foot of it in chalk in a street four hundred yards from a bench stone could be read by a man who cannot read if a person with a good voice stood under it, and that he had not wanted to write it and that he had written it.

---

And a woman of thirty of no office read all five out loud in that passage twice, standing underneath them, with about eleven people in the passage both times and a lamp at the top of the steps, and the second time was because a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay had been in the middle of a conversation and had not heard the bottom line the first time and said so out loud and was not asked to be quieter about it.

She did not put her own name under it and she did not put his name at the head of it and she said out loud, once, at about twenty past one, that she was not going to be the one who keeps those five lines, and that a woman of thirty who decides what gets recorded in a room is not the person who should be holding a wall in a street, and that a wall is read by whoever can read it and that is the whole of a wall.

The man of about thirty-five said that was right and that he had chosen the wall for that reason and not in spite of it, and neither of them was thanked by anybody and both of them said nothing back.

The chalk is not white, it is the yellow they use on the marks, and it took on a flat face like that in about four minutes and it comes off in about a month in this weather unless it is gone over again. The rain came in off the head of that street at about two and made a dark place in the middle of the fourth line and not on any of the other four, and the man of about thirty-five looked at the dark place for a while and did not go up and paint over it, and said that a line with a wet place in it is a line and that it is the ones with no marks on them that go first.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, and he said the thing that the two pieces of chalk are worth, and he said it in about seventy-one words, and about nine people heard all of it and nobody agreed with all of it and nobody disagreed with any of it.

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. **Four lines of chalk upstairs cost nothing and one line of refusal upstairs cost a man a question he will be asked in three months by somebody who thinks he is owed an answer, and that is the first cost on any public wall in this harbor that anybody has been able to name without a figure on it.**"

Nothing that stands in that harbor was paid on that Wednesday, nothing was advanced, nothing was cut back, nothing was bettered, nothing was struck and nothing was closed, and no figure has been asked of anybody in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that day, and nobody was thanked for the five lines or the two readings or the refusal and the chalk has been rained on once since and has not run.

---

**THE FLAT END OF THE JOINER'S YARD AT THE TOP OF FISH STREET CARRIED FOUR LINES OF CORRECTION AND ONE LINE OF REFUSAL IN THE SAME HAND OFF THE SAME PIECE OF CHALK ON THIS DAY, AND THE REFUSAL IS NOT SMALLER THAN THE CORRECTION AND IS NOT IN A LIGHTER HAND. ALL FIVE WERE READ OUT LOUD TWICE IN THE PASSAGE UNDERNEATH BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE WHO PUT NO NAME UNDER ANY OF THEM AND DECLINED TO KEEP THEM. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE OF THE FIRST LAY STOOD UNDER HIS OWN REFUSAL FOR TWO AND A HALF HOURS AND WAS ARGUED WITH BY NOBODY. THE SEALED SHEET IS STILL ON THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AND THE TWO PAPERS THAT WERE ON THAT BOARD BEFORE IT ARE STILL ON THAT BOARD AND NEITHER HAS BEEN OPENED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-sixth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the flat end of the joiner's yard at the top of Fish Street was chalked at about eleven in the morning in a large hand by a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay who was not asked to do it by anybody, being that the account on the board at the head of that mole says nine boats went inside a line of boats and seven came back and does not say that there was no Crown hull inside that line at any hour of that night, being that it names no person and does not say that nobody was hurt and that those are two different silences and only one of them is a favour, being that it was made from returns and that the returns themselves are not on the paper and are not on any wall in that harbor and have not been read out in any room, being that that harbor is one body among the bodies on that coast and that a sheet with a seal on it does not turn it into the body that decides anything, and being a refusal in a fifth line at the same size and off the same piece of chalk, being that the man of about thirty-five of the first lay will not have his crossings put into that account or any other and does not want his name on it and does not want his daughter's name on it and is not going to be asked about it again; and that a woman of thirty of no office read all five out loud twice in the passage underneath, and that the second reading was because a man of about forty-six who mends nets had not heard the bottom line the first time and said so out loud, and that she put no name under any of the five and declined out loud to be the person who keeps them; and that about eleven people were in that passage and nobody argued with the fifth line and nobody thanked the man of about thirty-five and nobody thanked the man of about twenty-seven and nobody thanked the woman of thirty; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said one thing about that fifth line in three days, being that it is the only one of the five that is about a person and the only one of the five that cannot be taken back, and the man of about thirty-five said that he could take it back any time he liked and that the point was that a refusal with his own name at the foot of it in chalk in a street could be heard by a man who cannot read if a person with a good voice stood under it; and that the paper of four lines in that street was on the top of that same wall on its hundred and forty-seventh day and the new chalk went in under it and covered none of it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the man of about thirty-five wanted his own hand at the foot of the fifth line is his own, and is that a refusal without a name on it can be put on a wall by anybody at all and can be read as the refusal of a whole street, and that about eleven people in that passage would have heard the same five words and believed the same five words whichever man they belonged to, and that a refusal has to have one man on it or it is a mood; and that the reason the man of about twenty-seven wrote all five rather than the four is his, and is that the man asked him to write five and he wrote five and that he would rather not have written the fifth and had not been thanked and had not been asked to write it again; and that the reason a woman of thirty of no office read the wall out twice and did not keep it is hers, and is that a room and a wall are two different instruments and that a room takes a decision and a wall takes a fact, and that a woman who decides what gets recorded in a room has no business being the keeper of a fact in a street where about eleven people will hear it and cannot be shown it again; and that the reason nobody thanked the man of about thirty-five for a refusal that will cost him something in the spring is his own, and is that a man who is thanked for a refusal is a man who has been thanked for saying no, and that on that wall there were four lines about a paper and one line about a man and the four are worth more in this harbor than the one, and that the whole of the trouble of the next three months is going to be about the four.*
