# Chapter 578

*Friday the Eighteenth Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the End Stall at the Back of That Market Working from About Six in the Morning With a Boy of Twenty-Two on a Stool and a Slate With a Figure in Chalk on the Lid of It, a Man of About Thirty-Five of the First Lay Asking at About Eleven in the Morning on Those Boards What Was in His Own Boat Before She Went Out on the Thursday and His Boat Not Being in That Harbor, a Man of About Fifty-Six of the First Lay Who Lands Fish and Is on Nobody's Crew Reading the Sixth Line of That Account Out Loud Off the Copy on the Wall of That Room and Stopping at the Ring of Black Chalk, That Reading Answering the Question of a Man Who Was Not in the Room About a Boat That Was Not in the Harbor, and the Crossing Column for That Quay Being Read Out and Argued With and Corrected in the Open by Two Men Who Were Standing on That Quay at That Hour, the Whole of It Carrying No Name, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the eighteenth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and at the end stall at the back of that market from six in the morning with a boy of twenty-two on a stool and about nine people on the boards at eleven.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the whole of what was done at that end stall was done on a slate and a wall and in the open, and not one line of it is on that sheet. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-six days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-eight; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and sixteen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-second night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-fifth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventieth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-seventh; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and eighteen days. The third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and a boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is not on the water. Four heads stand on a wall in the room over that market, being claims, refusals, restricted records and public correction, and no fifth head is on that wall on account of a paper, a night, a failure or a victory. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-six days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That end stall opens at six and the fish came in off two boats before seven, and the weight went on the beam and the chalk figure is on the lid of the slate at the end of that stall and has been since the first of December, and it does not know what day of the week it is.

A boy of twenty-two stood on a stool at that stall from about half past six and wrote the weights into a book as they came over the beam, and nobody said anything to him about the water and nobody asked him where he had been and he is not on the water.

---

At about eleven in the morning a man of about thirty-five of the first lay came up onto those boards and asked out loud, in front of about nine people, what was in his own boat before she went out on the Thursday, and his boat is not in that harbor and has not been since Thursday and is not due back until Sunday.

Nobody could tell him. A man of about forty-four who keeps the crossings for that crew read the crossing column for that quay off a slate and the line said the hour she went out and it said nothing whatever about what was aboard her, and the man of about thirty-five said that he had not asked about the hour, he had asked what was in her.

And then a man of about fifty-six of the first lay, who lands fish at that stall and is on nobody's crew and has said so out loud before, took a piece of chalk and went up the stairs into the room over that market and read the sixth of those six lines out loud off the copy on the wall of that room, in front of everybody who followed him up, and he stopped at the ring of black chalk at the foot of it and did not go past the ring.

He read it twice. The line says that any person in that harbor who holds a particular that differs from any line of the account may set it out in his own words and in his own hand anywhere on a public wall in that harbor, and that his name does not have to be on it.

And the thing that happened when he had read it twice was that a question a man had asked about his own boat on a working morning in a market was answered, and it was answered correctly, and the man of about thirty-five was not in the room and his boat was not in the harbor, and nobody in that room could have said a word against it, and the answer it got was that a thing he had never seen and had never heard of was the place where the answer to his own boat now lived.

Nobody made a speech about it. A woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay said one short thing, in about thirty-eight words, and then stopped, and nobody took it up.

"He has just read that out in a market on a Friday and made a man's question into a wall, and that wall is a Crown wall and I am not able to do one thing about it."

---

And the man of about thirty-five said the other half of it himself, out loud, at about a quarter to twelve, in about a hundred and eighty words, and he did not have to be asked.

"I want to be fair about this, and I am the one it is being done to, so I will say it. **A page I can argue with.** I asked for the crossing column and two men who were standing on that quay at that hour stood up on those boards and told me the hour is an hour later than the slate says, and they were right, and it is a wrong hour in a book in a market and it is wrong in front of nine people and I can put my hand on it.

That other thing cannot be argued with by anybody in this room, and I did not know it could not be argued with until about a minute ago, and my boat is at sea and I am the only man in this room who cannot check a word of it. I do not know which of the two of those is about me. I have a feeling I know and I would rather somebody did not say it out loud in a market."

The crossing column was corrected on the slate in front of about nine people, and the two men who corrected it were not asked their names and the correction was not copied out, and the wall copy of the six lines was not touched and did not come off that wall.

---

Nobody asked the man of about fifty-six who had set him on to do that, and he answered that question before it was finished being asked, and it was in about seventy-two words, and nobody thanked him and nobody thanked the man of about thirty-five either.

"Nobody set me on. I land fish and I read. There is no name in what I did this morning and I would like it to stay that way, because a thing with a name in it in a market on a Friday is a party doing a thing, and a party doing a thing in a market gets asked about it by about nine people by the middle of the afternoon."

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and twenty-seven words on what a thing with a seal on it does that a thing without one does not, and nobody in that slip was asked whether he was on the right side of it.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and there is no line on that sheet for a morning at a fish stall and there is not going to be one.

**A man of about fifty-six who lands fish read the sixth line of that account out loud on a working morning in a market and a question about a man's own boat was answered on the spot by a thing none of us put there, and the man it was about was not in the room and his boat was not in the harbor. Forty feet away a slate with a wrong hour on it got argued with and corrected in front of nine people by two men who were standing there at the hour.**

Both of those happened at eleven o'clock on a Friday, and I have had nineteen days to say the second half of that sentence in a room and I have not said it, and a man with a piece of chalk said it this morning in about nine seconds and then went back down to his stall.

I am not going to say the second half of it now either. It is on that slate and it is on that wall and I am not putting it in a room."

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX OF THE FIRST LAY, WHO LANDS FISH AT THAT END STALL AND IS ON NOBODY'S CREW, READ THE SIXTH LINE OF THE ACCOUNT OUT LOUD OFF THE COPY ON THE WALL OF THE ROOM OVER THAT MARKET AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND STOPPED AT THE RING OF BLACK CHALK AT THE FOOT OF IT. A QUESTION A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE ASKED ABOUT HIS OWN BOAT, WHICH IS NOT IN THAT HARBOR AND IS NOT DUE BACK UNTIL SUNDAY, WAS ANSWERED ON THE SPOT BY THAT LINE, AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM COULD HAVE SAID A WORD AGAINST IT. THE CROSSING COLUMN FOR THAT QUAY WAS READ OUT AT THE SAME HOUR AND WAS ARGUED WITH AND CORRECTED IN THE OPEN BY TWO MEN WHO WERE STANDING ON THAT QUAY AT THAT HOUR, AND THE WRONG HOUR IS A WRONG HOUR IN A BOOK IN A MARKET IN FRONT OF NINE PEOPLE. A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAID IN ABOUT TWENTY-SIX WORDS THAT A MAN'S QUESTION HAD BEEN MADE INTO A WALL, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE SAID IN ABOUT FIFTY-ONE WORDS THAT HE DID NOT KNOW WHICH OF THE TWO OF THOSE WAS ABOUT HIM. THE WHOLE OF IT CARRIES NO NAME AND THE MAN WHO DID IT SAID NOBODY SET HIM ON AND ASKED FOR IT TO STAY THAT WAY. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about six in the morning the reason the boy of twenty-two is on that page at all is his own, and is that he has been at that stall for twenty days writing weights and nobody has said a word to him about the water and nobody has asked him where he has been, and that a page of days that only carried the days anybody talked about would be a page of the men of about thirty and older, and that the only instrument in that harbor with a place for a hand that is not asked anything is this one; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason a man of fifty-six was the one who did it is his own, and is that he asked nobody and nobody asked him, and that the whole of what has gone wrong in nineteen days in that harbor has been done by men who were asked, and that the reason he went up those stairs with a piece of chalk is that he lands fish and reads and that is the entire of what he is; and that at about a quarter to twelve the reason the man of about thirty-five was left to say the second half of it himself is his own, and is that a woman of about forty-four said thirty-eight words and then stopped, and that she stopped because she has been in a room on the third of this month and on the seventh and has said the shape of a thing twice already and said on the seventh that she was not going to say it a third time, and that the man of about thirty-five had not said it before and was owed the saying of it by nobody and did it anyway; and that at about half past five the reason he refused to say the second half of that sentence out loud at a bench stone is his own, and is that a man with a piece of chalk said it at eleven in a room full of people who had come up a stair to hear it, and that the same sentence said by him at half past five to an empty slip is not the same sentence, it is the same sentence with him at the front of it, and that he has been the front of nineteen days of things and is tired of it and is not going to do it in a room about a thing that is already done and needs nothing from him; and that at about ten past six the reason the wrong hour on that slate is on the page and not just the right answer is his own, and is that a page of days that only ever carries what happened is a page nobody can use, and that the correction of a crossing column in the open by two men who were standing on the quay at that hour is the single most useful thing that has happened in that harbor in twenty days, and that it happened forty feet from a thing with a seal on it and cost nothing and took about four minutes, and that he is putting both halves of that morning on the same line of this leaf on purpose and that nobody is to read it as a speech.*
