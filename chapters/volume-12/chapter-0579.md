# Chapter 579

*Saturday the Nineteenth Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Room over the Joiner's Shop at the Top of Fish Street Let Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Nine People In It and Four Lines of Correction and One Line of a Man's Own Refusal in Chalk on the End Wall of the Yard Underneath, a Man of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets Writing Out a Refusal on a Page in About Three Hundred and Twenty-Six Words at About Half Past Two and It Being Read Out in That Room by a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands and Read Out a Second Time at the Asking of a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Who Cannot Read, the Cost of It Named Out Loud and Being That He Is Now the Only One of Six Who Can Put a Wrong Hour in It and Cannot Be Found Out in It, the Offer of Four Lines Still Behind a Door With Nothing Written Under Its Fourth Line and Not on That Page, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the nineteenth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street with about nine people in it at two o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the page of refusal written in that room at about half past two is a page and not a line, and it is on no sheet in that harbor and carries no name under it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seventeen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and nineteen days. The eleven words of a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons are on a page and were not answered and nobody is going to answer them this month with a better argument. The offer of four lines is behind a door in the room over that market with nothing written under its fourth line, and a man of about forty-four's no is on a page in a room four hundred yards from it and is not on that paper. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-seven days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The room over that joiner's shop was let out at about two o'clock in the afternoon and about nine people went up into it, and the four lines of correction and the one line of a man's own refusal were in chalk on the flat end wall of that yard underneath them, going about a month out of every rain since the twenty-sixth of January, and nobody standing on those boards looked up at them and about four of them could not have read them if they had.

A man of about forty-four of the first lay came in last and stood with his back to the stair head, and he said what he wanted before he said anything else, and what he wanted was for it to be written out and read back to him before it went on anything anywhere, and that it is a page and not a line and that he is not going to have a line.

---

A man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay wrote it out on a page in that room at about half past two, in a hand that is not the man's, and it is in about three hundred and twenty-six words, and nobody else in that room wrote a word of it and nobody else was asked to.

"I want this written down and read back to me before it goes on anything, and I want the two of us to be clear that it is a page and not a line.

I have said no and I am not going to unsay it to anybody in this room. Four of the five said no, and the fifth has not been asked, and I have not spoken for him and I am not going to. I am the man whose crossings those are. Nobody else's hand is on them, and nobody else is going to be able to say what is in them, and that is the whole of the reason, and I would say it again this afternoon in front of a worse room than this one.

**And here is what it has cost me, and I am naming it in here because it is not money and there is nobody in this room who is able to pay it.** From this afternoon those crossings stop being a record and they start being six men's memory. They were a record on Thursday, because six men could be found out in them. From today it is one man and a page, and I am the only one of the six of us who can read it, and so there is exactly one person on that mark now who can put a wrong hour into it, and nobody will ever find it out, and I will not be found out by anybody.

I have had a thing I could be caught out in since I was twenty-two years old. I have given it up, and I have given it up out loud in a room with about nine people in it, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be asked about it again either, and I would rather have the thing back than have the paper."

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands read it out in that room, word for word, once, in about nine people, and he read it without stopping and he did not say anything of his own before it or after it.

Then a man of about thirty-four of the second lay, who cannot read, and who said no in the open on the fifth of this month and has not been asked anything since, put his hand up, and asked for it to be read out a second time, and it was read out a second time, word for word, the same way.

"That page says a man is going to be alone with a page," he said. "I was one of the four who said no on Monday. Nobody is going to be alone with a page as long as there is a market and a man on the step who can read, and that is not a speech, it is a thing I can do, and I have been able to do it since I was nineteen and I have not been asked to do it by anybody."

Nobody answered him. Nobody argued with him. Nobody thanked the man of about forty-four, and nobody thanked the man of about thirty-four either, and the page went into the inside of that man's coat at about a quarter to three and is not on that bench stone and is not on the sheet that carries the money and is not on the sheet of four heads in the room over that market, and the two of those were named out loud separately in that room before anybody went down those stairs.

Nobody asked the man of about fifty-three anything and he was not in that room. Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one for the particular she holds and she was not in that room. Nobody sent for anybody and nobody was sent for.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and seventy-eight words on what a page with a man's whole weight in it costs, and nobody in that slip was asked to hold it.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and a refusal went onto a page in a room four hundred yards from that board at about half past two this afternoon and it is not on any sheet in this harbor and it has no name under it and no line under it either.

**A man of about forty-four stood with his back to a stair head and said out loud that from this afternoon there is exactly one person on that mark who can put a wrong hour into it and nobody will ever find it out, and that he has given up a thing he could be caught out in since he was twenty-two, and that it is not money and that nobody in that room was able to pay it.** That is the first time in twenty days that a person in this harbor has said what a thing cost and meant something that is not on a slate.

And a man who cannot read put his hand up in that room and had it read out twice and said in about eighty-five words that nobody is going to be alone with a page as long as there is a man on a step who can read, and nobody argued with him and he has not been asked to be a reader and I am not going to ask him.

I have not written the first half of that on the first column and I am not going to. A page a man put his coat over is a page and not a day."

---

**A REFUSAL WAS WRITTEN OUT ON A PAGE IN THE ROOM OVER THAT JOINER'S SHOP AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT A HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO WORDS, IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE MAN'S, BY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX WHO MENDS NETS, AND IT WAS READ OUT IN THAT ROOM IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS, AND READ OUT A SECOND TIME AT THE ASKING OF A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THE SECOND LAY WHO CANNOT READ. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR OF THE FIRST LAY SAID OUT LOUD THAT FOUR OF THE FIVE SAID NO AND THAT THE FIFTH HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AND THAT HE HAS NOT SPOKEN FOR HIM, AND THAT FROM THAT AFTERNOON THE CROSSINGS OF THAT MARK STOP BEING A RECORD AND START BEING SIX MEN'S MEMORY, AND THAT HE IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE SIX WHO CAN READ IT, AND THAT HE HAS GIVEN UP THE ONLY THING HE COULD BE CAUGHT OUT IN SINCE HE WAS TWENTY-TWO, AND THAT IT IS NOT MONEY. THE PAGE WENT INTO THE INSIDE OF HIS COAT AND IS ON NO SHEET AND CARRIES NO NAME UNDER IT. NOBODY ANSWERED HIM AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason that room was let out and not the room over the market is his own, and is that the two rooms are four hundred yards apart and that the two of them have been used for two different things this month, and that a woman of thirty said in thirty words in that room on the tenth what a price is paid by and that a man of about thirty-three said in about two hundred and fifty words in the other one on the ninth what a one book would have done, and that both of those were in rooms and both of them are on the leaf, and that a page about a man's own crossings belongs under the chalk and not over the floor of a market; and that at about half past two the reason he wrote it in a hand that is not the man's is his own, and is that the man of about thirty-four on the fifth of this month asked for that and it was the only thing anybody asked for in nineteen days, and that the man of about forty-four asked for it again on Saturday without being asked to, and that a man who asks for his own words to be written by another hand is a man who expects to be read out to more than once, and that reading it out twice is not a favour to him, it is the only way a page stays true to the mouth that made it; and that at about a quarter to three the reason the man of about thirty-four was in that room at all is his own, and is that nobody sent for him and nobody asked him and he came, and that the first two words out of his mouth in that room were a request and not an opinion, and that this harbor has spent twenty days arguing about what it should say and has not once asked a man who cannot read what he would like to be able to read, and that a man who cannot read in a room with about nine people in it is doing arithmetic of a different kind, and that the arithmetic he did this afternoon came out at one and he gave it away; and that at about half past five the reason he did not put a man's coat over a page on the first column is his own, and is that the first column takes one entry a day and takes the day, and that the day was a page going into a coat at a quarter to three, and that a page in a coat is not a day and cannot be looked up by anybody including him, and that he put the man who read it out twice on it instead, because a thing that is read out twice in a room in front of nine people is a thing that has happened twice and can be checked twice; and that at about ten past six the reason the losing of a man's own fallibility is the only price in twenty days that is not on a slate is his own, and is that he can put a mark and its answer on a page and cannot put on it the thing a man gives up when he is the only one left who can be found out, because that thing is not given up in a room and is given up on a Tuesday morning in March in about four fathoms of water with nobody watching, and that if a coast is going to be honest about what it costs then the honest figure is the one that only that man knows and he has said it out loud once and put it in a coat.*
