# Chapter 554

*Tuesday the Twenty-Fifth Day of January, the Sixty-Sixth Wage Tuesday, the Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Read Out on the Step at the Back of That Market at About Ten o'Clock in the Morning in Front of About Forty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read the Column on the Lid and Not Moving, Nobody Paid a Penny Out of That Chest, a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Being the Sixty-Sixth, the Question Asked in the Room over That Market on Monday Left Standing and Not Answered by Anybody on This Day, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking Out Loud in That Yard Whether Two Pages With No Figure on Either of Them Are Being Put Over the People of This Harbor, and Being Answered in About Seventy Words, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the twenty-fifth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe and was the sixty-sixth wage Tuesday of that harbor. That cistern was not charged on it. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the sixty-sixth. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-four days. There is a sealed sheet on the board at the head of that mole with six lines on it and no figure on it, and the first two lines of it are the same two things that the wage column has said every Tuesday for eleven weeks, and neither of those two is a figure of money. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-two days and nobody has set a figure against it and he was at the west quay for about two hours on the Sunday with a handline and came in with about twenty fish in it and said one word to a man he was working beside.**

---

The step at the back of that market was read out from at ten o'clock in the morning and there is a lid on that step and on the lid of it is the column, twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, in a clerk's hand. About forty people were standing on those boards and about eleven of them cannot read the lid. Nothing on that step moved and the reading took four minutes, and behind the door of that front room the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and nobody in that market asked for it to be opened, and the sixty-sixth week stands fallen due at the close of that day.

---

And a man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood at the end of that slip at about two in the afternoon and asked out loud, in front of about nine people, whether two pages with no figure on either of them were being put over the people of that harbor, and he was answered in about seventy words and did not ask it again and was not thanked for asking it.

"**Yes. One of them has a seal and a man in an office behind it and the other has a bench stone and a slate on it, and both of them have no penny on them, and the one with the seal is going to be believed in about a month and the one with the slate is going to be believed by about four people for about three weeks.**"

He said that was a straight answer and that he had expected something worse, and he asked one more thing at the end of it and only once.

"Who is the four?"

"About four men of this party cannot read that leaf and there is not one of them in the room over that market on a Monday afternoon. It is four men I know by name and it is not four of anybody else's."

He wrote that on the back of a slate and took it home and nobody thanked him for it and he was not asked to bring it back and did not bring it back.

---

And the man who keeps the leaf said out loud at about three in the afternoon, in the open, at the end of that slip, that the question a woman of thirty of no office asked in that room on Monday had not been answered by anybody and was not going to be answered in a slip by him, and he said it in about eighty-one words and about six people heard all of it.

"It is not a question this yard can answer and it is not a question that yard can answer either. **It is hers, it was asked in her room, and it has an hour on it and a name on the minute against it, and a thing with a name and an hour on it is a thing that can wait three months, and a thing that can wait three months is the only kind of waiting this coast has got.**"

A man of about thirty-five of the first lay said that waiting was not the same as saying yes and he was told out loud and once that nobody had said yes, and then the two of them stopped, and nobody in that harbor was asked about it again on that Tuesday.

They stopped for about a minute and a half. The lamp on that bench stone had a crack across the top of the chimney and he has never had it replaced and it has burned that way since the autumn, and in that minute and a half he found that he had been holding the slate in his left hand for long enough that the back of it had gone warm, and that he could not remember deciding to pick it up, and that this was the second time this month he had done something to his own hand without deciding to and that he had a rule about not doing that and had broken it twice and was not going to put it on a page.

---

And at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and the day had not touched it, and he said one more thing about the Tuesday than he has said on any other Tuesday.

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. **Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty envelopes in a chest behind a shut door, read out on a step at ten this morning for the sixty-sixth time, and it did not move, and that is a harbor finding out what it owes and not a harbor paying anything.**"

Nobody was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Tuesday, and nobody was thanked for the sixty-sixth wage Tuesday and nobody was thanked for the seventy words and nobody was thanked for the two lines on a slate and the door of that shed stood open on the slip from ten in the morning until about six at night.

---

**THE COLUMN WAS READ OUT ON THAT STEP AT ABOUT TEN AND DID NOT MOVE AND NOBODY WAS PAID A PENNY OUT OF THAT CHEST AND THE SIXTY-SIXTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. THE QUESTION A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ASKED IN THAT ROOM ON THE MONDAY WAS NOT ANSWERED BY ANYBODY TODAY AND IS NOT GOING TO BE ANSWERED IN A SLIP BY ANYBODY IN THIS YARD. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS ASKED OUT LOUD WHETHER TWO PAGES WITH NO FIGURE ON EITHER OF THEM ARE BEING PUT OVER THAT HARBOR AND WAS ANSWERED IN ABOUT THIRTY WORDS AND ASKED ONE MORE THING ONCE, BEING WHO THE FOUR ARE, AND WAS TOLD THAT THEY ARE FOUR MEN HE KNOWS BY NAME AND NOT FOUR OF ANYBODY ELSE'S. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-fifth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe and was the sixty-sixth wage Tuesday of that harbor, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and that nobody was paid a penny out of that chest and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of that day, being the sixty-sixth, and that the column was read out on the step at the back of that market at about ten o'clock in the morning by a clerk of that harbor authority with his own name and the hour at the foot of the reading in front of about forty people of whom about twenty cannot read the column on the lid, and that the chest stood shut behind that door from ten until four and nobody asked to have it opened; and that the question asked in the room over that market on the twenty-fourth of this month, being whether that harbor will accept that paper as its account of the fourteenth, was not answered by anybody on that day and was not answered by anybody on this day, and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud at about three in the afternoon in about eighty-one words that it is that woman's question and that it has an hour and a name against it in her own minute and that a thing with a name and an hour on it is a thing that can wait three months; and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said that waiting was not the same as saying yes and was told out loud and once that nobody had said yes, and that the two of them stopped and that nobody in that harbor was asked about it again on that day; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked at about two in the afternoon in front of about nine people whether two pages with no figure on either of them were being put over that harbor, and was answered in about seventy words, being that one of them has a seal and a man in an office behind it and the other has a bench stone and a slate on it and that both have no penny on them and that the one with the seal will be believed in about a month and the one with the slate will be believed by about four people for about three weeks; and that the same man asked one more thing once, being who the four are, and was told that they are four men the man who keeps the leaf knows by name and that there is not one of them in that room over that market on a Monday afternoon, and that he wrote the answer on the back of a slate and took it home and that nobody thanked him for it and that he was not asked to bring it back and did not bring it back; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence; and that the third column took no line and the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the man of about thirty-three was not thanked for asking his question is his own and is that a man who asks a question in the open on a Tuesday afternoon in front of nine people has spent something and a man who says nothing back to him has not given it back, and that being answered straight is not the same as being thanked and that the two of those are very far apart in that harbor and that about eleven people on a slip cannot tell the difference and are right not to try; and that the reason the four men were named as four men he knows by name and not as a figure is his own, and is that a figure of four can be carried up that coast to a mill or a chandler or a yard and used against them without anybody ever having to say a name, and that a name on a slate is heavy and slow and can only be used on the person it is on, and that on this coast a person is a better lock than a number; and that the reason a cistern was not charged on a Tuesday is that harbor's own and not this yard's, and is that the cistern is a Monday and a Thursday and that the figure in chalk is neither, and that a harbor that pays a day of a berth fund on a Tuesday and charges a cistern on a Monday and a Thursday and reads a column on a Tuesday is a harbor that has decided what kind of place it is going to be, and that it decided that in the spring and has not been asked to decide it since; and that the reason the six lines on that sealed sheet are not in the first column of that leaf is his own, and is that the first column takes one entry a day and that the entry for a day is the day, and that a Crown account is not the day and is not a thing that happens in this yard, and that putting it in a column that a stranger can count by counting days would be the way to make a paper sound like a person.*
