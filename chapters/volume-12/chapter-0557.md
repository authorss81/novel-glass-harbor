# Chapter 557

*Friday the Twenty-Eighth Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Three People Asked in That Yard in About Nine People in About Twenty-Four Minutes to Hold One Channel Each, Being a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands and a Woman of About Forty-Four Who Keeps a Shut Store on the Second Lay and a Man of About Fifty-Three Who Owns a Boat of About Fourteen Tons, Each of Them Saying Yes and Each of Them Saying One Thing He or She Would Not Do, a Man of About Fifty-Three Asking Out Loud in About Nineteen Words What Happens to the Other Two If One of Us Stops It and the Other Two Are Not There, and That Question Not Being Answered by Anybody, the Man of About Thirty-Two Beginning an Answer at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon and Stopping Himself at About Ten Words, Three Names Going Onto a Second Page of Hours Beside the First at About Half Past Four in the Afternoon With the Third Rule Copied Out From the First and Nine Words Written Under the Copy in a Different Ink and No Answer to Them, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the twenty-eighth day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-seven days. The second page of hours in that shed, which is not the page that went out on the water on the Thursday and carries no name of any man who went in it, carries three names on it and the third rule copied out from the first page and nine words under the rule in a different ink and no answer to the nine words, and the three of those names are not a crew and are not a body and have not asked for anything and have not offered for anything. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-five days and he has not been asked to hold one of those three channels and nobody thought of asking him and he is not on the water.**

---

And the three of them were asked in that yard at about one o'clock in the afternoon, in about nine people, in about twenty-four minutes, one at a time and out loud, and all three of them said yes and all three of them said one thing they would not do, and none of the three was thanked and the man who asked did not say a word back when the first of them answered.

The first one asked was a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, and he said yes in four words and then said the thing he would not do in about twenty.

"I will hold one. I will not stand in that yard at two in the morning and wait to be asked whether I am wanted."

The second one asked was the woman who keeps that shut store on the second lay, who is of about forty-four, and she said yes in four words and then said the thing she would not do in about fifty-nine, and about nine people heard it and none of them understood all of it.

"I will hold one. **I will not be one of three when one of the three is not here, because on the Sunday you asked me a second question in front of nine people and I had nothing to say, and there was a man doing sums on a slate in that room who is deaf in the ear you would shout in.**"

Nobody asked her which man and nobody asked her about the slate and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that the page of hours carries a rule about three people and that a page of hours does not carry a rule about a man with a slate, and that the two of those are not the same instrument and neither of them can carry the other.

---

And the third one asked was a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons, and he said yes in one word, and he asked his question out loud at about ten past one in the afternoon, in about nineteen words, in front of about nine people, and it has not been answered by anybody in that harbor and it was written under the rule on that second page in a different ink at about half past four in the afternoon and no answer was written under it.

"**What happens to the other two if one of us stops it and the other two are not there.**"

A man of about thirty-five of the first lay, who has his own hand at the foot of the fifth line of chalk in a street four hundred yards from that slip, said out loud that that was the question and that it had been the question since the middle of December, and that a page of hours with a rule about three people on it and two of the three asleep is a page that is about a thing that has not happened yet.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said that the other two would carry it until one of them could not, and he said that in about ten words and then he took it back himself and said it was not his to say, and the man of about fifty-three said that a man who takes ten words back is a man who has just answered the question and that it was not an answer.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two began an answer to it and stopped himself, and everybody on that slip waited for about half a minute and nobody filled the half minute, and he has not started it again.

"I had it. I had it for about ten words and it was the wrong ten words and I am not going to say them in front of nine people and have them carried up that coast in a slip of somebody's, because..."

He stopped there and did not finish it and about nine people on that slip stood there and about two of them were about to say something and did not, and the man of about fifty-three said one thing and only one thing about it and then the three of them went and did their own work and a second page of hours went on that bench stone at about half past four in the afternoon beside the page that went out on the water on the Thursday, and it carries three names on it and the third rule copied out from the first page and nine words under the rule.

The nine words are these, and they are the one question and nothing else, and there are nine of them and no answer is under them, and the same nine words are the only copy of that question anywhere in that harbor.

1. *The other two, if one stops it. Not answered.*

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and said in about ninety-five words what three people are and what three people are not, and he had it off the page and not off anybody's mouth, and nobody in that harbor was asked whether it was true.

"Ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence. Unmoved today and the only thing on that page of hours is three names and a question. **Three people who have each said yes are three people and not a crew and not a body, and nothing binds anybody who has not said it, and the two of those facts are the reason a page of hours can be believed at all and the reason it is going to fail on a Tuesday in a fortnight.**"

No sum owing to that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Friday, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that day, and nobody was thanked for three yeses and nobody was thanked for a question of nineteen words and nobody was thanked for a man who took ten words back.

---

**THREE PEOPLE WERE ASKED IN THAT YARD TO HOLD ONE CHANNEL EACH AND ALL THREE SAID YES AND EACH SAID ONE THING THEY WOULD NOT DO, AND NONE OF THE THREE IS A CREW AND NONE OF THEM IS A BODY AND NOTHING BINDS ANYBODY WHO HAS NOT SAID IT. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE ASKED OUT LOUD, IN ABOUT NINETEEN WORDS, WHAT HAPPENS TO THE OTHER TWO IF ONE OF US STOPS IT AND THE OTHER TWO ARE NOT THERE, AND NOBODY ANSWERED IT. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO BEGAN AN ANSWER AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND STOPPED HIMSELF AT ABOUT TEN WORDS AND DID NOT START IT AGAIN. THE QUESTION WENT UNDER THE RULE ON THE SECOND PAGE OF HOURS IN A DIFFERENT INK AND NO ANSWER WENT UNDER IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-eighth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that three people were asked in that yard at about one o'clock in the afternoon in about nine people in about twenty-four minutes to hold one channel each, one at a time and out loud, being a man of about thirty-three who hires hands and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a store on the second lay and a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons, and that all three said yes and that each of the three said one thing he or she would not do, being that the first will not stand in that yard at two in the morning and wait to be asked whether he is wanted, being that the second will not be one of three when one of the three is not here, and being that the third said nothing at all about a thing he would not do and said his question instead; and that the man of about fifty-three asked out loud at about ten past one in the afternoon in about nineteen words what happens to the other two if one of us stops it and the other two are not there, and that nobody answered it; and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said that that had been the question since the middle of December, and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands answered it in about ten words and took it back himself and was told by the man of about fifty-three that a man who takes ten words back has answered the question and that it was not an answer; and that the man of about thirty-two began an answer at about two o'clock in the afternoon, stopped himself at about ten words, did not finish it, was waited on for about half a minute by about nine people and has not started it again; and that a second page of hours went on that bench stone at about half past four in the afternoon beside the page that went out on the water on the Thursday and carries no name of any man who went in it, with three names on it and the third rule copied out from the first page and nine words under the rule in a different ink, being the other two if one stops it, not answered, and that no answer is under them; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence; and that no person on that water is a member of anything and no sum owing to that harbor was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the man of about thirty-two stopped himself at ten words is his own, and is that the ten words he had were a promise, and that a promise made in front of nine people on a slip in that harbor is the one kind of sentence that gets carried up that coast inside a month in somebody's mouth, and that the man who hires hands has just shown him what happens to a sentence that leaves that slip; and that the reason the man of about fifty-three said nothing about a thing he would not do is his own, and is that he has already said once in that harbor the thing that is true about that coast and did not say a second thing beside it, and that a man who has said one true thing out loud in a room is a man who cannot be asked to say two, because the second one is always the one that is cheaper for him and worse for everybody else; and that the reason a second page of hours has three names on it and a question under the copied rule with no answer is the man of about thirty-eight's, and is that a page with three names and an answer under the rule would be a page that says what happens, and that what happens is a thing a person finds out at about two in the morning, and that a page of hours is not allowed to find out anything in advance of the person who is on the water; and that the reason the woman of about forty-four named a man with a slate on the second day is hers, and is that the third rule of that page is about three people being in a room and that there is a fourth kind of person who is in the room and is not in the room, and that on this coast that kind of person has a name and a slate and has had one for a year, and that no rule on any page of hours has ever been written with him in it and that is the whole of what she said and she has not said it a second time.*
