# Chapter 551

*Saturday the Twenty-Second Day of January, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of That Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going Into It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, Two Notes Off the Outer Water at About Half Past Ten at Night at Morrow Quay Counted on Two Watches and Getting the Same Figure of Forty-Seven Seconds Between Them, the Same Two Notes Counted at That Port Four Days Down This Coast and at a Harbor About Nine Miles Up It, Three of the Five Marks Answering and Two of Them Not, Nobody at That Port Having a Name for Whoever Is Putting It Out, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the twenty-second day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and forty-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighty-nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixtieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-one days. The board at the head of that mole carries two papers with a seal on them and no figure on either, and one of the two has been on that board since the third of December and has not been opened and is not going to be opened, and the light that is meant to stand over that board is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this evening and there were nine hulls there on the twenty-first, and nobody in that harbor has said out loud that there are fewer. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been forty-nine days and he has not been to the road with the pump on it and nobody has sent for him.**

---

And the shipyard on that quay took the afternoon sun on a hull of about nine tons and on a north wall that is a fortnight old, and about four hundred yards away the same sun was on the front of a chandler's counter and on the flat of a bench stone, and the man who keeps the leaf put a slate on that stone at about ten in the morning and did not write on it, because there is no figure to go on it and a slate with a figure on it every day of the year is a thing a man stops seeing.

---

And at about half past ten at night two notes came off the outer water off the front of that quay, and they were not a bell, and no hand was on anything.

The man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay heard them first and did not go outside to hear them and stood in the door of that shed with the door open on the slip. The man of about thirty-three who hires hands was about nine feet behind him and came out onto the slip and stood beside him and did not say anything for a while, and then the two of them stood there until a third one came, and nobody went to get a third one.

"It was not a bell," the man of about thirty-three said afterwards, on the slip, to about four people. "A bell goes on and then it goes on some more. That went and it stopped."

They counted it on their own watches and not on a clock in that shed, and each of them wrote a figure on the back of his own hand, and the figures were the same figure, and the figure was forty-seven seconds. There had been two notes and the gap between them had been forty-seven seconds, and the gap after the second note had been longer than that and neither of them had counted the second gap to the end of it.

"That is a figure and it is the first figure this harbor has had off that water in a year, and there is not one penny of that on any sheet of mine and I am not going to put one there."

Nobody answered him and nobody thanked him and both of those things were correct.

---

And he was on that slip by himself at about a quarter to eleven and there is no fire in that shed tonight, and he stood there and worked out the thing he had been avoiding for about an hour, which is that he could not tell a single person in this harbor what the two notes had been, and that the reason he could not was not the dark and was not the water. It was that a man who has counted one gap and written it on the back of his own hand has a thing, and a man who has a thing gets asked what it means by somebody within a week, and he does not have an answer, and he has found out over ten days that a man who is asked a question he cannot answer ends up being the man who is wrong about everything else he is right about.

There was a lamp on that bench stone and the flat of it was wet where the rain had come in under the door and had gone out again. He turned the slate over and looked at the two hands, which were not his own, and the two figures on them, which were the same figure, and he did not write anything under them, and he went up the quay.

---

And the same two notes were heard on the same night at that port four days down this coast, and at a harbor about nine miles up it, and the count of forty-seven seconds was put on the back of a slate at that port by a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay and got the same figure there. Three of the five marks on that coast answered that night and two of them did not, and the two that did not are the two that did not, and there is no name at that port for whoever is putting it out, and a man there was asked on the Saturday morning by a woman of about thirty-four of that commonwealth whether he thought it was a boat and he said he did not think it was a boat.

None of that was in Morrow Quay on the Saturday night. Morrow Quay had a figure of forty-seven seconds and a man who cannot read wrote it on the back of his own hand so that a man who could read could look at the hand, and the two of them stood on that slip until about a quarter to eleven and then went to their own houses, and the door of that shed stood open on the slip the whole of the time and there is no fire in that shed tonight.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and the day had not touched it and did not tell the four men on that slip that the sheet and the number were the only two things in that harbor a stranger could check.

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. Not moved this morning and not moved this afternoon, and the reason it did not move is that the day had nothing in it that costs anything, and a day with nothing in it that costs anything is not a good day and is not a bad day and is the ordinary kind. **Forty-seven seconds, twice, on two watches, on the back of two hands, and about eleven people in that market cannot read either hand.**"

Nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Saturday, and nobody was thanked for the two watches and nobody was thanked for the forty-seven seconds and the door of that shed was open on the slip from ten in the morning until about a quarter to eleven at night.

---

**NOTHING WENT OUT ON ANY LINE OF THAT SHEET ON THIS SATURDAY AND NOTHING WENT INTO IT IN ANY HAND AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR ANYTHING. ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE CAME INTO THAT YARD AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT WENT OUT OF IT AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND TWENTY-EIGHT PLUS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT LESS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THAT BAG UNDER THE SLATE. TWO NOTES CAME OFF THE OUTER WATER OFF THAT QUAY AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AT NIGHT AND THEY WERE NOT A BELL AND NO HAND WAS ON ANYTHING, AND TWO MEN COUNTED THE GAP BETWEEN THEM ON TWO WATCHES AND BOTH OF THEM WROTE FORTY-SEVEN SECONDS ON THE BACK OF THEIR OWN HANDS AND THE TWO FIGURES ARE THE SAME FIGURE, AND THE SAME TWO NOTES AND THE SAME FIGURE ARE ON A SLATE FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST, AND THREE OF FIVE MARKS ANSWERED AND TWO OF THEM DID NOT AND NOBODY HAS A NAME. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-second day of January at about ten minutes to eleven at night in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that nothing went out on any line of that sheet in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence; and that two notes came off the outer water off the head of that quay at about half past ten at night and were not a bell and no hand was on anything, and that the gap between the two of them was counted on two separate watches by a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay and by a man of about thirty-three who hires hands and that each of them wrote his figure on the back of his own hand and that the two figures are the same figure, being forty-seven seconds, and that the second gap after the second note was longer than the first and was not counted to the end of it by either of them; and that the same two notes were heard on the same night at that port four days down this coast and at a harbor about nine miles up it, and that the count of forty-seven seconds is on the back of a slate at that port and that three of the five marks on that coast answered and two of them did not and that nobody at that port has a name for whoever is putting it out; and that the man who keeps the leaf put a slate on that bench stone at about ten in the morning and did not write on it; and that no figure of forty-seven seconds is on any line of that sheet and none was put there and none was asked for; and that there are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole on this day and there were nine hulls there on the twenty-first of this month and no one in that harbor has said out loud that there are fewer; and that a question was asked in that yard at about a quarter past ten at night on the twenty-first of this month and is entered in a minute as asked with no name against it and is not asked again and is not the thing that is on the slip tonight, and that the thing on the slip tonight has a figure on the back of two hands and the question in that yard has no figure at all on any paper in the world; and that the third column took no line and nobody was appointed to it and a boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of it and his surname is on no page of that leaf and he is not on the water; and that the fourth column took no line and stands at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither closed nor struck.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about ten minutes to eleven at night in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the man of about thirty-three would not put the figure of forty-seven seconds on the sheet is his own, and is that a sheet with money on it has one kind of figure on it and a sheet that carries what a coast heard would carry a second kind, and that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can check the first kind in a minute and has no way at all of checking the second, and that a man who puts both on one piece of paper teaches every reader of that paper to stop believing the first; and that the reason he was asked to come and stand on that slip and did not say what he thought it was is his own, and is that the two of them had heard the same two notes and had each of them got the same figure off his own watch, and that two men with two watches is the only kind of proof that harbor has ever had that it has ever put any weight on, and that a figure got by two men with two watches is a figure and not a belief and that there is no argument in that harbor that can put an argument against it; and that the reason the second gap was left uncounted is that of the two men on that slip, and is that a man who has counted one gap and can count the second one has found out that the sound is a repeating thing and not a pair of things, and that neither of them wanted to know that at about half past ten at night on a Saturday in January with four people listening and no light in that shed but a lamp on a bench stone; and that the reason a man of about thirty-one at that port was asked on the Saturday morning by a woman of about thirty-four of that commonwealth whether he thought it was a boat, and said that he did not think it was a boat, is his own, and is that a man crewing in a boat at night hears an oar and a man standing in a dark yard hears a hull, and that neither of them knows what is out there, and that the whole of what either of them said about it is a negative, and that the negative is the only part of it that anybody has written down.*
