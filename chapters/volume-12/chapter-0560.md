# Chapter 560

*Monday the Thirty-First Day of January, a Rota Day, No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, That Cistern Having Been Charged Twice in the Ten Days of This Month and on the Twenty-Fourth and the Twenty-Seventh and on No Other of Them, Twenty-Two Trips in Eleven on Each of Those Two Mornings and Not One of Them Walked on by Anybody and Not a Penny of Either of Them on Any Sheet, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, Ten Days Closed in About Four Hundred and Twenty Words at About Half Past Five in the Afternoon in Front of About Eleven People of Whom About Five Cannot Read What Is on That Bench Stone, Three Figures Off That Sheet That a Stranger Can Take in a Minute, One Figure Off the Back of Two Hands That Two Men Counted on Two Watches, One Figure Off a Slate Four Days Down That Coast, and One Thing on That Mole That Nobody in That Harbor Can Check and Cannot Ask About, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Standing at the End of That Slip Saying Out Loud That Her Own Question of the Twenty-Fourth Is Still Hers and Is Still Standing and Is Not Going to Be Answered on a Slip, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the thirty-first day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. No charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and is going to still be off it. That cistern was charged twice in the ten days of this month, being the twenty-fourth and the twenty-seventh, and on no other day of them, and the water that went into it on those two mornings was not used for anything at any hour of either of those two days. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred days. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and the mole head is dark and the light that is meant to stand over that board is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. The proof is in a locked case on a long table in the room over that market and it was not read out on any of the ten days of this month and nobody in that harbor has got an answer out of it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-eight days and no figure has been set against it and nobody has sent for him.**

---

There is no charge on that cistern this morning and there has not been one since the twenty-seventh, and the name at the head of that rota board has been off it since the spring. Twenty-two trips went into that cistern in the ten days of this month, eleven on the twenty-fourth and eleven on the twenty-seventh, and there was not one of the twenty-two that anybody walked on, and not one of them is a line on any sheet in this harbor and not one of them is a figure.

---

And the last of the ten days was the ordinary kind. A man of about thirty-five of the first lay saw his own five lines of chalk at the top of Fish Street going a little soft at the edges of the letters and said out loud that it would want doing again in about a fortnight, and nobody told him to do it and nobody thanked him for saying it and he did not do it. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay came down the quay at about two o'clock in the afternoon and stood at the end of that slip for about ten minutes and nobody asked him anything, and he did not say anything, and he went back up the quay. The three names are still on the second page of hours and the nine words are still under the copied rule with no answer under them, and the page they are on is still not the page anybody goes in the water on.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon, on the flat of that bench stone, with the door of that shed open on the slip and about eleven people standing in two hundred yards of it of whom about five cannot read what is in his hand, the man who keeps the leaf closed ten days in about four hundred and twenty words, and he read them off his own hand, and it was not a speech and nobody called it one and he did not raise his voice and he did not say it twice.

"Ten days. **One thousand six hundred and eighty pence came into this yard and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence went out of it, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way, and there are twenty-eight pence in that bag, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and it has been twenty-eight every day of the ten and it is not going to be nothing this year.** One line went out on that sheet in the ten days, being four hundred and forty-eight pence, being a bottle of air at two hundred and eighty and a shackle and a link of chain and three fathoms of line, and not one person is in it. Twenty-two trips went into that cistern and the water in it is at the top of the butt. Two walls came down bare and went up again unrepaired. One man went into about four fathoms for twenty-five minutes and came up with a new shackle. Three people said yes to holding one thing each and one of the three asked a question in nineteen words that nobody has answered, and one man of about thirty-four asked to be asked, and a man of about forty-six said no for the second time in a year, and a sheet came round nine houses and was carried back to the bench it started from."

He said the second half of it standing up and slower, and this is what he said, and it was about a hundred and eighty of the four hundred and twenty words.

"And what the ten days proved is two things and not one. A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take three figures off that sheet in a minute and find that they agree, and those are the only three figures this harbor has got. And two men can count a gap between two sounds on two watches and write it on the back of their own hands, and the same figure is on a slate four days down that coast, and it was heard in three harbors on one night, and that is the first thing in a year that a person in this market who cannot read a page can go out and check for himself. **The other thing is on a board at the head of that mole and it has six lines and a seal and no figure on it, and every line of it is true, and there is no way in this harbor to ask it a single question, and that is why the two things I have just said out loud are worth anything at all.**"

Nobody in that slip said a word. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands looked at the boards for a while. Nobody thanked him, and the five men of that eleven who cannot read what was in his hand had all of it said out loud to them in the open and that was the whole of the use of it.

---

And the woman of thirty of no office had come down the quay to that slip on her own at about ten past five and had stood at the end of it for about twenty minutes, and she said one thing at about twenty to six, out loud, in front of everybody, and it was about a hundred and twenty-eight words, and it was the last thing said on that slip that day.

"That question of mine from Monday is still mine and it is still standing where I put it, and it has not been answered by anybody and I am not going to answer it out here, and I am not going to answer it in that room either until I have been down to the people who cannot read and heard what they make of six lines that are all true. **The column is read out again tomorrow at about ten and it will be the sixty-seventh time and it will not move, and that is a harbor finding out what it owes, and it is not me and it is not anybody on this slip and it goes on happening whether any of us ever answers anything.**"

She said it once and went up the quay and nobody in that slip asked her anything and nobody in that slip thanked her and the man of about thirty-four of the second lay, who had gone back up the quay at about two o'clock, was not there when she said it and was not sent for.

---

Nothing owed by that harbor or by anybody in it was paid, advanced, cut back, bettered, struck or closed on that Monday, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that day, and nobody was thanked for ten days and nobody was thanked for the four hundred and twenty words and nobody was thanked for a question that is still standing and nobody was thanked for a column that will not move.

---

**TEN DAYS ARE CLOSED AND ONE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE CAME INTO THAT YARD AND ONE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY WENT OUT OF IT AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IS IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND IT HAS BEEN TWENTY-EIGHT ON EVERY DAY OF THE TEN. ONE LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET IN THE TEN DAYS AT FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO MARKS, TEN SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND NOT ONE PERSON IS IN IT. A FIGURE OF FORTY-SEVEN SECONDS WAS COUNTED ON TWO WATCHES BY TWO MEN, WRITTEN ON THE BACK OF TWO HANDS, AND PUT ON A SLATE FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST, AND THREE OF FIVE MARKS ANSWERED IT ON ONE NIGHT. A SHEET WITH SIX TRUE LINES AND A SEAL ON IT AND NO FIGURE ON IT IS ON THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AND CANNOT BE ASKED ANYTHING. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAYS HER QUESTION IS STILL STANDING. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL DID NOT CHANGE AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the thirty-first day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday, and that that cistern is charged on the twenty-fourth and the twenty-seventh of this month and on no other of the ten days, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that the ten days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day are closed on that leaf at two hundred and ninety-three and one thousand three hundred and thirty-nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first figure nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those ten days and that the derivation of them is on the page and is not on the page; and that one thousand six hundred and eighty pence came into that yard over the ten days and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that one line went onto that sheet in the ten days and that line is the four hundred and forty-eight pence of the twenty-seventh, being air for one man for one descent at two hundred and eighty pence a man and a shackle and a link of chain and three fathoms of line, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on any of the ten days; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence; and that twenty-two trips went into that cistern in the ten days, being eleven on the twenty-fourth and eleven on the twenty-seventh, and that no pence of either charge is on any sheet and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it; and that the two walls came down bare on the twenty-third and the thirtieth and went up again unrepaired and not whole; and that the man who keeps the leaf closed those ten days in about four hundred and twenty words at about half past five in the afternoon of this day in front of about eleven people of whom about five cannot read what was in his hand, and that three figures can be taken off that sheet in a minute and that a figure of forty-seven seconds was counted on two watches and put on the back of two hands and is on a slate four days down that coast, and that a sheet with six true lines and a seal on it and no figure on it is on the board at the head of that mole and cannot be asked anything; and that a woman of thirty of no office came down that quay on her own at about ten past five and said at about twenty to six in about a hundred and twenty-eight words that the question she asked in that room on the twenty-fourth is still hers and is still standing and is not going to be answered on a slip or in that room until she has been down to the people who cannot read, and that the column is read out again tomorrow at about ten for the sixty-seventh time and will not move; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay came down that quay at about two o'clock in the afternoon, stood at the end of that slip for about ten minutes, said nothing, was asked nothing and went back up that quay, and that he was not there at twenty to six and was not sent for; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the ten days are put in a hand and read off it and not said out of a head is his own, and is that a man who has said the same kind of thing out loud for ten days has a rhythm in it by the Friday and about five people who cannot read would have got the rhythm instead of the figures, and that the whole of a smooth page is that the listener cannot tell which of the words were the ones that mattered; and that the reason the second half of the four hundred and twenty words was said standing up and slower is his own, and is that the first half is money and can be checked by anybody and the second half is a comparison, and a comparison that is said at the same speed as a figure is a figure, and that on that slip there are about five men who have been handed figures for a year by people who meant well; and that the reason the two things that were proved are not set against the one thing on that mole is his own, and is that a man who says that the six true lines are worth nothing because they cannot be checked has said the thing that the man who sealed them wants him to say, and that a sealed sheet is only dangerous to a coast when somebody on that coast agrees that a thing which cannot be checked is worth nothing, and that he is not going to be the one who says it, and that the whole of the ten days was spent keeping two different kinds of thing in the same harbor without setting one against the other, and that that is not a victory and is the first time it has been possible to do it at all; and that the reason the three yeses and the one refusal and the one asking to be asked are counted in the same four hundred and twenty words as the air and the shackle is his own, and is that a man who lists the costs of ten days in the order of what he can put a figure on has written a bill and not a cost, and that a refusal on a wall in chalk with a man's own hand at the foot of it is going to be asked about in three months by somebody who thinks he is owed an answer, and that the woman of about forty-four who is one of the three is going to find that the one she was worried about is a man doing sums on a slate and not a man in a boat, and that neither of those two has a figure and that there is no line on any sheet in this harbor that can carry either of them.*
