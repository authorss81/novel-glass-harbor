# Chapter 572

*Saturday the Twelfth Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge on That Cistern on It, a Paper of Four Lines Coming Ashore at About Seven in the Morning in a Bagged Canvas Slip With No Crown Hand on the Slip, Carrying a Crown Instrument Made at That Port for a Mark in Exchange for a Copy of a Local Crew's Own Crossings, Read Out at About Eleven in the Morning in Front of About Twenty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read It, and Read Out Again on the Step at the Back of That Market at About Half Past Eleven for Those of Them, Correctly, a Man of About Twenty-Six of the Third Lay, One of the Six Who Keep That Mark, Saying Out Loud in About a Hundred and Seventy-Eight Words That It Is Not for Them to Decide Alone, and a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Not Pushing Him After That, the Paper Not Going onto the Board at the Head of That Mole, a Boat Going Down That Coast on the Saturday and Up It on the Monday, a Day and a Night Against Four Days Each Way for the Cart, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the twelfth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that market with twenty people in it, and in a slip outside a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street with about nine people on it.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the offer that came ashore at about seven in the morning was not priced and is not on that sheet and nothing about it is on that sheet. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and ten days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twelve days. The page on four tacks on the boards of that market carries four names and five marks and no name of this party is on it and nobody has asked for one. The two pages of hours on that bench stone are the two they have always been, being one with two names on it and one with three, and the nine words under the copied rule on the second of them have no answer under them. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The slip came into that harbor at about seven in the morning on the Saturday and put a canvas bag over the side without a hand on it, the way the other two came, and the bag had no Crown writing on it anywhere, and a man of about thirty-three who hires hands carried it up the front street under his arm to the room over the floor of that market and put it on a table and did not open it in front of anybody, and then opened it.

It is a paper of four lines. Here they are, and there are four of them.

1. *A Crown instrument, made at that port, will be given for the mark named at the head of this paper, and it will carry a number and a seal, and the number will be used in place of the hand of any person keeping that mark.*
2. *The keeper of that mark will give once a copy of the crossings of that mark as he keeps them, in his own hand, and the copy will be held in that port in a place where any person may go and look at it.*
3. *No person is asked for a name. No person is asked to give up the keeping of that mark. The mark, and any thing floating in it, is not the subject of this paper.*
4. *This paper is not a claim on any boat and not a claim on any person, and any person may write under it what they make of it.*

The man of about thirty-three read all four out loud in that room at about eleven in the morning in front of about twenty people, of whom about eleven could not read what was in front of them, and he read the third line slowly because that is the line with the two sentences in it that are not saying the same thing as each other.

---

A man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay took it out to the step at the back of that market at about half past eleven and read it out again there, for the same eleven people, standing on the step with his back to the wall so they could hear him.

He read every word of it correctly, including the third line, and he read the second line twice because a woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay asked him from the floor to read the second line again and say it slow, and he did. Nobody corrected him on anything and nobody said a word about the last time.

He said one thing afterwards, out loud, in about fifty-five words, to about forty people on those boards, and it was not a speech and nobody called it one.

"I have been a reader all my life and I have had a word wrong in this market once, on the eighth, and it is still the worst thing I have done here. **Today I read four lines I did not write and I got nothing wrong, and that is what a man is for.**"

---

And it was after that, on the same boards, that a man of about twenty-six of the third lay stood up, and he is one of the six men who keep the third mark, and the crossings of that third mark are in the hand of one man and in no other, and he said out loud in about a hundred and seventy-eight words that it is not for them to decide alone.

"I have read those four lines twice and there is nothing in the first three of them that anybody could object to, and I want that said first, because in about four minutes everybody in this market is going to talk about the fourth one and forget that I said the first three.

**This is not ours to decide by ourselves.** I am one of six on that mark and I have the other five twenty miles off on the ground or four days off that coast and I do not have four days, and the other five have not been asked anything at all this week. I am not going to hand a paper a yes on behalf of five men who are not standing here, and I am not going to hand it a no either, and that is the whole of what I have come up here to say.

A thing that needs six answers does not get one answer off a slip in a market, whatever the fourth line says about writing under it."

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands opened his mouth once and shut it again and did not push him, and a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that he had been ready to say that six men are not in the room and could be written to, and the man of about twenty-six said that they could, and that a letter goes down on a boat and a boat takes a day and a night against four days each way for the cart, and that a boat goes down that coast on the Saturday and comes back up it on the Monday, and that a letter sent down on the Saturday would be read on the Wednesday and the answer would be on this quay on the Monday, and that he cannot hold a room past the middle of the week.

Nobody answered him. Nobody thanked him. The paper was not nailed to the board at the head of that mole and it is not on that board, and the fourth line under it has nothing written under it, and the paper went back into the canvas bag and the bag went behind the door of that room where the man of about thirty-three keeps things he has not decided about.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and fifty-six words on what a paper with four lines on it costs before anybody has said no to it, and nobody in that slip was asked what he thought of the offer.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and no line went onto that sheet at about seven in the morning when that paper came ashore or at any other hour of that day, and that is not an oversight and I am not going to let it look like one.

**Four lines of paper came into that harbor with a mark on the head of them and two sentences on the third of those lines that say a thing is not a claim on a boat, and the two sentences are on the paper and not in anybody's mouth, and a man of twenty-six stood up in a market and said out loud that the first thing a person does with a paper that needs six answers is not to give one of them off a slip. That is the second time in nine days that a man in this harbor has stood up and said what a paper cannot do, and the first time was a woman of thirty and the two of them were four hundred yards apart and neither of them has been thanked and neither of them was thanked for the other one.**

There is no figure on that slip this afternoon. There is a mark, a number, a seal and a copy, and I have not got the price of any of the four, and a man who says no on Monday has not been written down anywhere yet."

**A PAPER OF FOUR LINES CAME ASHORE AT ABOUT SEVEN IN THE MORNING IN A BAGGED CANVAS SLIP WITH NO CROWN HAND ON THE SLIP AND WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE ROOM OVER THAT MARKET AND AGAIN AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN ON THE STEP AT THE BACK OF IT, CORRECTLY, INCLUDING THE THIRD LINE, BY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX WHO MENDS NETS. IT WAS NOT NAILED TO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AND IT IS NOT ON THAT BOARD, AND ITS FOURTH LINE HAS NOTHING WRITTEN UNDER IT. IT IS AN INSTRUMENT FOR A MARK AGAINST A COPY OF A CREW'S OWN CROSSINGS, ITS THIRD LINE SAYS IN ITS OWN WORDS THAT THE MARK AND ANY THING FLOATING IN IT IS NOT THE SUBJECT OF THE PAPER, AND ITS FOURTH LINE SAYS IN ITS OWN WORDS THAT IT IS NOT A CLAIM ON ANY BOAT AND NOT A CLAIM ON ANY PERSON. A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX OF THE THIRD LAY, ONE OF THE SIX WHO KEEP THE THIRD MARK, SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT EIGHTY-TWO WORDS THAT IT IS NOT FOR THEM TO DECIDE ALONE, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS DID NOT PUSH HIM AFTER THAT. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day, an offer that has not been refused and has not been accepted not being a thing that yard prices.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about seven in the morning the reason the paper was not nailed to the board at the head of that mole is his own, and is that a board at the head of a mole is a place a thing gets to be the account of that harbor, and that this paper is not anybody's account and has nobody's seal on it that anybody here can see, and that if it went on that board then the six men of the third mark would be answering a thing that had the two sealed sheets standing over it, and that a man who has one paper standing over him is a man making a decision, and that is precisely what a man of twenty-six stood up in a market at about half past eleven and said he was not going to do on behalf of five men; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the third line was read slow is his own, and is that the third line is two sentences that are not saying the same thing as each other, one being that no person is asked for a name and the other being that the mark and any thing floating in it is not the subject of the paper, and that a paper like this does not want a person, it wants the hand of one man, and that the difference between the two sentences is the difference between this harbor being asked and this harbor being given a job, and that only one of those two sentences is load bearing and it is the second one; and that at about half past eleven the reason he read four lines he did not write without a word wrong is his own, and is that on the eighth of this month he read the fifth line of a six-line account as the word rents and a woman of about forty-four corrected him out loud in front of about forty people, and that the harbour of that mistake sat in about eleven heads for about a minute, and that a man who has been the harbor's one wrong mouth once is not going to be its one wrong mouth twice, and that the whole difference between the eighth and the twelfth is that on the eighth he did not know the document and on the twelfth he had read it four times in an hour, and that knowing a document is the only protection a reader has and that it is not protection; and that at about half past five the reason he said that the second time in nine days a man or a woman has stood up and said what a paper cannot do is his own, and is that the first of those two was a woman of thirty on the tenth in a room four hundred yards away and this one was a man of twenty-six on a slip, and that the two of them were four hundred yards apart and did not know about each other, and that he is not going to be the man who goes and tells one of them what the other one said, because the first thing that happens to that is that a man of twenty-six in a market becomes a man who has been given an argument by somebody with a leaf, and that is the beginning of a book; and that at about ten past six the reason no line went onto that sheet this morning is his own, and is that a yard prices what it did and not what it was asked to do, and that a man who has not been asked for anything has not done anything, and that the second column of that leaf is not a substitute for the first and is not going to be used as one, and that the offer will be priced on the day somebody says no to it and not one day before, and that a day when nothing is priced is a day and not a gap.*
