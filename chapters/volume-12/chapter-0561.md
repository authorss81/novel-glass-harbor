# Chapter 561

*Tuesday the First Day of February, the Sixty-Seventh Wage Tuesday, the Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Read Out on the Step at the Back of That Market at About Ten o'Clock in the Morning in Front of About Forty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read the Lid on It and Not Moving, Nobody Paid a Penny Out of That Chest, a Further Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Being the Sixty-Seventh, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Coming Back Down That Quay at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With What Eleven People Who Cannot Read Said About Six True Lines, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Tuesday the first day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on the boards of the floor of that market where the wind comes under the doors.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe and was the sixty-seventh wage Tuesday of that harbor. That cistern was not charged on it. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and nobody was paid a penny out of that chest, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the sixty-seventh. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety-nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and thirty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and one days. The sheet with six true lines and a seal on it is on the board at the head of that mole and has been there eight days and it has not been taken down, and beside it on that board are two papers that have not been opened, one of them saying that the sea is under martial custody and one of them carrying a seal and no figure and dating from the third of December. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this morning and the mole head is dark. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty-nine days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The step at the back of that market was read out from ten o'clock in the morning and it is twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and there are twenty envelopes in a chest in the front room behind that step, and nothing on that step moved. The reading took four minutes and about forty people were standing on the boards in front of it and about eleven of them cannot read the lid. The chest stood shut from ten until four, nobody in that market asked for it to be opened, and the sixty-seventh week stands fallen due at the close of that day.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office came back down that quay from the top of Fish Street, on her own, and stood at the end of that slip, and she had been to the eleven people of that harbor who cannot read, one at a time, and she had asked each of them what the six lines say and what they make of it.

Nobody asked her to. Nobody knew she had gone. She had a folded paper in the inside of her coat and she took it out and put it flat on the bench stone and she said the whole of it out loud in front of about nine people, and it was two hundred and seventy-five words, and nobody thanked her for it and nobody asked her where she had got it from, because she said where she had got it from before anybody could ask.

"They do not want to say it in a slip. That is the first of it, and about half of them said it to me before I had finished asking. The half that did not say it said it afterwards.

The first line, that nine went in and seven came back. One of them said out loud that seven is the number he has believed since he was a boy, and that he would like to know what the other two were doing. The third line, the gear in the bottom and none on the deck and nobody standing on either boat for about two hours. One of them said he has been in a boat that came in with her gear in the bottom and her man not on her, and that he was twelve at the time, and that that is not a thing a man reads off a paper and is told what it means.

The fifth line is the one that stopped most of them, and it is the line that says the account is made from returns and stops at what the returns contain. Six of them wanted to know what a return is. **Not one of them had ever seen one, and not one of them had ever been told that anything they took part in went into one.**

The sixth line is the offer, and that one belonged to all of them, and about four of them said the same thing about it in four different ways, and what they said was that it is the only one of the six that has anybody in it at all."

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood at the end of that slip and said out loud that the sixth line had been on the board at the head of that mole since the twenty-fourth of January and that in those eight days not one person of that harbor had put a particular on a public wall anywhere, and that she had gone up a hill and collected eleven answers instead, and that a man cannot hire anybody out of eleven answers.

He said it once. The woman of thirty said back that he was right about that, and that she had not gone up there to hire anybody, and that eleven answers were not on a wall and were not in a minute and would be in nobody's memory by Sunday, and that a man who wants a thing on a piece of paper can either wait or can go up the hill and write on the wall himself.

He said that he cannot read a wall either. Nobody said anything for about a minute. Then a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that there were two men standing in that market who could not read a wall, and that neither of the two of them had ever asked anybody to write one.

---

And at about half past three she said out loud that she has not answered her own question of the twenty-fourth of January and is not going to answer it on a slip in front of nine people, and that what she did this afternoon is not an answer to it and is not going to be used as one, and that whoever answers it will have to be somebody who was in that room on the twenty-fourth and who can say what the answer costs.

Nobody asked her who she thought that was. She was not thanked. The paper went back inside her coat.

---

And at about half past five that afternoon, with the door of that shed standing open on the slip, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and put into about a hundred and twenty-four words what eleven answers and a column of twenty-six lines are worth when they happen on the same morning.

"Ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence. Unmoved, and it is the sixty-seventh Tuesday and it is the same figure it was on the fifty-fourth Tuesday before it. **A woman went up that hill to eleven people today and came down with what they said, and not one of those eleven answers is on a page and a man read twenty-six lines out loud on the step at ten this morning to about forty people and eleven of them could not see what a line is. Those two things happened on one morning in one harbor and neither of them is a figure, and that is the whole of what today was.**"

Not one item standing in that harbor changed hands on that Tuesday, the request for the remainder was not put to anybody, and not one voice on that slip reached for the words people use when they have been handed something. The eleven answers went back up the hill in a fold of paper inside a coat.

---

**THE COLUMN WAS READ OUT ON THAT STEP AT ABOUT TEN AND DID NOT MOVE AND NOBODY WAS PAID A PENNY OUT OF THAT CHEST AND THE SIXTY-SEVENTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A WOMAN OF THIRTY WENT TO THE ELEVEN PEOPLE OF THAT HARBOR WHO CANNOT READ, ONE AT A TIME, AND CAME BACK WITH WHAT THEY SAID ABOUT SIX LINES THAT ARE ALL TRUE, AND NOT ONE OF THOSE ELEVEN ANSWERS IS ON A PAGE AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS IN A MINUTE. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE SAID OUT LOUD THAT TWO MEN IN THAT MARKET CANNOT READ A WALL AND THAT NEITHER OF THEM HAS EVER ASKED FOR ONE. A WOMAN OF THIRTY DECLINED OUT LOUD TO ANSWER HER OWN QUESTION OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH ON A SLIP AND SAID THAT WHOEVER ANSWERS IT WILL HAVE TO HAVE BEEN IN THAT ROOM. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the first day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe and was the sixty-seventh wage Tuesday, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and that nobody was paid a penny out of that chest and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of the day, being the sixty-seventh; and that a woman of thirty of no office went to the eleven people of that harbor who cannot read, one at a time, on this day, and asked each of them what the six lines on that sealed sheet say and what they make of it, and that she came back down that quay at about two o'clock in the afternoon and said the whole of it out loud at the end of that slip in about two hundred and seventy-five words in front of about nine people, being that about half of them said before she had finished asking that they did not want to say it in a slip, being that one of them said that seven is the number he has believed since he was a boy and would like to know what the other two were doing, being that one of them said he has been in a boat that came in with her gear in the bottom and her man not on her and that he was twelve at the time, being that six of them wanted to know what a return is and that not one of them had ever seen one, and being that about four of them said that the sixth of those lines is the only one that has anybody in it; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that a man cannot hire anybody out of eleven answers and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that there are two men in that market who cannot read a wall and that neither of them has ever asked for one; and that she said out loud at about half past three that she has not answered her own question of the twenty-fourth of January and will not answer it on a slip and that whoever answers it will have to have been in that room on the twenty-fourth and be able to say what the answer costs; and that the eleven days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at two hundred and ninety-four and one thousand three hundred and forty-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those eleven days and that the derivation is on the page and is not on the page; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a day of the berth fund was paid on a Tuesday is his own and is that the two men in that shed take a boat out at about ten and have it back at about four whatever a Tuesday is, and that the figure in chalk on a slate at that end stall does not know what day of the week it is and has not known since the first of December, and that the man who hires hands wanted four more hands before the spring tides and is not going to get them on this coast in February, and that is a fact about his own yard and not about any paper, and that he put it into the open today because a man who cannot hire anybody out of eleven answers is a man who has been told something true about his own business by a person of no office at two o'clock in the afternoon; and that the reason the eleventh answer about the returns is the one that matters is his own, and is that the fifth of those six lines names a thing the six lines are made out of, and that the people who cannot read are the only people in that harbor who have never been asked where anything they did went, because everything else they are handed has been written for somebody with a hand for it, and that six of eleven wanting to know what a return is is not a complaint against the paper, it is the paper being honest about itself in the only direction it can be; and that the reason he did not put any of the eleven answers on any sheet is his own, and is that he read them at about half past four and by then he had forgotten the exact wording of nine of them, and that a thing a man has forgotten the wording of by half past four is a thing that cannot go on a page without becoming a different thing, and that the first column takes the day and the day is the day; and that the reason he said out loud at half past five that a man read twenty-six lines to about forty people and eleven of them could not see what a line is is his own, and is that both halves of that sentence happened in one morning in one market and that neither of the two halves had anything to do with the six lines, and that a harbor that cannot read its own wage column and cannot read its own account is going to be told what both of them are by somebody who can, and that he would rather that were a Tuesday when nothing moved than a Wednesday when something did.*
