# Chapter 586

Saturday the twenty-sixth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at four in the afternoon.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing, and the covers were on it. One line went onto that sheet at about four in the afternoon in that shed at one thousand and thirteen pence, being five marks, seventeen shillings and ninepence, with the twenty-fourth of this month against it, being the day the attempt stopped, and the twenty-sixth against it, being the day that yard settled it, and both days are on the face of the one line and were said out loud in that yard's own mouth before a figure was written against either of them. Nothing else went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes the whole of what the attempt cost.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninetieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixty-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-six days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the demand for the returns is in a basket that went down that coast at about four yesterday afternoon and that basket is a day and a night of a boat away and comes back on the Monday. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-four days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

At about four yesterday afternoon a basket went down that coast in a boat, and the paper in it is the second asking, and the boat takes a day and a night and a cart takes four days each way, and about nine people were on that quay at the time and not one of them said that the first asking had come back empty.

---

And at about four in the afternoon on that Saturday one line went onto that sheet for the first time since the fifth of this month, and about nine people were in that shed, and the person whose yard it is said the whole of it out loud before she wrote a figure against it, in about one hundred and fifty words, and she priced hours and material and nobody in that room had asked her to.

She named the day first, before any figure, and she said it the way a yard says a day.

"**The day against this line is the twenty-fourth of this month, and the twenty-fourth is not the day this yard did the work. The twenty-fourth is the day it stopped.** The air was used on the twenty-fourth and the coal was burnt on the twenty-fourth and the victual was eaten at one in the morning on the twenty-fifth, and the twenty-fourth is the day a wedge went into a hanger at about four fathoms and the work came to nothing, and that is a day of that kind and I am naming it as that before there is a figure on this page at all. This yard settles on a Saturday and this line is settled on the twenty-sixth, and both of those are on the face of the one line and either one of them can be taken off it by a stranger with a leaf and a pencil."

Then she said the second thing, which is the thing that figure is not, and it was in about one hundred and eight words.

"**And this is not fifteen thousand three hundred and twenty pence, which is the figure an older bill on this sheet was wrongly given at, and it is not five thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence, which is what that one was reconstructed at, and it is not anything added to either of those two, because I have not added anything to either of those two and I do not know how to.** This yard is not going to be asked for the difference between this figure and either of them and this sheet is not going to be made to balance by asking that harbor for anything."

Then she said the third thing out loud, before the figure was written, in about eleven words, and it is on no sheet and it is not going on one.

Then she wrote six figures and a sum, and the multiplication is printed at every one of the six.

1. *Air, one bottle for one man for one descent, at two hundred and eighty pence a man, that being the figure that bottle came in at on the thirtieth of December and has not come down since. One times two hundred and eighty is two hundred and eighty pence, being one mark and ten shillings.*
2. *Coal for that pump, four hundredweight at forty-five pence a hundredweight, that being a figure which has gone up twice since the autumn. Four times forty-five is one hundred and eighty pence, being one mark, one shilling and eightpence.*
3. *Victual, six men at eight pence a head, being supper for six men in that shed from about ten at night until about four in the morning on the twenty-fourth. Six times eight is forty-eight pence, being four shillings.*
4. *And out of the store at the back of that yard, a shackle and a link of chain and six fathoms of new line, at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence. One times one hundred and sixty-eight is one hundred and sixty-eight.*
5. *And two days asked for by name at the figure in chalk, being the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth of this month. Two times one hundred and sixty-eight is three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence.*
6. *And one sheet bought blank at a penny a sheet at a counter in a room off that market, out of the holder's own coat. One times one is one penny.*

Two hundred and eighty and one hundred and eighty and forty-eight and one hundred and sixty-eight and three hundred and thirty-six and one is one thousand and thirteen pence, being five marks, seventeen shillings and ninepence.

Then she wrote the three sums under the six figures, on the face of the line, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all of them off in about a minute and find that they agree.

One hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three plus one thousand and thirteen is one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six. One hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six less ten thousand six hundred and forty is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six. And ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three plus one thousand and thirteen is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked, once, whether the hours of Thursday night were on that line, and the person whose yard it is said, in about one hundred and ninety-seven words, that they are not and are not going to be.

"**The hours of Thursday night are not on that line and they are not going onto that line and no figure in this country prices them, and I priced them on Tuesday on a bench stone so that the page said it out loud before this afternoon happened, and that page is not a line on that sheet and is not in any minute.** The five hundred and eight pence from Tuesday is in the six figures on that page, all three parts of it, in the air and in the coal and in the victual, and the three hundred and thirty-six of the two days asked for by name is in there, and the hundred and sixty-eight of the gear is in there. What is not in there is sixteen hours and a quarter of a man. Nobody is going to make me put a man's night at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day the same as a day asked for by name, because those are two different things and if this sheet says they are the same thing then this sheet is not a record of what a yard costs, it is an argument."

Nobody argued with her, and nobody asked her a second time, and the line was settled on the twenty-sixth with both days on the face of it, and the person whose yard it is said a third thing out loud before the figure was written in about eleven words and that it is on no sheet.

Then at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, and the sheet had moved for the first time in three weeks, and he used about three hundred and forty-two words on a figure of one thousand and thirteen pence.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. It moved this afternoon for the first time since the fifth of this month and it moved by one thousand and thirteen pence, being five marks, seventeen shillings and ninepence, and the sum of the six figures on that line is that figure and not one of the six of them is a person.

**One pin came out of a clapper arm on Thursday and the whole of what it cost is a bill, and the bill is a third payment on that sheet and it is neither of the two figures that were already on it and it is not their sum and it is not anything added to either, and the woman who wrote it said all of that out loud in that shed before she wrote a single stroke of it.** Five marks, seventeen shillings and ninepence for a bottle of air and four hundredweight of coal and six men at eight pence and a shackle and a line and two days asked for by name and one sheet bought blank at a penny. I have been putting figures on that leaf since December and I have never had a day like this one, and a day like this one is not a good day and it is not a bad day, it is a day that cost what it cost.

Sixteen hours and a quarter of a man is not on that sheet and is not going on it, and that is the same as the seven hours and twelve minutes of the fifteenth and it is the same as every other hour in this harbor that cannot be checked by anybody. The pin and the wedge are on that bench stone. The third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and there is no date against it anywhere in this harbor and I am not going to put one on it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for the bill, and the six figures stayed on the face of that line in the yard's own hand where a stranger can take every one of them off it.
