# Chapter 594

*Sunday the Sixth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, a Wipe, the Ninety-Sixth Time, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Again at About Ten to Twelve, Unrepaired and Not Whole and About Thirteen Minutes Both Ways and Nobody Having Said Why It Is Getting Slower, Four Heads Standing on a Wall in the Room Over the Floor of That Market and No Fifth Going on It on Account of a Crown Account, a Night, a Failure or a Victory, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Who Cannot Read Counting Four Things on That Wall in About ninety-nine Words and Not Having Been Asked to Count Them, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Asking in About two hundred and sixty Words Whether a Fifth Head Is Going on That Wall and Getting the Answer No in About ninety-nine Words, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About five hundred and sixty-two Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the sixth day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at half past five in the afternoon.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was the ninety-sixth time that wall has come down, and it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the covers were on it at nine in the morning and were on it at four in the afternoon. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the four heads on that wall in the room over the floor of that market are on no sheet and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in any room over any fish market, and neither of those two was in a room with the other one at any hour of that day. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went into that yard at about ten in the morning and the same figure went out of it at about four in the afternoon, that figure being one mark and eightpence, and the bag was holding twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, two shillings and fourpence of it, and is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of a wage of four marks. The sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is kept in that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was answered by nobody. Nothing has gone into a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of what that day came to was taken out of that bag, or out of the owner of any boat, or out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes the whole of what a day asked for by name costs, being a day somebody sold and being a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard at four in the afternoon, and that is not a line on that sheet.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and ninety-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and thirty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninety-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and sixteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventy-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty-four days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is still standing in that boathouse with the paper in it. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been ninety-two days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That market trades on a Sunday and traded all morning and the room over the floor of it had about nine people in it by a quarter to eleven, and the four heads are on the end wall of that room at the height of a man's shoulder, and there have been four of them there since the autumn.

Under each of those four heads there is a line in a small hand saying what that head is, and the four of them are a claim, a refusal, a restricted record, and a public correction. There is no fifth head on that wall and there is no space cut for one and there is no nail in that wall above the fourth of them.

---

And at about half past ten on that Sunday the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare for the ninety-sixth time, and about four people in that harbor were standing near enough to see it happen, and it went up again at about ten to twelve at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole.

It took about thirteen minutes both ways. That is longer than the eleven minutes of the twenty-seventh of this month and longer than the nine of the twentieth of last month and longer than the four of the thirteenth, and neither of the two men who do it has said a word about that and neither has said a word about it since the thirteenth of last month.

---

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay came into the room over the floor of that market at about eleven and stood in front of that wall with his back to the stairs and did not go up to it, and he cannot read, and nobody asked him anything, and he said one thing out loud in about 99 words to a man of about fifty-six who was standing beside him and had not said anything to him.

"**I cannot read what is up there and I have known that since I was nineteen, and I have counted the four of them every Sunday for eleven weeks because counting is the thing I can do with a wall.** There are four and there have been four every time I have looked, and I am not saying that as an argument for anything, I am saying it because a man of about thirty-three has been talking about a fifth one for a fortnight and I would like somebody in this room to have counted before that conversation starts."

The man of about fifty-six told the rest of that room what he had said, in about nine words, and about four people heard it and about nine were not told.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands came up those stairs at about half past eleven and asked, in about 260 words, whether a fifth head was going onto that wall, and a woman of about thirty-one of the first lay answered him in about 99 words with her own particular still in her own pocket.

"**No, and I am not the one who decides it, and neither are you, and the only person who could put a fifth head on that wall is a person who has been asked to by somebody in this room and there has not been one.** There is a sheet of paper on a board at the head of this mole with a seal on it and six lines on it and every one of the six is true, and there is another one sealed beside it, and there is a wall of your own across the lane with eight lines of chalk on it and nothing under it. **A head on that wall is a claim that a person may hold this harbor to, and I am not going to hold this harbor to a thing it did not do, and I would be a fool to think that a paper I cannot argue with is a claim I have been given.** A night, a failure, a bill, a Tuesday morning, four things went up at a fish stall on Saturday. Not one of them is a fifth head and not one of them is going to be, and if somebody comes up those stairs in six months with a fifth head and a reason, then that is a different argument and I will listen to it and I will probably say no, and that is a thing that happens on a wall and not a thing that happens on a piece of paper nailed up in the dark."

She said it once, she did not raise her voice, and nobody argued with her and nobody agreed with her out loud, and about four people who had come up those stairs to hear something went back down them.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 562 words on a wall that came down bare that morning and on four things on another wall that did not, and nobody in that slip was asked why a wall is getting slower.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet this morning and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and no charge went on that cistern and the covers were on it all day, and a day asked for by name is a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard at four o'clock and is not a line on that sheet and is not a line on a Tuesday either.

**A wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten this morning for the ninety-sixth time and went up again at about ten to twelve at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January, and it did not go up whole, and it took about thirteen minutes both ways, which is two minutes longer than the last time and nine minutes longer than the time before that.** Nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower and neither of the two men who do it has said why, and I have not asked either of them, and I am not going to ask, and I have put the figure in here because in nine weeks four people are going to say it always takes four minutes.

The four heads on the wall in the room over the floor of that market are the four heads. I have written that down in a slip at half past five on a Sunday because a man of about thirty-three has been asking about a fifth one for a fortnight and because somebody in that room counted the four of them at eleven o'clock this morning and said so out loud, and he cannot read what is up there and he has never been asked to do anything in that room and was not asked to do anything today. There is no fifth head on that wall and none is going on it for a paper with a seal on it, and there is no fifth head going on it for a night, and there is none going on for a failure, and there is none going on for a bill.

**The sheet that carries the money is in that yard and is on no wall in any room over any fish market, and the four heads are on a wall in a room over that market and are on no sheet in that yard, and those are two different things that have been kept apart for eleven weeks and I have put both of them in the same slip on purpose this evening so that nobody can say they were confused.**

The third column is empty and stayed empty today and the fourth took nothing and stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and there is no reason for that on a page of mine. I entered one line on the first column and five on the second at about six this evening, and neither of the first two figures of that leaf has been read out loud in any room this month, and the forty-fourth day of that leaf went in at about six this evening and forty-four entries and two hundred and twenty entries are on it now."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four counted heads, and that shed stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the wall behind that reeve's bench stood at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and not whole.

**THE WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY, BEING THE NINETY-SIXTH TIME, AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO TWELVE AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK ABOUT THIRTEEN MINUTES BOTH WAYS AGAINST ABOUT ELEVEN ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF THIS MONTH AND ABOUT NINE ON THE TWENTIETH OF LAST MONTH AND ABOUT FOUR ON THE THIRTEENTH, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. THE FOUR HEADS ON THE WALL IN THE ROOM OVER THE FLOOR OF THAT MARKET ARE THE FOUR HEADS, BEING A CLAIM, A REFUSAL, A RESTRICTED RECORD AND A PUBLIC CORRECTION, AND NO FIFTH HEAD WENT ON THAT WALL ON ACCOUNT OF A CROWN ACCOUNT, A NIGHT, A FAILURE OR A VICTORY. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THE SECOND LAY WHO CANNOT READ COUNTED FOUR THINGS ON THAT WALL IN ABOUT 99 WORDS AND WAS NOT ASKED TO COUNT THEM, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE ASKED IN ABOUT 260 WORDS WHETHER A FIFTH HEAD WAS GOING ON AND WAS ANSWERED IN ABOUT 99 WORDS THAT THERE IS NOT GOING TO BE ONE AND THAT SHE IS NOT THE ONE WHO DECIDES IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ON NO WALL IN ANY ROOM OVER ANY FISH MARKET AND THE FOUR HEADS ARE ON NO SHEET IN THAT YARD, AND THE MONEY ON THAT SHEET IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE HOLDS.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I have written thirteen minutes down twice on a Sunday is that it is the fourth figure I have for that wall and that three of the first three were taken by a man standing at it with a watch, and that a man standing at it with a watch on a Sunday is a man who has decided that the wall matters more than the four things he has come out for, and that I did it with the fourth one and I am not going to do it again, and that the fifth time anybody counts that wall I would like it counted by a person who does not care, and that the only person in that harbor who is likely to care is me; and that at about eleven o'clock the reason a man of about thirty-four who cannot read counted four things on a wall in about 99 words and nobody asked him to is his own and I have not asked him about it, and that I found out about it from a man of about fifty-six who told four people in a room at about eleven and I did not go and ask the man himself, and that a man of about thirty-four who asked to be asked in the open on the fifth of this month and was not asked is not going to be asked by me in a slip either, and that if he ever stands in front of that wall and says a number out loud again then it is because he has decided to and not because nine people agreed he ought to; and that at about half past eleven the reason the fourth of those four heads is a public correction and has been since the autumn, and is the one that nobody in this harbor has ever used, is that I asked a man of about thirty-three in the second week of December and he said that a correction anybody can see is the only one of the four that costs nothing to keep and that he had not written it down because he could not think of a correction, and that a man who cannot think of a correction is a man who has not been in a room where one was needed, and that that is the fourth column of a wall and not a column of a leaf and I am not going to put the two together tonight; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the four heads and the sheet that carries the money were both named out loud in the same slip this evening is mine and is that a slip which mentions a wall and a figure in the same breath is going to be read by somebody as a wall with a figure on it, and that this harbor has one of those already on a board at the head of a mole and it is not ours, and that naming the two of them apart in one breath is the only way I know of stopping a slip being copied into a sentence about both of them; and that at about five o'clock the reason I have not written down the one thing a woman of about thirty-one said in that room, being the part about a paper she cannot argue with, is that a page behind a press already carries a sentence very like it in a hand that is not mine, and that two people in this harbor arriving at the same shape of sentence without hearing each other is a fact and not an agreement, and that I have entered the arrangement in this column and not in that leaf because a leaf is a count of days and this is two people.*
