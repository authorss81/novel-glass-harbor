# Chapter 552

*Sunday the Twenty-Third Day of January, a Wipe, the Ninetieth Time, the Wall behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Unrepaired at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Not Whole, Four People Walking Past It Between Then and the Middle of the Day and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket under It, the Two Men on That Cistern Raising the Covers and Looking Down and Putting the Covers Back and Not Putting a Pail in It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, a Woman of About Forty-Four Who Keeps a Shut Store on the Second Lay Giving an Account of Two Notes off the Outer Water in About a Hundred and Twenty-Five Words in Front of About Nine People and Being Stopped by a Second Question Asked of Her by the Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands, and That Question Not Being Asked a Second Time, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Six Pence, Being Six Hundred Marks and Sixpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the twenty-third day of January, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on the boards of the floor of that market where the wind comes under the doors.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare for the ninetieth time at about half past ten in the morning and went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and it did not go up whole, and four people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out, being six hundred and sixty-six marks, seven shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and fifty days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and fifty-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ninety days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fifty-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seventy-four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and twenty-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and forty-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixty-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ninety-two days. The figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall is one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, and it has not moved since the first of December and the figure in chalk does not know what day of the week it is. There are twenty-six lines in that harbor and one hundred and four marks against them and twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room, and the column was last read out on the eighteenth of this month and it did not move then and it is going to be read out again on the twenty-fifth. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been fifty days and nobody in that harbor has set a figure against it and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The wall came down bare and it went up again unrepaired and not whole, and the four people who walked past it between half past ten and the middle of the day did not put a bucket under it, and the two men who were on that cistern raised the covers, looked down into it, put the covers back on, and did not put a pail in it, because this is a Sunday and the cistern is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and not on any other day of the week.

---

And the woman of about forty-four who keeps a store on the second lay with its shutter up since the middle of December stood at the end of that slip at about one o'clock in the afternoon and gave an account of the two notes of last night, in about a hundred and twenty-five words, in front of about nine people, and the whole of it was hers and none of it was a figure of money and she was not thanked for any part of it.

"I had the shutter up and both the windows shut in that store and I was doing a sum on a slate at the back with the lamp on the counter. **It came out of the water and it did not come off the land, and I know that because the roof over that yard is on and the doors of the joiner's shop are shut at that hour and I could not hear a hammer on that quay if a man were swinging one.** The first was flat. The second was flat. There is no ring on it at all, and I have stood in that store for nineteen years and I know what a bell does and neither of those two was it."

A man of about thirty-five of the first lay said that his own boy had heard it and had not been able to say what it was, and about nine people on that slip agreed with her and nobody disagreed with her and nobody asked her for a second thing.

---

And then the man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked her a second question, and the asking of it is what stopped her, and she did not answer it and nobody in that harbor has asked her it since.

"**And what do you make of it.**"

She stood there with her hands on the counter of her own store and did not answer, and the man of about thirty-three who hires hands looked at the boards and the man of about forty-six who mends nets said out loud and once that that was the only question anybody in that harbor could have asked her and that it was the wrong one to ask a woman who cannot read a page with four lines on it in a slip full of people on a Sunday.

Nobody said a word about it after that. The man who keeps the leaf said out loud that the question was in the air and that it was not going on the sheet and was not going in the minute either, and that a question in the air on a Sunday is not the same thing as a question in a yard at night with an hour against it, and that a thing cannot be asked twice and be still the same thing the second time.

He could have written it down. There was a slate on that bench stone and he had carried it out with him at ten in the morning and it had lain on the flat of it all afternoon with nothing on it, and putting four words on it would have cost him nothing and would have turned the asking of it from a thing nine people remember in three months into a thing that had happened. He did not do it, and he could not have said out loud why he did not, and the not-doing of it sat on him the rest of that afternoon the way a dropped line sits on a man until somebody else picks it up.

She went back to her store at about twenty past one and shut the door of it and did not take the shutter down and nobody has asked her a question since.

The shop next to hers took in a delivery of barrels on the Saturday and the man who brought them had stacked six of them in the passage and they were still stacked there at half past five, and nobody could get through that passage without going between them, and about nine people went through it anyway, twice, because there was nowhere else to stand with a lamp.

---

And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and said nothing else at all about the sound except the part that belongs to that sheet.

"Ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence. A wipe, being the ninetieth of them, and the wall came down and went up unrepaired and two men looked down into that cistern and put the covers back. **A woman stood in that slip this afternoon and said where a sound came from and what shape it was and who can and who cannot hear a hammer, and none of those three things is a figure and there is no line on any sheet in this country that a man can put them on without that sheet becoming a thing that cannot be checked.**"

Nobody was asked for the rest of it, no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise, and nobody was thanked for anything on that Sunday, and nobody was thanked for the hundred and twenty-five words and nobody was thanked for the silence that came after the second question and the door of that shed stood open on the slip from ten in the morning until about half past five.

---

**THE WALL CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE NINETIETH TIME AND WENT UP AGAIN UNREPAIRED AND NOT WHOLE AND FOUR PEOPLE WALKED PAST IT AND NONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. TWO MEN RAISED THE COVERS OF THAT CISTERN AND LOOKED DOWN AND PUT THE COVERS BACK AND DID NOT PUT A PAIL IN IT. A DAY OF THE BERTH FUND WAS PAID AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND A DAY WAS PAID AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE FIGURE IN CHALK DID NOT MOVE. A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR GAVE AN ACCOUNT OF TWO NOTES OFF THE OUTER WATER IN ABOUT NINETY WORDS IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND IT WAS ALL TRUE, AND A SECOND QUESTION WAS ASKED OF HER IN A SLIP AND SHE DID NOT ANSWER IT AND IT WAS NOT ASKED AGAIN, AND THAT SECOND QUESTION IS NOT ON THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY AND IS NOT IN THE MINUTE. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-third day of January at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, being the ninetieth wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the two men on that cistern raised the covers and looked down into it and put the covers back on and did not put a pail in it; and that the wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare for the ninetieth time at about half past ten in the morning and went up again unrepaired at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and did not go up whole, and that four people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a woman of about forty-four who keeps a store on the second lay with its shutter up since the middle of December gave an account of the two notes heard on the night of the twenty-second in about a hundred and twenty-five words at about one o'clock in the afternoon in front of about nine people, being that the two notes came out of the water and did not come off the land, being that she knew it because the roof over that yard is on and the doors of the joiner's shop are shut at that hour and she could not hear a hammer on that quay if a man were swinging one, and being that the first of the two was flat and the second of the two was flat and that there is no ring on either of them, and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said that his own boy had heard it and could not say what it was, and that about nine people agreed with her and none disagreed and nobody asked her for a second thing; and that a second question was then asked of her out loud by the man of about thirty-three who hires hands, being that he asked her what she made of it, and that she did not answer it, and that the man of about forty-six who mends nets said out loud and once that that was the only question anybody in that harbor could have asked her and that it was the wrong one to ask in a slip full of people, and that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that the question was in the air and was not going onto that sheet and not going into that minute and that a thing cannot be asked twice and be the same thing the second time; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and six thousand six hundred and forty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and six pence short, being six hundred marks and sixpence; and that no penny of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a day of the berth fund was paid on the ninetieth wipe is his own and is not the reason the last wipe carried, and is that the wall went down bare at half past ten this morning and the cistern covers went back on at about eleven and the second question was asked in the open at about ten past one, and that all three of those things happened on somebody's time and not on this yard's account, and that a fund that pays on five days has a choice on the sixth of not paying and that the choice is always made by somebody who is not the man who lifted the cover; and that the reason the second question was not written down anywhere is his own, and is that a question that has an hour against it and a name against it is answered inside a day by the man whose name it is, and that a question asked in the open on a Sunday with about nine people on the slip has no hour and no name and is in nobody's book, and that a thing in nobody's book is worth exactly what a person remembers of it in three months and that is the whole value of it; and that the reason the hundred and twenty-five words of that woman are not on that sheet is his own, and is that a sheet with money on it can be checked in a minute by a stranger with a leaf and a pencil, and that a woman saying where a sound came from and what shape it was is a thing a stranger cannot check at all because a stranger was not on that slip at one o'clock with a lamp on a counter, and that a man who puts both on one piece of paper has thrown away the one that could be checked in order to carry the one that cannot; and that the reason he asked it in the open rather than not at all is his own, and is that a question said out loud in front of nine people on a Sunday has been heard by nine people and that a question not asked has been heard by nobody and that a man who wants a thing to be on the record has to put it where a record can happen.*
