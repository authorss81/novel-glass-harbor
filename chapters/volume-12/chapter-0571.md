# Chapter 571

*Friday the Eleventh Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands, a Slate Four Days Down That Coast, and a Woman of About Thirty-Six Who Keeps a Light at the Top of a Point in a Harbor About Nine Miles Up That Coast, Each of Them Having Counted the Interval Between One Answer and the Next Answer on One Night in January, the Three Figures Being Ninety-Four Seconds and All Three of Them the Same, Entered at About Ten in the Morning in a Crossing Column and Not Said in a Room, Two Times Nine Being Eighteen Lines a Stranger with a Leaf and a Pencil Can Take off a Page of Days, a Berth-Fund Day of One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In at About Ten and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Out at About Four, the Sheet That Carries the Money Unmoved and Nothing Going Into It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the eleventh day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and that door that stands open on a slip whether anybody is on that slip or not.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the ten days of this month that had gone before it left the sheet exactly where those ten days found it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and nine days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eightieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and eleven days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the demand for the returns went into a boat's basket on the fourth of this month and that boat was due back about the sixth. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-nine days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The three figures came in on three different days and were not put together until the eleventh.

The first was in the mouth of the man of about thirty-three who hires hands, who had stood at the head of that mole on the night of the twenty-second with a watch on his wrist and had written the interval on the back of his own hand, and who gave the figure to the man who keeps the leaf on the morning of the ninth without being asked for it and without anybody being in the room at the time.

The second was on a slate, four days down that coast, in the hand of a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay, and it was under the forty-seven seconds he had put there on the twenty-second, and it had been carried up that coast in a basket and was on the bench stone in that shed at about two o'clock on the tenth.

The third came in a letter with no seal on it and no name on the outside of it, put in at a counter in a harbor about nine miles up that coast by a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a light at the top of a point there, and it had been in a boat and a boat takes a day and a night, and it was on that bench stone at about four o'clock on the same afternoon.

All three of them were the same figure. Ninety-four seconds from one answer to the next answer, and that is not the same quantity as the forty-seven seconds under it on that other slate, and this yard did not add the two of them together and is not going to.

---

And at about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf made a crossing column out of the back of an old slate, and he made it in that shed with the door standing open and nobody on that slip at that hour, and he did not read it out to anybody afterward and has not read it out to anybody since.

The column has three lines in it, one for each of the three places, and the figure is on all three of them, and against the three of them he wrote the name of the thing that counted it, being a watch on a man's wrist at the head of a mole, a man's own count against a boat's wheel in four days down that coast, and the clock on the wall of a light room about nine miles up that coast. Three times ninety-four seconds is two hundred and eighty-two seconds, and that is four minutes and forty-two seconds, and the three of them agree to the second, and he wrote the two hundred and eighty-two down because a stranger with a leaf and a pencil is entitled to the sum as well as to the three.

Under those three lines he put a fourth line, and the fourth line is a count and not a time.

Against the night of Friday the twenty-first and against the night of Saturday the twenty-second, in the crossing column of that port as it stands on a page of days, the number of vessels of that port that were inside that line of boats when the answer came is nine on the twenty-first and nine on the twenty-second. Two times nine is eighteen lines of crossing on a page of days, and the eighteen can be looked at, and the nine is on both of the two nights and is the same nine that the two sheets on that board at the head of that mole put inside that line at ten o'clock at night and at six in the morning.

**It is off the crossings and not off an estimate, and it is in a column and not in a room, and it is the first figure this coast has had off that water in a year that is not a gap.** Nobody in that harbor has said that figure out loud in a room and it is not going to be said out loud in a room, because a figure said in a room is a thing somebody in that room is going to remember as a thing they were told, and a figure in a column is a thing anybody can go and look at and be wrong.

---

He had written those six lines out twice in that shed since the second of this month and had found at a quarter past twelve on the seventh that the second of the two was not the same document as the first, and he has not written them out again since. On the eleventh he wrote a column instead, and the difference between the two is that a column of crossings is a thing with a wrong figure in it somewhere and a sheet with a seal on it is not, and a man who has spent eleven days refusing to be the reason a number in a room cannot be checked has now put a number on a board that a man with a watch can come back in a month and take off and put against the watch.

He said one thing about it to nobody, because there was nobody there, and the thing he said was that a column can be found out and an account cannot, and that he does not yet know which of the two of those is worth more to a coast, and that he is not going to say the rest of it out loud in a slip where it would be agreed with.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and took two hundred words to say what three figures from three places cost, and nobody in that slip was asked to hold a watch for anybody.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and nothing went into that sheet and nothing came off it and there is no line on it for a man with a watch. **Three people in three places counted one thing on one night in January and the three of them got ninety-four seconds and the sum of the three is two hundred and eighty-two seconds and that is four minutes and forty-two seconds, and under it there is a count of nine vessels answering one signal off the crossings of that port on two nights, being eighteen lines, and it agrees with the two sheets on that mole and it is the first figure that coast has had off that water in a year that a stranger can put a pencil to.**

It is in a column and it is not in a room, and that is the whole of what I have for it. A thing in a room gets agreed with. A thing in a column gets checked, and getting checked is worse and it is the only one of the two that has ever caught anybody."

He was not thanked for the arithmetic and nobody asked him for the fourth line and the slate stayed on that bench stone with the back of it scored into three.

---

**THREE FIGURES OF THE INTERVAL BETWEEN ONE ANSWER AND THE NEXT ANSWER, COUNTED IN THREE PLACES BY THREE PEOPLE WITH THREE DIFFERENT THINGS, ARE NINETY-FOUR SECONDS AND ALL THREE OF THEM ARE THE SAME, AND THE SUM OF THEM IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-TWO SECONDS, BEING FOUR MINUTES AND FORTY-TWO SECONDS, AND IT IS IN A CROSSING COLUMN ON A SLATE IN THAT SHED AND WAS NOT SAID IN A ROOM. UNDER IT A COUNT OF NINE VESSELS OF THAT PORT INSIDE THAT LINE OF BOATS WHEN THE ANSWER CAME, ON TWO NIGHTS, BEING EIGHTEEN LINES, OFF THE CROSSINGS AND NOT OFF AN ESTIMATE, WHICH IS THE SAME NINE THE TWO SHEETS ON THAT BOARD PUT INSIDE THAT LINE AT TEN O'CLOCK AT NIGHT AND AT SIX IN THE MORNING. NOTHING WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY. THE FIGURE OF FORTY HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE DID NOT COME DOWN ANY BOARD. THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Friday the eleventh day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the three figures of the interval between one answer and the next answer came in on three different days, being the figure of a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, given to the man who keeps the leaf on the morning of the ninth with nobody in the room, being the figure on a slate four days down that coast in the hand of a man of about thirty-one who crews two out of the second lay, carried up that coast in a basket and on that bench stone at about two o'clock on the tenth, and being the figure in a letter put in at a counter in a harbor about nine miles up that coast by a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a light at the top of a point there, in a boat, a boat taking a day and a night, on that bench stone at about four o'clock on the same afternoon; and that all three of them are ninety-four seconds, and that three times ninety-four is two hundred and eighty-two seconds, and that two hundred and eighty-two seconds is four minutes and forty-two seconds, and that the two of the quantities are not the same quantity and were not added together; and that at about ten in the morning the man who keeps the leaf made a crossing column out of the back of an old slate in that shed with the door standing open and nobody on that slip at that hour, and did not read it out to anybody afterward and has not read it out to anybody since, and that the column has three lines in it one for each of the three places with the figure on all three and the thing that counted it named against each, and the fourth line under them being a count of the number of vessels of that port inside that line of boats when the answer came, being nine on Friday the twenty-first and nine on Saturday the twenty-second, and two times nine being eighteen lines of crossing on a page of days, and that the nine is the same nine the two sealed sheets on the board at the head of that mole put inside that line at ten o'clock at night and at six in the morning, and that it is off the crossings and not off an estimate; and that the man of about thirty-two has not written those six lines out again since the seventh of this month, having found at about a quarter past twelve on that day that the second of the two times he wrote them was not the same document as the first, and that on the eleventh he wrote a column instead, and that he said one thing to nobody in an empty shed, being that a column can be found out and an account cannot and that he does not yet know which of the two is worth more to a coast; and that the twenty-one days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and four and one thousand three hundred and ninety-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those twenty-one days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day, the four lines of the offer not being priced because no such lines exist yet, the first of them not having come ashore.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason he made that column with the door standing open and nobody on the slip is his own, and is that the three figures were in that shed on that bench stone and that about nine people come past that shed in a day and that a slate lying face up on a bench stone in that harbor is read by whoever walks past it, and that he did not want a man of about thirty-three to have handed a figure to him in a room, and that the reason a column is a better home for a figure than a wall is his own, and is that a wall copy in that harbor has a ring of black chalk at the foot of it and no name under it and has been the same six lines for nine days while eleven people have been told what is on it in eleven different ways; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the three figures were allowed to sit on that bench stone for a day and a half before he did anything with them is his own, and is that he wanted to see whether a fourth one came in, and that it did not, and that three is a figure and that two is a figure and that three is the number of places that the same thing can be counted in before a man in this house is allowed to write the word same; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason he wrote the sum down as well as the three is his own, and is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil who is handed three figures and a column and no sum has to do a sum in a shed to check the man who wrote them, and that a man who makes a stranger do arithmetic in order to trust him has already lost the thing arithmetic was for; and that at about half past five the reason he said that a thing in a room gets agreed with and a thing in a column gets checked is his own, and is that he has been in a room on the ninth of this month and heard a man of about thirty-three say that one book would have worked, and that the man was not wrong, and that this yard has been printing that figure on a chalk on a slate at an end stall for a year, and that the whole of what this afternoon prints is the other side of the same coin, and that he is not going to be the man who puts the two coins on a table in a slip and asks nine people to choose; and that at about ten past six the reason nothing went onto that sheet today is his own, and is that there is no line in that yard's book for a man counting a thing on a watch, and that the first thing a yard like this does when a figure arrives that it cannot check is to write a line for it, and that he has not got to the bottom of what the line would be for and he is not going to put one on in the meantime, and that a slate with a sum on it is the cheapest instrument in that harbor and it is the only one there that anybody can hold upside down and still be right.*
