# Chapter 583

*Wednesday the Twenty-Third Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, Two Slates Written in About Ten o'Clock in the Morning in That Shed With a Reason on Each in the Words of the Man Who Is Going to Carry It and an Hour at the Foot of Each, a Bell Rung at the Front of That Market at About a Quarter to Eleven and Four Lines of an Order Read Out off a Paper Made at Veyr, a Public Copy of the First Fragment Read Out Once at About Eleven in the Morning in the Room Over That Market in About Twenty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read, a Man of About Thirty-Three Saying in About One Hundred and Forty-Five Words That He Knows Which Hand Was on the Rope and Is Not Going to Be the Reason a Paper in This Harbor Works Out Which of Six It Was, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying in About One Hundred and Fifty-Five Words That a Record Which Stops in the Middle of a Person's Three Sentences Is a Record of a Person About to Say the Thing That Matters, the Third of Those Three Not Being on the Sheet, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Three Hundred and Five Words Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the twenty-third day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at ten o'clock in the morning.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing, and the covers were on it and the rope through the ring of it was not pulled. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the two slates and the public copy that were read out in that shed and in the room over that market are neither of them on that sheet and neither of them is in any minute. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixtieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-three days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the demand for the returns is going back down that coast in a basket on Saturday and it is a second asking. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-one days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

At about ten o'clock in the morning the two men who are going to be in a boat on Thursday wrote their reasons out in that shed, one slate each, in the words of the man who was going to carry them, and the hour went at the foot of each slate in the same hand that wrote the reason.

The first slate is the one of the man of about thirty-two, and it is in about one hundred and sixty words.

"**One branch of that bell is hanging on a chain on a stone off this mole and I am going to put a wedge into the hanger of it and drive the wedge and knock the pin out of the clapper arm, and that is the whole of what I am going to do.** Nothing else gets touched and nothing gets cut and nothing gets carried up that I cannot carry up with one hand. I am coming back up on the air and not before, and the air is twenty-five minutes and the twenty-five is on the sheet and not in my head, and I do not get an extra one because it goes badly. Nobody is to touch that branch tonight and nobody is to ring anything off that boat, and if the answer is going while I am down there then the answer is going without me and I will hear about it afterwards like everybody else."

The second slate is the one of the man of about forty-one of the first lay, who is the tender on the surface, and it is in about one hundred and thirty-two words.

"**I have the chain weight in my right hand from the hour we stand off and I do not take that hand off it until he is in the boat.** I have not got the two of us in that habit and I have not pulled a cutoff in that water in my life, and I would rather have done that once with somebody in it below me than go a whole winter without knowing that I could. If the line comes slack I pull it and I do not ask him and I do not wait to be told by anybody, and I want that written on the sheet with my name at the head of it, because a rule nobody can pull is not a rule, it is a hope."

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands read both slates out in that shed, and the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read them out a second time, and nobody asked either of those two men whether his own words were the right words, and both slates went behind the press in that shed with the two hours still at the foot of them.

---

At about a quarter to eleven in the morning a bell was rung at the front of that market, which is what that office has instead of a man in a room, and a clerk of it read out four lines off a paper with no seal on it, and the paper had been made at Veyr and had come down that coast in a bagged canvas slip with no Crown hand on the slip.

1. *A public copy of the first fragment has been made at that port from a record held at that port, and it is sent to every harbor on this coast that keeps a copy of any part of any record.*
2. *Every house, yard and shed that keeps such a copy is to have that public copy read out to it once, in a working place and on a working day, with about twenty people in the room when it is read.*
3. *The day of that reading is to be written on the foot of the paper and the paper given back to the man who brought it. No person is to be asked anything and no person is to be named for anything.*
4. *A house that has had that copy read to it once keeps the copy. A house that has not had it read to it once gives the copy back.*

Nobody in that market argued with the third of those four lines, and about eleven people who were standing there could not read any of the four and were told what was on it by the man of about forty-six.

And at about eleven o'clock in the morning the public copy of the first fragment was read out once, in the room over the floor of that market, with the market trading underneath it and about twenty people in that room, and the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read it, and about eleven of those twenty could not read it and were told what was on it by him, and he read it the one time only.

These are its six lines and there are six of them.

1. *This is a public copy of part of a record kept elsewhere. It was made at that port and it carries no seal, and the hand it is written in is not the hand the record is kept in.*
2. *It is part of a part. The rest of the record is not on this sheet and is not to be had on this sheet.*
3. *It is of the night of Friday the twenty-first, in a room over a floor in that harbor, and there were nine people in that room, and the room is described from the outside and not from the inside.*
4. *A bell was rung in that room by hand at about ten o'clock, and one person put a hand on the rope of it and did not take that hand off again until the room was empty, and nothing else in that room was sounded after that and before the room was empty.*
5. *One person in that room is recorded as having said three things. The first is that a voice out of a bell can be held by one hand on a rope and cannot be held twice on the same night. The second is that the man who held the rope is not named on this sheet and is not to be named on this sheet, and that nobody at that port is to be asked who he was.*
6. *The third of those three is not on this sheet, because the copy stops, and the foot of this sheet says in its own words that a copy that stops is a copy of part of a part and cannot be put with any other record, because there is nothing to put it with.*

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, in about one hundred and forty-five words, what the fourth of those six lines is worth in that harbor.

"**There was a hand on that rope at ten o'clock on Friday and I know which hand it was and there are four other people on this quay who know, and the fifth of those six lines says in its own words that nobody at that port is to be asked.** I am not going to ask. I am not going to stand in a room and put that question to four men I have known since I was a boy so that a piece of paper in this harbor can be made to stand up. It is a hand on a rope. It is true. It is the only true line in that sheet that says anything at all about what happened on that coast, and it is true the way a lamp is true, and it is not going to be the reason."

A woman of thirty of no office said out loud, in about one hundred and fifty-five words, what a record is that stops in the middle of three sentences.

"**The third of those three is not on the sheet, and a person who has said one thing and half of the next thing has not been made into a record of what they thought. They have been made into a record of being about to say it.** Whether what they were about to say was the thing that matters or a thing about the weather is not on that sheet and is not knowable by anybody standing in this room, and the sheet itself says in its own words that it cannot be put with any other record because there is nothing to put it with. That is eleven weeks of work in this harbor that has come ashore and it has come ashore with the middle taken out, and nobody is to be asked anything, and that is a document and not a person and it cannot be asked what the middle was."

A man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons said out loud, in front of those twenty people, that the third of those six lines is the only one of the six anybody in that room could do anything with, that he had gone and looked at the thing it says, and that it is right, and that the other five of the six are the shape of a night and not the night. Nobody asked him for a second thing and nobody in that harbor has said to him that he is wrong about anything.

The day was written on the foot of the paper in a hand that is not the clerk's, and the paper went back down the front street with the man who had brought it, and nobody has asked for that paper back.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about three hundred and five words on what a piece of paper that stops in the middle of a person is, and nobody in that slip was asked whether the reading had satisfied the second of those four lines.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and the seventy-first week stands behind the seventieth and will be read out on Tuesday whether that column moves or not.

**Eleven weeks I have wanted one sheet with a night on it, and one came down that front street this morning with six lines on it and the middle taken out of it, and it is the truest thing that has come into this harbor since the autumn, and it cannot be used for anything at all.** That is not me being careful. That is the sixth of its own six lines, written on the foot of it by whoever made it. A record that stops is a record that cannot be put with anything, and what I have wanted for eleven weeks is a thing to put with something.

The hand on the rope at ten o'clock is true and I cannot check it, and I have no business writing it on that bench stone in my own hand either, because I was in that room and I was not going to be able to prove that I was, and a figure in my hand that I cannot check is the worst kind. The two slates are behind that press with two hours at the foot of them and two names not on them yet. The third of those four columns took nothing today and nobody has been appointed to it, and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it and no date against it anywhere in this harbor, and I am not going to supply one."

Nobody was thanked for the reading and nobody was thanked for the arithmetic, and the public copy of the first fragment went back down that front street in a canvas slip with the middle still out of it.

**TWO REASONS WERE WRITTEN OUT ON TWO SLATES AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN THAT SHED, ONE IN THE WORDS OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO IN ABOUT one hundred and sixty WORDS AND ONE IN THE WORDS OF THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE OF THE FIRST LAY IN ABOUT one hundred and thirty-two WORDS, WITH AN HOUR AT THE FOOT OF EACH SLATE, AND NEITHER MAN WAS ASKED WHETHER HIS OWN WORDS WERE THE RIGHT WORDS. A BELL WAS RUNG AT THE FRONT OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN AND FOUR LINES OF AN ORDER MADE AT VEYR WERE READ OUT, AND THE THIRD OF THOSE FOUR SAYS THAT NO PERSON IS TO BE ASKED ANYTHING AND NO PERSON IS TO BE NAMED FOR ANYTHING. THE PUBLIC COPY OF THE FIRST FRAGMENT WAS THEN READ OUT ONCE, AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING, IN THE ROOM OVER THE FLOOR OF THAT MARKET, IN ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT ELEVEN COULD NOT READ IT, AND ITS SIXTH LINE SAYS IN ITS OWN WORDS THAT IT IS PART OF A PART AND CANNOT BE PUT WITH ANY OTHER RECORD BECAUSE THERE IS NOTHING TO PUT IT WITH. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FIVE WORDS THAT HE KNOWS WHICH HAND WAS ON THE ROPE AND THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO BE THE REASON A PIECE OF PAPER IN THAT HARBOR WORKS OUT WHICH OF SIX IT WAS. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIVE WORDS THAT A RECORD THAT STOPS IN THE MIDDLE OF A PERSON'S THREE SENTENCES IS A RECORD OF A PERSON ABOUT TO SAY SOMETHING. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE SAID THAT THE THIRD OF THOSE SIX LINES IS THE ONLY ONE ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM COULD DO ANYTHING WITH AND THAT IT IS RIGHT, AND WAS ASKED FOR NO SECOND THING. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED FOR THE READING. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason a reason had to be written in the words of the man who is going to carry it is mine and is that a yard which writes a reason for a man is a yard which has taken the words out of his mouth, and that in the winter of the twelfth a slip came round that harbor for men to put their reasons on for the harbour fund and that eleven men put reasons on it in a clerk's words and not one of them said a word about the fund again, and that the second of those two slates is the only page I have seen in this harbor in a year that says a thing out loud about what a man is going to do if it goes wrong; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the fourth of those six lines is worth nothing on that quay is mine and is that I can check the fifth of those six lines against a page of days and the first, second and fourth of them against a room, and that the second half of the fourth is the only part of that sheet that is about a night nobody can go and look at, and that the man who held that rope is not named on it and is not to be named on it and that the paper has therefore taken the only witness out of its own account and left the fact in, and that a fact without a witness is a shape; and that at about ten past twelve the reason nobody wrote the day of that reading in this shed's own book is mine and is that the second of those four lines asks for the day on the foot of that paper and not in a book, and that a reading that goes into two books is a reading that will be in two versions inside a month, and that the day on the foot of a paper that has gone down that front street is in one place and can be looked at by whoever holds the paper and by nobody else, and that is the correct number of places for a day like that; and that at about half past four the reason the third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing is not mine, and is that neither of those two columns is mine to write in, and that a man who puts a line in the third of them because a day was hard is a man who has appointed himself to something that four people have not agreed he may be, and that the hand at the head of the third one has been a boy's hand since the autumn and that hand is on no page in this harbor and I have not asked him about it and am not going to; and that at about six the reason I have written nothing in that room today, when a sheet with a night on it came into it for the first time in eleven weeks, is my own and is that I have spent eleven weeks wanting a thing I could put next to another thing, and that what came down that front street this morning is the first honest document that has come into this harbor since the autumn and it is a document with a hole in the middle of it, and that a man who writes a hole in the middle of it onto a bench stone in his own hand has stopped being a man who holds records and has started being the man who holds the reason there is a hole, and that there are nine people in that harbor who cannot read and that the day the middle of that record comes back or does not come back will be found out by a clerk and not by me.*
