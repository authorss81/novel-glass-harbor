# Chapter 564

*Friday the Fourth Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, a Paper Asking for the Returns of That Port and the Harbour Returns of the Vessels Engaged in That Line Going Onto the Board at the Head of That Mole at About Eleven in the Morning Beside Three Others and Nine Names Going Onto the Foot of It by the Middle of the Day, Four People in That Harbor Spending the Whole Afternoon Finding Out That Neither of Those Two Things Has Ever Been in That Harbor at Any Hour, a Man of About Fifty-Three Saying Out Loud That a Demand Nailed to a Board in This Harbor Goes to Nobody, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Friday the fourth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and at the head of that mole where there are four papers on a board and a lamp that is out over the top of it.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and four days. There are now four papers on the board at the head of that mole, being the account of the night of the fourteenth, the paper saying that the sea is under martial custody, the paper with a seal on it and no figure on it from the third of December, and the demand for the returns, and the first three of those four have not been taken down and two of them have not been opened. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and the mole head is dark and the light that is meant to stand over that board has been out since the seventh of May. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-two days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven in the morning a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay nailed a fourth paper to that board, and he was not asked to do it by anybody, and there was no wind to speak of and the nail went in on the second strike.

This is what that paper says, and it is four lines, and the fourth of them is the same shape as the sixth line of the account above it on the same board.

1. *The account above is made, on the face of it, from the returns of that port and from the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line.*
2. *Those returns are asked for, to be produced in some place in this harbor where a person may go and look at them, and to be produced whole and not a part of them.*
3. *A person who holds a copy of any of them is asked to say so, and is not asked to give the copy away and is not asked for a name.*
4. *Any person in that harbor may put a name at the foot of this paper, and about nine names went on it by the middle of the day, and about eleven people of that harbor who cannot read it were told what it said by about nine people who can.*

---

And four people in that harbor spent the whole of that afternoon finding out that neither of those two things has ever been in that harbor at any hour of any day in this writer's life or anybody else's.

The man of about fifty-three went to the west basin and came back and said that a harbour return is a thing made out at the end of a month by a harbour office and sent on to an office two days sail away, and that this harbor has never made one and has never been sent one, and that the vessels engaged in that line belong to two other harbors. A woman of thirty of no office went into the front room behind that step at the back of that market and came out with a book of the harbor's own, and the book goes back to the eighteenth of May past and there is nothing in it about a return. The man who keeps the leaf stood in that shed at about three o'clock and said out loud that he had gone through his own hands and could not find a single figure in a year that had come off a harbour return, and that this yard's figures came off chalk and twine and a slate. The man of about thirty-three who hires hands sent a boy to the mole head to look at the board and the boy came back and said there were four papers on it and one of them was about the water and the other three were not.

---

The man of about fifty-three said out loud, on the quay at about half past three, that a demand nailed to a board in this harbor goes to nobody, and that the returns are of a port four days down that coast and of the harbour offices of two other harbors, and that a cart takes four days each way on that coast road and a boat takes a day and a night, and that the paper on that board will be read by whoever walks past it, and that whoever walks past it on that coast is not going to be the man who keeps the returns.

He said that he was not against the demand. He said that the demand was the right demand and that the wall in the room over that market is where he would put it if he were the one putting it, and that a paper on a board in the open is the only instrument in this harbor that costs nothing to make and can be read by anybody at any hour.

---

A woman of thirty of no office said the thing that the rest of them had been circling for two days, out loud, in about fifty-six words, on the step at the back of that market, and she said it once and she did not put it on a page.

"**A sheet of paper on a wall cannot be asked where its own figures came from, and there is nobody in this harbor who has ever held one, and nine names went on the foot of that paper this morning by people who want to see it and none of them is going to see it.**"

A man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that the fifth of the six lines above it on that board says in its own words that it stops at what the returns contain, and that asking for the returns is the only question anybody in this coast can ask that is not the same as saying the six lines are wrong, and that a person who asks for a thing and is refused it has lost nothing except the hour.

---

And at about half past five that afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, with the lamp with the crack in its chimney burning the way it has burned since the autumn, and said in about a hundred and forty-five words what four papers on one board is, and nobody disagreed with him and nobody agreed with him.

"Ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence. Unmoved, and the demand cost a nail and about a quarter of an hour of a man who writes and it is the cheapest instrument this harbor owns. **Four people spent this afternoon looking for two kinds of paper that have never been here, and that is not a failure, it is the answer, and the answer is that this coast has spent two winters arguing with six lines it could not check the inside of. A boat takes a day and a night and a cart takes four days and the paper goes in a basket at four o'clock, and if it comes back it will come back to a board that three other people are reading at the same time and cannot hear it.**"

Nobody on that quay was asked for the remainder of it and no debt in that harbor was touched, and not one person on that mole had the gift in their mouth, and the nine names went on the foot of that paper and stayed there in the weather.

---

**A PAPER ASKING FOR THE RETURNS THE ACCOUNT IS MADE FROM WENT ONTO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING, IN FOUR LINES, WITH ABOUT NINE NAMES ON THE FOOT OF IT BY THE MIDDLE OF THE DAY AND WITH NOBODY ASKING FOR THE COPY OR FOR A NAME. FOUR PEOPLE SPENT THE AFTERNOON FINDING OUT THAT NEITHER THE RETURNS OF THAT PORT NOR THE HARBOUR RETURNS OF THE VESSELS ENGAGED IN THAT LINE HAS EVER BEEN IN THAT HARBOR. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE SAID OUT LOUD THAT A DEMAND NAILED TO A BOARD IN THIS HARBOR GOES TO NOBODY, AND THAT HE IS NOT AGAINST IT, AND THAT A BOAT TAKES A DAY AND A NIGHT AND A CART TAKES FOUR DAYS EACH WAY. A WOMAN OF THIRTY SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WORDS THAT A SHEET OF PAPER ON A WALL CANNOT BE ASKED WHERE ITS OWN FIGURES CAME FROM. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the fourth day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay nailed a fourth paper to the board at the head of that mole at about eleven in the morning and was not asked to do it by anybody, and that that paper carries four lines, being that the account above it is made on the face of it from the returns of that port and from the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line, being that those returns are asked for whole and in some place in that harbor where a person may go and look at them, being that a person holding a copy of any of them is asked to say so and is not asked to give the copy away and is not asked for a name, and being that any person in that harbor may put a name at the foot of it, and that about nine names went on that foot by the middle of the day and that about eleven people of that harbor who cannot read it were told what it says by about nine people who can; and that four people in that harbor spent the afternoon of that day looking for those two kinds of paper and did not find either of them, being that a man of about fifty-three said a harbour return is made out at the end of a month by a harbour office and sent on to an office two days sail away and that this harbor has never made one and has never been sent one and that the vessels engaged in that line belong to two other harbors, being that a woman of thirty of no office went into the front room behind the step at the back of that market and found a book of the harbor's own that goes back to the eighteenth of May past with nothing in it about a return, being that the man who keeps the leaf said out loud at about three o'clock that he had been through his own hands and could not find a figure in a year that had come off a harbour return, and being that a boy sent to the mole head came back and said there were four papers on that board; and that the man of about fifty-three said out loud on that quay at about half past three that a demand nailed to a board in this harbor goes to nobody and that a cart takes four days each way on that coast road and a boat takes a day and a night, and that he is not against the demand; and that a woman of thirty of no office said out loud on the step at the back of that market in about fifty-six words that a sheet of paper on a wall cannot be asked where its own figures came from and that there is nobody in that harbor who has ever held one and that nine names went on the foot of that paper by people who want to see it and none of them is going to see it; and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that the fifth of the six lines says in its own words that it stops at what the returns contain and that asking for the returns is the only question on that coast which is not the same as saying the six lines are wrong; and that the thirteen days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and seven and one thousand three hundred and nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those thirteen days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the demand went on the board rather than into that shed is his own, and is that a demand made by a yard is a demand made by the party, and that a sheet nailed to a board at the head of a mole is the only instrument in that harbor that belongs to nobody, and that the second column of that leaf is not checkable from a mole and the first column is, and that the whole of what has happened in that harbor this month is that things which can be counted have gone on the thing which can be counted and things which cannot be counted have gone on walls; and that the reason he went through his own hands for a year looking for a figure off a harbour return is his own, and is that he was not looking for one to use against anybody, he was looking for one to put beside the fifth of those six lines, and that there is not one on this coast, and that a man who has spent a year keeping a leaf with three things a stranger can check on it has never once written down where the wage figures come from either, and that they come from a lid in chalk on a step and have come from there for eleven weeks; and that the reason the man of about fifty-three was not argued with is his own, and is that he said the demand was the right demand in the same breath as he said it goes to nobody, and that a man who says a thing is right and useless in the same ten minutes has done the hardest half of a thing that anybody on that coast can do this month, and that the other half is the part where somebody carries it; and that the reason no figure is set against the nine names at the foot of that paper is his own, and is that a name at the foot of a demand is a person who has spent nothing and gets nothing and that putting a figure on that would make the most honest nine people in that harbor into a bill, and that the first column takes the day and the day is the day and the day was a nail and a boat basket.*
