# Chapter 581

*Monday the Twenty-First Day of February, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Twenty-Seventh Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Night Written Out on the Back of a Sheet With an Hour Against Every Part of It and No Name at the Head of It and No Signature at the Foot of It, Read Out at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon in About Nine People and Read Out Twice, a Man of About Thirty-Three Saying in About One Hundred and Sixty Words That a Page With No Name on It Can Be Argued With by Anybody and Held To by Nobody, a Woman of About Forty-Four Saying in About One Hundred and Forty-Nine Words That the Only Part of It Nobody Will Argue With Is the Part Where Nobody Signs It, Nothing Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Two Hundred and Seventy-Three Words Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the twenty-first day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at two o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-seventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern was not used for anything at any hour of that day. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and nineteen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninetieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-one days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the offer of four lines is behind a door in the room over that market and is not on that board and nothing is written under its fourth line. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-nine days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The hundred and twenty-seventh charge went into that cistern at about eight in the morning, one butt on a shoulder up that ladder, eleven times, and the eleven butts went up and not one of them was carried on by anybody standing on the covers to save the climbing.

The two men who do it were on that ladder from about a quarter to eight and the covers were back on it by about a quarter past nine, and the butt behind that market stood full to the top of the second hoop until about four o'clock, and nobody in that market drew a bucket off it, and there is still no figure anywhere in that harbor for a new length of the hose at the top of it.

---

And at about eleven o'clock that morning a man of about thirty-two wrote a sheet out in that shed, and at about two o'clock in the afternoon it was put face up on that bench stone with about nine people in the room and the door standing open, and he said out loud before anybody looked at it that there was no name at the head of it and no signature at the foot of it and that both of those were on purpose.

The night is written out in six parts and there is an hour against every one of the six, and here they are.

1. *About half past twelve in the afternoon: the sheet is made and the air is counted out in that shed, and the two men who are going are told the depth and the figure of the air and nothing else.*
2. *About one o'clock: the boat is taken off the slip with the oars shipped, and nothing is rung from that boat or from anywhere else.*
3. *About ten past two: one man goes over the rail in about four fathoms and the boat holds herself over him on the oars.*
4. *About thirty-five minutes past two: he is in the boat and the air is off him.*
5. *About half past three: the boat is alongside that quay.*
6. *About five o'clock: this page is read out in that shed and nobody signs it.*

The man of about twenty-seven, who wrote the six lines on the wall of the room over that market and is on nobody's crew and wrote those six lines for nobody, read the six parts out loud in that shed with the door open, and read them a second time at the asking of a woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay, and he got nothing wrong on either reading.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands then said out loud, in about one hundred and sixty words, what a page with an hour against every part of it and no name at the head of it is for.

"**A page like that can be argued with by anybody in this harbor and held to by nobody at all, and that is not a fault in it. That is the whole of what it is for. Put a name at the head of it and the next thing that happens is that the name has to be defended, and a name that has to be defended is a name that is being used.** I have been in this harbor thirty years and every page that ever went up on a wall with a name at the head of it stopped being a thing anybody could say no to and became a thing somebody was going to be shouted at about. This one has got six hours on it and nobody's name, and I can stand here this afternoon and say the half past three is wrong, and if I am wrong then the page is wrong and not me."

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay said out loud, in about one hundred and forty-nine words, that the sixth of those six parts is the one nobody in that room is going to argue with.

"**Nobody signs it. That is the sixth part, and it is the only part of that page anybody can be certain of, and I have watched men in this market sign things they have never read.** A man of about thirty-three has told nine people that a page with no name on it is a thing that can be argued with, and he is right, and the reason he is right is that there is nothing on that sheet anybody can take off a wall and put in a drawer. Put a name on it and the first man who comes down here with a bad hour on it takes a piece of chalk and writes under it, and then the page belongs to the two of them and not to the harbor. I keep a shutter up on a store and I know what a shutter is for."

The man of about thirty-two said out loud that the two names go at the head of that sheet on the day and not before it, and that the man who is going to be in the water and the man who is going to be on the surface of it will both put their names on it on the day, and that a name on it on the day is not a signature on it tonight, and that nobody in that room was going to be asked tonight whether the half past three was right or whether the air was enough.

About four of those nine people could not read the six parts and were told what was on them by the man of about twenty-seven, and he told them the same thing twice and not one word different, and nobody wrote anything on that page, and nobody copied it out, and it is not on that sheet and it is not in any minute.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and seventy-three words on what a night with an hour against every part of it is, and nobody in that slip was asked to sign the page that had been read out in it three hours before.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet and nothing came off it, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came in and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out, and the bag is twenty-eight pence.

**Six parts and an hour against every one of them, and no name at the head of it and no signature at the foot of it, and it settles nothing. I have been putting an hour against things on this leaf since December, and this afternoon a man of about thirty-two put six of them on a sheet and then would not put his name to it, and that is the only difference between his page and mine and it is the whole of it.** The two names go on at the head of it on the day. That is what a name is for on a page of hours. A name is not an agreement. A name is a person saying that those hours are the hours, and that if they are the wrong hours then the wrong hours are his and not this harbor's.

That cistern was charged this morning for the hundred and twenty-seventh time and not one bucket came out of it. The column on the step is read on a Tuesday and not today. The third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing today and the fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and there is no date against it anywhere in this harbor, and I am not going to put one on it."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was asked for the rest of it, and the sheet with the six parts on it went in behind the press in that shed at about ten past six with the six hours still on it and no name on it.

**A NIGHT WAS WRITTEN OUT ON THE BACK OF A SHEET IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN SIX PARTS WITH AN HOUR AGAINST EVERY PART OF IT AND NO NAME AT THE HEAD OF IT AND NO SIGNATURE AT THE FOOT OF IT, AND IT WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE BY A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN WHO IS ON NOBODY'S CREW AND IT WAS READ TWICE, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY WORDS THAT A PAGE WITH NO NAME ON IT CAN BE ARGUED WITH BY ANYBODY AND HELD TO BY NOBODY AND THAT A NAME THAT HAS TO BE DEFENDED IS A NAME THAT IS BEING USED, A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-NINE WORDS THAT THE SIXTH OF THOSE SIX PARTS IS THE PART WHERE NOBODY SIGNS IT, AND THE TWO NAMES GO AT THE HEAD OF THAT SHEET ON THE DAY AND NOT BEFORE IT. THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SEVENTH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. NOTHING WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY AND NOTHING CAME OFF IT. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED FOR ANY PART OF IT. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-first day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-seventh time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and that the water that went into that cistern was not used for anything at any hour of that day, and that the hose at the top of it has been dead since the spring and that there is no figure anywhere in that harbor for a new length of it; and that at about eleven in the morning the man of about thirty-two wrote a sheet out in that shed with a night on it in six parts and an hour against every one of the six, and that it was put face up on that bench stone at about two o'clock in the afternoon with about nine people in that room and the door standing open, and that he said out loud before anybody looked at it that there was no name at the head of it and no signature at the foot of it and that both of those were on purpose; and that the six parts are that about half past twelve the sheet is made and the air is counted out in that shed and the two men who are going are told the depth and the figure of the air and nothing else, that about one o'clock the boat is taken off the slip with the oars shipped and nothing is rung from that boat or from anywhere else, that about ten past two one man goes over the rail in about four fathoms and the boat holds herself over him on the oars, that about thirty-five minutes past two he is in the boat and the air is off him, that about half past three the boat is alongside that quay, and that about five o'clock this page is read out in that shed and nobody signs it; and that the man of about twenty-seven read the six parts out loud twice and got nothing wrong on either reading and is on nobody's crew, and that about four of those nine people could not read the six parts and were told what was on them by him and he told them the same thing twice and not one word different; and that a man of about thirty-three said out loud in about one hundred and sixty words that a page with no name on it can be argued with by anybody and held to by nobody, and that a woman of about forty-four said out loud in about one hundred and forty-nine words that the sixth of those six parts is the part where nobody signs it, and that the man of about thirty-two said out loud that the two names go at the head of that sheet on the day and not before it and that nobody in that room was going to be asked tonight whether the half past three was right; and that nobody wrote anything on that page and nobody copied it out and it is not on that sheet and it is not in any minute, and that it went in behind the press in that shed at about ten past six with the six hours still on it and no name on it; and that the thirty-one days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and fourteen and one thousand four hundred and forty-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those thirty-one days.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason eleven trips went up that ladder and not one of them was carried on is the same one as every charge since the autumn, and is that a butt of water weighs about sixty pounds on a shoulder at the top of a ladder and that a man who is asked why he did not save the climbing will say that a man standing on a cover to be spared the climb is a man who will be standing on that cover when somebody else needs to get past him, and that neither of those two men has ever been on that rota board and that the name at the head of it has been off since the spring; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the man of about thirty-three said what he said about a name at the head of a page is his own, and is that in the winter of the twelfth of this harbor a paper went up on the front of the customs house with a man's name at the foot of it and that man was not in the harbor and had not been asked, and that the paper was true and that the man's family found out about it from a stranger in a bar, and that a page with a name on it does not stay a fact for long, it becomes a thing two families are arguing about; and that at about ten to three the reason the woman of about forty-four said the sixth part was the only certain one is her own, and is that her shutter is the only part of that building that is hers and that a shutter can be put up at six in the evening by one person with no argument whatever, and that she has watched a thing get a name put on it in this market four times in thirty years and that on three of those four times the name was put on it by a man who was frightened, which is worse; and that at about six the reason the man who keeps the leaf said that a name is not an agreement is his own, and is that for six weeks he has been putting a name against a figure on that leaf every night and that the name has never once been read out in that shed by anybody but him, and that if a name on a page of hours means anything at all it means the hours can be argued with by the man whose hours they are, and that is the whole of what a name is worth on paper and it took him six weeks and a Monday to be able to say it in eleven words; and that at about ten past six the reason that sheet went in behind the press rather than onto that bench stone is his own, and is that a page of hours left lying face up on a bench stone in that harbor for an evening will be read on that quay on the morning by nine people who were not in that room, and that the six parts of that page are the parts of a night that two men are going to try, and that a night that nine strangers have read before it has happened is a night somebody will argue about at the fish stall instead of at the water.*
