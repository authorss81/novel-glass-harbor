# Chapter 577

*Thursday the Seventeenth Day of February, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Twenty-Sixth Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, Four Marks Asked One at a Time and Out Loud on the Boards of That Market at About Eleven in the Morning in Front of About Nine People What They Can Hear, Three of Them Answering in Their Own Mouths, Being a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands, a Woman of About Forty-Four Who Keeps a Shut Store on the Second Lay, and a Man of About Twenty-Six of the Third Lay, and the Fourth Not Answering, and That Fourth Being the Piece of Worked Lead in a Hole in a Stone Under About Four Fathoms Which Is Not One of the Five on the Page on Four Tacks, and Nobody at That Port or in That Harbor Having a Name for Whoever Put It In, a Bellwright of That Harbor Not Asked for a Figure and His Chest Shut, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the seventeenth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on the boards at the back of that market with about nine people standing on them at eleven in the morning.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-sixth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and about eleven people came past that butt in the course of the morning and not one of them opened the tap on it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and fifteen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and seventeen days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for a figure in nineteen days and his chest stays shut and the eight marks and a day of a boat stay unargued. There is a fourth mark in that water and it is not one of the five on the page on four tacks and it is a piece of worked lead in a hole in a stone under about four fathoms, and nobody at that port has a name for whoever put it in and nobody has sent for one. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-five days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That cistern was charged for the hundred and twenty-sixth time at about eight in the morning. Both men were at the covers before the light was properly up off the market and the first butt went up that ladder at about ten past eight, and there were eleven of them, and the last one was down at about a quarter past nine.

The butt behind that market stood full to the top of the second hoop by ten and stayed that way until the middle of the afternoon, and the hose at the top of it has been dead since before the frost and there is no figure anywhere in that harbor for a new length of it. Nobody walked on any of the eleven trips and nobody opened the tap.

---

They did it on the boards at the back of that market at about eleven in the morning, in the open, in front of about nine people, and the page on four tacks came off the boards of that market and stood up on a trestle at the head of the steps, and a man of about forty-six who mends nets read the four names off it and then stopped, because there are five marks on that page and four names.

The first mark was asked, out loud, by a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, and the question was what it can hear, and he answered in his own mouth and nobody answered for him.

"It can hear the answer, and it can hear my own crew's oars, and on a dark night I cannot tell you which of the two I hear first, and I have been out on that water thirty years and I have never once been able to tell you that. **I would not put that in a book.**"

The second mark was asked, and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay answered in her own mouth.

"It can hear the answer. It can hear nothing else at that hour. My shutter is up and the shutter is why, and a shutter is not a place a man listens from, and that is the whole of what I have got. I have had a shutter and a lamp on a counter for nineteen winters and I have never once pretended to that counter that I was hearing a thing in the water."

The third mark was asked, and a man of about twenty-six of the third lay, one of the six who keep it, answered in his own mouth, and about nine people on those boards laughed at the end of it and one of them did not.

"It can hear the answer and it can hear a gull, and the gull comes in on about the same interval about an hour later, and I have been listening to that gull since Wednesday, and I am telling you that because a man who has been listening to a gull for four days is a man who has stopped listening, and I would like somebody to tell me when mine starts."

Nobody said when his started. A woman of about thirty of no office said out loud, in about seventy-seven words, that the fourth mark was not one of the five on that page, and it was a piece of worked lead in a hole in a stone under about four fathoms, and that nobody at that port had a name for whoever put it in and nobody in that harbor has one either, and that a man who has been at this for four days has been asking a piece of lead.

"It is not answering because it is lead in a hole in a stone, and that is not a thing anybody can put a hand on, and this morning we have asked four things on the water and got three answers and the three answers came out of three people's mouths and the fourth did not come out of anybody's, and the difference between those two is a person and there is no person on that mark." She said it and she did not say what should be done instead, and nobody in that room said what should be done instead either.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said one thing afterwards, out loud, and it was not a motion and nobody seconded it. He said that the correct answer to a piece of worked lead in a hole in a stone is a man in a boat sitting at it at night with a slate, and that the three of the five marks that answered on the night of the twenty-second of January answered because somebody was awake near them, and that a thing that answers is a thing somebody is listening to, and that nobody in this harbor has got a boat that is not going, and that he is not going to write that down on a page on four tacks and he is not going to be asked to.

Nobody argued him out of it and nobody thanked him. He said out loud, in about twenty-nine words, that no person in that harbor had been asked for a buoy this morning and that the question had been what a thing on the water could hear and that those are two different things and that the party is not asking anybody for anything today.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and sixty-nine words on three answers and no answer, and nobody in that slip was asked which of the three mouths was the right one.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and there is a page on four tacks in that market with four names on it and five marks and it stood on a trestle at the head of the steps this morning with about nine people round it, and it is not on that sheet.

**Four marks were asked this morning what they can hear and three of them answered in a person's mouth and the fourth one did not answer anybody, and the one that did not answer is a piece of worked lead in a hole in a stone, and the reason it did not answer is that it is a thing and not a person, and the whole of this month has turned on that difference and this morning it was said out loud on a step by a woman of thirty in thirty-four words.**

Three of the five marks answered on the night of the twenty-second of January and that is a fact about the water and not a claim on anything, and I have got no figure for why three answered and two did not, and I have not got a figure for why those three answers were worth taking and that one was not, and a man of about thirty-three says a man in a boat with a slate is the answer to it and that he has not got a boat that is not going.

I have not written any of that down on the tacks and I am not going to."

---

**FOUR MARKS WERE ASKED ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN THE OPEN IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WHAT THEY CAN HEAR, ONE AT A TIME, AND THREE OF THEM ANSWERED IN THEIR OWN MOUTHS, BEING A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS WHO SAID HE CANNOT TELL ON A DARK NIGHT WHICH OF TWO SOUNDS HE HEARS FIRST AND WOULD NOT PUT IT IN A BOOK, A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A SHUT STORE ON THE SECOND LAY WHO SAID A SHUTTER IS NOT A PLACE A MAN LISTENS FROM, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX OF THE THIRD LAY WHO SAID A GULL COMES IN ON ABOUT THE SAME INTERVAL AN HOUR LATER AND THAT HE HAS BEEN LISTENING TO THE GULL SINCE WEDNESDAY. THE FOURTH DID NOT ANSWER IN ANYBODY'S MOUTH, AND IT IS A PIECE OF WORKED LEAD IN A HOLE IN A STONE UNDER ABOUT FOUR FATHOMS, AND IT IS NOT ONE OF THE FIVE ON THE PAGE ON FOUR TACKS, AND NOBODY HAS A NAME FOR WHOEVER PUT IT IN. THE PAGE CARRIES FOUR NAMES AND FIVE MARKS AND NO NAME OF THIS PARTY IS ON IT. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR A BUOY. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the seventeenth day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-sixth time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and that both men were at the covers before the light was properly up off that market and the first butt went up that ladder at about ten past eight and the last one was down at about a quarter past nine, and that about eleven people came past that butt in the course of the morning and not one of them opened the tap on it; and that the four marks were asked on the boards at the back of that market at about eleven in the morning, in the open, in front of about nine people, one at a time and out loud, what they can hear, and that the page on four tacks came off the boards and stood up on a trestle at the head of the steps, and that a man of about forty-six read the four names off it and stopped, the page carrying five marks and four names; and that the first mark was asked by a man of about thirty-three who hires hands and answered in his own mouth, being that it can hear the answer and his own crew's oars and that on a dark night he cannot tell which of the two he hears first and has never been able to and would not put it in a book; and that the second mark was asked and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay answered in her own mouth, being that it can hear the answer and nothing else at that hour, and that her shutter is up and the shutter is why, and that a shutter is not a place a man listens from, and that she has never once pretended to that counter that she was hearing a thing in the water; and that the third mark was asked and a man of about twenty-six of the third lay, one of the six who keep it, answered in his own mouth, being that it can hear the answer and a gull, and that the gull comes in on about the same interval about an hour later, and that he has been listening to that gull since Wednesday and that a man who has been listening to a gull for four days is a man who has stopped listening and that he would like somebody to tell him when his own starts; and that about nine people on those boards laughed at the end of that and one of them did not; and that the fourth mark was not one of the five on that page and is a piece of worked lead in a hole in a stone under about four fathoms, and that a woman of thirty of no office said out loud in about seventy-seven words that it did not answer because it is a thing and not a person, and that this morning four things on the water were asked and three answers came out of three people's mouths and the fourth came out of nobody's, and that the difference between those two is a person and there is no person on that mark, and that she did not say what should be done instead and nobody in that room said it either; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that the correct answer to a piece of worked lead in a hole in a stone is a man in a boat at it at night with a slate, and that the three of the five marks that answered on the night of the twenty-second of January answered because somebody was awake near them, and that a thing that answers is a thing somebody is listening to, and that nobody in that harbor has got a boat that is not going, and that he is not going to write that down on a page on four tacks and is not going to be asked to; and that he said out loud in about twenty-nine words that no person in that harbor was asked for a buoy this morning and that the question had been what a thing on the water could hear and that those are two different things; and that nobody argued him out of it and nobody thanked him; and that the twenty-seven days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and ten and one thousand four hundred and twenty-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those twenty-seven days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason the charge went in at ten past eight is his own, and is that he was standing on the boards at the head of the steps waiting to write nothing down and could hear the ladder from there, and that the two men carrying that butt have never once been asked by anybody why they do it and have never once been thanked for it and that he has counted eleven trips on that rota since the first of December and has counted the same eleven on every charge, and that a man who counts the same number every week for ten weeks without telling anybody is doing the whole of this month's work; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the three answers are on the first column and the fourth silence is on it as well is his own, and is that a first column takes one entry a day and the entry for a day that four things were asked on the water is the four things and what came back, and that a day on which three people said what they can hear and one thing on the water could not say anything is one day and not two, and that anybody who reads that column in six weeks is going to be able to count that three of them were mouths and one of them was not without being told; and that at about half past eleven the reason he is not going to put the man in a boat with a slate on the tacks is his own, and is that a page on four tacks in a market is a public thing, and that a man in a boat at that mark at night is a man who cannot be reached between the hours of about ten and about four, and that there is no rule in that harbor yet about people who cannot be reached, and that writing a need for an unreachable man onto a public page before the rule exists is how a page stops being a list of people and starts being a list of duties, and that this harbor has four names on that page and he is not going to be the one who turns it into eleven; and that at about half past five the reason he wrote that a woman of thirty said it in seventy-seven words and did not say what should be done instead is his own, and is that on the third of this month the same woman said out loud in about two hundred and four words that she holds a particular and is not giving it, and that a person who hands a room the shape of a thing once a week is a person the room stops waiting for, and that stopping for it is the only way to keep it hers; and that at about ten past six the reason the gull is on that page is his own, and is that it is the first time in nineteen days that a man in that harbor has said out loud that he has stopped listening, and that it was said in about nine seconds and under a laugh, and that a page of days is the only instrument that harbor has which can hold a thing that was said under a laugh without asking the man to say it again.*
