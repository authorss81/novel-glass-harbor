# Chapter 567

*Monday the Seventh Day of February, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Twenty-Third Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, Three People in One Room above That Shed for About an Hour at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With the Second of Two Pages of Hours on the Bench Stone in Front of Them, a Night Written Out in Three Parts With an Hour Against Each and the Hour at Which Each of the Three Could Stop It Written Against That, Two Hours in the Middle of That Night With One Name on Them, the Rule About Who May Stop It Read Out in Its Own Words From the Page and Not Improved, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the seventh day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over that shed with a door that stands open on a slip and about nine people on the boards outside it.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-third time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern at about eight in the morning was not used for anything at any hour of that day. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything, and nothing was priced on any page in that shed at any hour of that day. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and seven days. The second of the two pages of hours in that shed carries three names on it and the third rule copied out from the page that went out on the water on the twenty-seventh of January and nine words under that copy in a different ink with no answer under them, and all three of those names were in that room this afternoon and none of them is a crew and none of them is a body. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-five days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eight in the morning the hundred and twenty-third charge went into that cistern, eleven trips up that ladder by hand, and nobody walked on any of it, and the two men who carried it were at it from a quarter to eight and had it done by about a quarter past nine. The butt behind that market stood full to the top of the second hoop again by the time those two men came down off that ladder. The hose that goes in at the top of that cistern has been dead since the spring and there is no figure anywhere in that harbor for the cost of a new length of it.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a room over that shed was let out for an hour, and three people went into it, and about nine people stood on the boards outside the door for most of the hour, and the door stood open the whole time.

The three were the man of about thirty-three who hires hands, the woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay, and the man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons. The second of the two pages of hours went on the bench stone in front of them and the nine words under the copied rule went with it and there is still no answer under them.

The man of about thirty-two wrote out the night in three parts on a page in a hand that is not the hand of any of the three, with an hour against each part, and against each part he wrote the hour at which that person could stop it. Nobody went in the water. No boat left that quay. Nothing on that page was agreed to and nothing on it was signed and no person on any water in this harbor is a member of anything.

---

They got to the middle of the night and stopped there for about ten minutes, and the reason is arithmetic and it was not anybody's fault and it was the same on the second attempt and the third.

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that he had told that room on the twenty-eighth that he will not stand in that yard at two in the morning and wait to be asked whether he is wanted, and that he was not changing it, and that a page which has an hour against his name that he has said out loud he will not be in is a page that has told the truth about itself, and that the man of about thirty-two wrote the hour in anyway because the man who wrote it is not him.

The man of about fifty-three said that on a Tuesday tide he is four hours down that coast with a boat of about fourteen tons and that no page in that room can put him on a quay in that harbor at any hour of a night, and the woman of about forty-four said that there is a store on the second lay with a shutter up and a lamp on the counter and that a person behind that shutter is not in a room, and that this is the second time she has said the same shape of thing and she was not going to say it a third time.

So the page came out with two hours in the middle of the night carrying one name on them, and the rule on the face of that page needs three.

---

The man of about thirty-two read the third rule out loud in that room, in the words it is printed in, and about nine people outside the door heard it through the open door, and he did not improve one word of it.

"Any one of three named people may stop this. A thing that can be stopped by any one of three people is also a thing that cannot be kept running by anybody if those three are not in the room. Neither half of that is fixed and both halves are printed here on the same day."

He said out loud, after it, that the hour was up, and that the page was going to be put down on that bench stone and not carried to any water by anybody, and that nothing on it had been priced and nothing on it had been settled, and that a man who asks to be asked is not on it and is not a part of it and is not one of the three.

The man of about fifty-three said one word, which was no, and nobody asked him what he meant by it, and the hour was over.

---

And at about half past five that Monday, with the page back on that bench stone and the nine words still under the copied rule with nothing under them, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud and said in about a hundred and fifty-nine words what an hour in a room above a shed came to, and nobody in that slip asked for a figure for it.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and nothing was priced today and there is no line on that sheet for an hour spent in a room. **Three people went into a room for an hour and wrote a night out on a page and found out at about ten to three that two hours of it have one name on them, and that is the whole of what an hour bought, and it is not on a sheet and it is not on a wall and it is not a thing anybody has been thanked for. Two hours of a Tuesday night in this harbor with one name on them is worth more than the six lines on that board and it cannot be put anywhere anybody else can see it, and I have been trying for ten days to build a thing that can be.**"

No further account was demanded of anybody in that shed on that Monday, and not one person in that harbor had the words for a gift anywhere near his mouth, and the page went back onto the bench stone where the other one was, and the nine words were still under the rule with nothing under them.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THIRD TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. THREE PEOPLE WERE IN ONE ROOM FOR ABOUT AN HOUR WITH THE SECOND OF TWO PAGES OF HOURS IN FRONT OF THEM AND THE DOOR STOOD OPEN AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE STOOD OUTSIDE IT. A NIGHT WAS WRITTEN OUT IN THREE PARTS WITH AN HOUR AGAINST EACH AND THE HOUR AT WHICH EACH OF THE THREE COULD STOP IT, AND TWO HOURS IN THE MIDDLE OF THAT NIGHT CAME OUT WITH ONE NAME ON THEM. THE THIRD RULE WAS READ OUT IN ITS OWN WORDS AND WAS NOT IMPROVED. THE PAGE WAS PUT DOWN ON THAT BENCH STONE AND WENT TO NO WATER AND NO PERSON ON THAT WATER IS A MEMBER OF ANYTHING AND A MAN WHO ASKS TO BE ASKED IS NOT ON IT. NOTHING WAS PRICED AND NOTHING WAS SETTLED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

and that a room over that shed was let out for an hour at about two o'clock in the afternoon and that three people went into it, being a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, a woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay, and a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons, and that about nine people stood on the boards outside the door for most of the hour and that the door stood open the whole time; and that the second of the two pages of hours went on the bench stone in front of them with the nine words under the copied rule and with no answer under the nine words; and that a night was written out on a page in three parts in a hand that is not the hand of any of the three with an hour against each part and the hour at which that person could stop it written against that part, and that nobody went in the water and no boat left that quay and nothing on that page was agreed to and nothing on it was signed and no person on any water in that harbor is a member of anything; and that the page came out with two hours in the middle of the night carrying one name on them and that the rule on the face of that page needs three; and that the man of about thirty-three said out loud that he said on the twenty-eighth that he will not stand in that yard at two in the morning and wait to be asked whether he is wanted, and that he was not changing it, and that the man of about thirty-two wrote the hour in anyway; and that the man of about fifty-three said that on a Tuesday tide he is four hours down that coast and that no page in that room can put him on a quay in that harbor at any hour of a night, and that the woman of about forty-four said that a person behind a shutter is not in a room and that this is the second time she has said the same shape of thing and she was not going to say it a third time; and that the man of about thirty-two read the third rule out loud in that room in the words it is printed in and did not improve one word of it, and that about nine people outside the door heard it through the open door, and that he said out loud after it that nothing on the page had been priced and nothing on it had been settled and that a man who asks to be asked is not on it and is not one of the three, and that the man of about fifty-three said one word and that nobody asked him what he meant by it; and that the seventeen days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and one thousand three hundred and seventy-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those seventeen days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the page is not carried to any water is his own, and is that the third rule on the face of it says in its own words that a thing like that cannot be kept running by anybody if those three are not in the room, and that a page written out in a room above a shed is on the quay by nine in the morning and in another harbor by Wednesday if anybody takes it, and that a rule which can be stopped by one of three is only honest while the three of them are in a room, and that they were in a room this afternoon for about an hour and they are not going to be in a room on Tuesday at two in the morning, and that the page says so and the page is not going to be improved; and that the reason the man of about thirty-two did not take the two hours out of the night is his own, and is that a page of hours which leaves a hole in it is a page that a man of about thirty-four cannot read and cannot be shown, and that on Saturday he said out loud that he was not going to ask a man twice, and that writing the hour of a man who has said he will not be there is not asking him, and that the hour being written is what makes the page honest and the hole being left in it is what makes it unusable, and that he does not have a third thing to do with it and is not going to look for one; and that the reason the man of about fifty-three said one word and nobody asked him what he meant is his own, and is that the man said one true thing out loud in a room in this harbor four hundred yards from that bench stone and it is on a page and was not answered, and that every time that man has opened his mouth since, about nine people have gone quiet, and that a quiet room is not rudeness and is the correct answer to a man who has said one true thing and cannot say a second; and that the reason an hour in a room is not priced is the person whose yard it is's and not his, and is that she prices hours and material and that a sheet with money on it can be checked in a minute by a stranger with a leaf and a pencil, and that an hour in a room cannot be checked by a stranger at all because a stranger was not in the room, and that the moment an hour of thinking goes onto that sheet the sheet becomes a thing that cannot be taken off in a minute, and that this yard is not going to be the place where that happens.*
