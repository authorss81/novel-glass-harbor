# Chapter 573

*Sunday the Thirteenth Day of February, a Wipe, the Ninety-Third Time, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Again at the Angle It Has Been at Since the Twelfth and Not Going Up Whole, the Light at the Head of That Mole Out as It Is on Every Clear Night and With No Figure for It on Any Board in That Harbor, a Boat That Went Out of That Harbor on the Fourth of This Month With Four Lines of Paper in Her Basket Coming Back In at About Two in the Afternoon on the Thirteenth and Nothing in That Basket at All, the Paper Going Back into the Same Basket for the Saturday Run, Nobody Saying Out Loud in That Harbor That the Returns Were Never Asked For, a Berth-Fund Day of One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In and the Same Out, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the thirteenth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and that door standing open on a slip with nobody on it at that hour.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was the ninety-third wipe, and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that cistern was not charged on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten in the morning and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and did not go up whole. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the boat that came in at about two in the afternoon was not paid for by anybody and nobody asked her for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and eleven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fiftieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirteen days. Four lines of correction and one line of a man's own refusal are in chalk on the flat end of a joiner's yard at the top of Fish Street and the chalk goes in about a month and has gone on going in every rain since the twenty-sixth of January. The second of the two pages of hours on that bench stone carries the third rule copied out from the first and nine words under the copy in a different ink with no answer under them. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-one days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The wall came down bare at about half past ten and it took two men about four minutes and neither of them said anything while they did it, and it went up again in the same four minutes at the angle it has been at since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole, and the boards at the join of it are still showing weather on the face of them.

A woman of about thirty-one came past it at about a quarter to eleven with a barrow of ice going down to the end stalls and she stopped, and stood about a stride off it, and looked at the bare boards and the angle, and she did not put the barrow under it and she did not put her hand on it, and she went on down the street with the ice.

The man who keeps the leaf came past at about eleven, looked at it, and did not stop, and the light at the head of that mole was out as it is on every clear night and has been since the autumn, and there is no figure for it on any board in that harbor and there never has been.

---

The boat came in at about two in the afternoon, and it is a boat of about nineteen tons out of the second lay and she was alongside that quay at two o'clock and did not take a line from anybody but her own, and the master of her is a man of about fifty-two and he came up the front street with the basket under his arm.

The basket was empty. It was empty before she came in, and he looked at it on the quay before he came up, and it was empty when it went out on the fourth, and there was a piece of tarpaulin in the bottom of it and nothing else.

He put the basket down at the foot of the board at the head of that mole with the four lines of paper in it going back into it, and he said out loud, in about a hundred and ninety-six words, what had happened to it at the other end, and nobody stopped him and nobody asked him a second question.

"That basket went into my forepeak on the Wednesday morning of the fourth and it came out of it on the quay at that port on the Friday, and the man at the harbour end of that line read your four lines standing on the steps, and he had about nine people round him while he did it.

He said three things and I am not going to improve any of them. He said there is no such thing in that office and there never has been one. He said they looked in three rooms and a press and a locked press. And he said that the returns of that port and the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line are not in the building and that if they are anywhere they are in a different building and that he has never been in it.

I am not telling you he did not look. I am telling you he looked and could not find them and that is a different sentence and I have watched about nine people turn it into the other one in the ten minutes since I came up this street."

---

Nobody in that harbor said out loud on that Sunday that the returns had never been asked for, and the master of that boat was not asked whether anybody at that port had refused to answer, and he was not asked whether the man at the harbour end had a reason, and nobody thanked him for a basket he carried a day and a night for and for four days of a cart road that he did not walk.

A man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons asked him one question, and it was one question, and the answer to it was that there is a signature on a slip at that port for the basket going ashore and not for the paper inside it, and the man of about fifty-three said nothing back about that and has not said anything about it since.

The basket went into the forepeak again at about four in the afternoon and the paper goes back down that coast on the Saturday in it, and the basket is empty again now, and a boat takes a day and a night and a cart takes four days each way.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday, with a wall bare behind it since half past ten and a basket behind him since two o'clock, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and fifty-three words on what an empty basket is, and nobody in that slip had anything to put in it.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and a wall came down bare this morning for the ninety-third time and a woman with a barrow of ice stopped at it and went on, and neither of those two things was put anywhere.

**A boat came in at two this afternoon with a basket that was empty before she came in, and the man who brought it up said in about a hundred and ninety-six words that a man at the harbour end of that line read four lines off our steps and looked in three rooms and a press and a locked press and could not find them. Nine days, a day and a night down and a day and a night up, and nobody was paid for any part of it and no line went onto that sheet and nobody in this harbor has said the word never about those returns, and a man said out loud on the steps this afternoon that he was not going to let nine people turn a sentence into a different sentence.

An empty basket is the cheapest thing that has come into this harbor this month and it is the only one of the four that came back with nothing on it, and I am not going to write anything under it, because the man who carried it did not ask for a line and I have got nothing to put in one."

---

**THAT WALL CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE NINETY-THIRD TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT UP AGAIN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE CAME PAST IT WITH A BARROW OF ICE AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN AND STOPPED AND STOOD ABOUT A STRIDE OFF IT AND DID NOT PUT ANYTHING UNDER IT AND WENT ON. A BOAT OF ABOUT NINETEEN TONS CAME INTO THAT HARBOR AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON WITH A BASKET THAT WAS EMPTY BEFORE SHE CAME IN, AND THE MASTER OF HER SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINETY-SIX WORDS THAT THE FOUR LINES WERE READ AT THE HARBOUR END OF THAT LINE AND THAT THREE ROOMS AND A PRESS AND A LOCKED PRESS WERE LOOKED IN AND THAT THE RETURNS ARE NOT IN THAT BUILDING. NOBODY SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT HARBOR THAT THE RETURNS WERE NEVER ASKED FOR, AND THE MASTER WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION AND IT WAS ABOUT A SLIP WITH A SIGNATURE ON IT AND NOTHING ELSE. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE BASKET WENT INTO THE FOREPEAK AGAIN AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND THE PAPER GOES BACK DOWN THAT COAST IN IT ON THE SATURDAY. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirteenth day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the ninety-third wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that that cistern was not charged on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing; and that the wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten in the morning and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and did not go up whole, and that it took two men about four minutes to come down and four minutes to go up and neither of them said anything while they did it, and that a woman of about thirty-one came past it at about a quarter to eleven with a barrow of ice going down to the end stalls and stopped about a stride off it and looked at the bare boards and the angle and put the barrow under neither the wall nor anything else and went on, and that the man who keeps the leaf came past at about eleven and looked at it and did not stop, and that the light at the head of that mole was out as it is on every clear night and has been since the autumn and that there is no figure for it on any board in that harbor; and that a boat of about nineteen tons out of the second lay was alongside that quay at about two in the afternoon and took a line from nobody but her own, and that the master of her is a man of about fifty-two and that he came up the front street with the basket under his arm, and that the basket was empty before she came in and he looked at it on the quay before he came up and it was empty when it went out on the fourth and there was a piece of tarpaulin in the bottom of it and nothing else; and that he said out loud in about a hundred and ninety-six words that the basket went into the forepeak on the Wednesday morning of the fourth and came out on the quay at that port on the Friday, and that the man at the harbour end of that line read the four lines standing on the steps in front of about nine people, and that he said there is no such thing in that office and there never has been one, and that they looked in three rooms and a press and a locked press, and that the returns of that port and the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line are not in that building and that if they are anywhere they are in a different building that he has never been in, and that he was not telling anybody the man did not look and was telling them he looked and could not find them, and that is a different sentence; and that nobody in that harbor said out loud on that day that the returns had never been asked for, and that the master was not asked whether anybody had refused to answer and was not asked whether the man at the harbour end had a reason, and that nobody thanked him for a basket he carried a day and a night for and a cart road he did not walk; and that a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons asked him one question, and that the answer was that there is a signature on a slip at that port for the basket going ashore and not for the paper inside it, and that the man of about fifty-three said nothing back about that and has said nothing about it since; and that the basket went into the forepeak again at about four in the afternoon and the paper goes back down that coast in it on the Saturday, a boat taking a day and a night and a cart taking four days each way; and that the twenty-three days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and six and one thousand four hundred and four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those twenty-three days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day, an empty basket not being a thing that yard prices.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten the reason he wrote the two minutes of that wall down as four minutes down and four minutes up and not as a wipe is his own, and is that a wipe is a day and this is a thing that happens on every Sunday and the two of them are not the same size and he is not going to let a Sunday in this leaf look bigger than it is; and that at about a quarter to eleven the reason the woman with the barrow of ice is on that page at all is his own, and is that she was on the slip on the third of this month with a particular she has said out loud she is holding and is not giving, and that a woman who stops in front of a bare wall and puts nothing under it is doing the same thing she did in that room, and that he is not going to say so out loud in a slip and he is not going to leave it off the page either; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason he wrote down what the master said rather than what anybody else said about what the master said is his own, and is that a man who carries a basket down a coast for nine days and comes back with it empty has been made into a sentence by about nine people already this month and every one of those nine sentences is a sentence about a thing that did not happen, and that the only version of it that is checkable is the one the man said himself with an hour against it; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the basket is going back down on the Saturday and he wrote that down and not a hope is his own, and is that a demand goes into a basket on the fourth and comes out on the thirteenth and goes back in on the thirteenth, and that a thing which has now been asked three times is not a thing nobody was asked for, and that he is not going to be the man who says the third one out loud because the man of about fifty-three said his one question and stopped and a man in this harbor who keeps adding sentences to a refusal does not get to keep the refusal; and that at about ten past six the reason nothing went under the empty basket in either column is his own, and is that the first column takes the day and the day is a basket that came back, and that the second column is not a place for a man's disappointment, and that he has been this close three times this year to writing a line that said a thing was not found, and that the line would have been worth more to whoever read it after him than anything else in the yard, and that it would also have been the end of the sheet as an instrument, because a sheet with a line that says a thing was not found is a sheet that somebody else decides what goes on it next.*
