# Chapter 569

*Wednesday the Ninth Day of February, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, a Room over That Market Full at About One o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Nine People in It and the Six Lines of the Account on the Wall of That Room in a Large Hand, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying Out Loud in About Two Hundred and Fifty Words, in Front of Those Six Lines, That He Has Been Wrong Since the Twenty-Fourth of January, by the Admission That If One Man in That Port Had Held the Whole of That Record in One Book Then Two Boats Would Not Be on the Ground off That Water With Their Gear in the Bottom and Nobody Standing on Either of Them, Nobody Answering Him, a Man of About Fifty-Three Sitting in That Room for the Whole of It and Saying Nothing, a Woman of About Thirty-One Sitting in That Room for the Whole of It and Saying Nothing, Nobody Thanked, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the ninth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a room over the floor of that fish market with about nine people in it and six lines on the wall of it.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and nine days. The six lines on the wall of that room are the six lines of the account that came ashore on the twenty-fourth of January and they are word for word with the sheet on the board at the head of that mole, and the sheet itself has not been taken off that board and was not in that room and did not come into it, and the man of about thirty-three said out loud at the start of what he said that he was saying it in front of a copy and that he would have said it in front of the paper and could not, because a paper with a seal on it does not come into a room. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-seven days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

And at about one o'clock in the afternoon that room was let out and about nine people went up those stairs, and the six lines were on the wall of it in a large hand with a ring of black chalk at the foot of them and no name under the ring, and the sheet that carries the money is on no wall in that room and is not in that room.

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood under the wall and started by saying what he wanted, and he wanted four more hands on his books before the spring tides, and he said that he could not get them while that harbor was arguing about a piece of paper, and that he was not blaming anybody for that, and that he put the first of the two things out first on purpose, because he was about to say the second one and he wanted his own want in the room before it.

---

And then he said the other thing, out loud, in two hundred and fifty words, in front of those six lines, to about nine people, and it was not a speech, and nobody called it one, and he did not raise his voice, and he did not say it twice.

"I have been wrong since the twenty-fourth of January. I said on that slip and I have said it on a step and in a market and to anybody who has stood still long enough to hear it, and I am not taking any of it back this afternoon and I am not going to be quiet about it either.

If one man in that port had held the whole of it — every crossing, every hour, every boat, one book, one hand, one man who could not be got at and could not be tired and could not be bought a drink — then on the night of the fourteenth, when the man at the harbour end of that line found that nine had gone in and not all of them had come out, there would have been one page in front of him with a figure on it, instead of eleven men who each had their own idea and would not put any of it on paper for anybody.

**Two boats would not be on the ground off that water with their gear in the bottom and nobody standing on either of them.** That is the whole of what I am saying. It is not a small thing and it is not a clever thing and I have had it in my mouth since the twenty-fourth and I could not get it out.

I am not saying that page is right. I am saying it would have worked."

---

Nobody answered him. A man of about thirty-five of the first lay took his hand off the back of a chair and put it in his pocket and left it there. Nobody made a speech. Nobody said that he was right and nobody said that he was wrong and nobody said that it was a hard thing to say, and about nine people stood in that room and about two of them looked at the floor and the rest of them looked at the man of about thirty-three and at nothing else.

The man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons was in that room for the whole of it and said nothing at all, and he has said nothing in that room since, and nobody in that harbor has said to him today or on any day this month that he was wrong about anything.

The woman of about thirty-one was in that room for the whole of it and said nothing at all, and the particular she said out loud on that slip on Thursday is the particular that would have gone into that one book, and nobody in that room asked her for it and she did not offer it.

The man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that room and said nothing at all, and about four of the nine people in that room cannot read what is on that wall, and one of the four is a man who cannot read.

Nobody thanked the man of about thirty-three. He said that he had not come for that and then he said that he would have said it if he had, which was a different thing and was the last thing he said in that room.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday, with a door coming down at about two in the afternoon and not one answer in it, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and spent about a hundred and eighty-two words on what was paid in that room and where it went, and nobody in that slip had any part in it.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet and nothing came off it and there is no line on it for what a man said in a room at one o'clock this afternoon. **A man stood in front of six true lines and said out loud that if there had been one of him instead of eleven of them then two boats would not be on the bottom off that water, and about nine people heard it and not one of them answered and not one of them was thanked and that is the whole of the transaction.** I am putting it in the second column of this leaf and not in the first, because the first column takes one entry a day and the entry for this day is the day, and a day is a door that came down at about two in the afternoon with a knock on it and I did not hear the knock and the door is the door."

Nothing in that harbor was asked of anybody on that Wednesday, no standing item was touched, and not one mouth in that shed or on that quay found the word for a gift, and the six lines stayed on that wall with a ring of black chalk at the foot of them and no name under it.

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS SAID OUT LOUD, IN ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY WORDS, IN FRONT OF THE SIX LINES ON THE WALL OF THAT ROOM, THAT HE HAS BEEN WRONG SINCE THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF JANUARY, AND THAT IF ONE MAN IN THAT PORT HAD HELD THE WHOLE OF THAT RECORD IN ONE BOOK THEN TWO BOATS WOULD NOT BE ON THE GROUND OFF THAT WATER WITH THEIR GEAR IN THE BOTTOM AND NOBODY STANDING ON EITHER OF THEM, AND THAT HE WAS NOT SAYING THAT THE PAGE IS RIGHT AND WAS SAYING THAT IT WOULD HAVE WORKED. NOBODY ANSWERED HIM. NOBODY MADE A SPEECH. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WAS IN THAT ROOM FOR THE WHOLE OF IT AND SAID NOTHING, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WAS IN THAT ROOM FOR THE WHOLE OF IT AND SAID NOTHING, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT READ WAS IN THAT ROOM AND SAID NOTHING. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SEALED SHEET DID NOT COME INTO THAT ROOM. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the ninth day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that the room over the floor of that market was let out at about one o'clock in the afternoon and about nine people went up those stairs, and that the six lines on the wall of that room are the six lines of the account that came ashore on the twenty-fourth of January and are word for word with the sheet on the board at the head of that mole, and that the sheet itself was not taken off that board and was not in that room, and that the man of about thirty-three said out loud at the start of what he said that he was saying it in front of a copy and would have said it in front of the paper and could not, because a paper with a seal on it does not come into a room; and that the man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood under that wall and said that he wanted four more hands on his books before the spring tides and that he could not get them while that harbor was arguing about a piece of paper, and that he said that first because he was about to say the second thing; and that he then said, out loud, in two hundred and fifty words, that he has been wrong since the twenty-fourth of January, being that if one man in that port had held the whole of it, every crossing, every hour, every boat, one book, one hand, one man who could not be got at and could not be tired and could not be bought a drink, then on the night of the fourteenth, when the man at the harbour end of that line found that nine had gone in and not all of them had come out, there would have been one page in front of him with a figure on it instead of eleven men who each had their own idea and would not put any of it on paper for anybody, being that two boats would not be on the ground off that water with their gear in the bottom and nobody standing on either of them, and being that he was not saying that the page is right and was saying that it would have worked; and that nobody answered him and nobody made a speech and nobody said that he was right and nobody said that he was wrong, and that about nine people were in that room and about two of them looked at the floor; and that the man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons was in that room for the whole of it and said nothing at all and that nobody in that harbor has said to him that he was wrong about anything, and that the woman of about thirty-one was in that room for the whole of it and said nothing at all and that the particular she told that slip on the third of this month is the particular that would have gone into that one book and that nobody in that room asked her for it and she did not offer it, and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in that room and said nothing at all, and that about four of the nine people in that room cannot read what is on that wall and one of the four is a man who cannot read; and that nobody thanked the man of about thirty-three and that he said that he had not come for that and then said that he would have said it if he had; and that the nineteen days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and two and one thousand three hundred and eighty-four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those nineteen days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day, and that the admission of that afternoon went into the second column of that leaf and not into the first, the first column taking one entry a day and the entry for that day being the day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the admission went in here and not in the first column is his own, and is that the first column takes the day and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that column by counting days, and that a thing which is counted by days cannot carry a thing a man said in a room at one o'clock, and that if he had put it in the first column he would have made it look like a thing that happened to that harbor rather than a thing that happened to one man, and that the difference between those two is the whole of what this month is about; and that the reason nobody answered is theirs and not his, and is that about nine people had a man of about thirty-three telling them that he had been wrong for eight days and not one of them had anything to offer in place of it, and that the correct answer to that in a room is the quiet, and that the quiet was the whole of the afternoon; and that the reason he will not put the man of about fifty-three's silence on any page is his own, and is that a man's silence in a room is not a thing anybody is entitled to write down and to carry up a coast, and that the man of about thirty-three who hires hands has just shown him what happens to a sentence that leaves that slip, and that a sentence about a man who said nothing at one o'clock on the ninth of February is going to be in somebody's mouth by the middle of March and it is going to be in it as an answer to something he never said; and that the reason he said out loud at half past five that a day is a door with a knock on it is his own, and is that he has been standing in that yard for eighteen days putting a thing on a leaf every night at about six and the leaf is the only instrument in that harbor that has never once asked anybody anything, and that on the ninth of February a man stood in a room and gave that harbor the first thing it has been given this month and it was a thing with a person's whole weight in it, and that a day counter cannot hold it, and that this is the first time in his life he has wanted to write something down that he cannot write, and that he is not going to start a second book.*
