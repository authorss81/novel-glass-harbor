# Chapter 584

*Thursday the Twenty-Fourth Day of February, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Twenty-Eighth Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, the Two Names Put at the Head of the Sheet That Was Read Out on Monday With No Name on It, a Man of About Thirty-Two Over the Rail at About Ten Past Two in the Afternoon in About Four Fathoms and on the Floor at About a Quarter Past Two and His Helmet Off at About Thirty-Three Minutes Past Two and In at About Thirty-Five Minutes Past Two, Being Five Going Down and Eighteen on the Floor and Two Coming Up and Twenty-Five of Twenty-Five, a Man of About Forty-One of the First Lay Named as the Tender on the Surface With His Own Hand on the Chain Weight and a Cutoff He Could Pull Without Asking, Nobody Going in Twice and Nobody Going in at the Top of That Flood Which Was at About Eleven in the Morning, One Branch Stopped and Not One Answer Lessened, Nobody Killed and Nobody Hurt, a Man of About Thirty-Two Saying in About One Hundred and Fourteen Words on the Quay at About Half Past Three That the Answer Was Not Coming out of That Bell but Through It, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying in About One Hundred and Nine Words That He Is Not Going to Be the Man Who Turns Four Fathoms Into a Lesson, a Woman of About Forty-Four Saying in About One Hundred and Twenty-Six Words That It Was a Tuesday and That Nobody in That Harbor Is Going to Be Given a Word for It, Nothing Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Three Hundred and Forty-Five Words Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the twenty-fourth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in about four fathoms off the head of that mole, and in a shed with a roof on it and a bench stone in it at half past five in the afternoon.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-eighth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern was not used for anything at any hour of that day. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the sheet that carries the money does not carry a line for what a man spent in about four fathoms this afternoon, and there is no line on it for the two days either and there will not be one until that yard settles it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and eighty-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and eighty-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixty-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and seventy-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and twenty-four days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the offer of four lines is behind a door in the room over that market and is not on that board. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-two days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That cistern was charged for the hundred and twenty-eighth time at about eight in the morning and the covers were back on it by about a quarter past nine, and the hose at the top of it is dead and was not looked at and the butt behind that market stood full to the top of the second hoop until about four o'clock, and no bucket came out of it, and the two men who carry it up that ladder are the same two men and neither of them has ever been on that rota board.

---

At about one o'clock in the afternoon the two names went at the head of the sheet. It is the same sheet that was read out in that shed on Monday with no name at the head of it and no signature at the foot of it, and there are still six parts on it and an hour against every one of the six, and the fifth part says about half past three and the sixth part says that nobody signs it, and it is not signed, and it is not on that sheet and it is not in any minute.

The man of about thirty-two is the man who goes over the rail. The man of about forty-one of the first lay is the tender on the surface with his own hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he can pull without asking anybody's leave. There was an independent line on the bottom and a bottle of air on it, and the air on that bottle is twenty-five minutes and the figure of it is two hundred and eighty pence a man, and it was counted out at about one o'clock in that shed by the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay and the neck of it was read to him twice.

The two slates with a reason on each in the words of the man carrying it and an hour at the foot of each were on that bench stone from about ten o'clock in the morning and they went into the boat with them at about twenty to two.

The top of that flood was at about eleven in the morning, and nobody went in at the top of it, and the two of them sat in that shed for an hour and did nothing, and the top of that flood was two hours and ten minutes past the top of the flood in January.

---

The boat went off that slip at about twenty to two in the afternoon with the oars shipped and nothing rung from it, and it stood off the head of that mole at about five past two in about four fathoms, and it held itself over the place on the oars.

At about ten past two one man went over the rail. He was on the floor at about a quarter past two, and his helmet was off at about thirty-three minutes past two, and he was in the boat at about thirty-five minutes past two. That is five going down, eighteen on the floor and two coming up, and five and eighteen and two is twenty-five, and it is twenty-five of twenty-five. One person was in that water and the same man was not in it twice. Nobody went into it at the top of a flood.

At about two minutes to the half he said that he wanted up. The man of about forty-one said up, and neither of them said anything else about it, and the cutoff was never touched.

The branch is a branch of that bell and it hangs on a chain on a stone, and the chain is older than anybody in that boat, and under about four fathoms of green water in the slack of a flood the stone is a pale shape about the size of a door. He put the flat wedge in against the hanger where the bell's arm sits on the shank, and drove it with the drift in his other hand, and then got the pin out of the clapper arm with two fingers and it came up into his hand, and the arm came over and sat against the bell on the wedge, and the branch stopped.

And the rest of it did not stop.

That is the whole of what happened. He had it in his hands for eighteen minutes and the branch bell under that stone had been answering for three weeks at an interval of ninety-four seconds, and when the pin came out the answering did not go down, it came through. He said afterwards that it was like a hand on a board with water behind the board, and that the bell was a hole in the side of a thing rather than a thing with a sound in it. It is a thing made of seized vessels and a paper, and it does not know that it has been broken, and it did not answer him, and nothing in that water answered him.

---

He came alongside that quay at about half past three in the afternoon and sat on the thwart for a while with his hands on his knees, and the aftertaste came up out of it about ten minutes after that and stayed for the rest of the afternoon, and there is a pressure behind his left ear that is not a pain, and his hands were not steady enough at about four o'clock to hold a wedge, and none of those three things has been looked at by anybody.

He said one thing on that quay at about half past three, in about one hundred and fourteen words, and nobody thanked him for it and no figure was set against the twenty-five minutes of air.

"**That bell was a hole in the side of something and I took the pin out of the hole and the something went on doing whatever it was doing to a great many other things.** I felt it come through the hanger. I have been putting wedges into things in this harbor since I was nineteen and every one of them was a thing that stopped, and this afternoon the thing did not stop and I am not able to tell the nine people standing on this quay a single useful thing about why. What I can tell them is what it felt like, and what it felt like is water behind a board."

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud, in about one hundred and nine words, that he was not going to be the man who turned four fathoms into a lesson, and he said it before anybody else in that slip had found anything to say.

"**I have watched this harbor make a lesson out of a dozen things this year and I have watched the lesson get used on the next thing every time, and it has never once been the lesson.** A man went down and a branch stopped and the answering did not stop, and that is not a lesson, it is a Wednesday. The lesson people will make out of it is that all the others are further away and harder, and that is a thing a man says to get a man to stop, and I am not going to be that man today, in this slip, on this quay."

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay said out loud, in about one hundred and twenty-six words, that it was a Tuesday, and that nobody in that harbor was going to be given a word for it, and that the one of the three of them who had gone down was not going to be asked how it felt again this month.

"**It was a Tuesday. He went down at ten past two and he was up at thirty-five minutes past two and everybody in this harbor is still here and the thing on the water is still on the water, and that is what a Tuesday is.** And nobody in this market is going to give him a word for it this afternoon, because the moment this harbor gives a man a word for what he did at two o'clock it starts asking him for the same word at two o'clock on Thursday, and there is a thing in about four fathoms that has been answering for three weeks and I would like to see what this harbor does when the second one does not come up."

Nobody argued with her, and nobody told the man of about thirty-two that he had done well, and about nine people stood on that quay until about five o'clock and then went home, and the wedge and the pin came up out of that water in the same pocket and went on that bench stone and are not on that sheet.

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about three hundred and forty-five words on a pin out of a clapper arm, and nobody in that slip was asked what a man should do on Saturday.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and nothing went onto that sheet today and nothing came off it, and there is no line on it for a bottle of air or for a wedge or for a day of a man in a boat, and there will be a line on it on Saturday or there will not be one at all, and if there is one it will be that yard's own figure and not mine and not anybody's guess.

**One pin came out of a clapper arm this afternoon at about four fathoms and the answering did not stop, and I have had a leaf in front of me for twenty days with a figure on it that I could not get at, and today a man went and got at the thing itself and came back up with the same figure on the slate that has been on it for three weeks, being nine, and being eighteen lines off the crossings, and the pin is on that bench stone in my hand now and it does not alter the slate by a single stroke.** That is the worst kind of afternoon there is. It is not a bad day. It is a day where a man has spent a bottle of air and a day of a boat and got nothing, and the only thing it settles is that the next man who goes is going with a different idea in his head than the one this man went with.

The top of that flood was at eleven and the man went in at ten past two and nobody went in at the top of it. Twenty-five minutes of air and twenty-five minutes of air and not one minute more. The third column took nothing today and the fourth took nothing and stands at one hundred and twenty-five, and there is no date against it anywhere in this harbor, and I am not going to put one on it."

Nobody was thanked for the descent and nobody was thanked for the pin, and the pin went into a jar on the shelf in that shed with three other things in it that nobody has ever explained, and the wedge stayed on that bench stone.

ONE BRANCH STOPPED AND THE ANSWERING WENT ON THROUGH IT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN WORDS THAT THE ANSWER WAS NOT COMING OUT OF THAT BELL BUT THROUGH IT AND THAT IT FELT LIKE WATER BEHIND A BOARD. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND NINE WORDS THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO BE THE MAN WHO TURNED FOUR FATHOMS INTO A LESSON. A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAID IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIX WORDS THAT IT WAS A TUESDAY AND THAT NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS GOING TO BE GIVEN A WORD FOR IT. NOBODY DIED AND NOBODY WAS HURT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NOTHING WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**
