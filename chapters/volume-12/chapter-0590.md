# Chapter 590

the Leaf Having Said One Short Thing to the Page and Not to the Room in About Forty-Nine Words and Not One Word About a Man on Any Part of It, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, the Leaf at Three Hundred and Twenty-Three and One Thousand Four Hundred and Eighty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five on Forty Days Counted from the Twenty-Second Day of January and Neither of the First Two Figures Read Back Aloud, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, and a Relay Still Answering Out on the Water at Ninety-Four Seconds at About Ten Past Six With About Four People Hearing It and None of Them Saying Anything*

Wednesday the second day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and about nine people standing in it at three o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, being a Monday and a Thursday thing, and the covers were on it. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the four lines that were read out on that bench stone at about three o'clock in the afternoon are on no sheet in this harbor. About ten in the morning one hundred and sixty-eight pence came in to that yard and about four in the afternoon one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and twenty-eight pence was in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and that bag is short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes the whole of what the attempt cost and is still standing.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and eighty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and ninety; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twenty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and ninety-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twelve days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and sixty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and eighty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and thirty days. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it came up that coast this morning at about nine o'clock with the paper still in it and the basket empty for the second time, and about four people on that quay saw it come up and not one of them said what was in it or what was not in it. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been eighty-eight days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

That basket came up that coast this morning at about nine o'clock with the paper still in it and the basket empty for the second time, and nobody in that harbor has looked inside it, and the basket is standing in the boathouse of the man of about fifty-two who carried it down there on the twenty-sixth in the same basket it had come back in on the thirteenth, and that man has not been asked about it by anybody and has not been thanked for it by anybody and has said nothing about it since Friday.

---

And at about three o'clock in the afternoon a page of four lines went onto that bench stone, and about nine people were in that shed, and the door stood open, and the man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay read the four of them out loud twice and got nothing wrong on either reading, and about four of those nine people could not read them and were told what was on them by him.

1. *The interval from one answer to the next answer is ninety-four seconds. It has been counted four times in four places by four people with four different things, and the sum of the three that were counted together is two hundred and eighty-two seconds, being four minutes and forty-two seconds, and the fourth was not added in, and that is on the face of this line and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can see it.*
2. *A branch of that bell was stopped on Thursday the twenty-fourth of this month at about four fathoms with one man in the water and a named tender on the surface. The count of vessels answering the one that was stopped is in a column on the back of an old slate in this shed and is not on this page and is not in a room.*
3. *That column carries a count taken off the crossings of two nights of this month and not off an estimate, and it is thirty-four days above a figure on the same slate for the twenty-first and twenty-second of January, and neither figure is the answer to the other one, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take both off that slate in a minute and be wrong in front of the man who keeps this leaf.*
4. *The third payment on the sheet that carries the money is one thousand and thirteen pence, being five marks, seventeen shillings and ninepence, with the twenty-fourth of this month against it and the twenty-sixth against it. Both of those are on the face of that one line, and the six figures and the three sums under them are on the face of that one line, and the twenty-fourth is the day it stopped and not the day that yard did the work.*

The man who keeps the leaf said one thing about that page himself, and it was in about forty-nine words, and it was said to the page and not to the room, and nobody asked him a second question about it.

"**Those are four lines and not one of them is about a man. I have been putting days on this leaf since December and this is the first page I have ever put on that bench stone that a stranger can take four figures off and be angry with.**"

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands asked, once, out loud, whether a page of four lines was going to be argued with, and the man who keeps the leaf answered him out loud in about one hundred and eleven words.

"**Yes, and it is the only page in this harbor anybody can argue with, and that is because there is not one word on it about anybody. Four figures and two days and a count that is on a slate instead of on this page, and a man with a leaf and a pencil can take all five off and come back tomorrow and take all five off again and find that they are the same.** What I cannot do, and have not been able to do for twenty days, is put a man's name on that page. That is not a fault in the page. That is the page working."

Then a woman of about thirty-nine who mends net on the boards asked whether the fourth of those four lines was the reason a bay was in it somewhere, and a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons said that a thing which comes round again next month is a bay, and that he has one and pays for the upkeep of it, and that a barrel-maker said the same thing in that shed yesterday in fewer words and for money. Nobody asked him for a second thing and nobody said that he was wrong.

Nobody copied that page out, and nobody put a name on it, and it is not on that sheet and it is not in any minute, and it went in behind the press in that shed at about half past four with four lines on it and not one name.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about four hundred and fifty-four words on four lines and a slate, and nobody in that slip was asked what happens now.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved since Saturday afternoon at four o'clock, and nothing went onto that sheet today and nothing came off it, and the bag is twenty-eight pence, and that is the whole of what these ten days have cost this sheet, being one line at one thousand and thirteen pence and no line at all on any of the other nine.

**The third payment is standing on that sheet with its own figure and its own two days on the face of it, and the thing on the water went on answering at ninety-four seconds through the whole of it, and neither of those is a speech and I am not going to make one of them.** One thousand and thirteen pence is what a pin out of a clapper arm costs. Ninety-four seconds is what a branch that has been stopped still gets answered in, four times counted, and the count of what answered it is on that slate and not in a room and I am not going to say it here.

Nothing improved in ten days. The column on that step was read out on Tuesday and did not move and the seventy-first week stands behind the seventieth. The figure of four hundred and fifty pence has not come down that board since the first of December. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office is not paid and not advanced and not reduced. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare on Sunday for the ninety-fifth time and went up not whole. The third column took nothing in ten days and nobody was appointed to it, and the fourth took nothing in ten days and still stands at one hundred and twenty-five with no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, and I have not entered that reason in either column on any of these ten days.

Three hundred and twenty-three and one thousand four hundred and eighty-nine, none, and one hundred and twenty-five, are the figures on that leaf at the close of this day, and that is forty entries and fifty entries on forty days counted from Saturday the twenty-second day of January, and it is ten more entries on that first column and fifty more on that second than there were on the thirtieth of this month, and I have not read either of the first two of those figures back out loud to anybody this month and I am not going to tonight, and the reason the fourth column has no line in it is not on any page and is not going on one this month either."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for the four lines, and the door of that shed stood open on a slip with nobody on it, and the slate lay face up on that bench stone with five lines on it, and the wedge lay on the corner of it.

---

And at about ten past six on that Wednesday, when about four people were walking back up that quay, it answered. It comes down out of the west about a mile off and it goes through that headland the way it has gone through it for three weeks, and the next one came ninety-four seconds after the one before it, and the next one came ninety-four seconds after that one, and about four people heard all of it, and none of them said anything, and the light at the head of that mole was out and is out on every clear night and there is no figure for it on any board in that harbor.

**A PAGE OF FOUR LINES WENT ONTO THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT THREE O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND WAS READ OUT TWICE, AND THE FIRST OF THOSE FOUR LINES SAYS THAT THE INTERVAL FROM ONE ANSWER TO THE NEXT ANSWER IS NINETY-FOUR SECONDS AND HAS BEEN COUNTED FOUR TIMES IN FOUR PLACES BY FOUR PEOPLE WITH FOUR DIFFERENT THINGS AND THAT THE SUM OF THE THREE THAT WERE COUNTED TOGETHER IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-TWO SECONDS, BEING FOUR MINUTES AND FORTY-TWO SECONDS, AND THAT THE FOURTH WAS NOT ADDED IN, AND THE SECOND OF THOSE FOUR LINES SAYS THAT A BRANCH OF THAT BELL WAS STOPPED ON THURSDAY THE TWENTY-FOURTH AT ABOUT FOUR FATHOMS WITH ONE MAN IN THE WATER AND A NAMED TENDER ON THE SURFACE AND THAT THE COUNT OF VESSELS ANSWERING THE ONE THAT WAS STOPPED IS IN A COLUMN ON THE BACK OF AN OLD SLATE IN THAT SHED AND IS NOT ON THAT PAGE AND IS NOT IN A ROOM, AND THE THIRD OF THEM SAYS THAT THE COUNT IS OFF THE CROSSINGS OF TWO NIGHTS OF THIS MONTH AND NOT OFF AN ESTIMATE AND IS THIRTY-FOUR DAYS ABOVE A FIGURE ON THE SAME SLATE FOR THE TWENTY-FIRST AND TWENTY-SECOND OF JANUARY, AND THE FOURTH OF THEM SAYS THAT THE THIRD PAYMENT ON THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS ONE THOUSAND AND THIRTEEN PENCE, BEING FIVE MARKS, SEVENTEEN SHILLINGS AND NINEPENCE, WITH THE TWENTY-FOURTH AGAINST IT AND THE TWENTY-SIXTH AGAINST IT, BOTH BEING ON THE FACE OF THAT ONE LINE, THE TWENTY-FOURTH BEING THE DAY IT STOPPED AND NOT THE DAY THAT YARD DID THE WORK. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE ASKED WHETHER A PAGE OF FOUR LINES WAS GOING TO BE ARGUED WITH AND WAS ANSWERED IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN WORDS THAT IT IS THE ONLY PAGE IN THAT HARBOR ANYBODY CAN ARGUE WITH AND THAT IS BECAUSE THERE IS NOT ONE WORD ON IT ABOUT ANYBODY, AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID IN ABOUT FORTY-NINE WORDS THAT THOSE ARE FOUR LINES AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS ABOUT A MAN AND THAT IT IS THE FIRST PAGE HE HAS EVER PUT ON THAT BENCH STONE THAT A STRANGER CAN TAKE FOUR FIGURES OFF AND BE ANGRY WITH. NOTHING WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY ON ANY OF THE TEN DAYS AND THE SHEET STANDS AT NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE, THE LEAF STANDS AT THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE AND ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINE AND NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE ON FORTY DAYS COUNTED FROM THE TWENTY-SECOND DAY OF JANUARY, AND NEITHER OF THE FIRST TWO OF THOSE FIGURES HAS BEEN READ BACK ALOUD TO ANYBODY IN THIS HARBOR, AND THE THIRD COLUMN TOOK NOTHING IN TEN DAYS AND THE FOURTH TOOK NOTHING IN TEN DAYS. AND A RELAY WAS STILL ANSWERING OUT ON THAT WATER AT NINETY-FOUR SECONDS AT ABOUT TEN PAST SIX WITH ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE HEARING IT AND NONE OF THEM SAYING ANYTHING.**
