# Chapter 574

*Monday the Fourteenth Day of February, a Rota Day, the Hundred and Twenty-Fifth Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Man of About Twenty-Six of the Third Lay Coming Back Up That Coast on the Boat at About Ten in the Morning After a Day and a Night Down and a Day and a Night Up, a Shed at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Nine People In It and a Board Off the Back of a Press Laid on That Bench Stone With a Night Written on It in Three Parts and Three Names Copied Onto It Off the Second Page of Hours and the Third Rule Written on It in the Words It Is Printed In and Not Improved, That Board Settling Nothing and Pricing Nothing and Going to No Water, and Then a Man of About Forty-Four of the First Lay Who Keeps the Crossings for That Crew Refusing the Offer Out Loud, in the Open, in About Two Hundred and Sixty-Eight Words, Four of Five Asked and the Fifth Not Asked, Nothing Going Onto the Sheet That Carries the Money, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the fourteenth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and nine people standing in a room above the boards of that quay at half past two in the afternoon.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-fifth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern at about eight in the morning was not used for anything at any hour of that day. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and the offer of four lines was refused in the open at about half past two in the afternoon and is not on that sheet and is not priced. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and seventy-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and seventy-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and twelve days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventy-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and ninety-six days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fifty-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and sixty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighty-third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and fourteen days. The six lines on the wall of the room over that market are a copy and not the paper and they were put up on the second of this month by a man of about twenty-seven who was not asked to do it and there is a ring of black chalk at the foot of them and no name under the ring. The board at the head of that mole carries five papers and two of them have never been opened and the demand for the returns went back down that coast in a basket yesterday and goes again on Saturday. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been seventy-two days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

---

The hundred and twenty-fifth charge went into that cistern at about eight in the morning. It went in the way it has gone in every Monday and Thursday since the autumn, being by hand up that ladder, one butt on a shoulder, eleven times, and the eleven butts were carried up and not one of them was carried on by anybody standing on the covers to save the climbing.

The two men who do it were on that ladder from about a quarter to eight and the covers were back on it by about a quarter past nine, and the butt behind that market stood full to the top of the second hoop until about four o'clock, and nobody in that market drew a bucket off it, and the hose at the top of it has been dead since the spring and there is no figure anywhere in that harbor for a new length of it.

---

The boat that comes up that coast on the Monday was alongside that quay at about ten in the morning, and a man of about twenty-six of the third lay came up the front street off her with a paper in his coat that he did not give to anybody, and he had had a day and a night down and a day and a night up and he slept on the forepeak of a boat going both ways and nobody paid him for it and there is no line for a place on a boat.

He said one thing on the boards at about half past ten, and it was about thirty-seven words, and about nine people heard it.

"I asked four of the five. Four said no. The fifth has been out on the ground since Friday and has not been asked, and I am not going to stand in a market and say five."

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon that room over that shed was let out for about three quarters of an hour, and about nine people went into it, and the door stood open and about nine more stood on the boards outside it.

A board off the back of a press was laid on that bench stone. On it was written a night in three parts with an hour against each part, and against each part the hour at which that person could stop it, and beside the three parts were three names copied out of the second of the two pages of hours, being a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, a woman of about forty-four who keeps a shut store on the second lay, and a man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons.

The man of about thirty-two said out loud that he was not asking those three anything, that he had carried their names off that page and put them on a board and that copying a name onto a board is not asking a man a question, and that a name on a board and a man in a room are two different things and he was not going to stand in a room and pretend otherwise.

Then he read the third rule out loud in that room, in the words it is printed in, and did not improve one word of it, and about nine people outside the door heard it through the open door.

"Any one of three named people may stop this. A thing that can be stopped by any one of three people is also a thing that cannot be kept running by anybody if those three are not in the room. Neither half of that is fixed and both halves are printed here on the same day."

Nobody went in the water. No boat left that quay. Nothing on that board was agreed to and nothing on it was signed and no person on any water in this harbor is a member of anything. It settled nothing and it priced nothing and there is no line on that sheet for three quarters of an hour in a room, and the board went back against the wall of that shed at about a quarter to three and went to no water and is not on that bench stone tonight.

---

And at about half past two, with the board still standing against the wall behind him and about nine people in that room, a man of about forty-four of the first lay stood up and refused the offer out loud, in the open, in about two hundred and sixty-eight words, and he keeps the crossings for that crew and they are in his hand and in nobody else's, and he said what he wanted before he said the answer.

"I have been asked by four of the five and the fifth has not been asked, and I am going to say that number out loud because the whole of this morning's trouble is about numbers. Four. Not five. Not six.

No. That is the answer and I am not going to dress it up for a room.

A Crown instrument for that mark is a number and a seal, and a number and a seal is a better hand than mine, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to nine people that none of the six of us ever wanted better than our own. That is a lie about our own work and I will not tell it.

**A copy of those crossings in my hand, once, in that port, in a place anybody may go and look at it, is not a copy of a night's work. It is the only thing on this coast that cannot be argued with, because six men cannot all be wrong in the same way at the same hour, and that paper does not want six men. It wants one hand, and the hand it wants is mine, and I am the one who would be the one they found out in if it was wrong.**

So it is no. The mark is ours, the crossings stay in our hands, nobody is being asked for a buoy and nobody is being asked for a name, and I have said those last two out loud so that they are in my mouth and not only on their paper."

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that four of six is not a decision and that he is not going to call it one and that the paper is going back behind the door of the room over the market where it stood yesterday. Nobody answered the man of about forty-four. Nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and the four of the five who said no did not say it again for him.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about two hundred and twenty-seven words on what four lines of paper cost on the day they are refused, and nobody in that slip asked him whether the refusal was the right one.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and the refusal was made at about half past two this afternoon by a man whose crossings those are, in about two hundred and sixty-eight words, and there is no line on that sheet for it and there is not going to be one tonight.

**Four of the five that mark keeps said no and the fifth has not been asked, and a man said the number out loud in a market at about half past ten rather than let anybody fill it in for him, and a board went against the wall of that shed at about a quarter to three having settled nothing and priced nothing, and the third rule came off a page of hours onto that board in the words it is printed in and one word of it is not different.**

I have been putting things on that sheet since December and I have had nothing to put on it this month, and a man of forty-four has just refused a number and a seal in front of nine people and what he has refused with is four out of six and a hand of his own, and I could not price that if I tried, and I am not going to try."

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIFTH TIME AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A NIGHT WAS WRITTEN ON A BOARD IN THREE PARTS WITH AN HOUR AGAINST EACH AND THREE NAMES COPIED OFF THE SECOND PAGE OF HOURS, THE THIRD RULE WAS READ OUT IN THE WORDS IT IS PRINTED IN AND WAS NOT IMPROVED, NOTHING WENT IN THE WATER, NO BOAT LEFT THAT QUAY, THE BOARD SETTLED NOTHING AND PRICED NOTHING, AND NO PERSON ON THAT WATER IS A MEMBER OF ANYTHING. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR OF THE FIRST LAY, WHO KEEPS THE CROSSINGS FOR THAT CREW AND HAS THEM IN HIS HAND AND NOBODY ELSE'S, REFUSED THE OFFER OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN IN ABOUT A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FOUR WORDS, HAVING BEEN ASKED BY FOUR OF THE FIVE AND NOT BY THE FIFTH, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT A NUMBER AND A SEAL IS A BETTER HAND THAN HIS AND THAT A COPY OF THOSE CROSSINGS IN HIS HAND IS THE ONLY THING ON THAT COAST THAT CANNOT BE ARGUED WITH, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT NOBODY IS BEING ASKED FOR A BUOY AND NOBODY IS BEING ASKED FOR A NAME. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID OUT LOUD THAT FOUR OF SIX IS NOT A DECISION. NOBODY ANSWERED AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten the reason the man of about twenty-six said four and not five out loud in a market is his own, and is that the whole of the two days of that paper in that harbor has been a fight over a number, and that a number said by somebody in a slip gets taken up by the next man and added to, and that a man who says four has to say four twice before he has said it once; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason he carried three names off a page of hours onto a board and said out loud that he was not asking them is his own, and is that he put the man of about thirty-three on that board at about two o'clock on the seventh of this month in a room with three people in it and got a yes, and that the third of the three on that page at that time had one hour in the middle of the night with one name on it and the rule on the face of the page needs three, and that a thing that cannot be kept running when those three are not in the room cannot be kept running by writing their names on a board either, and that he has not improved the rule and is not going to; and that at about half past two the reason a man of forty-four said out loud that a number and a seal is a better hand than his own is his own, and is that the first half of that sentence is an argument for the paper and that the man of about thirty-three who hires hands said that thing out loud in a room on the ninth of this month in about two hundred and fifty words and was not answered and was not thanked, and that a man of about forty-four got to the same place in nine days by a different road and said it in eleven seconds, and that the man who keeps the leaf put both of those in this leaf and is not going to put them together; and that at about half past five the reason he said he could not price the refusal and was not going to try is his own, and is that the person whose yard it is prices hours and material and that a refusal is neither, and that the day a refusal is priced on that sheet is the day that sheet is a list of who has been made comfortable, and that this yard has eleven refusals on it already at the same size as the contributions and not one figure among them; and that at about ten past six the reason he wrote the figure of four out loud in the leaf at all, being that the man said it in a market first and this is only the second telling of it, is his own, and is that a number a man says in a market is his and goes on his page, and a number a man says in a slip is a figure and can be checked, and that the four is going on the first column and not into the second because a first column takes one entry a day and the entry for that day is the day, and that this day is a cistern and a board and a no, and that the no is in it because a man said it out loud in the open in front of nine people and did not get a speech made about it afterward.*
