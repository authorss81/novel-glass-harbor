# Chapter 566

*Sunday the Sixth Day of February, a Wipe, the Ninety-Second Time, the Wall behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare at About Half Past Ten in the Morning and Going Up Again at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Not Whole, Eleven People of That Harbor Going Past It Between Then and the Middle of the Day and Not One of Them Putting a Bucket Under It, the Two Men on That Cistern Raising the Covers at About Eleven and Looking Straight Down and Putting the Covers Back On, a Second Sealed Sheet With Six True Lines and No Figure on It Coming Ashore at About Two in the Afternoon in a Bagged Canvas Slip and Going Onto the Board at the Head of That Mole Beside Four Others, Its Fifth Line Saying in Its Own Words That It Does Not State What Was Sounded on That Coast on That Night, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Three Pence, Being Six Hundred and Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Elevenpence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the sixth day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and at the head of that mole where a clerk of that harbor authority nailed a second sheet to a board at about two in the afternoon in front of about twenty people.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, being the ninety-second wipe. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare at about half past ten in the morning and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and it did not go up whole, and eleven people of that harbor went past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it. That cistern was not charged on it, being a Monday and a Thursday, and the two men who do it raised the covers at about eleven, looked straight down into it, and put the covers back on, and one of the two said out loud that a cistern is a Monday and a Thursday thing and that on a Sunday the most a man can do is look at one. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand two hundred and sixty-three pence out, being six hundred and seventy marks, five shillings and threepence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and seventieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and forty-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and six days. The first sealed sheet is on that board and has been there thirteen days, and the demand for the returns is on that board and went into a boat on Friday afternoon, and the paper saying that the sea is under martial custody is on that board and has not been opened, and the paper with a seal on it and no figure on it from the third of December is on that board and has not been opened. There are five papers on that board at the close of this day. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-four days and nobody has set a figure against it and he has not been to the road with the pump on it.**

---

That wall came down bare at about half past ten and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and it did not go up whole, and the eleven people who came past it between half past ten and the middle of the day went past it, and one of them was a man carrying a basket and he shifted the basket to the other arm about a yard short of it and then put it back.

The two men who do that cistern were there at about eleven. They lifted the covers and looked straight down into it and put the covers back on, and neither of them put a pail in it, and one of the two said out loud, to the other and not to anybody else, that a cistern is a Monday and a Thursday thing and that on a Sunday the most a man can do is look at one.

---

And at about two o'clock in the afternoon a clerk of that harbor authority, who is the same man who read the first one on the twenty-fourth of January, brought a bagged canvas slip up the front street and nailed a second sheet to that board at the head of that mole, and there was no Crown hand on the slip at all and about twenty people stood on the stones under it.

The sheet has a seal at the foot of it and a name under the seal and no figure of money anywhere on it, and the clerk read all six lines out loud word for word and stopped at the seal and did not read the name under it, and nobody on those stones asked him to.

These are the six lines, and there are six of them.

1. *Nine boats of that port were inside that line of boats at ten o'clock at night on Friday the twenty-first, and nine were inside that line at six o'clock in the morning.*
2. *Two of those nine had been out of that line earlier on the same day and came back into it, one at about eleven o'clock at night and one at about two in the morning, and those two hours are taken from the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line and from nothing else.*
3. *No boat of that port was found on the ground off the outer water on the morning of Saturday the twenty-second, and no boat of that port was reported missing on that night by any person in that harbor.*
4. *No person is recorded as lost, and no person has been charged, and no person is named on this sheet.*
5. *This account is made from the harbour returns of the vessels engaged in that line and it stops at what those returns contain. It does not state what was sounded on that coast on that night, and it does not attribute anything to anybody.*
6. *Any person in that harbor who holds a particular that differs from any line of this account may set it out in his own words and in his own hand anywhere on a public wall in that harbor, and his name does not have to be on it.*

A woman of thirty of no office said one thing out loud about the fifth of those six lines, in about fifty-seven words, and she said it on the stones and not in a room, and about eleven people of those twenty could not read it and about nine of them heard her say it.

"**The fifth of those six lines says out loud on its own face that it does not say what was sounded on that coast on that night. That is the one thing two men in this harbor counted on two watches and wrote on the backs of their own hands, and it is not on that sheet.**"

Nobody answered her and nobody argued with her and the clerk rolled the empty bag up and put it under his arm and went back down the street.

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands stood at the foot of that board and said out loud that there are now two sheets on it, one for the night of the fourteenth and one for the night of the twenty-first, that they are thirteen days apart, that every line of both of them is a particular a man could go and look at, and that the only thing on that coast anybody in that harbor can check with a pencil and two watches is a gap of forty-seven seconds, and that it is on neither sheet, and that it is four days down that coast on a slate in a hand a man of about thirty-one put there.

A man of about fifty-three who owns a boat of about fourteen tons said out loud that the two sheets do not contradict each other and that he had read both of them through twice and that there was not a figure on either of them that a man could put beside a figure on the other and find them out. Nobody asked him to say anything else, and nobody in that harbor has said to him today that he is wrong about anything, and he was not thanked.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday, with eleven people gone past a bare wall and a clerk of that harbor authority gone back down the front street carrying an empty bag, the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and spoke for about a hundred and seventy words on what a second true paper costs, and nobody in that slip had an answer to it.

"Ninety-six thousand six hundred and twenty-three pence short, being six hundred and three marks, eleven shillings and elevenpence. Unmoved, and a second nail went into a board at the head of that mole this afternoon and it cost nothing and the clerk that read it is paid out of a harbor office and not out of this yard. **A wall came down bare this morning for the ninety-second time and eleven people went past it. A second paper arrived this afternoon about a night that nothing was known to have happened on, and it is right, and it is complete, and it has a seal on it, and it says on its own face that it does not say what was sounded. That is two correct accounts on one board in thirteen days and the only thing a man in this harbor can prove is a gap of forty-seven seconds, and I would not swap the gap for either sheet and I would not put the gap on a board.**"

Nobody was asked for anything else on that Sunday, and no figure was set against that second nail, and nobody on that quay found the words for a thing given, and the two sheets stayed on that board side by side in the weather.

---

**THAT WALL CAME DOWN BARE FOR THE NINETY-SECOND TIME AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND WENT UP AGAIN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND ELEVEN PEOPLE WENT PAST IT AND NOT ONE OF THEM PUT A BUCKET UNDER IT. A SECOND SHEET WITH SIX TRUE LINES AND A SEAL ON IT AND NO FIGURE ON IT CAME ASHORE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND WENT ONTO THE BOARD AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE, AND ITS FIFTH LINE SAYS IN ITS OWN WORDS THAT IT DOES NOT STATE WHAT WAS SOUNDED ON THAT COAST ON THAT NIGHT, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY SAID THAT OUT LOUD IN ABOUT FIFTY-SEVEN WORDS ON THE STONES UNDER THAT BOARD. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE SAID OUT LOUD THAT THE TWO SHEETS DO NOT CONTRADICT EACH OTHER AND WAS ASKED NOTHING FURTHER AND WAS NOT TOLD HE WAS WRONG. THE BOARD CARRIES FIVE PAPERS AND TWO OF THEM HAVE NEVER BEEN OPENED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND ELEVENPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a second sheet arriving is not a good day is his own, and is that the first of the two is thirteen days old and has not been taken down and has not been argued out of the harbor, and that a thing which arrives twice is a thing which is going to arrive a third time, and that on this coast the third one will be about a night that somebody in that harbor was on, and that the man who is on that board when it is nailed up will be about nine people and about eleven of the nine cannot read it; and that the reason he would not put the gap of forty-seven seconds on a board is his own, and is that a figure on the back of a hand is checkable by the man who wrote it and by nobody else, and that a figure on a wall would be checkable by anybody with a watch and a stop, and that if that figure went on that board in that harbor then three of the five marks on that coast would be asked about it by somebody in an office, and that the two men who counted it would be the first two people in that harbor anybody came for, and that he is not going to be the reason a coast where children are frightened of what they cannot verify gets handed one more thing to be frightened of; and that the reason the man of about fifty-three was asked nothing further is his own and is that the man has said one true thing out loud in a room in this harbor and there is not a second one coming, and that a man who says that two papers do not contradict each other has done the whole of what a careful man can do with two papers and has spent the best part of a year of work on it, and that asking him for a second thing is how a harbor turns a man into an office; and that the reason a day of the berth fund was paid on the ninety-second wipe is his own and is that the two men who carry a boat out of that yard at ten went out on the water on the water and came back at four whatever day it was, and that the only thing different about a Sunday in this harbor is that the wall comes down and nobody puts a bucket under it, and that is the ninety-second time and that is a figure and it is a count of Sundays and not a count of men.*
