# Chapter 600

*Saturday the Twelfth Day of March, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe and No Charge Placed on That Cistern on It, the Read-Back of That Leaf Gone Through in That Yard at About Four o'Clock in the Afternoon in About Eleven People of Whom About Five Cannot Read, in the Yard's Own Mouth and in the Open, All Four Columns Including the Empty One, With Both Definitions Read Out in the Same Words and the Same Order, and the Derivation of Each of the Four Figures Printed on the Face of the Reading From Two Hundred and Eighty-Three and One Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Nine, the Count at the Hour of That Reading Being Three Hundred and Thirty-Two and One Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty-Seven and at the Close of That Day Three Hundred and Thirty-Three and One Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty-Nine, Read a Second Time at About a Quarter to Five in the Same Words and the Same Order, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five in About four hundred and sixty-two Words Unmoved at Ninety-Seven Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Six Hundred and Ten Marks and Three Shillings, a Man of About Fifty-Eight of the Third Lay Who Pulls Kelp Asking at About Six o'Clock at the Boards at the Back of That Market in About ninety Words Whether a Coast Can Accept a Method That Requires Honest Uncertainty Rather Than a Perfect Leader and Being Answered by Nobody, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the twelfth day of March, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a yard with a bench stone in it, and about eleven people standing in that yard at four o'clock in the afternoon.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the covers were on it at eight in the morning and were on it at four in the afternoon, and there is a Monday and a Thursday in the next month and both of them will be charged and neither of them is a hundred and thirty-third time of anything. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nothing came off it in any hand, and no line has gone onto it in any hand since Saturday the twenty-sixth of last month, and that sheet is at one hundred and eight thousand two hundred and seventy-six pence out, being six hundred and seventy-six marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and is ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings, and a stranger can take those three figures off it and find that they agree, and that figure is on that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth this evening and was not answered by anybody. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in the afternoon, one mark and eightpence each way, and there were twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so that the bag stands six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, short of it. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of this month came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and that includes the whole of a descent and a bill and four days of a cistern and a page of hours and a wall and a form in a coat and fifty days of a man keeping a leaf.**

**The nine below are the nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall, and they are the last nine of fifty days, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and ninety-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for three hundred; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and thirty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and twenty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and seventy-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and ninety-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninety-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and forty days. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers and neither of the two sealed ones has been opened, and the basket with the second asking in it is in that boathouse with the paper in it and nobody has looked inside it. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been ninety-eight days and no figure has been set against it and he is not on the water and nobody has sent for him.**

That market was working and the boards at the back of it had about nine people on them and the stall was taking the first boat of the day off the beam, and the man of about fifty-six who lands fish had the slate face up on the lid with the interval on it and nothing else on it, and the wedge was on the corner of the bench stone in the yard below.

---

And at about four o'clock in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf read that leaf back in that yard, in the open, with the door standing open, in about eleven people of whom about five cannot read what is in his hand, and he read the definitions first, and they are the definitions.

1. *The first column takes one entry a day, whatever else that day prints, and the entry against the day itself goes in after that day's reading and not before it, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that column by counting days and cannot be stopped from doing it.*
2. *The second column takes five lines a day, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, and that column is the ledger's own and is not a count of anything that anybody can check from a mole.*

Then he said the derivation of each of the four figures out loud, and then he gave the four figures, and the derivation of each of them was on the face of the reading and not in a book.

"The first column. **Two hundred and eighty-three were on that leaf before the first of those fifty days, and one has gone in on each of the fifty days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day, and not one of those fifty days has not got an entry against it, and two hundred and eighty-three and fifty is three hundred and thirty-three at the close of it, and the entry against this day goes in after this reading and not before it, so the count at the hour of this reading is three hundred and thirty-two.**"

"The second column. **Five have gone in on each of those same fifty days, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the two hundred and fifty that have gone in, two hundred and forty-five went in on the forty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and two hundred and fifty less two is two hundred and forty-eight, and one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and forty-eight is one thousand five hundred and thirty-seven at the hour of that reading, and one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and fifty is one thousand five hundred and thirty-nine at the close of it.**"

"The third column. **None. Nobody has been appointed to it, nobody has offered for it, a boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of it, his surname is on no page of that leaf, and he is not on the water.**"

"The fourth column. **One hundred and twenty-five. It has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it, it is neither closed nor struck, and the reason it took no line is not on this page and is not going to be read out in this room.**"

He read the whole of it a second time at about a quarter to five, in the same words and in the same order, and about five of those eleven people heard both of the readings and could not read either of them, and nobody stopped him at a word, and a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay stood at the end of that slip through both of them and said nothing at all.

The four columns are at three hundred and thirty-three, one thousand five hundred and thirty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the close of that day, and at three hundred and thirty-two, one thousand five hundred and thirty-seven, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the hour of that reading.

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and used about 462 words on fifty days and on a yard where about five people could not read any of it, and nobody in that slip was asked what comes out of a leaf.

"Ninety-seven thousand six hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and ten marks and three shillings. Unmoved since Saturday afternoon at four o'clock on the twenty-sixth of last month, and nothing has gone onto that sheet in any hand in any line on any of the ten days of this run, and the third payment on it is the only thing that has moved in seventeen days, and that was a bill for a pin out of a clapper arm and it is on that sheet with two days on the face of it. The bag is twenty-eight pence, and it has been twenty-eight pence on every day of this month including the Sunday, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight.

**Fifty days are on that leaf now, and about five people in this yard could not read a word of what I said at four o'clock and had all of it said out loud to them in the open, and that is the whole of the use of saying it twice.** A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that first column by counting days and cannot be stopped from doing it, and that is the only reason I have ever kept it. I read the definitions first and I read the derivation of each figure with the figure, and the base of that derivation is the figure on the last day of January, being two hundred and eighty-three and one thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, and not the figure on the second of this month, and if anybody in this harbor ever derives it off the second of this month they will add their own ten days twice and their own figure will not come out of it, and that is the only warning I have to give about it.

The third column took nothing in fifty days and nobody was appointed to it and a boy's hand is still at the head of it and his surname is on no page. The fourth column took nothing in fifty days and stands at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither closed nor struck, and the reason it took no line is not on a page of mine. Nothing on the poverty list came down any board in fifty days. The column on that step did not move on the eighth of this month and the seventy-second week stands behind the seventy-first. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office is not paid and not advanced and not reduced. The wall behind that reeve's bench came down bare on Sunday for the ninety-sixth time and went up not whole. The bag is twenty-eight pence."

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for fifty days read out twice, and that yard stood empty by about six and the door stood open on a slip with nobody on it.

---

And at about six o'clock on that Saturday evening, at the boards at the back of that market, with about nine people on them and the stall shut and the boards being swept, a man of about fifty-eight of the third lay, who pulls kelp and is on nobody's crew, said one thing out loud, in about 90 words, and named no person, and nobody answered it.

"**Can a coast take on a way of doing things that needs somebody to say out loud what they do not know.** Because that is what those four lines are, and what that sheet at the end stall is, and what the two quays did on Thursday morning, and it is not a man and it is not going to become a man, and there is nobody in this harbor who can be the one that everybody checks against. **I would like to know whether a coast can take that.**"

Nobody answered it. A man of about thirty-three who hires hands opened his mouth and shut it again, and a woman of thirty of no office stood at the end of those boards and said nothing, and the man who keeps the leaf said afterwards, out loud, in about 37 words, and nobody thanked him for that either.

"**I am not going to answer it, and there is nothing in that yard or on those boards that could, and I have said that out loud once and I am not going to say it twice.**"

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay wrote it in the market book as asked, on the twelfth day of this month, with no name against it and no answer against it, and the market book is not a record of anybody's opinion and is not on any sheet in this harbor, and the paper with the five papers on it at the head of that mole carries a sixth thing on it in a market book four hundred yards away and not on the board.

THE THIRD COLUMN IS READ AS NONE AND NOBODY WAS APPOINTED TO IT, AND THE FOURTH IS READ AS STANDING AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE WITH NO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIXTH LINE IN IT AND IS NEITHER CLOSED NOR STRUCK. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TEN MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND A STRANGER CAN TAKE THOSE THREE FIGURES OFF IT AND FIND THAT THEY AGREE. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT OF THE THIRD LAY WHO PULLS KELP ASKED AT A WORKING PLACE IN ABOUT 90 WORDS WHETHER A COAST CAN TAKE ON A WAY OF DOING THINGS THAT NEEDS SOMEBODY TO SAY OUT LOUD WHAT THEY DO NOT KNOW RATHER THAN A PERFECT LEADER, AND IT WAS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND IT IS IN A MARKET BOOK AS ASKED WITH NO NAME AGAINST IT AND NO ANSWER AGAINST IT. NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**
