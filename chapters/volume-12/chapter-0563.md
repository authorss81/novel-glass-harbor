# Chapter 563

*Thursday the Third Day of February, a Rota Day, That Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Twenty-Second Time at About Eight in the Morning in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, the Name at the Head of That Rota Board Still Off It, a Woman of About Thirty-One Standing at the End of That Slip at About One o'Clock in the Afternoon and Saying Out Loud in Front of About Nine People What She Was Asked to Say in That Room on the Twenty-Fourth of January and What She Said Instead and Would Not Say, About Two Hundred and Four Words of It, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying Out Loud That She Is Only Saying It Because a Man of About Fifty-Three Said It Yesterday and Her Answer to That in About Twenty-Four Words, the Shortfall Said Out Loud at About Half Past Five Unmoved at Ninety-Six Thousand and Four Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence, Being Six Hundred and Two Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Thursday the third day of February, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and in a shed with a roof on it, a bench stone, and a door that stands open on a slip.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. That cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-second time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and the water that went into that cistern at about eight in the morning was not used for anything at any hour of that day. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand on that day and nothing went into it in any hand, and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out, being six hundred and sixty-nine marks, four shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that day came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, two hundred and sixty-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred and sixty-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and sixty-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighty-five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its hundred and fortieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its hundred and fifty-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventy-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and three days. The six lines of that account are on the board at the head of that mole and they are also on the wall of the room over that market in a large hand with a ring of black chalk at the foot of them and no name under it, and the two of those are two instruments and the wall one can be washed. There are nine hulls about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and the mole head is dark. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat can hear out of the left side of his head and it has been sixty-one days and nobody has set a figure against it and he is not on the water and has not been sent for.**

---

That cistern was filled at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it, and the two men who carried it up that ladder were at it from a quarter to eight and had finished by about a quarter past nine. Neither of those two men has ever been on that rota board, which has had a name at the head of it left off since the spring, and neither of them has ever asked to be put on it, and the butt behind that market is full to the top of the second hoop.

---

And at about one o'clock in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-one of the first lay, who crews four days in ten, stood at the end of that slip and said out loud in front of about nine people what she was asked to say in that room over the market on the twenty-fourth of January and what she said instead, and it was about two hundred and four words, and she put no name on anybody, and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her a question afterwards.

She said at the start of it that she was not there because anybody had asked her to be there, and that she was there because a man of about fifty-three had stood in a room yesterday afternoon for an hour and a half and had said out loud to about twenty people that he could not take six lines down and that the reason he stopped was a woman of about thirty-one who stood up in that room ten days ago, and that a thing like that ought to be said by the person it is about and not carried about by a man in somebody else's mouth.

"I was asked for a particular about that night. I was asked it out loud, in that room, by a man in that room, and I am not going to say his name and nobody is going to make me. I said that I had not been on the water and that I did not know, and I said that I would say nothing else, and I said it in my own mouth and I have not said anything else since and I am not going to start this afternoon either.

What he did not tell you, and what I am telling you because I would rather it came out of my mouth than his, is that I did have a particular. **I have had it since I was told about those two boats, and I have not put it on a wall, and I am not putting it on a wall this afternoon, and the sixth of those six lines does not require me to and nobody in this slip is going to make me.**

The offer on that paper is a thing somebody made from a long way off. It is not a question and nobody here has to take it up."

---

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that she was only saying it because a man of about fifty-three had said it yesterday, and about four of the nine people on that slip said out loud that he was right, and she answered him in about twenty-four words and did not say them twice.

"**He said it and I am saying it and he is not wrong, and that is the whole of why I am standing here.**"

He said that was the straightest thing anybody had said on that slip in a fortnight and then he said the other half of it, which was that a woman of about thirty-one telling a room she has got a particular and is not giving it is a thing that costs her about as much as putting it on a wall would have cost her and a great deal more than putting it on a wall would have gained, and that he was not going to argue with her about it because he had said on the Saturday that a man who cannot read a page has been handed a smooth page, and that a woman with a particular in her hand and nobody able to see it is the same trouble with a different coat on.

Nobody argued with her after that. A man of about thirty-five of the first lay said one thing, that she had done the only thing on that slip in a fortnight that the paper itself allows anybody to do, which is to say nothing publicly except her own account of herself, and that he was not going to be the man who asked her what it was.

---

At about half past four the man who keeps the leaf was at the end of that slip with the slate in his left hand and nothing on it, and he found that he had been standing there for about ten minutes waiting for a person to speak, and he knew exactly what that was, because it is what he had been doing with a page of hours for thirteen days, and he put the slate down on the bench stone and left it there until about half past five and did not pick it up once.

He had a list at home of things done in that yard since the autumn that have not been thanked for, and it had nineteen lines on it on Sunday, and it had nineteen lines on it this afternoon, and he did not add a twentieth, and the reason he did not add one is that the list is his and a thing that is in it is a thing he thinks he is owed, and this afternoon nothing on that slip was owed to anybody and one thing on it was taken.

---

And about half past five came round on that Thursday with the wind still coming under the doors of that market, and the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud at that bench stone and gave in about a hundred and sixty-five words what a particular in a hand is worth and what it is not worth, and nobody argued it.

"Ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence. Unmoved, and eleven trips went into that cistern this morning at no cost to anybody and not one of them is a figure. **A woman of about thirty-one stood at the end of that slip this afternoon and told about nine people that she has got a particular and is not giving it, and that is the first thing said out loud in that harbor in ten days that nobody in it can check and nobody in it can repeat, and she said it in her own mouth so that no man would be carrying it about for her. That is not a record and it is not going on anything and it cannot be got off a wall. It is also the only thing said on that slip this month that cannot be argued with by anybody, because there is nothing there to argue with.**"

Nothing further was required of anybody on that Thursday, and no voice on that slip or in that shed found the gratitude in its throat, and the particular stayed where it was, which was in a woman of about thirty-one of the first lay.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SECOND TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT. A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE SAID OUT LOUD, IN ABOUT A HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SEVEN WORDS, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, WHAT SHE WAS ASKED TO SAY IN THAT ROOM ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF JANUARY AND WHAT SHE SAID INSTEAD, AND NAMED NOBODY, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT SHE DOES HOLD A PARTICULAR AND IS NOT PUTTING IT ON A WALL. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE SAID SHE IS ONLY SAYING IT BECAUSE A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE SAID IT YESTERDAY, AND WAS ANSWERED IN ABOUT NINETEEN WORDS AND WAS NOT RIGHT AND WAS NOT WRONG. NOBODY ASKED HER WHAT THE PARTICULAR IS AND SHE WAS NOT THANKED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS NINETY-SIX THOUSAND AND FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND TWO MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THERE ARE TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE IN THE BAG UNDER THE SLATE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the third day of February at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and twenty-second time at about eight in the morning in eleven trips by hand and was walked on by none of it and that the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and that the water that went into that cistern was not used for anything at any hour of that day; and that a woman of about thirty-one of the first lay who crews four days in ten stood at the end of that slip at about one o'clock in the afternoon and said out loud in front of about nine people what she was asked to say in the room over that market on the twenty-fourth of January and what she said instead, being that she was asked for a particular about that night out loud in that room by a man in that room and that she put no name on anybody, being that she said that she had not been on the water and did not know and would say nothing else, and being that she said out loud that she does hold a particular and has had it since she was told about the two boats and has not put it on a wall and is not putting it on a wall; and that she said at the start of that account that she was not there because anybody had asked her to be there and that she was there because a man of about fifty-three said out loud on the day before that he could not take six lines down and that the reason he stopped was her; and that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said out loud that she is only saying it because he said it and about four of those nine people said he was right, and that she answered him in about twenty-four words, being that he said it and she is saying it and that is the whole of why she is standing there; and that a man of about thirty-five of the first lay said out loud that she had done the only thing on that slip in a fortnight that the paper itself allows anybody to do, which is to say nothing publicly except her own account of herself, and that he was not going to ask her what it was; and that nobody asked her what the particular is; and that the twelve days from Saturday the twenty-second day of January to this day stand on that leaf at three hundred and six and one thousand three hundred and four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that neither the first of those figures nor the second has been read back out loud in any room or on any slip on any of those twelve days; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of this day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-four pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is ninety-six thousand and four hundred and fifty-four pence short, being six hundred and two marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason he did not add a twentieth line to the list at home is his own, and is that a list of what a man is not thanked for is a list of what he thinks he is owed, and that a woman standing in the open at the end of that slip saying that she is keeping a thing and is not giving it is not a thing anybody is owed and is not a thing anybody has done for him, and that the reason the list has sat at nineteen lines for four days is that nothing has happened this month that belongs on it and that a man who keeps adding to it because he has nothing else to do with it is a man who has started keeping score; and that the reason a demand was not made of her for the particular is his own, and is that a person who says out loud in a market that she is holding a thing and will not hand it over has just spent the whole of what she had, and that asking her for it in front of nine people is the same act as asking a man of about thirty-four for the answer to a question he has already answered; and that the reason the eleven trips into that cistern cost nothing and still are not nothing is his own, and is that a harbor that has one dead hose and a butt behind a market and eleven trips up a ladder on a rota two days a week is a harbor that is one bad week from drinking water out of the mole, and that there is no sheet in this country a man can put that on, and that a cistern is the cheapest thing in this batch and the one thing in it that nobody is going to argue about; and that the reason he put the slate down on the bench stone at about half past four and did not pick it up is his own, and is that he had been standing at the end of that slip waiting for a person to speak, and that a man who has written a rule about who may stop a thing for thirteen days and has been waiting for somebody to say something on a slip for the same thirteen days is doing the same thing in two places and does not know it, and that the woman of about thirty-one broke it by speaking first and about four seconds before he would have.*
