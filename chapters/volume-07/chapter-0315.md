# Chapter 315

*Saturday the Twenty-Ninth of May, Eight Boats of the Third Lay Lying Off, a Sentence Carried Four Days and Changed in the Front of It, a Trade Named in the First Four Words, and Fourpence a Candle at a Counter That Is Not Going to Sell Any More of Them*

Saturday the twenty-ninth of May, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it. The hearing room and the market wall were not in the said day, and nothing was put on that market wall on the said day and nothing came off it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The chandler of the front street does not stand in a fish market on a Saturday morning, and he was in his own shop, and the man of about thirty-six of the third lay went and found him there and then went and stood in the fish market two doors down from where the shop is, in about nine people's hearing, and said it in a market, because a man in a market is a man in a market.

He had been carrying it for four days. He said so before he said it, in about nine people's hearing, and about nine of them heard it and the rest of that market on a Saturday morning was doing what a market does.

"**A man of about sixty said something in a shop on Tuesday morning and I was in that shop and I heard it and I have had it four days,**" he said. "**And I have changed the front of it. That is what I have done with four days. I have not added anything to it and I have not taken anything out of it. I have put four words on the front and those four words are my doing and I did them on purpose on Thursday night at my own table.**"

"**Then say it,**" said a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime over his shoulder.

"**The chandler of the front street put a sentence into this harbor on Tuesday morning.**"

Nobody counted it in that market. The chandler's wife counted it at about half past eleven that same morning, at her own counter, to a man of about thirty-one who was not a customer and had come in to ask her whether her husband was all right, and the counting is at the foot of this and the count is fifteen.

---

"**Now the rest of it,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, "**because a thing in a market is not a thing until somebody says where it came from.**"

"**It came out of a man's mouth,**" said the man of thirty-six, "**on a Tuesday, in that shop, in about nine people's hearing, and he said on that Tuesday that he had counted it and that it was twenty-one words, and I have counted it too and it is twenty-one, and that is the sentence:** a restriction that arrives in a sentence and not in a boat is a thing a man cannot be angry at. **And what I have done is put my four words on the front of it, and it is a sentence about him now, and it is true, and it is not a sentence about a boat.**"

"**Say the rest of his sentence,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight.

"**And a man who cannot be angry at it will be angry at something else by Friday.**"

"**Then it is Friday,**" said a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime.

"**It is Saturday,**" said the man of about thirty-six, "**and it is the day after Friday, and that is the part I want somebody to write down. He did not know on Tuesday what the day was going to be. He said *by Friday* the way a man says a figure in a trade. And I have been angry since Wednesday and I have not got a boat that has been out, and I have got a boat of about thirty tons lying off that mole with eleven weeks of a season on her, and there is nothing in this harbor I can be angry at that has got a hull on it, and I have found one thing with a trade in it, and I have said it in a market, and I would like somebody to tell me which part of that is not what he meant.**"

Nobody told him.

Calder Marr was at the end of the aisle and had been there about two minutes and said nothing at all, and Nessa Pike wrote it all down in the small book she carries and afterwards, in the shed, he wrote three of the lines into the second column in his own right hand and slowly.

---

The chandler heard about it at about eleven and came down and stood at the end of the market and listened to the whole of it, twice, and did not interrupt, and then he went back up the front street and told his wife the whole of it in the words it had been said in, and stood behind his own counter for the rest of that day and did not come down again.

At about half past eleven his wife counted it out at her own counter, because she counts, because it is a trade she has had for thirty years and she counts out loud, and the man of about thirty-one who was not a customer had come in and asked her whether her husband was all right and had not been given anything for it.

"**Fifteen words,**" she said, "**and the first four of them are my trade, and I did not put them there.**"

"**I know you did not,**" said the man of thirty-one.

"**Then that is what I am going to say and then I am going to shut up about it, and I am saying it to you because you are the only person who has come in here this week who has not wanted an argument. He is not going to sell that man candles again. That is the whole of the cost of it. A man buys at my counter for nine years and then he does not, and there is nobody going to write that down anywhere except in me, and I am writing it down here because I am the only one who is going to know it.**"

"**He has a boat of about thirty tons lying off that mole.**"

"**He has,**" said the chandler's wife. "**And a candle at this harbor is fourpence, and it has been fourpence for as long as I have been in that shop, and a man in a wheelhouse burns one a night in this weather, and that is about sixty of them between now and the end of June and that is four shillings, and that is the number. That is not a hardship and I know it is not a hardship and he knows it is not a hardship. It is a price of fourpence and it is going to be a price of nought, and it is the nought that is going to sit on that counter for the rest of this month.**"

---

Nobody in that yard went and told the chandler to sell candles to a man, and nobody in that yard went and told the chandler not to, and nobody in that yard went and told the man of thirty-six that he was wrong or that he was right, and the reason for the first of those is the ninth day of April past and the reason for the second is that this yard has no instrument and no standing and no way of knowing whether a sentence in a market about a trade is true, and there is no column in that book that a sentence in a market about a trade can go in that anybody can check.

"**I want to say the whole of this and then I am going to stop,**" said Calder Marr, that night, in the shed. "**A man of about thirty-six has said a true thing in a market and it has cost a chandler fourpence a candle and about sixty candles between now and the end of June, which is four shillings, which is the same as a man going without a candle a night for two months. I am not going to write four shillings against a man's anger in that book, because four shillings is the price of the candles and the thing that happened is not the candles.

"**And I am not going to go and put a candle in that man's wheelhouse out of a bag with two hundred and sixty pence in it, and the reason is not the money. The reason is that the moment this yard puts a candle in a boat of a man who has been named in a market, then this yard is a party to a quarrel between two men in this harbor, and I have known since the first of May what that costs and I have paid it three times this month for other people and I am not going to pay it a fourth time for myself.**"

"**Write that down as what it is.**"

"**Write it down as what it is,**" said Calder Marr, "**and not as what it is not, and put the day against it and no name in it.**"

---

And on the same morning, in about nine people's hearing at the foot of that mole's steps, a man of about thirty-four who cannot tell what four of the lines of that notice mean said out loud that there are about eleven people on this coast who cannot read it, and that nobody in that yard has gone and told one of them anything, and that nobody in that yard is going to.

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-ninth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that eight boats of the herring third lay were lying off that mole inside that harbor's own line on the said day, with not one of them past the line named in the fifth clause of the notice of the seventeenth day of May past, and that there were eleven of them on the twenty-fourth day of May past and nine of them on the twenty-sixth; and that on the said day at about ten in the morning in the fish market of that harbor, in about nine people's hearing, a man of about thirty-six of the third lay said out loud a sentence of fifteen words, being: *the chandler of the front street put a sentence into this harbor on Tuesday morning*, and said that he had carried the said sentence four days and had put the said four words on the front of it on the Thursday night preceding at his own table on purpose.*

*And that on the said day the said man said out loud the whole of a sentence said by a man of about sixty of the first lay in a shop in that town on Tuesday the twenty-fifth day of May past, being: *a restriction that arrives in a sentence and not in a boat is a thing a man cannot be angry at*, and twenty-one words of it, together with the second part of it, being: *and a man who cannot be angry at it will be angry at something else by Friday*; and that the said man said out loud that it was the day after Friday, and that he had not got a boat that had been out, and that there was nothing in that harbor he could be angry at that had a hull on it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the chandler of the front street of that harbor came down into that market at about eleven in the morning of the said day, listened to the whole of the said sentence twice, said nothing, went back up that street and told his wife the whole of it in the words it had been said in, and stood behind his own counter and did not come down again for the remainder of that day; and that on the said day, after he had come back up the front street and told his wife the whole of it in the words it had been said in and had stood behind his own counter and not come down again, his wife counted the said sentence out loud at fifteen words at her own counter at about half past eleven in the morning, to a man of about thirty-one who was not a customer and had come in to ask her whether her husband was all right and had been given nothing for it, and said that the first four words of the said sentence are her trade and that she did not put them there; and that she said out loud that her husband is not going to sell that man candles again, and that a candle at that harbor is fourpence and has been fourpence for as long as she has been in that shop, and that a man in a wheelhouse burns one a night in that weather, which is about sixty of them between that day and the end of June and four shillings, and that it is a price of fourpence and it is going to be a price of nought.*

*And that the holder of that writ was at the end of that aisle for about two minutes and said nothing at all, and that three of the lines went in this column in his own right hand and slowly; and that no person of that yard went and told the said chandler to sell candles to the said man, and no person of that yard went and told him not to, and no person of that yard went and told the said man of about thirty-six that he was wrong or that he was right; and that the reason for the first is the ninth day of April past, and that the reason for the second is that this yard has no instrument, no standing, and no column that a sentence in a market about a trade can go into that anybody can check.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said evening that he is not going to write four shillings against a man's anger in that book, because four shillings is the price of candles and the thing that happened is not the candles; and that he is not going to put a candle in that man's wheelhouse out of that bag, and gave the reason out loud, being that the moment this yard puts a candle in a boat of a man who has been named in a market, this yard is a party to a quarrel between two men in that harbor, and that it has known since the first day of May what that costs and has paid it three times in one month for other people and is not going to pay it a fourth time for itself.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fortieth line:*

*What it is not: — that fourpence a candle and about sixty of them and four shillings is not the cost of a sentence in a market and is the price of the thing the sentence was about; that a trade named in the first four words of a sentence is not a charge and a chandler who stops selling is not a villain and has not been asked to do anything by anybody; and that a harbor has been arranged wrongly in eight days by a man standing in a market with four words he put on the front of a sentence at his own table on purpose, and that not one boat moved and not one sheet of paper moved and no person in that market was asked anything by anybody.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and sixty pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fourpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and eighty pence, being two marks and five shillings. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was not occupied at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty-two days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eighteenth night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of about thirty-four cannot tell what four lines of that notice mean and said again on that mole in about nine people's hearing that nobody in this yard has gone and told one of the eleven people he can name what is on that wall and that nobody is going to.**

**And a sentence of fifteen words went into a market this morning with a trade in the first four of them, and by Monday a man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons lying off that mole will buy no candles at that counter at fourpence, and he is not sorry, and this yard has not told him not to be, and has not told him to be. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
