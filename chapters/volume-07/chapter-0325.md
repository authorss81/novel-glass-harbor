# Chapter 325

*Wednesday the Ninth of June, About Nine People in a Market, a Boat's Name in a Man's Mouth, a Bag Not Opened, and Four Minutes Stood on Purpose*

Wednesday the ninth of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence. The far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it.**

**And the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn is in that harbor and is going nowhere on any day of these ten, and is of no office, and is not on the crew, and her forty marks stand first after food and are not reduced and are not paid.**

---

The man of about thirty-one with the coaster out of Ashtide came into the fish market on the ninth of June at about ten in the morning with his bag over his shoulder, because he had come to buy salt pork for his boat, and about nine people were in that market around the end stall.

He bought the salt pork and paid for it, and while the woman of about fifty-eight was wrapping it, a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime said out loud, in about nine people's hearing, that the man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons was not to be trusted with anything, the same thing he had said on the first of June at the same stall.

The man of thirty-one did not answer him, the same as on the first of June, and looked at the slate instead, where the two figures for lamp oil stand with both dates on the board. Then he said one thing himself, out loud, because he had been in that market with a bag since March and nobody had asked him anything since March, and he was tired of being a bag.

"My boat is the *Ashtide Rose*," he said. "She is a coaster out of Ashtide, of about forty tons. I have had her nine years. I have a Crown chart in this bag that I bought fair at Ternwatch six years past, and it has no storm route across Bramble Strait on it. If any man wants to know what a chart does not have on it, he can ask me what mine does not have. I am not opening it in a market. I am telling this market that I have it and that it is shut."

Nobody asked him to open it. Calder Marr was at the end of the aisle and had been there about four minutes, and Nessa Pike was beside him with the small book in her hand, and this yard stood in that market for about four minutes on purpose and did not ask him one question.

That is the second time this yard has stood four minutes on purpose. The first was on the twenty-third of May, when the chart was not opened and not shown and not asked for, and this yard did not ask him one question. This is the second, on the ninth of June, when the boat got a name in a man's own mouth, and the chart was not opened and not shown and not asked for, and this yard did not ask him one question.

The man of thirty-one picked up his salt pork and went out of the market with his bag shut, and nobody followed him, and nobody wrote to him, and nobody thanked him.

---

In the afternoon, in the shed, Nessa Pike opened the third of the four books past the first page, being the book of the lighter called the *Tern's Debt*, kept in pencil, half of it rubbed out and written over.

It holds the same water. In a hand that presses hard enough to go through the paper, under a date nineteen years past, stands *Skower Deep, 9 faddom, foule E.*, and on the next page, rubbed out and written over in a lighter hand, *Skour Deap, nine fathoms, foul to eastward*. A fourth spelling and a fifth, a fourth hand, a fourth date, the same sounding under all of them, and figures that do not agree by three quarters of a fathom.

She wrote all of it down in the second column, with the dates and the hands against them, and under them the same line as on the eighth: that a line of figures in three books identifies a difference in three books and not a truth about a sea.

"Three books," said Calder Marr, looking at the bench where the three lay open. "Different spellings, different hands, different names for the same soundings, different figures that do not agree. It is a labor fact. Several men of this trade put figures in several books at several different times. It is the same water under all of it."

"And no current chart has it," said Nessa Pike. "The one on the reeve's wall has nothing across the middle water. His bag has nothing across the middle water. That is two charts with nothing on them."

"That is what a chart does not have on it," said Calder Marr. "Write it as what it is. Three books opened and one still shut, and a bag in a market still shut, and a boat with a name on her, and nobody asked for anything."

She wrote it, and then the shed was quiet for a while except for the water under the mole, and Calder Marr looked at the three open books and said the thing he had held back in the market.

"He named her himself," he said. "The Ashtide Rose, about forty tons, nine years his. No man in this yard gave her that name on a page. He said it in about nine people's hearing with his own mouth, and that matters, because a boat's name in somebody's mouth is how a market remembers a thing. If any later man says this yard named her to make a story, then the page says otherwise."

"And the bag," said Nessa Pike. "Shut in March and shut now. Twice stood four minutes on purpose, once in May and once today, and twice asked nothing. That is the discipline of it. A man with a chart in a bag is a man with a bag until he opens it himself."

"He will open it or he will not," said Calder Marr. "It is his. He bought it fair at Ternwatch six years past, on his own word in a market, and nobody in this yard has held it. If he lays it open on a stall himself one Saturday, then forty people will see what is not on it, and this yard will see it with them and touch nothing. That is the only way this yard gets to look at a chart without becoming a party to a custody."

---

That evening the man of about thirty-one who is not the man with the bag came up the front street to ask the chandler's wife whether her husband was sleeping, because he had heard the husband coughing in the night through the wall, being a neighbor two doors down.

He is about thirty-one, of no lay, a rope-maker's hand, with no boat and no bag and no chart, and he is not the man of the third lay with the coaster out of Ashtide, and the two are not one man and may not be run together. He has one trade and one lodging and one thing he is not, being that he is not a witness to anything and has not been made one.

The chandler's wife told him her husband was sleeping and thanked him for asking, which is the first thanks anybody in that street has said in ten days, and the man went back down the street, and Nessa Pike wrote down that there are two men of about thirty-one in this harbor and that this yard knows the difference and has entered it.

She wrote it carefully, with the rope-maker's trade against the one and the coaster and the bag against the other, and under it that each has one trade and one lodging and one thing he is not, and that neither is a witness to anything.

"A harbor full of men of about thirty," she said, when it was done. "About thirty-one with a chart in a bag, and about thirty-one with rope on his hands, and about thirty-four with a ladder in his book, and about thirty-six with his father's book in his house, and about forty-one back to warps. I have them apart on the page and I am going to keep them apart, because a man run together with another man is a man wronged."

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the ninth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a coaster out of Ashtide, being the Ashtide Rose of about forty tons, came into the fish market of that harbor at about ten in the morning of the said day with a bag over his shoulder, in about nine people's hearing, and said out loud the name of his boat and that he has a Crown chart in the said bag bought fair at Ternwatch six years past with no storm route across Bramble Strait on it, and did not open it and was not asked to; and that the holder of that writ and the ship's hand stood in that market for about four minutes on purpose and did not ask him one question; and that the book of the lighter called the Tern's Debt in pencil was opened past the first page on the said day, holding Skower Deep and Skour Deap, nine fathom, dated nineteen years past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said man was refused a question by this yard in the middle of March past and has asked nothing since, and is a person of no office, and is not a witness and has not been made one, and may not be cited in a room in any volume; and that the said chart was not opened in that market and was not shown and was not asked for; and that there are two men of about thirty-one in this harbor, the said man of the third lay with a coaster and a chart in a bag, and a rope-maker's hand of no lay with no boat and no bag, and the two are not one man.*

*And that this yard did not go back to the copyist's counter in the room off the fish market on the said day and did not send anybody there; and that the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fiftieth line:*

*What it is not: — that a boat with a name in a man's own mouth is not a chart opened and not a route offered; that four minutes stood on purpose in a market is not a question asked and not a custody entered into; and that three books holding one water under five spellings are not one book agreeing with itself, and no man in this shed has said so.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-three days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twenty-ninth night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of about thirty-one has a chart in a bag that this yard has not asked to see and is not going to.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
