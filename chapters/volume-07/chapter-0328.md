# Chapter 328

*Saturday the Twelfth of June, About Forty People in a Market, Five Books and a Chart Opened by Its Owner, and a Storm Route Across Bramble Strait That No Current Chart Includes*

Saturday the twelfth of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence. The far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it.**

**And the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn is in that harbor and is going nowhere on any day of these ten, and is of no office, and is not on the crew, and her forty marks stand first after food and are not reduced and are not paid.**

---

The fish market on a Saturday morning holds about forty people when the weather is fair, and the weather on the twelfth of June was fair, and about forty people were in it at about eleven in the morning when the thing happened that this work will have to carry.

The man of about thirty-six of the third lay came in with his father's book under his arm. The man of about thirty-one with the coaster out of Ashtide came in with his bag over his shoulder. The woman of about fifty-eight stood behind the end stall with her slate turned round so that the two oil figures showed. Calder Marr stood at the end of the aisle with Nessa Pike beside him, and neither of them had asked anybody to come and neither of them had told anybody to stay away.

What happened was not in a room with a door shut. It was in a market, out loud, in about forty people's hearing, and it began the way the eleventh had begun, with the father's book opened at the twine-marked page.

"Read it again," said a man of about forty-five at the front of the stall. "Read it so that all of us hear it, and not nine of us."

So the man of about thirty-six read it again, out loud, the three spellings and the three figures and the three dates, *Skar Deep, Skarre Depe, Skar Deap*, eight and three quarters, nine, nine and a half fathom, twenty-one, fifteen and eight years past, the middle water of Bramble Strait.

Then Nessa Pike stepped forward two paces, because she is the one who counts, and she said out loud, in about forty people's hearing, what stands in this yard's four books, with every spelling exactly as it stands, and every figure, and every date.

"*Skur Deep, nine fathom. Scurr Deepe, nyne fadom. Skere Depe, niene fathoms. Skower Deep, nine fathom. Skour Deap, nine fathoms. Skorr Depe, eight and three quarters. Skor Deep, nine. The depe off the Skore, nine.* Four books, seven hands, eighteen years. The same water. No current chart has it."

About forty people heard that. Nobody wrote it down except Nessa Pike in the small book she carries, afterwards, in the shed.

Then the man of about thirty-one with the bag did the thing nobody in this yard asked him to do and nobody in this yard could have asked him to do. He laid his bag on the end stall's table himself, and he opened it himself, and he took out his own Crown chart himself, bought fair at Ternwatch six years past, and he laid it open on the table beside the father's book, and he put his hand flat on the middle water of Bramble Strait, where there is no line.

"My boat is the *Ashtide Rose*," he said, with his hand on the empty water. "Coaster out of Ashtide, about forty tons, nine years mine. This chart is mine. It has soundings along both shores and nothing across the middle. No storm route. Whatever those books hold, this does not hold it. That is what a chart does not have on it."

He folded it himself and put it back in his own bag himself and shut the bag, and nobody in this yard touched it at any hour of that morning, and nobody in this yard asked him to open it before he opened it or thanked him after.

The woman of about fifty-eight looked at the table where the book and the chart had lain side by side, and she said the one thing anybody said that morning that went in no column, because it is a thing about a market and not about a sea.

"That is five books and a chart in one market," she said. "Five books holding one water under ten spellings, and a chart holding nothing across it. Nobody in this market opened anybody else's book and nobody held anybody else's chart. That is the whole of how it was done."

---

The man who was not in that market is the man this work most wants to be in it. A man of sixty-one at a far bench was on the crown of that mole all morning working on the eleven pieces of his boat, and he was not sent for, and he was not asked, and he asked nothing, and he was not in that market at any hour of that day.

Calder Marr knew it before anybody told him, because he had looked up the hill at about half past eleven and seen the figure on the crown, small against the sky, with the hook in his left hand.

"He is not here," he said to Nessa Pike, quietly, at the end of the aisle. "That is the man. Sixty-one, bandaged in the right hand, hurt in the spring, eleven pieces on the crown, not thanked and not going to be. He is the one who can say whether those soundings are any use, and he is up there, and he is not going to be asked, and he is not going to be sent for."

"Then the route stands as it stands," said Nessa Pike. "Five books and a chart looked at by forty people and touched by none but their owners. The soundings are of a coast that has moved. The safer road has to be given up. Both prices are in the first column. Nobody has said yes. Nobody has been asked."

In the shed that night, the holder of that writ said one thing out loud that is the turn of this whole work, and would not have it softened.

"A party that has always said that a record a man cannot check is worth less than one he can has been handed the best record it has ever been offered, and the offer is only good if it stops checking it. Five books holding one water, and a Crown chart of about nine years standing that omits it, and this harbor holds two copies of that chart, and this yard has held neither. The soundings are of a coast that has moved. The route can be used only if a crew gives up the Ternwatch road for the season. Both prices went in the first column before any use. Nobody has agreed to anything. Nobody has refused anything in a room. That is where it stands."

Nessa Pike wrote the turn down word for word, and then she read it back out loud, because that is the rule of that shed with anything that matters, and he listened without stopping her.

"Five books and a chart in one market," she read. "Nobody's book touched but its owner's. Nobody's chart touched but its owner's. About forty people heard it. The man who could say whether it is any use was on the crown and was not sent for. That is the whole of the twelfth."

"That is the whole of it," said Calder Marr. "And tomorrow the books go up the crown in the sun, because the man of about thirty-six said they would, and any man who knows that water can look at them, and this yard will lay its four beside his one and touch nothing but its own. If the man of sixty-one steps forward, then forty people will see what he does. If he does not, then forty people will see that too. Either way this yard asks him nothing."

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twelfth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about forty people's hearing, a man of about thirty-six read out loud from his own father's book three soundings for the middle water of Bramble Strait, and the ship's hand said out loud what stands in this yard's four books under seven further spellings, and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a coaster called the Ashtide Rose of about forty tons laid his own Crown chart open on the said table himself and put his hand on the middle water where no route stands, and folded it himself and put it back himself; and that the said route is a storm route across the said strait that no current chart includes, and the chart that omits it is a Crown chart of about nine years standing that this harbor holds two copies of, neither of which this yard has held.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said market held about forty people and the said reading was out loud and not in a room with a door shut; and that nobody in this yard touched any book but its own and nobody in this yard touched the said chart at any hour of that morning, and the said man was refused a question by this yard in the middle of March past and has asked nothing since and is a person of no office and is not a witness and has not been made one and may not be cited in a room in any volume; and that a man of sixty-one at a far bench was on the crown of that mole all morning and was not in that market at any hour of that day, and was not sent for and was not asked.*

*And that this yard did not go back to the copyist's counter in the room off the fish market on the said day and did not send anybody there; and that the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that five books and a chart in one market are not one book agreeing with itself and not a chart disproved; that forty people hearing a thing out loud is not forty people agreeing to it; and that the best record this party has ever been offered is not a truth about a sea, and this yard has not refused it for the old reason and has not accepted it for free.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-six days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-second night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of sixty-one was on that crown all morning and was not in that market and was not sent for.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
