# Chapter 336

*Sunday the Twentieth of June, Six Men Ashore and One of Them Hurt and Not Saying So, a Wreck in Eleven Foot of Water Off the South End That Nobody May Have, a Coaster of About Seventy Tons In at Four in the Afternoon with Four Days and Nine Hours Out of Her, and a Figure the Yard Can Take and Not See*

Sunday the twentieth of June, on that mole and in that fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and has been nothing in it since twenty pence went out of it on the said nineteenth day of June past for two quarts of lamp oil at ten pence, and nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she did not come up that hill and nobody asked her to. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and at the low water of the said afternoon about nine people stood on that crown and looked at a wreck a quarter of a mile off the south end and one of the nine was sixty-one and nobody in that yard went over to him and nobody asked him one word about what was lying out there.**

---

A wreck in eleven foot of water inside that harbor's own line is not salvage, is not a claim, is not a claim window, is not a charter, is not a way of getting past anything, and is not this yard's business, and there is no instrument in this country by which a party of this yard's standing could lay a claim on her, and this yard has none and is not going to acquire one and is not going to be the reason one is wanted.

What is true about her is the plain thing. She belongs to six men. The six men are all ashore. There is no second boat. There is no money in that harbor among those six men to buy a crane or a lighter or a day's water, and there is no office in that harbor with a season to ask, and the office that has no season cannot be asked because it cannot answer.

A man of about thirty-eight said it out loud on the quay at about ten in the morning, in about nine people's hearing, and he said it once.

"**She is on the foul and she is going down by the head all week and there is a finger a minute in her at the last of the flood, and the flood is over now, and the next one is about seven hours off, and there is a man of about fifty-five in this harbor with a boat on the hard and a line about her who could float anything, and he is not going to move her, and he said so in May and nobody has asked him again, and I am not going to stand on this quay and ask him, because he told us the answer in May for nothing and the answer has not got any better in a month.**"

"**And nobody is going to go out to her with a hook,**" said a man of about forty-five.

"**Nobody is going to go out to her with a hook,**" said the man of about thirty-eight, "**and this yard has no boat, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and there is nothing in that yard's bag and there has not been since yesterday morning. That is the whole of the answer and it is a figure and not a feeling.**"

About nine people heard that and one of them repeated the figure afterward on the front street, which is what happens to figures.

---

The man of about forty who is Rowan Teel came up those steps at about half past nine in the morning on the said Sunday with his right forearm inside his shirt and did not say so and was not asked, and he worked — that is the wrong word; he stood at the head of the slip for about an hour and then went and sat on the bench at the foot of that mole with his coat over his knees, in the sun, like a man waiting for a boat.

A woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall saw him from the market and came out and stood in front of him and said one thing and it was a thing about a trade and not about a man.

"**You are holding that arm the way a man holds an arm that has been in the water,**" she said, "**and I am not going to ask you about it, and I am going to tell you that the bonesetter comes down that hill on a Monday and not on a Sunday, and that he is sixty and that he has set about four hundred things and that he is the only man in this harbor who will look at anything at all, and that is me telling a man what I know about my own stall's customers and not me asking a man one word about his arm.**"

"**Thank you,**" said Rowan Teel, and nothing else, and she went back to the market.

He was at that bonesetter's on the Monday and the bonesetter's figure and the bonesetter's saying are on the face of the twenty-first of June and not on this one.

---

The coaster of about seventy tons came into that harbor at about four in the afternoon on the said Sunday and took her berth on the west quay, and four days and about nine hours out of her, being out of that harbor since about ten past to seven on the morning of the Wednesday past, and she was not stopped and was not spoken to and was not followed and nobody went down to her and nobody went aboard.

She came in with the stove out of her wheelhouse and a fender of somebody else's tied to her and nothing else wrong with her that a man on that quay could see, and her master of about fifty and his two men came off her at about twenty past four and went up the front street, and one of the two men stopped at a chandler's and the other went to his house, and that is what happened on the eighteenth day of May past and it is what happened on the twentieth day of June past.

Calder Marr was on the crown of that mole when she came round the point and he did not go down to the slip and he did not go to the west quay, and he did not go and look at her wheelhouse, and the reason is the reason of the first of May past and is the same reason and is not improved on for a boat of seventy tons or a boat of nineteen tons or a boat of any tonnage whatever.

She was out of sight from that mole for about six hours of the four days and the nine hours, and this yard saw none of it, and there is no page anywhere in this harbor that says where she was or what happened to her or what was said to her, and there is not going to be one written by anybody in this shed.

What there is, is a price, and the price is a figure this yard can take.

"**A boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence,**" said Nessa Pike, writing in her own left hand, "**and that boat was out four days and about nine hours with three men in her, and four days of one man is six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence. This yard has a figure for a whole day and none for a part of one, and the ninth hour is not in that figure and is not going to be put in it. This yard has a figure for one man and none for three, and invented neither, and the whole of it is entered as a figure and not as a complaint against anybody.**"

"**And it was not paid to this yard,**" said Calder Marr, "**and not one penny of it came to this yard, and this yard has no claim on it and is not going to say it has, and the number goes in the second column as a price and not in the first as a finding.**"

---

In the shed that night there were three of them and the woman of no office said the thing that had to be said, and she said it standing at the bench with her coat on, because she had not come up to stay.

"**Say what the week cost,**" she said, "**and say it in figures, because I have noticed that when a person of this yard says what a thing cost in words, the words are better than the thing.**"

"**A boat of about nineteen tons and six men in her, and one man of the six hurt in the water, and four days and about nine hours of a boat of about seventy tons off this coast with three men in her that this yard saw nothing of, and a day and a half of two persons of this yard in the water off the south end, and a man of about thirty-four of this harbor's own second lay who gave four figures out loud on a step and is not a man this harbor is going to be able to leave alone, and twenty pence that is not in the bag, and a harbor that is poorer on this Sunday than it was on the fourteenth of June.**"

"**Now say what it bought.**"

"**Nothing,**" said Calder Marr. "**It bought six men ashore and it bought one hurt man and it bought a wreck in eleven foot of water and it bought a fortnight of a coast going hungry and it did not buy one page of that seventy-ton boat's week. Nobody died. I am going to say that word out loud because somebody in this shed is going to want to say it and I would rather it was said by me and said flat: nobody died. And I am not going to be relieved about it in this shed tonight, and I am not going to be told anybody is, and if either of us starts, I will stop it.**"

Nessa Pike wrote it and read it back and then said the thing she is the one to say, because she is the one who counts.

"**Six men wrote their names in a book on the quay at half past two on Friday afternoon under a date and a figure. That is the only page in that harbor that six men can check and that this yard cannot, and it is a page in a book that belongs to six men, and this yard has not got it and is not going to get it and is not going to be asked for it. I am putting that in the second column and I am putting the word *not* in it three times.**"

"**Three,**" said Calder Marr.

"**There will be four tomorrow,**" she said, "**when Rowan Teel is asked whether he will write his name under that line, and he will, and nobody will ask him why, and I am not going to be here for it.**"

"**You will be here for it.**"

"**I will be here for it,**" she said, "**and I am not going to enjoy it and I have not enjoyed a day of this month and that is my own business and it is not a figure for anything.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twentieth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a boat of about seventy tons of this harbor came in and took a berth on the west quay at about four in the afternoon of the said day, being out of that harbor since about ten past to seven on the morning of the sixteenth day of June past and being four days and about nine hours out, and that she came in with the stove out of her wheelhouse and a fender belonging to somebody else tied to her, and that her master of about fifty and his two men came off her at about twenty past four in the afternoon and went up the front street, and that no person of that yard went down to the slip, went to the west quay, went aboard, or went and looked at her wheelhouse, and that the reason is the reason of the first day of May past and was not improved on for a boat of that size; and that she was out of sight from that mole for about six hours of the said four days and nine hours, and that no page in that harbor says where she was or what happened to her.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the price of four days of one man in a boat is six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that the said boat was out four days and about nine hours, and that this yard has a figure for a whole day and none for a part of one and invented none, and that the said figure was not paid to that yard and not one penny of it came to that yard and no claim was entered and none is going to be entered; and that the said boat had three men in her and that this yard has a figure for one man and no figure for three and invented none; and that a wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul ground off the south end of that mole in about eleven foot of water inside that harbor's own line and is not salvage and not a claim and not a claim window and not a charter and not a way of getting past anything, and that there is no instrument in this country by which a party of that yard's standing could lay a claim on her, and that this yard has none and is not going to acquire one; and that a man of about thirty-eight said out loud on that quay on the said morning in about nine people's hearing that nobody was going to go out to that wreck with a hook, and that a man of about fifty-five in that harbor has a boat on the hard with a line about her and said in May past that he was not going to move her, and that nobody asked him again on the said day and this yard did not ask him anything.*

*And that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said out loud to a man of about forty on the said morning, unasked and not asking a question, that the bonesetter comes down that hill on a Monday and not on a Sunday, and that he is sixty and has set about four hundred things and is the only man in that harbor who will look at anything at all, and that the man said thank you and nothing else; and that the said woman told the oil figure out loud at a chandler's counter on the said nineteenth day of June past before it was asked for, being ten pence a quart and a barrel up six shillings since the first of June past and not coming down.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-first line:*

*What it is not: — that a wreck a quarter of a mile off a mole is not a thing anybody has a right to and is not a thing anybody is going to be thanked for not having; that six men ashore is not a rescue and is not a victory and is not a result; and that nobody dying is not the same as nothing being lost, and this yard said that word out loud in its own shed on the twentieth day of June and said it flat, and is not relieved about it and was not comforted by anybody and did not ask to be.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-four days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fortieth night, and neither of them has moved. The mark of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, and it was put to a use it has never been put to on the eighteenth day of June past, and the figure that use gave was right, and the boat was on the ground, and nobody has said one word in that harbor about that sentence since it was said on that mole at a quarter to six yesterday afternoon. A boat of about seventy tons is in with four days and about nine hours out of her and a stove out of her wheelhouse and this yard saw six hours of that time and has taken a price for the rest and not a word. A man of about forty is hurt in the water and has not said so. A harbor can be poorer on the twentieth day of June than on the fourteenth and is.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
