# Chapter 302

*Sunday the Sixteenth of May, a Shipwright of About Sixty, a Copper Pipe at Ternwatch, Three Shillings a Week for a Slip, and a Boat's Man of About Forty Who Went Out to a Tender at Five and Came Back at Half Past Five With Nine Words in His Mouth*

Sunday the sixteenth of May, on the hard and the slip, at a chandler's on the front street, and off the north side of that mole in the evening.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it, and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it. A man of sixty-one was not on that far bench at any hour of the said Sunday and was asked nothing and asked nothing; the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it; and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The shipwright was sixty and had been in this harbor thirty-eight years and worked out of a shed at the head of the hard with one apprentice of about twenty-two who was not there on the Sunday because Sundays are not days.

"**She wants a copper pipe,**" he said, of the boat on the slip. "**She has wanted one since the twenty-eighth of April and it is not a new want, it is an old want that has got worse. There is a length of it under the housing that has gone green and if it goes green through she will take water and if she takes water she is on the hard for a month instead of a week.**"

"**Where is the pipe.**"

"**Ternwatch. There is a smith's counter at Ternwatch that sells copper by the foot and he does not cheat anybody and he has been there forty years.**"

"**Can you go.**"

The shipwright looked at the water.

"**It is a day's water with a foul bottom at one end and about four hours of it in a month when you would go, and I would want a man, and a man is four marks a day, and out of what.**"

"**Out of my boat,**" said the man of about fifty-five. "**I have a boat.**"

"**You have a boat with a line on her and a copper pipe in her and three weeks of nothing.**"

"**I have a boat and I will get the pipe on her and be back on the Tuesday and nobody will have any of it.**"

"**That is right,**" said the shipwright. "**And I will tell you the whole of it while you are standing there wanting me to say the other thing. A day's water is a day's water. If you go on the Tuesday you go on the Tuesday whatever the pipe is, and if you are caught out past the bar on the Tuesday you are a boat that went out on the Tuesday, and a boat that went out on the Tuesday is a boat somebody has a figure about.**"

The man of about fifty-five did not say anything to that and went to look at his wheelhouse, and the door of the wheelhouse was shut and had been shut since Saturday.

"**The slip is three shillings a week,**" said the shipwright, to nobody. "**That is what it is. It is not a rent. It is a fee for being allowed to lie there and it does not stop and it is not a punishment and there is nobody to say I am charging you for the privilege of a blockage, because I am not. It is three shillings a week and it has been three shillings a week since I came and if the three shillings do not come, the boat does not go off the slip, and that is all there is to say about it.**"

---

The chandler is on the front street and has been there thirty-one years, and he is a man of about fifty, and on Saturday the fifteenth a man in a blue coat came into his shop and asked him, in about nine people's hearing, what boats had been out of Morrow Quay in a fortnight and where they had gone to.

He told Nessa Pike about it on the Sunday at about eleven, at the counter, with about four people in the shop, and he told it the way a man tells a thing he is not ashamed of and is not proud of either.

"**I gave him nine,**" he said. "**Not nine boats. Nine words. I said, *I sell candles and rope, not other men's water.* That is nine words and it is true and it is what I would have said to any man who came in and asked me, and if you want to know what I would have said to a man who came in and asked me on a Tuesday before all this, it is the same sentence, because I have said it to about four of them since I came.**"

"**Did you give him a list.**"

"**I did not give him a list.**" The chandler put a hand flat on his counter. "**And I have been standing here since Saturday working out whether I could have named nine boats, and I can name eleven and I could not have sworn to nine, and if I had said eleven I would have named one that has been on her keel out of the south channel since the ninth of April, and that is a lie and I would not have known it was a lie.**"

"**Then nothing happened.**"

"**Nothing happened to me,**" said the chandler. "**And a man in a blue coat went down this street to a chandler and asked a question and was given nine words, and if he wants to ask it again he can come back and I will give him the same nine words, and if he asks it a third time in a fortnight I will give him the same nine words in a louder voice.**"

Nobody in that shop improved on it.

---

The tender came in at about four in the afternoon and lay off the north side with a line out to a buoy, and about forty people were on that mole inside an hour, which is more than that mole has held on an ordinary Sunday since the herring fleet filled.

She was a Crown tender of about sixty tons and she had two guns that were covered and a boat on her davits and no flag showing and one light burning at her masthead, and she lay there for three hours and went out again at about seven and a quarter.

At about five o'clock a boat's man of about forty, who is not of this yard and owns a third of the boat he rows and works the rest of it for two other men, went out to her in a dinghy with a painter and came back at about half past five and moored her and came up the steps and stood at the top of them.

He was not asked anything. He was asked nothing by anybody on that mole and there were about forty people on it.

He said it because somebody said *did you go out to her*, which is a question anybody may ask anybody, and he answered it, and then he answered the rest of it because forty people were standing there and there is no way of answering the first of a thing in front of forty people without the rest of it coming out.

"**He asked me one question,**" said the boat's man. "**He asked me whether a crate had gone off that mole on the fourteenth of May. That is a crate and a mole and a day, in that order, and I have been on this water thirty-one years and there was a crate on that mole on the fourteenth of May and everybody standing here knows it was there.**"

"**What did you say.**"

"**I said I had not been here on the fourteenth, and I did not know, and I was not going to say.**"

"**And.**"

"**And he said, *Then you'll want it again from who wrote it.***"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds, and then about eleven people said something at once and the boat's man put his hand up flat, the way a man does on a mole.

"**That is the whole of what he said to me,**" he said, "**and it is nine words and I counted them, and I want to say the thing about the counting before any of you ask me why I counted. I counted them because I have been in a boat with a line in it for an hour and a quarter and there was nothing in my head but nine words and a man's face, and I am forty-one years old and I have never in my life come off a boat with nothing in my head except what was said to me.**"

---

Calder Marr was standing on the steps and he had not asked one word of it and had not been asked one word of it.

"**I am not going to tell you what you should have said,**" he said.

"**Nobody thinks you could.**"

"**Nobody thinks I could and I am saying it anyway, because in about nine days there is going to be a version of this afternoon in a book on that tender, and in that version one of these three things is going to be true. It is not going to be that you were asked a question about a crate on a mole. And it is not going to be that I stood here and told you what to say. And it is not going to be that you are a man who does not tell.**"

"**Then what is it going to be.**"

"**It is going to be a man who did not tell, and that is the same words, and the difference is going to be a thing you cannot get back out of it.**" He did not raise his voice and about forty people were on the mole. "**And I will tell you the reason I am not going to help you with it, and the reason is a rule and the rule is from the first of May and it has not been improved on. You are not of this yard. A man of forty who rows a boat he has a third of does not get his words off this hill. If he does, then next month there is a man on that tender who says *ask the man at the shed*, and that is how a trade stops being able to say one word to anybody.**"

The boat's man nodded and went down the steps and did not say thank you and was not thanked.

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the sixteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a tender of about sixty tons came into the water off the north side of that mole at about four in the afternoon of the said day and lay there about three hours with a line out to a buoy, one light burning at her masthead, no flag showing, two guns covered, and a boat on her davits, and went out again at about a quarter past seven; and that about forty people were on that mole between about five and seven o'clock in the afternoon of the said day, which is more than that mole has held on an ordinary Sunday since the first of April.*

*And that a boat's man of about forty went out in a dinghy with a painter at about five in the afternoon of the said day and came back at about half past five and moored and came up the steps; and that he was asked nothing by any person and that he was asked one question by a man on the said tender, being whether a crate had gone off that mole on the fourteenth day of May, and that he said he had not been there on the fourteenth, that he did not know, and that he was not going to say; and that the man on the said tender said, *Then you'll want it again from who wrote it*; and that these are nine words and the said man counted them and said so out loud on the steps of that mole in about forty people's hearing.*

*And that a boat lies on the slip of this harbor's own hard with a green length of copper under her housing that wants replacing, and that the copper is at Ternwatch, and that Ternwatch is a day's water with a foul bottom at one end and about four hours of it in a month when a man would go, and that a boat's man is four marks a day; and that a slip of this harbor is three shillings a week; and that neither figure was paid and neither was asked for and neither is going to be.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a chandler of the front street of this harbor was asked on Saturday the fifteenth day of May past, in about nine people's hearing in his own shop, what boats had been out of this harbor in a fortnight and where they had gone, and that he gave nine words and no list, and that he has said out loud in about four people's hearing that the same nine words would have been given on any Tuesday in the year; and that a man in a blue coat of about fifty with a book was in that shop and is at the head of that slip and on that tender and on that mole, and that he is not a servant and is not an officer and has not given his name to anybody and has not been asked anything by anybody.*

*And that the price of the said nine words is not a figure and is on a page of a man of about fifty and goes in no column of this yard's book, being that he cannot now be certain which of the boats he could have named was out and which was not; and that the reason against the line of this yard's correcting it is that this yard did not ask him and was not on that street.*

*And that the holder of that writ asked that boat's man nothing on the said day and was asked nothing, and that the reason against the line of the asking is that a man who does not tell is a man who owns a piece of an answer and this yard has no standing to take a piece off him and never has and is not going to acquire one.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that nine words said to a boat's man on a tender are not an order and are not a threat and are not a charge, and that *who wrote it* is not a name and is not a person and is not anybody this yard can put in a column; that a chandler's answer in nine words is not a list and is not a surrender and is not a thing this yard may afterwards correct; and that a rule which has kept nine people off that hill since the first of May past is a rule and not a wall, and that this yard cannot say yet what it is keeping them off from.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and the bag holds two hundred and sixty-one pence, being one mark, eight shillings and fivepence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and seventy-nine pence, being two marks, four shillings and elevenpence, and it is not going to be paid. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-nine days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fifth night, and neither of them has moved. A tender came off the north side of that mole this evening and went out again at about a quarter past seven, and about forty people watched it go, and nine of those forty could have named a crate.**
