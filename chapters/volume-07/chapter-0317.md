# Chapter 317

*Monday the Thirty-First of May, the Fifty-Sixth Time That Wall Came Down, the Fifty-First Charge Walked on None, Sixty-Three Words Said at a Mole Head in About Nine People's Hearing and Counted by the Man Who Said Them, and One Mark and Eightpence at a Box on a Corner at Four o'Clock*

Monday the thirty-first of May, on that mole in the morning, and at a box on the corner of that mole in the afternoon.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-sixth time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on the said day. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said day by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-first of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing went out of this yard on the said day. One mark and eightpence came into it, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence. And the far bench was not occupied on the said Monday at any hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

He came up those steps at about eleven in the morning and about nine people were on that mole, and he was not asked up there and he was not asked anything.
He had the two candles still. He has carried them for ten days and has not lit either and has not said why and nobody has asked.

He said the thing at about a quarter past eleven and it took him a long time to get to the middle of it, and about nine people heard all of it, and this is the whole of it:

"**The day a man in this harbor works out that it is cheaper to be angry at a boat than it is at a sentence, this harbor will get a captain, and it will be one of ours, and it will be a good one, and it will be a man who is angry and who is telling the truth about being angry.**"

Then he said:

"**That is sixty-three words and I counted them on Friday night in my own house and I have counted them four times since, and I am telling this mole I counted them because I am not going to have anybody in this harbor saying afterwards that I was careful with it. I have been careful with it for ten days. I have been careful with it since the twenty-first of May and it has got me a chandler's counter shut and a man of about thirty-six with no candles and a man of about forty-one with a bone in his foot.**"

---

A man of about thirty-eight of the second lay, who had been standing at the top of those steps with a basket and who has a boat of about nineteen tons of his own, said:

"**Then this harbor wants somebody to speak for it, and there ought to be a—**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I had not finished.**"

"**You were going to finish it in about nine people's hearing on a mole,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to stop you there in front of all of them so that it is on this mole and not in a drawer in a house four streets off, and I am not going to tell you what I think should come next, and I am not going to refuse you the next thing either. I am going to leave a space on this mole where the next thing was going to go, and everybody who was standing here is going to remember that there was a space.**

"**And the reason is the ninth day of April past, and I am not going to give you the reason, and that is not me being careful, that is me being consistent, and I have been consistent about it for fifty-one days and I have paid for it about nine times and I am not going to stop on a Monday.**"

"**That is not an answer to him.**"

"**It is not an answer to him and that is deliberate,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I want the difference between that and a refusal written down in about nine people's hearing, because it is the difference between a party that will not answer a question and a party that will not have the question. I will not have the question. I am not going to answer it and I am not going to put my hand over it, and if you are both of those things on the same morning on a mole then one of them has got to give, and it is not going to be me.**"

Nobody said a word for about half a minute.

---

"**Have you got a name,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, who had come up the hill for no reason anybody could see.

"**No,**" said the man of about sixty of the first lay, "**and I am not going to go and look for one, and I want to say why one more time and then I am going to stop being the man who says things on this mole.**

"**I have asked this harbor two questions. One on the second of December and one on the eleventh of March, and I was answered neither time, and then I said a sentence in a shop on the twenty-first of May that had a chandler in it by Friday, and I said a sentence in a shop on the twenty-fifth that got counted in eleven markets by somebody I have never met, and this morning I have said the third thing. That is three. The correct number of times to ask a question in a harbor is three and not four, and I have not got a fourth and I am not going to go and look for one, and I am not going to be a man who comes up this mole in September with a fourth.**

"**And I am not asking you for anything. I have not got anything to give this harbor and I have not got anything to ask it for and I have not got a name and I have not got a boat. I have got sixty-three words and I have counted them and I have said them out loud on a mole in front of about nine people, and that is the whole of what I have got, and I am going down to the water now and I am going to leave the candles.**"

He put the two unlit candles on the stone at the top of those steps, which is not a thing anybody had done on that mole before, and went down the steps, and nobody picked them up for about a quarter of an hour, and then the chandler of the front street came up for them at about twelve, and did not go into the market, and went back down the front street with them in his coat, and nothing was said to him about it and nothing was said to them.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-six of the third lay came up that mole with about nine tons of salt on a lighter alongside the west quay and a coaster of about thirty tons of his own with her line on her ring bolt and a slip at three shillings a week, and he hired the boat.

He had asked a question on the west basin walk on the Thursday and had been told the price on that walk, and he came and paid the price, and there was no haggling and no asking for a discount and no asking whether this was the yard's own account or somebody's.

"**It is mine and it is for me,**" he said, "**and it is nine tons of salt off a lighter that is lying inside this harbor's own line, and I cannot get my own boat off the soft ground today and I want the salt in her before the tide tomorrow, and there is nobody in this harbor to hire.**"

"**It is a mark and eightpence for the day with a man in her,**" said Calder Marr, "**and the five things get said to that man before the painter goes and not after, and not one of the five of them has changed since the twenty-eighth, and you are getting exactly what the boat's owner of about fifty-five got on Friday and you are not getting a better one because you asked at a quarter past four instead of at half past two.**

"**And I am going to say one more thing out loud because he is standing there and he can hear it. You asked me on the west basin walk on the Thursday and I gave you a price and I did not give you a reason, and I gave you neither of the two things I gave a man in a market on the Wednesday. You did not ask. I noticed that you did not ask, and I have written down that I noticed.**"

The man of about thirty-six did not say anything about that and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went into that yard's own book, and the man in the boat was about twenty-six and gave his name out loud when it was asked for and it went in, and the five things were said to him by the man of about thirty-six himself before the painter went and not after.

They were alongside the west quay at about six and the salt came across on a hand barrow by the quay and not over the rail, and nothing was landed from outside that harbor and nothing was taken out of it.

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the thirty-first day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the slate wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning of the said day for the fifty-sixth time since the first of May past and the counter did not restart and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and that no sitting was held in that room on the said day; and that the cistern was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning of the said day and that this party walked none of it, being the fifty-first of the charges walked on none of since the third day of August past.*

*And that a man of about thirty-six of the third lay, with nine tons of salt upon a lighter lying inside that harbor's own line and a coaster of about thirty tons of his own with a line on her ring bolt, hired a boat of about thirty tons with a man in her for a day at a box on the corner of that mole at about four in the afternoon of the said day for one mark and eightpence, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, on his own account and for his own purpose; and that the said hire was not salvage and not a claim and not a claim window and not a charter and was not a way of getting past any line, and that nothing was landed from outside that harbor on the said day and nothing was taken out of it; and that the man in the said boat was about twenty-six and gave his name out loud when it was asked for and it went in, and that the said five things were said to him by the said man of about thirty-six and not by that yard, before the painter went and not after.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about sixty of the first lay, who has been on that water forty-one years, said out loud at a mole head in about nine people's hearing at about a quarter past eleven in the morning of the said day a sentence of sixty-three words, being: *the day a man in this harbor works out that it is cheaper to be angry at a boat than it is at a sentence, this harbor will get a captain, and it will be one of ours, and it will be a good one, and it will be a man who is angry and who is telling the truth about being angry*; and that he said that he had counted it in his own house and had counted it four times since, and that he had been careful with it for ten days and that being careful with it had got a chandler's counter shut, a man of about thirty-six with no candles, and a man of about forty-one with a bone in his foot.*

*And that the said man said out loud that he had asked this harbor two questions and been answered neither, that he had said a sentence in a shop on the twenty-first of May past that had a chandler in it by the Friday, that he had said a sentence in a shop on the twenty-fifth that was counted in eleven markets by a person he has never met, and that this was the third; and that he said that the correct number of times to ask a question in a harbor is three and not four, and that he has not got a fourth and is not going to go and look for one; and that he said he was not asking that harbor for anything and had nothing to give it and nothing to ask it for and no name and no boat; and that he left two unlit candles at fourpence each on the stone at the top of those steps and went down to the water, and that the chandler of the front street came up for them at about twelve and went back down that street with them in his coat.*

*And that a man of about thirty-eight of the second lay began to say, in about nine people's hearing on that mole, that this harbor wanted somebody to speak for it, and was stopped by the holder of that writ before he finished; and that the holder of that writ said out loud in the same hearing that he was not going to tell that man what he thought should come next and was not going to refuse him the next thing either, and that he was going to leave a space on that mole where the next thing was going to go so that everybody standing there would remember that there was a space; and that the said holder said that not answering that question and refusing it are two different things and that he is not going to be both of them on the same morning, and that the reason for stopping the said man is the ninth day of April past and was not given, and that no person in that mole named a body and no person in that mole proposed one.*

*And that the holder of that writ told the said man of about thirty-six on that mole that he had given him a price on the west basin walk on the Thursday and had given him neither of the two reasons he gave a man in a market on the Wednesday, that the said man had not asked, and that the holder of that writ had written down that the said man had not asked.*

*And that this yard asked nobody anything else on that mole on the said day, and did not go into that market after it, and did not ask the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall why she had come up that hill for no reason anybody could see, and did not ask the chandler of the front street what he was doing at the top of those steps at about twelve; and that none of those four is on a page anybody can check and that this yard wrote all four down anyway, because a line against a thing this yard did not ask is the only kind of line in this column that cannot be got wrong afterwards.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-second line:*

*What it is not: — that a man who says out loud that he has counted his own words is not making a speech and is not asking for anything and has not got a name and is not going to go and look for one; that a captain is not a person and this harbor has not got one and is not going to be given one by this yard, and that nobody in that harbor on that Monday named a body or proposed one; and that a party which stops a man from finishing a sentence on a mole is not a party that has answered him, and a space where a sentence was going to go is not an answer and is not going to be given one in this harbor.*

---

**One mark and eightpence came into this yard on the said Monday, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and nothing went out of it, and there are four hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, which is two marks and nine shillings, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and twelve pence, being one mark, four shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid. The wall came down bare for the fifty-sixth time and the cistern was charged for the fifty-first and walked on none of it. The far bench was not occupied at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty-four days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twentieth night, and neither of them has moved, and about eleven people on this coast cannot read that notice and nobody in that yard has gone and told one of them anything.**

**And on a mole head in about nine people's hearing this morning a man of about sixty of the first lay said sixty-three words he had counted four times, and a man of about thirty-eight was stopped four words from finishing a sentence, and neither of those things was answered, and at four o'clock this afternoon this yard took one mark and eightpence from a man of about thirty-six to shift nine tons of salt inside its own harbor line. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
