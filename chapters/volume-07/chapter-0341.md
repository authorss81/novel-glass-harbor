# Chapter 341

*Friday the Twenty-Fifth of June, the Eleventh Day of the Ninety, Eleven Men Asked One Question Each in Eleven Rooms, Ten Said Yes and One Said No, and One of the Ten Said Yes and Then No and Then Yes Again with All Three Hours on a Sheet, a Woman of No Office Told in a Shed What It Was Going to Cost Her, and a Sheet Opened on a Bench With a Name Against Every Line*

Friday the twenty-fifth of June, on the quay and in three rooms and on the front street, and in the shed on the mole at about half past four in the afternoon.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday, and the fifty-ninth charge falls on Monday the twenty-eighth of June next and the sixtieth on Thursday the first of July next. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and that holder was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on it, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, bandaged in the right hand, with a boat's hook in his left hand and eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked and not going to be, not sent for and not asked.**

**And this yard's own count of the three standing figures, made in that shed on the said day and not taken out of any other book: in these ten days there is one wipe of that wall to come, being the sixtieth on Monday the twenty-eighth; two charges of that cistern to be walked on none, being the fifty-ninth on the twenty-eighth and the sixtieth on the first of July; and one wage Tuesday, being Tuesday the twenty-ninth, for the thirty-sixth week, so that thirty-six weeks will stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the close of these ten days.**

---

The question was one question and it took eleven men all day to ask it, and it was asked in eleven places, and it was put to each man on his own ground and by somebody who had no standing whatever to put it, and the man who asked it did not go with it.

That last part was the condition and Calder Marr made it himself and would not give it up.

"**I am not going round this harbor with a list,**" he said, at the head of the slip, at about nine in the morning. "**I am going to ask one man one question in the place where he works, and I am going to say the same words to every man in the same order, word for word, and if a man says no then nobody goes back to him and nobody tells him why anybody else said yes, and if a man says yes then nobody goes back to him either.**"

The question as it was put, in eleven places, on the twenty-fifth of June, was this, and it was said the same way every time, and it is fifty-one words and this yard counted them twice on the Friday morning and is not going to count them again:

*Will you take your own boat out of this harbor past the mole head on the night of this day next, and across the bar, and into the strait, and bring back flour and oil and physic, on your own account, and without a declaration, which is a breach, and which nobody can make you keep to and nobody can pay you for?*

And the answer was given in a chandler's, in a net loft, in a kitchen, in a boatyard, on a hard, in a shed, at a counter, in a fish market, on a mole, and twice on a quay, and the yard has it that way and not as a list.

---

The two prices were named before anybody was asked anything, in the first column, at about half past nine in the morning, and both of them are in this yard's own book with the dates against them and neither has been argued since.

The first was entered on the fifth of June and is the soundings: they are of a coast that has moved, and there is no figure anybody may measure out, and it is not a defect to be mended, and there is no figure for how far to be approached.

The second was entered on the sixth of June and is the road: the route can be used only if a crew gives up the Ternwatch road inside the line, for flour and coal and oil, of about eleven tons to a lighter, for the season.

Calder Marr said both of them out loud on the quay before the first man was asked, and he said them again to each man separately, and to the two who said no he said them too, and neither of them was asked until after he had said them.

---

**The eleven were these, and each of them is one man with one trade and one boat, and none of them is a person of this yard and none of them was a witness to anything and none of them is a symbol.**

**Elias Voke**, fifty-one, herring skipper of the *Anna Ray*, who has nineteen winters disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city, and a season of nine marks that is not going to be paid, and a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November past. He said yes in about nine people's hearing at the end stall of the fish market and he said it in one sentence and he did not look at anybody when he said it.

**Iver Sallow**, about thirty-one, of the third lay, of the *Ashtide Rose* out of Ashtide, about forty tons, nine years his, with a Crown chart of about nine years standing in a bag that he opened himself on the twelfth of June past and that nobody has touched since. He said yes in the boatyard and said that he had been waiting since the twelfth to be asked and was not going to say what for.

**Roy Kettle**, about thirty-six, of the third lay, of a coaster of about thirty tons, eleven weeks of a season standing against her and no passage past that mole head since the seventeenth day of May past. He said yes at a counter in the front street, out loud, to a chandler, and the chandler heard it and is not the man's keeper and is not going to be asked about it.

**Peder Lund**, about thirty-eight, of no lay and no standing whatever, of a boat of about thirteen tons that he keeps himself and pays for himself. He said yes in a fish market on the second day of June past and had got four words out of it and been stopped, and this time he said the whole of it in one go and it took nine seconds.

**Ivor Pride**, about forty-seven, of the third lay, of a boat of about forty-five tons with the Monday tide on the fish quay. He said yes at the head of that slip at about eleven in the morning and the first thing he said after it was that the tide was on the Monday and that he was giving it, and that he wanted the hour on it.

**Alban Pree**, about sixty, of the first lay, forty-one years on that water, who asked a question twice in the year past and was answered neither time, and who asked this volume's question out loud on a mole head on the thirty-first day of May past in sixty-three words which he counted four times. He said yes in his own kitchen with his wife in it and she did not stop him, and what he said afterwards, unasked, on the front street, was that he had never once been asked to be in a thing and that he had said yes and that the first time a man is asked is the last time he gets to be brave for free.

**Tam Rundle**, about thirty-four, of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait, the *Mary Rend*, who works for nobody and has a copied line in his coat that is not his and no paper at all. He said yes at the packet's own landing place, alone, to Calder Marr and to nobody else, and the only thing he asked was whether the sheet would carry the name of his boat.

**Jens Ower**, about twenty-nine, of no lay, of a lighter of about eleven tons, which is the figure of the Ternwatch road and the same figure twice. He said yes at a counter in the front street and the first thing he said was the price of the road he was giving up.

**Adam Slee**, about sixty, a shipwright of this harbor with a bench at the back of the chandler's and an apprentice of fourteen. He said yes in his own shop with two things attached: he would take oil and physic and would not take flour, being a shipwright, and he would know what was in his own bottom before he got to the bar.

**And one said no, and one of the ten said it three times.**

**Josiah Mear**, about fifty-five, of a boat of about twenty-four tons on that slip with a line about her moulds that has been staying on since the twenty-eighth of April past, said no on the front street at about half past ten in the morning, in about nine people's hearing, and he gave the same reason he gave in May and he gave it better and not shorter, and the first part of it was a green length of copper under her housing that wants replacing at Ternwatch, a day's water with a foul bottom at one end and about four hours of it in a month, a boat's man at four marks a day, three weeks of nothing and a slip at three shillings a week, and the second part of it was this.

"**And I will say the other half since you have asked me in the open and there are nine of you standing here. A man may not move a boat off a hard in this harbor now without somebody able to say afterwards that he moved her for a reason somebody else gave him, and you are standing there without a list and without an instrument, and if I go out on Saturday night on your errand then for the rest of this season there is no boat in this harbor anybody can move.**"

Nobody improved on it. A man of about forty-five said *Aye*. The end-stall woman said nothing. Calder Marr said one word, which was *no* — the yard is not going to argue a man out of a no, and it has said so before and it said so again and it is the same refusal and not a new one.

**Hamo Birt**, about fifty, of the second lay, of a boat of about twenty-two tons, who has a form in his coat with a name and a date against the name and a box against its fourth line that is not filled, and who laid that paper on a table in an office on the west basin on Monday the fourteenth of June where no clerk holds it, said yes at about eleven in the morning at the head of that slip, in about nine people's hearing, in three words, and then said it again at about four in the afternoon in the front street, in about nine people's hearing, the other way round, and gave a reason that was better than his trade's.

"**This morning I said yes and I want it written down that I said yes and the hour I said it, and I am going to say the other thing now and I want that written down too with the hour of it, and I am not going to be asked why because I would not have an answer that was any use to anybody. I have already put a paper on a table in that office that nobody will take, and the box on it is not filled and I did not fill it, and that is the only paper I have ever put anywhere and I did it because the sentence on the wall says a master may be held to the outer marks without one. If I go out on Saturday night without one, then on Monday morning that paper of mine is not a thing I did and it is a thing somebody else did, and a man who puts a paper on a table wants to be able to say whose hand it was in.**"

Calder Marr was in the front street at about four in the afternoon because he was going to the shed, and he heard it, and he asked him nothing, and Hamo Birt went home with his boat tied, and the sheet was not written that night and was written on the Saturday morning, and both hours are on it and the sheet does not choose between them.

That is eleven men asked and one man saying no and ten men saying yes, and one of the ten said it three times across one day, yes at eleven o'clock in the morning, no at four in the afternoon, and yes again at eight o'clock the next evening, all three hours on the sheet and the sheet not choosing between them, and nobody has asked him about any of the three and nobody is going to.

---

The sheet was opened in that shed at about half past four in the afternoon and it is on the bench now and it was on the bench then.

It is written on the back of a sheet that was in the drawer in that shed, one of seven pieces of paper in that drawer with the place of the charter empty, and it is written in the ship's own left hand, four stitches in a right hand since the twentieth of February past, and it says at the head of it:

> *The night of the twenty-sixth day of June next. Eleven boats were asked and eleven answered. Forty decisions are to go on here and every one of them is one a man could have refused, and the refusals go on here too and they are not smaller than the rest. The time is the time the man said it. The price is the price he said he was paying. Nobody was sent for. Nobody was asked twice by anybody but themselves. — N. P., ship's hand.*

Calder Marr read that over her shoulder and made her change one word in it, and she wrote his objection in the margin with a date against it and both words stand.

"**You have written *a man could have refused*,**" he said, "**and I want *could have refused it and said so*, because I have got two men on here who did exactly that and I am not going to have this sheet make them sound like they had a choice and were slow.**"

"**Put your own name to that,**" she said, "**because it is your objection and this is not my book.**"

He wrote three words in the margin and they are there and they are not a signature and they are not going to be improved on.

---

And then the woman of no office came up that hill at about half past four in the afternoon, which she does not have to do, and sat on the bench, and he told her.

He told her in plain words and he did not soften a figure and he did not put it in a book first, which is what he did on the sixteenth day of June past and was caught at for on the nineteenth.

"**I am going out on the night of this day next, in a boat of about thirteen tons belonging to a man of about thirty-eight of no lay, at his own asking and on his own account, and this yard is not paying one penny of it and has no instrument and did not advise it and is not going to advise it, and I am not calling it anything. I will be off that mole head at about half past seven in the evening and I will be back at about half past five in the morning, and there is a line described in the fifth clause of a notice nailed up outside the market and I am going past it without a declaration, and that is a breach, and it is a breach that a man's boat commits and not this yard's, and I have gone over that wording about nine times this morning and I cannot get it to say anything else.**"

She sat on that bench for a while with her hands in her lap and then she said four things and they were all true and none of them was kind.

"**Then it is not yours, and you are going anyway, and I want the second of those two written down before I say the other three.**"

"**Write it.**"

"**That is what a Tuesday cost me on the nineteenth of June, and you did it in the ordinary way and I caught you in the ordinary way, and I am not going to be caught twice, and I would like it noticed that I have noticed.**"

"**It is noticed.**"

"**Forty-one days of nothing in a bag in that shed, and you are going to be on that water in a boat that belongs to a man who is not you, and I am not going, and I am not going because there is a man of about thirty-four in that harbor whose daughter is nineteen and who cannot read, and there is nobody in this harbor going to tell him a single word about any of this, and if I go up that hill with you tonight then for about a week there is one more person in this town who knows what is happening, and that is a thing I can do without, and I am not going to do it.**"

"**You are right,**" he said, "**and I am not going to argue it, and it is a good reason and it is the reason you gave, and I would have used a worse one.**"

"**And the fourth thing,**" she said, "**is that I am not going to sit on this bench at a quarter to five in the afternoon and be told a thing by a man, and that if I am going to hear it I am going to hear it out of a book that a stranger can pick up and check, and that is the only difference between you and a notice, and I would like it written down that I said that.**"

She was asked to write it and would not, on the same ground she gave on the twenty-second, being that she is of no office and has never written in that book and will not be the second hand in it. The ship's hand wrote it with the attribution in and no name against it.

And then Mara Venn went down that hill at about ten to five in the afternoon, and forty marks first after food stand against her name in that yard's book, neither reduced nor paid, not reduced by a night of somebody else's boat and not paid by a question, and she goes nowhere in these ten days and nobody sent for her.

---

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-fifth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the two prices of the said route were named out loud on the quay before any man was asked anything, being that the soundings on the said water are of a coast that has moved and that there is no figure for how far to be approached, and that the said route can be used only if a crew gives up the Ternwatch road inside the line for the season, of about eleven tons to a lighter; and that eleven men of that harbor, each with one trade and one boat, were asked one question in eleven places on the said day, in about nine people's hearing in six of them, and that the question was put by a man of no standing whatever and that the condition he made and would not give up was that nobody went back to any man a second time, and that eleven answered, ten saying yes and one saying no; and that the ten who said yes were Elias Voke of the *Anna Ray*, Iver Sallow of the *Ashtide Rose*, Roy Kettle of a coaster of about thirty tons, Peder Lund of a boat of about thirteen tons, Ivor Pride of a boat of about forty-five tons, Alban Pree of a boat of about thirty tons, Tam Rundle of the screw-steam packet *Mary Rend* out of Bramble Strait, Jens Ower of a lighter of about eleven tons, Adam Slee of a boat of about twenty-six tons, and Hamo Birt of a boat of about twenty-two tons; and that the one who said no was Josiah Mear of a boat of about twenty-four tons on that slip, who gave the reason about a length of copper under her housing and about a man not being able to say afterwards whose errand he went on, and that the yard said the one word no to him, being that it does not argue a man out of a no and has said so before; and that the said Hamo Birt, who has a form in his coat with a box not filled on it, said yes at about eleven in the morning at the head of that slip in three words and said it again at about four in the afternoon in the front street the other way round, and gave a reason about a paper on a table and whose hand was in it, and asked for both hours to be written down and was not asked why and did not say; and that he was not asked anything by anybody in that yard on either occasion and has not been asked since and is not going to be.*

*And that at about half past four in the afternoon of the said day in that shed a sheet was opened on that bench, being the back of one of seven pieces of paper in the drawer in that shed with the place of the charter empty, written in the ship's own left hand, and that it carries at the head of it the night it is for, the number of boats, and that forty decisions are to go on it, and that the times are the times the men said them and the prices are the prices they said they were paying, and that nobody was sent for and nobody was asked twice by anybody but themselves; and that the holder of that writ read that over her shoulder and made her change one word in it and she wrote his objection in the margin with a date against it and both words stand; and that he wrote three words in the margin of that sheet and they are not a signature.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ told a woman of no office on the said afternoon in that shed, in plain words and without softening a figure, that he was going out on the night of the said day next in a boat of about thirteen tons belonging to a man of about thirty-eight of no lay, at that man's own asking and on his own account, and that this yard was not paying a penny of it and had no instrument and did not advise it and was not going to advise it; that he was going past the line described in the fifth clause of the notice without a declaration, and that that is a breach, and that the breach is committed by that man's boat and not by a vessel of this yard's, and that there is no vessel of this yard's on the water and none is going out; and that she said four things and none of them was kind, and that the first of them was that it is not his and he was going anyway and that she wanted the second of those two written down before she said the other three; and that the third of them was that there is a man of about thirty-four in that harbor two days down that coast with a daughter of nineteen who cannot read, and that nobody in that harbor is going to tell him one word about any of this, and that she is not going up that hill tonight because there is one thing she can do without and she is going to do without it; and that the fourth of them was that if she is going to hear a thing she is going to hear it out of a book a stranger can pick up and check, and that that is the only difference between a man and a notice, and that she asked for it to be written down that she said that; and that she was asked to write it in that book and would not, on the same ground she gave on the twenty-second day of June past, and that the ship's hand entered it with the attribution in and no name against it.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that eleven men answering in eleven rooms in one day, ten of them saying yes and one of them saying no, is not a vote and is not a majority and is not a body and is not a thing anybody in this harbor can speak for afterwards, and that the one man who said no in the open in about nine people's hearing is not a veto and is not a refusal of the other ten and is not going to be held against him by anybody in this yard; and that a question asked by a man of no standing whatever in eleven places is not a summons and did not go round again after anybody said no; and that a sheet with a name and an hour against forty lines is not a fleet and is not a wing and is not a guard, and this yard has not got a word for what it is and is not going to find one.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. A boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, so that bag cannot buy a day. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four with a boat's hook in his left hand and his right hand bandaged, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked and was in none of the eleven rooms and is not going to be. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-nine days up the crown of that mole, nine feet apart, and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-fifth night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end of that mole in eleven foot of water and is going down by the head, and six men of that harbor are ashore with no boat and were not asked anything today, and nobody in this yard asked them anything. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this yard took the figure of ninety on the fifteenth day of June past, being the day six men of that harbor were told to stand by, and this is the eleventh day of the ninety, and nobody in this country has won anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
