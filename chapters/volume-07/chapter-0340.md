# Chapter 340

*Thursday the Twenty-Fourth of June, the Fifty-Eighth Charge Walked on None, a Leaf With Four Columns Read Back in Full With the Count on the Face of It, the Whole Standing Inventory Once and Nowhere Else, and the Figure the Bag Is Short of the Wage of Four Marks*

Thursday the twenty-fourth of June, in the shed on the mole, and on the crown of that mole in the morning.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-eighth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Thursday and no sitting was held in that room on it.**

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and twenty envelopes stand on that bench in that chest and did not move, and thirty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and there has been nothing in it since twenty pence went out of it on the nineteenth day of June past for two quarts of lamp oil at ten pence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at that bench was asked for nothing and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn is in that harbor and going nowhere on the said day, of no office, not on the crew, and her forty marks stand first after food and are neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, bandaged in the right hand, with a boat's hook in his left hand and eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked and not going to be, not sent for and not asked.**

**In these ten days the wall came down bare once, being the fifty-ninth on Monday the twenty-first of June past, and the next will be the sixtieth on Monday the twenty-eighth, which is outside these ten; the cistern was charged on three rota days, being the fifty-sixth on Thursday the seventeenth, the fifty-seventh on Monday the twenty-first and the fifty-eighth on the said Thursday, and this party walked on none of them; and there were two wage Tuesdays, being Tuesday the fifteenth for the thirty-fourth week and Tuesday the twenty-second for the thirty-fifth, and the column did not move on either, so that thirty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. Those three counts are this yard's own and are not taken out of any other book.**

---

The four men came at about ten and went at about half past, and nobody in that shed saw them come and nobody saw them go, because the shed was empty at ten and the crown was where the work was. Nessa Pike was on the crown with a warp that wanted splicing, and Calder Marr was at the foot of the steps looking at the fouling of the second seam from the hard, the way a man looks at a thing he has finished with and is not going to touch again.

He did not go into the water. There was no boat, and no reason, and the use was closed on Monday night in front of two people and it stays closed. He stood on the hard at about half past eleven with his hands in his pockets and looked at six feet of stone for about a minute, and the band where it had been wiped on Friday was a shade paler than the rest, and that was all there was to see, and he went back up the steps.

Tobias Wren was on that crown all morning, working on the eleventh piece of a boat with the hook in his left hand, and about nine people came and went on that crown in the course of the morning, and one of them stood for a while and watched him work, and nobody asked him one word about a boat that is a wreck off the south end, and nobody asked him one word about a figure that was right, and nobody thanked him for refusing twice, and he was not sent for and is not going to be.

The far bench had its man on it from ten until four, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the day, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why, and the day was the tenth of ten in which neither of them has said it.

At about noon the man of about fifty of the second lay went past the foot of those steps on his way down to the hard, with his coat buttoned, and he did not come up, and nobody called him up, and the paper with his name and the date against it has been on a table in that office on the west basin since Monday the fourteenth, and the box against its fourth line is not filled, and the clerk of that office is not holding it and is not going to hold it, and this yard has not seen it and is not going to.

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and nobody else, and the read-back was in the ship's own left hand and went into her own book and not onto the wall of anywhere. The woman of no office was on the bench, and she had no mending in her hands, and she had come up that hill in the afternoon for the reading and for nothing else, and nobody had asked her to.

**The first column of that leaf has seventy-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were sixty-three of them at the close of Monday the fourteenth of June past and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Tuesday the fifteenth to Wednesday the twenty-third of June, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own makes seventy-three.

The nine are: a wage Tuesday with thirty-four weeks and a form on a table with a box not filled, and six men told to stand by on a boat of about nineteen tons with no second boat; a coaster of about seventy tons out past the line in the morning on a paper on a table, watched from a hundred and thirty yards and not followed; six men out to the bar and no further and back with nothing, and a man of sixty-one asked to take a boat out and refusing, and one sentence out of a man's own book about what a figure and an hour can tell; two persons of that yard in the water off the south end with a lead and a line, four figures read off a page that was not copied, and a figure given to six men on a quay into a book of their own; twenty pence out for two quarts of oil, a lamp in a shed window from two o'clock, and a boat of about nineteen tons found on the foul at twenty to six with eleven foot over her and six men ashore; a coaster of about seventy tons in with eight days out of her and a stove out of her wheelhouse, a wreck that is no salvage and no claim, and a price of eight days of one man that this yard has no figure for three of; a wall bare for the fifty-ninth time and a cistern charged for the fifty-seventh, a forearm set for sixteen pence out of a man's own hand, and a name written on a seventh line in a book on the steps of that mole; a wage Tuesday with thirty-five weeks and a market in which nobody proposed anything and a man stopping himself two words into a sentence; and a launch book read aloud on a table by its owners, and a man on his own feet on the floor of that market saying out loud what he did on the eighteenth and what it cost on the nineteenth, and comforted by nobody.

**The second column has two hundred and forty-three lines in it at the hour of the read-back.** Two hundred and eight of them were in it at the close of the fourteenth of June past and eight went in to the close of the sixteenth, and twenty-four went in between the seventeenth and the hour of the reading on the twenty-third, and three went in that evening, and three this afternoon, and two hundred and forty-six at the close. The said figure is that book of that yard's own and is not a count of paragraphs, and the first column's seventy-two and the fourth column's sixty-four, at the hour of the read-back, are paragraph counts and are checkable by anybody with a leaf and a pencil.

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The four lines above his named line are four, nameless, and are not struck out, and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered, and the three nots standing against the eighth of June were not asked about on that day or after it.**

**And the fourth column has sixty-four lines in it at the hour of the read-back and sixty-five at the close, and the sixty-fifth is the last thing in that book.**

---

And then the three of them said what the ten days were, in about three lines each, and none of it was argued with and none of it has been improved on since, and it is about three different objects and all three of it is true.

**The first was about the notice, and the holder of that writ said it.**

"**Ten days ago this yard had twenty pence in a bag and it has nothing now, and twenty pence went out on Saturday morning for two quarts of oil at ten pence and nothing came in on any day, and the notice of the seventeenth of May is still on that market wall and has not been taken down and nothing has been put on that wall and nothing has come off it, and the two older naval notices on that wall are still on that wall, and the clerk keeps his own page and does not mention it. And a woman of about fifty-eight has two figures and two dates on a slate and the rest of nine, and the cheapest thing in this harbor went up six shillings on the first of June and is not coming back down, and this yard has said out loud that it does not know whether those two things are in the same world and is not going to try to find out.**"

**The second was about the harbor, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**A boat of about nineteen tons with six men in her went on the foul off the south end on Saturday evening and all six are ashore and the boat is on the bottom going down by the head, and there is no second boat and the line about the moulds of the boat on that hard is staying on, and not one hull in this harbor is a boat six men can have. A coaster of about seventy tons went eight days past the line on a paper on a table and came back with a stove out of her wheelhouse, and no page in this harbor says where she was. And a man of about forty was hurt in the water off that foul and paid sixteen pence out of his own hand and wrote his own name on a line in a book, and that is a bill and a name and neither of them is this yard's. That is ten days in this harbor in which six men lost the only boat that goes out when a thing is in the water, and I have been the one who counts since the sixteenth of February and I am telling this shed that I do not have a column for what it costs to be one.**"

**The third was about the yard itself, and the woman of no office said it from the bench, and she was not asked for it.**

"**There is nothing in that bag and twenty pence went out of it on Saturday morning for a light in this window, and nobody in this room is going to be able to tell afterwards whether those were ten good days or ten bad ones. A mark on the face of the second seam was put to a use it has never been put to on this coast, off four figures out of a book that belongs to a man who never gave his name to anybody, and the figure was right and the boat was on the ground. A man stood on the floor of a fish market yesterday morning on his own feet and said so, and was comforted by nobody, and has not been improved on by anybody in this yard and is not going to be. And that is what it is.**"

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-fourth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said day by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a woman of no office; and that the said leaf holds seventy-three entries in its first column, being sixty-three on the fourteenth day of June past, nine from the fifteenth to the twenty-third, and one this evening; two hundred and forty-six lines in its second, being two hundred and eight, eight, twenty-four, three and three; none in its third; and sixty-five in its fourth, being fifty-five on the fourteenth day of June past, nine from the fifteenth to the twenty-third, and one this evening, which is the last thing in that book.*

*And that the whole of what came into that yard in the ten days ended on the said day is nothing, and the whole of what went out of it in the said ten days is twenty pence, being two quarts of lamp oil at ten pence on the nineteenth day of June past, and nothing in a bag plus nothing minus twenty pence is nothing in that bag, and that a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, so that bag cannot buy a day; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.*

*And that the said route stands as it stands in each book, with its spellings and its dates, and no current chart includes it; and that the price of the moved coast stands with no figure for how far to be approached, and the price of the Ternwatch road stands at about eleven tons to a lighter for the season, and that nobody has agreed the said road and nobody has refused it in a room; and that the said mark was put to the said use once, on the eighteenth day of June past, and the use is closed, and that four figures and no page were taken, and that no fifth figure will be asked for at any hour of any day; and that the said sentence was said on the twenty-third day of June past in about forty people's hearing, on the holder of that writ's own feet, and was not improved on afterwards and he was comforted by nobody.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was not touched on any of the ten days, that nothing was put on the market wall of that harbor and nothing came off it, that the two older naval notices further along that wall are still on it, and that the hearing room and the market wall were not in any one of those ten days on the same day; and that no part of that yard's method, its figures or its fourth column is on that wall in that harbor.*

*And that about eleven people on that coast cannot read the said notice, and that no person in that yard went and told one of them anything, and that nobody in that harbor is going to; and that the chandler's note is at thirty-one, not re-written, its three counts of weeks at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven; that the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing was measured; that four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days; that the plate in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, its note open, carried, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter out of a shed; that the case of five bells is not raised; that the lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second day of July are not touched; and that five pages are in five drawers in five towns and that yard has not looked in four of them.*

*And that nine communities hold the records one in each, and that the said practice is of six centuries standing, and that the office that answers it is of three hundred years standing, and that the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities; and that a can upon a stake at the outer marks is not on a chart of about nine years and was there in the last week of March, and is not a route, and was not followed and was not put on any chart and was not asked what it is for.*

*And that a man of sixty-one on the crown of that mole was asked on the seventeenth day of June past to take a boat out, and refused, and was asked nothing and thanked nothing, and is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice and is not this yard's injury; and that the said refusal is not the refusal of the thirteenth day of June past and the two are kept apart in this book, and that the man at the far bench across that crown is a different man and is not in this book at all; and that nobody in that market on the twenty-second day of June past proposed a body, a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard, and that the word for none of them was said, and that the new question of the thirty-first day of May past was not answered there and is not answered here.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that ten days with twenty pence out and nothing in are not ten days standing still, because a mark was read for a use it had never been read for and a boat went on the ground and six men came ashore and a man said so on his feet in a market; that a figure that was right is not a figure that saved a boat, and that a boat on the ground is not a wrong figure and not a wrong mark and not a wrong man; and that the thing that has no name in it did not cost a single day of these ten and is not a stage anybody in this harbor has stood on, and that the Storm-Reader stage is taken in shared work with a crew at a cost and not before, and not as a solution to a problem of police or price.*

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is nothing, and the whole of what went out of it in these ten days is twenty pence, and nothing plus nothing minus twenty pence is nothing. A wage of four marks was not paid on either of the two wage Tuesdays and thirty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope, and the next falls due on Tuesday the twenty-ninth of June for the thirty-sixth week, which is outside these ten days, and will not move on it either. The wall came down bare once in the ten days and the cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them. The far bench was occupied on all ten of the ten days, being the fifteenth, sixteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth, twentieth, twenty-first, twenty-second, twenty-third and twenty-fourth, from ten until four on each of them, and was not approached on any of them — and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on all ten and went up no coast road on any of them.**

**And here is the whole of where this yard is at the end of it. A mole-head light not lit, its lens out since the seventh of May, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for oil is a barrel at four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted to a woman on the first of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and on the February figure that light was out in October. A woman of about fifty-eight wrote the new figure on a slate at an end stall that morning and left the old one visible above it with both dates on the board, and she has not been asked about either and is not going to be. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-fourth night. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-eight days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, the chest not opened, the bellwright not asked in forty days and no digit of anything he said printed. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid, not reduced by oil and not paid by a question. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June standing unasked, the third column unfilled and unappointed. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, and nobody has asked whose either is. Seven pieces of paper in that drawer and the charter's place empty, and one of the seven is a copy of a drowned city, and a round of glass is in a house four days up that coast, and a page went on a wall there in April.**

**And on the twenty-fourth of June next there is a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay who walked plainly on the eleventh after ten days without weight and fourteen off the water and is back to warps and not in a boat, and a man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons and no candles at a counter in the front street and eleven weeks of a season and his father's book in his house, and a chandler of about fifty of the front street whose counter is shut to that one man and open to everybody else, and a wife of his who counted about sixty candles at fourpence on a Sunday morning and called the difference a price of nought, and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a chart in a bag that he opens himself or not at all, and a man of about thirty-one of no lay with no boat and no bag who is not that man, and a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and gave his name to nobody and is not Garrow Fisk of a pressure house at Ternwatch, and six men of that harbor's own water with no boat and a wreck on the foul off the south end going down by the head, and a man of about forty of the six with a hurt arm and sixteen pence paid out of his own hand and his own name on a line in a book, and a boat of about seventy tons that went eight days past the line on a paper on a table and came back with a stove out of her wheelhouse, and a boat on the hard with a line about her moulds and the line staying on, and seven boats of the herring third lay lying off inside their own harbor line, and a man of fifty-one with nineteen winters disputed and a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November and a season of nine marks that is not going to be paid, and a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand who refused twice and would not say the two things he could say either time, and a mark on the face of the second seam fouled about a foot and seven inches where it was on the nineteenth of May and put once to a use it had never been put to and closed, and a mark four hundred yards off it of a make nobody in this harbor makes, and the season of both at nine marks and neither paid, and a man of about fifty of the second lay with his name and a date on a paper on a table in an office that has no season and a box in it that is not filled.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and a harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and nobody in this country won anything this month, and the cheapest thing in this town went up six shillings on the first of June and is not coming back down, and a boat of about nineteen tons with six men in her is on the bottom off the south end and six men are ashore with no boat, and five books hold one water under eleven spellings and a chart of about nine years has nothing across it and a man who has seen that water said so and would not say more, and this is the twenty-fourth of June and this is the tenth day of the ninety.**
