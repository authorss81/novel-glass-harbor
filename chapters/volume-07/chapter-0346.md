# Chapter 346

*Wednesday the Thirtieth of June, a Sheet Read Back in Full in a Shed on a Mole With the Eleven Men Who Wrote It in the Room, Forty Lines With a Name and an Hour and a Price and a Payer Against Every One of Them, a Man of About Fifty Disputing One Word of It and the Correction Entered Beside the Wrong Figure and Not Over It, and Six Things That Happened After the Sheet Stopped Which Are Not on It*

Wednesday the thirtieth of June, in that shed from about ten in the morning until about two in the afternoon.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, and the sixtieth charge falls on Thursday the first of July next. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said Wednesday and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed and was not asked to be. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, and was not in that shed, and about nine people were on that crown and nobody said one word to him about a sheet.**

---

The ten who had gone out came, and the one who had not came as well, and nobody was asked to come and nobody was sent for and Josiah Mear came at about ten in the morning and stood at the back with his hands in his pockets for two hours and was not asked one word about his refusal and did not expect to be.

The rule was read out before the sheet was opened, and Calder Marr read it, and it is the rule of the first of May past and he has not improved on it in two months.

"**A man of this yard in about forty people's hearing is a man who has said he is going to do a thing and has named the price of it and is not to be asked to make it nicer. I am going to read forty lines out loud and any man here may stop me on any one of them, and if a man stops me, both readings go on the sheet with the hour of the correction against it and the wrong one is not scratched out, because a sheet with a wrong word on it and a correction beside it is worth more to a stranger than a sheet with no wrong words on it.**"

Then the sheet was opened and it is read here in full, as it stands, with the head of it and the forty lines and the places of the copies, and the copies were not made from this reading, they were made from the finished sheet on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth and went out on that same afternoon, and this is the yard's own copy and it is the one that is in the drawer with the charter's place empty.

> *The night of the twenty-sixth day of June next. Eleven men were asked and eleven answered, and ten of the eleven went out. Forty decisions are on this sheet and every one of them is one a man could have refused and said so, and the refusals are on it too and they are not smaller than the rest. The time is the time the man said it. The price is the price he said he was paying. Nobody was sent for. Nobody was asked twice by anybody but themselves. — N. P., ship's hand.*
>
> *And under the head of it, in the same hand and dated the twenty-ninth day of June past, one line, being that three words of that head were changed on the twenty-fifth day of June past at the asking of the holder of that writ, from *a man could have refused* to *a man could have refused it and said so*; and that the words as they stood at half past four in the afternoon of the twenty-fifth are not scratched out and are on the copy in the end stall and on the copy in the bag with the chart in it; and that a sheet which shows a stranger what it said before it was corrected is worth more than one which does not.*
>
> **1.** *The twenty-fifth, half past ten in the morning. JOSIAH MEAR, twenty-four tons: no. Entered on the twenty-sixth at ten in the morning because the sheet was not opened until half past four on the twenty-fifth and I said it before that, and it is first on here because I said it first and I said it in the open. Price: a season I do not get either way. Payer: me, by staying on the hard.*

> **4.** *The twenty-sixth, ten minutes past nine in the morning. ELIAS VOKE, Anna Ray: I will bring back flour and I will not bring back physic, there being physic at fourteen pence and none in this harbor and I have a lighter's room. Price: two days of a season and nineteen winters. Payer: me.*
>
> **5.** *The twenty-sixth, twenty-five minutes past nine. IVER SALLOW, Ashtide Rose: nobody is to touch the chart in my bag, being it is nine years old and it is mine and it has nothing in the middle of that water. Price: nothing. Payer: nobody.*

> **8.** *The twenty-sixth, ten past eleven. IVOR PRIDE, forty-five tons: I am two men short of a crew of five and I am going with a boy of seventeen and a man of sixty and I am saying that out loud now and not in the morning. Price: two men. Payer: me.*

> **9.** *The twenty-sixth, forty-five past eleven. ALBAN PREE, thirty tons: I will take the wheel for four hours, and four hours is not a figure anybody in this harbor has got, and I am not going to invent one at eleven o'clock in the morning. Price: four hours, unpriced. Payer: me.*

> **10.** *The twenty-sixth, half past twelve. TAM RUNDLE, Mary Rend: I want the name of my boat on this and I want it in your hand and not mine, being I cannot write. Price: nothing. Payer: nobody.*
>
> **11.** *The twenty-sixth, twenty past one. JENS OWER, lighter, eleven tons: I am going to the north end of the bar and no further, and I am saying it now at one o'clock in the afternoon and not at midnight. Price: the outer two miles. Payer: me.*
>
> **12.** *The twenty-sixth, two o'clock in the afternoon. ADAM SLEE, twenty-six tons: I will not take flour. I am a shipwright and I have not looked at her bottom since the twenty-fourth of April past. Oil and physic go and flour stays. Price: nothing. Payer: nobody.*

> **13.** *The twenty-sixth, ten past three. IVOR PRIDE, forty-five tons: The tide on the Monday is gone and it is gone because of last night and not because anybody took it off me, and I wanted the hour. Price: one tide. Payer: me.*
>
> **14.** *The twenty-sixth, four o'clock in the afternoon. HAMO BIRT, twenty-two tons: I have untied her at last and nobody asked me to and nobody is going to.*
>
> **15.** *The twenty-sixth, eight o'clock in the evening. HAMO BIRT, twenty-two tons: I said yes on the twenty-fifth at eleven and no at four and I am saying yes again, and both go on and the sheet does not choose between them. Price: the slip at the head of that mole for the three weeks from the fourth of June to the twenty-sixth, being three shillings a week, that is one hundred and eight pence, which I am not paying anybody, I am losing it. Payer: me. — The price on this line was entered first as *nothing* at eight o'clock on the twenty-sixth and was corrected at half past eleven in the morning of the thirtieth at the asking of the man whose name is on it, and the wrong figure is not scratched out.*
>
> **16.** *The twenty-sixth, twenty to ten at night. PEDER LUND, thirteen tons: If I find it different then it is mine and not yours, and you are not to come aboard and ask me about it in the morning. Price: a stranger with a lead would have to be here tonight. Payer: me.*
>
> **17.** *The twenty-sixth, fifty-one minutes past ten. ELIAS VOKE, Anna Ray: I will hold her to nine fathom on the line and I will not lift a foot of it for anybody on this coast. Price: two days of a season and nineteen winters. Payer: me.*
>
> **18.** *The twenty-sixth, twenty past eleven. TAM RUNDLE, Mary Rend: My light goes out here and it stays out until we are through, and a light in a strait is a thing somebody will come for. Price: a night's passage, about nine tons. Payer: me.*
>
> **19.** *The twenty-seventh, five minutes past midnight. ADAM SLEE, twenty-six tons: I will not take flour. Price: two days off my bench and my apprentice of fourteen goes without. Payer: me.*
>
> **20.** *The twenty-seventh, forty-eight minutes past midnight. JENS OWER, lighter, eleven tons: A lighter will not cross a bar at night. I am at the north end of the bar and I have not gone one mile past it, and I am saying it at this hour so that nobody can say in the morning that I said something else. Price: the outer two miles, said twice. Payer: me.*
>
> **21.** *The twenty-seventh, six minutes past one in the morning. COLL MABRY, forty-five, third lay, in the forty-five tons: two fingers of my left hand off the warp, and I am off the water for nine days, and I have a girl of eleven and it is her I am thinking about, and I am putting that on the sheet so that I cannot say in a week that I did not say it tonight. Price: nine days, and I have no figure for a day of my own and I am not going to make one up. Payer: me, and whatever the nine days cost, and nobody here.*
>
> **22.** *The twenty-seventh, nineteen minutes past one. IVER SALLOW: I have got my own hand on my own chart in the middle where the strait is, and I am saying so out loud so that if I am wrong afterwards there is somebody who heard me say I was holding it. Price: nothing. Payer: nobody.*
>
> **23.** *The twenty-seventh, thirty-one minutes past one. ALBAN PREE: Sea by hand and not by instrument, being I have no instrument. First reading and second reading are not the same and I am going with the second. Price: my hands, at sixty. Payer: me.*
>
> **24.** *The twenty-seventh, forty-four minutes past one. ROY KETTLE: Half speed. I am not going to be hurried by anybody on this coast tonight. Price: an hour I have not got. Payer: me.*
>
> **25.** *The twenty-seventh, fifty-eight minutes past one. Price: none given. Payer: me.*

Price: nobody in this country can check this in a year. Payer: me.*

> **39.** *The twenty-seventh, forty-seven minutes past three. PEDER LUND, thirteen tons: Nobody has come aboard and asked me about the two feet, and I said on a quay last night that nobody would, and I am saying at this hour that nobody has, and if anybody comes tomorrow I will say the same thing again. > **40.** *The twenty-seventh, two minutes past four in the morning. ELIAS VOKE, Anna Ray: That is the sheet. Nobody writes on it after this. I have had two days of a season and I am not writing anything at four in the morning that I would have to read at ten.*

> *And under the fortieth line, in the ship's own left hand, the places of the copies: one to a market wall in a town four days up that coast, put up by whoever puts it up and not by this yard, this yard not having written to that town and not going to; one to the end stall in the fish market; one to a man of about thirty-one of the third lay who has a chart in a bag, in the bag with the chart in it; one to a man of about fifty-one of that harbor who owns five sixths of a boat on the foul off the south end of that mole; and one to this yard, in a drawer with the charter's place empty, where nobody is going to get at it, being the one that will be lost.*

---

The dispute happened at about half past eleven in the morning of the thirtieth and not on the twenty-ninth, and it is entered above in the fifteenth line, and it took ninety seconds.

Hamo Birt stood up, which he had not done, and said that the word against the fifteenth line was wrong, and that it was entered at eight o'clock on the Saturday evening as *nothing*, and that it was not nothing, and that he had a boat of about twenty-two tons lying tied at the head of that slip with a line doubled and the third turn on since the fourth of June, and that a slip in that harbor is three shillings a week and that three weeks of it is one hundred and eight pence.

"**I am not paying that one hundred and eight pence to anybody,**" he said. "**I am losing it. A man does not get to have a loss and call it nothing on a piece of paper, and I have watched a man in this harbor do that about a matter of eleven pounds and I am not going to be him.**"

Nessa Pike read the line out both ways, the wrong one and the right one, and asked him which of the two he wanted, and he said both, and she put both on with the hour of the correction, and Calder Marr read the whole of the fifteenth line out again in that shed with the correction in it and nobody scratched anything out.

And a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay, who keeps a whole fouling ladder in his own book and gave his name to nobody and was not asked anything in that shed, said one sentence from the back of it, and it went into the second column and nowhere else, and he was not asked a question and his name is not in that book and is not going to be.

"**A man who will not go on a slip for three weeks for a thing he does not agree with is the only one of you I would have believed, and he went, and I have got no way of saying that to him without it meaning something else, so I am not going to say it to him.**"

Nobody said it to him and nobody told him he had said it, and he has not been asked one word and is not going to be.

And there were six things that happened after four o'clock in the morning and they are not on the sheet, and Calder Marr said so out loud, and this is what he said and nobody improved on it.

"**Six things happened after that line and I have not written them on it and I am not going to. The third rule at the head of that sheet is that it stops when somebody says it stops and not one line after, and a rule that is bent the first time it is inconvenient is not a rule, and I would rather have a sheet of forty lines that ended at four o'clock than forty-four lines that somebody could say afterwards were put in after the man who wrote the fortieth had gone to bed. What they were is in the first column of that yard's own book, which is a different book with a different rule in it, and the two books have never been the same and this is the first time anybody has found out that they are not.**"

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the thirtieth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said sheet of forty lines was read back in full in that shed on the morning of the said day from about ten o'clock, in the presence of the ten men who had gone out and the one man who had not, and that the rule of the first day of May past was read out first, being that a man of this yard in about forty people's hearing names the price of a thing and is not to be asked to make it nicer, and that any man present might stop the reading on any line, and that a correction goes on beside the wrong figure with the hour of it against it and the wrong one is not scratched out; and that the copy read on the said morning is the yard's own and is in a drawer with the charter's place empty, and that the four other copies were written out on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth and one of them went up that coast on the same afternoon on a boat going for coal; and that one correction was made, on the fifteenth line, at the asking of the man whose name is on it, being that a price entered as *nothing* was entered as the hire of a slip at the head of that mole at three shillings a week for the three weeks from the fourth of June to the twenty-sixth, being one hundred and eight pence, and that the man said that a man does not get to have a loss and call it nothing on a piece of paper; and that no other line was touched at the reading and no other line is touched anywhere; and that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay said one sentence from the back of that shed and it went into the second column and nowhere else, and was not asked a question, and his name is not in that book and is not going to be.*

*And that six things happened after four o'clock in the morning of the twenty-seventh and are not on the said sheet and were not written on it, and that the holder of that writ said out loud that the third rule at the head of that sheet is that it stops when somebody says it stops and not one line after, and that a rule bent the first time it is inconvenient is not a rule, and that he would rather have a sheet of forty lines that ended at four o'clock than forty-four lines a man could say afterwards were put in after the man who wrote the fortieth had gone to bed; and that what those six things were is in the first column of that yard's own book, which is a different book with a different rule in it, and that the two books have never been the same and that the said day was the first time anybody found out they were not.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay, who keeps a whole fouling ladder in his own book in his own hand and gave his name to nobody and was not asked anything in that shed, said that a man who will not go on a slip for three weeks for a thing he does not agree with is the only one of the eleven he would have believed, and that the man went, and that he had no way of saying that to him without it meaning something else, and so was not going to say it to him; and that nobody said it to him and nobody told him he had said it and he has not been asked one word and is not going to be.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-first line:*

*What it is not: — that a sheet of forty lines is not a roll and is not a muster and is not a list of a crew of anybody, and the eleven men on it are eleven men and not eleven boats of a navy and not eleven boats of anybody's; and that a correction written beside a wrong figure is not an erasure and is not a thing anybody is going to find in a year and have to guess at, and a page that can be checked wrong is worth more than a page that cannot be checked at all; and that a book that stops at four o'clock and does not get the six things that came after it is not an incomplete book and is not a failure of a record, and that two books in one shed with two different rules in them is not a system and is not anybody's design and is not going to be described as one by anybody in this harbor.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and the sixtieth charge falls on Thursday the first of July next. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-four days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fiftieth night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor have no boat and one of them is going to lose a berth and there is no figure anywhere in this harbor for what a berth is worth. A sheet of forty lines with a name and an hour and a price and a payer against every one of them went up that coast on the twenty-eighth in the coal of an ordinary boat, was put on a market wall in a town four days up that coast on the evening of the second of July past by whoever put it up, and this yard does not know who and did not write to ask and is not going to. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the sixteenth day of the ninety, and nobody in this country has won anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
