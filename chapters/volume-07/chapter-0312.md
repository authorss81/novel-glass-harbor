# Chapter 312

*Wednesday the Twenty-Sixth of May, Nine People in a Fish Market, a Man of About Forty-Three Reading the Fourth Line Off His Own Form Out Loud, a Man of About Fifty Who Is Not Going to Choose and Says So, and a Yard That Does Not Advise*

Wednesday the twenty-sixth of May, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it. And the far bench was not occupied on the said Wednesday at any hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The man of about forty-three of the second lay had a form in the inside of his coat and he had been carrying it since Monday and he had not been to a room with it and he had not been out past that mole-head with it.

He was in the fish market from about ten until about eleven and he read the fourth line off his own form out loud twice, in about nine people's hearing, to nobody in particular, and then he put it on the end stall's table and asked the man of about fifty whether anybody in this harbor was going to tell him what to put in the box.

The fourth line, as it is on the form, reads:

*Whether she carries material which is the subject of an unsettled claim.*

The man of about fifty of the second lay has a coaster of about seventy tons alongside and two men in her and a cargo of salt out of a yard up that coast, and on Tuesday the eighteenth of May he carried a properly made declaration to the harbor authority on the west basin in a coat and a clerk of about sixty told him he could not put it in a book, and he went out at eleven without it and was not asked for it and was not stopped and came back in at six and took his berth.

He had had eight days to think about it and he had thought about it every one of them, which he said out loud, and about nine people heard him say it.

"**I have got two boxes on that sheet,**" he said. "**One for the name of the boat and one for her master, and one for what she is carrying in general words, and that last one I can do, and I did it on Monday at a desk in Ternwatch with two men standing by me putting their names under mine, and one of them owes my wife's brother a shilling and he did not do it to oblige me, he did it because he did not read the sheet.**"

He put his thumb on the form.

"**And there is a fourth one, and the fourth one is a yes or a no, and there is no third thing to write, and I have been sitting in my own kitchen in front of that sheet since Monday night with the ink out and I have written nothing.**"

"**Then write a no,**" said the man of about forty-three.

"**A yes is a handing over and a no is a lie,**" said the man of about fifty.

Nobody had counted that. Then the woman of about fifty-eight at the end stall counted it, because she counts, because it is a trade she has had for thirty years and she counts out loud.

"**That is twelve words,**" she said, "**and I have not got a figure for what a yes is worth and I have got one for what a candle is, and I would rather somebody read me the fourth line than tell me a candle is fourpence again.**"

---

"**Say the rest of it,**" said the man of about forty-three.

"**A yes is a handing over and a no is a lie,**" said the man of about fifty, "**and that is the whole of it, and I have had eight days and I have had about forty men in this market ask me since Tuesday and I have not said it to any of them until now, and I am saying it to you because you have the sheet in front of you and you can see that it is not me who has written the words.**"

He took his hand off the form.

"**If I write yes, then I have handed a person to an office, and I do not know which person, and neither will the man who reads it, because there is no man in an office that will read it, and a thing that is handed to nobody is not a thing that has been given up, it is a thing that has been said out loud in a market by me about my own cargo, in a harbor of about forty people, and there is a Crown chart of about nine years standing in this harbor that does not have a can on a stake on it, and I have not asked anybody about that and I am not going to.**

"**And if I write no, then I have written a false thing on a form made out at a desk in Ternwatch, and the only way I can find out whether it is false is by asking somebody, and the only people who could tell me are the people who would be given the answer by my writing it, and I am not going to do that either.**"

"**Then what are you going to do,**" said the man of about forty-three.

"**I am going to not write anything,**" said the man of about fifty, "**and I am going to go out on Friday morning with the sheet in my coat with the box empty, and that is not a plan, that is the absence of one, and I am a man of fifty with a crew of two and a season and a house, and I have been at this eight days and I have got nothing.**"

---

The man of about forty-three turned round then, in about nine people's hearing, and asked the man of about thirty-two who was standing at the end of the aisle with his hands behind his back.

"**You hired a boat off that mole on the nineteenth and you had three soundings taken off the south end inside your own harbor line,**" he said. "**You have been in that room and you have got a yard. Have you an opinion about a form.**"

Nobody in that market said anything. The woman of about fifty-eight put her hand flat on the slate at the back of her board.

"**I have an opinion about everything,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to give it to you, and I am going to say the reason out loud so that nobody in this market has to guess at it later.**

"**A party with no instrument may still be hired by a private person for a private purpose, and I have a rule in this yard about that and it is not new. And that rule is not what stops me here. What stops me here is a rule I made on the first of May and I have never improved on it and I am not going to improve on it for a form. I do not draft, I do not copy, I do not witness, I do not countersign, and I do not advise. That is five things and they have all been true since the first of May and none of them is about a harbor authority and all of them are about a man standing in a market with a sheet in front of him asking a stranger which way to tick a box.**"

"**You could just say which way you would tick it.**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I want to be exact about why, because I have said the word *no* to about nine people in this market in eight days and this is the first of them that has been a fair question. If I tell you which way to tick that box, then in about four years there is a page somewhere with a man's name against it and the page says a man of this yard advised a master of a coaster of about seventy tons on the fourth line of a naval declaration, and whether I was right or wrong will not matter, because the page will be the thing anybody reads. A man in this country who has no paper has one advantage and it is the only one he has got, and it is that nothing anybody does for him is a party to anything, and I am not going to give that up for a box in a market.**"

"**Then I am asking a shopkeeper.**"

"**Then you are asking a shopkeeper,**" said Calder Marr, "**and the shopkeeper is about fifty-eight and she has told you the price of a candle and she has told you the price of a sheet of paper, and neither of those is an answer to your question and I would not expect it to be.**"

The man of about forty-three stood in that market with the form in his hand for about another minute and then he put it back in his coat, and he said out loud, so that about nine people heard it, that he was not going to be the man who read a line off somebody else's sheet in a fish market, and he went out of the market, and he is not going to be asked again by anybody in this yard.

---

Afterward the man of about fifty said one more thing, to nobody, at the end of the aisle, and Nessa Pike wrote it down and it went in the second column.

"**On Tuesday the eighteenth I went out with no paper and I came back at six and nothing happened, and I have had eight days to work out what that was, and what it was, is that nothing happened. A Tuesday on which nothing happens is a Tuesday, and it does not go round again, and I am fifty-one next year and I am not going to find out a second time.**"

He did not say his age. He is fifty and he is going to be fifty-one and he did not say either number, and the man of about forty-three heard him say it and did not write it down.

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-sixth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of about forty-three of the second lay of this harbor carried a declaration form made out at a desk in Ternwatch in the inside of his coat from the Monday the twenty-fourth of May past to the said day and had not been out past the mole-head of this harbor with it; and that on the said day at about ten in the morning in the fish market of this harbor he read the fourth line of the said form out loud twice in about nine people's hearing, being: *whether she carries material which is the subject of an unsettled claim*, and put the said form on the table of the end stall of the said market and asked a man of about fifty of the second lay what to put in the box.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about fifty of the second lay, who has a coaster of about seventy tons alongside that harbor and two men in her and a cargo of salt out of a yard up that coast, and who on the eighteenth day of May past carried a declaration to the harbor authority on the west basin and was refused a lodging of it and went out at about eleven without it and came back in at about six and took her berth, said out loud in that market in about nine people's hearing on the said day, being: *a yes is a handing over and a no is a lie*; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall of that market counted the said sentence out loud at twelve words and said that she had not got a figure for what a yes was worth and had got one for what a candle was.*

*And that the said man of about fifty said out loud in the same hearing that he had had the form since the Monday night preceding and had written nothing in the box and would write nothing in the box, and gave two reasons out loud, being that a *yes* hands a person to an office where there is no person to read it and would be a thing said out loud in a market by a man about his own cargo, and that a *no* is a false thing on a form and that the only way to find out whether it is false is to ask the people who would be given the answer by his writing it; and that he said he was going out on the Friday morning following with the box empty and said out loud that that is the absence of a plan and not a plan.*

*And that a man of about forty-three of that lay asked the holder of that writ in that market in about nine people's hearing for an opinion about a form, and was refused, and the reason was given out loud in five parts, being that this yard does not draft and does not copy and does not witness and does not countersign and does not advise, and that those five things have been true since the first day of May and none of them is about a harbor authority and all of them are about a man standing in a market asking a stranger which way to tick a box; and that the holder of that writ said out loud that a party with no instrument has one advantage, which is that nothing anybody does for it is a party to anything, and that he was not going to give that up for a box in a market; and that no person of that yard drafted, copied, witnessed, countersigned or advised on any declaration for any vessel on the said day or on any other day of its life.*

*And that the man of about fifty said out loud afterward at the end of that aisle that on the eighteenth of May he went out with no paper and came back at six and nothing happened, and that a Tuesday on which nothing happens is a Tuesday and does not go round again.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a form with an empty box in it is not an obstruction and is not a breach and is not a refusal and is not going to be one; that a *yes* and a *no* are not two answers to one question but one answer and its own opposite, and that the whole of the difference between a man who is honest and a man who is careful is which of the two he can live with; and that a party with no paper in this country has one advantage and this yard has just said that advantage out loud in a market in about nine people's hearing and has not given it up and is not going to.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-nine days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fifteenth night, and neither of them has moved. A form made out at a desk in Ternwatch went into a market in about nine people's hearing with the fourth box in it empty, and this yard was asked in that market which way to tick it and said no in about nine people's hearing and gave five reasons, and a sheet of paper is a penny and forty sheets are a shilling and that figure has not moved in nine years and did not move today.**

**And there were nine boats of the herring third lay lying off that mole this evening where there were eleven on Monday, and a man of about fifty has a form in his coat with a box in it that is not filled and is not going to be filled by anybody in this yard. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
