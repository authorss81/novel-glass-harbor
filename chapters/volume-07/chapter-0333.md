# Chapter 333

*Thursday the Seventeenth of June, the Fifty-Sixth Charge Walked on None, Six Men of a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Gone Out and Back With Nothing Found, a Man of Sixty-One on a Crown Asked and Refusing Again, and an Offer Made on a Quay in About Nine People's Hearing*

Thursday the seventeenth of June, on the quay below that mole in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-sixth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Thursday and no sitting was held in that room on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at that bench was asked for nothing and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that shed on the said morning and going nowhere and of no office and not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, and he was not sent for and was not asked and is not going to be thanked.**

---

They went out at half past eight in the morning, six men in a boat of about nineteen tons called the *Osprey*, of that harbor's own water, with the six of them her owners and nobody else her owner and no second boat in the harbor with any of them in it.

A man of about fifty-one of the six stood on the quay and said the whole of it out loud, in about nine people's hearing, before he got in, because he is a man who has been on this water thirty-four years and says a thing once.

"**We are going out to the bar and no further, and I am going to say why in the open, because nobody is going to be able to say afterwards that we did not know.**" He put his hand flat on the mooring ring. "**A boat of about seventy tons of this harbor went out at ten past to seven yesterday morning past that line, and she has not come back, and there are three men in her. And there is a notice on the market wall that says no vessel shall leave, or pass a line, without a form received by an office that issued nothing after the thirty-first of March, and we have no form, and we are not going to get one, and I am not going to stand here and ask any of you to tell me that a notice can be gone round. We cannot go past that line. That is the first fact and it is the whole of the first fact.**"

"**The second,**" said a man of about thirty-two of the six.

"**The second is that we are nineteen tons and we have six of us and there is no second boat, and the bar is four hundred yards off the outer marks and it has not been narrowed by anything since the seventeenth of May past, and outside the bar there is a channel we have no business in. So we are going to look in our own water, from the mole to the bar, for anything of hers that comes back in, and for anything of anybody's that is in it. And the ebb will be at the low water about two o'clock and it will make again after that for about two hours, and after that we are on the last of it. If we are not on this quay at half past four we are a boat of nineteen tons with six men on the ebb past the outer marks in a sea that has got up, and I have done that once and I am not doing it twice for a boat of seventy tons that a man of about fifty has a paper for.**"

He got in. They were gone by ten past nine and they were back on the quay at about five in the afternoon, and they had found nothing, and a man of about thirty-eight said so out loud, once, with his hand on the gunwale, and nobody asked him a second question about it.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon a man of about forty-four of the six went up the crown of that mole, where a man of sixty-one was working on eleven pieces of a boat with a boat's hook in his left hand and his right hand bandaged, and Calder Marr was standing at the far end of that crown with a book under his arm and did not move and did not go over and did not go to the shed.

The man of forty-four asked him for a thing and it took a long time to say and it was not said well.

"**My father was in your yard in nineteen hundred and some,**" he said. "**I am not asking you about him. I am asking whether you would take the *Osprey* out with us in the morning, because there is no second boat and you built a boat of that size and you are the only man on this mole who has, and I know what I am asking. I know you have got a hand. I have watched you work with it. I am asking anyway because we have got no other man to ask and because I would rather be refused out loud than not ask.**"

"**No,**" said Tobias Wren.

He did not stop working. He put the hook down flat on the piece of strake he was trimming and turned round about half of himself and said the rest of it in about nine people's hearing, several of whom were forty yards off on the parapet and one of whom was holding a book.

"**I have a right hand that cannot hold a tiller, and I am sixty-one, and I am not going to find that out in front of six men on the ebb off that mole.**"

The man of about forty-four stood there for a moment and then said, "**Aye,**", and went down the steps, and nobody asked Tobias Wren one word about it, and nobody asked him a second time, and nobody thanked him for refusing and he did not say he wanted to be thanked and he was not going to be thanked, and he was not sent for and was not made a door and was not made a sacrifice and was not made a teacher, and he is not this yard's injury.

Calder Marr stood at the far end of that crown with a book under his arm for the whole of it and watched a man refuse and did not go over, and did not go to the shed, and put the refusal down in nothing at all in his own head, and has not since.

---

The six came in at about five, and at about ten past five, on the quay, in about nine people's hearing, a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay said a thing.

He had a hand lead in his hand and he had been standing at the top of the slip with it since the six were in sight, and he had not come up the hill and nobody had gone down to get him.

"**I am going to say one thing and then I am going home,**" he said, "**and I am not going to say it twice and I am not going to say it in a room, and if anybody wants to make a thing of it afterwards that is their trade and not mine.**"

Nine people stopped.

"**I keep a book. It is mine. It is not that yard's and it is not anybody's, and I have never shown it to anybody and I am not going to show it to anybody.**" He shifted the lead to his other hand. "**In it there is the fouling of that mark on the face of that seam. What height the water stood at that stone, on a date. What height it stood at that stone, on another date. Going back a good number of years, and it is mine because I have kept it since I was twenty-two, and I am not telling you why.**"

"**That is a tide table,**" said a man of about forty-four.

"**It is not a tide table and I am not going to say what it is, and I am not going to tell you whether I have ever used it and I am not going to tell you what I think it is for.**" He looked at the six men standing in their wet oilskins. "**What I will say is what a man can do with it, and it is one sentence and then I am going. A man can take a figure out of that book and an hour off a clock and know how much water is over any ground inside this harbor without going and standing on the ground. That is all it is. It is not a chart. It is not a bottom. It does not tell you where anything is and it will not tell you where anything has been. It tells you how much water you have, and where you have got that, you can go and find out for yourself with a lead and a line, and it takes a morning to do.**"

Nobody asked him one word.

Calder Marr came down the steps then, and stopped, and gave the reason out loud before he said anything else, in about nine people's hearing.

"**I am not going to ask you one question,**" he said, "**and I am going to say the reason out loud because you have just given something away in front of nine people and you are owed that much. The reason is the first day of May past and it is the same reason and it is not a new one, and it is that this yard does not ask a man of this harbor anything, and a yard that asks is a yard that has begun to need him, and a man who gives his name to nobody is a man who has done that on purpose.**"

"**I have,**" said the man of about thirty-four, "**and I am not giving it to you.**"

"**I know you are not, and I am not going to have it written down, and if it is written anywhere in that shed tonight it will not be in that book, and I have told the ship's hand so.**"

Then he said the other thing, and he said it to the six men and not to the man with the lead.

"**The offer was made at ten past five this evening, on a quay, in about nine people's hearing, and not in a room, and this yard has taken it and has asked for nothing and knows nothing. I am not going to say that I am going to do anything with it tonight, and I am not going to say that I will not.**"

The man of about fifty-one of the six said the thing that decided the morning, and he said it once.

"**We go out again in the morning. If a man of that yard puts a hand in that water at half past six and gives us a figure, then we will be in water we know the depth of at the bar and off the foul at the south end, and we will be in and out on the flood. And if nobody does, we go out with a hand lead and a good hand and two hours on the ebb, and that is what we have done for nineteen years, and it has killed nobody yet, and that is not a thing anybody should say out loud, and I have said it.**"

---

In the shed that night the woman of no office asked the question and got a true answer again, and this time the true answer was going to be false by nine o'clock in the morning.

"**What are you going to do with it.**"

"**Nothing,**" said Calder Marr. "**There is nothing to do with it tonight. It is a figure in a man's book and a morning's work and a lead, and there is no work in this shed for it and no money in this bag for it and no boat in this harbor that this yard may put anybody in on its own account.**"

"**You had that answer ready.**"

"**I had it ready and it was true at ten o'clock last night, and I have said to that man in about nine people's hearing that I would not say tonight whether I was going to do anything, and I have kept to that, and I am not going to say tonight, and tomorrow at half past six in the morning I am going to be standing on that mole with a hand lead and a line and Nessa Pike, and I am not going to be able to say on Friday morning why I said it on Thursday, and I have got a night, and a night is what a day is.**"

Nessa Pike wrote it down and did not improve on it, and then she wrote one line more and read it out, because she reads out anything she is not sure of.

"**That is the second time a man of sixty-one has refused this yard something in front of people this month, and the two refusals are not the same refusal, and this yard has not asked him for either, and I want the two of them kept apart in this book and not run together, because the man at that bench across that crown is a different man and he is not going to be in this book at all.**"

"**He is not going to be in this book at all,**" said Calder Marr, "**and neither am I, and the reason is not mine and it is older than this month and I am not going to improve on it.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the seventeenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that six men of that harbor's own water, being the owners of a boat of about nineteen tons called the Osprey, went out in her at about ten past nine in the morning of the said day to the bar and no further and came back on the quay at about five in the afternoon of the said day and found nothing, and that a man of about fifty-one of the six said out loud on that quay in about nine people's hearing that they could not pass the line named in the fifth clause, and that the bar is four hundred yards off the outer marks and stands and has not been narrowed by anything, and that the ebb would be at the low water about two o'clock and would make again for about two hours, and that a boat of nineteen tons with six men on it past the outer marks on the ebb after half past four is a thing he has done once and is not going to do twice.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about sixty-one on the crown of that mole was asked at about half past three in the afternoon of the said day by a man of about forty-four of the six, on his own account and not sent by anybody, to take that boat out with them, and that he said no, and that he said one further thing, being that he has a right hand that cannot hold a tiller and is sixty-one and is not going to find that out in front of six men on the ebb off that mole; and that nobody asked him one word about it and nobody asked him a second time and nobody thanked him and he was not sent for and is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice and is not this yard's injury; and that the holder of that writ was on that crown at the far end of it at the hour of it and did not go over to him and did not leave the crown, and that this refusal is not the refusal of the thirteenth day of June and is a different thing and the two are kept apart in this book.*

*And that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay said out loud on that quay at about ten past five in the afternoon of the said day, in about nine people's hearing and not in a room, that he keeps a book of his own in which there is the fouling of the mark on the face of the second seam and the height of the water at that stone on a number of dates, and that he did not say what the book is, or why he keeps it, or whether he has ever used it; and that he said one sentence about what a man can do with it, being that a man can take a figure out of that book and an hour off a clock and know how much water is over any ground inside that harbor without going and standing on the ground, and that it is not a chart and not a bottom and does not tell a man where anything is or has been; and that nobody asked him one word; and that the holder of that writ gave the reason of the first day of May past out loud before anything else, being that this yard does not ask a man of this harbor anything and a yard that asks is a yard that has begun to need him; and that the said man said he had not given his name and was not going to, and that the holder of that writ said he was not going to have it written down and told the ship's hand so.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that a man who refuses to take a boat out is not a man who could not, and is not a coward and is not a door and is not a sacrifice and is not owed anything by anybody for refusing; that an offer made on a quay in nine people's hearing is not an agreement and is not a contract and is not a custody and is not evidence of anything about the man who made it; and that a figure in a man's own book that will tell a man how much water he has is not a chart and does not tell him where anything is, and this yard has not taken it and has copied nothing out of it and has asked nothing about it.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down. The cistern was charged and walked on none, being the fifty-sixth. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-one days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-seventh night, and neither of them has moved. The mark of the second seam stands fouled a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, and it was not looked at by anybody of that yard on the said day. Six men of that harbor have been out and come back with nothing and are going out again in the morning. A man of sixty-one was asked to take a boat out and said no and was asked nothing and was not thanked. A man of about thirty-four gave one sentence on a quay and was asked nothing and has not given his name to anybody and is not going to. This yard has a morning and no money, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and this yard has not hired anybody and is not going to, and a party with no instrument cannot hire a rescue.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
