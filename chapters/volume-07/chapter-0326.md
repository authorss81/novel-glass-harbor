# Chapter 326

*Thursday the Tenth of June, the Fifty-Fourth Charge Walked on None, the Fourth Book Opened, Four Books Holding One Water, and a Man of Sixty-One Looked At and Not Asked*

Thursday the tenth of June, on the crown of that mole in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-fourth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why.**

---

Calder Marr went up to the crown of that mole on the tenth of June at about eleven in the morning to look at the two objects of nobody's, because he looks at them most days without touching them, and the man of sixty-one was up there working on the eleven pieces of his boat with his bandaged right hand and a boat's hook in his left.

Tobias Wren is sixty-one in these chapters, hurt on the eighth of May past across a sheet of plate glass, bandaged in the right hand until the second of June, back on the water since the second, in eleven pieces of a boat on that crown, not thanked and not going to be, not sent for, not met, not asked good morning, and not made a teacher and not made a door and not made a sacrifice.

Calder Marr stood about thirty feet from him and looked at him for about a minute, the way any man on a crown looks at another man working, and the man looked up once and nodded the way men nod who have worked the same water forty years and have nothing to say to each other, and neither brother said why.

He may be looked at and he may not be asked. He asks nothing. He is not sent for. He is not thanked. He is not a teacher, not a door and not a sacrifice, and that hold is not improved on.

"He was up there," said Calder Marr afterwards, in the shed. "Working. With the hook in his left. I looked at him for about a minute and I did not go over. Put that down as what it is."

Nessa Pike put it down: that the holder of that writ looked at a man of sixty-one at a far bench for about a minute on the tenth of June and did not go over to him, and that the man asked nothing and was asked nothing.

---

In the afternoon, in the shed, they opened the fourth book past the first page, being the stitched book with no vessel's name on the cover, in three hands.

It holds the same water. Under a date twenty-seven years past, in a cramped hand, stands *Skorr Depe, eyght fathoms and three quarters, foule to the easte*, and under a date nine years past, in a flowing hand, *Skor Deep, nine fadome, foul ground E.*, and on the last page, in a hand that leans backwards, *the depe off the Skore, nine fathoms, keep her full and by*. Six spellings now, seven across the four books, four hands in this book alone counting the cover, four dates spread over eighteen years, the same sounding under all of them, and figures that do not agree by a quarter of a fathom at the closest and three quarters at the widest.

Nessa Pike wrote all of it down in the second column, with every spelling exactly as it stands, and every figure, and every date, and under them the same line as on the eighth and the ninth: that a line of figures in four books identifies a difference in four books and not a truth about a sea.

"Four books," she said, when it was done. "Seven spellings. Four dates. One water. It is a sequence of figures that several men of this trade put in several books at several different times, with different spellings, in different hands, and with different names for the same soundings. It is a labor fact."

"It is," said Calder Marr. "And no current chart includes it, and the chart that omits it is a Crown chart of about nine years standing that this harbor holds two copies of, and this yard has held neither copy. That is the route as it stands in each book, with its spellings and its dates. It crosses Bramble Strait. Its soundings are of a coast that has moved. Its usefulness is a labor fact and not a memory."

He sat for a while looking at the four books lying open on the bench, and the woman of no office mended a second cuff and said nothing, because there was nothing in the room that wanted saying.

"This yard has always said that a record a man cannot check is worth less than one he can," said Calder Marr at last. "A route remembered across several vessels is checkable by going there. That is what we have said. Write that down, because these ten days are going to hand us the best record we have ever been offered, and the offer is only good if we stop checking it, and I want it on the page that we knew what we were saying before it was turned."

She wrote that down too, in the second column, with no name against it.

The woman of no office looked up from her mending and asked the one question anybody asked that evening, and she asked it once.

"Four books holding one water," she said. "Seven spellings. If a sixth book came into this shed tomorrow with an eighth spelling, would you open it?"

"Yes," said Calder Marr. "If it came the way these came, with its owner carrying it. The man of about thirty-six carries his father's book himself. That is why the eleventh of June matters. A book carried by its owner into a market is a book nobody takes. This yard opens what it owns and looks at what it is shown and holds nothing that is not its own. That is the whole of the custody of it."

"Then the stitched book is the oldest and the newest at once," said Nessa Pike, turning its pages without touching the writing. "Twenty-seven years past in one hand and nine years past in another, and no vessel's name on the cover. Three hands and none of them alike. It held the same water before any of us were at sea, and it holds it still, and it agrees with nothing to the quarter of a fathom."

---

That evening a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay went past the shed going down to the water with a lead line over his shoulder, and he gave his name to nobody, as always, and nobody asked him for it.

He keeps the whole fouling ladder for the mark on the face of the second seam in his own book — four inches on the twelfth and the twenty-third of March past, about a foot on the twenty-sixth, a foot and a half on the fourth and the thirteenth of May, and a foot and seven inches on the nineteenth — and nobody is going to explain it. He is not Garrow Fisk, who is a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch, and the two are not one man and may not be run together. He came out in the hire of the nineteenth of May and cost that bag nothing beyond the hire, and he has come out in no boat since, and that ladder was not advanced in the last ten days and was not advanced on this day, because nobody of that yard was in the water.

"He is going down to look at nothing," said Nessa Pike, watching him go. "Or to look at something that is not ours to look at."

"He is going down," said Calder Marr. "Put him down as going down, and put down that the mark was not looked at by any person of this yard on this day, and that the ladder stands where it stood on the nineteenth of May, fouled about a foot and seven inches, and that nobody is going to explain it."

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the tenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning of the said day and this party walked none of it, being the fifty-fourth charge walked on none of since the third day of August past; and that the stitched book with no vessel's name on the cover, in three hands, was opened past the first page on the said day, holding Skorr Depe, Skor Deep and the depe off the Skore, eight and three quarters to nine fathoms, dated twenty-seven and nine years past; so that four old vessel books in that shed now stand open past the first page, holding one water under seven spellings, in seven hands, across eighteen years, and no current chart this harbor carries includes the said water as a route.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ looked at a man of sixty-one at a far bench for about a minute on the said morning and did not go over to him, and that the said man asked nothing and was asked nothing, and is not sent for and is not thanked and is not made a teacher and is not made a door; and that this yard has always said that a route remembered across several vessels is checkable by going there and that a record nobody can check is worth less than one a man can.*

*And that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay, who keeps the whole fouling ladder of the second seam in his own book and gave his name to nobody, went past that shed on the said evening with a lead line, and that the said mark was not looked at by any person of this yard on this day and stands fouled about a foot and seven inches where it was on the nineteenth of May, and that he is not Garrow Fisk of a pressure house at Ternwatch; and that this yard did not go back to the copyist's counter in the room off the fish market on the said day and did not send anybody there.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-first line:*

*What it is not: — that four books holding one water are not one book agreeing with itself and not a sea commanded; that a man looked at for a minute is not a man asked and not a man thanked; and that no stage has been taken in this shed, and the Storm-Reader stage is taken in shared work with a crew at a cost and not before, and not as a solution to a problem of police or price.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was charged for the fifty-fourth and walked on none of it. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-four days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirtieth night, and neither of them has moved, and the mark on the face of the second seam stands fouled about a foot and seven inches where it was on the nineteenth of May and was not looked at by any person of this yard.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
