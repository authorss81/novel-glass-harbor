# Chapter 311

*Tuesday the Twenty-Fifth of May, the Thirty-First Week With No Envelope, a Man of About Sixty of the First Lay Back Into a Shop He Said He Would Come Back Into, Sixteen Words He Counted on Friday and Twenty-One He Counted This Morning, and a Restriction That Has Arrived in a Sentence*

Tuesday the twenty-fifth of May, at a chandler's on the front street in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and thirty-one weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, the thirty-first of them being this day. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

He had said on Friday the twenty-first that he would come back into that shop on Monday, and Monday was yesterday, and he had not been in that shop yesterday, because yesterday at about half past five in the afternoon he had been standing on the stone at the top of that mole's steps in front of about nine people saying that eleven people on this coast cannot read a notice.

He came in at about ten on the morning of the twenty-fifth and he stayed until about one.

He put the two candles on the counter. He had not lit either of them. They were fourpence each and they had been in his coat since Friday and they had not got any smaller.

"**You said Monday,**" said the chandler, who is about fifty and has been thirty-one years in the front street and who is not a keeper of anything and has not been thanked for nine words.

"**I did say Monday,**" said the man of about sixty, "**and Monday came and I was on your mole steps at half past five and I did not come in here, and that is not a trick and it is not me being clever. I stood on those steps and I looked down at this shop and I could not make myself come in here on that afternoon, because on that afternoon I had a thing in my mouth that was worth more than being in here, and I would like that written down about me by somebody, because I have not been in a shop and said anything for forty-one years that I meant to keep.**"

Nobody wrote it down. The chandler said so.

"**Then write it down yourself,**" said the chandler.

"**I have never written one of these down in my life,**" said the man of about sixty, "**and I am not starting on a Tuesday morning.**"

---

The chandler's is eleven feet wide and it has a counter and a floor of boards and a smell of tallow and rope and wet wool, and on the morning of the twenty-fifth there were about nine people in it, which is a good morning for that shop, and the man of about sixty knew it was a good morning for that shop before he came in, which is how a man of forty-one years on a water can count a room.

He had the three things with him still. He put them on the counter in the order he had put them there on Friday, and nobody could see what they were, because they were three sentences and not three objects.

"**On Friday I said in this shop that I had three things and that I was not going to say the thing that holds the three of them together in a shop,**" he said. "**I have come back on the twenty-fifth to say it, and I have come back in about nine people's hearing on purpose, and I want to say the next part before anybody in this room works out that I have done it on purpose.**"

"**Go on,**" said a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime over his shoulder.

"**A restriction that arrives in a sentence and not in a boat is a thing a man cannot be angry at,**" said the man of about sixty.

He put one finger on the counter and he did not take it off.

"**That is twenty-one words and I counted them on the way up the hill this morning and I have counted them four times since, and I have never in my life counted a sentence of my own, and I am telling this room I counted it because in about four days somebody is going to say that I was careful with it and I was not, I was the opposite of careful with it, and I would like the twenty-one to be on a person other than me.**"

"**Then where does it go,**" said the man with the hod.

"**And a man who cannot be angry at it will be angry at something else by Friday.**" He took the finger off the counter. "**That one I have not got down to a number and I am not going to. That one is the reason for the twenty-one.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about a minute, which in that shop is a long time, and then a woman of about forty-five who had come in for a fathom of rope said:

"**You told us on Friday that a sentence said in a chandler's on a Friday morning is in eleven markets by Sunday.**"

"**I did.**

"**That is sixteen words.**"

"**It is sixteen words,**" said the man of about sixty, "**and I counted that one too, on Friday night, in my own house, which is a thing I would not have done at forty and did at sixty-one because a man of sixty-one has nothing else to do with an evening.**"

The chandler put his two hands flat on his own counter, the way a man does when he is about to say a thing he has not said before.

"**Then you have just done it,**" he said. "**You have put it in a shop on a Tuesday morning with nine of my customers standing in it, and you have said yourself that it will be in eleven markets by Sunday, and I am the chandler you named on Friday as one of the three places a man in this harbor ought to be angry at, and I did not put your name on anything, and I would like that noticed.**"

"**I did notice it at about the fourth word,**" said the man of about sixty, "**and I did it anyway, and I am going to say why, and the why is the only reason it is worth doing. On Friday I said a sentence said in a shop comes back with a man's name on it that the man did not put there, and I have been three days thinking about whether that is a true thing or a coward's thing, and it is a true thing, and a true thing that only a man says to himself in his own house is worth nothing at all to anybody else. So I said it out loud. I said it in here. I did not name one person. And when it comes back round this coast with a name on it that I did not put there, it will be a name, and it will not be mine, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

"**And if it comes back with mine on it.**"

"**Then it will have been worth saying,**" said the man of about sixty, "**and I will not be able to tell you afterwards whether that was justice or whether I was simply the first man in this harbor with nothing to hit and a mouth on me.**"

The chandler looked at him for a while.

"**There is a man of about fifty-one in this harbor with a stove standing on his afterdeck,**" he said, "**and he has been going about since Sunday saying that he cannot get a berth. He was in here on Saturday and he bought nothing and he stood at my counter for a quarter of an hour and he did not buy a candle at fourpence out of a purse that is not mine to look into. I did not ask him what he had. And I am telling you that because you have told me you cannot tell afterwards which was which, and I cannot tell either, and I have watched it three times on this coast and I have never been able to tell afterwards, and that is what I have instead of a reason.**"

Nobody improved on it and it went in the second column, because nobody can check it and because a man said it out loud on a Tuesday morning in about nine people's hearing.

Then the man of about sixty bought nothing, and took his two unlit candles, and went down the front street and down to the water, and was not thanked.

---

Nobody in that yard went after him and nobody in that yard sent anybody, and the reason is the ninth day of April past and it is that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second, and it is the third time in seventeen days that the reason has been held and the third time it has cost somebody else and not this yard.

The man's forty-one years were on the water and the wage column did not move on the day he came back into the shop.

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-fifth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the said column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and twenty envelopes stand on that bench in that chest and did not move, and that thirty-one weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them.*

*And that a man of about sixty of the first lay of this harbor, forty-one years on that water, who asked a question in a market in this harbor on the second day of December past and again on the eleventh day of March past and was answered neither time, came into a chandler's on the front street of this harbor at about ten in the morning of the said day and stayed until about one, and said out loud in that shop in about nine people's hearing a sentence of twenty-one words, being: *a restriction that arrives in a sentence and not in a boat is a thing a man cannot be angry at*; and that he said out loud in the same hearing, and unnumbered, being: *and a man who cannot be angry at it will be angry at something else by Friday*; and that he said that he had counted the first of those in twenty-one words four times on the morning of the said day and had not counted the second.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the same man said out loud on the said day that on Friday the twenty-first of May he had named three honest targets for a coming anger in that same shop, being a man with a book, a chandler, and the harbor authority of this harbor, and had said that all three are wrong, and had stopped on purpose; and that on the said day he said that he had come back into that shop in about nine people's hearing on purpose, and that he had noticed in about the fourth word that he was doing the thing he had described on the Friday, and did it anyway, and gave the reason out loud, being that a true thing said only to a man in his own house is worth nothing to anybody else; and that he said that when the sentence came back round that coast with a name on it that he did not put there, it would be a name and not his, and that if it came back with his own name on it he would not be able to tell afterwards whether it was justice or whether he had been the first man in that harbor with nothing to hit and a mouth on him.*

*And that the said man said out loud that the sentence he had said in that shop on the twenty-first, being *a sentence said in a chandler's on a Friday morning is in eleven markets by Sunday*, is sixteen words, and that he had counted it in his own house on the Friday night; and that the chandler of the said shop, about fifty, thirty-one years in that street, said out loud in about nine people's hearing that the man of about fifty-one with a stove on his afterdeck had stood at his counter on the Saturday preceding for about a quarter of an hour and had not bought a candle at fourpence and that the chandler did not ask him what he had, and said that he had watched it happen three times on that coast and had never been able to tell afterwards which was which; and that neither man improved on it and that it went in that column because nobody can check it and because it was said out loud.*

*And that no person of that yard went down to the water on the said day, sent nobody after the said man, went and looked for nothing, and was told nothing that was not already in that shed's own book; and that the reason against the line of the sending is the reason of the ninth day of April past, and that this is the third time in seventeen days that reason has been held and that the third time it cost somebody else and not this yard.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a sentence said out loud in a shop in nine people's hearing is not a rule and is not a warning and is not a speech and is not the beginning of anything; that a man who counts his own words is not a man who has been careful with them, and he said so himself in that shop before anybody else worked out that he had said anything at all; and that a sentence which cannot be printed against a man because it names nobody is not therefore safe, and that this yard does not know that it is not safe and is not going to find out on his behalf.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. A wage of four marks was not paid on the said day and the thirty-first week has now fallen due with no envelope standing against it. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-eight days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fourteenth night, and neither of them has moved. A man of about sixty of the first lay went into a shop on the front street this morning and said twenty-one words he had counted four times and one he had not counted at all, and he took two unlit candles at fourpence out of that shop, and he was not thanked, and nobody in that yard sent anybody after him.**

**And a harbor was closed on paper on the seventeenth of May and it is the eighth day of it, and nothing has come into this yard in eight days and nothing has gone out of it in eight days, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one mark and eightpence, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and that bag cannot buy a day and has not tried. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
