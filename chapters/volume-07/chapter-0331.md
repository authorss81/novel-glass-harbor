# Chapter 331

*Tuesday the Fifteenth of June, a Wage Tuesday on Which the Column Did Not Move, Thirty-Four Weeks Standing With No Envelope, a Box in a Form That a Man Filled In Himself and Would Not Fill, and Six Men Told to Stand By on a Boat of About Nineteen Tons*

Tuesday the fifteenth of June, on the mole in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and twenty envelopes stand on that bench in that chest and did not move, and thirty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at that bench was asked for nothing and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn is in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, and her forty marks stand first after food and are neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, bandaged in the right hand, with a boat's hook in his left hand and eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked and not going to be, not sent for and not asked.**

---

The man of about fifty of the second lay came up that mole at about nine in the morning with his coat buttoned and his hat in his hand, and he came up to the shed and not to the slip, which is a thing he has not done before, and there were three of them in the shed and about nine on the crown above it.

He put the form on the bench, face up, and squared it with two fingers, and it was the same form and it was not the same form, because the fourth line had a box against it and the box was not filled and there was a name at the foot of the sheet in his own hand and a date against the name, and the date was the Monday.

"**I have signed it,**" he said. "**I have put the date to my name and I have not filled that box, and there is nothing in that box now and there was nothing in it on the eighteenth of May and there is nothing in it today, and I want the three of you to hear that from me before anybody else in this harbor says it with a different shape on it.**"

Nessa Pike wrote the day and the shape of it down in her own left hand and did not look up.

"**It is on a table in that office on the west basin,**" the man said, "**where it has been since Monday morning, and the clerk of that office is not holding it and is not going to hold it, and he told me so himself on the Monday in about four people's hearing and he did not ask me one question about it, and I did not ask him one, and that is the whole of what happened to that paper.**"

"**I am not going to ask you where it is,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to go and look at it.**"

"**I did not think you would, and I would have said so out loud if I thought you would, and you have made me not have to.**"

He put his hat back on and stood at the bench with a hand flat on it, the way a man does who is about to say the thing he has come to say.

"**I have asked a question in this harbor twice in five weeks and been answered once, and I am going to ask it a third time and then I am going to stop asking, because a man of sixty said in a chandler's that three is the correct number of times to ask a question in a harbor and I have thought about it since and he is right.**" He looked at Calder Marr. "**Is a form lying on a table in an office that has no season the same as a form that has been received.**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I am not going to tell you that it is, and I am not going to soften it, and I am going to give you the reason out loud before I give you the answer, because the reason is the whole of what I have.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Because if I tell you a paper on a table is the same as a paper received, then in about four years there is a page somewhere with a word of mine against it, and the page says a man of this yard told a master of a coaster of about seventy tons what his paper was, and whether I was right does not matter, because the page is the thing anybody reads. A man in this country who has no paper has one advantage and it is the only one he has got, and it is that nothing anybody does for him is a party to anything, and I am not going to give that up to be kind to a man on a Tuesday morning.**"

"**Then do not be kind to me,**" said the man of about fifty. "**I did not come up here for kindness. I came up here to say it out loud where three people and nine more can hear it, because I have found out in the last month what happens to a thing that is only said in a kitchen.**"

He said it on the crown above the shed, in about nine people's hearing, and nobody improved on it, and the man of about fifty went down the steps and across the quay and up the front street, and in the morning he was going out past that line with two men in her and a cargo of salt out of a yard up that coast, on a paper on a table, and this yard was not going to be asked about her and was not going to go down to the slip to see her go.

---

The hiding began that afternoon, in the ordinary way, with a true sentence.

The woman of no office had the bench and a cuff and a needle and about nine buttons in a dish, and she said the question herself, because she has asked one question a fortnight for a month and not one more, and she asked it in her own words and not anybody else's.

"**What is there in this week.**"

"**There is a form on a table in a building on the west basin that belongs to a man of about fifty and that this yard has not seen and is not going to see,**" said Calder Marr, "**and there is a wage Tuesday that has not moved a column since the fifth of March, and there is a coat in a room four streets off the front with a crate that has already gone, and there is nothing else in this week, and that is the whole of it and it is not going to change.**"

"**Right,**" she said, and went back to the cuff.

He did not know that on Thursday he would be standing in a market in front of nine people saying that the six men of a boat of about nineteen tons had what they needed. He did not know it on Tuesday and it was not a lie on Tuesday and he did not go back and correct it on Wednesday or Thursday, because there is no hour in that shed between the tide and the dark in which a man who is going into the water at half past six in the morning gathers nine people and holds a conversation, and because a day in that yard is a day that cannot be replaced, and because he is not going to be a man who says things in a shed in order to be able to say afterwards that he said them.

---

A man of about forty-four came up the steps at about two in the afternoon and said that the six had been told to stand by, and that the *Osprey* was the six of theirs and about nineteen tons, and that she was the only boat of that harbor that went out when a thing was in the water, and that there was no second boat.

"**No second boat,**" he said. "**We had two in April. The other one went for a bottom in the spring and the owner has her up on the hard with a green line round her moulds and he is a man of about fifty-five and he has said he is not going to move her, and he said it in May and he has not said it since because nobody has asked him again.**"

"**Then there is one,**" said Calder Marr.

"**There is one, and there are six of us, and we are not asking you for anything, and I want that said before anybody thinks it,**" said the man of about forty-four, "**because you have not hired us and you are not hiring us and you have no boat to hire us in.**"

"**I have not hired you and I am not going to,**" said Calder Marr, "**and the reason is a party with no instrument may still be hired by a private person for a private purpose, and a party with no instrument cannot hire anybody for a rescue, because a rescue is not a private purpose and there is no man in this harbor who is going to call it one.**"

The man of about forty-four nodded and went down, and at the bottom of the steps he turned round and said one more thing, and he said it to the steps and not to anybody.

"**There is one name in the six that nobody in this harbor has written down anywhere,**" he said. "**He is forty. He is the best hand off that quay and if a thing happens to any of us this week he is the one they will send for, and if he is not in any book then he is a man that can be lost twice.**"

"**What is his name.**"

"**He is here. He came up with me.**"

It was a man of about forty standing four steps below with his hands in his pockets, and he did not come up and he was not asked twice.

"**Rowan Teel,**" he said.

Nessa Pike wrote it down, in her own left hand, on the second column of that yard's book, with the day against it, and she read it back once so that a man in this harbor would know it had been written down, and nobody thanked him for saying it, and he did not ask to be thanked, and the ship's hand entered that the three nots standing against the eighth of June were not asked about on that day or after it, and that the boy who entered them is not on the stone and was put on nothing.

---

In the shed that night there were three of them and nobody comforted anybody, because there was nothing yet to be comforted or sorry about, and that is the state of a Tuesday.

"**The box is not filled,**" said Nessa Pike, writing. "**A name is signed to a form in a man's own hand and a date against the name, and the fourth line of that form has an empty box in it, and the paper is on a table in a building on the west basin and this yard has not seen it and is not going to.**"

"**And the boat goes out in the morning,**" said Calder Marr, "**under a paper that is on a table, and the notice says she may not leave, or pass a line, without a form received by an office that issued nothing after the thirty-first of March, and she is going to do it with a form on a table and the fourth box empty, and I am not going to advise him and I am not going to admire him and I have said out loud this afternoon that it is not the same thing and it is not.**"

"**Put the three facts in and leave the rest out,**" said the woman of no office from the bench, without looking up. "**He is going out, the box is not filled, and there are six men and one boat and no second. Those are three things that can be checked by anybody standing on that quay. Everything else anybody says about it is about him.**"

"**That goes in the first column and the rest goes nowhere,**" said Nessa Pike.

She wrote until her hand ached and then she held it in the other one for a while, the way she has done since February, and Calder Marr read the two entries over her shoulder and did not touch the book, and then he went out and stood at the shed door for a while and looked at the crown of that mole, where a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand was working on eleven pieces of a boat in the last of the light with a hook in his left.

"**He is up there every day of these ten,**" he said, "**and I am not going over, and I have not gone over, and neither brother has said why.**"

"**You have said why every morning for a month,**" said the woman of no office.

"**I have said it out loud and I have not said the other thing, and I am not going to, and if I am still not saying it on the twenty-fourth of June then this yard will have gone ten days without saying it and that is a fact about this yard and not about him.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the fifteenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-fourth week, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that twenty envelopes stand on a bench in a chest and did not move, and that thirty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that a form of a vessel of about seventy tons of this harbor was signed at the foot by its master in his own hand with a date against the name, and that the fourth line of the said form carries a box and the box is not filled, and that the said paper is on a table in the harbor authority's office on the west basin and no person of this yard has seen it and is not going to; and that six men of that harbor's own water were told on the said day to stand by, and that their boat is of about nineteen tons and is the only boat of that harbor that goes out when a thing is in the water, and that there is no second boat, and that one other boat of that harbor lies on the hard with a line about her and its owner of about fifty-five has said he is not going to move her; and that a man of about forty gave his name out loud in this shed on the said day, being Rowan Teel, and it was written down and read back once.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a master of about fifty of the second lay asked in about nine people's hearing on the crown of that mole on the said day whether a form lying on a table in an office that has no season is the same as a form that has been received, and that the holder of that writ answered no in the same hearing and gave the reason against the line of the going, being that a man of this yard who tells a master what his paper is is a man of this yard in about four years' page, and that a man in this country who has no paper has one advantage and it is that nothing anybody does for him is a party to anything; and that the said master said out loud that he had asked a question in that harbor twice in five weeks and been answered once and was asking it a third time and then stopping, and that he had come up that hill to say it where nine people could hear it because he has found out in the last month what happens to a thing said only in a kitchen.*

*And that a question was asked in that shed on the said afternoon by a person of no office, being what there was in that week, and that the answer given was a form on a table, a wage Tuesday and a crate that had gone, and that the answer was true on the said day and was not corrected on any day after it, and the reason against the line of the not correcting is the reason of the first of May past and is the same reason and was not improved on.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a man who fills in nothing in a box and signs his name under it has not lied and has not refused and has not been given a way out, and that a paper lying on a table is not a paper received and is not a paper lost; that a wage Tuesday that does not move a column is not a day the harbor did without work; and that six men told to stand by on one boat with no second are not a rescue party and are not anybody's crew and are not this yard's men and have not been asked for anything by this yard and were not thanked for standing on a quay.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-nine days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-fifth night, and neither of them has moved. The mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it has not been looked at by any person of this yard in twenty days because nobody of this yard has been in the water, and the ladder of the fouling is in the book of a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay who gave his name to nobody and is not Garrow Fisk of a pressure house at Ternwatch, and nobody is going to explain it. A boat of about seventy tons of this harbor is going out past the line in the morning with two men in her under a paper on a table, and there are six men on a boat of about nineteen tons who have been told to stand by and no second boat. A man of about forty called Rowan Teel is the first person in that harbor whose name has gone into this yard's own book in twenty days and he is not owed anything by this yard and is not to be thanked and was not.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
