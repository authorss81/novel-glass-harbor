# Chapter 306

*Thursday the Twentieth of May, the Forty-Eighth Charge Walked on None, Nine Things on the Back of a Slate in a Fish Market, and a Tenth Figure That Is the Price of a Sheet of Paper*

Thursday the twentieth of May, in the fish market, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-eighth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

She keeps the slate on the back of the board because the front of the board is for what she has and the back of the board is for what she had.

**The nine are these, and they are written in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and nobody in that market has ever copied them:**

> **FEBRUARY. THE NINETEENTH OF MAY.**
> Lamp oil, a barrel — **31s 6d.** No quotation.
> A sack of flour — **4s 9d.** No quotation.
> Salt, a bushel — **1s 3d.** No quotation.
> New rope, a fathom — **2s 6d.** No quotation.
> Canvas, a fathom — **1s 11d.** No quotation.
> Coal, a hundredweight — **1s 2d.** No quotation.
> Buntangs for a boat, a set — **9s.** No quotation.
> Tar, a cask — **5s 4d.** No quotation.
> Physic, a small bottle — **1s 2d.** None in this harbor.

"**I have written *no quotation* eight times on that slate and I want the ninth thing understood,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight. "**A market price is a sentence and not a bill. I have not had a bill for any of them since the ninth of April. A man comes into my market and asks me what a barrel of lamp oil is and I say I have none, and that is the true answer, and I have said *I have none* about two hundred times since April and about forty of those people had money.**"

"**And the February figures.**"

"**The February figures are the last ones I paid,**" she said. "**Paid, mind. Not asked. Not quoted. Paid. And a barrel of lamp oil that cost me thirty-one shillings and sixpence in February is not in this market in May and the figure on the slate is not a price anybody is charging me, it is a figure I am holding on to so that there is one in this harbor.**"

She put her thumb on the slate.

"**And here is the part I have said once and I am not saying it again, so write it down and do not come back. None of those nine figures is set in this market. Not one. I have never set one and I could not. Everything on that slate comes off a manifest and a bill of lading out of a merchant's agent at Ternwatch, and the agent's price is set in an office in a city four hundred miles from here by four people whose names I do not know, and not one of them has ever been to this quay and not one of them is ever going to come to this quay, and they do not have to, because they have never once had to be in the room where the price is used.**"

A man of about forty-five at the front of the stall said:

"**Then why is it a blockade.**"

"**Because they do not have to be in the room,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight, "**and that is the whole of it, and it is why it is worse than a closure. A man with a boat in the harbor with a cannon on it, I can stand in front of. I have done it. You can stand in front of a thing. You cannot stand in front of a figure on a manifest in a city.**"

---

And then she turned the slate over, which she does about twice a month, and on the other face of it there was one line and no heading.

> **A sheet of paper — 1d. Forty sheets — 1s.**

"**Wrapping paper,**" she said. "**For fish. That is the only thing I buy it for and that is the only thing it has been for in nine years, and it has not moved, and it is not going to move, and it is the only figure on that slate that has not moved and I would like one person in this market to look at it for a minute, because I have a feeling it is going to.**"

About nine people looked at it.

Nessa Pike looked at it for about a minute, and then wrote four lines in a small book she carries and put the book away and did not say a word about it in the market, and said it that night in the shed, and only to the holder of that writ, and it went in the first column because it is a figure and the figure is checkable.

---

The second thing that happened on the said Thursday happened at about seven in the evening in that shed and it was not a figure and it was not entered in any column and it was entered here.

"**I have been thinking about the woman with the drawer under the bed,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**Have not.**"

"**I have been thinking about her all afternoon. She is a woman of about fifty-eight, she has got a paper with a seal on it that she has never looked at and never will, and she is a person in this town, and if anybody in this harbor is going to be asked to do something about a paper that cannot be lodged anywhere, it is her, and if anybody in this harbor is going to need a person to be told something, it is her, and I have had her name in my head since about noon and I did not put it there on purpose.**"

"**Say the reason.**"

"**Say the reason and do not make it smaller than it is.**"

"**The reason is that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second,**" said Calder Marr. "**And the second one would be a woman of about fifty-eight with a drawer under her bed. And this yard decided that once, on the ninth of April, out loud, in about forty people's hearing, and it has not been contradicted since and it is not going to be contradicted because a price went on a slate.**"

"**It is a different reason now. It is not the same reason. In April it was that we did not know her. Now we know her and we know she is frightened, and frightened is worse than unknown.**"

"**It is a worse reason,**" he said, "**and it gets the same answer, and I want that written down in the words it was said in and not improved on, because in about four years somebody is going to want to know when a rule was convenient and when it was not, and the answer is going to be that both times it was on the ninth of April and on the twentieth of May and both times it cost somebody else and not this yard.**"

Nobody improved on it. It went in the second column with a reason against the line and no name in it.

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twentieth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall in the fish market of this harbor keeps a figure on the back of a slate for nine things, being a barrel of lamp oil at thirty-one shillings and sixpence, a sack of flour at four shillings and ninepence, a bushel of salt at one shilling and threepence, a fathom of new rope at two shillings and sixpence, a fathom of canvas at one shilling and elevenpence, a hundredweight of coal at one shilling and twopence, a set of buntangs for a boat at nine shillings, a cask of tar at five shillings and fourpence, and a small bottle of physic at one shilling and twopence; and that all nine of those figures are the figures she last paid in February and are not prices charged to her and are not quotations, and that on the said day she had not been offered a different figure in any of the nine, and that there is no physic in this harbor at any figure.*

*And that on the said day the said slate carried a tenth figure, being one sheet of paper at one penny and forty sheets at one shilling, for wrapping fish, which is the only use it has ever had in nine years, which has not moved, and which the said woman asked one person in that market to look at; and that a shipwright of this harbor looked at it for about a minute and wrote the figure in a small book she carries.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the woman of the end stall said out loud in about forty people's hearing, in that market, on the said day, that none of the nine figures on her slate is set in that market and that every one of them comes off a manifest out of a merchant's agent at Ternwatch, and that the agent's price is set in an office in a city four hundred miles from that harbor by people who have never come to that quay and are never going to; and that she said that a market price is a sentence and not a bill, and that she has had no bill for any of the nine since the ninth of April past, and that about forty people who asked her in that period had money.*

*And that a question was put to this yard in that shed on the said evening about a woman of about fifty-eight of this harbor with a paper with a seal on it in a drawer under her bed, and that the answer was refused out loud with the standing of the ninth day of April past and with the words *this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second*; and that the reason for the refusal on the said day was given as worse than the reason in April and not as better, being that in April the yard did not know her and on the said day the yard knows she is frightened, and that the answer is the same answer and is not improved on.*

*And that this yard bought one sheet of paper for one penny at a copyist's counter in a room off that same fish market on the twenty-seventh day of April past, and that the counter sells forty sheets for a shilling, and that this yard was told on the said Thursday that the figure for a sheet of paper has not moved and has been the same for nine years, and that no person in that yard said one word about why the sheet was entered, and that the reason against the line of the saying is that the reason is a second-column fact and it carries no name and it has a date against it and that is all it is.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-first line:*

*What it is not: — that nine figures written on the back of a slate are not a blockade and are not a price list and are not a protest, and that the woman who keeps them is not a heroine and has not been thanked and is not going to be; that a figure which has not moved is not a comfort and is not a promise and is not a stage; and that a party which has refused to make a second address in this country is not a party that has a rule about houses and is a party that has a rule about its own finding out.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence. The cistern was charged for the forty-eighth and walked on none of it. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-three days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and ninth night, and neither of them has moved, and a mole-head light is not lit, and a woman in a market is holding on to nine figures from February and one from nine years ago, and a sheet of paper is a penny.**
