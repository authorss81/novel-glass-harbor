# Chapter 323

*Monday the Seventh of June, the Fifty-Seventh Time That Wall Came Down, the Fifty-Third Charge Walked on None, a Crown Chart of About Nine Years Standing Looked At From a Doorway, and Two Copies in That Harbor Neither of Which This Yard Has Held*

Monday the seventh of June, at the reeve's office on the west basin in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-seventh time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on the said day. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said day by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-third of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence. The far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why.**

---

Calder Marr went down to the west basin on the seventh of June because a warp he had left with a shipwright on the hard needed fetching, and the reeve's office is on the way back, and the door of it was open because the morning was warm.

He did not go in. He has not been in that office since the eighteenth of May, when a master of fifty carried a declaration there and was told three true things, and he is not going to be the man who goes in and asks for something to look at.

On the wall inside, opposite the door, there is a framed Crown chart of that coast and the strait beyond it, under glass, hung there since before anybody in this harbor can remember exactly when. The clerk says it came in about nine years past with a set of sailing directions, and that this harbor holds two copies of it, this one and one in the office's back room in a drawer, and that neither of them has ever been taken out of that building.

Calder Marr stood in the doorway for about a minute and looked at it through the open door, and the clerk looked up from his table and nodded, and neither man said anything.

What he saw was what he expected to see. The strait called Bramble Strait is on it, with soundings along both shores, and the middle water is empty of any line except the soundings, and there is no storm route across it on the face of it. Whatever old vessels in this yard's own keeping may hold, this chart does not hold it, and the chart omits it, and that is the whole of what a minute in a doorway can show.

He walked back up the hill, and in the shed he told Nessa Pike what he had done, and she wrote it down in the second column with the reason against it, being that a man who looks at a chart from a doorway has not held it and has not borrowed it and is not a party to any custody of it.

"I did not touch it," he said. "I did not ask to touch it. I stood where any man going past an open door stands. There are two copies in that building and I have held neither of them. If a route is on no current chart, then this is one of the charts it is not on, and that is a thing a man can go and look at without taking anything."

"That is the chart that omits it," said Nessa Pike. "About nine years standing. Two copies in that harbor. Write it as what it is."

"Write it as what it is," said Calder Marr, "and put the seventh of June against it, and no name in it, except the name of the strait, which is Bramble Strait, and the fact that no route across it is on that chart."

---

In the afternoon the man of about fifty of the second lay came up the mole with his coat buttoned though the day was warm, because he keeps a form in his coat and he keeps the coat buttoned over it.

He carried a declaration to the harbor authority on the eighteenth of May and was told three true things in the same order, and on the twenty-sixth he read the fourth line off his own form twice in a market, being whether she carries material which is the subject of an unsettled claim, and he said he would go out on the Friday with the box empty and that that is the absence of a plan and not a plan, and he went out and came back with nothing happening.

He stood at the head of the slip and looked at the water for a while, and Calder Marr mended the warp and did not ask him anything, because the man has a box in a form that is not filled and this yard does not draft and does not copy and does not witness and does not countersign and does not advise, and it said all five of those things out loud in a market and is not going to say them again on a slip.

"He is still carrying it," said Nessa Pike afterwards, in the shed. "The form. In his coat. The box empty."

"He is," said Calder Marr. "And he is not going to fill it in a shed on a mole, and we are not going to help him fill it, and that is the whole of it. A man with a form in his coat is a man with a coat. Put him down as what he is and for no more."

She put him down as what he is: a man of about fifty of the second lay with a form in his coat and a box in it that is not filled, who stood at the head of that slip on the seventh of June and looked at the water and asked nothing and was asked nothing.

---

That evening a boy came up from the hard to say that the man of about thirty-one with the coaster out of Ashtide had been down on his own deck all afternoon with his bag beside him, and had not opened it, and had caulked a seam and gone back up the town without speaking to anybody.

"He carries it everywhere," said the boy, and went back down.

"He does," said Calder Marr. "Since March. And he was refused a question by this yard in the middle of March, and he has asked nothing since, and he is not a witness and has not been made one, and this yard did not ask him one question on the twenty-third of May when he stood in a market for about four minutes on purpose, and is not going to ask him one now."

After the boy had gone, Nessa Pike sat for a while with the pencil in her left hand, looking at the bench where the four shut books lay under the sailcloth, and she said the thing she had been turning over since the doorway.

"A chart under glass on a wall is a strange thing to be two of," she said. "One on the wall and one in a drawer in the back room, and neither ever taken out of the building. That is what the clerk said, and he has been a clerk twenty-two years, and he is owed his own quarter, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has said before."

"He did," said Calder Marr. "And I stood where any man stands going past an open door, and I looked for about a minute, and I touched nothing. If any later man in this harbor says this yard borrowed a chart, or copied one, or carried one down a hill, then that man is saying a thing no page of this book will bear out, because the page says doorway and minute and no entering."

"Then it is written," said Nessa Pike, and wrote the minute and the doorway and the no entering under the day's line, with the reason against it, being that a custody has a handle on the inside and this yard has no handle on that building.

The woman of no office finished the cuff she was mending and bit off the thread, and folded the work, and put it on the bench beside the shut books without touching any of them.

*First column, entered on Monday the seventh day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the slate wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning of the said day for the fifty-seventh time since the first of May past and no sitting was held in that room; and that the cistern was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning of the said day and this party walked none of it, being the fifty-third charge walked on none of since the third day of August past; and that a Crown chart of that coast and the strait called Bramble Strait, of about nine years standing, framed under glass on the wall of the reeve's office on the west basin, this harbor holding two copies of it, was looked at by the holder of that writ from the doorway of the said office on the said morning for about a minute without entering and without touching, and that no storm route across the said strait is on the face of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said chart came in with a set of sailing directions about nine years past and neither copy has ever been taken out of that building, on the clerk's own word in that office, the said clerk being unnamed and about sixty and owed a quarter since the first of April; and that this yard has held neither copy and is not a party to any custody of either; and that this yard did not go back to the copyist's counter in the room off the fish market on the said day and did not send anybody there.*

*And that a man of about fifty of the second lay with a form in his coat and a box in it that is not filled stood at the head of that slip on the said day and looked at the water and asked nothing and was asked nothing; and that a man of about thirty-one with a coaster out of Ashtide was down on his own deck all afternoon with his bag beside him and did not open it; and that the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a chart looked at from a doorway is not a chart held and not a chart borrowed and not a custody entered into; that two copies in one building neither of which has been taken out of it are not two copies this yard has seen inside and are one wall seen once; and that a route omitted from a chart is not a route disproved by it, and no man in this shed has said so.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall came down bare for the fifty-seventh time and the cistern was charged for the fifty-third and walked on none of it. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-one days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twenty-seventh night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand worked on that crown and was not thanked and is not going to be.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
