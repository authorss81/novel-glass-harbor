# Chapter 327

*Friday the Eleventh of June, About Nine People in a Market Again, a Storm Route Read Out Loud From Three Books, and Fourteen Days Off the Water Up With This Day*

Friday the eleventh of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence. Four old books lay open on that bench and a fifth was in its owner's house, and that is the whole of what the ten days have produced.**

**A man of sixty-one sat on that bench from ten until four and was asked nothing and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift. The holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said day, was in that harbor from the market to the shed, and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn is in that harbor, going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, and her forty marks stand first after food, not reduced and not paid.**

---

Cobb Terrell's fourteen days off the water are up with this day, because he was hurt on the twenty-eighth of May and the bonesetter said fourteen, and he came down to the shed in the morning walking plainly, without favoring the foot, and said so.

"Fourteen," he said. "I have counted it the same three ways. I am to be on the water from today. I am not asking this yard for a boat, because this yard has twenty pence in its bag and cannot buy a day. I am telling this shed that the bill is paid and the foot is mended, and that what is left is the other thing the bonesetter talked about, which is finishing with me in its own time."

"You are mended," said Nessa Pike, and wrote it down.

"I am mended," said Cobb Terrell. "And I am going back to the west quay to mend warps for any man who wants them, and not in a boat, because a man who has been hurt in a boat this yard paid does not get back in one the day he is mended."

He went back up the hill, and nobody thanked him and he did not ask to be thanked, because that is how it is in that street.

---

At about eleven in the morning in the fish market, in about nine people's hearing around the end stall, the man of about thirty-six of the third lay said out loud that his father kept a book with soundings in it that no chart has, the same thing he had said on the crown on the sixth of June to one man, and this time he said it to nine.

He had brought the book with him. It is his father's book, kept in a square hand, with the dates at the foot of every page, and he laid it on the end stall's table beside the slate with the two oil figures on it, and he opened it at a page marked with a twist of twine.

"My father," he said. "Three ways of spelling one rock in three years. *Skar Deep, Skarre Depe, Skar Deap.* Eight and three quarters, nine, nine and a half fathom. Twenty-one, fifteen and eight years past. The middle water of Bramble Strait. No current chart has it. I have carried this book since he died and I have never read it out loud anywhere. I am reading it now because a yard on this mole has four books with the same water in them, and I heard that on the west quay, and I am not going to have it said that I kept mine shut while theirs stood open."

The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall looked at the page and nodded, because she has kept nine February figures on the back of a slate for a month and knows what a figure looks like.

"That is the same water," she said. "Different spellings, different hands, different names for the same soundings. It is in your father's square hand and it is in their four books. It is a storm route across that strait and no current chart includes it. That is what it is."

About nine people heard it. Calder Marr was at the end of the aisle and Nessa Pike beside him with the small book, and neither of them said anything, because a thing read out loud in a market by its owner is not a thing this yard has taken.

The man of about thirty-six shut the book and picked it up and went out of the market, and nobody followed him, because a book carried out of a market by its owner is a book that was never in anybody's custody but his.

---

In the shed that night, Nessa Pike wrote the father's three spellings down in the second column exactly as they had been read, with the three figures and the three dates, and under them the line that has now gone in four times: that a line of figures in five books identifies a difference in five books and not a truth about a sea.

"It is found," said Calder Marr, looking at the entry. "Not in a room with a door shut. In a market, in about nine people's hearing, out loud, with a boat's name nowhere in it yet, but with a father's name in it, which is more. The man who was not in that market is the man this yard most wants in it, and he was on the crown all day and was not sent for."

"Write that as what it is," said Nessa Pike.

"Write it as what it is," said Calder Marr, "and put the eleventh of June against it, and no name in it but the father's book, which is his son's to carry, and the strait, which is Bramble Strait, and the fact that no current chart has the route."

He sat for a while with his hands on the bench, and the four books lay open beside the entry, and the fifth was back in its owner's house.

"This party has always said that a record a man cannot check is worth less than one he can," he said. "This is the best record this party has ever been offered, and it is in five books now, and every book disagrees with every other by a quarter of a fathom or three quarters, and the soundings are of a coast that has moved, and the only way to check it is to go there, and the only kind that could get a blockade broken is the kind that cannot be checked at all. Write it down, because I am not going to refuse it for the old reason, and I am not going to accept it for free either."

She wrote it, and then she closed the small book and put the pencil down, because her left hand ached the way it always aches when she writes long, and she held the hand in the other for a while the way she has done since February.

"Five books," she said, when the ache had eased. "Four of ours and one of his. Eleven spellings if you count his three with our eight. And the chart on the reeve's wall with nothing across it, and his chart in his bag with nothing across it. It is all of a piece. The books say there is water where the charts say there is nothing, and the price says the water has moved and the road has to be given up. Nobody in this shed made any of that. It came the way it came."

"No," said Calder Marr. "Nobody in this shed made it. That is why it is worth something. A thing this yard made would be worth what this yard is worth, which is twenty pence and four columns nobody can check. This came out of other men's work, spread over eighteen years, and it disagrees the way honest work disagrees."

He stood up and went to the door of the shed and looked down the hill at the harbor, where seven boats of the third lay were lying off inside the line with their riding lights coming on, and not one of them had passed the line on this day and not one had broken any rule.

"Seven boats," he said, without turning round. "Where there were eleven on the twenty-fourth of May. A chandler's counter shut to one man. A man mended in the foot and back to warps. A barrel of oil up six shillings and not coming down. That is the harbor tonight. And five books holding one water that no chart has. That is the shed tonight. Neither is an answer to the other, and I am not going to put them in one sentence in either direction."

---

---

*First column, entered on Friday the eleventh day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of about thirty-six of the third lay read out loud in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about nine people's hearing, from his own father's book in a square hand, soundings for the middle water of Bramble Strait under three spellings, being Skar Deep, Skarre Depe and Skar Deap, with figures of eight and three quarters, nine and nine and a half fathom, dated twenty-one, fifteen and eight years past, no current chart including the said water as a route; and that a man of about forty-one named Cobb Terrell is not to be on the water no longer, fourteen days being up with the said day, and walked plainly on the said morning.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said father's book was laid on the end stall's table beside the slate and opened at a page marked with twine, and read by its owner, and shut by its owner and carried out by its owner, and was never in any custody but his; and that the woman of about fifty-eight said out loud that it is the same water as in this yard's four books; and that the holder of that writ and the ship's hand were at the end of that aisle and said nothing.*

*And that this party has always said that a route remembered across several vessels is checkable by going there and that a record nobody can check is worth less than one a man can, and that the said five books are the best record this party has ever been offered, with every book disagreeing by a quarter to three quarters of a fathom, and that this yard will not refuse it for the old reason and will not accept it for free; and that this yard did not go back to the copyist's counter in the room off the fish market on the said day and did not send anybody there.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-second line:*

*What it is not: — that a book read out loud by its owner in a market is not a book taken and not a custody entered into; that five books holding one water are not one book agreeing with itself; and that the best record this party has ever been offered is not a truth about a sea, and no man in this shed has called it one.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-five days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-first night, and neither of them has moved, and Cobb Terrell said out loud at the foot of that slip that the bill was paid and the foot was mended and went back up the hill to mend warps for any man who wanted them, and not in a boat, and a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand worked on that crown and was not thanked and is not going to be.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
