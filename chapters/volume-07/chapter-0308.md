# Chapter 308

*Saturday the Twenty-Second of May, the Far Bench Occupied from Ten Until Four and Not Approached, a Copy of a Notice Bought for a Penny in a Room off the Fish Market, Two Hundred and Eleven of One Nine-Line Sheet of a Drowned City Since the Twenty-Seventh of April, and a Woman Who Says She Is Not Going to Stop*

Saturday the twenty-second of May, in a room off the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it. And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Saturday, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The room off the fish market has a counter in it and two stools and a shelf of ink and a window that does not open, and a woman of about fifty-nine has been in that room for twenty-two years, and she is a copyist, and she will take anybody's money for a copy of anything whatsoever and has never once in twenty-two years asked a living soul what it was for.

She took the notice of the seventeenth off the board when Nessa Pike brought it, and she copied it in about eleven minutes, and she did not read it aloud, and it cost a penny, and the copy is in the ship's own hand for her own reasons and is not on that yard's account and is not for that yard.

**And the penny was out of that hand and not out of that yard's book, and the ship's hand said so when the copyist asked her for it, and the copyist looked at her for a second and then said *that is the first time in twenty-two years anybody has told me which pocket a penny came out of*, and neither of them improved on it.**

Then she put the copy down on the counter and squared it, and said:

"**I have had those eight clauses four times this week.**"

"**You have.**"

"**I have had them four times this week and they are five days old,**" said the copyist, "**and I have had two hundred and eleven of one nine-line sheet since the end of April, and I am going to tell you why I am not going to stop copying either of them, and you are going to hear it, and then you are going to take your paper and go up that hill, and if you come back and ask me again I am going to charge you again.**"

"**Nobody in that yard sent anybody to this room.**"

"**I know,**" said the copyist. "**You are the first person in three weeks who has come in here and asked me for a copy of a thing instead of asking me to stop.**"

---

She took a book off the shelf under the counter. It is a ruled book with a limp cover and about four years of entries in it, and it is not a record of anything anybody asked her for, because nobody asks her for anything, and it is a list in her own hand of what she has copied and when and what it was for, because **a copyist who does not keep a book of what she copied is a copyist who cannot be asked anything, and that is not a defensive act, it is the oldest caution in this trade and every copyist who has ever been any good has kept one.**

She did not open it on the counter and she did not hand it over.

"**I am going to say a number and I am going to say it out loud and I am not going to open the book, and if you write the number down then you have got a figure and I have said it in a room, and neither of us has got a page.**"

"**Say the number.**"

"**Two hundred and eleven,**" said the copyist, "**since the twenty-seventh day of April, of one nine-line sheet about a chamber in a drowned city, and not one word of the eight clauses you have just paid me for is in it. I have never seen a person bring me a paper I could not read, and I have never asked, and I am not going to start on the twenty-second of May because a naval notice went on a wall on Monday.**"

Nessa Pike wrote the figure in the small book she carries and put the book in her pocket and did not ask to see the ruled book and did not ask to see the page.

"**And I am not going to stop,**" said the copyist.

"**You do not have to explain it to me.**"

"**I am going to explain it to you, because nobody else in this harbor is going to and I have been standing in this room with it for three weeks.**" She put both hands flat on the counter. "**A copyist who stops copying is a copyist who has admitted she copied something. That is the whole of it. I have two hundred and eleven copies of a nine-line sheet about a chamber in a drowned city in this town, and a man with a pen somewhere is going to come into this room one day and ask me to say that I copied two hundred and eleven of it, and the day I say it out loud to a person with a pen I am not a copyist any more, and I am a woman who has admitted a thing. And a trade can be taxed and priced and put in a schedule and made to account for itself on a Tuesday, and a confession cannot be put in a schedule at all, and the difference between those two is my whole living and the whole of my name and it is the only thing I have got that a person with a pen cannot take off me with a form.**"

"**We are not going to ask you to stop.**"

"**Then I have nothing to be afraid of and I have had nothing to be afraid of since the twenty-eighth of April and I have not slept properly for three weeks, so one of us is getting something out of this.**"

---

"**Now,**" said the copyist, "**since you are the first person in three weeks who has paid me for a thing instead of asking me to stop, I am going to tell you the four I know, and I am telling you and not asking you for anything.**

"**The first is a yard. The twenty-seventh of April, about half past two in the afternoon. A man of about thirty-two with split hands who did not sit down and was in a hurry and gave me the money out of his own hand and not out of a bag, and the paper he gave me the original of was better paper than the rest, and he asked for one and he paid for one and he did not ask for a second, and I have not seen him since and I have not been asked by anybody about him.**"

"**The second is a man who pays cash and takes his own paper away and is a stranger, and I have not had him twice.**

"**The third is a woman. She came in on the ninth of May and she bought nine of them in one day — nine, at a penny each, nine pence — and she carried them out in a cloth against her chest with both arms and she did not look at one of them in this room and she did not have one folded and she did not have one creased. I have thought about that woman for a fortnight. I do not know what a person does with nine.**"

Nessa Pike did not say one word about the woman with the cloth, then or afterwards, and wrote nothing down about her.

"**And that is all four,**" said the copyist, "**and there are two hundred and seven more and I have told nobody any of this and I have not thought about telling anybody, and I am telling it to you because it is nine lines and a penny and I have made two hundred and eleven of them and a trade is a trade.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-second day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a copy of the notice of the seventeenth day of May was made at a counter in a room off the fish market of this harbor on the said day at about half past ten in the morning at a penny, and that the copy was made in about eleven minutes by a copyist of about fifty-nine, and that the said penny was the ship's own and did not go out of that yard's book; and that no person of that yard had been in that room at any hour before the said day and that nobody had sent anybody.*

*And that a copyist of about fifty-nine in that room stated out loud, in a room with two people in it, that she has made two hundred and eleven copies of one nine-line sheet about a chamber in a drowned city since the twenty-seventh day of April past, and that the said sheet is the sheet this yard bought for a penny on that day, being a copy of a copy of a municipal sheet, and that the said number is entered in that book of that yard as a figure said out loud by a person and not as a page, and that the said book of the copyist was not opened on that counter and was not handed over and was not asked for; and that this yard did not ask to see it and is not going to ask to see it; and that the eight clauses of the notice of the seventeenth are a different sheet and are not the sheet of the two hundred and eleven, and that four copies of them have been made at that counter since the seventeenth and this is the fourth, and that the first three were not made by this yard and this yard has not asked who made them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said copyist said out loud in that room that she is not going to stop copying the said nine lines, and gave one reason for it, being that a copyist who stops copying is a copyist who has admitted she copied something, and that a trade can be put in a schedule and a confession cannot, and that the difference is her living and her name; and that she said out loud in the same breath that a naval notice went on a market wall on Monday the seventeenth and that this yard sent nobody to that counter on the seventeenth and has not sent anybody there since the twenty-seventh of April, and that the two sheets have nothing in them in common except that they were copied on the same counter for a penny each, and that she said that as a person who copies and not as a person who has worked it out.*

*And that the said copyist said out loud in that room that she knows four of the buyers, and named them as follows, being a yard on the twenty-seventh day of April past at about half past two in the afternoon, which is this yard, a man of about thirty-two with split hands who did not sit down, who paid for one and did not ask for a second, and about whom nobody has asked her; a man who pays cash, takes his own paper away, and is a stranger, of whom she has not had him twice; a woman who came in on the ninth day of May past and bought nine of them at a penny each and carried them out in a cloth against her chest with both arms, and who did not look at one of them and did not have one folded and did not have one creased, and about whom the said copyist has thought for a fortnight and does not know what a person does with nine; and that those are all four and that there are two hundred and seven more.*

*And that the holder of that writ asked no question about the woman and no question about the cloth and the ship's hand asked no question about the woman and no question about the cloth; that the woman's nine were not followed and were not written against and will not be followed by any part of this yard, now or in nine years; and that the reason against the line of the following is that following it would make this yard the party that looks, and this yard said out loud on the ninth day of May past that it is not going to go and look in one of four drawers it cannot look in, and a woman with a cloth is a drawer that has not been given a number yet.*

*And that the holder of that writ did not go over to a man of sixty-one at a far bench on the said day at any hour, and the said bench was occupied from about ten until about four, and this yard entered that and entered that neither brother has said why.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that two hundred and eleven is not a conspiracy and is not a conspiracy's figure and is not a number with a man behind it, and that the reason it is not a conspiracy is that it is a price list of a penny a sheet and the reason this yard cannot be sure of that is that nobody in this harbor has ever been sure of a price; that a copyist who is not going to stop is not a heroine and is not a coward and has not been thanked and is not going to be; and that a woman who bought nine of them and carried them out in a cloth is not a party to anything until somebody can say what she did with them, and this yard is not going to be that somebody, and it is not going to be that somebody by asking her, and it is not going to be that somebody by asking anybody; and that two sheets bought of one copyist in one morning are not a conspiracy either, and this yard is now the only party in this harbor that has done it, and that is a fact about this yard and is not entered as what caused anything.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and the bag holds ninety-two pence, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-five days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eleventh night, and neither of them has moved. A sheet of paper is a penny and forty sheets are a shilling, and about two hundred and eleven of one nine-line sheet about a chamber in a drowned city went through one room off one fish market in this town in twenty-five days, and a woman of about fifty-nine is not going to stop, and nobody in this yard has asked her to and nobody in this yard is going to; and a naval notice went on a market wall in this town on Monday, and it is a different sheet, and it has been up five days and it has been copied four times.**
