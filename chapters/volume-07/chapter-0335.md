# Chapter 335

*Saturday the Nineteenth of June, Two Quarts of Lamp Oil at Ten Pence and the Whole of That Yard's Money Gone, a Boy in the Front Street in Front of a Woman, a Tuesday That Was Not Asked For, and Twenty to Six in the Evening with a Figure That Was Right and a Boat on the Ground*

Saturday the nineteenth of June, at a chandler's counter on the front street in the morning, at the shed on the mole in the early afternoon, and at the foot of that mole's steps in the evening.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day. Twenty pence went out of it at about half past ten in the morning, being two quarts of lamp oil at ten pence, and there is nothing in that bag now and there has been nothing in it since half past ten in the morning, and a bag with nothing in it is not a bag that is going to have something in it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that front street at about eleven in the morning of the said day and was in that shed at about one in the afternoon and went nowhere else, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid and neither touched by anything in the said morning. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he was not told by anybody in that yard that there was a light burning in that shed from two o'clock and he did not come down and he was not asked to.**

---

The chandler is on the front street and has been there thirty-one years and his counter is shut to one man in this harbor and open to everybody else, and the holder of that writ is everybody else.

The man of about thirty-six was at that counter when he came in, buying nothing, standing at the end of it with his hands behind his back, because his wife's counters are four shillings for about sixty candles at fourpence and that is the price of nought and he has said so out loud and his wife has counted it out loud and neither of them has improved on it since the sixth day of June past.

The chandler's wife counted the oil before Calder Marr had finished asking for it.

"**Two quarts at ten,**" she said. "**And I will say the figure out loud because that is what I do, and it is a quart and it is not a barrel, and a barrel of it went up six shillings on the first of June and is not coming down, and a quart of it is ten pence and I have not had a complaint about ten pence since the ninth of April.**"

"**Two quarts,**" said Calder Marr, and put twenty pence on the counter, which was all of it, and the whole of what that yard had in the world.

He carried them up the hill in two jars with a cord between them and Nessa Pike took them off the bench without a word and got a lamp out of that shed and put oil in it, and neither of them said the figure out loud again.

---

The woman of no office was in the front street at about eleven that morning buying nothing, because she has not had money in her hand since the fourth of May past and the forty marks that stand first after food have not been reduced and have not been paid, and she was standing outside a shop with a basket on her arm doing the thing she does, which is to be in a street where things are said.

The boy of about seventeen was the one who carried a word up that hill for a penny, and he had two pence and no errand, and he was in front of her in the queue and he said, out loud, to a man in front of him and not to a woman behind him:

"**Two quarts of oil, up the mole, off that yard, and they have paid for it in coin and the bag's empty, and it's for the six that go out at half five.**"

He did not see her. A boy carrying a word for a penny is looking at the man in front of him and is not looking behind him, and a man who is seventeen and is carrying nothing but two pence is not doing anything wrong, and he did not know that there was a woman four feet behind him with a basket on her arm.

She stood in that street for about a minute. Then she went up the hill, which is a long way and takes about nine minutes and is the only hill in that harbor that goes up to a shed.

---

At about one in the afternoon she was in that shed and she asked one question, and she had not asked a question for three days, and it was the only one she asked.

"**Who is the light for.**"

"**For the six.**" He did not turn round. "**They go out at half past five in the morning on the flood and they were to be in at about half past four, and there is no light on that mole head because the lens has been out since the seventh of May past and a light costs oil and the oil has a new price, and if there is a lit window in a shed on that mole at a quarter to six in the evening then a man in a boat past the outer marks has got one thing to come in on, and that is the whole of the reason and I am not going to improve on it.**"

"**Did you buy that oil out of the bag.**"

"**I bought two quarts of it out of the bag at ten pence and the bag was twenty pence and the bag is nothing.**"

"**When did you decide to buy it.**"

"**On Tuesday night, at about eleven, in this shed, and Nessa Pike was here and I said it out loud to her and she wrote it in the second column with the day against it, and I did not say it to you.**"

She put the basket down on the bench and took her coat off over it, and that was the ordinary of it, and it took her about as long as taking a coat off takes.

"**Say the rest of it,**" she said, "**because I have been standing on that hill for nine minutes working out how to ask this and I have not got a better way and I am not going to improvise one on a Saturday.**"

"**On the eighteenth of June I was in the water off the south end of this mole from twenty to seven in the morning until twenty past two in the afternoon, and I put a mark of this yard's on the face of the second seam to a use that has never been put to it on this coast, and I did it off a book that belongs to a man of about thirty-four of this harbor who gave his name to nobody and gave four figures out loud on a step and nothing else, and I did not ask him one word and I did not tell you one word, and on the seventeenth of June at ten o'clock at night in this shed I told you that there was nothing to do with it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I am not going to be able to tell you why, and I am not going to improve on the reason, and the reason is that on the Friday there is a job at half past six in the morning and there is no hour between the tide and the dark on the Thursday to hold a conversation in, and because a day in that yard is a day that cannot be replaced, and because I am not a man who says things in a shed so that he can say afterwards that he said them. And all three of those are true and none of them is good enough and I am not going to pretend one of them is.**"

Nessa Pike kept her eyes on the page. She was the ship's hand and she writes left-handed and she had four stitches in a right hand since the twentieth of February, and she was not going to be asked about this and was not going to offer anything, and that is the whole of what she did in that room.

"**I am not going to say you should have told me,**" said the woman of no office. "**I have gone up and down that street three times in the last ten minutes trying to find a version where saying that is true and there is not one, because I would have said no. I would have said no, and I would have been entitled to, and I have thought it through and I am not sure I would have been wrong to.**"

"**You would not have been wrong.**"

"**I would have been entitled,**" she said, "**and that is a different thing and I know it is a different thing and I am not going to have you agree with me about it, because the second I agree with you about it I am doing you a kindness and we do not do that here.**"

She picked up her coat and folded it over her arm, and then she did not put it on, and that was the part of the afternoon that stayed in that shed.

"**Here is what I lost,**" she said. "**It is not a record. I have no record. Nobody has ever been able to take anything off me that I was keeping, and you have never once in six volumes pretended otherwise and it is the one thing about this yard I have never had to check. On Tuesday I had a whole week in front of me. On that Tuesday I would have spent a day of it on this, and I would have won it, and I would have been half right, and I would have come up here on the Wednesday and asked you again and it would have been finished by the Thursday. That is what is gone. Not a fact. A Tuesday.**"

"**Yes,**" said Calder Marr.

"**And the money,**" she said. "**Twenty pence. That is what it came to and I want you to hear me say the figure and I want you to hear me not say whether it was well spent.**"

"**It was for the six.**"

"**It was for the six,**" she said, "**and I am not saying it was well spent and I am not saying it was badly spent, and I would like you to hear me not say either, because I have thought about it and the not-saying is deliberate and it is mine and not yours.**"

She put her coat on and picked up the basket and went out of that shed, and she went down the hill, and she did not go down to the slip, and she did not look up the hill, and she has not been back up it to say anything else about it and nobody has asked her to and nobody in that yard is going to.

---

The lamp went into the window of that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon with the door open, and it burned there until about midnight, and it was the only light on that mole head and anybody at all might have carried one, and nobody organized it and nobody named anybody to do it and nobody asked anybody to be in a place at an hour.

A man of about thirty-eight came up the hill at about half past four and said there was no boat on the mole steps and no light on the outer marks, and a woman of about fifty-eight said the same thing and had closed her stall, and about nine people stood on that crown in front of an open shed door until about a quarter to six in the evening.

At about a quarter to six a man of about forty-four came up that hard alone, in a wet jersey, with no hat, and he came up the steps at a walk and stopped at the top of them, and there were about nine people on that mole.

"**We are off her,**" he said. "**All six of us and we are ashore and two boats that were out on their own account have us in them, and the boat of about nineteen tons is on the foul off the south end of this mole with a hole in her and there is eleven foot of water over the foul where she is lying and she is making water at about a finger a minute and the last of the flood is going now.**"

He said the next thing without being asked, and he said it to the lamp in the window.

"**The figure was right.**"

Calder Marr said: "**Say the other half of it.**"

"**We were told nine foot on the bar and eight and a half off the foul at four o'clock this afternoon, and we had the nine and we had the eight and a half and we believed both of them, and at twenty to six this evening there is eleven foot on the foul where that boat is lying, and that is where we found her, and I do not know why there is eleven foot there and neither do you, and I have thought about it in a boat and I have not got it.**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I want that written down in those words and not one word of it better. The figure was right. The boat was on the ground.**"

Nobody said anything else on that mole, and about nine people stood there for about a quarter of an hour, and the lamp burned in that window, and the six men were in that harbor's own front room by nine o'clock, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything about the fouling of the second seam and nobody said anything at all about what a foot and seven inches of shell on a mole's own stone had done or had not done.

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the nineteenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a boat of about nineteen tons of about six men was found on the foul ground off the south end of that mole at about a quarter to six in the afternoon of the said day, that is to say at about twenty minutes to six, inside that harbor's own line, with a hole in her and eleven foot of water over the foul where she was lying and the last of the flood going; and that the said boat was upon that foul ground at the hour of four o'clock, when the water off the foul was eight and a half foot, and that the flood then came over her and the said eleven foot was over her at twenty to six; and that all six of that crew were off her and ashore on the said evening and were got in by two boats that were out on their own account and were not of that yard and were not hired by anybody; and that a man of about forty-four of the said six came up that hard alone at about a quarter to six in the afternoon and said out loud, in about nine people's hearing and unasked, that the figure the six had was right, and that there was nine foot on the bar and eight and a half off the foul at four o'clock and there was eleven foot on the foul at twenty to six, and that he did not know why and neither did the holder of that writ; and that the holder of that writ said out loud in the same hearing, being the figure was right and the boat was on the ground, and asked that it be written in those words and not one word of it better.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that twenty pence went out of that yard on the said morning at a chandler's counter on the front street, being two quarts of lamp oil at ten pence, being the whole of what that yard had, and that the bag is nothing and nothing has been in it since; and that the counter of that chandler is shut to one man and the holder of that writ is not that man, and that the man of about thirty-six of the third lay was at that counter buying nothing at the hour of it, and that a wife of about fifty told the figure out loud before it was asked for; and that a lamp burned in the window of that shed from about two o'clock in the afternoon of the said day until about midnight with the door open, and that nobody organized it and nobody named anybody to do it and nobody asked anybody to be in a place at an hour.*

*And that a boy of about seventeen who carries a word up that hill for a penny said out loud in the front street at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in front of a woman and not to her, that two quarts of oil had gone up the mole off that yard and had been paid for in coin and the bag was empty and that it was for the six that went out at half past five; and that a woman of no office, of no office, not on the crew, whose forty marks stand first after food and are neither reduced nor paid, went up that hill on the said day and asked one question, being who the light was for, and was answered in full, and said out loud that she would have said no on the said Thursday and would have been entitled to and is not going to have that agreement made with her, and that what she lost was not a record, of which she has none, but a Tuesday, of which she had a whole week in front of her on the fifteenth day of June and has not now; and that she named the figure of twenty pence and said that it was for the six and said neither that it was well spent nor that it was badly spent, and that the not-saying is hers and not the yard's; and that she went down that hill and did not go down to the slip and did not look up the hill and has not come back up it to say anything else about it and nobody has asked her to.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixtieth line:*

*What it is not: — that a figure that was right is not a figure that saved a boat, and that a boat on the ground is not a wrong figure and is not a wrong mark and is not a wrong man; that a thing kept from a person in a shed is not a protection and is not a kindness and is not a lie, and that the cost of it is not a figure and does not go in a column; and that a light in a window bought with the last money in a bag is not a rescue and is not a debt and is not anybody's gift to anybody.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and twenty pence went out of it, being two quarts of lamp oil at ten pence, and there is nothing in the bag now, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-three days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-ninth night, and neither of them has moved. A boat of about nineteen tons with six men in her is lying on the foul ground off the south end of that mole in eleven foot of water inside that harbor's own line with a hole in her, and all six of that crew are ashore, and a man of about forty of that crew is hurt and has not said so, and this yard did not hire any of them and was not in that boat and is not going to be able to say that it was not in it. The fouling of the second seam is a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other and nobody in this yard has said one word about it tonight and nobody in that harbor is going to.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
