# Chapter 320

*Thursday the Third of June, the Fifty-Second Charge Walked on None, a Leaf With Four Columns Read Back in Full With the Count on the Face of It, the Whole Standing Inventory Once and Nowhere Else, and the Figure the Bag Is Short of the Wage of Four Marks*

Thursday the third of June, in the shed on the mole, and at the foot of that mole's steps in the afternoon.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-second of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it. The far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

**In these ten days the wall came down bare once, being the fifty-sixth on Monday the thirty-first of May past; the cistern was charged on three rota days, being the fiftieth, the fifty-first and the fifty-second, and this party walked on none of them; and there were two wage Tuesdays, being Tuesday the twenty-fifth of May past and Tuesday the first of June past, and the column did not move on either of them, so that thirty-two weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. Those three counts are this yard's own and are not taken out of any other book.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and nobody else, and the read-back was in the ship's own left hand and went into her own book and not onto the wall of anywhere.

**The first column of that leaf has fifty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were forty-three of them at the close of Monday the twenty-fourth of May past and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Tuesday the twenty-fifth to Wednesday the second of June, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own makes fifty-three.

The nine are: fourpence a candle and twenty-one words counted by a man in a shop; a form with a fourth box in it empty in about nine people's hearing; a man of about thirty-six and a clerk of about sixty and a room with a window onto the west basin; a shackle of chain out of about nine feet of mud off that west quay and a broken stud and a man of about forty-one across the top of the left foot; a sentence of fifteen words with a trade in the first four of them; a man in a blue coat of about fifty with a book at the head of a slip and the standing reason given out loud before the answer; two unlit candles left on the stone at the top of those steps and nine tons of salt moved inside that harbor's own line; a sheet dated the twenty-ninth of May and a barrel of lamp oil at four hundred and fifty pence; and two marks, seven shillings and fourpence at a counter over a chandler's, and a man of sixty-one back on the water with a bandaged right hand and a boat's hook in his left.

**The second column has one hundred and seventy-four lines in it at the hour of the read-back.** One hundred and forty-three of them were in it at the close of the twenty-fourth of May past and thirty-one went in between the twenty-fifth of May and the second of June, and three this afternoon, and one hundred and seventy-seven at the close. The said figure is that book of that yard's own and is not a count of paragraphs, and the first column's fifty-two and the fourth column's forty-five are paragraph counts and are checkable by anybody with a leaf and a pencil.

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The four lines above his named line are four, nameless, and are not struck out, and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered.**

**And the fourth column has forty-four lines in it at the hour of the read-back and forty-five at the close, and the forty-fifth is the last thing in that book.**

---

And then the three of them said what the ten days were, in about three lines each, and none of it was argued with and none of it has been improved on since, and it is about three different objects and all three of it is true.

**The first was about the notice, and the holder of that writ said it.**

"**Eight days ago a man of about sixty stood on a mole and said sixty-three words he had counted four times, and this yard did not answer him and did not refuse him, and left a space on that mole where a man's next sentence was going to go, and about nine people standing there remember there was a space. That notice is still on that market wall and has not been taken down and nothing has been put on that wall and nothing has come off it, and the two older naval notices on that wall are still on that wall, and the clerk keeps his own page and does not mention it. And a woman of about fifty-eight has two figures and two dates on a slate and the rest of nine, and the cheapest thing in this harbor went up six shillings on Tuesday and is not coming back down, and this yard has said out loud that it does not know whether those two things are in the same world and is not going to try to find out.**"

**The second was about the harbor, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**Seven of the herring third lay were lying off that mole this afternoon where there were eleven on the twenty-fourth of May, and not one of the seven has passed the line and not one of them has broken any rule of that harbor. A chandler's counter in the front street is shut to one man and open to everybody else, and the thing that shut it was four words a man of about thirty-six put on the front of a sentence at his own table on purpose on the Thursday night, and this yard sent nobody and asked nobody and told nobody and did not put a candle in a wheelhouse out of a bag with twenty pence in it. And a man of about thirty-six went out on the second of June with nine tons of salt in his boat and was not asked for a declaration and was not stopped and was not followed and came back in on the third. That is two Tuesdays in this harbor on which nothing happened and the first of them was the eighteenth of May past and this one is going in tonight, and neither of them can be checked by anybody, and I have been the one who counts since the sixteenth of February and I am telling this shed that I do not have a column for what it costs to be a Tuesday.**"

**The third was about the yard itself, and the woman of no office said it from the bench, and she was not asked for it.**

"**There is twenty pence in that bag and it went in twice and it went out once, and I have asked where it came from and I was told all of it, and I am not going to ask again, and I do not think anybody in this room is going to be able to tell me afterwards whether that was a good afternoon or a bad one. Two hires inside a line this yard cannot cross, for other people's money, for other people's purposes, and a man hurt in the first of them, and a bill for him out of a book with a hundred and sixty-nine pence less in it than it had. And nobody in this harbor can be angry at that yard for it, because there is no sentence about it and there is nobody to be angry at, and that is what it is.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the third day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said day by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ; and that the said leaf holds fifty-three entries in its first column, being forty-three on the twenty-fourth day of May past, nine from the twenty-fifth to the second of June, and one this evening; one hundred and seventy-seven lines in its second, being one hundred and forty-three, thirty-one and three; none in its third; and forty-five in its fourth, being thirty-five on the twenty-fourth day of May past, nine from the twenty-fifth to the second of June, and one this evening, which is the last thing in that book.*

*And that the whole of what came into that yard in the ten days ended on the said day is three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, in two hires at a box on the corner of that mole, one on Friday the twenty-eighth day of May past and one on Monday the thirty-first, on a private person's account and for a private person's purpose, and that neither of them was salvage and neither was a claim and neither was a claim window and neither was a charter and neither was a way of getting past any line; and that the whole of what went out of that yard in the said ten days is four hundred and eight pence, being two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, to a bonesetter of about sixty at a counter over a chandler's on Wednesday the second day of June; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence is a boat of about thirty tons with a man in her for a day, and that the said bag cannot buy one and did not buy one.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was not touched on any of the ten days, that nothing was put on the market wall of that harbor and nothing came off it, that the two older naval notices further along that wall are still on it, and that the hearing room and the market wall were not in any one of those ten days; and that no part of that yard's method, its figures or its fourth column is on that wall in that harbor.*

*And that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall of that fish market changed one figure on the first day of June and one only, being a barrel of lamp oil from three hundred and seventy-eight pence to four hundred and fifty pence, and left the February figure visible above the new one on the same board with both dates against it; and that the other eight of the nine figures on that slate did not move on that day, and that the small bottle of physic at fourteen pence with *none in that harbor* against it did not move, on purpose; and that nobody in that yard has been to that stall to look at the board since the twentieth day of May past and is not going to.*

*And that about eleven people on that coast cannot read the said notice, and that no person in that yard went and told one of them anything, and that nobody in that harbor is going to; and that the chandler's note is at thirty-one, not re-written, its three counts of weeks at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven; that the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing was measured; that four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days; that the plate in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, its note open, carried; that the case of five bells is not raised; that the lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second day of July are not touched; and that five pages are in five drawers in five towns and that yard has not looked in four of them.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that a harbor can be arranged wrongly in ten days by a man naming a trade in a market in about nine people's hearing, and that not one boat moved and not one sheet of paper moved and no person in that market was asked anything by anybody; that twenty pence is not the last of it and is not the whole of it, because the whole of it is a man of about forty-one who is not to put weight on the left foot for ten days and is not to be on the water for fourteen, and a man of about thirty-six who will buy no candles at that counter, and a chandler who did not ask and was not asked, and a clerk of about sixty with a quarter owed him who said the same three things in the same order twice; and that the thing that has no name in it is not the condition and is not a stage and did not cost a single day of these ten, and that a party which has taken money and been to a bonesetter is exactly like every other thing that moves things inside a blockade, and has not been made into anything else.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, and it is two private hires at one mark and eightpence on Friday the twenty-eighth of May and Monday the thirty-first, and it is not salvage and not a claim and not a claim window and not a charter and not a way of getting past any line, and every person in each of those boats was told all five of those things before the painter went and not after. The whole of what went out of that yard in these ten days is four hundred and eight pence, being two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, to a bonesetter of about sixty, at a counter over a chandler's, on Wednesday the second of June. A wage of four marks was not paid on either wage Tuesday and thirty-two weeks stand fallen due with no envelope, and the next falls due on Tuesday the eighth of June for the thirty-third week and will not move on it either. The wall came down bare once in the ten days and the cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them. The far bench was occupied on three of the ten days, being the twenty-eighth, the thirtieth and the second, and was not approached on any of them, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on all ten and went up no coast road on any of them.**

**And here is the whole of where this yard is at the end of it. A mole-head light not lit, its lens out since the seventh of May, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for oil is a barrel at four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted to a woman on the first of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and on the February figure that light was out in October. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twenty-third night. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and fifty-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, the chest not opened, the bellwright not asked in twenty days and no digit of anything he said printed. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid, not reduced by a crate and not paid by a question. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand, the third column unfilled and unappointed. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, and nobody has asked whose either is. Seven pieces of paper in that drawer and the charter's place empty, and a round of glass is in a house four days up that coast, and a page went on a wall there on the fourth of April and has not been taken down, and a man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait is not on the page and is not going to be asked anything. A can upon a stake at the outer marks is not on a Crown chart of about nine years and was there in the last week of March, and nobody has followed it and nobody in that yard has asked the man of about thirty-one one question about the chart he carries in a bag, and he is not a witness. About eleven people on this coast cannot read that notice, and no person in that yard has gone and told one of them anything, and nobody in that harbor is going to resolve it. A man of about thirty cannot read at all, two days down that coast, and is a man of that yard in about forty people's hearing and is not one, and has not been written to. The plate in eleven feet in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, its note open and carried, and the mechanism by which it is ever reached is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter out of a shed, and that sentence is on a page and has not been improved on. The case of five bells is not raised and the four people of that coast are not asked anything and the limit they wrote stands. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing was measured. The chandler's note is at thirty-one, not re-written, its three counts of weeks at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days. Five pages are in five drawers in five towns and this yard has not looked in four of them. The manifest is the *Aster Gannet*'s and the compartment is not named and the three documents are three.**

**And on the third of June next there is a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay who is not to put weight on the left foot for ten days and is not to be on the water for fourteen, and a man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons and no candles at a counter in the front street, and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a chart in a bag that he will not open, and eleven men in seven boats lying off inside their own harbor line with a stove on one afterdeck, and a man of fifty-one with nineteen winters disputed and a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November and a season of nine marks that is not going to be paid, and a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand who is not this volume's injury and is not going to be made a door, and a mark on the face of the second seam fouled about a foot and seven inches where it was on the nineteenth of May, with the whole of that ladder in the book of a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay who gave his name to nobody and was not asked for a figure and is not going to be, and a mark four hundred yards off it of a make nobody in that harbor makes, and the season of both at nine marks and neither paid.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and a harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and nobody in this country won anything this month, and the cheapest thing in this town went up six shillings on Tuesday and is not coming back down, and a man in a blue coat with a book came up a mole on a Sunday and said out loud that the instrument is bad and is not false, and nobody in that harbor improved on either half of it, and a man of about thirty-one has a chart in a bag that this yard has not asked to see and is not going to.**
