# Chapter 303

*Monday the Seventeenth of May, the Fifty-Fourth Time That Wall Came Down, the Forty-Seventh Charge Walked on None, a Crate Going Up That Coast on the Deck of a Coal Boat, and a Notice Going Up at Nine on a Wall That Was Bare at Eight*

Monday the seventeenth of May, on the mole and in the shed, in the fish market, and on the mole steps at seven in the morning.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-fourth time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on the said day or on any other day that this yard knows of. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-seventh of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**The said Monday brought nothing into this yard and took nothing out of it. Nobody sat on the far bench at any hour of the said Monday, the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of it, the man of sixty-one at that bench was asked nothing and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift.**

---

She came up the steps at about a quarter to seven with the crate on her own back, because she would not have two men carry it up a hundred and forty feet of stone and said so before anybody offered.

"**I am going to say the reason once,**" she said, "**on a step, at seven in the morning, with nobody in it, and then I am not going to say it again on this coast.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**Because you cannot keep it and I will not ask you to, and because a thing this yard has read and then put in a drawer in a shed is a house, and I said that out loud on these steps in nine people's hearing on the twenty-fourth of April past and I am not going to unsay it to be comfortable.**" She shifted the crate. "**And because nobody in this country has one and I am the only person I know who has ever held one, and that is not a reason to keep it. It is a reason to put it somewhere it is not a weapon in a shed.**"

"**Your forty marks.**"

"**Stand first after food, and are not reduced, and are not paid, and are not going to be paid by a crate, and if anybody in this harbor thinks a round of glass has bought a shilling of it they are welcome to say so to me in about forty people's hearing any day this week.**"

Nobody thanked her. He did not go down to the slip. She did not look up the hill. She went down the mole steps to the coal boat at about a quarter past seven and it was on the tide and it was gone before the market filled.

And it was not a goodbye and neither of them called it anything, and the second of them is the whole of what that is.

---

**The wall came down bare at eight.** It was bare because the last paper on it had come off at about four o'clock the previous afternoon, and that paper was the yard's own fourth return, which is the only paper that wall has ever held that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and it had been under a thumbtack and four drawing pins, which is what that clerk does at the end of a day.

The man who does it is a clerk of that office and he is about sixty and he has been a clerk for twenty-two years, and he is a man who says what a box holds and not one word past it, and he did not give his name and nobody asked him for it. He wiped the slate and did not restart the counter and wrote his own page and did not mention it to anybody, and at about nine o'clock in the morning he came out with a sheet of printed paper under a board and two cords and four nails and put it on the outside of the wall, on the outside of whatever was there, at about a quarter past nine.

**The other two papers on that wall were naval notices, of the second of December past and the twenty-second of June past, further along the same wall and not on the same square of it, and this yard did not take either of them down, and did not take this one down, and nobody in this harbor has taken any of the three down since.**

Nessa Pike read it standing up. She is twenty-seven and she has a burn scar across one forearm and a wrench she rarely puts down, and she read it once through and then read the five clauses that carry anything out loud on that mole in about nine people's hearing, and she read them as they are written and she did not stop at any of them.

"**It says this, and I am reading it and not explaining it. You can have the rest of it off the wall.**"

> **NOTICE — CLOSED-SEA SQUADRON, MORROW QUAD DISTRICT**
> *Issued at Morrow Quay the seventeenth day of May. Countersigned at a desk in Ternwatch. This notice is nailed up outside the market and is not a copy; the market copy is the original.*
>
> **1.** No vessel shall leave the port of Morrow Quay, or pass the line described in clause five, without a master's declaration in the form of the second schedule, made in writing before two witnesses, and lodged with the harbor authority at Morrow Quay before noon.
>
> **2.** The declaration shall state the vessel and her master, her cargo in general terms, and whether she carries material which is the subject of an unsettled claim.
>
> **3.** A master who declares truly is not detained, and his vessel is not held.
>
> **4.** A master who does not carry a declaration may be detained, and his vessel held to the outer marks.
>
> **5.** The line runs from the mole-head of Morrow Quay to a can upon a stake at the outer marks, and thence to the point named in the first schedule.
>
> **6.** This notice is not a closure of the port. Vessels may enter without a declaration.
>
> **7.** It continues until a notice says that it does not.
>
> **8.** Nothing in this notice is a judgment upon any vessel or any master, and no master is accused of anything by it.
> *Soren Vey*, closed-sea squadron.*

"**Say the last one again,**" said a man of about forty-five at the foot of the steps.

"**The last one is *nothing in this notice is a judgment upon any vessel or any master*. It is a courtesy and it is thirteen words and it is the only sentence in there that anybody put in on purpose for a person to read.**"

---

The market was full by about ten and about forty people were inside it, and a man of about thirty-four who is not of this yard and does not read out for a living and has no reason in the world to be in that market on a Monday morning read the whole of it out loud, once, standing at the end of the end stall, and then said what he could not do with it.

"**I can read it. I cannot tell you what four of those lines mean and I am not going to stand in here and guess at them in front of you, because if I guess and I am wrong then in a month somebody will say I read it wrong, and I will not be able to say that I only guessed, because I will have said it out loud in a market.**"

"**Which four.**"

"**Four, five, seven, and the last half of two. Clause five is a mole-head and a can on a stake and a point on a schedule, and I have been out of this harbor about nine times in my life and I know that mole-head and I do not know that can.**"

"**Say that again and slower,**" said a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, and he said it again and slower, and about nine people in that market said they had not been out there this month either, and one said he had not been out there since February, and the man of thirty-four said *then there are nine of us* and nobody improved on it.

**And the woman of about fifty-eight put her hand on the side of her board and said the thing that is the whole of the notice, and she said it once, and about forty people heard it, and nobody has improved on it since.**

"**That is not a closure and that is what makes it worse than a closure, and I want somebody to write it down so that it is written down. A closure has a door in it and you can stand at a door. This has got a door in the sixth line and the sixth line is the true sentence, and every man who comes up this quay in the next four months is going to walk through that door and be glad of it, because he can come in without asking anybody anything. And the door is the price of every barrel he cannot carry out without a piece of paper off a desk in Ternwatch. And I am not saying that from the yard. I am saying it from a board, and I have had that board nine years, and I have never once had a thing on it go up more than it did this morning.**"

Then she said the one figure, and she said it as a figure and not as a speech, and this yard entered it in the first column because a person can go and look at a barrel.

"**Lamp oil was thirty-one shillings and sixpence a barrel in this market in February. It is not going to be quoted in this market this month, and that is not because anybody here has raised it. The mole-head light is not lit because a light costs oil, and its lens has been out since the seventh of May, and on the figure I have got it will be out in October.**"

---

Then a man of about thirty-two asked the question that about nine people had been standing on since the middle of the first clause, and he asked it badly, and it was still the right question.

"**Lodged with the harbor authority. Where is that?**"

Nobody answered him for about eleven seconds, which is a long time in a fish market.

"**The harbor authority is on the west basin,**" said Nessa Pike. "**I have been in that room. There is a bench, a table, a slate wall and a clerk, and the clerk is the man who wiped this morning and put that up at a quarter past nine, and he is a real clerk and he is owed his own quarter and he has been for two months.**"

"**Then a man can lodge it.**"

"**A man can try,**" said Nessa Pike.

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the seventeenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a notice of the closed-sea squadron, in eight clauses, in the words of the fifth clause, signed with a name and a rank and no title, was put up on the outside of the market wall of this harbor at about a quarter past nine in the morning of the said day, on a stretch of that wall which had been taken down at about four o'clock the previous afternoon and was the yard's own fourth return, and that there were two other naval notices further along the same wall and neither was taken down by this yard and neither was taken down by anybody at any hour of the said day, and that the clerk who put it up did not mention it to any person and kept his own page and did not restart the counter on the said wall.*

*And that the said notice states in its first clause that no vessel shall leave the port or pass a line described in the fifth clause without a master's declaration made in writing before two witnesses and lodged with the harbor authority at Morrow Quay before noon; in its second that the declaration shall state the vessel and her master, her cargo in general terms, and whether she carries material which is the subject of an unsettled claim; in its third that a master who declares truly is not detained and his vessel is not held; in its fourth that a master who does not carry a declaration may be detained and his vessel held to the outer marks; in its fifth that the line runs from the mole-head of that mole to a can upon a stake at the outer marks and thence to a point named in the first schedule; in its sixth that the notice is not a closure of the port and that vessels may enter without a declaration; in its seventh that it continues until a notice says that it does not; and in its eighth that nothing in it is a judgment upon any vessel or any master.*

*And that a crate went off that mole on the deck of a coal boat at about a quarter past seven in the morning of the said day and was in a house four days up that coast by the said evening, and that this yard did not go down to the slip and did not see it off and that no person of this yard laid a hand on it, and that the said round of glass is in a house that this yard cannot reach.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a person of no office of this harbor, thirty, carried a crate up a hundred and forty feet of stone on her own back at about a quarter to seven in the morning of the said day and gave a reason for it once on the steps at about seven o'clock and did not give it twice, and that the said reason was that a thing this yard has read and then put in a drawer in a shed is a house; and that her forty marks first after food were not reduced and were not paid and were not reduced by a crate, and that nobody in that yard thanked her and she did not thank anybody and that it is the fourth of those and that neither of them has said one word about it on any of the four.*

*And that the notice on the said wall names a harbor authority which issued no instrument after the thirty-first day of March past and whose season ended on that day, and that the clerk of that office is a real clerk and is owed his own quarter and has been for two months, and that a declaration cannot be lodged with an office that has no season; and that nobody in that market or in that yard said out loud on the said day that the said notice was drafted so as to be impossible, and that the reason against the line of the saying is the reason of the twenty-fourth day of April past about a person who keeps a page, being that a person who says *they did it on purpose* has stopped being the party that says what a thing is not, and there is no evidence either way and there may never be any and this yard has entered that it does not know.*

*And that the clerk of that harbor authority is not the clerk of the circuit office of this coast, who is a different man in a different building and has not been up this hill since the twenty-eighth of October past; that the two of them are the two mouths of a clause on this coast and are not one man; and that the said clerk of the harbor authority did not give his name on the said day and was not asked for it.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a notice which is careful in every clause is not a kindness and is not a trap and is not a mistake, and that *nothing in this notice is a judgment upon any vessel or any master* is a courtesy and is the sentence a person reads twice; that a door in the sixth line is not a kindness and is not a rope, and that nine people in a market who have not been out past the mole-head this month is not a finding and is not a count of anything; and that a light which is not lit because oil has a price and a lens has been out since the seventh is not a small thing and is not going to be a smaller thing in October.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and sixty-one pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fivepence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and seventy-nine pence. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the fifty-fourth time and the cistern was charged for the forty-seventh and walked on none of it and the far bench was not occupied at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and sixth night, and neither of them has moved. Eight clauses are on a wall in this harbor in about nine people's hearing and one of them is going to cost the cheapest thing in this town about four months.**
