# Chapter 310

*Monday the Twenty-Fourth of May, the Fifty-Fifth Time That Wall Came Down, the Forty-Ninth Charge Walked on None, a Leaf With Four Columns Read Back, Three True Things Said About Three Different Objects, and a Man of About Thirty-Four of a Screw-Steam Packet Out of Bramble Strait Standing on the Mole Steps With a Copied Line in His Hand*

Monday the twenty-fourth of May, in the shed on the mole, and on the mole steps at about half past five in the afternoon.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-fifth time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on the said day or on any other day that this yard knows of. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten on the said morning by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the forty-ninth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**The said Monday brought nothing into this yard and took nothing out of it. The far bench was occupied on it from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said Monday, the man of sixty-one at that bench was asked nothing and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and nobody else.

**The first column of that leaf has forty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were thirty-three of them on the fourteenth of May and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Saturday the fifteenth to Sunday the twenty-third, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own makes forty-three.

The nine are: a crate on the mole steps for a fourth day and a tender lying off the south end all night; a copper pipe at Ternwatch and a boat's man at four marks a day and three shillings a week for a slip; a notice of eight clauses going up at a quarter past nine on a wall that was bare at eight; a boat of about seventy tons that went out at eleven without a piece of paper and came in at six; three soundings off the south end and a mark fouled to about a foot and seven inches; nine figures on the back of a slate and a tenth figure that is the price of a sheet of paper; eleven boats of the herring third lay lying off with about fourteen men in them and nine words carried up that hill; two hundred and eleven copies of one nine-line sheet of a drowned city in a room off the fish market, and a copy of the notice bought at the same counter for the same penny; and a stove on the afterdeck of a boat of about fifty tons, and a can on a stake that is not on a chart of nine years.

**The second column has one hundred and thirty-nine lines in it at the hour of the read-back.** One hundred and fifteen of them were in it on the fourteenth of May and twenty-four went in between the fifteenth and the twenty-third, and four this afternoon, and one hundred and forty-three at the close.

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth of May and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The four lines above his named line are four, nameless, and are not struck out, and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered.**

**And the fourth column has thirty-four lines in it at the hour of the read-back and thirty-five at the close, and the thirty-fifth is the last thing in that book.**

---

And then the three of them said three things, and none was argued with afterwards and none has been improved on since, and they are about three different objects and all three of them are true.

**The first was about the notice, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**There are two true sentences in that thing and they are four lines apart and nobody in this harbor has put them next to each other. Clause six says this is not a closure of the port and vessels may enter without a declaration, and that is a true sentence, and it is the reason eleven boats of the third lay will get in and land and the reason a boat of about seventy tons came back in at six o'clock on Tuesday instead of at four. And clause one says no vessel shall leave, or pass a line, without a form lodged with an office that issued nothing after the thirty-first of March, and that is also a true sentence, and it is the reason the same eleven boats are lying off with a stove on one of them. The two sentences were both read out loud on that mole on the seventeenth of May in about nine people's hearing in about thirty seconds by a man of thirty-four who is not of this yard and who does not read out for a living. I am not going to stand on these steps and say that anybody wrote them four lines apart on purpose, because I do not know it, and I am not going to stand here and say nobody did, because I do not know that either, and I have been getting that wrong in my own head for seven days and I am writing it down so that I have to look at it.**"

**The second was about the copies, and the holder of that writ said it.**

"**A price list of a penny a sheet, and about two hundred and eleven of them, and I want to say three things about that in the right order and I am not going to get the order better than this on the first attempt. One: I cannot make it a conspiracy, because a conspiracy has a man behind it and the best I can do with two hundred and eleven is a counter off a fish market and a woman of fifty-nine who has a book in her own hand and will not stop. Two: the reason it is *probably* not a conspiracy is that nobody in this harbor has ever been sure of a price, and that is not a compliment, it is the ninth figure on a slate this week. Three:**" He stopped. "**Three, and I am not going to do it. There is a version of this where I put a woman with a cloth in front of a person with a pen, and I wanted to do it on Saturday, and I did not do it, and I am not going to do it, and the reason is that the only reason I would be able to do it is that I am good at finding things out, and that is the whole of what I am and I am not going to be the other thing as well.**"

**The third was about the wall, and a man of about sixty of the first lay said it from the top of the steps, and he had not been asked up there and was not asked anything.**

"**Seven days that sheet has been up and I have read it every day of the seven, and I have read it every day because I can read.**" He put his hand on the rail of that mole. "**And there are about eleven people on this coast who cannot read it. Not cannot read a notice — cannot read. A man of about thirty of this town, two days down this coast, who is a man of that yard in about forty people's hearing and is not one. A woman of fifty-eight in a house four streets off that cannot do more than a name. And nine or ten I could name if I sat down and I am not going to sit down. Seven days a sheet has been up and nobody in this town has said one word about that, and I am saying it now, and I have not got anything else to offer and I am not asking that yard for anything.**"

Nobody asked him for anything else and he went down the mole steps.

The boat came in at about five in the afternoon and lay off the south end with her bow to the west, and there were about nine people on the mole and they counted, because about nine people were on most of the evenings that week.

**He was about thirty-four and he came ashore in a dinghy with a painter and came up the mole steps on foot, and he was not carrying anything except a sheet of paper folded in four in the breast of a coat, and no person in that yard had sent for him and no person in that yard knew he was coming.**

He did not go up the hill. He stood at the top of the steps, which is the stone, and he unfolded the sheet and held it at his side and he read it out loud, and about nine people heard it, and he read it in the words it is written in and did not comment on it and did not name anybody.

"**Page of a sheet, put up on a market wall in a town four days up this coast on the fourth of April past, carried in about sixty boats: *a page in a shed on the mole at Morrow Quay, in a drawer, fourth of five.*"** He folded it again. "**That is the line. That is all of it. I have been four days on the water to read that line off a wall and I have not been sent and I am not going to tell you who I work for because I do not work for anybody, and I would like that said in that order and not the other one about.**

"**I am not going up your hill. I have not been asked anything and I am not asking anything. I am standing on a step because a step is a place where things get said in front of people, and whatever is written in your drawer, if anything is, is a thing that somebody with a pen somewhere is now going to go and look for, and I would rather it was said out loud in front of nine people on a Monday afternoon than found quietly by somebody who did not tell anybody he was looking.**"

"**Did the market there take it down,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No,**" said the man of about thirty-four. "**I asked. They said no. And I am not going to tell you their reasons either, because I have been in about eleven markets on this coast in four days and I have noticed that the people who put a thing up and the people who would not take it down are not the same sort of person and there is no rule about it and I have not got time to find one.**"

"**Then this is the whole of what you came for.**"

"**It is.**" He looked at the crate, which was not on the steps any more, and at the bench, and at the two tarpaulins on the crown. "**And I will say the last of it, because I have four days of water in me and I am not going up your hill. That line is on a wall in a town four days up this coast and it is on this shed in a drawer and it is on about sixty boats, and it is on those boats because a yard printed it, in its first year, with a place for every copy of itself on the face of it. I have not got anything against that. I have got a sheet of paper, and I have come four days to say it out loud on a step, and I am going back on the tide at about seven and I am not going to write to you.**"

"**We are not going to write to you,**" said Calder Marr. "**And nobody in this yard is going to go up that hill with you and nobody is going to hand you anything and nobody is going to ask you one more question, and the reason is the ninth of April past and it is the reason that nine people have been kept off this hill since the first of May, and it has not been improved on in three weeks and it is not going to be improved on for you.**"

"**Good,**" said the man of about thirty-four, and went down the steps, and was not thanked, and did not ask for anything, and was not on the page.

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-fourth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ; and that the said leaf holds forty-three entries in its first column, being thirty-three on the fourteenth day of May, nine from the fifteenth to the twenty-third, and one this evening; one hundred and forty-three lines in its second, being one hundred and fifteen, twenty-four and four; none in its third; and thirty-five in its fourth, being twenty-five on the fourteenth of May, nine from the fifteenth to the twenty-third, and one this evening, which is the last thing in that book.*

*And that three people said three things on the said afternoon about the said notice, the said copies, and the said wall, and that the three differ in what they are about and not in whether they are correct; and that a man of about sixty of the first lay said the third of them from the top of those steps without being asked up there and was not asked anything and went down again and asked for nothing.*

*And that a man of about thirty-four came ashore in a dinghy at about five in the afternoon of the said day and came up the mole steps on foot and did not go up the hill and stood on the stone at the top of the steps and read out loud, in about nine people's hearing, a line off a sheet folded in four, being: *a page in a shed on the mole at Morrow Quay, in a drawer, fourth of five*; and that the said line is carried on a sheet put up on a market wall in a town four days up that coast on the fourth day of April past, which is a copy of the public provenance duplicate and is in about sixty boats; and that the man who read it said that he had been four days on the water to read it off a wall, that he is not going to say who he works for, that he does not work for anybody, and that he came to say it out loud in front of people rather than have it found quietly; and that he went down the steps at about a quarter to six in the afternoon of the said day and went off on the tide at about seven and was not thanked and was not on the page.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that no person of that yard sent for the said man and no person of that yard knew he was coming, and that he was not asked one question by that yard about the sheet, the wall, the town four days up that coast, or the sixty boats; and that the reason against the line of the asking is the reason of the ninth day of April past, and that the same yard printed ten copies of the public provenance duplicate in its first year with the place of every copy of itself on the face of each of them, and that a community put one of them on a wall of its own and this yard did not put it there and has not written to that town and is not going to.*

*And that the said leaf's third column has not been filled on any of the ten days and nobody has been appointed to it and no name of the boy of twenty-two is in any column of that book and none has ever been; that the chandler's note is at thirty-one, not re-written, its three counts of weeks at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, and was not said out loud a fifth time; that the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing measured; the notice of the thirty-first of October stands with its four clauses and no tenth acknowledgement; four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days; the plate in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, its note open, carried for a fourth volume; the case of five bells is not raised; the lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second of July are not touched; five pages are in five drawers in five towns, not looked in, and a page of them went up on a wall in a town four days up that coast on the fourth of April past; and a round of glass is in a house in that town and this yard has not written and is not going to write.*

*And in the fourth column of the said leaf, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that a line read out loud by a stranger on a mole step in about nine people's hearing is not a discovery, is not a claim, and is not the same thing as a door being opened, and that this yard did not open one and was not asked to and will not; that a party which has spent its whole working life refusing to be findable and is now findable in ten days has not been defeated by anybody and has not been saved by anybody and has not been warned; and that eleven people on this coast who cannot read a notice is not an instrument and is not a complaint and is the only thing said out loud on that mole in ten days that this yard cannot put in a column and is not going to.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence. The figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. What came in in the ten days ended on the said Monday is nothing at all, and what went out is one mark, eight shillings and a penny. The wall came down bare twice in the ten days, the fifty-fourth on Monday the seventeenth and the fifty-fifth on Monday the twenty-fourth, the counter not restarting and the clerk keeping his own page. The cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them, the forty-seventh, the forty-eighth and the forty-ninth. There was one wage Tuesday in these ten days, being the eighteenth, and the column did not move on it, and thirty weeks have fallen due with no envelope, and the next falls due on Tuesday the twenty-fifth for the thirty-first week and it will not move on that either. The far bench was occupied on three of the ten days, the nineteenth, the twenty-second and the twenty-fourth, and was not approached on any of them, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on all ten and went up no coast road on any of them.**

**And here is the whole of what this yard is at the end of it. A mole-head light not lit because a light costs oil and its lens out since the seventh of May, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for oil is a barrel at thirty-one shillings and sixpence in February, and on that figure it will be out in October. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirteenth night. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and forty-seven days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, the chest not opened, the bellwright not asked in ten days. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid, and she is four days up that coast in a house with a round of glass in a drawer and is not back until the twenty-eighth. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June stand, the third column unfilled and unappointed. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, and nobody has asked whose either is. Seven pieces of paper in that drawer and the charter's place empty, and one of the seven is a copy of a drowned city, and a round of glass is in a house four days up that coast, and a page went on a wall there in April. A line was put on a boat at this harbor's own slip and a copper pipe is four days away in Ternwatch and a slip is three shillings a week. Eleven boats of the herring third lay lie off that mole with about fourteen men in them, none of them past the line, all of them inside, and a stove burns on the afterdeck of one of them, and a season of nine marks is not going to be paid. A can upon a stake is not on a Crown chart of nine years standing and was there in the last week of March. Nothing in that shed was signed, nobody in that yard was thanked, nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and nobody in that country has been told one word of it by anybody who is not in it.**

**And on the twenty-fourth of May next there is a sheet of paper folded in four going back down a mole in a man's breast pocket with a line on it that names this shed, and eight clauses on a market wall in this town in about nine people's hearing every day of the next four months, of which the sixth is a door and the first is a lock and they are four lines apart; a mole-head light not lit and an oil price from February on a slate in a fish market; a man of about sixty of the first lay on a mole step saying that about eleven people on this coast cannot read a notice nobody has mentioned; a man of fifty-one with a stove on his afterdeck and nineteen winters disputed; the mark of the second seam fouled about a foot and seven inches, and a mark four hundred yards off it of a make nobody in this harbor makes, and the season of both at nine marks and neither paid; and the boy not on the stone, and a day lost to a thing that has no name in it, and a name said out loud once in a boat ten days ago that is neither the boy's nor the holder's and was not said again.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and a harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and nobody in this country won anything this week, and the cheapest thing in this town has gone up and is not coming back down, and a sheet a stranger can read out loud on a mole step is a bigger instrument than a boat, and this yard printed it.**
