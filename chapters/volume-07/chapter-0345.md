# Chapter 345

*Tuesday the Twenty-Ninth of June, the Thirty-Sixth Week and a Column That Did Not Move, About Forty People in That Fish Market, a Man on His Own Feet on the Floor of It Saying Out Loud That He Asked Eleven Men Who Had Boats and Did Not Ask Six Men Who Had Not Got One, and Neither a Comfort Nor a Word Spoken to Him Afterwards for a Minute and Three-Quarters*

Tuesday the twenty-ninth of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in that shed at about four in the afternoon.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and it was the only wage Tuesday to fall due inside these ten days, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and twenty envelopes stand on that bench in that chest and did not move, and thirty-six weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday, and the sixtieth charge falls on Thursday the first of July next. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said Tuesday and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that market at about eleven in the morning of the said day and said nothing to anybody and was not comforted and was not comforted afterwards either. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, and he was not in that market at any hour of the said day and about forty people were in it and he is the man this yard most wants in a market and did not go and get him.**

---

He was told about it on the Sunday night and he did not refuse it and he did not go and ask anybody's leave.

The end stall gave him the table again, the same table, and the same thing happened in about nine people's hearing at half past ten on the morning of the twenty-ninth, and by eleven the market held about forty, and nobody put a notice up anywhere and no paper was read out of a drawer.

And a man of about thirty-eight of no standing whatever, who had said at the end stall three weeks before that anybody who wanted to hear the book of that boat of about nineteen tons could come and hear it off the owner's own table, said the two sentences that got the people there, and they were the same two sentences, and a man of about fifty-one said afterwards that the man of about thirty-eight was a man who could not finish a sentence and had got very good at the beginnings of them.

Calder Marr walked out from the end of the aisle at about eleven and stood on the floor of that market, on his own feet, in about forty people's hearing, and said the following, and he said it once, and he did not say it again on that day or after it, and nobody in that market asked him a question about it and nobody improved on it.

"**On Friday the twenty-fifth day of June past I asked eleven men of this harbor one question in eleven places, and I said the two prices out loud to every one of them before I asked any of them, being that the soundings on that water are of a coast that has moved and there is no figure anybody may measure out, and that the road can be used only if a crew gives up the Ternwatch road for the season, of about eleven tons to a lighter. Ten of them said yes and one of them said no and said it in the street in the same words he said it to me in May, and one of the ten said it three times across that day and the next, yes at eleven o'clock in the morning, no at four in the afternoon, and yes again at eight o'clock on the Saturday evening, and all three of his hours are on the sheet.**"

"**I was in a boat of about thirteen tons on the night of the twenty-sixth that belonged to a man of about thirty-eight of no lay, at his own asking, on his own account, and this yard paid no penny of it and had no instrument and did not advise it and is not going to advise it, and there was no vessel of this yard's on that water and there was no lead of this yard's on that water, and I went past a line that is described in the fifth clause of a notice nailed up outside this market without a declaration, and that is a breach, and the breach is that man's boat's and not mine.**"

"**And here is the part I have come to say, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be asked about it afterwards.**"

"**Six men of this harbor lost the only boat that goes out when a thing is in the water, on Saturday the nineteenth day of June past, at twenty minutes to six in the evening, and all six of them are ashore, and that boat of about nineteen tons is on the foul off the south end of this mole in eleven foot of water and is going down by the head, and there is no second boat in this harbor and there was no second boat in this harbor last night either, and there is no instrument in this country by which a party of my standing could lay a claim on her and this yard has none and is not going to acquire one.**"

"**I did not ask them. There was nothing to ask them with. I asked eleven men who had boats and I did not ask six men who had not got one, and that is the choice, and it is mine, and I am not going to hand it to a tide or to a bell or to a man with a hook, and I am not going to stand here and say that was the right thing to do, because I do not know that it was, and I have not got a figure for what it cost and I am not going to invent one, and I am not going to be told in this market that the eleven were braver than the six, because the eleven were in a position and the six were not, and a man is not brave for being in a position.**"

He stopped. He did not add anything, and he did not look round for anybody's face, and about forty people in a fish market in the open stood there and about a third of them said nothing and the rest said a great deal, and none of it was to him.

He was not comforted by anybody in that market and nobody tried, and after it there was nothing in that market for about a minute and three-quarters in which anybody said anything at all to him, and two women near the front of the aisle looked at each other, and neither of them said anything either.

---

The first thing said was not said to him, and it was said by the man of about fifty-one of the six.

"**I am not going to thank you and I am not going to say that was the right thing, and I would like both of those to be written down as said by me.**"

He put his hand flat on the end stall's table, which is the thing a man does when he is about to be asked a question and does not want it asked.

"**I want the six of us on a page with our names and the nineteenth of June and twenty minutes to six in the afternoon and the price, which is a boat, and I am not going to read it out in a market and I am not going to be asked to. I am going to write it tonight in a book of ours and I am going to put it on that mole and not in here, and I do not know what is going to happen to it and I do not care.**"

Then the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, over the shoulder of her slate, without being asked.

"**And I will say the other half of it because nobody else is going to. There is physic in this market that was not in it on Friday and there is four ton of flour in a shed on that quay and there is eleven ton of oil in a lighter, and I gave the physic away before eleven o'clock on Monday morning and I would do it again, and I have not moved a figure on that board and I am not going to move one, and a figure on a board is not moved because a boat came in at half past seven in the morning.**"

And Josiah Mear, of about fifty-five, of a boat of about twenty-four tons on that slip, who had said no on the Friday and whose refusal is the first line on a sheet in a drawer in a shed on that mole.

"**I said no,**" he said, "**and my name is at the head of that thing with the hour on it, and nobody has taken it off and nobody has read it out as an example of anything, and I said no and my boat is on a hard with a line on her and I am the only man in this market this morning who is glad about that and I am not going to explain why and I would like it noticed that I am not going to.**"

And a chandler of about fifty, from behind his own counter, one sentence, no figures, being a man who has a board of his own with four marks against the yard on it and has had it since the first of May past and has said out loud four times in seven months that he is not going to say it a fifth time.

"**I would like it recorded that I have got nothing to say about it,**" he said, "**and that is a thing I have wanted to say for six weeks and I have wanted to say it about other things and I am not going to.**"

And Elias Voke, fifty-one, of the *Anna Ray*, standing at the end of the aisle with his hands in a wet coat, who said four sentences and had them all out in about nine seconds and then stopped and did not say a fifth.

"**I have nineteen winters in a list in a city and I have a season of nine marks that is not going to be paid and I have got four ton of flour. I did not ask anybody for permission and nobody asked me for any. I would like it on the sheet that I have got a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November and I have not had one word about a thing, and that is all, and I am not going to be a man in a market this morning.**"

---

And a man of about thirty-eight of no standing whatever stood at the end of the aisle for about forty minutes of that market and said nothing at all, and that is entered.

He has had three sentences in that market this month and a man of about fifty-one said out loud on the twenty-second of June that he was not going to be a man in a market who has had four, and on the twenty-ninth of June he stood at the end of the aisle for about forty minutes and said nothing, and about nine people were within ten feet of him the whole time, and he did not begin anything, and nobody finished anything for him, and the space that was left on a mole head in this harbor on the thirty-first day of May past was not filled and is not a smaller space for a man having stood in it for forty minutes and said nothing.

Nobody proposed a body. Nobody proposed a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard. The word was said in no mouth in that market on the said morning, and the man who asked the question of this coast on a mole head on the thirty-first day of May past in sixty-three words which he counted four times was in that market at about half past eleven and did not ask it again and has not asked it since and is not going to.

---

In that shed at about four in the afternoon the leaf was read back in full, all four columns including the empty ones, in the ship's own left hand, in the presence of the holder of that writ, and it was read in front of the woman of no office, who asked for nothing and was asked nothing.

"**The first column has seventy-seven entries in it at the hour of this reading,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and that is a count of entries and a stranger with a pencil can count it. The second column has two hundred and sixty-three lines in it at the hour of this reading, and that is this book of ours and is not a count of anybody's paragraphs. The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not mine, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since. And the fourth column has sixty-nine lines in it at the hour of this reading, and that is a count of lines and a stranger can count that one too, and it will be seventy on Wednesday and seventy-five on Sunday, and the seventy-fifth is going to be the last thing in that book.**"

She read the third column in full, as she has read it at the close of every week since the sixteenth day of February past, and the hand at the head of it kept its place, and nobody asked the boy one word about the three nots standing against the eighth of June, because he was not on the stone and was put on nothing and was not in that shed and was not in that market.

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-sixth week, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and that the column did not move, and that thirty-six weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about forty people's hearing and not in a room and with no paper read out of a drawer, the holder of that writ stood on the floor of that market on his own feet and said out loud, once, and was not asked a question about it and it was not improved on by anybody: that on Friday the twenty-fifth he asked eleven men of that harbor one question in eleven places, having named the two prices out loud to every one of them before he asked any of them, and that ten said yes and one said no and one of the ten said it three times with all three hours on the sheet; and that he was in a boat of about thirteen tons on the night of the twenty-sixth belonging to a man of about thirty-eight of no lay, at that man's own asking and on his own account, and that this yard paid no penny of it and had no instrument and did not advise it, and that no vessel of this yard's was on that water; and that six men of that harbor lost the only boat that goes out when a thing is in the water on Saturday the nineteenth at twenty minutes to six in the evening and are ashore, and that he did not ask them, and that there was nothing to ask them with, and that the choice was his and he was not going to say it was the right one and had no figure for what it cost and was not going to invent one, and was not going to be told in that market that the eleven were braver than the six because the eleven were in a position and the six were not.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that nobody comforted the holder of that writ in that hearing and nobody tried, and that about a minute and three-quarters passed in which anybody said anything to him, and that two women near the front of that aisle looked at each other and neither of them said anything either; and that the first thing said after it was said by a man of about fifty-one of the six and not to him, being that he was not going to thank him and was not going to say it was the right thing, and that he wanted the six of them on a page with their names and the nineteenth of June and twenty minutes to six in the afternoon and the price, being a boat, and was not going to read it out in a market and was not going to be asked to; and that the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said the other half, being that there is physic in that market that was not in it on Friday and that she gave it away before eleven o'clock on Monday and would do it again, and that she has not moved a figure on that board and is not going to move one, and that a figure on a board is not moved because a boat came in at half past seven in the morning; and that a man of about fifty-five whose refusal is the first line on that sheet said that he said no and his name is at the head of it with the hour on it and nobody has taken it off and that he is glad his boat is on a hard and is not going to explain why; and that a chandler of about fifty said that he would like it recorded that he has got nothing to say about it, and that it is the first time in six weeks he has wanted to say that about anything and he is not going to say it about anything else; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one said four sentences and stopped and would not say a fifth, and asked for it to be on the sheet that he has a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November past and has had not one word about a thing; and that a man of about thirty-eight of no standing whatever stood at the end of that aisle for about forty minutes and said nothing at all, and that he has had three sentences in that market in a month and is not going to have a fourth, and that nobody finished anything for him and the space left on a mole head in the month past was not filled.*

*And that nobody proposed a body, a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard in that hearing, and that the word for none of them was said, and that a man of about sixty of the first lay who asked the question of this coast out loud at a mole head on the thirty-first day of May past in sixty-three words which he counted four times was in that market at about half past eleven and did not ask it again.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventieth line:*

*What it is not: — that a thing said on a man's feet on the floor of a market in about forty people's hearing a second time is not a repetition and is not a summing up and is not the first sentence improved on by anybody; and that a man not being comforted after it is not a man being shunned and was not a man being shunned on the twenty-third day of June past and is not one on the twenty-ninth; and that a man of about fifty-one saying he is not going to thank him and is not going to say it was the right thing is not a reproach and is not an insult and is not going to be put on any page as one by anybody in this yard; and that a chandler saying he has got nothing to say about it is not a refusal to speak and is not a thing anybody in that market is going to argue him out of before noon.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and the sixtieth charge falls on Thursday the first of July next. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-three days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-ninth night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor have no boat and one of them is going to lose a berth and nobody in that harbor has a figure for what a berth is worth to a man of fifty-one. A man of about forty-five of the third lay is off the water for nine days with two fingers of his left hand stiff. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the fifteenth day of the ninety, and a man said a thing out loud on the floor of a market this morning and was not comforted by anybody and has not been improved on by anybody in this yard and is not going to be, and nobody in that market proposed anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
