# Chapter 304

*Tuesday the Eighteenth of May, the Thirtieth Week With No Envelope, a Clerk With a Quarter Owed Him for Two Months, and a Boat of About Seventy Tons That Went Out at Eleven Without a Piece of Paper*

Tuesday the eighteenth of May, in the shed on the mole, at the harbor authority's office on the west basin in the morning, and off the mole in the afternoon.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and thirty weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, the thirtieth of them being this day. Nothing came into that yard on it and nothing went out of it. The wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it. And the far bench was not occupied on the said Tuesday at any hour, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

He is a clerk of the harbor authority of Morrow Quay and he is about sixty and he has been a clerk for twenty-two years, and he did not give his name on the Monday afternoon and was not asked for it, and on Monday afternoon and on Tuesday morning he was a man in a room with one man in it. He is not the clerk of the circuit office of this coast, who is a different man in a different building and who has not been up this hill since the twenty-eighth of October past.

The man was fifty and of the second lay and had a coaster of about seventy tons alongside that came in on the Friday before last and was going out on the Tuesday for a cargo of salt out of a yard up the coast.

"**I have the form,**" he said. "**I had it made at a desk in Ternwatch on Monday at nine in the morning and two men in a chandler's shop stood beside me while I wrote it, and they have both put their names to it, and I have got it here in a coat.**"

"**Let me see it.**"

The clerk looked at it for about a minute and a half and put it down on the table in front of him and did not move it toward any book.

"**This is a proper form,**" he said. "**They have got the paper right and the ink is the ink they use at Ternwatch and the two names are real names, because I know both of the men and one of them owes my wife's brother a shilling. There is nothing wrong with this.**"

"**Then lodge it.**"

The clerk put both hands flat on the table.

"**I cannot lodge it. I have told you the reason and I told you the reason yesterday afternoon and you have come back this morning, which is fair, because yesterday afternoon I said it quickly.**"

"**Say it slowly.**"

"**I have no season.**" He said it the way a man reads a line off a page. "**The season of this office ended on the thirty-first of March and it ended with nothing issued, and there is no instrument of this authority in existence after that day, and there is no book of this authority that a paper can be put into, and there is no person in this room except me, and I have no power to receive anything, and I have had none since the first of April.**"

"**You could write it in a book.**"

"**I could write it in any book on that table and in a week there would be a page in it with nothing at the top of it, and I have spent twenty-two years learning that a page in a book in a room is the same thing as a page in a drawer in a house, and it is the same thing, and I have watched it twice.**" He turned his hands over. "**And I am not going to be the man who invents a season in the first week it is convenient to have one.**"

"**Then what do I do.**"

"**I do not know,**" said the clerk.

"**You have been a clerk twenty-two years.**"

"**I have been a clerk twenty-two years and I have never once been asked what to do about a notice that names my office and my office is not there, and I have had about a night to think about it and I have come up with nothing and I am not going to stand in this room and make one up, because a thing I make up in May is a thing somebody is still looking at in nine years and it will have my name at the top of it.**"

The man of fifty stood in that room for a while and did not say anything and then said:

"**Then I will go out without it.**"

"**That is a decision about your boat and not about my office,**" said the clerk, "**and I would be obliged if you did not make it in here, and I am saying that because there are four people in this harbor who will be told where you were this morning and one of them is me.**"

---

The boat went out at about eleven in the morning.

That is the whole of it. She was the coaster of about seventy tons and she had a master of fifty and two men and she had a cargo of salt and she had no declaration in her and she was not asked for one and she was not stopped and she was not spoken to and she was not followed, and she lay off the south end of that mole for about four hours in a flat calm doing nothing at all, and she came back in at about six in the afternoon and took her berth and the two men came off her and went up the front street and one of them stopped at the chandler's and the other went to his house.

Nessa Pike watched her come in and watched her take her berth and wrote three lines in the second column and did not say one word about it on the mole and did not say one word about it in the shed until about half past nine that night.

"**She has not got a paper and she went out and she came back,**" she said, when she did say it.

"**She has not got a paper,**" said Calder Marr. "**And a man of fifty stood in a room on the west basin this morning and was told by a man who has been a clerk for twenty-two years that he could not put a piece of paper in a book, and he was right to be told it, and the man was right to say it, and neither of them is a fool and neither of them is a villain and a Tuesday has gone past and everybody in this harbor is exactly where they were on Monday.**"

"**Write that down as what it is and not as what it is not.**"

"**I will write it down as what it is,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and what it is, in my hand, on that leaf, is that in nine months this harbor will have a record of a Tuesday in May on which nothing happened, and that is a thing that cannot be checked by anybody, because the only man who could check it is on a boat and is owed a quarter by this office and has not been asked anything by anybody.**"

---

At about four in the afternoon the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall came down the market and stood at the foot of the mole steps and said four words to the holder of that writ and went back up.

"**Thirty shillings and sixpence,**" she said.

"**For what.**"

"**Nothing yet,**" she said. "**That is the February price for a barrel of lamp oil and I am going to keep saying it in this market until somebody quotes me a different one, and when they do I am going to put it on the board the day it is quoted to me and not the day I hear about it. That is all. You did not ask me for that either.**"

And she went back up, and he did not ask her for that either.

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the eighteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday and a wage of four marks was not paid on it and no envelope was made and the column did not move and the envelopes did not move, and that thirty weeks have fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them.*

*And that a boat of about seventy tons with a master of fifty and two men left the port of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day without any declaration, and was not asked for one and was not stopped and was not spoken to and was not followed, and lay off the south end of that mole until about three in the afternoon, and came in again at about six in the afternoon of the said day and took her berth, and that both her men went ashore and one of them stopped at a chandler's on the front street and the other went to his house; and that nothing was landed and nothing was taken aboard and no person of this yard was in that boat at any hour of the said day.*

*And that the said round of glass was in a house in a town four days up that coast on the said day, and that this yard did not send anybody to that town on the said day and has not sent anybody to that town at any hour of its life, and that no person in this harbor knows what a person in that house has done with it and this yard has not written and is not going to write.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a clerk of the harbor authority of this harbor, about sixty, in that office on the west basin on the Monday afternoon preceding and on the said morning, was asked by a master of fifty to lodge a declaration, and that he could not and said so, and gave three reasons, being that the season of his office ended on the thirty-first day of March past with nothing issued, that there is no book of that office into which a paper can be put, and that he has no power to receive anything and has had none since the first of April.*

*And that the said clerk said the words *I do not know* out loud in that room, and that a man of fifty then said he would go out without the paper, and that the said clerk asked him not to make that decision in that room on the ground that four people in that harbor would be told where he had been; and that the holder of that writ was not in that room at any hour of the said day and was not asked anything and was told about it on that mole at about one in the afternoon.*

*And that no person in that office and no person in that yard said out loud on the said day that the said notice was drafted in order that it could not be complied with, and that the reason against the line of the saying is the reason of the twenty-fourth day of April past, and that there is no evidence either way, and that a man of sixty who has been owed a quarter by his own office for two months is not evidence of anything and is a person.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that a boat which went out without a piece of paper and came back is not a victory and is not a defeat and is not a finding that a notice is not worth the paper it is printed on, and that eleven weeks from now a different master on the same ground may be held to the outer marks and it will not be the same Tuesday; that a clerk who says *I do not know* in a room with one man in it is not a hero and has not been thanked and is not going to be; and that a figure kept on a board by a woman with a stall until a different one is quoted to her is not a price and is not a protest and is the only instrument in that market that anybody in it controls.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and sixty-one pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fivepence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and seventy-nine pence, being two marks, four shillings and eleven pence, and it is not going to be paid. Thirty weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, and the wage falls due again on Tuesday the twenty-fifth of May for the thirty-first week and the column will not move on it. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-one days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and seventh night, and neither of them has moved, and a piece of paper is a penny and forty pieces of paper are a shilling, and this yard bought one of them on the twenty-seventh of April past for a sheet of nine lines.**
