# Chapter 313

*Thursday the Twenty-Seventh of May, the Fiftieth Charge Walked on None, Two Men and a Clerk and a Window Onto the West Basin, a Quarter Owed by That Office and Said Out Loud in a Room, and a Day That Does Not Go Round Again*

Thursday the twenty-seventh of May, at the harbor authority's office on the west basin in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fiftieth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall did not come down on the said Thursday and the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it. The fish market was not in the said day and nothing was said in it.**

**And the far bench was not occupied on the said Thursday at any hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

Two men went into that room between about ten and about one on the morning of the twenty-seventh and they went in separately and they were not in it together, and the second one knew the first one had been in it, and neither of them was sent by anybody.

The clerk of that harbor authority is about sixty and he has been a clerk for twenty-two years. He did not give his name on the seventeenth of May and he was not asked for it, and nobody in that town is going to ask him for it. He is not the clerk of the circuit office of this coast, who is a different man in a different building and who has not been up this hill since the twenty-eighth of October past. He is owed a quarter by his own office and has been since the first of April.

The first man was fifty-one. He had a coaster of about seventy tons and two men in her and a cargo of salt out of a yard up that coast and he had been in that room on the eighteenth of May and had been told he could not put a paper in a book.

He was in there for about twenty minutes and he did not raise his voice and he was not asked anything and he asked for nothing and when he came out onto the quay he stood in the sun for about two minutes and then went up the hill, which is not a thing a man does at this harbor in this month.

---

The second man was about thirty-six and he has a coaster of about thirty tons of his own with eleven weeks of a season standing against her and a slip at three shillings a week and a line on her ring bolt, and he has not been out past that mole-head since the seventeenth of May and he is not going to on Friday, and he came in at about half past eleven with a declaration in his hand that he had had made at a desk in Ternwatch on Monday and that two men had put their names under.

He put it on the table.

"**Lodge it,**" he said.

"**I cannot.**"

"**You said that to a man on Tuesday and I have heard about it,**" said the man of thirty-six. "**I want to say something before you say the three things. I have been in about eleven markets in nine days and I have heard a sentence said in a chandler's on Tuesday morning that I am not going to repeat in this room, because I have got about nine words in it that I would like to keep to myself, and I have not got anything to do with a chandler and I am not going to be able to tell you why I have brought it up here.**"

"**Then do not say it.**"

"**I am going to say it, because this is the only room in this harbor with a door on it that a person shuts,**" said the man of thirty-six, "**and I have got nothing else and I have brought a sheet.**"

---

The clerk put both hands flat on the table, the way he had put them flat on the table eight days before, and he gave the same three things in the same order and he gave them slowly.

"**The season of this office ended on the thirty-first of March past and it ended with nothing issued. There is no book of this authority that a paper can be put into. And I have had no power to receive anything since the first of April.**"

"**I know all three of those. I have had them off a man in a market. Say something I have not had.**"

"**I said four words in this room on Tuesday the eighteenth of May and I have thought about them every day since, and I am not going to unsay them and I am not going to improve on them, and if you have come up this hill to be told something new you have come up it on the wrong morning.**"

"**Then say the other thing.**"

"**The other thing is that I am not going to hold it,**" said the clerk. "**That is not in the three. The three are about this office. This one is about me. There is a difference and you may have it. There is no book in this room, and there is no power in this room, and there is a man in this room, and the man in this room is not going to put a piece of paper in his coat and carry it about for you. In about four years somebody is going to want to know where that sheet was on the twenty-seventh of May, and there is one answer to that, and it is that it was on the table of a room on the west basin with nobody holding it.**"

"**You could hold it.**"

"**I could,**" said the clerk, "**and that is the whole of what is wrong with this month.**"

---

The man of thirty-six stood up then, and he did not go out straight away, and he said the thing, and he said it to the room and not to the man in it.

"**I have got eleven weeks of a season on a boat of about thirty tons and I have a house and a woman in it and I have been out on that water every year of my life since I was nine, and I have not been out since the seventeenth of May, and I am not going out on the Friday, and that is a day.**" He put his hand flat on the table the way the clerk had put his. "**And I would like it understood that I am not saying that to you. You are a man of about sixty and you have been a clerk for twenty-two years and you are owed a quarter by the people who employ you and there is nobody in this room to pay you. I am saying it to the room.**"

"**I have been owed that quarter since the first of April,**" said the clerk, "**and I have not mentioned it to a living soul since, and you have said it out loud in a room with a window onto the west basin, and I am not going to be angry with you about it and I am not going to thank you for it, and I would like you to notice what you have just done, which is that you have not taken anything off me. There is nothing on me you can take.**"

"**You are the harbor authority of this harbor.**"

"**I am a man in a room,**" said the clerk, "**and the harbor authority of this harbor is a chair and a table and a slate wall that comes down every Monday at eight whether there is a sitting in it or not, and I have not been in that room on a Monday since the first of April, and I have a quarter owing and a form on my table that I am not going to keep, and if you want a thing to be angry at in this harbor you will have to go and find something that is not a man who cannot help you.**"

---

He came out onto the quay at about one and Calder Marr was standing on the mole end of the west basin walk with his hands behind his back and had been there, by his own account, for about a quarter of an hour and had not gone in.

"**You were in there.**"

"**I was on this walk,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have not been in that room and I am not going to be, and I am not going to be asked about a form in a market or a quay or a mole, and I have said that five times this week and I am not going to say it a sixth time to you in particular.**"

"**I am not asking you what to tick.**"

"**Then what are you asking me.**"

"**I am asking whether a boat of about thirty tons with a man in her comes to this mole at a mark and eightpence for a day,**" said the man of thirty-six, "**and whether anybody in this harbor will hire me a man, and I am asking it on a quay and not in a room because a man who asks a question in a room is a man in somebody's business, and I have read that somewhere this week and I have not got it right and I have not got it wrong.**"

"**A boat of about thirty tons with a man in her is a mark and eightpence for the day,**" said Calder Marr, "**and that is a man's boat and not this yard's, and it is hired at a box on the corner of that mole, and a party with no instrument may be hired by a private person for a private purpose, and that has been the rule since the first of May and it is not a door and I am not going to sell you a door.**"

"**That is a no with a price on it,**" said the man of thirty-six.

"**That is a price,**" said Calder Marr. "**Whether it is worth anything to you is between you and a box on the corner of a mole, and I am not going to be the one who says it is not.**"

The man of thirty-six looked at him for a second and then he went up the hill, and he did not say thank you and was not thanked, and nothing was put in his hands.

---

The clerk kept his own page that day as he keeps it every day, and did not mention the notice, and did not mention the two men, and told nobody in that room anything at all, and the two older naval notices further along that market wall were not taken down by anybody on the said day and nothing was put on that wall on the said day and nothing came off it.

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-seventh day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that two men went into the office of the harbor authority of this harbor on the west basin on the said day between about ten in the morning and about one in the afternoon and went in separately and were not in the room together and neither of them was sent by anybody; and that the first was fifty-one, with a coaster of about seventy tons and two men in her, and had been in that room on the eighteenth day of May past; and that the second was about thirty-six of the third lay, with a coaster of about thirty tons of his own and eleven weeks of a season standing against her and a slip at three shillings a week and a line on her ring bolt, and had not been out past the mole-head of that harbor since the seventeenth day of May past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a clerk of that harbor authority, about sixty, in that office twenty-two years, owed a quarter by his own office since the first day of April past, said out loud on the said day in that room that he was owed that quarter and had not mentioned it to a living soul since, and said that a man of about thirty-six had said it out loud in a room with a window onto the west basin and that he was not going to be angry with that man about it and was not going to thank him for it; and that the said clerk said out loud that he had said four words in that room on the eighteenth day of May past, that he had thought about them every day since, and that he was not going to unsay them and was not going to improve on them.*

*And that the said clerk gave the same three things on the said day that he gave on the eighteenth, in the same order, being that the season of that office ended on the thirty-first day of March past with nothing issued, that there is no book of that authority that a paper can be put into, and that he has had no power to receive anything since the first day of April; and that he gave a fourth thing that is not one of the three, being that he was not going to hold the form put on his table, and the reason given out loud being that in about four years somebody will want to know where that sheet was on the twenty-seventh of May and there is one answer to that and it is that it was on a table in a room with nobody holding it.*

*And that a man of about thirty-six said out loud in that room that he had eleven weeks of a season on a boat of about thirty tons, that he had not been out since the seventeenth day of May past, that he was not going out on the Friday following, and that that is a day; and that he said that he was not saying it to the clerk and that he was saying it to the room, and that the clerk said out loud in reply that he was a man in a room and that the harbor authority of that harbor is a chair and a table and a slate wall that comes down every Monday at eight whether there is a sitting in it or not, and that he has not been in that room on a Monday since the first of April past.*

*And that the holder of that writ was on the west basin walk outside that office for about a quarter of an hour and did not go in, and was told on that walk at about one in the afternoon that a boat of about thirty tons with a man in her is a mark and eightpence for the day, that it is hired at a box on the corner of that mole, and that a party with no instrument may be hired by a private person for a private purpose and that the rule is of the first day of May past and is not a door; and that no person of that yard went into that office, drafted, copied, witnessed, countersigned or advised on any declaration, went down to the water, or was asked anything by the said man of about thirty-six.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a man who is angry at an office is not a man who has been answered by an office, and that an office which has issued nothing since the thirty-first of March is not an obstacle and is a hole, and that a hole is harder to be angry at than a wall because a man can put his hand in a wall; that a quarter owed by an office to its own clerk is not a scandal and is not a grievance that anybody has standing to collect and was said out loud by a man who did not have standing and will have to live in a harbor that knows it; and that a day is a day and there is no figure for one and this yard is not going to invent one.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence. The cistern was charged for the fiftieth and walked on none of it. The wall did not come down. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and sixteenth night, and neither of them has moved. A clerk of about sixty with twenty-two years in a room on the west basin has been owed a quarter by his own office since the first of April and said so out loud in that room today to a man he had told three true things to eight days before, and a man of about thirty-six lost a day and it does not go round again. A boat of about thirty tons with a man in her is a mark and eightpence and this yard cannot buy one and has not tried.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
