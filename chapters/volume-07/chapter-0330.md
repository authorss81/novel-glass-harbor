# Chapter 330

*Monday the Fourteenth of June, the Fifty-Eighth Time That Wall Came Down, the Fifty-Fifth Charge Walked on None, a Leaf With Four Columns Read Back in Full With the Count on the Face of It, the Whole Standing Inventory Once and Nowhere Else, and the Figure the Bag Is Short of the Wage of Four Marks*

Monday the fourteenth of June, in the shed on the mole, and at the foot of that mole's steps in the afternoon.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-eighth time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on the said day. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said day by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-fifth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past.**

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence. The far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why.**

**In these ten days the wall came down bare twice, being the fifty-seventh on Monday the seventh of June past and the fifty-eighth on Monday the fourteenth; the cistern was charged on three rota days, being the fifty-third, the fifty-fourth and the fifty-fifth, and this party walked on none of them; and there was one wage Tuesday, being Tuesday the eighth of June past, and the column did not move on it, so that thirty-three weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. Those three counts are this yard's own and are not taken out of any other book.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and nobody else, and the read-back was in the ship's own left hand and went into her own book and not onto the wall of anywhere.

**The first column of that leaf has sixty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back.** There were fifty-three of them at the close of Thursday the third of June past and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Friday the fifth to Sunday the thirteenth of June, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own makes sixty-three.

The nine are: four old books in a drawer and the soundings named as of a coast that has moved; a man going past a shop and the safer road named as to be given up; a Crown chart of about nine years looked at from a doorway with no route across the middle water; a wage Tuesday with thirty-three weeks and two books opened with three spellings; a boat's name in a man's mouth in about nine people's hearing and a bag not opened; four books holding one water and a man looked at for a minute and not asked; a father's book read out loud in about nine people's hearing and fourteen days off the water up; five books and a chart in about forty people's hearing with nobody's book touched but its owner's; and four minutes looked at on a crown by forty people and a refusal of the two things that could be said.

**The second column has two hundred and five lines in it at the hour of the read-back.** One hundred and seventy-seven of them were in it at the close of the third of June past and twenty-eight went in between the fifth and the thirteenth, and three this afternoon, and two hundred and eight at the close. The said figure is that book of that yard's own and is not a count of paragraphs, and the first column's sixty-two and the fourth column's fifty-four, at the hour of the read-back, are paragraph counts and are checkable by anybody with a leaf and a pencil.

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The four lines above his named line are four, nameless, and are not struck out, and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of the ten days and was put on nothing and nobody asked him one word about the omissions he has entered, and the three nots standing against the eighth of June were not asked about on that day or after it.**

**And the fourth column has fifty-four lines in it at the hour of the read-back and fifty-five at the close, and the fifty-fifth is the last thing in that book.**

---

And then the three of them said what the ten days were, in about three lines each, and none of it was argued with and none of it has been improved on since, and it is about three different objects and all three of it is true.

**The first was about the notice, and the holder of that writ said it.**

"**Ten days ago this yard had twenty pence in a bag and it has twenty pence now, and nothing came in and nothing went out, and the notice of the seventeenth of May is still on that market wall and has not been taken down and nothing has been put on that wall and nothing has come off it, and the two older naval notices on that wall are still on that wall, and the clerk keeps his own page and does not mention it. And a woman of about fifty-eight has two figures and two dates on a slate and the rest of nine, and the cheapest thing in this harbor went up six shillings on the first of June and is not coming back down, and this yard has said out loud that it does not know whether those two things are in the same world and is not going to try to find out.**"

**The second was about the harbor, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**Seven boats of the herring third lay were lying off that mole this afternoon where there were seven on the third of June, and not one of the seven has passed the line and not one of them has broken any rule of that harbor. A chandler's counter in the front street is shut to one man and open to everybody else, and this yard sent nobody and asked nobody and told nobody. And a man of about forty-one walked plainly on the eleventh of June after ten days without weight and fourteen off the water, and went back to warps and not in a boat, and that is a mending and not a stage. That is two Tuesdays in this harbor on which nothing happened that nobody can check, and I have been the one who counts since the sixteenth of February and I am telling this shed that I do not have a column for what it costs to be one.**"

**The third was about the yard itself, and the woman of no office said it from the bench, and she was not asked for it.**

"**There is twenty pence in that bag and nothing went in and nothing went out, and nobody in this room is going to be able to tell afterwards whether those were ten good days or ten bad ones. Five books holding one water under ten spellings, and a Crown chart of about nine years with nothing across it that this harbor holds two copies of, and a man of sixty-one who looked at the pages for four minutes and would not say either of the two things he could say. The soundings are of a coast that has moved and the safer road has to be given up, and both prices went in before any use, and nobody said yes and nobody refused in a room. And that is what it is.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the fourteenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said day by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty ones, in the presence of the holder of that writ; and that the said leaf holds sixty-three entries in its first column, being fifty-three on the third day of June past, nine from the fifth to the thirteenth, and one this evening; two hundred and eight lines in its second, being one hundred and seventy-seven, twenty-eight and three; none in its third; and fifty-five in its fourth, being forty-five on the third day of June past, nine from the fifth to the thirteenth, and one this evening, which is the last thing in that book.*

*And that the whole of what came into that yard in the ten days ended on the said day is nothing, and the whole of what went out of it in the said ten days is nothing, and twenty pence plus nothing minus nothing is twenty pence, and that a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, so that bag cannot buy a day; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.*

*And that the said route stands as it stands in each book, with its spellings and its dates, and no current chart includes it: in the book of the Morrow Lass in a round hand, Skur Deep, nine fathom, eleven years past; in the book of the Gull's Rest in a tall hand, Scurr Deepe, nyne fadom, seventeen years past, and in a short hand, Skere Depe, niene fathoms, twenty-three years past; in the book of the lighter called the Tern's Debt in pencil in two hands, Skower Deep, nine fathom, and Skour Deap, nine fathoms, nineteen years past; in the stitched book with no vessel's name in three hands, Skorr Depe, eight and three quarters fathoms, twenty-seven years past, Skor Deep, nine fathom, nine years past, and the depe off the Skore, nine fathoms, undated; and in the father's book of a man of about thirty-six in a square hand, Skar Deep, eight and three quarters, Skarre Depe, nine, and Skar Deap, nine and a half fathom, twenty-one, fifteen and eight years past; and that the chart that omits it is a Crown chart of about nine years standing that this harbor holds two copies of, and a man's own chart bought fair at Ternwatch six years past omits it likewise.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was not touched on any of the ten days, that nothing was put on the market wall of that harbor and nothing came off it, that the two older naval notices further along that wall are still on it, and that the hearing room and the market wall were not in any one of those ten days on the same day; and that no part of that yard's method, its figures or its fourth column is on that wall in that harbor.*

*And that about eleven people on that coast cannot read the said notice, and that no person in that yard went and told one of them anything, and that nobody in that harbor is going to; and that the chandler's note is at thirty-one, not re-written, its three counts of weeks at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven; that the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them and nothing was measured; that four returns stand and there is no fifth and no finding of worked glass was made in these ten days; that the plate in the mole's own foundation stone is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, its note open, carried, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter out of a shed; that the case of five bells is not raised; that the lead plug and the chest and the undertaking of the second day of July are not touched; and that five pages are in five drawers in five towns and that yard has not looked in four of them.*

*And that nine communities hold the records one in each, and that the said practice is of six centuries standing, and that the office that answers it is of three hundred years standing, and that the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities; and that a can upon a stake at the outer marks is not on a chart of about nine years and was there in the last week of March, and is not a route, and was not followed and was not put on any chart and was not asked what it is for.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that ten days with nothing in and nothing out are not ten days standing still, because five books were opened and a chart was looked at from a doorway and a bag was opened by its owner in a market; that a route nobody can check is not a route disproved and not a route proved, and this yard has not refused it for the reason it has always given and has not accepted it for free; and that the thing that has no name in it did not cost a single day of these ten and is not a stage anybody in this harbor has stood on, and that the Storm-Reader stage is taken in shared work with a crew at a cost and not before, and not as a solution to a problem of police or price.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is nothing, and the whole of what went out of it in these ten days is nothing, and twenty pence plus nothing minus nothing is twenty pence. A wage of four marks was not paid on the one wage Tuesday and thirty-three weeks stand fallen due with no envelope, and the next falls due on Tuesday the fifteenth of June for the thirty-fourth week, which is outside these ten days, and will not move on it either. The wall came down bare twice in the ten days and the cistern was charged on three rota days and walked on none of them. The far bench was occupied on all ten of the ten days, being the fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, tenth, eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth and fourteenth, from ten until four on each of them, the thirteenth's hours being the hours the forty stood round the books, and was not approached on any of them — and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on all ten and went up no coast road on any of them.**

**And here is the whole of where this yard is at the end of it. A mole-head light not lit, its lens out since the seventh of May, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for oil is a barrel at four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted to a woman on the first of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and on the February figure that light was out in October. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-fourth night. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and sixty-eight days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved. Nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April past. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved, and nobody was sent for him. Seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, unargued, the chest not opened, the bellwright not asked in thirty days and no digit of anything he said printed. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid, not reduced by a crate and not paid by a question. Forty marks stand whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots of the eighth of June standing unasked, the third column unfilled and unappointed. Nine feet of chain on a nail in that shed wall and a bar of square iron in the drawer, neither sold nor given away, and nobody has asked whose either is. Seven pieces of paper in that drawer and the charter's place empty, and a round of glass is in a house four days up that coast, and a page went on a wall there on the fourth of April and has not been taken down, and a man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait is not on the page and is not going to be asked anything, and a man of about fifty-five with a boat on the hard and a line on her is on the hard still.**

**And on the fourteenth of June next there is a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay who walked plainly on the eleventh after ten days without weight and fourteen off the water and is back to warps and not in a boat, and a man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons and no candles at a counter in the front street and eleven weeks of a season and his father's book in his house, and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a chart in a bag that he opens himself or not at all, and a man of about thirty-one of no lay with no boat and no bag who is not that man, and a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and gave his name to nobody and is not Garrow Fisk of a pressure house at Ternwatch, and seven boats of the herring third lay lying off inside their own harbor line with a stove on one afterdeck, and a man of fifty-one with nineteen winters disputed and a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November and a season of nine marks that is not going to be paid, and a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand who looked at five books for four minutes and would not say either of the two things he could say, and a mark on the face of the second seam fouled about a foot and seven inches where it was on the nineteenth of May, with the whole of that ladder in the book of a man nobody is going to explain, and a mark four hundred yards off it of a make nobody in this harbor makes, and the season of both at nine marks and neither paid, and a man of about fifty of the second lay with a form in his coat and a box in it that is not filled.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it, and a harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and nobody in this country won anything this month, and the cheapest thing in this town went up six shillings on the first of June and is not coming back down, and a man in a blue coat with a book came up a mole on the thirtieth of May and said out loud that the instrument is bad and is not false, and nobody in that harbor improved on either half of it, and five books hold one water under ten spellings and a chart of about nine years has nothing across it and a man who has seen that water said so and would not say more.**
