# Chapter 348

*Friday the Second of July, Eleven Tons of Oil Standing on a Quay and a Figure on a Slate That Did Not Move, a Word That Came Down a Coast Road on a Boat That Went Up for Coal, a Shipwright's Bench With Nothing on It and a Man Off the Water on the Sixth Day of Nine, and a Harbor That Is Poorer on the Second of July Than on the Fourteenth of May*

Friday the second of July, on the fish quay, in the fish market, and in that shed in the evening.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said Friday and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked, not sent for, not asked.**

---

The oil was still on the quay where it was put on Monday morning, and it had been in an open shed with a slate hung on the door of it and no name on the slate for four days, and a man of about twenty-eight of that harbor's own second lay had put a rope round it and a lock on it and he said he had done that on Monday and he had not asked anybody and he had told nobody and he was telling this yard now because a stranger had asked him about it.

There is no figure anywhere in this harbor for eleven tons of lamp oil bought at night by eleven boats on no declaration, and a chandler of about fifty has three hundred and seventy-eight pence on a board in his own mind from February and four hundred and fifty pence on the end stall's slate from the first of June, and neither of those figures is the price of anything that came in on Monday.

The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said it out loud at about eleven in the morning of the said Friday, in about nine people's hearing, to a chandler's wife of about fifty-two who had come over with a slate of her own and no figure on it.

"**You are going to ask me for a figure and I am going to tell you that I have not got one, and I am not going to put one on this board, and here is the reason and it is the same reason as the twenty-ninth of April past and it has not got any better since.**"

"**A woman who puts a figure on a board cannot take it off again,**" said the chandler's wife.

"**A woman who puts a figure on a board cannot take it off again,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight, "**and I have got a barrel of lamp oil on that board at four hundred and fifty pence, quoted the first of June, and the February figure of three hundred and seventy-eight left visible above it with both dates, and on the February figure that mole-head light was out in October and it has been out since the seventh of May past and it is out now. And eleven tons of oil came into this harbor on Monday morning at about half past seven in a lighter a ton light, and it did not go into a chandler's shop and it did not go into a factor's and it did not go through a court of anybody's, and I do not know what a ton of it costs and neither does the man who brought it, and if I put a figure on that board this morning it will be a figure I made up in a market, and in a year somebody will come and read that board and think I knew.**"

"**So it is not coming down,**" said a man of about forty-five.

"**It is not coming down and it is not going up either, and the two of those are the only two sentences I have got about it and I have had them since the first of June.**"

And a man of about thirty-one of no lay with a rope-maker's hand said one thing at the end of that aisle that nobody argued with and nobody agreed with, being that a thing that comes in on a boat at night at nobody's price is not the same thing as a thing that comes in at a price, and that this harbor is going to find that out the hard way and not in a month.

Nobody told him he was wrong and about nine people let him say it.

---

The word came down the coast road at about two in the afternoon of the said Friday, on the boat that had gone up for coal on Monday the twenty-eighth with a sheet of paper in the coal, and the man who brought it down had not been sent for it and had not been asked for it and did not know what it was until he was asked what it was, and this is the whole of what he said.

"**There is a sheet of paper on the wall of the market in that town, outside, on the same sort of wall you have got your notice on, and it went up last night, and it has forty lines on it and I did not read it, and it has got a lot of names on it and most of them are the same names you say every day, and the woman at the counter told me that nobody paid for it and that it was not one of the harbor's and that there is one of them already on that wall from the spring.**"

Nobody in that yard went and looked at it and nobody in that yard is going to. This yard did not write to that town and has not written to that town and is not going to, and the sheet says on its own face that whoever puts it up is not this yard, and the man who brought the word down was not asked a question about the wall and was not thanked and has not been asked one word since and is not going to be.

"**Forty lines,**" said Iver Sallow, of about thirty-one, of the third lay, at the head of the slip at about half past two in the afternoon, and that was all he said about it, and he had his copy in the bag with the chart in it and nobody knew that except that he said so himself and nobody asked to see it.

---

And the harbor was poorer.

A shipwright of about sixty had nothing on his bench on the said Friday for the first time since the third of April past, and his apprentice of fourteen was at home on the Monday and the Tuesday and came back on the Wednesday, and the shipwright said the thing himself at about four in the afternoon on the front street to about nine people, and he did not ask anybody for anything.

"**Two days off my bench and my boy at home two days and I put that on line nineteen of that sheet and it is in your hand and not mine, and that is the whole of my week and I would like it read in that order and not the other way round.**"

A lighterman of about twenty-nine was without a road. The Ternwatch run of about eleven tons to a lighter for the season was the lightest work he could get in this harbor and he had given it up in a market on the Friday of the twenty-fifth in about nine people's hearing, and on the said Friday it was still not somebody else's, and he said so to nobody in particular at about eleven o'clock and his words went into the second column with a reason against the line and no name against it.

A herring skipper of fifty-one was two days further into a season of nineteen winters that is being made up in a city, and had a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November past, and had not had one word about a thing, and said so again on the front street on the said Friday and to the same effect as on the twenty-ninth.

A man of about fifty-one had lost a berth and had not said what a berth is worth and nobody in that harbor had a figure and about nine people heard him say on the quay that he was still working it out and that he was four days into it.

And a man of about forty-five of the third lay was on the sixth day of nine off the water, with two fingers of his left hand stiff, and had not been to the bonesetter of about sixty who has a room over a baker's and who came down that hill twice in June, and nobody in that yard had sent him and nobody in that yard was going to and he had not asked anybody for anything.

And six men of that harbor still had no boat, and the wreck of about nineteen tons was still on the foul off the south end of that mole, and on the said Friday a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay went down to the hard at about low water and looked at that wreck from the shore with a glass and did not go to her and told nobody what he saw and was not asked.

---

*First column, entered on Friday the second day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of about twenty-eight of that harbor's own second lay had put a rope and a lock round about eleven tons of lamp oil standing in an open shed on the fish quay of that harbor since the morning of the twenty-eighth of June past, and had told nobody, and told this yard on the said day only because a stranger had asked him about it; and that there is no figure anywhere in that harbor for oil bought at night by eleven boats on no declaration, and that a figure of four hundred and fifty pence quoted on the first of June with the February figure of three hundred and seventy-eight pence left visible above it and both dates on the board did not move on the said day and is not going to move; and that a mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past, and that on the February figure that light was out in October.*

*And that a word came down a coast road on the said day at about two in the afternoon, on the boat that had gone up that coast for coal on the twenty-eighth with one of the four copies written out, in the coal, being that there is a sheet of forty lines on the outside of a market wall in a town four days up that coast, that it went up on the evening of the second day of July past, four days after it left this harbor in the coal, and that nobody paid for it, that it was not put up by this yard, and that there is one of this yard's already on that wall from the spring; and that the man who brought the word down did not know what it was and was not sent for and not asked for and did not read the sheet; and that nobody in that yard went to look at it and nobody in that yard is going to, and that this yard did not write to that town and has not written to it and is not going to; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay said the words *forty lines* at the head of that slip at about half past two in the afternoon and that was all he said about it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said out loud in that fish market at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about nine people's hearing and to a chandler's wife of about fifty-two, that she has not got a figure for what came in on Monday morning and is not going to put one on that board, and that a woman who puts a figure on a board cannot take it off again, and that the same sentence has not got any better since the twenty-ninth day of April past; and that a barrel of lamp oil stands on that board at four hundred and fifty pence quoted the first of June with the February figure of three hundred and seventy-eight pence visible above it and both dates, and that on the February figure that mole-head light was out in October and it has been out since the seventh day of May past; and that eleven tons came in on Monday morning in a lighter a ton light and did not go into a chandler's shop or a factor's or a court of anybody's, and that she does not know what a ton of it costs and neither does the man who brought it, and that a figure put on that board that morning would be a figure made up in a market, and that in a year somebody will come and read that board and think she knew.*

*And that a man of about thirty-one of no lay with a rope-maker's hand said out loud at the end of that aisle that a thing which comes in on a boat at night at nobody's price is not the same thing as a thing which comes in at a price, and that this harbor is going to find that out the hard way and not in a month, and that nobody told him he was wrong and about nine people let him say it and none of them agreed with him; and that a lighterman of about twenty-nine was without a road, the Ternwatch run of about eleven tons to a lighter for the season having been given up in a market on the twenty-fifth in about nine people's hearing, and that on the said day it was still not somebody else's, and that he said so to nobody in particular at about eleven o'clock, and that his words were entered here with a reason against the line and no name against it; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one was two days further into a season of nineteen winters being made up in a city, with a hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November past and not one word about a thing, and that he said so on the front street on the said day to the same effect as on the twenty-ninth; and that a man of about fifty-one had lost a berth and had still not said what a berth is worth and that about nine people heard him say on that quay that he was four days into working it out; and that a shipwright of about sixty had nothing on his bench for the first time since the third of April past, and said at about four in the afternoon on the front street in about nine people's hearing that two days off his bench and a boy of fourteen at home two days is the whole of his week and that he would like it read in that order and not the other way round, and that he asked nobody for anything.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a thing coming into a harbor is not the same thing as a harbor being fed, and eleven tons of oil on a quay under a slate with no name on it is not a cheaper barrel and is not a full one and is not going to be on a board in a year; and that a figure that has gone up and has not come down is not a bad thing that can be corrected by anybody in this town and is not going to be corrected by anything that came in on a boat; and that a man who gave up a road in a market in about nine people's hearing has not been given a better one and has not been given anything at all, and that a man who lost a berth has not been given a boat, and that neither of them is going to be given one by this yard and neither of them asked.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-six days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fifty-second night, and neither of them has moved. The mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and eight inches where it was on the twenty-fourth of June past, and a mark four hundred yards off it is of a make nobody in that harbor makes, and the season of both is nine marks and neither has been paid and neither is going to be. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor have no boat. A man of about forty-five of the third lay is on the sixth day of nine off the water with two fingers of his left hand stiff and has not been to a bonesetter. Thirty-six weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them and the column has not moved on the one wage Tuesday that has fallen in these ten days. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the eighteenth day of the ninety, and a working harbor of that coast is poorer on the second day of July than on the fourteenth day of May, and nobody in this country has won anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
