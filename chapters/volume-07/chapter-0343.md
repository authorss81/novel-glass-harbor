# Chapter 343

*Sunday the Twenty-Seventh of June, the Thirteenth Day of the Ninety, Ten Boats Out of a Harbor on a Breach and Back in Again Before Six in the Morning, Eleven Spellings of One Water Read Out on the Water and No Way of Choosing Between Them, Two Fingers of a Working Man's Hand Taken by a Warp at One in the Morning With No Bonesetter on That Coast, and a Sheet That Stopped at Four O'Clock Because a Man of Fifty-One Said So*

Sunday the twenty-seventh of June, on the water, from the flood of the evening before to the flood of that morning.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and nothing will be in it tomorrow, because the man who left this harbor at half past seven last night came back in it at half past five this morning and went straight to the quay and has not been in that shed since.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, by the same man as on every other day of these ten, and the holder of that writ was not in that harbor between about half past seven the evening before and about half past five that morning and did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on the quay at about half past six in the morning and she had not been up the hill and nobody asked her to be. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, and he was not thanked and not sent for and not asked and did not come down and did not stand at the head of the slip at half past five in the morning, and about nine people were on that crown and none of them said one word to him about any of it.**

---

They had the bar at about a quarter to nine and Calder Marr had ten boats' worth of ears and gave them three things and no fourth, and he gave them the three things standing up in a boat of about thirteen tons with the water going under her quarter and about a hundred and thirty yards from the nearest of the other nine.

"**There is no page in this harbor that says which of these figures is the right one, and I am not going to supply it and I have got nothing to supply it with.**"

Then he read them out. Eleven of them, in the order the yard's own book gives them, with the year against each one, in a flat voice, holding the sheet in his left hand because the ship's hand could not hold a sheet and write on it at the same time and she had told him so on the quay and he had listened.

*Skur Deep, nine fathom, foule ground to the eastard*, eleven years. *Scurr Deepe, nyne fadom, foule grounde to the estwards*, seventeen. *Skere Depe, niene fathoms, foul bottom east*, twenty-three. *Skower Deep, 9 faddom, foule E.*, nineteen, rubbed out and written over twice. *Skour Deap, nine fathoms, foul to eastward*, nineteen, same page. *Skorr Depe, eyght fathoms and three quarters, foule to the easte*, twenty-seven. *Skor Deep, nine fadome, foul ground E.*, nine. *the depe off the Skore, nine fathoms, keep her full and by*, undated. And three more out of the father's book of a man of about thirty-six, which is not this yard's, and which were read out by the owner on the eleventh of June past in a market and are not this yard's to read tonight, and the man of about thirty-six said the three of them himself at about ten past nine and would not let anybody else say them, and nobody tried.

"**Eight and three quarters to nine and a half,**" said Calder Marr, when he had finished. "**Three quarters of a fathom, which is a yard and a quarter, and I have got eleven books' worth of years on them and the oldest is twenty-seven and the newest is nine. That is a difference and a difference is not a truth. The soundings are of a coast that has moved. There is no figure in this harbor for how far a coast moves and there is nobody here who can measure it and I am not going to invent one at half past eight in the evening on a boat of thirteen tons.**"

"**And the third thing,**" he said, "**and this is the one I would like a stranger to check in a year. A line on a page is a thing a person left. The person is not out here. It does not command anything. If the water is different from what the books say then the water is different, and I am not going to be a man up a coast afterwards who says he told them, and I am not going to be a man up a coast afterwards who says he did not tell them either. If one of you finds that out at two in the morning it belongs to you and not to me, and there is a line on this sheet with your name and an hour on it and I want it in.**"

About eleven men said various things and one of them said the only one worth putting on the sheet, and the ship's hand put it on, and it is line sixteen.

> *9.40. PEDER LUND, thirteen tons: If I find it different then it is mine and not yours, and you are not to come aboard and ask me about it in the morning.*

He said it to Calder Marr and Calder Marr said the one word, which was *no*, meaning he was not going to come aboard and ask him about it in the morning, and he has not, and he is not going to.

---

Between ten at night and one in the morning the sheet took six more lines, and Nessa Pike wrote them in her own left hand on a thwart with a coat under it, and this yard has them exactly as they went on and is not going to improve any of them.

> *10.31. ELIAS VOKE, Anna Ray: I will hold her to nine fathom on the line and I will not lift a foot of it for anybody on this coast. Price: two days of a season and nineteen winters. Payer: me.*
>
> *11.20. TAM RUNDLE, Mary Rend: My light goes out here and it stays out until we are through, and a light in a strait is a thing somebody will come for. Price: a night's passage, about nine tons. Payer: me.*
>
> *12.05. ADAM SLEE, twenty-six tons: I will not take flour. I am a shipwright and I have not looked at her bottom since the twenty-fourth of April past and I will not put four ton of flour in her on the word of a man in a market. Oil and physic go and flour stays. Price: two days off my bench and my apprentice goes without. Payer: me.*
>
> *12.48. JENS OWER, lighter, eleven tons: A lighter will not cross a bar at night. I am going to the north end of the bar and no further, and that is two miles short of the strait, and that is where I said I would be and I would like it written that I said it before I said anything else. Price: the outer two miles of my work and a road of about eleven tons. Payer: me.*

At about one in the morning, on the far side of the strait, in about thirteen tons of weather that no book in this harbor has a word for, a warp came up on the boat of about forty-five tons with a bight in it that the bitt was not where it had been, and it took two fingers of the left hand of a man of about forty-five of the third lay called Coll Mabry, who has been in that water twenty-six years and who said nothing for about a minute and then said one sentence, which was about the warp.

Adam Slee the shipwright had it bound in about four minutes with a strip cut off the leech of a sail, because there is no bonesetter on that coast at one in the morning and there is not one at four and there is not one at six, and there is a bonesetter of about sixty in that harbor with a room over a baker's who came down a hill in June and would not come up one and who was not asked and was not sent for and does not know about any of it.

And Coll Mabry said out loud, on the water, in about ten boats' hearing, that he was going to be off the water for nine days and that he had a wife and a girl of eleven and that the girl was the one he was worried about, and that he wanted that written on the sheet with the hour on it because he was not going to want to have said it in a market in a week.

> *1.06. COLL MABRY, forty-five, third lay, in the boat of about forty-five tons: two fingers of my left hand off the warp, and I am off the water nine days, and I have a girl of eleven and it is her I am thinking about, and I am putting that on the sheet so that I cannot say in a week that I did not say it tonight. Price: nine days, and I have no figure for a day of my own and I am not going to make one up. Payer: me, and whatever the nine days cost, and nobody here.*

Coll Mabry is ashore, alive, and has not been asked one word about it, and his two fingers were stiff on the fourth of July and there is a bonesetter of about sixty in that harbor whom he has not gone to, on his own account, and nobody in this yard has sent him.

---

And then Peder Lund's boat of about thirteen tons, which is about a fifth of what went out by the numbers and not by the tonnage, found the water on the remembered line at about two in the morning, and it was not what the books said, and here is what was said afterwards in about nine people's hearing in a shed on a mole and is on the sheet and is not going to be improved on by anybody in that shed.

"**She was on the line, the line I had off the twenty-seventh-year page, the eight and three quarters, and by my own hand on my own lead she was carrying two foot more than that over it, and I went on, and I went on because I had a boat of thirteen tons under me and a hundred and sixty of water over her and a man of about thirty-two at the wheel who has been on that water nine years and he said go,**" said Peder Lund, "**and I want it written that he said go and that I said go and that neither of us was asked and neither of us was told and we were both wrong or we were both right and there is nobody in that strait tonight who can tell us which, and I have been three weeks on that page and I have got the arithmetic of a coast that has moved and not one thing else.**"

"**Two foot,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Two foot on the oldest page and the oldest page is twenty-seven years and the newest is nine,**" said the man of about thirty-one of the third lay, from a boat of about forty tons. "**And I have a Crown chart of about nine years standing in a bag and it has nothing in the middle of that water and that has been true for nine years, and I have never once been able to make those two facts sit down together and I have not been able to tonight either.**"

Nobody made them sit down together. Elias Voke said that a route somebody left does not command the sea and said it once and did not say it again that night, and Calder Marr entered it in the first column with the date against it and it is the first time in the whole of this yard's life that this yard has entered a thing it had been told rather than a thing it had worked out, and he said so.

And that is the whole of what the trade bought, and it is this: a difference, and an hour against it, and a man's name against it, and no figure that anybody in that harbor can check afterwards because a stranger with a lead would have to be on that water on that night, and there was no stranger on that water on that night, and the sheet does not pretend there was.

---

The last line went on at about four o'clock in the morning and it is the last line on the sheet and it is the shortest.

> *4.02. ELIAS VOKE, Anna Ray: That is the sheet. Nobody writes on it after this. I have had two days of a season and I am not writing anything at four in the morning that I would have to read at ten.*

The ship's hand wrote it and put the hour against it and did not write anything after it, and the sheet has forty lines on it and the fortieth is that one, and the copies were not made until the afternoon of the twenty-eighth.

They came in past the outer marks at about half past five in the morning on the flood. The boat of the closed-sea squadron was lying there again with the same man in her and the same book open on the thwart, and he did not hail ten boats coming in, and the sixth clause of a notice nailed up outside a market in that harbor says that this notice is not a closure of the port and that vessels may enter without a declaration, and that is the clause, and nobody in that harbor read it out loud that morning and it is read out loud in a fish market in that harbor on the third day of July next, and this yard knows it is going to be read and has not read it and does not know the hour.

And a wreck of about nineteen tons was still on the foul off the south end of that mole where it has been since the nineteenth of June, going down by the head, and six men of that harbor were still ashore with no boat, and none of the ten had been asked to do anything about that and none of them was going to be.

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that ten boats of that harbor went out past the mole head and past the line described in the fifth clause of the notice on the flood of the evening of Saturday the twenty-sixth at about half past seven, without a declaration, which is a breach, and that no vessel of this yard's was among them and that this yard drafted, copied, witnessed, countersigned and advised nothing, and that the said holder of that writ and the said ship's hand were in a boat of about thirteen tons belonging to a man of about thirty-eight of no lay, at that man's own asking and on his own account, and that this yard paid no penny of it; and that at about a quarter to nine on that night the said holder of that writ read out in about ten boats' hearing the eleven spellings of one water in five books with the year against each, being eight fathoms and three quarters to nine and a half across twenty-seven years to nine, and said out loud that there is no page in that harbor that says which of them is right and that he was not going to supply one, and that the soundings are of a coast that has moved and that there is no figure for how far a coast moves; and that he said that a line on a page is a thing a person left and that the person is not out there and that it commands nothing; and that the man of about thirty-six of the third lay read the three soundings out of his own father's book himself at about ten past nine and let nobody else read them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten past two in the morning, a boat of about thirteen tons found two foot more water on the remembered line than the oldest page in the five books carries, and that the man of that boat went on, and that a man of about thirty-two at the wheel of that boat told him to go, and that neither was asked and neither was told; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay said that a Crown chart of about nine years standing has nothing in the middle of that water and that he has never been able to make that and a twenty-seven-year page sit down together and could not do it on that night; and that nobody made them sit down together; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one said out loud once in that night that a route somebody left does not command the sea, and that it was entered in the first column with the date against it, and that it is the first time in the whole of this yard's life that this yard entered a thing it had been told rather than a thing it had worked out, and that the said holder of that writ said so; and that a stranger with a lead could not check any of it, because a stranger with a lead would have had to be on that water on that night and there was no stranger on that water on that night, and the sheet does not pretend there was.*

*And that a man of about forty-five of the third lay named Coll Mabry lost two fingers of his left hand off a warp at about one o'clock in the morning, and that a shipwright of about sixty bound it in about four minutes with a strip cut off the leech of a sail, and that there is no bonesetter on that coast at one in the morning or at four or at six, and that the bonesetter of about sixty in that harbor with a room over a baker's was not asked and was not sent for and does not know about it; and that the said man said out loud in about ten boats' hearing that he was going to be off the water for nine days and that he had a girl of eleven and that it was her he was thinking about, and asked for the hour to be put against it so that he could not say in a week that he had not said it that night; and that he is ashore and alive and has not been asked one word about it.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a trade worked on the water by four people is not a power and does not settle anything and has not settled one thing on the coast in this month; and that eleven ways of writing down one water is not eleven pieces of evidence and is not a chart and is not going to be one, and a difference between two pages is not a truth about a sea; and that a page a man can check is a page a stranger can check, and that a stranger could not check this one, and that being unable to check it does not stop it being true and does not make it more true, and both of those are on the sheet and neither of them is in anybody's favor.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and the fifty-ninth charge falls on Monday the twenty-eighth. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ was not in that harbor between about half past seven the evening before and about half past five that morning. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-one days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-seventh night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor are ashore with no boat, and none of the ten was asked to do anything about that. A man of about forty-five of the third lay is off the water for nine days with two fingers of his left hand stiff and has not been to a bonesetter on his own account. Ten boats came in past the outer marks this morning at about half past five under the sixth clause of a notice that is not a closure of the port, and they brought flour and oil and physic and three women, and the price of the cheapest thing in this harbor has not moved and is not going to. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the thirteenth day of the ninety and nobody in this country has won anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
