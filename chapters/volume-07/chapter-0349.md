# Chapter 349

*Saturday the Third of July, Nine People at the End of the End Stall at About Eleven in the Morning, the Sixth Clause of a Notice Read Out Loud From a Copy in a Coat, Nine Men of That Harbor Saying Out Loud in Their Own Mouths That an Open Road Is a Thing They Are Answerable For and Not a Freedom, and a Man of the Second Lay of About Thirty-Eight Saying a Sentence Nobody in That Yard Can Improve On*

Saturday the third of July, in the fish market in the morning, and in that shed at about four in the afternoon.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said Saturday and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that market at about eleven in the morning and said nothing and was not thanked. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked, not sent for, not asked, and was not in that market at any hour of the said day and nobody sent for him.**

---

There were about nine people at the end of the end stall at about eleven on the morning of the third of July and there were about forty in that market and the forty were not at that end of it, and the nine were there because the word had gone round the front street in a day, the way it goes.

The man of about thirty-four who read the whole of that notice out loud in about forty people's hearing in that market on the seventeenth day of May past, and who is not of this yard and does not read out for a living and had no reason in the world to be in that market, came in at about ten past eleven with a sheet of paper in the inside of his coat.

He had copied it out himself, in his own hand, on the twenty-seventh of May past, in an evening, at a table in a room he has never said the name of, and he said so, and he said the date, and nobody asked him to and he offered it.

"**That is the sixth one and there are eight and I could read you the other seven this morning if you wanted me to, and I am not going to, because I have been asked for six and six is what I have been asked for and a man who cannot read four lines of a thing does not get to read the other two out of the same paper because the other two are longer.**"

"**Go on,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall.

"**This notice is not a closure of the port. Vessels may enter without a declaration.**"

He said it once and then he said what he could not do with it, which is the thing he said on the seventeenth of May and which he had not been asked for since and offered again because he had the paper in his hand.

"**I can read that. I have had six weeks to sit with that sentence and I have not been able to make it do anything on its own. It is a true sentence and it is on the same sheet as four other true sentences, and on the seventeenth of May past there were eleven boats of the third lay who got in and landed off it, and there are seven boats of the third lay lying off inside their own harbor line right now, and both of those are the same sentence and I cannot tell you which of them it is doing.**"

"**It is doing both,**" said a woman of about thirty-four who was at the end of that aisle.

"**Then it is doing both,**" he said, "**and I would rather somebody who works on that water said how than a man with a sheet in his coat, and I am putting the sheet back in my coat.**"

He put it back in his coat and he did not read the other seven and nobody asked him to.

---

Then the ten of them said what they were going to say about it, and this yard said none of it, and this is the record of that morning in the order it went.

**Elias Voke**, fifty-one, of the *Anna Ray*, first, and about four sentences and no more.

"**I want to say a thing and I have said it before in this market and I am saying it again because it is different now and it was not different before. An open road is not a freedom. A year ago an open road was a thing you went along and now it is a thing ten of us are answerable for every week, and if one of us stops then the other nine have his part to do, and there is nobody up this coast that any of us can go and complain to about it, and there is nobody up this coast that will come and help, and I have been on that water thirty-four years and I have never had that before and I have not decided whether I like it.**"

"**A man of fifty-one does not have to like it,**" said Coll Mabry, who is about forty-five and is on the seventh day of nine off the water. "**He has to be in it.**"

"**He does,**" said Voke, "**and I am not going to argue with you about the difference in a market.**"

**Iver Sallow**, about thirty-one, of the third lay, of the *Ashtide Rose*, second, and about six sentences and the only one of the morning that anybody asked a question about and got nothing for.

"**And the other thing, and I have thought about it for four days and I am going to say it in front of nine people and not in a shed. A thing on the water has to be looked at by somebody who is not being paid by the person who wants it looked at. I have had a chart of about nine years standing in a bag for nine years and it has been looked at by me, and I am the person who wants it looked at, and that is the whole of what I know about it and it is not enough, and on Wednesday morning a man of about thirty-four of this harbor said one sentence in that shed and then nobody said it to him, and I have been turning that over for four days and it is the same sentence.**"

Nobody asked him a question about it and nobody told him it was true, and he said that he had expected that and had not come for anything else.

And then a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime said out loud that all of that was a load of words and that what had come into that harbor on Monday was a lighter a ton light, and a woman of about fifty-eight said *I know it is*, and nobody argued with him and nobody told him he was wrong.

**Jens Ower**, about twenty-nine, of no lay, of a lighter of about eleven tons, third.

"**I gave up a road of about eleven tons to a lighter for the season in this market a fortnight ago in front of nine people and it was the right thing to do and I have had nothing since and I am going to have nothing until the season turns, and I am not saying that to anybody to be let off and I am not saying it to be thanked. A man who gives up a road does not get a better one. That is the whole of what I have learned this month and it took me two nights on the water and a ton of oil.**"

**Hamo Birt**, about fifty, of the second lay, of a boat of about twenty-two tons, fourth, and short.

"**I am going in behind a herring boat on Monday because I have said on a sheet that I would and I am fifty-one next month and it is the last thing I am going to be asked to do by anybody. I have a paper on a table in an office on the west basin and the box on it is not filled and I am not going to fill it and I am going to put a second one on the same table on Monday morning and leave it there, and there will be a man from the west quay in that office at ten who has one of his own, and I am not going to speak to him and I am not going to be spoken to.**"

Nobody in that market said a word about that, which he had asked for by not finishing it.

**Alban Pree**, about sixty, of the first lay, forty-one years on that water, fifth, and the only man there who said anything about himself at all, and said one sentence about it.

"**Forty-one years and I was never once asked to be in a thing until a man of no standing whatever asked me one question in a kitchen and I said yes before my wife could stop me, and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be, and I would like it written down that I did not do it for anything and that nobody is going to give me anything for it and that I knew that on the Friday.**"

And a packet man of about thirty-four out of Bramble Strait said nine words and they were about a light he had put out in a strait and had not lit again until he was inside the harbour, and nobody asked him a question and he went out of that market and up the front street and was not thanked and has not been asked one word since and is not going to be.

---

And then the man of about thirty-eight of no standing whatever, of the second lay of that harbor, who has had three sentences in that market in a month and a man of about fifty-one said out loud in June that he was not going to be a man in a market who has had four, got his fourth out, and it is the sentence that nobody in that yard can improve on, and it is nineteen words, and there is no name against it in any book and he would not give one and was not asked for one.

"**You can have a harbor or you can have a road, and the six men that have no boat are what that sentence is about.**"

And then he stopped, and about four people heard it and the rest of that market did not, and nobody improved on it, and nobody in that yard is going to improve on it, and it has not been written in that yard's book and will not be, being that he would not give a name and would not write it himself.

---

And nobody proposed anything.

Nobody in that hearing proposed a body, a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard, and the word for none of them was said in that market on the said morning, and no person in that yard proposed anything, and a man of about sixty of the first lay who asked the question of this coast out loud at a mole head on the thirty-first day of May past in sixty-three words which he counted four times was not in that market and was not asked and has not asked it since and is not going to.

And the notice of the seventeenth of May is on the outside of that market wall and was not touched on the said day and nothing was put on it and nothing came off it, and the two older naval notices further along that wall are still on that wall, and the clause that was read out loud that morning at the end of the end stall was read out of a copy in a coat and not off the wall, and the wall itself is not a thing anybody in that market went and stood in front of on the said day.

And the blockade is not lifted. It is broken enough that ten boats went out on a breach and came in on the sixth clause and brought back flour and oil and physic and three women, and it is on the face of that notice until somebody says otherwise, and nobody in that harbor expects anybody to say otherwise, and nobody in that harbor said that it was a lie or that it was obviously right, and a man of about forty-five of the third lay in that market, who is Coll Mabry and is on the seventh day of nine off the water, said the thing that everybody in that market thought and nobody said.

"**I do not think it is a lie and I do not think it is right and I have got two fingers of my left hand off and I am not going to say a word about which of the two it is.**"

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the third day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about nine people's hearing at the end of the end stall and not in a room, the sixth clause of the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was read out loud once from a copy that the reader had written out in his own hand on the twenty-seventh day of May past, and that he offered the date of his own copying unasked; and that the clause read was that this notice is not a closure of the port and vessels may enter without a declaration; and that he then said, offered and not asked for, that he can read it and had not been able to make it do anything on its own, and that it is a true sentence on the same sheet as four other true sentences, and that on the seventeenth day of May past eleven boats of the third lay got in and landed off it and that seven boats of the third lay are lying off inside their own harbor line, and that he cannot tell which of those the sentence is doing; and that a woman of about thirty-four at the end of that aisle said it was doing both, and that he said he would rather somebody who works on that water said how than a man with a sheet in his coat, and put the sheet back in his coat, and did not read the other seven clauses and nobody asked him to.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a herring skipper of fifty-one said out loud in that hearing, and had said before in that market and said it again because it was different now and had not been different before, that an open road is not a freedom but a thing ten of them are answerable for every week, and that if one of them stops the other nine have his part to do, and that there is nobody up that coast any of them can go and complain to about it and nobody who will come and help, and that he has been on that water thirty-four years and has never had that before and has not decided whether he likes it; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay said out loud that a thing on the water has to be looked at by somebody who is not being paid by the person who wants it looked at, that he has had a chart of about nine years in a bag for nine years and it has been looked at by him and he is the person who wants it looked at, and that that is the whole of what he knows about it and it is not enough; and that a lighterman of about twenty-nine said that a man who gives up a road does not get a better one, that he gave up a road of about eleven tons in that market a fortnight ago in front of nine people, that it was the right thing to do, and that he has had nothing since and is not saying it to be let off and is not saying it to be thanked; and that a man of about fifty of the second lay said that he has a paper on a table in an office and is not going to fill the box and is going to put a second paper on the same table on Monday morning and is not going to speak to the man from the west quay who has one already; and that a man of about sixty of the first lay said that in forty-one years he was never once asked to be in a thing until a man of no standing whatever asked him one question in a kitchen, and that he said yes before his wife could stop him, and that he has not been thanked and is not going to be and knew that on the Friday; and that a packet man of about thirty-four out of Bramble Strait said nine words about a light he had put out in a strait and had not lit again until he was inside the harbour, and was asked nothing and was not thanked; and that a man of about thirty-eight of the second lay, who has had three sentences in that market in a month and said in June that he was not going to be a man in a market who has had four, said a fourth in nineteen words, being that you can have a harbor or you can have a road and the six men that have no boat are what that sentence is about, and that about four people heard it and nobody improved on it and it is in no book and he would not give a name and was not asked for one; and that a man of about forty-five of the third lay said in that market that he did not think the notice was a lie and did not think it was right, and that he had two fingers of his left hand off and was not going to say a word about which of the two it was; and that a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime said all of it was a load of words and that what came into that harbor on Monday was a lighter a ton light, and that a woman of about fifty-eight said she knew it was, and that nobody argued with him and nobody told him he was wrong.*

*And that nobody in that hearing proposed a body, a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard, and that the word for none of them was said, and that no person in that yard proposed anything, and that a man of about sixty of the first lay who asked the question of this coast at a mole head on the thirty-first day of May past in sixty-three words which he counted four times was not in that market and was not asked and has not asked it since; and that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was on the outside of that market wall and was not touched on the said day, and that nothing was put on it and nothing came off it, and that the two older naval notices further along that wall are still on it, and that the clause that was read out loud that morning was read out of a copy in a coat and not off the wall, and that no person in that market stood in front of that wall on the said day.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that a blockade broken enough is not a blockade lifted and is not a blockade defeated, and the notice of the seventeenth day of May past is on that wall at the last page of this month and has not been withdrawn and is not withdrawn by anything said in that market; and that men of that harbor saying out loud that a road is an obligation is not a body and is not an undertaking and is not a thing that anybody in that harbor can be called to account for by anybody, being that there is nobody to call them to account; and that a sentence in nineteen words with no name against it is not a rule and is not going to be put in a book by this yard and is not going to be improved on by anybody in this yard, being that this yard did not say it and would not have said it as well.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-seven days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fifty-third night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor have no boat and have none this morning and had none last night. A man of about forty-five of the third lay is on the seventh day of nine off the water. A notice of eight clauses is on a market wall in that harbor with two older naval notices under it and has not been taken down and nothing has been put on it. A sheet of forty lines is on a market wall in a town four days up that coast. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the nineteenth day of the ninety, and nobody in this country has won anything and the notice has not been withdrawn.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
