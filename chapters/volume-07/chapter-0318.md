# Chapter 318

*Tuesday the First of June, the Thirty-Second Week With No Envelope, Nine Figures on the Back of a Slate and One of Them That Moved, Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight Pence Against Four Hundred and Fifty, a Sheet Dated the Twenty-Ninth of May Off a Boat, and a Mole-Head Light That Was Out in October*

Tuesday the first of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the count of envelopes on that bench in that chest stands at twenty and did not move, and thirty-two weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, the thirty-second of them being this day. The wall did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it. The far bench was not occupied at any hour of it, and a man of sixty-one at that bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift.**

---

The sheet came in on the Monday night in the pocket of a man of about forty-four of the second lay, who has a boat of about seventy tons and a bill from a merchant's agent at Ternwatch that he had been given at that desk on the Friday and had carried down that coast without opening it, because he was not a man who opens a bill.

He brought it to the market himself at about ten in the morning on the Tuesday because he wanted the fish market to hear it and not just his wife, and he said so out loud when he put it down on the end stall's table.

"**That came off a desk at Ternwatch on Friday and it is dated the twenty-ninth of May and I have had it in my coat since and I did not open it because I am not the man that opens it, and I have brought it down here because I would rather there were nine people in this market when you read it than one.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall read it out loud, and about forty people heard it, and a fish market on a Tuesday morning is a good room to read a price in.

"**It is a bill of an agent at Ternwatch for oil, and it is not a quotation, and it is dated the twenty-ninth of May, and the figure is four hundred and fifty pence for a barrel.**"

She put it down and went round the front of her own board and turned the slate over and uncapped the chalk and did not hesitate.

"**The day it is quoted to me,**" she said. "**The first of June. Not the twenty-ninth. I said in this market on the eighteenth of May that I would put it on the board the day it is quoted to me and not the day I hear about it, and I have said that in front of about nine people and I am not going to start being a different kind of woman now that it has happened.**"

Then she rubbed out the top line on the back of that slate and wrote the new one under it, and left the old one visible above it, on the same board, with both dates on it.

> **Lamp oil, a barrel — four hundred and fifty pence. Quoted to me the first of June.**
> **FEBRUARY. THE NINETEENTH. Lamp oil, a barrel — three hundred and seventy-eight pence. Paid.**

And the other eight lines under those two were untouched, and the ninth, which is a small bottle of physic at fourteen pence with *none in this harbor* written against it, is still a small bottle of physic at fourteen pence with *none in this harbor* written against it, and that was on purpose and everybody in that market understood that it was on purpose.

---

"**That is a rise,**" said a man of about forty-five at the front of the stall.

"**That is six shillings,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight. "**Three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and four hundred and fifty pence today, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence. That is seventy-two pence. That is six shillings exactly, and a man who wanted a plain figure has got one.**"

"**Who put it up.**"

"**Nobody in this harbor put it up, and I am going to say the whole of that, because a man of forty-four has just told nine people that he has had it in his coat since Friday and everybody in this market is now going to think that a boat brought it.**" She tapped the sheet with two fingers. "**A man in an office in a city four hundred miles from this quay wrote that figure on Friday and he has never been to this quay and he is not going to come to this quay and he does not have to, because he has never once had to be in the room where the price is used. I said that in this market on the twentieth of May and about forty of you heard me and I am not going to say it better today than I said it then.**"

"**Then it is not anybody's fault.**"

"**It is nobody's fault and it is a price,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight, "**and I have been standing at this stall since the ninth of April saying *I have none* to about two hundred people who had money, and from today I can say a figure instead of a sentence, and I would like everybody who is pleased about that to understand what has happened, which is that I have lost the only thing I had.**"

---

About half past eleven she said the other thing, out loud, in about forty people's hearing, and it went on the board afterwards in a hand that was not hers, and it was not improved on.

"**I have said out loud in this market for a month that a barrel of lamp oil at thirty-one shillings and sixpence in February means that the mole-head light at that mole head will be out in October, and I have said it about nine times and nobody has written it down anywhere except the market. Seventy-two pence is a fifth more oil. A light is not a fifth more light, it is a fifth less light, and a mole-head light is a thing you want all night, and I am not going to be the woman who has to say in October what it will be in June.**"

"**Then it will be September.**"

"**It will be whatever it will be,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight, "**and I have got a February figure and a June figure on one board with the dates on them, and in about four years somebody is going to want to know what a barrel of lamp oil was in this harbor on the first of June, and there are two figures and two dates and both of them are on a slate at a stall, and that is the whole of the record and there is nobody going to check it.**"

---

Nothing in that market said anything about it after about half past eleven, except two people. A man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime said to a man of about thirty-one, in about nine people's hearing, that a man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons was not to be trusted with anything, and the man of thirty-one did not answer him and looked at the two lines of figures on the slate instead, and neither of them had anything to do with this yard.

And the doctor of about fifty-five came down the market at about one in the afternoon and asked the woman of about fifty-eight for a small bottle of physic and she said she had none, and he said that was all right, he had not expected anything else, and he said out loud at her table that the man in a bed at Ashtide has had a prescription since the seventh of May and that he has been putting days on it since the seventh of May and that today is the twenty-fifth day and he had said nothing to anybody in particular and was not saying it to anybody in particular now either.

Nessa Pike wrote the twenty-fifth day down in the small book she carries and the doctor's two sentences went in the second column and not one word was put against the slate.

---

In the shed that night, the holder of that writ said one thing about the slate and would not say a second.

"**A price has gone up in that market today by six shillings on a barrel of lamp oil, and nobody in that harbor set it and nobody in that harbor will be able to say who did, and there is a form with a clause about unsettled claims on it that is worth a hundred times six shillings to eleven boats lying off this mole, and the two things are not in the same universe and there is nobody going to put them in it.**

"**And I am not going to go up to that stall and say that it is the notice, and I am not going to go up to that stall and say that it is not the notice, because I have said in about forty people's hearing twice in this month that I do not know and I am not going to know this month either. A price is a price and a figure is a figure and I have not got a figure for a market's anger and I am not going to invent one so that a shed has something in it.**"

"**Write that as what it is.**"

"**Write it as what it is,**" said Calder Marr, "**and put the first of June against it and no name in it, and leave the two figures on the slate where the woman of about fifty-eight put them, with the dates on them, because that slate is the only record in this harbor of what oil was on the first of June and there is nobody in this harbor going to improve on it.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the first day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday, and a wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and no envelope was made, and the said column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and twenty envelopes stand on that bench in that chest and did not move, and that thirty-two weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them.*

*And that a sheet dated the twenty-ninth day of May past, being a bill of a merchant's agent at Ternwatch and not a quotation, was brought to the end stall of the fish market of that harbor at about ten in the morning of the said day by a man of about forty-four of the second lay, who said out loud that he had carried it in his coat since the Friday preceding and had not opened it; and that the woman of about fifty-eight with the said end stall read the figure on it out loud in about forty people's hearing, and entered it on the back of her own slate on the said day and not on the twenty-ninth, being: a barrel of lamp oil, four hundred and fifty pence, quoted to her the first of June; and that the February figure of three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, was left visible above the said figure on the same board with both dates against them; and that the other eight of the nine figures on that slate did not move on the said day, and that the small bottle of physic at fourteen pence with *none in this harbor* against it did not move, and that the said figure is a rise of seventy-two pence, being six shillings exactly.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the woman of about fifty-eight said out loud in about forty people's hearing in that market on the said day that no person in that harbor set the said figure, and that a man in an office in a city four hundred miles from that harbor wrote it on the twenty-ninth of May past and has never been to that quay and is not going to come to it and does not have to, and that she had said the same thing in that market on the twentieth of May past and about forty people heard her and that she is not going to say it better; and that she said out loud that she has said *I have none* to about two hundred people since the ninth of April past of whom about forty had money, and that from the said day she can say a figure instead of a sentence, and that she called the loss of the sentence the only thing she had.*

*And that the said woman said out loud in about forty people's hearing that she had said in that market about nine times since the twentieth of May past that a barrel of lamp oil at the February figure means that the mole-head light at that mole head will be out in October, and that seventy-two pence is a fifth more oil and a light is not a fifth more light; and that a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime said out loud in that market that a man of about thirty-six with a boat of about thirty tons was not to be trusted with anything, and that the man of about thirty-one he said it to did not answer him and looked at the two figures on the slate instead; and that no person of that yard was in that market on the said day except the ship's hand, who entered the said figure and the two said sentences and put no word against the slate.*

*And that a doctor of about fifty-five asked that woman at her table for a small bottle of physic on the said day and was told there was none, and said out loud at that table that a man in a bed at Ashtide has had a prescription since the seventh day of May past and has been put on days since the seventh day of May past and that the said day is the twenty-fifth day; and that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said evening that he is not going to go up to that stall and say that the rise is the notice and is not going to go up and say that it is not the notice, and gave the reason out loud as the twenty-fourth day of April past, being that a person who says *they did it on purpose* has stopped being the party that says what a thing is not, and that a price is a price and a figure is a figure and that he has not got a figure for a market's anger and is not going to invent one so that a shed has something in it.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a figure quoted by a man four hundred miles off who has never come to this quay is not a price set in this market and is not a blockade and is not a hand on anything; that a rise of six shillings on a barrel of lamp oil is not the notice and the notice is not a rise of six shillings, and this yard has refused to put the two in one sentence in about forty people's hearing and is not going to; and that the cheapest thing in this harbor has gone up and is not coming back down, and that this yard did not do it and cannot unmake it and has a bag with four hundred and twenty-eight pence in it that is two marks and nine shillings.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and there are four hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, which is two marks and nine shillings, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and twelve pence, being one mark, four shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid. A wage of four marks was not paid on the said day and thirty-two weeks have now fallen due with no envelope standing against any of them, and the next falls due on Tuesday the eighth of June for the thirty-third week. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was not occupied at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty-five days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twenty-first night, and neither of them has moved, and a barrel of lamp oil in that market is four hundred and fifty pence today where it was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, and there are two figures and two dates on the back of a slate at an end stall and nobody is going to improve on them.**

**And a man in a bed at Ashtide has been put on days since the seventh of May and today is the twenty-fifth day and there is no physic in this harbor and a doctor of about fifty-five has said so at a table and to nobody in particular. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
