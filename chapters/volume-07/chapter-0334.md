# Chapter 334

*Friday the Eighteenth of June, Two of Them in the Water off the South End of That Mole before Six in the Morning, Four Figures Out of a Man's Own Book Read Out Loud on a Step and Copied on Nothing, and a Mark on the Face of the Second Seam Put to a Use It Has Never Been Put To*

Friday the eighteenth of June, off the south end of that mole from half past six in the morning until about two in the afternoon, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and a day of this yard's own labour was spent between about half past six in the morning and about two in the afternoon and was not paid and is not going to be paid, and this yard cannot replace a day at any figure.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in the water and was not asked to be. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, and he was not sent for and not asked and was not thanked, and nobody went near him on that crown between half past six and two in the afternoon and nobody told him that there was anybody in the water off the south end.**

---

The written reason went into the second column at about five o'clock in the morning, in the ship's own left hand, and it is there in full and no name is against it, and it read:

*that on the seventeenth day of June past a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay said out loud on a quay in about nine people's hearing that a figure out of a book of his own with an hour off a clock gives a man the height of the water at the mark on the face of the second seam; and that six men of about nineteen tons are going out on the said day on the ebb past the outer marks with a hand lead and a good hand and no figure; and that the holder of that writ proposes to take that height off that fouling with a lead and a line between about half past six in the morning and about two in the afternoon, and to give the said figure to the said six men, and to no other person, and that the use is limited to that one morning, that one boat, and that one day; and that no page of that man's book is to be copied out, carried away, opened again, or asked about, and that four figures read out loud by him and entered here are not a copy of a page; and that the fouling is to be left as it is found, and the band wiped where it is read and nothing else touched, and no mark scraped and no scale taken off and no line cut, and the seal put back.*

That is the rule of two custodians and it is the whole of it, and this yard is one party and a party of one is not a custody, and there were three parties in it, being two in the water and one on the steps, and the man on the steps was the man whose figures they were.

They went off the mole steps at about twenty to seven in the morning in a dinghy of about fifteen feet that belongs to nobody and that nobody was paid for, and there was a man in her of about thirty-two of the second lay who had come for his own reasons and took a hand lead and a line and did four soundings with the lead off the south end at the request of the man of about thirty-four and was paid nothing and was not thanked and asked nothing, and he was not of that yard and gave his name to nobody.

The mark is on the face of the second seam and the seam is six feet of the mole's own foundation stone at the foot of the parapet, and the fouling on it is weed and shell, and the band of it is a foot and seven inches deep on the weather side and clean on the other, and the top of the band is the highest the water has stood at that stone and the bottom of it is the lowest it has stood in living memory, and it is a mark that a man cannot read at a crown and can only read from a boat's gunwale with his face close to the stone.

Nessa Pike read the band three times with a hand lead hung on it to bring the same depth of water against the same inch of stone, and wrote the three readings down and the hours against them, and Calder Marr read the water on a staff of a marked plank held against the stone at the same three moments, and the two sets of figures agreed inside an inch, which is a thing two people can check and neither of them wrote in.

And then the man of about thirty-four came down the mole steps at about ten past seven with his book in his hand and did not get in the boat, and stood on the wet stone at the foot of them in about nine people's hearing — and there were nine because it is the front street and the front street goes down to the water — and read four figures out loud off one page, and no more.

"**Nineteenth of May past. The fourth day of May past. The twenty-sixth of March past. The twelfth of March past.**"

And when the ship's hand read the four back to him from her own book to be sure she had them right, he said, "**That is right, and shut it, and do not write what a band was on any of those days, because you will get it wrong and I will not correct you and we will both be embarrassed.**"

He put the book in his coat and went up the hill, and nobody followed him, and the two of them in the boat went on working until about two in the afternoon.

---

The thing that came out of it, and it came out of it the way a thing comes out of a morning's work and not the way a thing comes out of a discovery, was this.

Water is level. A man who knows the height of the water at one fixed stone at one hour knows the height of the water everywhere in that water at that hour, and he does not have to go and stand on it to find out. And that mark on the face of that mole's own stone has a band of fouling on it, and the band has a top, and the top is a height at a fixed stone; and the man of about thirty-four has been going down to that stone and writing down how deep that band was, on dated days, for a good number of years, and he never told anybody and never showed anybody and gave his name to nobody.

Which means that for any hour of any day, a man with four of those figures and a clock and a hand lead can say, to within an inch, how much water is over any ground inside that harbor — the bar, the foul off the south end, the channel, the mole itself — without going to it, and can go and be right about it when he gets there.

Nobody in that harbor had ever put it to that mark. Not in a book, not in a market, not in a shed, not in a conversation on a mole. In twenty-one years of this coast there is no page anywhere that says a fouled band on a mole's own stone is a record of the height of water, and there is no page anywhere that says four dated figures and a clock are a way of knowing a sea you are not standing in.

And that is what Calder Marr said to the ship's hand in about two hours' time in the dinghy, in a flat voice, with a lead line in his hand, and neither of them said the word *stage* and neither of them wrote it down.

"**It is not a chart,**" she said.

"**No. It does not tell you where anything is. It tells you how much water you have, and that is a different thing and it is a smaller thing, and it is a thing that a man with a lead can check for himself in a morning and does not have to take anybody's word for.**"

"**Then what have we got.**"

"**We have got four figures and an hour, and if we are right those six men will know at four o'clock this afternoon that there is nine feet on the bar and eight and a half off the foul at the south end, and they will be able to say so in a market on Monday, and neither of us will be standing in that market.**"

"**And if the band is wrong.**"

"**Then the band is wrong and we are two people in a boat with a lead and a wet book and no money, and six men go out on the ebb with a bad figure instead of no figure, and I would rather they had a bad figure than none, and I do not know that I am right, and there is nobody in this harbor I can ask.**"

---

They were in at about twenty past two and the six men took the figure on the quay at about half past two, in about nine people's hearing, from the ship's hand, in her own left hand, out loud, and the man of about fifty-one wrote it in the launch book of the *Osprey* on a line with the date against it and then wrote the six names under the date, which is what that book is for and is the only book on that quay anybody has written anything in twice.

Nessa Pike did not read the launch book. She was not asked to and she did not ask, and a book on a quay that belongs to six men is a book on a quay that belongs to six men.

At about ten past three a man of about forty-four came past the shed and said, in nine people's hearing, that six men were going out at half past five in the morning because they had a figure now and a flood to come back on, and that a man of about forty called Rowan Teel had said he would rather be out on a figure than be in on a quay, and that he had been asked nothing and had said it of his own accord.

And the man's name went in the second column of that yard's own book on the eighteenth day of June, being the second time in three days, and Nessa Pike said so out loud while she wrote it.

---

In the shed that night the woman of no office was at the bench with the cuff and the needle and the dish of nine buttons and she asked nothing, and she had asked nothing since Tuesday, and Calder Marr noticed that and said the thing about it out loud because it had to go somewhere.

"**You asked me on Tuesday what was in the week and on Thursday what I was going to do with a sentence on a quay, and today you have asked me nothing, and I am not going to read that as a kindness, and I am not going to read it as anything.**"

"**I have been mending a cuff,**" she said.

"**For two days.**"

"**For three days,**" she said, "**and I have not asked you anything because a man in a boat on Monday is going to be asked things and I am not going to be one of the people who asks a man in a boat a question the morning he goes out, and you taught me that in April and I have not forgotten it, and it is not a gift to you and it is not for you.**"

Then she said the other thing, and she said it into the cuff, and it went in the second column.

"**And I will tell you what I have worked out, and then I am going to sew. I have not asked you anything since Tuesday because on Tuesday you told me what was in that week, and it was true, and I believed it, and a true answer is not a smaller thing than a lie. You did not do a wrong thing on Tuesday. You have been somewhere I have not been since Tuesday, and I have not asked where, and that is my doing and not yours, and I am not going to pretend to you that I have been coming down here to be kept out of something, because I have not.**"

"**You are right,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to say anything else about it tonight, and I have said the reason for that out loud, which is that a thing said in a shed is a thing said in a shed, and it is enough for one night.**"

"**It is enough for one night,**" she said, "**and I would like a figure on how many nights are coming, and I have not asked for it, and I am not going to.**"

---

*First column, entered on Friday the eighteenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that two persons of that yard were in the water off the south end of that mole from about twenty to seven in the morning until about twenty past two in the afternoon of the said day, being the holder of that writ and the ship's hand, in a dinghy of about fifteen feet that belongs to nobody, with a man of about thirty-two of that harbor's own second lay in her who was not of that yard and gave his name to nobody and was paid nothing; and that a band of fouling on the face of the second seam was read three times with a hand lead against the same inch of stone and the water on it was read at the same three moments on a marked plank and the two sets of figures agreed inside an inch; and that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay came down those steps at about ten past seven in the morning, did not go in the boat, and read four figures out loud off one page of a book of his own in about nine people's hearing and no more, being the nineteenth day of May past, the fourth day of May past, the twenty-sixth day of March past and the twelfth day of March past, and that no page was copied and no page was carried away and the book went back in his coat and he went up the hill and nobody followed him; and that the fouling was left as it was found, the band wiped where it was read and nothing else touched, no mark scraped and no scale taken off and no line cut, and the seal put back; and that the said four figures with the hour of four o'clock in the afternoon of the said day went out on the quay at about half past two in the afternoon of the said day in about nine people's hearing, out of the ship's own left hand, and a man of about fifty-one of the six wrote them in the launch book of the boat of about nineteen tons on a line with the date against it and wrote the six names of that crew under the date.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the written reason for the use of the said mark was entered before the work at about five in the morning of the said day and is in this book in full and no name is against it, and that the use was limited to that one morning, that one boat and that one day and went no further; and that this yard holds four figures out of a man's book and not the page they came off, and that a party of one is not a custody and there were three parties in it, being two in the water and one on the steps; and that the said use of the said mark is a use it has never been put to on this coast, being that water is level and a dated band of fouling on a fixed stone is a record of the height of the water at that stone on that day, and that a figure and an hour and a clock give a man the height of the water over any ground inside that harbor without his standing on it; and that this is not a chart, does not tell a man where anything is or has been, does not say what is under any water, settles nothing, and is not a stage, and that no stage was taken in that shed on the said day and the Storm-Reader stage is taken in shared work with a crew at a cost and not before, and not as a solution to a problem of police or price.*

*And that a man of about forty called Rowan Teel said out loud on the quay on the said afternoon in about nine people's hearing and unasked that he would rather be out on a figure than be in on a quay, and that his name was entered in this book for the second time in three days; and that a woman of no office asked nothing of the holder of that writ on the said day, and said out loud, unasked, that a true answer given on the fifteenth day of June is not a smaller thing than a lie, and that she has not been kept out of anything and it is her own doing that she has not asked, and that she would like a figure on how many nights are coming and is not going to ask for it.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that a mark read for a use it has never been read for is not a discovery and is not a power and is not a name for anything; that four figures out of a man's own book taken in nine people's hearing and entered with a reason are not a custody and are not a copy of a page; and that a man who refuses to take a boat out and a man who is not asked to take a boat out are not the same man and are not in the same book, and this yard did not go over to the one and did not go near the other.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Friday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and did not go over to a man of sixty-one on the crown of that mole at the hour of a refusal on the said afternoon and did not leave that crown while it was happening. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-two days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-eighth night, and neither of them has moved. A day of this yard's own labour was spent in nine feet of water off the south end of that mole and was not paid and cannot be replaced at any figure. The fouling of the second seam is a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and the band was wiped where it was read and the seal was put back, and nobody in this yard has said what the fouling means and nobody in this harbor is going to. Six men of that harbor go out in the morning with a figure in a book of their own.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
