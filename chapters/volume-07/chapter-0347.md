# Chapter 347

*Thursday the First of July, the Sixtieth Charge Walked on None, a Trade Entered in the First Column With the Whole of What It Cost and What It Is Not For, a Man Who Set Himself a Day of Work on the First of July and Did Not Do It, and One Line in the Second Column With a Reason Against It and No Name in It*

Thursday the first of July, in that shed, and on the front street in the morning, and on that mole.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Thursday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the sixtieth of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Thursday, it coming down on a Monday, and the next is the sixty-first on Monday the fifth of July next, which is outside these ten. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said Thursday and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked, not sent for, not asked, and about nine people were on that crown and none of them said one word to him about a trade.**

**And this yard's own count of the three standing figures, made in that shed on the said day and not taken out of any other book: in these ten days the wall has come down bare once, being the sixtieth on Monday the twenty-eighth, and the next is outside these ten; the cistern has been charged twice and walked on none, being the fifty-ninth on the twenty-eighth and the sixtieth on the said day; and one wage Tuesday has fallen, being the twenty-ninth, for the thirty-sixth week, and the column did not move on it, so that thirty-six weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.**

---

The first column of that leaf took its entry for the said day at about half past nine in the morning and it is the longest thing anybody in that yard has put in it since the first of May past, and it is a trade and not a stage, and it is written with the whole of what it cost on the face of it, and no part of it is a power.

It is the entry for the night of the twenty-sixth of June and it is entered on the first of July, five days later, because the yard is not allowed to enter a thing on the day it happens when the thing is a thing a man did with a crew and not a thing a man did in a shed, and the rule for that is the rule of the first of May and has not been improved on.

> *First column, entered on Thursday the first day of July next at about half past nine in the morning in this shed: that on the night of the twenty-sixth of June the holder of that writ took the Storm-Reader stage in this coast's accounting, it being the reading of a remembered water against what a crew found on it, and that it was taken in shared work and with a crew and at a cost, and that the cost is named here whole. That it was a trade and not a power. That it settled nothing and has settled nothing. That the soundings it works from are of a coast that has moved, and that there is no figure for how far a coast moves and none was invented. That a line on a page is a thing a person left and the person is not on the water. That a seam identifies a difference and not a truth. That the mark on the face of the second seam was not looked at, not measured, not touched and not read on any day of that week, and the use of it put on the eighteenth day of June remains closed, four figures and no fifth at any hour of any day. That the season of the mark of the second seam is nine marks and is not paid and is not going to be paid. That the faultreader stage is spent and was not re-taken. That the bellwright stage is not improved. And that the Storm-Reader stage is now taken, once, on the night of the twenty-sixth, in shared work, with a crew, at a cost, and not before, and not as a solution to a problem of police or price, and that no part of it is a power and that it settles nothing. And that what a man on the mole could check about that week is the hour on nine lines and the price on nine lines and the name on nine lines, and what a man on the mole could not check is two foot of water on a remembered line in a strait at two in the morning, and that neither of those is a stage and one of them is a fact and the other is a claim.*

And then the woman of no office asked him one question about it at about eleven o'clock and it is in the book in her own words with no name against it, because she does not write in that book and has never written in it and will not.

"**You have written *a trade and not a power* at the top of it,**" she said, "**and I want to know what a power is in your own mouth, because you have never said that word out loud in as long as I have been in this shed, and I have heard it from men who sell things. Say it and then say what a trade is, and if they come out the same then what you have got is a thing with a better name on it.**"

"**A power is a thing one man can do that other men cannot,**" said Calder Marr, "**and that is the whole of the difference and I have never had one and I am not going to get one out of a page of somebody else's handwriting. A trade is a thing four people do together on a night and it stops when they stop and it does not go on working while they are asleep, and the one we did stopped at four o'clock in the morning because a herring skipper of fifty-one said so, and that is the difference between a trade and a power and it is not a small one.**"

"**And the two foot?**" she said.

"**The two foot is a difference and not a truth, and a man of thirty-eight has it in his own book with his own hand on his own lead, and I have it because he let me hear it and not because I took it, and I cannot check it and neither can you and neither can the man of thirty-four who keeps the fouling ladder and who said one sentence in that shed on Wednesday and was not asked a question. If a thing cannot be checked then it goes in the second column with a reason against the line, and that is where it is, and the reason is that a stranger with a lead would have had to be on that water on that night.**"

---

And the day itself did not go the way he had set it, and the entry is in the second column, and it has a reason against it and no name in it.

What he had set himself on the morning of the first of July, and he said so out loud in that shed at about ten o'clock to two people, was ordinary: warps on the bench, the splice that Cobb Terrell's mending had gone out of in June, a look at the fouling of the second seam from the hard without a lead and without touching anything, and the fish market at about eleven because a woman of about fifty-eight would have a second box of physic to give away that morning and somebody ought to be standing at the end of her stall who was not a customer.

He got up. He came down the mole at about half past nine in the morning. He sat on the bench in that shed for about an hour and a quarter and he did not splice the warp, and at about eleven o'clock he did not go to the fish market, and at about half past one in the afternoon he went down to the head of that slip and stood at the top of it for about twenty minutes and came back up, and at about two in the afternoon he started splicing the warp, and it took him until about six.

What is entered against it is that he was not able to, and no more than that.

*Second column, entered on Thursday the first day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ set himself a day of ordinary work on the said day and did not do it, and that the reason against this line is that he was not able to, and that there is nothing else against it and nothing is to be put against it, and that he has not said why he was not able to and was not asked why he was not able to, and that no name is against this line and no name is to be put against it, and that this is the first day in this yard's life that a day has been entered against that reason and it is entered with the reason and not with the name, and that no day of any week before this one may be entered against the same line afterwards, and that this line is a day lost and a lost day carries no figure, being that this yard has no figure for what a day of the holder of that writ is worth and has not invented one; and that he spliced one warp between about two in the afternoon and about six in the evening and it took four hours and it is good work and it is one warp, and that a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay spliced a warp in nineteen minutes on the eleventh day of June past and is back to warps and not in a boat and was not asked about it and did not hear about it.*

That is the whole of it, and Nessa Pike read it back to him in that shed at about half past nine in the evening and he asked her one question about it and she answered it in the plainest words she had.

"**Is there going to be a name on it?**" he said.

"**No,**" she said. "**And if there is ever a name on that line it will be put there by somebody who is not me and I will not write it and I will tell you now, in this room, so that you cannot say afterwards that nobody said. And it is not a stage. It is not on that sheet of forty lines and it is not going to be. A man of sixty-one on that crown is not it. A man of sixty-one on that bench is not it. I have been asked by neither of them and I am not going to ask either.**"

"**Write the last part of that down,**" he said.

"**I have,**" she said.

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the first day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern was charged at about ten in the morning on the said day and walked on none, being the sixtieth; and that this yard's own count of the three standing figures across these ten days is one wipe of that wall, being the sixtieth on the twenty-eighth, two charges of that cistern walked on none, being the fifty-ninth and the sixtieth, and one wage Tuesday, being the twenty-ninth for the thirty-sixth week, so that thirty-six weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a woman of no office said out loud in that shed at about eleven o'clock on the said day that the holder of that writ had written *a trade and not a power* at the head of that entry and had never said the word power out loud in as long as that yard has stood, and asked him to say what a power is and then what a trade is, and that if the two came out the same then what that yard has got is a thing with a better name on it; and that he answered that a power is a thing one man can do that other men cannot, and that this yard has never had one and is not going to get one out of a page of somebody else's handwriting, and that a trade is a thing four people do together on a night and that it stops when they stop and does not go on working while they are asleep, and that the one that was done stopped at four o'clock in the morning because a herring skipper of fifty-one said so; and that she then asked him about the two foot of water, and that he answered that two foot is a difference and not a truth, and that a man of about thirty-eight has it in his own book with his own hand on his own lead, and that this yard has it because that man let him hear it and not because this yard took it, and that this yard cannot check it and neither can the woman of no office and neither can a man of about thirty-four of that harbor who keeps a whole fouling ladder in his own book and said one sentence in that shed on Wednesday and was asked no question; and that a thing that cannot be checked goes in the second column with a reason against the line, and that the reason is that a stranger with a lead would have had to be on that water on that night.*

*And that the ship's hand said out loud in that shed on the said evening, when the holder of that writ asked her whether a name was going to be put against the line about the lost day, that no name is against that line and none is to be put there, and that if there ever is one it will be put there by somebody who is not her and she will not write it, and that she said it in that room at that hour so that he could not afterwards say that nobody had said; and that it is not a stage and is not on the sheet of forty lines and is not going to be, and that a man of about sixty-one on the crown of that mole is not it. A man of about sixty-one on the far bench across that crown is not it. She was asked by neither of them and is not going to ask either of them.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-second line:*

*What it is not: — that a day lost is not a stage and is not a power and is not a thing anybody in this harbor can put a name to and is not going to be named on that leaf, and that the reason against the line is a reason and not an explanation; and that two foot of water found on a remembered line is not a correction of five books and is not a truth about a coast and is not going to be entered as one by anybody in this yard; and that a trade four people did on a night and that stopped at four o'clock is not a thing this yard can do again next week on its own account, being that it would need ten boats and ten men who had boats and this yard has neither and did not have them last week and does not have them today.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Thursday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The cistern was charged for the sixtieth and walked on none, and the wall did not come down, and the next wipe is the sixty-first on Monday the fifth of July next, which is outside these ten. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-five days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fifty-first night, and neither of them has moved. A mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor have no boat. A man of about forty-five of the third lay is on the sixth day of nine off the water. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the seventeenth day of the ninety, and a day was lost in that shed today, and the day carries no figure, being that this yard has no figure for what a day of the holder of that writ is worth and has not invented one.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
