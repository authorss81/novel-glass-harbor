# Chapter 337

*Monday the Twenty-First of June, the Fifty-Ninth Time That Wall Came Down, the Fifty-Seventh Charge Walked on None, a Bonesetter Down a Hill He Does Not Go Up and a Figure of Sixteen Pence Out of a Man's Own Hand, a Boat on the Hard with a Line About Her, and a Name on a Line in a Book on the Steps of That Mole*

Monday the twenty-first of June, at the head of that slip in the morning, and on the steps of that mole in the late afternoon, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-ninth time since the first of May past, and the counter did not restart, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting was held in that room on the said day. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure at about ten in the morning on the said Monday by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of it, and that is the fifty-seventh of the charges this party has entered walked on none of since the third day of August past. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid.**

**And this yard's own count of the three standing figures, made in that shed on the said day and not taken out of any other book: one wipe of that wall inside these ten days, being the fifty-ninth on the said Monday, and the next on Monday the twenty-eighth, which is outside these ten; two charges of that cistern walked on none so far, being the fifty-sixth on Thursday the seventeenth and the fifty-seventh on the said Monday, and one more to come on Thursday the twenty-fourth; and one wage Tuesday so far, being Tuesday the fifteenth, and a second on Tuesday the twenty-second, and the column has not moved on the first and thirty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and at about one o'clock in the afternoon he was on that crown working on the eleventh piece of a boat and about nine people were on that crown and nobody went over to him and nobody said one word to him about a boat that is a wreck.**

---

The bonesetter came down that hill at about eleven in the morning on the said Monday because he does not go up one. He is about sixty and has been in that trade thirty-one years and has set about four hundred things and he set a man's left foot in a surgery room over a baker's on the twenty-ninth day of May past for two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and he came down that hill that Wednesday and took that money at a counter over a chandler's and said that he did not want to know the trade's business.

He came down it again on the said Monday and set a right forearm in the same room over a baker's and gave a figure of sixteen pence, and he said it out loud twice, and it was sixteen pence and it was not more and it was not less.

"**I am not going to be told how he came to be in that boat,**" he said, "**and nobody is going to tell me and I would like it kept that way, and I have said the same thing at this counter in June and I am saying it again in June and it is not a new thing.**"

"**You will not be asked,**" said Calder Marr, who had come down the hill with him because he was going past the baker's and had not gone in.

"**Then you should not have come down the hill at all,**" said the bonesetter, not unkindly, and went in.

Nobody in that yard went in after him and nobody asked him a question and nobody asked him a question on the way down the hill either, and the sixteen pence was not paid by that yard, because that yard had nothing, and it was paid by Rowan Teel out of his own hand in about six small coins before the bonesetter had finished wrapping it, and the bonesetter counted it once and did not count it twice.

What the bonesetter said afterward, at the top of the steps, in about nine people's hearing, and unasked, went in the second column and nowhere else.

"**He came in at about eleven and he did not tell me a thing about it and he did not tell me a thing about Saturday and I did not ask him and I am not going to. I have set about four hundred things and about a dozen of the men I have set came to me in the first ten minutes and told me the whole thing, and this one waited until I had the linen off his arm and then he said one sentence and it was about the tide, and he has not asked me for anything and he has not said that he is hard up and he paid in small coin, and I have set a dozen arms in this room in thirty-one years and I know what a man looks like when he has been in the water and had to be got off something, and I am not going to say one word about how, because he did not say it and it is not mine.**"

"**Sixteen pence,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Sixteen pence,**" said the bonesetter, "**and I have said it twice and it is neither more nor less, and you did not pay it and I am glad, because if you had paid it I would have had to ask you where the money came from, and I have been depending on not having to ask anybody anything in this harbor for thirty-one years.**"

---

At about four in the afternoon on the steps of that mole, in about nine people's hearing, a man of about thirty-eight asked the man of about fifty-five about the line.

He did not ask him to take the line off. That is not what anybody in that harbor wanted and it is not what was said. What was said was a plain question about a piece of wood, and it was asked by a man who had no standing whatever and knew it.

"**Is that line about her moulds coming off,**" said the man of about thirty-eight, "**or is that staying on.**"

The man of about fifty-five did not look at the boat on the hard and he looked at the man who had asked.

"**It is staying on,**" he said. "**And I said in May that it is, and nobody has asked me since, and you have now asked me and I am going to give you the same answer I gave in May and I am not going to give you a better one because a week has gone by.**"

"**I did not think you had.**"

"**A boat of mine is on a hard on that slip with a green length of copper under her housing that wants replacing since the twenty-eighth of April past, and the copper is at Ternwatch, and Ternwatch is a day's water with a foul bottom at one end and about four hours of it in a month when a man would go, and a boat's man is four marks a day, and I have three weeks of nothing and a slip at three shillings a week and I have not said yes to any of that and I am not going to today.**" He put his hand on the stem. "**And I will say the other half of it since you have asked in the open, which is that a man may not move a boat off a hard in this harbor now without somebody able to say that he moved her for a reason somebody else gave him, and I have watched this mole for a month and I know what this yard is, and I am not going to be a man with a line about her moulds who goes into the water on somebody else's errand and cannot say afterwards whose errand it was.**"

Nobody improved on it. The man of about thirty-eight said "Aye" and went and stood at the head of the slip for a while with his hands in his pockets, and nobody asked the man of about fifty-five one question about the pipe, and this yard asked him nothing, and it did not go and look at the line, and it did not go and look at the boat on the hard at any hour of the said day or of any day since the fifteenth of May past.

Then the book came out, on the steps, in about nine people's hearing, and it took four minutes.

It is a launch book and it belongs to the six of that boat of about nineteen tons, and it is a page and a half and it is not a record anybody can check except the six men who keep it, and it is not this yard's and it was not read out of it by anybody of this yard.

The man of about forty-four had it, and he did not read the whole of it. He read one line.

"**The eighteenth day of June. Out at half past five by the flood, in at half past ten. Six foot three at the bar and five foot nine off the foul at four o'clock, and the water was on the mark all the way.**"

And then he put his finger on the line under it, which was the six names, and he said:

"**And there is a seventh line and it is empty and it is the nineteenth of June and it is the reason we are all standing on these steps, and I am going to write a name in it in front of the nine of you and I am not going to be asked to and I am not going to be asked why.**"

It was Rowan Teel. He wrote his own name on the line under the nineteenth of June with his own left hand, and his right forearm was in a sling by then, and it took him a while, and about nine people watched him do it and none of them asked him one word.

"**That is the whole of it,**" he said, when he had done. "**I was hurt in the water off that foul on Saturday and that is my name and that is the day and that is the only place in this harbor where either of them is written down with the other, and I have been in this water thirty-one years and there is not one other thing about me written down anywhere.**"

Nessa Pike was not on those steps. She was in that shed, and she entered it afterward from what four people told her, and she entered the fact that four people told her, and she entered that she was not on those steps and did not ask to be told the line itself and asked for the date and the name only.

"**Say the name back to me,**" she said, when the ship's hand had it.

"**Rowan Teel.**"

"**Say the day back to me.**"

"**The nineteenth day of June.**"

She wrote both, and under them she wrote the third thing, and read it out once to nobody.

"**That is a name on a line in a book that a man can check, and it is the first page in this harbor this month that anybody can check, and it is not a page this yard made and not a page this yard holds and not a page this yard can be given a copy of, and four people who were not this yard carried it in here in their own heads and gave it to me of their own accord, and I am going to be careful about that for the rest of my life.**"

---

In the shed that night there were three of them and nobody comforted anybody.

"**A man of about thirty-four of this harbor's own second lay is now a thing this harbor can talk about,**" said Calder Marr, "**and that is the price of the eighteenth of June and it is mine, and I am not going to soften it and I am not going to go and tell him what he has become.**"

"**He is four figures and a man with a hand lead,**" said the woman of no office.

"**He is four figures and a man with a hand lead and about nine people on a quay and nine people on the steps, and this harbor can now say out loud that there is a man in it who has a book of fouling, and that is a sentence that did not exist in this harbor on the sixteenth of June past, and I put it there.**"

"**And nobody is going to go and tell him,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and nobody is going to go and thank him, and nobody is going to go and ask him for one more figure, and if any person in this shed ever goes down that slip with a question in his hand about that book I would like it entered that I said no and I would like it said in a voice that stops it.**"

"**It is not going to come to that,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have got one thing to put in the second column with a reason against it and no name in it, and the reason is not the rule of the first of May, because this is not a question. Write this. That the use of that mark is closed, that the four figures are four figures and no more, that no page of that book has been copied and none is going to be, and that if a person of this yard ever wants a fifth figure out of that book then the answer is no, and that the answer is going to be no at any hour of any day and is not going to be softened on the day a boat is in trouble.**"

"**That is a rule,**" said the woman of no office, "**and you have never made one in six volumes.**"

"**I have made two,**" he said, "**and one of them is from the first of May past and the other is from the twenty-fourth of April past, and this is the third, and it is not any worse than the other two, and I would rather have three rules in a month than none, and I am not going to pretend to you that it did not cost me anything to make it.**"

She looked at him for a moment longer than she usually does.

"**You made it on a Saturday,**" she said, "**and you did not tell me, and I have thought about that for two days and I have got to the same place twice and it is this: I do not think you did it to be able to come in here tonight and say it. I think you made it because the six men needed a fifth figure on Monday and you could not say no to them, and so you said no to the book instead.**"

"**Yes,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then it was a good rule,**" she said, "**and I am not going to be kind about it, because I have not got a figure for that either.**"

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-first day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at eight in the morning on the said Monday for the fifty-ninth time since the first day of May past, the counter not restarting, the clerk keeping his own page and not mentioning it, and no sitting held in that room on the said day; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at about ten in the morning on the said Monday and walked on none by this party, being the fifty-seventh of such charges walked on none since the third day of August past; and that a bonesetter of about sixty, thirty-one years in that trade, came down that hill on the said Monday and set the right forearm of a man of about forty of that harbor's own water in a surgery room over a baker's and gave a figure of sixteen pence out loud twice and it was neither more nor less, and that the said figure was paid by the said man out of his own hand in about six small coins and was not paid by that yard and that yard had nothing to pay it with; and that the said bonesetter said out loud at the top of those steps in about nine people's hearing and unasked, that the said man said nothing about how he came to be in the water and that the bonesetter is not going to say a word about it because it is not his.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this yard's own count of the three standing figures across the ten days from the fifteenth to the twenty-fourth day of June next is one wipe of that wall, being the fifty-ninth on the said Monday; two charges of that cistern walked on none so far, being the fifty-sixth on the seventeenth and the fifty-seventh on the said Monday, with one more to fall on the twenty-fourth; and one wage Tuesday fallen due without a column moving, being the fifteenth, with a second to fall on the twenty-second, so that thirty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the hour of this entry and thirty-five will stand at the close of the twenty-second; and that this count is this yard's own and is not taken out of any other book.*

*And that a man of about fifty-five of that harbor, being asked on the said afternoon on the steps of that mole in about nine people's hearing by a man of about thirty-eight of no standing whatever, said that the line about the moulds of the boat on that hard is staying on, and gave the same reason he gave in the month past and did not give a better one, being a green length of copper under her housing that wants replacing since the twenty-eighth of April past at Ternwatch, a day's water with a foul bottom at one end and about four hours of it in a month, a boat's man at four marks a day, three weeks of nothing and a slip at three shillings a week; and that he said out loud that a man may not move a boat off a hard in that harbor now without somebody being able to say he moved her for a reason somebody else gave him, and that this yard asked him nothing, went and looked at nothing, and did not go and look at that boat at any hour of the said day or of any day since the fifteenth day of May past.*

*And that a launch book of the six owners of a boat of about nineteen tons was held on the steps of that mole at about four in the afternoon of the said day in about nine people's hearing and read one line of by its owner, being the eighteenth day of June, and that a man of about forty wrote his own name on a seventh line under the nineteenth day of June with his own left hand and his right forearm in a sling, unasked and without saying why, and that four people carried that name and that day into that shed afterwards in their own heads and gave them of their own accord to the ship's hand, who was not on those steps and did not ask for the line itself and asked for the name and the day only.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said evening that the use of the mark on the face of the second seam is closed, that the four figures are four figures and no more, that no page of that man's book is or is going to be copied, and that if any person of that yard ever wants a fifth figure out of that book the answer is no at any hour of any day and is not going to be softened on a day a boat is in trouble; and that he said that he had made three rules in a month and that he would rather have three than none and was not going to pretend to a woman of no office that it did not cost him anything to make the third one; and that she answered, unasked, that she did not think he made it in order to be able to come in and say it, and that he agreed, and that she said it was a good rule and that she was not going to be kind about it.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a bill of sixteen pence paid out of a man's own hand is not a debt to this yard and is not an entry in this yard's book and is not a thing this yard can pay or be thanked for; that a name written by a man on a line in a book of his own is not a record this yard made and is not a record this yard can check, and four people carrying a name in their heads is not a custody; and that a third rule made in a month is not a better yard and is not a different kind of yard and is not going to be described as one by anybody in this harbor.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Monday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall came down bare for the fifty-ninth time and the cistern was charged for the fifty-seventh and walked on none. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and did not go over to a man of sixty-one on that crown at any hour of it. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-five days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-first night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end in eleven foot of water and is going down by the head and nobody is going to go out to her with a hook, and a boat with a line about her moulds is on a hard on that slip and the line is staying on and the reason has not got any better in a month. A man of about forty is hurt and paid his own bonesetter sixteen pence out of his own hand and wrote his own name on a line in a book on the steps of that mole in about nine people's hearing. Thirty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope and the column has not moved on the one wage Tuesday that has fallen due in seven days.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
