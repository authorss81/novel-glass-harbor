# Chapter 338

*Tuesday the Twenty-Second of June, the Thirty-Fifth Week and a Column That Did Not Move, Six Men Told in About Nine People's Hearing That There Is No Boat and No Money and No Instrument, a Man of About Thirty-Eight Who Began a Sentence and Put His Own Hand Flat on the Stall, and Nobody in That Harbor Proposing Anything*

Tuesday the twenty-second of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, and it was the second wage Tuesday to fall due inside these ten days, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and twenty envelopes stand on that bench in that chest and did not move, and thirty-five weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on the said Tuesday and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday, and the fifty-eighth charge falls on the Thursday after this one. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and has been nothing in it since the nineteenth day of June past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in the fish market at about eleven in the morning of the said day and said nothing to anybody. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and about nine people were on that crown in the afternoon of the said day and one of them was sixty-one and nobody went over to him.**

---

The fish market holds about forty people on a Tuesday morning and about nine of them were standing at the end of the end stall at about eleven, and it was said out loud there, as everything in that harbor is said, in about nine people's hearing, and it was not said in a room and it was not said on a mole.

The man of about fifty-one of the six of that boat of about nineteen tons said it, because it was his boat and his crew and there is a man in that harbor who has asked three questions in a month and stopped, and this was not a question, it was a fact, and he gave it as a fact.

"**There is no boat.**"

"**We know that,**" said a man of about forty-five.

"**I know that you know it. I am going to say it out loud in a market because I am not going to be a man who says it in a kitchen and then has it said back at me in a kitchen, and because there are nine of you standing here and I would like the ninth of you to hear a man say it in his own mouth.**"

"**Then say it in your own mouth.**"

"**There is no boat,**" said the man of about fifty-one. "**There is a wreck on the foul off the south end going down by the head, and there is a line about the moulds of a boat on the hard, and there is a man of about thirty-six with a coaster of about thirty tons of his own who has not been out past this mole head since the seventeenth day of May past and eleven weeks of a season standing against her, and there is a boat of about seventy tons alongside that went four days past the line on a paper on a table and came back with a stove out of her wheelhouse and nobody in this harbor has seen one hour of it. There is no boat. That is the whole of the boats.**"

"**And no money to get one,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, over the shoulder of her own slate, "**and I will say it as a figure and not as a feeling, because I have a slate and you all know what it is for. I have nine figures on the back of it and one of them went up six shillings on the first of June and is not coming down, and the other eight have not moved since February, and none of the nine of them is the price of a boat, and I have never had a price of a boat on that board in twenty-two years and I am not going to have one now, because I could not tell you what it was, and a woman who puts a figure on a board cannot take it off again.**"

"**And no instrument,**" said Calder Marr, from the end of the aisle, and he said it because nobody else in that market could say it and it was his to say.

"**You have got no instrument.**"

"**I have got no instrument and I have no boat and I have nothing in a bag that is a figure of anything, and I have a book with four columns in it and three of them a man can check, and none of those four things is a boat and none of them ever was and I am not going to stand in this market this morning and pretend that a page is a hull.**"

That went into a market and it was heard by about nine people and it is on this page, and it is the whole of what that yard had to offer in that market on that morning, and it cost the speaker something and nobody said so.

Nobody proposed anything. That is the sentence, and it is worth saying exactly. In about nine people's hearing in a fish market on the twenty-second day of June, nobody proposed a body, a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard, and nobody said the word for any of them, and nobody said the word *network*, and nobody suggested that a harbor needs somebody to speak for it, and no person in that yard proposed anything at all, and the reason is not a rule and is not a stance and is the plain fact that six men have no boat and that is what six men have.

---

A man of about thirty-eight at the end of the aisle began something at about half past eleven and stopped it himself.

He had been at that market on the thirty-first day of May past and had got about four words into a sentence before the holder of that writ stopped him, and the four unfinished words had been on a mole in the open ever since, and this yard had said out loud on that day that it was not going to refuse the sentence either, and it did not refuse it and it did not finish it and it left a space on that mole.

He got two words in this time.

"**There ought to be —**"

And then he put his hand flat on the end stall's table, the way a man does to stop himself, and looked at his own hand, and said:

"**No. I have had my three. I am not going to be a man in this market who has had four.**"

Nobody finished it for him. Nobody said what it ought to be. A man of about forty-five said "Aye" and a woman of about fifty-eight said nothing, and Calder Marr said nothing at all, and the space on that mole was left where it had been put in the month past, and it is not a smaller space for a man having stopped himself in it.

A man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime came through with the hod on his shoulder and said out loud, to nobody, that a man who was out on the ebb past the outer marks on Saturday evening is a man who should have had a bigger boat, and that this has been said before about a man of about thirty-six, and that nobody in that market agreed with him and nobody in that market told him he was wrong, and about nine people let a man with a hod say a thing they did not agree with and did not correct.

A man of about thirty-one of no lay with a rope-maker's hand was on the quay that afternoon mending a warp for no payment and for nobody, and he is not the man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a chart in a bag, and the two of them were about forty yards apart on that quay and nobody in that harbor ran them together, and the one with the chart in a bag was in the market at about one o'clock and said nothing to anybody about five books or a chart or a strait or a sound, and nobody asked him, and he is not a witness and has not been made one.

---

In the shed that night the woman of no office had a cuff and a needle and a dish of nine buttons and she said the thing that was owed, and she said it once.

"**You stood in a market this morning and said out loud that a page is not a hull, in about nine people's hearing, and I would like you to have heard how it sounded from the end of the aisle, and I am going to tell you and then I am not going to say anything about it for the rest of the month.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**It sounded like a man giving up the only thing he has got, in public, for nothing,**" she said. "**And it is not nothing, and I want that written down in the same second column, and I am not going to write it, because I am a person of no office and I do not write in that book and I have never once written in that book, and if I start now then this yard has a second person writing in it and that is a different kind of yard and I will not be the one who makes it different.**"

"**Write it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No,**" she said, and went back to the cuff.

The ship's hand wrote it and put the attribution in and did not put a name against it.

*That a woman of no office, in that shed, on the twenty-second day of June, said out loud that what the holder of that writ said in that market on the said morning sounded like a man giving up the only thing he has got, in public, for nothing; and that she said that it is not nothing; and that she was asked to write it and would not, and gave the reason, being that she is of no office and has never written in that book and will not be the person who makes it a second hand.*

"**There is going to be a day,**" said Nessa Pike, when that was in, "**when six men in this harbor have a boat again or have not, and whichever way it goes, this yard will not have been the one that put them in it or kept them out of it, and that is the same as the page is not a hull and I am not going to write anything nicer than that about it.**"

"**There is not going to be a day,**" said Calder Marr, "**in these ten days, and I am not going to promise one, and I am not going to be a man in that market next week saying that something will come, because a boat is a hull and a crew and a season and about three hundred pounds of something, and this yard has twenty pence of something and no way of getting any and no instrument to ask with, and if a man of about thirty-six with a coaster of about thirty tons takes her out past this mole head again then that is his boat and his risk and his bread, and this yard will not be in it and will not have said it should be.**"

"**That is three times you have said that in a month,**" said the woman of no office.

"**It is the third time and it is the same reason and I am not going to improve on it,**" he said, "**and I am going to say the fourth thing too, since we are counting, and the fourth thing is this. Six men are standing on a quay in this harbor with no boat, and on Thursday night this page gets read back in full and I am going to say that out loud in that shed in front of two people, and it is going to be the first time in the whole of this yard's life that this yard has entered a thing it cannot do and left it in.**"

"**You are not going to do it on Thursday,**" said the woman of no office.

"**No,**" he said. "**I am not going to do it on Thursday, because a thing said in a shed to two people is a thing said in a shed, and on Wednesday morning in a fish market in about forty people's hearing is the only place it is worth anything, and that is not a rule and it is a fact about what a sentence is.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-second day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-fifth week, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and that the column did not move, and that thirty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about nine people's hearing and not in a room, a man of about fifty-one said out loud that there is no boat, and gave the four boats in that harbor as they stand, being a wreck on the foul off the south end, a boat on the hard with a line about her moulds, a coaster of about thirty tons not out past that mole head since the seventeenth day of May past, and a coaster of about seventy tons four days past the line and in with a stove out of her wheelhouse; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said that she has nine figures on a slate and that none of them is the price of a boat and that she is not going to put one there because a woman who puts a figure on a board cannot take it off again; and that the holder of that writ said out loud in the same hearing that that yard has no instrument and no boat and nothing in a bag that is a figure of anything and a book with four columns of which three are checkable, and that a page is not a hull.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that nobody in that hearing proposed a body, a committee, an assembly, a fleet, a wing or a guard, and that the word *network* was said in no mouth in that market on the said day, and that no person in that yard proposed anything, and that the reason is not a rule and is not a stance and is the plain fact that six men have no boat; and that a man of about thirty-eight at the end of the aisle began a sentence at about half past eleven in the morning of the said day and stopped it himself, having got two words out, and said that he has had his three and is not going to be a man who has had four, and that nobody finished it for him and nobody said what it ought to be and the space left on that mole in the month past was not filled and is not a smaller space for his having stopped himself in it; and that a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime said out loud in that market that a man out on the ebb past the outer marks on Saturday evening should have had a bigger boat, and that about nine people let him say it and none of them agreed with him and none of them told him he was wrong; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a coaster out of Ashtide and a chart in a bag was in that market on the said afternoon and said nothing to anybody about five books or a chart or a strait or a sound and was asked nothing and is not a witness and has not been made one and may not be cited in a room this yard keeps.*

*And that a woman of no office said out loud in that shed on the said evening that what the holder of that writ said in that market in the morning sounded like a man giving up the only thing he has got, in public, for nothing, and that it is not nothing; and that she was asked to write it in that book and would not, and gave the reason, being that she is of no office and has never written in that book and will not be the person who makes it a second hand; and that the ship's hand entered it with the attribution in and no name against it; and that the holder of that writ said out loud in the same hearing that there is not going to be a day in these ten days when six men in that harbor have a boat again, and that he is not going to promise one and is not going to be a man in that market saying that something will come, and that a boat is a hull and a crew and a season and about three hundred pounds of something, of which that yard has twenty pence of nothing and no way of getting any.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a market in which nobody proposes anything is not a market in which something has been refused, and is not an agreement, and is not a failure; that a sentence a man stopped himself from finishing is not a question asked and is not a body proposed and is not an organization begun; and that a page is not a hull and a book with four columns is not a boat and is not going to be described as one by anybody in this harbor, and the fact that this yard said so out loud in about nine people's hearing is not a surrender and is not a speech.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Tuesday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and the fifty-eighth charge falls on the Thursday after this day. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-six days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-second night, and neither of them has moved. Thirty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them and the column has not moved on either of the two wage Tuesdays that have fallen due in these ten days. Six men of that harbor have no boat and no money and no instrument and no proposal, and the wreck of about nineteen tons is on the foul off the south end going down by the head, and a man of about thirty-six of the third lay with a coaster of about thirty tons has not been out past that mole head since the seventeenth day of May past, and about eleven tons to a lighter for a season is the price of a different road and nobody has agreed it and nobody has refused it in a room. A harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this yard took the figure of ninety on the fifteenth day of June past, being the day six men of that harbor were told to stand by, and this is the twenty-second of June and this is the eighth day of the ninety and nobody in this country has won anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
