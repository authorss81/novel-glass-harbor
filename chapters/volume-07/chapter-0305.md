# Chapter 305

*Wednesday the Nineteenth of May, a Man With Two Marks and One Shilling and Nine Days on a Prescription, One Mark and Eightpence and a Penny of Line, Three Soundings Inside the Harbor Line, and a Mark Fouled to About a Foot and Seven Inches*

Wednesday the nineteenth of May, at the head of the slip in the morning, off the south end of that mole in the afternoon, and in the shed on that mole in the evening.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day. One mark and eightpence and a penny of line went out of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not occupied at any other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of the said Wednesday, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The plasterer is thirty-eight and he has been out of work since the ninth of April and he has a boy of two and he is a man who asks things out loud because he has found out in the last six weeks that being quiet is more expensive than being asked to.

He asked it at the head of the slip in about nine people's hearing and he asked it in the plainest words anybody in that harbor has used since the sixth of March.

"**Is there a boat.**"

"**There is a boat,**" said Calder Marr. "**It is about thirty tons and it is a man's and it is not this yard's, and it is at a mark and eightpence for the day with a man in her, and that is the figure. What do you want it for.**"

"**I want it to carry a chest up the coast on Saturday.**"

"**For a person in a house.**"

"**For my wife.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**The doctor wrote for it on the seventh of May and the chemist at Ternwatch has it and it is four days up that coast and a boat up and back is two days and a chest does not go in a cart because the road turns to rock at the eleven-mile and I have tried the road.**" The plasterer was not embarrassed, which was the difficulty. "**I have two marks and one shilling. That is three hundred and fifty-two pence and I have said it out loud and I would like to be asked for the fare and not for the hire.**"

Nobody asked him for the fare and nobody offered him the hire.

"**I am going to say no,**" said Calder Marr, "**and then I am going to say why, and then you are going to decide whether the why is good enough, and if it is not good enough you are going to go and do something else and I am not going to stop you.**

"**I have no instrument. There is no charter, there is no permit, there is nothing with my name on it, and if you hire that boat off me on Saturday you are hiring a man in a boat and not a paper, and a man in a boat can be stopped on the water by anybody with a form, and if he is stopped with your wife's chest in him then the stopping is lawful and it is done to you and not to him.**"

"**He would not get stopped.**"

"**He would get stopped, and it is not about him, and you know it is not about him, because it is not about him on the Friday either. It is about a chest of physic in a boat and a man who cannot show anybody anything.**" He put his hand on the bollard and left it there. "**And there is the other one and it is worse. If a man of this yard carries a thing up that coast for a person in a house, then this yard is a man in that house's business. There are about forty people in this harbor who know what that yard is, and there is one man of about thirty, two days down that coast, who knows it in about forty people's hearing and is not of it and has not been able to refuse it, and I did that to him in the spring by putting a sheet in his daughter's mouth without asking him. I am not going to do it a second time with a chest of physic for a child of two.**"

"**Then who carries it.**"

"**I do not know,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have said that sentence three times in six days and I have got worse at it, and that is what a party with no instrument has and it is not going to get better this month.**"

The plasterer stood there a moment longer and then said:

"**There is a boat of about thirty tons goes up on the Saturday for eight marks.**"

"**There is.**"

"**That is one thousand two hundred and eighty pence,**" said the plasterer, "**and I have three hundred and fifty-two, and I have said both numbers out loud in about nine people's hearing on the head of a slip, and neither of them is going to change, and I am going up to the chemist's on Friday and I am going to ask him for two days' credit, and he is going to say no, and I am going to come down this coast with nothing in my hands.**"

He went up the mole steps. He was not thanked and was not asked anything else and nothing was put in his hands.

**And a doctor of about fifty-five came down the front street about two hours later and stood at the foot of those steps and said the thing that is not a figure, in about nine people's hearing, and then went up again.**

"**There is no physic in this harbor and there has not been since the middle of April,**" he said. "**I have been putting nine days on that chest since the seventh and I have run out of days about a week ago. I am not saying that to anybody in particular. I am saying it at a slip because a slip is where a man says a thing in this harbor.**"

---

The boat was hired at about half past four in the afternoon at a box on the corner of that mole, and it was about thirty tons and it was a man's and it was not this yard's, and one mark and eightpence went out of that yard's own book, and a penny of line went with it.

The hire was on this yard's own account and for this yard's own purpose. It was not salvage, it was not a claim, it was not work under an instrument, and it was not a way of getting past anything, and every person in that boat knew which of those four it was and the man who rowed it was told before the painter went and not after.

Three soundings were taken off the south end of that mole inside the harbor line at about ten feet, at about twenty-two feet, and at about thirty-one feet, off the ground under the crown, with a hand lead and a line and a man of about thirty-four of this harbor's own second lay who does that for a living and who was paid three shillings and sixpence out of the same bag and is not the boat's man and was not asked anything.

Nobody from that yard went out in her but four. **Tobias Wren was not in that boat and was not sent for and was not asked, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was occupied from ten until four and did not come down, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it.**

Fisk read the figures off the slate and showed the slate round the boat, and the slate said:

**THE MARK ON THE FACE OF THE SECOND SEAM, AT THE FACE, IS FOULED ABOUT A FOOT AND SEVEN INCHES ON THE WEATHER SIDE AND NOTHING ON THE OTHER SIDE, AND THE BOTTOM UNDER THE CROWN IS SAND AND NOT ROCK, AND THE TWO FIGURES OF THIRTY-ONE FEET AND TEN FEET ARE A MAN'S OWN LINE AND ARE NOT READ TWICE BECAUSE A LINE THAT IS READ TWICE IS A LINE THAT HAS BEEN CHOSEN.**

"**That is the whole of it,**" he said, "**and it took about twenty minutes and it is the first figure that has gone into that book in six weeks that a man in a boat can go and look at.**"

Then they stood in about four inches of water on the crown of that mole for about ten minutes looking at two things about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins, and neither of them has moved and neither of them was touched, and they are three hundred and forty-two days up that mole this evening.

And on the way back in, the man of thirty-four of the second lay said one thing, standing in the boat, and he said it to the slate and not to anybody.

"**It was four inches on the twelfth of March and it was four inches on the twenty-third, and it was a foot on the twenty-sixth, and a foot and a half on the fourth of May, and a foot and a half on the thirteenth, and it is a foot and seven inches today. I have written them all down myself in my own book on the day and nobody has ever told me to.**"

"**What do you make of it.**"

"**I make nothing of it,**" he said. "**I write it down because it is there and because a man in a boat can go and look at it, and a figure that a man in a boat can go and look at is the only kind there is, and if it is a season I will say it is a season when it is a season, and if it is not a season then nobody in this harbor will ever know, and that is also fine.**"

*First column, entered on Wednesday the nineteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a boat of about thirty tons was hired at a box on the corner of that mole at about half past four in the afternoon of the said day, for one mark and eightpence, with a penny of line, on this yard's own account and for this yard's own purpose, and that it was not salvage and not a claim and not work under any instrument and was not used as a way of getting past any line, and that the man who rowed her was told all four of those things before the painter went and not after.*

*And that three soundings were taken off the south end of that mole inside the harbor line on the said day at about ten feet, about twenty-two feet and about thirty-one feet, with a hand lead and a line and a man of about thirty-four of this harbor's own second lay who was paid three shillings and sixpence out of the same bag; and that the said soundings read, at the face, that the mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other side, and that the bottom under the crown is sand and not rock.*

*And that the fouling of the said mark is entered against its own days and is a figure a man in a boat can go and look at, being four inches on the twelfth, the fifteenth, the seventeenth, the nineteenth and the twenty-third of March past, about a foot on the twenty-sixth, a foot and a half on the fourth of May, a foot and a half on the thirteenth, and a foot and seven inches on the said nineteenth; and that the man who wrote the said figures down wrote them in his own book on the day and was asked to write none of them and was asked nothing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a plasterer of about thirty-eight asked at the head of that slip in about nine people's hearing for a boat to carry a chest up that coast for his wife, and that he had two marks and one shilling, being three hundred and fifty-two pence, and said the figure out loud, and that he was refused, and that the reason given out loud was in two parts, being that a party with no instrument cannot give a hirer a paper to produce, and that a man of this yard carrying a thing for a person in a house makes this yard a man in that house's business.*

*And that the said reason was given with the standing of the fourth day of April past about a page in a drawer in a house and the ninth of April past about a man of about thirty who cannot read, and that neither of those men was asked anything on the said day and was not written to; and that the said plasterer said out loud in about nine people's hearing that a boat of about thirty tons goes up on the Saturday for eight marks, being one thousand two hundred and eighty pence, against his three hundred and fifty-two; and that no person in that harbor made up the difference and that this yard has two hundred and sixty-one pence and did not offer any of it and would not have been permitted to.*

*And that a doctor of about fifty-five said out loud at the foot of those steps in about nine people's hearing that there is no physic in this harbor and that he has been putting nine days on a chest since the seventh and that he is not saying it to anybody in particular; and that no person in that yard was asked for a thing on the said day and did not go and look for one.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirtieth line:*

*What it is not: — that a refusal given with a reason that a man can repeat is not a kindness and is not a stone thrown and is not the last time it will be given; that a figure a man in a boat can go and look at is not proof of a season and is not a stage and is not a stage, and a mark fouling a foot and seven inches is not a month and not a week and nobody in that boat said one word about why a mark fouls faster in some months than in others and nobody is going to; and that a person with no instrument who says *I do not know* three times in six days is not becoming humble and is not learning and is out of his depth and has been since the first of March.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and one mark, eight shillings and a penny went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying that anybody is going to. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-two days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eighth night, and neither of them has moved, and a mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches, and this is the first figure that has gone into that book in six weeks that a man in a boat can go and look at.**
