# Chapter 314

*Friday the Twenty-Eighth of May, a Woman of Thirty Back Up a Hundred and Forty Feet of Stone With a Crate in a Boat, One Question About a Round of Glass in a Drawer, One Mark and Eightpence Into a Bag That Had Nothing Come Into It Since the Fifth, and an Anchor Fluke Out of Nine Feet of Mud Inside the Harbor Line*

Friday the twenty-eighth of May, on that mole in the morning, and at a box on the corner of that mole in the afternoon.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing went out of this yard on the said day. One mark and eightpence came into it, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and that is the whole of what has come into that yard since the fifth day of May past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

She came up in a coal boat at about seven in the morning and she came up those steps on her own back with a crate, the same as the seventeenth, and there were about nine people on that mole at that hour because there are about nine people on that mole most mornings.

She put the crate down at the top and she did not look up the hill.

He did not go down to the slip.

It was not a goodbye in May and it is not an arrival in June and neither of them has called it anything.

"**I came back because I said I would come back,**" she said, "**and nobody asked me to, and I want that said in that order, and I would like it said by somebody who was not there.**"

"**Nobody asked you,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I have been on that mole since half past five and I can say it that nobody asked you, and I would like it written down that I said it and would have said it either way.**"

Then the woman of no office asked her one question, and it was about the round of glass, and it was one question, and she asked it once.

"**Who is the second hand on it.**"

The three people on that mole did not answer her, and one of them — the holder of that writ — said out loud, in about nine people's hearing, that he had been asked that question on the twentieth day of May past about a different thing in a different house and had given the same answer then and was giving it now, and the answer was that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second, and that a party of one is not a custody, and that a second person holding the other end of that drawer is a second address in this country and was not agreed to on the ninth of April past and has not been agreed to since.

"**That is not an answer,**" she said.

"**It is the whole of what there is,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to give you a better one by making one up, and I am not going to give you one by finding a person, because finding a person is the second address and the second address is the ninth of April.**

"**And I am going to say the other half of it out loud, because you came four days up that coast with a thing in a drawer and nobody asked you to and you do not get a better answer from me for that. A page about a place does not come out of the place it is about, and a page a community put on a wall because a yard told it to is a page the yard is holding in a drawer in that town. That sentence is the twenty-fourth of April past and it is on a page and it has not been improved on since, and it is about the sheet on that wall and it is not about your drawer, and I am not going to let anybody use it about your drawer, including you.**"

"**Then it goes in nobody's column.**"

"**It goes in the second column with a reason against the line and no name in it, and that is what goes in the second column, and you are of no office and you are not on the crew and your forty marks stand first after food and they have not been reduced by a crate and they are not going to be paid by one, and none of that moved while you were away and none of it moved this morning.**"

She did not argue with that and she was not thanked and nothing was put in her hands.

---

The rest of that day was money, and it was the first money that had come into that shed since the fifth day of May, and it took about ninety seconds to agree and most of that ninety seconds was a man being told what he was agreeing to.

The boat's owner of about fifty-five came up the hill at about half past two. He has a boat of about thirty tons that lies inside this harbor on the west quay on soft ground with a green length of copper under her housing that wants replacing, a slip at three shillings a week that he pays for, and his own ground tackle in about nine feet of water under that quay where it has been since the winter.

"**I want my hook and my chain out,**" he said. "**Inside the line, on my own ground, my own iron, and I cannot get a man because there is nobody in this harbor to hire, and a man with a boat of about thirty tons was on your mole on the nineteenth at a mark and eightpence and I heard about it on the Friday from a man who heard about it on the Thursday.**"

"**That was this yard's own account and this yard's own purpose,**" said Calder Marr, "**and this is yours, and I am going to say the difference out loud before I say yes.**

"**A party with no instrument may be hired by a private person for a private purpose, and that has been true since the first of May and it is not new. What you are hiring is a boat. What is not salvage, is not a claim, is not a claim window, is not a charter, and is not a way of getting past anything — that notice on that market wall governs leaving this port and passing a line, and it does not govern one shackle of chain in nine feet of mud off a quay inside this harbor's own water, and there is nothing whatever in it that touches you this afternoon.**

"**And the other thing, which is the rule of the first of May and has never been improved on, is that a man in a boat this yard has paid is a man this yard can be asked about. That is not a warning to you. It is a statement of what has just happened to that man, and he has to be told it before the painter goes and not after, and I am going to make the boat's owner say it to him and not me.**"

The boat was hired at about half past two at a box on the corner of that mole, for one mark and eightpence, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went into that yard's own book and the penny was the ship's own and did not go out of that book.

The man in her is about forty-one and he is of that harbor's own second lay and he is a hired hand and he has done that work for nineteen years and his name is Cobb Terrell and the boat's owner said it out loud to him in about nine people's hearing and Cobb Terrell said it back, and it went in.

Then the boat's owner of about fifty-five told him all five of those things himself, in the boat, before the painter went, and Cobb Terrell said that he had heard it and that he did not mind, and then he went out and got his iron up out of about nine feet of mud off the west quay inside this harbor's own line, and it took about four hours, and the hook came up with the fluke and a stud broken off it, and the whole of that stud is in the mud off the west quay inside the line and nobody in this yard is going after it, because it is not this yard's iron and it is not this yard's mud and nobody in this yard was asked.

At about half past three in the afternoon the chain came up on the fluke and it swung, and Cobb Terrell had his weight on the gunwale where a man of nineteen with a boat's man of his own puts his weight when he is in a hurry in about four feet of water, and the stud came through the mud on the end of the chain and took him across the top of the left foot, and he got his hands on the gunwale and he did not go in the water and the boat's owner of about fifty-five had the chain off him in about two seconds.

He was not hurt in the water and there is no water in this paragraph. He was hurt in the mud, on a quay, in this harbor, in his working clothes, at about half past three in the afternoon of a Friday, in a job that was hired for a private person's private purpose at a mark and eightpence out of a bag with ninety-two pence in it.

**Nobody from that yard was on the water at any hour of that Friday and nobody from that yard was in that boat at any hour of that day, and the first person in this harbor to know anything about it was the boat's owner of about fifty-five telling the yard about it on the Saturday morning at about eight, and he told it as a fact and not as an accusation, and he did not ask for anything.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-eighth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a boat of about thirty tons was hired at a box on the corner of that mole at about half past two in the afternoon of the said day for one mark and eightpence, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, by a boat's owner of about fifty-five of that harbor, on his own account and for his own purpose, and that the said hire was not salvage and not a claim and not a claim window and not a charter and was not a way of getting past any line, and that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past governs leaving the port and passing the line named in the fifth clause of it and does not govern one shackle of chain in about nine feet of water off the west quay inside that harbor's own water; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard's own book on the said day and that it is the whole of what has come into that book since the fifth day of May past.*

*And that a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay, a hired hand, whose name is Cobb Terrell, went out in the said boat on the said afternoon and said his name out loud in about nine people's hearing, and that every person in that boat was told all five of the said things before the painter went and not after, by the said boat's owner and not by that yard; and that the said man took up a hook and a chain out of about nine feet of mud off the west quay inside that harbor's own line, and that a broken stud came through the mud on the end of that chain at about half past three in the afternoon and took him across the top of the left foot, and that he did not go into the water.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a woman of no office came up that mole at about seven in the morning of the said day from a boat going for coal, with a crate, on her own back, and said out loud that she came back because she had said she would and because nobody asked her to; and that she asked one question on that mole in about nine people's hearing about a round of glass in a drawer in a house four days up that coast, being who is the second hand on it, and it was asked once; and that the holder of that writ gave the same answer out loud that he gave on the twentieth day of May past about a different thing in a different house, being that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second, and that a party of one is not a custody, and that a second person holding the other end of the said drawer is a second address in this country and was not agreed to on the ninth day of April past and has not been agreed to since; and that the said holder said out loud in the same hearing the sentence of the twenty-fourth day of April past, being that a page about a place does not come out of the place it is about and a page a community put on a wall because a yard told it to is a page the yard is holding in a drawer in that town, and said that that sentence is about a sheet on a wall and is not about the said drawer and is not going to be used about it.*

*And that the said woman is of no office, is thirty, is not on the crew, is not restored, has not been forgiven, was not thanked, and that her forty marks stand first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and were not reduced by the said crate.*

*And that a man of about forty-one of the second lay of that harbor was hurt in the said boat at about half past three in the afternoon of the said day, and that no person of that yard was in that boat or on the water at any hour of it, and that the first person in that harbor to know anything of it was the said boat's owner of about fifty-five, who told that yard about it on the Saturday morning following at about eight as a fact and not as an accusation, and asked for nothing.*

*And that this yard did not do three things on the evening of the said day and did not do them on the Saturday morning either, being that it did not send anybody to the house of the said man of about forty-one, did not send anybody to look at the said foot, and did not pay one penny of anything; and that the reason against each of the three is the ninth day of April past and is the same reason as is against the line above it, and that this is entered in this column and not in the first because there is nobody in that harbor who can check it, and because a thing a yard did not do is the only kind of line in this column that this yard is certain of.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that a yard which has taken money is not a different yard from the yard that refused the money, and the difference between the two is one mark and eightpence; that a man hurt in a boat that a yard hired for a private person is not a casualty and is not a martyr and is not a cost that has been paid for, and the figure of what it will cost has not been said out loud by anybody in this harbor; and that a page is not a custody and a drawer is not a custody and a party of one is not a custody, and that this yard has now said that out loud in about nine people's hearing for the second time in three weeks and has not got better at it.*

---

**One mark and eightpence came into this yard on the said Friday, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and it is the first money that has come into that bag since the fifth day of May past, and there are two hundred and sixty pence in it, which is one mark, eight shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and eighty pence, being two marks and five shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing went out of that yard on the said day and not one penny of line went out of that bag in it. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty-one days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and seventeenth night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay was hurt in about nine feet of water off the west quay inside that harbor's own line in a job that was somebody else's and this yard's at one mark and eightpence.**

**And here is the whole of it. A notice that governs leaving a port was not touched, a shackle of chain came up out of nine feet of mud inside a harbor's own line, a woman of thirty came back up a hundred and forty feet of stone with a crate and was asked nothing and answered nothing, and a man of thirty-two stood on a mole and said out loud in nine people's hearing that a second hand on a drawer is a second address and that this yard is not going to make one. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it except one boat's owner of about fifty-five, who got a hook and a shackle he had paid for himself.**
