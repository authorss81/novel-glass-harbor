# Chapter 319

*Wednesday the Second of June, a Man of Sixty-One Back on the Water With a Bandaged Right Hand, a Bonesetter of About Sixty Down a Hill He Does Not Go Up, Two Marks, Seven Shillings and Fourpence Out of a Bag With Four Hundred and Twenty-Eight in It, and Twenty Pence Left*

Wednesday the second of June, at a counter over a chandler's in the afternoon, and on that mole in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day. Two marks, seven shillings and fourpence went out of it, being four hundred and eight pence, and with the one hundred and sixty-nine pence that went out on the nineteenth day of May past, being a boat and a penny of line, the whole of what has gone out of that bag since that day is five hundred and seventy-seven pence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

He was on the crown of that mole at about half past eight in the morning on the second of June with a right hand bandaged and a boat's hook in his left, and nobody in that yard sent for him and nobody met him and nobody had said anything to him since the nineteenth day of May.

He is sixty-one. He built the boat of about nineteen tons that is in eleven pieces on the crown of that mole himself, fifty years ago or near it. He hurt that hand on the eighth day of May past across a sheet of plate glass and a bonesetter said the palm and not the back and said the hand would grip again by the middle of June and two-thirds of what it gripped on the Friday and about nine parts in ten by Christmas, and he gave that figure himself and would not be argued out of it, and he was not thanked and said he was not going to be.

He came back to work on the second of June because the second of June is the day he said he would come back, and nobody asked him to and nobody said good morning to him, and he worked.

He is not this yard's injury and he is not this yard's sacrifice and he is not the man in a boat that this yard hired, and this yard knows the difference and has entered it and is not going to spend him.

Nessa Pike entered one line about it in the second column and it is the whole of what is on the page about him: **that the holder of that writ did not go over to him on the said day, and that nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and that neither brother has said why.**

---

The bonesetter came down that hill at about two in the afternoon because he does not go up one. He is about sixty and he has been in that trade thirty-one years and has set about four hundred things, and he set a man's left foot in a surgery room over a baker's on the Saturday morning last and gave the figure of two marks, seven shillings and fourpence and said that it would be neither more nor less, and he was told about it on the Monday and he came on the Wednesday and did not say no.

He took the money at a counter over a chandler's on the front street, and he counted it, and he did not count it twice.

"**That is right,**" he said. "**I have said a figure and you have paid a figure and there is nothing else in this transaction and I would like it kept that way. I do not want to know the trade's business. I do not want to know how he came to be in that boat. I have set about four hundred things and I have been wrong about the number of them twice and I am not going to be the third thing that is wrong in a foot.**"

"**You will not be asked again,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I know I will not be asked again, because nobody in this harbor asks anybody anything, and that is the one thing about this place that I have come to depend on,**" said the bonesetter, "**and I am not going to be a man who says something clever at a counter.**"

Then he said one thing about the man, and it was not asked for and it was not the yard's business, and it went in the second column.

"**He came on Saturday on a crutch with a sailcloth tied over it and he had paid me nothing, and he told me the whole of it, including the part where it was somebody else's boat and somebody else wanted his iron out of the mud, and he told it the way a man tells a thing that he has been turning over since it happened. And I have set about four hundred things and I can tell you that a man who is angry at a foot is a man who has not yet worked out what else he is angry at, and that foot is going to be mended long after the other thing has finished with him.**"

---

Nobody in that yard went to that counter with the bonesetter and nobody went with the money. Nessa Pike took it out of that shed's own book at about ten past two in the afternoon and walked up the front street with it in an envelope and put it on a counter, and that yard's own book has a line in it against the second of June which is the largest figure that has gone out of that book since the ninth day of April past, and which is a shade under twice what the same trade charged on the eighth day of May past for a hand, being two hundred and eight pence, and the yard entered that comparison because it is a figure and not because it is a feeling.

And in the shed that night, the holder of that writ put the whole of the price of the fortnight out loud, in about three people's hearing, and would not have it softened.

"**A party with no instrument may still be hired by a private person for a private purpose. I have said that in about nine people's hearing twice this month and I have never once had cause to regret saying it and I have got one today.**

"**On the twenty-eighth of May this yard took a mark and eightpence out of a bag that had nothing in it for three weeks, and it was not salvage and not a claim and not a claim window and not a charter, and every person in that boat was told all five of those things before the painter went. And on the second of June this yard paid a bonesetter two marks, seven shillings and fourpence out of its own book for a man it hired. And between the first of those two days a man of about thirty-six paid this yard a mark and eightpence for nine tons of salt inside our own line, which is the same work.**

"**So that is the price. It is one mark and eightpence. And a yard that has three marks standing to a man of Ashtide since the eighth of August past, and seven pence standing to its own shipwright, and eight marks and a day of a boat standing to that harbor's own bellwright, has gone and been the thing that moves things inside a blockade, and the answer is that it is exactly like the other things, and I am not going to stand in that shed and make it into anything else. A man in a boat this yard has paid is a man this yard can be asked about. That was true before we took the money. It is not more true because we have now been to a bonesetter. It is exactly as true and it is four hundred and eight pence more expensive.**"

"**Then is it worth it,**" said the woman of no office, from the bench, in her own words and not anybody's.

"**I am not going to answer that,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to refuse it, and I want that written down as two things and not one, and I want the reason for both of them written down as the same reason, which is that I have got a bag with twenty pence in it and a man with a foot and a book with four columns in it, and the only one of those three I know anything about is the book.**"

---

And that is what came out of it, and the only thing that was asked at that counter over a chandler's in the whole of that afternoon, and it was asked once, and it was asked at about half past three in the afternoon in about nine people's hearing, and she did not ask it a second time, and neither of them said why.

"**Where did the money come from,**" said the woman of no office.

"**Two private hires inside the harbor line on the twenty-eighth of May and the thirty-first of May, a mark and eightpence each, from a boat's owner of about fifty-five and a man of about thirty-six of the third lay, on their own accounts and for their own purposes, at a box on the corner of that mole, and neither of them was salvage and neither of them was a claim and neither of them was a charter and neither of them was a way of getting past anything, and both men in the boats were told all five of those things before the painter went and not after.**

"**And a man of about forty-one called Cobb Terrell was hurt in the first of those two boats on the Friday, in about nine feet of water off the west quay, on a job that was somebody else's, and the bill came to four hundred and eight pence and has been paid out of that book and there is twenty pence left in the bag. I am telling you all of it in one go because you asked once and I would rather you had all of it than the version I would have told you on Monday.**"

"**All right,**" she said.

And she asked nothing else that afternoon, and the ship's hand entered that, and entered that it was the first time in eleven days that a person had stood in that front street and heard money accounted for and then said one word, and that this yard has a rule about saying everything and has never had a rule about somebody saying nothing, and is not going to get one now.

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the second day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a bonesetter of that harbor, about sixty, in that trade thirty-one years, set the left foot of a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay in a surgery room over a baker's on Saturday the twenty-ninth day of May past and gave the figure of two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, being four hundred and eight pence, and said that it would be neither more nor less; and that the said figure was paid out of that yard's own book at a counter over a chandler's on the front street of that harbor at about ten past two in the afternoon of the said day, and that the said figure is the largest that has gone out of that book since the ninth day of April past and is a shade under twice the figure of the said trade for a right hand on the eighth day of May past, being two hundred and eight pence.*

*And that a man of sixty-one, who built the boat of about nineteen tons that is in eleven pieces on the crown of that mole, was on the crown of that mole at about half past eight in the morning of the said day with a right hand bandaged and a boat's hook in his left, and that he was not sent for and was not met and was not asked anything, and that he worked; and that no person of that yard went over to him at any hour of the said day and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift and neither brother has said why.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the said bonesetter said out loud at a counter over a chandler's in about nine people's hearing that he does not want to know the trade's business and does not want to know how the said man came to be in that boat, and that nobody in that harbor asked him and is not going to ask him; and that he said out loud, unasked, that the said man of about forty-one came to him on the Saturday last on a crutch and had paid him nothing and had told him the whole of it, and that a man who is angry at a foot is a man who has not yet worked out what else he is angry at, and that the foot will be mended long after the other thing has finished with him.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said evening that a party with no instrument may still be hired by a private person for a private purpose; that on the twenty-eighth day of May past that yard took a mark and eightpence out of a bag that had nothing in it for three weeks; and that on the second day of June that yard paid a bonesetter two marks, seven shillings and fourpence for a man it had hired; and that the price of the whole fortnight is one mark and eightpence, and that a yard that has three marks standing to a man of Ashtide since the eighth of August past, and seven pence standing to its own shipwright, and eight marks and a day of a boat standing to that harbor's own bellwright, has gone and been the thing that moves things inside a blockade and the answer is that it is exactly like the other things and is not going to be made into anything else; and that a man in a boat that yard has paid is a man that yard can be asked about, and that that was true before the money was taken and is exactly as true after a bonesetter and is four hundred and eight pence more expensive.*

*And that a woman of no office asked one question at that front street at about half past three in the afternoon of the said day in about nine people's hearing, being where the money came from; and that the holder of that writ answered it in full, including the said man's name, the day, the figure and the fact that the bag has twenty pence in it; and that she said two words and asked nothing else that afternoon; and that the ship's hand entered that it was the first time in eleven days that a person had stood in that street and heard money accounted for and then said one word, and that this yard has a rule about saying everything and has never had a rule about somebody saying nothing and is not going to get one now.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that a bill paid is not a bill discharged, and that four hundred and eight pence is a figure and not an apology; that an absence is not a stage and a man who is not to put weight on a foot for ten days is not a casualty and is not a martyr and has not been thanked and has not asked to be; and that a yard which has taken money and been to a bonesetter is not a different kind of yard and is not going to be described as one by anybody in this harbor.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and two marks, seven shillings and fourpence went out of it, being four hundred and eight pence, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what has come into that yard since the fifth day of May past is three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, and the whole of what has gone out since the nineteenth day of May past is five hundred and seventy-seven pence, being the hundred and sixty-nine of a boat and a penny of line on that Wednesday and the four hundred and eight of a bonesetter on the second of June, and twenty pence buys five candles at fourpence. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are about three hundred and fifty-six days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twenty-second night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of sixty-one is back on the water with a bandaged right hand and was not thanked and is not going to be, and he is not this yard's injury and is not going to be made a door.**

**And a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay was hired on Friday the twenty-eighth of May for a mark and eightpence, was hurt in that boat on that Friday, said his figure out loud twice in public of his own accord, and was paid by this yard on Wednesday the second of June out of that yard's own book. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
