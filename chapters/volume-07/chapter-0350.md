# Chapter 350

*Sunday the Fourth of July, the Twentieth Day of the Ninety, a Leaf Read Back in Full With the Count on the Face of It and the Definition of Every Figure on the Face of It, a Mole Head at Ten Past Five in the Evening With a Light Not Lit and a Man of Thirty-Four on a Step and a Man of Sixty-One on a Bench With a Shoe Beside Him That Is Not His, the Whole Standing Inventory Once and Nowhere Else, and a Figure That Is Short of a Wage of Four Marks and Is Not Going to Be Paid*

Sunday the fourth of July, in that shed at about four in the afternoon, and on that mole at about ten past five in the evening.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, so that bag cannot buy a day.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and on all ten of these ten days, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of any of the ten, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on all ten of the ten days and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four on all ten of the ten, being sixty-one, bandaged in the right hand, with a boat's hook in his left hand and eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked and not going to be, not sent for, not asked, not a teacher, not a door and not a sacrifice. HE IS NOT ALDEN MARR, AND NEITHER BROTHER WENT NEAR THE OTHER ON ANY HOUR OF ANY OF THE TEN DAYS.**

**And this yard's own count of the three standing figures, made in that shed on the said day and not taken out of any other book: in these ten days the wall came down bare once, being the sixtieth on Monday the twenty-eighth, and the next is the sixty-first on Monday the fifth of July next, which is outside these ten; the cistern was charged twice and walked on none, being the fifty-ninth on Monday the twenty-eighth and the sixtieth on Thursday the first of July; and one wage Tuesday fell, being Tuesday the twenty-ninth for the thirty-sixth week, and the column did not move on it, so that thirty-six weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.**

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and the woman of no office was on the bench and had come up the hill for the reading and for nothing else.

**The first column of that leaf has eighty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back, and eighty-three at the close.** There were seventy-three of them at the close of Thursday the twenty-fourth day of June past and nine have gone in since, one for every day from Friday the twenty-fifth to Saturday the third of July, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own entry is the tenth and makes eighty-three.

**The second column has two hundred and eighty-seven lines in it at the hour of the read-back and two hundred and eighty-nine at the close.** Two hundred and forty-six of them were in it at the close of the twenty-fourth day of June past, and three went in on the twenty-fifth, three on the twenty-sixth, three on the twenty-seventh, five on the twenty-eighth, three on the twenty-ninth before the reading of that day, one on the twenty-ninth after it, three on the thirtieth before the reading of that day, two on the thirtieth after it, three on the first of July, two on the first of July after it, three on the second, two on the second after it, three on the third and two on the third after it, and three on the fourth before this reading, and two hundred and eighty-seven at the hour of this reading, and two this afternoon after it, and two hundred and eighty-nine at the close. **The said figure is that book of that yard's own and is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's eighty-two and the fourth column's seventy-four, at the hour of this read-back, are counts of entries and of lines, and a day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count both of them. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments.**

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The four lines above his named line are four, nameless, and are not struck out, and they are in about sixty boats on that coast. He was not on the stone on any of these ten days and was put on nothing, and nobody asked him one word about the three nots standing against the eighth of June, and they were not asked about on that day or after it.**

**And the fourth column has seventy-four lines in it at the hour of the read-back and seventy-five at the close, and the seventy-fifth is the last thing in that book, and the column is not closed and nobody has closed it and nobody in this yard is going to.**

Then the three of them said what the ten days were, in about three lines each, and none of it was argued with and none of it has been improved on since.

**The first was about the water, and the ship's hand said it.**

"**On Friday the twenty-fifth of June past a man of no standing whatever asked eleven men one question in eleven places and ten said yes, and on the night of the twenty-sixth ten boats went out past the mole head and past a line described in the fifth clause of a notice without a declaration, which is a breach, and there was no vessel of this yard's on that water and this yard paid no penny of it and had no instrument and did not advise it. They came in past the outer marks at about half past five on the Sunday morning under the sixth clause, which says that this notice is not a closure of the port, and that clause has been on a market wall in this harbor since the seventeenth day of May past and has not been taken down. And a man of thirty-eight in a boat of about thirteen tons found two foot more water on a remembered line than the oldest page of five books carries, and went on, and I cannot check it and neither can anybody in this town, and it is in the second column with a reason against the line, and the reason is that a stranger with a lead would have had to be on that water on that night.**"

**The second was about the harbor, and the woman of no office said it from the bench, and she was not asked for it.**

"**Four boxes of physic were given away in that market before eleven o'clock on the Monday morning and about four tons of flour is in a shed on that quay with no name on a slate, and there is about eleven tons of oil in a lighter a ton light, and three women of about sixty-two and two of her sisters came in off that boat off a crossing they had been held on the other side of for eleven days going to a burial. And none of the nine figures on that board has moved and one of them has two figures and two dates on it and the February one is left visible above the June one, and that is the same board and the same rule and the rule is that a woman who puts a figure on a board cannot take it off again. And a man gave up a road of about eleven tons in that market on the Friday and has had nothing since. And a ship's bench had nothing on it for two days and an apprentice of fourteen was at home for two days. And a man of about fifty-one lost a berth and there is no figure in this harbor for what a berth is worth to a man of fifty-one and nobody invented one. And a wreck of about nineteen tons is on the foul off the south end of that mole going down by the head, and six men of that harbor are ashore with no boat, and there was no boat for them on the night of the twenty-sixth either, and that is the choice and it was said out loud on the floor of that market on the Tuesday in about forty people's hearing and was not improved on by anybody and he was comforted by nobody for a minute and three-quarters.**"

**The third was about this yard, and the holder of that writ said it, and he did not look at anybody while he said it.**

"**There is nothing in that bag and there has been nothing in it since the twentieth pence went out of it on the nineteenth day of June past, and in ten days nothing came in and nothing went out and there was a night this month when this yard worked a stage on a remembered water with a crew at a cost, and the cost was that the pattern is of a coast that has moved and cannot be commanded, and it is a trade and not a power, and it stopped at four o'clock in the morning because a herring skipper of fifty-one said so, and a page of forty lines with a name and an hour and a price and a payer against every one of them is on a market wall in a town four days up that coast, and this yard did not write to that town and does not know who put it up and is not going to write and find out. And on Thursday the first of July I lost a day, and the reason is against the line and there is no name in it and there is not going to be one, and a lost day carries no figure because I have no figure for what a day of mine is worth and I have not invented one. And none of it was signed and none of it was thanked and not one person in that harbor got a penny out of it, and I would like all three of those sentences read out at the end of this month by somebody who was not me.**"

And then at about ten past five in the evening he went up to the mole head, because that is where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it is out on the fourth of July as it was on the fourteenth of May, and a light costs oil, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted to a woman on the first day of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and on the February figure that light was out in October.

On the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins, the two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty-eight days old, and one of them has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and it has not been lashed back, and neither has moved, and nobody has asked whose either is.

Tobias Wren was on that crown at about ten past five with a boat's hook in his left hand and eleven pieces of a boat behind him and his right hand bandaged, and he did not look up at the mole head and nobody went over to him. A man of sixty-one was on that crown from ten until four on every one of these ten days. He is not that man.

Across on the far bench a man of sixty-one was not there, because it was after four, and had been there from ten until four on every one of these ten days, and there was one shoe on that bench beside him and the shoe is not his, and it has been on that bench since the third of May past and nobody has moved it and nobody has asked.

The mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it was not looked at on any of the ten days, and the mark four hundred yards off it is of a make nobody in this harbor makes, and the season of both is nine marks and neither has been paid and neither is going to be, and the use of the first was taken once on the eighteenth day of June past and closed, four figures and no fifth at any hour of any day, ladder in another man's book, nobody to explain it, not Fisk.

And at about ten past five in the evening a man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait came up the steps of that mole from the packet's own landing place and stopped on the third step from the bottom and stood there for about a minute, and he had a line in his hand that is not his, being a copy of the fifth clause of a notice made out in his own hand on the twenty-seventh of May past, and he did not go up to the crown and he did not go into the shed and he did not ask anybody anything, and he has not been asked one word about the line and is not going to be, and he is a man who works for nobody and is not a courier and is not a servant and is not a face of anything, and he went back down the steps at about a quarter past five and got into his packet and nobody watched him go and he was not thanked and he will not be.
