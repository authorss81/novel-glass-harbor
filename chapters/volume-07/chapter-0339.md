# Chapter 339

*Wednesday the Twenty-Third of June, About Forty People in That Fish Market, a Launch Book Read Aloud on a Table by Its Owners, and a Man on His Own Feet on the Floor of That Market Saying Out Loud What He Did on the Eighteenth of June and What It Cost on the Nineteenth*

Wednesday the twenty-third of June, in the fish market in the morning, and in the shed on the mole at about four in the afternoon.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday, and the fifty-eighth charge falls on the said day after this one. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark. Thirty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and the column has not moved on either of the two wage Tuesdays that have fallen in these ten days.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that market at about eleven in the morning of the said day and said nothing and was not comforted by anybody afterwards and did not ask anybody for anything. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four with a boat's hook in his left hand and his right hand bandaged, and he was not in that market at any hour of the said day, and nobody in that yard sent for him, and about forty people were in that market and he was not one of them and he is the man that yard most wants in a market and did not go and get him.**

---

There were about forty people in that fish market at about eleven on the twenty-third of June, which is what a market holds on a fair Wednesday, and a man of about thirty-eight had said at the end stall at about half past ten that morning that anybody who wanted to hear the book of that boat of about nineteen tons could come and hear it off the owner's own table, and the end stall gave him the table, and the word went round the front street the way words go, and about forty people came.

The table was the end stall's table, and the end stall is the woman of about fifty-eight's, and she gave it and she said yes, and nobody thanked her, and she is not owed anything by anybody in that market.

The launch book was laid open on it by the man of about forty-four of the six, and nobody touched it, and the ship's hand did not touch it, and the holder of that writ did not touch it, and it was read out loud by the man of about fifty-four of the six, whose eyes are not what they were, and he read it slowly and he read the two lines and the six names and he read nothing else.

"**The eighteenth day of June. Figures off the second seam, given to us on the quay at half past two. Nine foot on the bar and eight and a half off the foul at four o'clock this afternoon. Halloran, Teel, Sowerby, the two Cranes, and Meikle.**"

And the second line, which is the seventh line, and which is under the nineteenth of June.

"**The nineteenth day of June. Out at half past five by the flood. On the foul off the south end at twenty to six. Eleven foot. We were all off her.**"

About forty people heard that in a market in the open, and a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay was in that market about ten feet from that table and said nothing and was not asked, and a man of about fifty-five with a line about the moulds of a boat on that hard was in that market about fifteen feet from that table and said nothing and was not asked, and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a chart in a bag was in that market with his bag shut on his shoulder and said nothing and was not asked, and a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime was in that market and had nothing to say and said it.

Then Calder Marr walked out from the end of the aisle and stood on the floor of that market, on his own feet, in about forty people's hearing, and said the following, and he said it once, and he did not say it again on that day or after it, and nobody in that market asked him a question about it or improved on it.

"**On the eighteenth day of June this yard put the mark on the face of the second seam to a use it has never been put to on this coast. It was done off four figures out of a book that belongs to a man of this harbor who has never given his name to anybody and did not give it then, and I did not ask him one word and I am not going to. I took that height off that fouling with a lead and a line between twenty to seven in the morning and twenty past two in the afternoon and gave it to six men on that quay at half past two, and it went into a book of their own and not into a book of mine, and I have had no figure out of that book since and I have closed the use and I will not open it again.**"

"**On the nineteenth day of June at twenty minutes to six in the afternoon a boat of about nineteen tons with six men in her was found on the foul off the south end of this mole in eleven foot of water with a hole in her, and all six of that crew are ashore, and one man of about forty of that crew is hurt in the water and paid a bonesetter sixteen pence out of his own hand in small coin and was not paid by this yard, which had nothing to pay it with. That boat is on the bottom and is going down by the head, and there is no instrument in this country by which a party of this yard's standing could lay a claim on her, and this yard has none and is not going to acquire one and is not going to be the reason one is wanted.**"

"**The figure I gave them was right. The boat was on the ground. I have not said what the fouling of that mark means because nobody in this harbor knows and I am not going to stand in this market and guess at it, and I am not going to say that mark was wrong, and I am not going to say it was right. I asked for the morning. It is my morning. There is a wreck off that mole and a man with a hurt arm and a harbor that is poorer this morning than it was a fortnight ago, and I am the man who asked for it, and I am standing here on my own feet, and that is the whole of it.**"

He stopped. He did not add anything, and he did not look for anybody's face, and about forty people in a fish market in the open on the twenty-third day of June stood there and about a third of them said nothing and the rest said a great deal, and none of it was to him.

He was not comforted by anybody in that market, and nobody tried, and after it there was nothing in that market for about a minute and a half in which anybody said anything at all to him.

---

The first thing said was not said to him.

A man of about fifty-one of the six, who owns that boat with the other five and who was standing at the end of that table, said out loud, in about forty people's hearing, to the table and not to the man who had been standing on the floor:

"**That was not nothing that was done,**" he said, "**and I am not going to be a man in a market who lets a thing get said that way on a morning. Six of us had a figure we could check with a lead and a clock. That is six men who are not guessing. It cost us a boat and it cost one of us an arm. I am not going to pretend those are the same thing and I am not going to pretend the first one is worth what it cost, because I do not know, and I have been on this water thirty-four years and I have never known how to price a thing like that and I am not going to learn it in a fish market.**"

"**A man of about thirty-eight,**" said the woman of about fifty-eight, over the shoulder of her slate, "**said two days ago in this market that there is no boat, and he was right, and there is no boat this morning, and this morning is not a different morning because somebody stood up.**"

And then a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay, who had not been asked anything and who had said nothing since the seventeenth, said eight words, across ten feet of floor, to the man who had been standing on that floor, and nobody heard them at first, and about four people heard them afterwards.

"**You should not have done that for us.**"

"**No,**" said the man of about thirty-four, and that was all, and he put his hand on the end stall's table for a moment the way a man does, and went out of that market and up the front street, and nobody followed him, and he has not been asked one word since and is not going to be.

---

In that shed at about four in the afternoon the leaf was read back in full, all four columns including the empty ones, in the ship's own left hand, in the presence of the holder of that writ, and it was read in front of the woman of no office, who asked for nothing and was asked nothing and was not comforted and did not try.

"**The first column has seventy-one entries in it at the hour of this reading,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and that is a count of entries and a stranger with a pencil can count it. The second column has two hundred and forty lines in it at the hour of this reading, and that is this book of ours and is not a count of anybody's paragraphs. The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not mine, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said in this harbor. And the fourth column has sixty-three lines in it at the hour of this reading, and that is a count of lines and a stranger can count that one too, and it will be sixty-four when this afternoon's line goes in and sixty-five on Thursday, and the sixty-fifth will be the last thing in that book.**"

She read the third column as she has read it at the close of every week since the sixteenth of February past, in full, and the hand at the head of it kept its place and nobody asked the boy one word about the three nots standing against the eighth of June, because he was not on the stone and was put on nothing and was not in that shed.

Then the ship's hand put the pencil down and said the thing that was left.

"**I am the one who counts in this yard and I have counted since the sixteenth of February past, and I have got a column for what a thing is worth when a man can check it and I have got a column for what a thing is worth when he cannot, and I have not got a column for this morning, and I am not going to make one, and I have wanted to make one every hour since eleven o'clock and I am not going to.**"

"**Then there are three of us,**" said the woman of no office, "**who have been in a room with a thing in it today and none of us is going to say anything about it, and I would like that entered, and I am not going to enter it because I do not write in that book, and somebody else will.**"

"**I will enter it,**" said Nessa Pike.

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-third day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about forty people's hearing and not in a room, a launch book belonging to the six owners of a boat of about nineteen tons was laid open on the end stall's table by one of its owners and read out loud by another of them, being the two lines for the eighteenth and nineteenth days of June and the six names of that crew, and that nobody touched that book but its owners, and that the holder of that writ and the ship's hand did not touch it and did not ask for it; and that the holder of that writ then stood on the floor of that market on his own feet and said out loud, once, and was not asked a question about it and it was not improved on by anybody: that on the eighteenth day of June he put the mark on the face of the second seam to a use it has never been put to on this coast, off four figures out of a book belonging to a man of this harbor who has never given his name to anybody, that he took that height off that fouling with a lead and a line between twenty to seven in the morning and twenty past two in the afternoon and gave it to six men on a quay at half past two and into a book of their own and not his own, and that he has had no figure out of that book since and has closed the use; and that on the nineteenth day of June at twenty minutes to six in the afternoon a boat of about nineteen tons with six men in her was found on the foul off the south end in eleven foot of water with a hole in her, all six of that crew being ashore, and one man of about forty of that crew being hurt in the water and having paid a bonesetter sixteen pence out of his own hand; and that that boat is on the bottom and there is no instrument in this country by which a party of that yard's standing could lay a claim on her; and that the figure was right and the boat was on the ground; and that nobody in that harbor knows what the fouling of that mark means and he is not going to guess at it and did not say that mark was wrong and did not say it was right; and that he asked for the morning, and it is his morning, and there is a wreck off that mole and a man with a hurt arm and a harbor poorer than a fortnight ago, and he said so on his own feet and stopped there.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that nobody in that hearing comforted the holder of that writ and nobody tried, and that about a minute and a half passed in which anybody said anything to him; and that a man of about fifty-one of the six said out loud, in about forty people's hearing and not to the man who had spoken, that the six men had a figure they could check with a lead and a clock, and that it cost them a boat and cost one of them an arm, and that he is not going to pretend the first is worth what it cost because he does not know and has never known how to price such a thing; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said that this morning is not a different morning because somebody stood up; and that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay said eight words across ten feet of floor to the man who had been standing on that floor in about four people's hearing, being that he should not have done that for them, and that the other man said one word, being no, and went up the front street, and was not asked one word then or since.*

*And that the leaf of the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about four in the afternoon of the said day, all four columns including the empty ones, in the ship's own left hand, in the presence of the holder of that writ and of a woman of no office; and that the figures at the hour of that reading are seventy-one in the first column, being a count of entries and checkable; two hundred and forty in the second, being that book of that yard's own and not a count of paragraphs; none in the third; and sixty-three in the fourth, being a count of lines and checkable; and that the sixty-fifth will be the last thing in that book and will be entered on the twenty-fourth day of June next; and that the ship's hand said out loud that she has a column for what a thing is worth when a man can check it and a column for what a thing is worth when he cannot and has not got a column for the morning of the twenty-third and is not going to make one, and that she has wanted to make one every hour since eleven o'clock and is not going to; and that three persons were in a room with a thing in it on the said day and none of them is going to say anything about it, and that one of them entered it and the other two did not, and that neither of those two writes in that book and will not.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that a thing said on a man's feet on the floor of a market in about forty people's hearing is not a confession and is not a plea and is not a sentence of any court and is not a thing anybody can be punished for and is not the end of anything; that a man not being comforted after it is not a man being shunned and is not a man being avoided; and that eight words said across ten feet of floor to a man who gave you a thing you did not ask for are not a reproach and not a forgiveness and are not going to be improved on by anybody in this harbor.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and the fifty-eighth charge falls on the twenty-fourth. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy-seven days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-third night, and neither of them has moved. The leaf was read back in full at about four in the afternoon and stands at seventy-one, two hundred and forty, none, sixty-three at the hour of the reading, and the third column is empty and unappointed and the hand at the head of it is where it was. A man stood on the floor of a fish market this morning on his own feet and said a thing out loud and was not comforted by anybody and has not been improved on by anybody in this yard and is not going to be. A wreck of about nineteen tons is on the foul off the south end going down by the head, and six men of that harbor have no boat, and a man of about forty has a hurt arm and sixteen pence of a bill paid out of his own hand, and a harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast and this is the ninth day of the ninety.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
