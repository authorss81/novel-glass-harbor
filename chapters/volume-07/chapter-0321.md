# Chapter 321

*Saturday the Fifth of June, a Bag With Twenty Pence in It, a Foot That Is Not to Carry Weight, Four Old Books in a Drawer in That Shed, and the Soundings Named as of a Coast That Has Moved*

Saturday the fifth of June, in the shed on the mole in the morning, and at the foot of the slip in the afternoon, and in that shed again in the evening.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence. A man of about forty-one of the second lay, hurt on the twenty-eighth of May past, is not to put weight on the left foot for two more days and is not to be on the water for six more. The far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it.**

**And the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn is in that harbor and is going nowhere on any day of these ten, and is of no office, and is not on the crew, and her forty marks stand first after food and are not reduced and are not paid.**

---

Cobb Terrell came down to the shed on the fifth of June on a crutch with a sailcloth tied over it, because the bonesetter had told him to keep the foot up and he had kept it up for eight days and was tired of the room over the baker's, and he sat on the bench by the door and put the foot out in front of him on an upturned bucket and did not put weight on it.

He is about forty-one, of that harbor's own second lay, a hired hand, and he gave his name out loud on the twenty-eighth of May and it went in, and he said his own figure out loud twice of his own accord, being two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and it was paid out of that yard's own book on the second of June at a counter over a chandler's.

"You are not to put weight on that foot for ten days," said Nessa Pike, standing in the doorway with the small book in her hand. "That is what the man said, and today is the eighth day."

"I know what day it is," said Cobb Terrell. "I have been counting them on the ceiling. Two more days of sitting still and then I can stand on it, and six more days after that before I can go in a boat. Fourteen off the water. I have got that too."

"You came down the hill on a crutch," said Calder Marr.

"I came down to look at something that is not a ceiling," said Cobb Terrell. "I am not asking for work and I am not asking for money. I am telling this shed that the foot is mending and that the rest of me is idle, and that a man who is idle in June hears things."

"What things," said Nessa Pike.

"That a man of about thirty-one with a coaster out of Ashtide has a Crown chart in a bag," said Cobb Terrell, "and that he was refused a question by this yard in the middle of March, and that he has asked nothing since. That is all I have heard. I heard it from a man on the west quay yesterday who was mending a warp. I am not carrying it anywhere. I am putting it on that bench because it was put on me."

Calder Marr wrote nothing while the man was speaking, and afterwards he wrote one line in the second column in his own right hand, and the line was that a man of about forty-one who is not to put weight on the left foot for ten days and is not to be on the water for fourteen came down to that shed of his own accord on the fifth of June and sat on the bench and said what he had heard and asked for nothing.

---

In the afternoon Calder Marr opened the drawer in that shed where the old vessel books are kept, because the drawer had stuck in the damp and Nessa Pike had put her shoulder to it and it had come open all at once, and there were four of them in it under a folded sailcloth.

They are not this yard's books. They are the books of vessels this yard has broken up or bought for spars or taken for a debt since the first year it had a shed, and they came with the boats and stayed when the boats went, and nobody has opened any of them in years.

The first is the book of the *Morrow Lass*, kept by a master called Hendry Cott, in a round hand, with the dates at the head of every page. The second is the book of the *Gull's Rest*, kept by two men, the first half in a tall hand and the second in a short one, with no dates on three pages out of four. The third is the book of a lighter called the *Tern's Debt*, kept in pencil, half of it rubbed out and written over. The fourth is a stitched book with no vessel's name on the cover and the name *Sella's boat* on the first page in a hand that is not Sella Brigg's, and the writing in it is in three hands.

Nessa Pike carried them to the bench one at a time and laid them out and did not open any of them past the first page, because a book a man has not been asked to open is a book he has not opened.

"There is a storm route across Bramble Strait in some of these," said Calder Marr. "That is what an old man on the west quay told me in April, and I told him I did not trade in remembered weather, and he said that was my business. I am not opening any of them today. I am telling this shed that they are here, and that they are four, and that they are old, and that no current chart this harbor carries has the route in it."

"Then why open the drawer," said Nessa Pike.

"Because the drawer stuck," said Calder Marr, "and because a drawer that sticks in June will stick in October, and because I want it written down that there are four books in it before anybody in this harbor tells me what is in them."

She wrote that down in the second column, with the four names and the four hands against them, and no figure against any of them.

---

At about six in the evening a man of about thirty-one went past the foot of the slip with a bag over his shoulder, going down toward the hard, and he did not look up the hill and nobody on the hill called down to him.

He has a coaster out of Ashtide. He was refused a question by this yard in the middle of March, in about nine people's hearing, and he has asked nothing since, and he is a person of no office, and he is not a witness and has not been made one, and this yard stood in a market on the twenty-third of May for about four minutes on purpose and did not ask him one question.

"He is going down to his boat," said the woman of no office from the bench, where she was mending a cuff with a needle. "He goes down every evening and comes back every morning, and he has had that bag since March, and nobody in this street has asked him what is in it since March."

"We are not going to ask him either," said Calder Marr. "A man with a chart in a bag is a man with a bag. If he opens it, he opens it. If he does not, it is a bag."

"Then why watch him go past," said the woman of no office.

"Because he went past," said Calder Marr, "and because a man this yard refused in March going past in June is a thing a shed writes down, and for no other reason."

That night in the shed, the holder of that writ said one thing out loud that went in the first column before anything else goes in it, and he said it because a price has to be named before anybody agrees to anything, and nobody has agreed to anything.

"The soundings on any old route across that strait are of a coast that has moved," he said. "I am putting that in the first column tonight, before any book is opened and before any figure is read out loud anywhere. It is not a figure anybody in this harbor may measure out, and it is not a defect to be fixed, and no leaf of this yard's book may approach a figure for how far. It is the price, and it goes in before the first use and not after it."

"Write that as what it is," said Nessa Pike.

"Write it as what it is," said Calder Marr, "and put the fifth of June against it, and no name in it."

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the fifth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that four old vessel books are in the drawer of that shed under a folded sailcloth, being the book of the Morrow Lass in a round hand, the book of the Gull's Rest in two hands, the book of a lighter called the Tern's Debt in pencil, and a stitched book with no vessel's name on the cover, in three hands, and that none of them has been opened past the first page on the said day; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay with a coaster out of Ashtide and a Crown chart in a bag went past the foot of that slip on the said evening going down toward the hard, and was not asked one question and was not followed.*

*And that the soundings on any old route across Bramble Strait that may stand in any such books are of a coast that has moved, and that this is not a figure anybody of that yard may measure out and is not a defect to be fixed, and that no figure for how far it has moved may be approached, and that this is the price and goes in the first column before any first use and not after it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay, named Cobb Terrell, hurt on the twenty-eighth day of May past, not to put weight on the left foot for ten days and not to be on the water for fourteen, came down to that shed of his own accord on the said day on a crutch and sat on the bench and said what he had heard on the west quay and asked for nothing; and that the holder of that writ wrote one line against it in his own right hand and slowly.*

*And that four old vessel books were found in a drawer that had stuck in the damp, and were carried to the bench one at a time and laid out and not opened past the first page; and that this yard did not go back to the copyist's counter in the room off the fish market on the said day, and did not send anybody there, and did not ask the woman at it anything; and that a man of about thirty-one with a bag went past that slip and was watched going past and for no other reason.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that four old books in a drawer are not a route and no figure in any of them has been read out loud anywhere; that a bag going past a slip is not a chart opened and a man refused in March is not a man asked in June; and that a coast that has moved is not a thing anybody in this harbor can measure out on a stone and call it mended.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty-nine days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twenty-fifth night, and neither of them has moved. Cobb Terrell went back up the hill on that crutch at about seven and told the room over the baker's that a foot is a thing a man can count, and nobody thanked him and he did not ask to be thanked, and a man of sixty-one with a bandaged right hand worked on that crown and was not thanked and is not going to be.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
