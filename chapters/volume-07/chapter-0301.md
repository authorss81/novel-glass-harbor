# Chapter 301

*Saturday the Fifteenth of May, a Crate on the Mole Steps for a Fourth Day, the Last of Six Hundred and Sixty Pence Going into One Front, and a Tender of About Sixty Tons Lying Off the South End From Ten at Night Until First Light*

Saturday the fifteenth of May, in the shed on the mole, on the mole steps, and in a street four streets off the front.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into that yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it. The far bench was not occupied on the said Saturday at any hour of it, the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, a man of sixty-one at that bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift.**

---

The crate was on the mole steps for a fourth day and it was under a tarpaulin that had come off a handcart on the eleventh, and about nine people a day went past it on their way to the hard and to the water and to the slip, and on Saturday the fifteenth about nine went past it again and none of them stopped.

Nessa Pike took the tarpaulin off at about eight in the morning to look at it in daylight because a round of glass that has been under a wet cloth for four days has a wet cloth's opinion about it, and she looked at it for about two minutes and put the tarpaulin back.

"**It is going up this coast on Monday,**" she said. "**On the deck of a boat going up for coal, and it is a crate and it is a coal boat and that is all it is going to be.**"

"**Then it does not need looking at every morning.**"

"**It needs looking at every morning for the four days before it goes, and it is not going to get one after Monday.**"

That was the whole of the conversation and neither of them improved on it.

---

There was no work in the street four streets off the front and there had been none since the ninth of April, and the last money that had come into a trade on that street was six hundred and sixty pence, being five marks and fourpence, and it had gone into one front on the eighth of May past, and a front is four panes and a glazier is paid by the pane, and what a pane is in that street is that trade's figure and is not in any column of this yard's book and never has been.

The glazier was a man of about forty-four and he came down that street twice a day because there was nowhere else to be, and on Saturday morning he stood outside a house with a pane out of it and said the thing that was true, which is that a pane out of a front in May is not a job in May, because the household that owns a front and has a pane out of it has already had the man who does that, and the man who does that has already been paid, and the second visit is a courtesy and not a contract.

"**Two months,**" he said. "**Not a job in two months. And I have a boy of nineteen who I pay on Fridays out of what I get, and I pay him on Fridays, and I have paid him on every Friday since the sixth of March and I am not going to be the man who does not.**"

Nobody asked him anything and he said it to the street.

---

The man whose boat is on the hard came up the hill at about eleven in the morning, and he is the man of about fifty-five who stood on his own step in June past and said out loud that the next man who came down that street with a thing off a wreck would be able to say that this yard asks, and he has not been told anything since and this yard has not asked him for anything.

"**They have put a line on my boat,**" he said. "**Or they are going to. I want to know whether a man is coming to this slip on Monday morning.**"

"**I do not know,**" said Calder Marr.

"**You do not know.**"

"**I do not know, and I am not going to go and look, and I am going to tell you why I am not going to go and look, and then you can decide whether the reason is good enough for you.**"

The man waited. He was a man who had been answered with reasons before and had a fair idea of the price of one.

"**There are three of us in this yard and I have asked nobody in this harbor what any office is doing this week and I am not going to start on a Saturday. If I go down to that slip and stand there looking at your boat, then in about nine days somebody is going to be able to say that this yard was interested in your boat, and you are the man who said the thing on your own step, and that is how a trade gets to be a thing people can be asked about.**" He put his hand flat on the bench. "**That is the reason. It is not a good enough reason for you. It is the only one I have and it is the one that has kept nine people off this hill since the first of May.**"

"**Aye,**" said the man, and did not go, and stood there about a minute looking at the bench.

"**Say the other thing,**" said Calder Marr. "**The thing you have been chewing on since you came up. You have a copper pipe in a boat on a hard and the pipe is at Ternwatch and the nearest Ternwatch is a day's water, and a boat's man is four marks a day, and there is a line on your boat.**"

"**There is a line on my boat.**"

"**Then that is the thing to be chewing on and not me.**"

---

He came back at about half past four in the afternoon and he did not come up the hill, he called up it from the bottom of the steps, which he had not done before.

"**There has been one on her.**"

"**Who.**"

"**A man in a blue coat, about fifty, with a book, and he did not ask me one question, and he did not come up here, and he did not go along the hard, and he wrote in the book on the tail of my boat and he put a line round the ring bolt and he tied it and he went.**"

"**Did he touch anything else.**"

"**He touched the tiller and he looked at the gear and he looked at the wheelhouse door and he did not open it, and he looked at the water for about a minute with his face to it, and I do not know what a man does that for and I have been standing on this slip for twenty minutes trying to work out what a man does that for.**"

"**He was looking at how much water is under her,**" said Calder Marr. "**He was deciding whether he could put her somewhere and leave her.**"

The man did not say anything for a moment.

"**I have a boat on a hard with a line on her and a pipe four days away,**" he said, "**and I want to say to you that I have not slept since Tuesday and I have not told my wife and I am standing on a slip talking to a man who is not the harbor authority and has not been for two months.**"

"**I know.**"

"**Are you going to do anything.**"

"**No.**"

"**You are not going to do anything and you have told me so and you have not told me why and I noticed.**"

"**I have told you why once already this morning and it is the same why at four o'clock as it was at eleven, and if it is a better why now it is not a better why.**"

The man went down the steps. Halfway down he stopped and turned round.

"**There is a tender lying off your south end,**" he said, "**and I have seen her since about ten last night, and you are a fool if you have not.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the fifteenth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a crate stood on the mole steps on the said day and had stood there four days, since the twelfth, under a tarpaulin that came off a handcart on the eleventh, and that about nine people went past it on the said day on their way to the hard and to the water and to the slip and that none of them stopped; and that the tarpaulin was taken off at about eight in the morning and put back on at about eight and nothing was done to the round of glass inside it.*

*And that the man whose boat is on the hard of this harbor came up that hill at about eleven in the morning of the said day, and that at about half past four in the afternoon of the said day he did not come up that hill and called up it from the bottom of the steps, which he had not done before, and that at about half past four a man in a blue coat of about fifty with a book was on the said hard and put a line on the ring bolt of the said boat and tied it, and wrote in a book on the tail of her, and touched her tiller, and looked at her gear, and looked at the door of her wheelhouse and did not open it, and stood looking at the water for about a minute with his face to it, and did not ask the owner one question and did not go along the said hard and did not come up that hill; and that the owner of the said boat did not open the door of the said wheelhouse after the man had gone and did not go into her.*

*And that a tender of about sixty tons lay off the south end of that mole from about ten o'clock on Friday the fourteenth until first light on the said Saturday, and that nobody in that harbor went out to her and nobody came in from her and nothing was landed and nothing was taken aboard, and that no person in that yard entered her and no person in that yard was asked anything and that about nine people in that harbor stood on a mole and looked at her in the dark and did not go.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ did not go down to that slip on the said day and did not look at a boat, and that the reason against the line of the going is the reason given out loud on the first day of May past about a man this yard has paid being a man this yard can be asked about, and that the reason was given a second time on the said afternoon, in the same words and not a shorter way of putting it, and was not improved on; and that the owner of the said boat said on the said afternoon that the holder of that writ had told him he was not going to do anything and had not told him why and had noticed.*

*And that a man of about forty-four who glazes fronts of glass was not asked anything on the said day and said out loud on that street that he has paid a boy of nineteen on every Friday since the sixth day of March past and is not going to be the man who does not, and that this is a fact about a trade and not a claim against anybody and goes in no other column and carries no figure against this yard.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the twenty-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a line put on the ring bolt of a boat at a slip is not an order and is not a search and is not a person, and that a man looking at the water with his face to it for a minute is not a thief and is not a surveyor and is not anybody this yard can name; that a tender lying off a mole all night with a light burning and nobody going out to her is not an event and is not a non-event and is not entered as either; and that a party which has spent its whole working life on this coast refusing to be anybody's instrument is not thereby outside the only instrument anybody in this country has ever been frightened of, and that standing outside a thing is not the same thing as being safe from it, and this yard has not got as far as working out which of the two it is.*

---

**Two hundred and sixty-one pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fivepence, and the same figure it held on Friday the fourteenth and the figure it will hold until a boat is hired. Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and thirty-eight days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and fourth night, and neither of them has moved. A tender lay off the south end from ten at night until first light and nobody went out to her. A line is on the side of a boat on a hard. And there is nothing in that shed this evening that says so.**
