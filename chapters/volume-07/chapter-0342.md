# Chapter 342

*Saturday the Twenty-Sixth of June, a Refusal Entered First on a Sheet With the Man Who Said It Asking for His Own Name on It and the Hour on It, Three Rules Set Out Loud at the Head of That Sheet, Eleven Boats Off a Mole Head at Half Past Seven in the Evening Past a Line Described in a Notice, and Nobody Hailed*

Saturday the twenty-sixth of June, on the front street and in that shed, and off the head of that mole at about half past seven in the evening.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, bandaged in the right hand, hook in the left, eleven pieces of a boat behind him, not thanked, not sent for, not asked, and not one word of this night's work was said to him and none of it will be.**

---

The refusal went on first, at about ten in the morning, on the front street, in about nine people's hearing, at the asking of the man who had said it.

That was not the yard's idea and Calder Marr gave it away as fast as anybody asked for it.

"**I am not putting your no at the bottom of a sheet with nine yeses on above it,**" he said. "**If there is going to be a piece of paper in this country about Friday night then the first thing on it is a man of about fifty-five who said no to me in the street yesterday, in the same words he said it in May, and his name on it, and the hour he said it, and nobody's hand on it but the ship's.**"

Josiah Mear came out of the boatyard at about ten and looked at the sheet for a while and then said what he wanted said.

"**I want the hour on it, and I want the reason on it, and I want the reason to be the one I gave and not a shorter one, and I want my name at the top of it and not the bottom, because I said it first and I said it in the open.**"

Nessa Pike wrote what he said and read it back to him twice and he corrected one word, and the word is the word he spoke.

And a man of about forty-five who was standing there said that a refusal on a sheet was going to frighten the nine, and a man of about thirty-eight said no it was not, and Josiah Mear said himself that it was not and that he had thought about that and did not care.

"**You put my no first because it is the only one of the twelve in that street that nobody can undo,**" he said. "**A man can change his mind about going out. Nobody can change his mind about having been asked.**"

---

The rule for the night was set out loud in that shed at about eleven in the morning, to the ten who were going and to the man who had said no yesterday and who was standing at the back with his hands in his pockets, and it is three sentences and it is at the head of the sheet under the night:

*That a decision goes on this sheet at the moment a man says it and not afterwards; that a man may say a thing and take it back and both go on and the hour of each goes on and nobody is to be asked why he took it back; and that this sheet stops when somebody says it stops and not one line after.*

"**And if I want to add a line at seven o'clock in the morning,**" said Elias Voke, "**when I can see what came in?**

"**Then you add a line,**" said Calder Marr, "**with your name and the hour, and the sheet says that it stopped, and somebody with a pencil can count the lines and see that a line is on it that was not in the yard of the man who wrote it, and that is the whole point of the thing and I would rather a man added a line at seven in the morning than that any man of the eleven went out tonight without saying what he was doing.**"

Voke thought about that for a while and said it was a fair thing to want.

And then, because a sheet that lives in one shed is a sheet nobody can check, Nessa Pike took four more sheets out of the drawer in that shed — the drawer holds seven pieces of paper and the place of the charter is empty — and ruled four columns on the back of each of them, being the columns of the sheet itself, and put the four of them in a fold on the bench, and wrote on the outside of the fold, in her own left hand, the four places the four of them were going:

*One to a market wall in a town four days up that coast, to be put up by whoever puts it up and not by this yard, and this yard is not writing to that town and has not written to that town and is not going to. One to the end stall in the fish market, to the woman of about fifty-eight. One to a man of about thirty-one of the third lay who has a chart in a bag, being his own book and his own. One to a man of about fifty-one of that harbor who owns five sixths of a boat on the foul off the south end of that mole. This one is the yard's.*

"And the fifth is ours and it is going in a drawer with the charter's place empty and nobody is going to be able to get at it," she said, "and that is the one that will be lost, and I have written it down first so that it is the first thing anybody finds when somebody reads this in a year."

Nobody argued with her about that and Calder Marr said that she had put it in the right place and that he was not going to say so twice.

---

The boats were ready by about four in the afternoon and none of them was ready in the way a boat is ready for a fair crossing, and every man of the nine had it said out loud in about nine people's hearing that his boat was not fit and was going anyway.

*Anna Ray*, about fifty tons, third lay, nineteen winters in a list: her bottom was foul and had been since April and there was no time to clean it.
*Ashtide Rose*, about forty tons: nine years old and sound and carrying a full season's paint, and Iver Sallow said that out loud, that a man ought to know what he has got.
The coaster of about thirty tons: a stove that had been out of her wheelhouse on Sunday the twentieth and had been put back in on Monday and worked.
The boat of about forty-five tons: two men short of a crew of five and the two were a boy of seventeen and a man of sixty.
The boat of about thirty tons of the first lay: Alban Pree at the wheel, and he said he would take the wheel for four hours and that four hours is not a figure anybody in that harbor has got and that he was not going to invent one.
The packet *Mary Rend*: sound, and the only boat of them with a lead line of any kind, and a lamp, and Tam Rundle said he was going to put the lamp out at the north end of the bar and would not have it burning in a strait.
The lighter of about eleven tons: a lighter will not cross a bar at night, and Jens Ower said so, and said he was going to the north end of the bar and no further, and that is about two miles short of the strait and that is where he said he would be.
The boat of about twenty-six tons of the shipwright: sound, and Adam Slee said that the last thing he did before he let go was to look at his own bottom, being a shipwright, and that nobody was to laugh at him for it and nobody did.
The boat of about twenty-two tons: tied up at the head of the slip with a line doubled and the third turn on, and Hamo Birt had untied her at about four o'clock and no one had asked him to and nobody has asked him since.

And the ninth of the nine was Peder Lund's boat of about thirteen tons, and about a fifth of what went out by the numbers and not by the tonnage, and nobody in that harbor had a figure for what thirteen tons can do on a crossing of a strait in a sea, and Calder Marr said that out loud on the quay and it went in the second column with a reason against the line.

---

They made it off the mole head at about half past seven in the evening, on the flood, and there is no page in this harbor that says where any of them was afterwards except the sheet, and the sheet says what the men said and the hour they said it.

Hamo Birt came alongside the head of that mole at about eight o'clock in the evening with his boat's line already in his hand, and about nine people were on that mole and the far bench had been empty for about four hours by then and nobody went near it.

"**I am not going to say why,**" he said, to nobody in particular, and he was not a man who had said yes out loud in a market and he said it worse than that and better. "**Put eight o'clock on the Saturday against four o'clock on the Friday and let the sheet have both. I have read a thing in this shed this morning with three rules at the top of it and the middle one is a man may say a thing and take it back and both go on, and I have been a man who takes things back for thirty years and there has never once been anywhere to put it.**"

Nobody asked him. The sheet was in the ship's own left hand in a boat of about thirteen tons lying thirty yards off the head of that mole, and the holder of that writ was in that boat, and he wrote the line down on the sheet and read it out of it, and the ship's hand wrote it in at about a quarter past eight in the evening, and the reading of it went across thirty yards of water to a boat of about twenty-two tons and a man in it said that it was right. Both hours are on that sheet and the sheet does not choose between them, and the first rule at the head of it — that a decision goes on at the moment a man says it — was the first one used and it was used against the man who had already given the yard his no.

There was a boat of the closed-sea squadron lying at the outer marks with one man in her and a book open on the thwart in front of him, and he did not hail them, and none of the nine spoke to him, and Calder Marr was in the boat of about thirteen tons and saw him and looked at him for about four seconds and then looked away, and what he entered in the second column that night is that he was not hailed, and that this yard has no instrument and no power and has never had either, and that a man with a book who does not hail a boat is not the same thing as a man with a book who cannot, and that nobody in this yard knows which of the two that was and is not going to find out and did not ask him and is not going to.

He put that in with a reason against the line and no name against it, and the reason is the sentence itself and it is the second time in a month that this yard has written down a thing it cannot price.

---

And Nessa Pike went out on that boat of about thirteen tons because she is the ship's hand and because the stage that was going to be worked on the water is a trade and a trade is worked by somebody with a book, and she said so out loud on the quay before she went aboard, in about nine people's hearing, and she said the whole of it in a way nobody in that harbor had heard her say anything.

"**I am going because I am the one who counts in this yard and there is going to be something to count, and I am going in a boat that is not ours on a man's own hook, and my right hand has four stitches in it since the twentieth of February past and I write with the left and I am going to write with the left in a boat at night and about thirteen tons is the smallest thing I have ever been in. If it goes wrong then it goes wrong in a boat that belongs to a man of about thirty-eight and not in anything of this yard's, and if it goes right then the sheet is a sheet and not a instrument and I want that in your book and I want it in yours.**"

Calder Marr said that he was not going to argue it and that he was not going to thank her for it, and Mara Venn, who was standing on the quay and had not come up the hill, said the one thing she said all evening, and she said it to Nessa and not to him.

"**Write it out plain,**" she said, "**and do not let him have it out of you in his own words.**"

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-sixth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a refusal was entered first upon the said sheet on the said morning at about ten, in about nine people's hearing on the front street, at the asking of the man who had said it, being that of a boat of about twenty-four tons on a slip in that harbor; and that he asked that the hour be put against it and the reason be put against it in the words he had spoken and not a shorter one, and that his name stand at the head of it and not the foot, being that he said it first and said it in the open, and that the ship's hand wrote what he said and read it back to him twice and he corrected one word, and that the word is the word he spoke; and that the three rules of that sheet were then set out loud in that shed at about eleven in the morning to the ten men going and to the man who had said no on the Friday, being that a decision goes on it at the moment a man says it and not afterwards, that a man may say a thing and take it back and both go on with the hour of each, and that it stops when somebody says it stops and not one line after; and that four further sheets were then taken out of the drawer in that shed, which holds seven pieces of paper with the place of the charter empty, and ruled on the back of each of them the columns of the said sheet, and put in a fold on that bench with the four places written on the outside of it in the ship's own left hand, being a market wall in a town four days up that coast, to be put up by whoever puts it up and not by this yard, and this yard is not writing to that town and has not written to it and is not going to; and the end stall in the fish market; and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay who has a chart in a bag, being his own book and his own; and a man of about fifty-one of that harbor who owns five sixths of a boat on the foul off the south end of that mole; and that the fifth copy is the yard's and goes in a drawer with the charter's place empty where nobody is going to get at it, and that the ship's hand said the copy that will be lost is the one of this yard's and wrote it first so that it is the first thing anybody finds; and that at about four in the afternoon each of the nine said out loud in about nine people's hearing that his boat was not fit and was going anyway, with the fouling of the *Anna Ray* since April, a full season's paint in the *Ashtide Rose*, the stove out of a wheelhouse on the twentieth and back in on the twenty-first, two men short of a crew of five, a lamp in a packet that is to be put out at the north end of the bar, a lighter that will not cross a bar at night and will go to the north end of the bar and no further, a shipwright going to look at his own bottom, and a boat of about thirteen tons for which nobody in that harbor has a figure; and that at about eight o'clock in the evening of the said day a man of about fifty of that harbor, who had said yes at about eleven o'clock on the Friday and no at about four o'clock on the Friday and both hours being on the said sheet, came alongside the head of that mole in a boat of about twenty-two tons which was tied there with a line doubled and the third turn on, and said, in about nine people's hearing and to nobody in particular, that he was coming and that he was not going to say why and that the sheet had his no on it at four o'clock on the Friday and was welcome to put this beside it with eight o'clock on the Saturday against it, and that nobody asked him; and that eleven boats let go from the head of that mole at about half past seven in the evening on the flood and passed the mole head and the line described in the fifth clause of the notice without a declaration, which is a breach, and that no vessel of this yard's was among them and none is going out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a boat of the closed-sea squadron lay at the outer marks with one man in her and a book open on the thwart, and that he did not hail any of them, and that the holder of that writ was in the boat of about thirteen tons and looked at him for about four seconds and then looked away; and that what is entered against that line is that he was not hailed, and that this yard has no instrument and no power and has never had either, and that a man with a book who does not hail a boat is not the same thing as a man with a book who cannot, and that nobody in this yard knows which of those two it was and is not going to find out and did not ask him and is not going to; and that the reason against the line is the sentence itself, and that this is the second time in a month that this yard has entered a thing it cannot price.*

*And that the ship's hand went out on the boat of about thirteen tons because she is the ship's hand and the trade was to be worked on the water, and that she said out loud on that quay before she went aboard, in about nine people's hearing, that she was going because she is the one who counts in this yard and there would be something to count, and that she was going in a boat that is not this yard's on a man's own hook, and that her right hand has four stitches in it since the twentieth day of February past and she writes with the left, and that about thirteen tons is the smallest thing she has ever been in; and that if it goes wrong then it goes wrong in a boat that belongs to a man of about thirty-eight and not in anything of this yard's, and if it goes right then the sheet is a sheet and is not an instrument; and that the holder of that writ said he was not going to argue it and was not going to thank her for it; and that a woman of no office who was standing on that quay and had not come up the hill said the one thing she said all evening, to the ship's hand and not to him, being that she was to write it out plain and not to let him have it out of her in his own words.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the sixty-seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a refusal entered first on a sheet is not a veto and is not a casting of a vote against the rest and is not going to keep any man out of anything, being that a refusal stops one man and does not stop the sea; and that a sheet that stops when somebody says it stops is not a record that can be added to afterwards by a man who is not on it, and is not going to be found a month from now with a line on it that nobody can say who put there; and that eleven boats going out on a breach is not a fleet and not a blockade run in reverse and not a thing anybody in this harbor is authorised to do and nobody in this harbor is authorised to do it and eleven men have said so in their own mouths on the face of the sheet.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged, and the fifty-ninth charge falls on Monday the twenty-eighth. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day and went off the mole head at about half past seven in the evening in a boat that is not his. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and eighty days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and forty-sixth night, and neither of them has moved. A wreck of about nineteen tons lies on the foul off the south end going down by the head and six men of that harbor have no boat, and a man of about fifty-five of that harbor said no in the street yesterday in the same words he said it in May and his refusal is the first line on a sheet in a drawer in that shed with his name on it, and a man of about fifty with a paper on a table in an office untied his boat at four o'clock this afternoon and came alongside the mole head at eight o'clock this evening and nobody asked him to and both of his hours are on that sheet. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a harbor can be closed on paper in eleven days and it takes about ninety to stop a coast, and this is the twelfth day of the ninety and nobody in this country has won anything.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
