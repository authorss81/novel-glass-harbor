# Chapter 309

*Sunday the Twenty-Third of May, a Man of About Thirty-One of the Third Lay With a Crown Chart in a Bag Who Says the Can Was There in the Spring, a Herring Skipper of About Fifty-One With a Stove Out of His Wheelhouse, and a Season of Nine Marks That Is Not Going to Be Paid*

Sunday the twenty-third of May, in the fish market, off that mole in the afternoon, and in the shed on that mole in the evening.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, and the slate wall did not come down on it, and the cistern was not charged on it. Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it. That bench stood empty from the light to the dark on the said Sunday, and the man of sixty-one who sits on it three days out of ten was not on it at any hour of it and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

The man is about thirty-one and he has a coaster out of Ashtide and he came into the market on the Sunday with a chart in a bag, and he is a person of no office and he asked this yard a question in the middle of March and was refused and has not asked it since.

He put the chart on the end stall's table and did not open it and did not let anybody else open it and said the thing in about nine people's hearing, and about nine of them heard it, and the rest of the market was doing what a market does on a Sunday.

"**That notice names a can on a stake at your outer marks. I have been out past that mole-head in April and again on the eleventh of May and I have not been out since, because of the notice and because of a fouling on the middle of the eleventh, and I am not going to go out now.**" He put two fingers on the bag. "**And that can is not on this chart. I have had this chart nine years. I have never been asked to show it to anybody and I am showing it to nobody, and I am going to tell you three things and then I am going to pick it up.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**One. The can is not on it. Not on the outer marks, not on the mole-head approach, not in the approaches table in the back, and there is nothing in this chart about a can on a stake, and I have looked at it four times since Monday and I have looked at it wrong twice because I could not find it and the third time I found it.**" He did not open the bag. "**Two. It was there. I was out there in the spring, the last week of March, in a coaster, and I passed within about a cable's length of it and I remember it because my mate said something about it and I can tell you what my mate said and I am not going to.**

"**Three. I am not going to say where I got this chart and nobody in this market is going to ask me, and I would like the nine of you to notice that nobody has, and I would like whoever is in this yard to hear me say that I know that, and that is the whole of what I came in here for.**"

Nobody asked him. A man of about fifty at the fish counter said *that chart's no good to anybody anyway* and a woman of about twenty-six said *my father had one of those* and neither of them asked.

Then Calder Marr, who had been standing at the end of the aisle for about four minutes and had said nothing, said the one thing.

"**Then that is the whole of what you came in here for, and you have got it, and I am not going to ask you one question.**"

"**No,**" said the man of thirty-one. "**You are not, and I noticed at the time, because you had about four minutes.**"

"**I had about four minutes and I used them on not asking you, and I am going to tell you what that cost, because you are the second man in a month I have not asked something for and both times I have written the price down afterwards instead of before, and both of those are wrong and both of them were the same mistake.**"

"**Write it down, then,**" said the man of thirty-one, "**and pick up your chart.**"

He picked up his chart and went out of the market and sailed on the Monday.

---

The *Anna Ray* was lying off the north side of that mole on the Sunday with about eleven men in her and a stove standing on her afterdeck, because a stove is a stove and it came out of a wheelhouse in about a quarter of an hour on the Saturday morning with a spanner and a cold iron and no assistance, and it has been burning since.

She has been asking, through a boy hired to carry messages, for two days.

Elias Voke is fifty-one and has nineteen winters of moorage dues, two berths and a warp disputed and unpaid, and a hard at Ashtide that was applied for on the ninth of November past and has not been answered, and a season of this herring year that is nine marks and is not going to be paid and that nobody in this country has ever paid him and that he has stopped asking about twice.

He came ashore at about eleven in the morning on the Sunday with two of his own men and stood at the foot of the mole steps and was not asked for anything by anybody in that yard, and nobody from that yard went out to him, and he was on the steps for about four minutes and then went back down to his boat.

"**I am not going to stand on your slip and ask you for a stove,**" he said, to the mole, generally. "**You have not got one and I know you have not got one and everybody in this harbor knows you have not got one. I have said what I came to say which is that I have got one and I have got eleven men and I have got a boat of about fifty tons lying off your own water with a stove on her afterdeck, and the reason I am saying it out loud on a Sunday morning in about nine people's hearing is that I have said it in three other places in three days and I am going to keep saying it until somebody writes it down, and the place where a man gets written down in this harbor is on a mole and not in a room.**"

"**You should write to the harbor authority,**" said a man of about forty-five.

"**I have written to the harbor authority,**" said Elias Voke. "**On the Friday. And it is a chair and a table and a slate wall and a clerk, and the clerk took it, and the clerk said he had no season, and I have never in my life been given a better answer than that and I have never been given a worse one.**"

He went back down.

---

And in the shed, in the evening, with the first column open in front of them, the holder of that writ said the thing that went in the second column and neither of them improved on it.

"**There is a boat of about fifty tons lying off that mole with a stove out of her wheelhouse on her afterdeck, and a man of fifty-one standing on the steps of this mole at about eleven o'clock this morning telling nine people that he has said it in three other places, and this yard did not go down to that water and did not ask him for anything and did not offer him a stove it has not got.**" He shut the book. "**That is the second time this yard has been able to do exactly nothing about a man who is cold. The first was a man of twenty-eight carried up about eleven feet off this mole on the ninth of April, and he is in a bed at Ashtide until the second of June. Both of those are the same sentence and this yard did not write either half of it, and a party that cannot help a man is not the same as a party that has decided not to, and I have not got the figure yet that tells those two apart and I am not going to invent one.**"

"**Then say that is what it is and stop there.**"

"**I have said it,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I am not going to say it again in a shed until somebody in this harbor has done something about it, and I do not know when that will be, and that is the honest length of it.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-third day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of about thirty-one, of a coaster out of Ashtide, said out loud in that fish market on the said day at about ten in the morning in about nine people's hearing, that a can upon a stake at the outer marks of this harbor is not upon a Crown chart of about nine years standing that he carries, in the outer marks, in the mole-head approach, or in the approaches table at the back of it; and that the said can was there in the last week of March past; and that nobody in that market asked him one question and no person of that yard asked him one question and that the said chart was not opened in that market and was not shown to any person.*

*And that a boat of about fifty tons was lying off the north side of that mole on the said day with about eleven men in her and a stove standing on her afterdeck which had been taken out of her wheelhouse with a spanner and a cold iron at about ten o'clock on the Saturday morning preceding and had been burning since; and that the master of the said boat, fifty-one, came ashore at about eleven in the morning of the said day with two of his own men and stood about four minutes at the foot of the mole steps and went back down; and that no person of that yard went out to the water on the said day and no person of that yard offered a stove and that shed has no stove.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of that writ stood in that market for about four minutes and asked the said man of about thirty-one no question, and said out loud that he was not going to and that he had used the four minutes on the not-asking and that he was going to write the price of it down afterwards instead of before, which is the second time in one month he has done that and which is the same mistake both times; and that the reason against the line of the asking is the reason of the twenty-fourth day of April past about a person who keeps a page and the reason of the ninth day of May past about a drawer that has not been given a number, being that a man who is not asked is a man who may say the next thing, and that the said man did in fact say one more thing, which is that he knows he was not asked.*

*And that the said man of about thirty-one is a person of no office, was refused a question by this yard in the middle of March past, has asked nothing since, is not a witness, has not been made one, and is not going to be cited in a room by any part of this yard, now or afterwards; and that the chart he carries is not this yard's, has not been asked for, will not be asked for, and has not been read in that market by any person.*

*And that the master of the said boat of about fifty tons, fifty-one years old, nineteen winters disputed and unpaid, a hard at Ashtide applied for on the ninth of November past and not answered, said out loud on that mole in about nine people's hearing that he was not going to ask that yard for a stove; and that a season of nine marks standing against that man is not going to be paid, and that it has been said out loud in that harbor every season since this yard began keeping the figure against him, and that it was said out loud this month in a market and on a mole and in that shed, and that it has not been paid once.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the thirty-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that a can upon a stake that is not on a chart of nine years and was there in March is not a lie and is not a discovery and is not a route, and nobody in that market looked at the chart and this yard has not asked for it and is not going to; that a stove on an afterdeck is not a hardship and is not a symbol and is eleven men and a spanner and a cold iron on a Saturday morning; and that twice over, then, a party has been unable to do anything about a man who is cold is not a decision and is not a policy and has not been turned into one on the page, and the difference between those two has not got a figure and this yard is not going to give it one.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and there are ninety-two pence in the bag, which is seven shillings and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and forty-eight pence, being three marks, five shillings and eightpence. The far bench was not occupied at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and forty-six days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and twelfth night, and neither of them has moved. A stove is burning on the afterdeck of a boat of about fifty tons lying off that mole, and a season of nine marks is not going to be paid, and a can upon a stake is not on a chart of nine years and was there in the last week of March, and nobody in that harbor has asked anybody about any of the three.**
