# Chapter 332

*Wednesday the Sixteenth of June, a Boat of About Seventy Tons Past the Line at Half Past Six in the Morning Under a Paper on a Table, Not Back by Dark, and the Mark of the Second Seam Looked At from the Crown for the First Time in Twenty Days*

Wednesday the sixteenth of June, on the crown of that mole at half past six in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, being one shilling and eightpence, being the same figure it has held since the second day of June past. A wage Tuesday came and went yesterday and did not move a column, and thirty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and the holder of that writ was in that harbor on the said day and went up no coast road on any day of these ten, and neither brother has said why. The woman of no office was in that harbor on the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his left hand and a bandaged right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked and is not going to be.**

---

He was on the crown at half past six because he goes up there most mornings before the light comes off the water, and there is nothing in that he has ever put in a column, and the man of about thirty-four of the second lay who comes up at six with a hand lead was on the hard and not on the crown.

The coaster came out of the west basin at about ten past to seven with the tide under her and made up half a mile of the channel and stood off the mole head for about two minutes with a man on her wheelhouse top looking up at the light on that mole, and the light is out, its lens having been out since the seventh of May, and there is nothing to see him against.

Then she went past the line.

Calder Marr watched her do it from about a hundred and thirty yards and did not go down to the slip, and there were about nine people on that mole and on the hard at that hour and every one of them watched a boat of about seventy tons leave a harbor on a paper that was lying on a table in a building four streets away, and nobody hailed her, and nobody followed her down the front street, and nobody in that harbor said one word about it to anybody else for four hours.

Nessa Pike came up at about half past eight and stood beside him and neither of them said anything for a while, and then she said:

"**She did not hail.**"

"**No.**"

"**Nine people watched her go and not one of us has anything to write down, because what we watched was a boat leaving a harbor, and a boat is allowed to leave a harbor, and the whole of what is wrong with it is on a piece of paper that a man is carrying in his coat and that this yard has never seen.**"

"**Write that,**" said Calder Marr, "**word for word, and write underneath it that the holder of that writ watched her cross that line from a hundred and thirty yards and did not go down to the slip, and the reason is the reason of the first of May past, and it is the same reason and it has not been improved on and it is not going to be improved on because the boat is seventy tons and it went out on a paper on a table.**"

She wrote it, and her left hand ached within a page and she held it in the other one and went on.

---

The boy came up the steps of that mole at about eleven in the morning with nothing in his hands.

He was about seventeen and he is one of five or six boys in that harbor who will carry a word up a hill for a penny and have carried for three years, and he was sent by a woman who has the two men's berths off that quay and wanted a man on those steps to know that a boat was not in, and she did not want to come up herself and she did not say why and nobody asked her why because nobody in that harbor asks anybody anything.

"**There is a boat of about seventy tons of this harbor that went out at half past six this morning,**" the boy said, standing on the bottom step with the street behind him, "**and she is not in, and her master is in her and the two men are in her, and I am to say that to a man on these steps and I have said it to nine people and there were nine of you standing.**"

"**Who sent you.**"

"**A woman with the two berths.**"

"**What else did she say.**"

The boy thought about it.

"**She said if anybody asks, she has not sent me, and she did not say why, and I am not to say why,**" he said, "**and she gave me a penny and that is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to stand here and be asked about it twice.**"

Nobody asked him about it twice. Calder Marr gave him nothing, because there is nothing to give and because a boy who carries a word up a hill for a penny is not to be paid twice for one word, and the boy went down the steps and across the quay and up the front street and about nine people watched him go.

What went in the second column of that yard's book that night was not the boat. It was that a woman on a quay sent a boy up a hill to say three true things and a fourth thing that was a refusal of a reason, and that a man of sixty had said in a chandler's in the last month that three is the correct number of times to ask a question in a harbor, and that a woman on a quay has arrived at the same number by a different road and got there first.

---

At about two in the afternoon the tide was off the stone at the south end of that mole, which is the only hour in a day when the second seam of that mole's own foundation shows itself to anybody standing on the crown, and Calder Marr went down the steps and across the crown and stood at the parapet and looked at about six feet of the second seam and the fouling on it.

It is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, and it was fouled about a foot and seven inches on the nineteenth day of May past, and it has not been measured and not touched and not looked at by any person of that yard in twenty days, because for twenty days nobody of that yard has been in the water and there is nothing on that stone to read from a crown except at this hour of this tide, and nobody had come up here at this hour on any of the twenty days because there was nothing to come up for.

He did not measure it. He did not touch it and he did not go down into the water and he did not have a lead with him and he did not go and get one.

At about a quarter past two, at the foot of those steps, a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay was standing on the wet stone looking at the same six feet of the same seam, with a hand lead in his hand and a line coiled over his forearm, and he had been there some time. He went down the hard as soon as the two of them came down the steps, at about the pace of a man who was not going to be caught at it, and he did not speak, and he was not asked, and he is not Garrow Fisk of a pressure house at Ternwatch and the two of them may not be run into each other.

The fouling says a thing about the water at that stone at that hour. It does not say where the bottom is under it, and it does not say where anything is, and nobody in that harbor is going to say why it has gone on fouling for as long as that man has been keeping the figures of it, and he keeps them in his own book and gave his name to nobody on the day he was asked in May and he did not give it today either.

---

In the shed that night the three of them had a thing they had not had in a fortnight, which was a thing to be careful about, and they were careful about it in the wrong order, and Calder Marr said so.

"**I am going to say the wrong thing first and then the right thing, and I am putting them in that order on purpose, because if anybody writes this down tomorrow they are going to want the right thing at the top.**"

"**Say them,**" said the woman of no office.

"**The wrong thing is that a boat of about seventy tons went past that line this morning on a paper on a table with the fourth box empty, and that the notice was put up on a market wall on the seventeenth of May past in eight clauses, and that the man who put it up is not in this harbor and is not on this mole and is not in any room this yard keeps, and that this yard could go up that market wall this afternoon with a bucket and a brush and take it down.**"

"**And.**"

"**And I am not going to, and I gave the reason out loud on the fourteenth of May and I am giving it again now because a sentence said in a shed is a sentence said in a shed and this one is about a thing that has been on a wall for a month, and the reason is that this yard has said out loud that it does not know whether a price and a sentence are in the same world and is not going to find out by taking a sheet off a wall.**"

"**The right thing,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**The right thing is that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was at the foot of these steps at a quarter past two this afternoon with a hand lead in his hand, looking at the fouling on that stone, and he went down the hard when we came down, and I did not go after him and I did not ask him one word, and that is the second time in a fortnight I have stood next to that man and said nothing, and there is a figure in this shed tonight that is going to want him to say something.**"

"**Then do not ask him,**" said the woman of no office.

"**I am not going to ask him,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I want it written that I said that on the sixteenth of June and not on the eighteenth, and that on the eighteenth I am going to be in the water, and that a man who says out loud on the day before that he is not going to ask a man something has bought himself a night to think about whether he meant it, and whether that is the same as keeping the rule or is a way of getting round it with a good reason, and I do not know.**"

Nessa Pike wrote all of it down in the second column, and the last four lines of what she wrote were a reason and no name, and then she put the pencil down because the hand had gone from aching to something else.

"**Twenty-four lines in the second column since Friday,**" she said. "**Four on Friday, four on Saturday, four on Sunday, four on Monday, four on Tuesday, and four this evening, being two hundred and sixteen in all, and there were two hundred and five in it at the read-back on Monday afternoon, and the figure of that ledger is its own figure and is not a count of anybody's paragraphs.**"

"**Say what the first column's figure is and say how you count it, because one day a stranger with a pencil is going to count them,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Sixty-four in the first column at this hour,**" she said, "**being what was in it when I sat down, and it will be sixty-five when I have put this night's line in, and that is a count of entries and a stranger can count it; and fifty-six in the fourth at this hour and fifty-seven when this line goes in, and the fifty-seventh is the last thing in that book. Three columns checkable and one that is not, and that has been the shape of it since the twenty-seventh of March past and it is not going to be tidied for a stranger.**"

The woman of no office had the cuff and the needle and the dish of nine buttons and she did not look up, and she said one thing into the cuff, and it went in the second column and nowhere else.

"**He was right yesterday,**" she said. "**He was asked what was in the week and he answered it and it was true, and today there is a boat out of this harbor that has not come back and I do not know one single thing about her that he had not told me on the fourteenth, and that is not a lie and it is not a kindness either, and I am not going to say anything about it tonight, and I am saying it here so that somebody has to look at it.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the sixteenth day of June next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a coaster of about seventy tons of this harbor, with a master of about fifty and two men in her and a cargo of salt out of a yard up that coast, left that harbor at about ten past to seven in the morning of the said day, crossed the line named in the fifth clause of the notice of the seventeenth day of May past at about ten past to seven, and did not hail, and that about nine people on that mole and on that hard watched her do it; and that the holder of that writ watched her cross that line from about a hundred and thirty yards and did not go down to the slip and did not go and look at the paper in that office, and the reason is the reason given out loud on the first day of May past, given the same way and not improved on, and the boat being of seventy tons and the paper being on a table are not reasons and are not held to be; and that a boy of about seventeen who carries a word up that hill for a penny came up those steps at about eleven in the morning and said three true things in about nine people's hearing and a fourth thing that was a refusal of a reason.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and that it was looked at from the crown of that mole at about two in the afternoon of the said day at the low water of that tide and was not measured and not touched and no person of that yard was in the water on the said day; and that a man of about thirty-four of that harbor's own second lay stood at the foot of those steps at about a quarter past two in the afternoon of the said day with a hand lead and a line and looked at the same six feet of the same stone and went down the hard when two persons of that yard came down the steps, and was not asked one word, and gave his name to nobody on the said day as he gave it to nobody on the nineteenth day of May past, and is not Garrow Fisk of a pressure house at Ternwatch and the two may not be run into each other; and that the whole fouling ladder of that mark is in that man's own book and nobody in that harbor has seen one page of it and nobody is going to.*

*And that the holder of that writ said out loud in that shed on the said evening that he was not going to take the notice off that market wall, and gave the reason of the fourteenth day of May past again and not a shorter way of putting it, and said that a sentence said in a shed is a sentence said in a shed; and that he said that on the day before he goes into the water he was not going to ask a man of about thirty-four one word about a book, and that saying so a day beforehand is a night to think about whether he meant it and is not the same as keeping the rule and may be a way of getting round it with a good reason, and that he did not know which it was.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the fifty-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a boat leaving a harbor under a paper on a table with an empty box against its fourth line is not a boat that has done a wrong thing and is not a boat that has done a right thing and has not been asked; that nine people watching is not nine people knowing; and that a mark's fouling read from a crown at low water is not a bottom and not a depth and not a place, and is not a use anybody has ever put it to, and nobody in that harbor has said what it means.*
---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Wednesday and nothing went out of it, and there are twenty pence in the bag, which is one shilling and eightpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twenty pence, being three marks, eleven shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and seventy days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and thirty-sixth night, and neither of them has moved. The mark of the second seam stands where it stood, fouled a foot and seven inches on the weather side, looked at for the first time in twenty days and not measured, and the ladder of it is in a book in this harbor and no person in this yard has seen a page of it. A boat of about seventy tons of this harbor is out past the line and has not come back, and this yard was not asked anything about her, did not go down to the slip, and did not go into the water, and a boy of about seventeen came up those steps and said three true things and was not asked about them twice and was not paid twice for one word.**

**Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
