# Chapter 316

*Sunday the Thirtieth of May, a Man in a Blue Coat of About Fifty With a Book at the Head of a Slip, the Standing Reason Given Out Loud Before the Man Answered, the Answer Given Once and Not Improved On, and Two Marks, Seven Shillings and Fourpence Said in a Market on Purpose*

Sunday the thirtieth of May, at the head of a slip on that mole in the morning, and in the shed on the mole in the evening.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down on it and the cistern was not charged on it, the cistern standing on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it. The fish market was not in the said day.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to the man on it at any hour of the said day, and a man of sixty-one at a far bench was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift.**

---

Cobb Terrell was sitting on the capstan on that mole with his left foot up on a thwart at about ten in the morning with a sailcloth tied over it, and he had been to a bonesetter of that harbor on the Saturday morning on a crutch, on his own, and had paid nothing, and the bonesetter is about sixty and has been in that trade thirty-one years and did not say no and has not been asked anything since.

He said the figure out loud, in about nine people's hearing, and he said it in a fish market on the Saturday following and again at the head of a slip on the Sunday, and he said both times that he was saying it out loud on purpose.

"**Two marks, seven shillings and fourpence,**" he said. "**That is four hundred and eight pence. He did not say it would be more and he did not say it would be less. He set it and he put it in a sling and he said I am not to put weight on that foot for ten days and I am not to be on the water for fourteen. And I am saying it out loud in a market and on a slip because I have noticed what happens in this harbor when a thing is said once in a room, and I would like a figure to be in about nine people's ears that I have not asked anybody to put it there.**"

"**Who did you tell first,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**The boat's owner of about fifty-five. He told your yard on the Saturday morning at about eight and he told me last that he was going to.**"

Nobody in that yard had gone and looked at the foot and nobody had been sent, and the reason for that is the ninth day of April past and it is that this yard has one address in this country and is not going to make a second, and it has been held four times in twenty-one days and the fourth time it cost a man of about forty-one a crutch and a bonesetter's bill that this yard has not yet paid.

---

The man in the blue coat came up that mole at about half past ten with a book under his arm and stood at the head of that slip and looked at the water for a minute and a half with his face to it, which is a thing that men do on that mole.

He is about fifty. He is not an officer and he has no rank and he is not named and no person in that yard sent for him, knew he was coming, asked him a question, or thanked him, and there is nobody in that harbor who could say what he is, and a working man of a closed-sea squadron who has been given one page and a boat and has used them is what he is.

He looked at Cobb Terrell sitting on the capstan with his foot up on a thwart and then he looked at the slip.

"**There is a yard on this mole,**" he said, "**and there is a page on a wall four days up that coast with a line on it that names a drawer in that shed, and that page is a copy of a sheet that yard printed itself. I have got the sheet. I have not got the page, and I have not been up there, and I am not going to.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I am going to ask you one question and I am going to ask it because you are the only man I have seen come off a boat in this harbor in four days, and you are sitting on a capstan and you are not in a hurry, and I would rather ask a man on a capstan than a man in a room.**"

"**Ask it,**" said Cobb Terrell.

"**What is that yard doing on this harbor.**"

---

The holder of that writ was about nine feet away and he came over before Cobb Terrell said anything at all, and he said the reason out loud, and he said it to the man in the blue coat and not to the man on the capstan.

"**Before he answers you, I am going to tell you what you are about to get out of him, and it is not a thing I can stop and there is nothing in this harbor that would let me, and I would rather you had it from me than work it out on a slip.**

"**He was in a boat that this yard hired on Friday afternoon for a mark and eightpence, and a man in a boat that this yard has paid is a man this yard can be asked about, and that is the rule of the first of May past and it has never been improved on and it is not going to be improved on today. You are not doing anything wrong and I am not going to stand here and tell you that you are. I am telling you that when you ask him a question this afternoon you are not asking a man about a boat. You are asking a man who has been paid by that yard, and that is a different man, and the difference is one mark and eightpence, and you will not get an answer that is only about the boat.**"

"**That is fair,**" said the man in the blue coat.

"**It is the rule and it is on a page in that shed and I have said it out loud to about nine people in this month. Ask him.**"

The man in the blue coat turned round and asked Cobb Terrell again, in the same words, and Cobb Terrell looked at him for about four seconds and then he said:

"**I am not going to tell you what you should have said.**"

Nobody in that place improved on it and nobody argued with it and about nine people heard it, and that is the second time in this harbor that sentence has been said on a slip and the first was on the sixteenth of May past in about forty people's hearing in a market, by that same man, to a boat's man of about forty who had been out to a tender and had come back with nine words in his head.

"**I am going to say why, and I am going to say it to you and not to him, because you are the one asking. I said that sentence on the sixteenth of May to a boat's man of about forty in a market, and I gave the reason for it then, and the reason was the first of May. And I am not going to be a man who has a sentence for it when it is easy and a better sentence when it is not, because a better sentence is a chosen sentence, and a chosen sentence is one I picked today about a man I do not like being asked about, and that is exactly the thing the first of May is for.**"

"**And did he not know?**" said the man in the blue coat.

"**He was in the market on the sixteenth with about forty other people and he has had a fortnight to work out what he heard,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I am not going to insult him by saying he did not.**"
---

The man in the blue coat put the book under his other arm then and he said three things, and about nine people heard all three, and none of the three was a threat and none of the three was a lie and this yard is not going to say afterwards that any of the three was either.

"**One. We have not got a room with men in it deciding this. I have got a notice and a form and a desk at Ternwatch and a line drawn from a mole-head to a can on a stake, and a master who will not tick a box, and a man in an office who cannot write anything down. I have been given one page and a boat and I have used them, and I have not been given anything else, and I have not been told to want anything else.**

"**Two. I do not know whether that is right, and you do not know either, and I have been on four days of water and I have not found anybody on this coast who does know, and I would like to go on not knowing, because the last two men on this coast who were certain about it were certain about it very hard and one of them is a mile and a quarter from where I am standing.**

"**Three. And I am going to be straight with you about the part of it I can see. A restriction that gets a man's paper into a room he cannot reach is a bad instrument. I do not say it is a false one. I say it is bad, and I have been using it for nine days, and I have watched a boat of about thirty tons lie off this mole with a stove out of her wheelhouse while it was bad.**"

Then he went down that mole and got into a dinghy and went out past the mole-head, and nobody followed him and nobody watched him go and nobody in that yard asked him one more question.

---

Cobb Terrell said one more thing after he had gone, to the mole, and to nobody.

"**He asked me what that yard does on this harbor and I have been in this harbor thirty-one years and I could not have told him either,**" he said. "**And he did not look at my foot once, and I noticed that too, and I am putting that down with the figure.**"

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirtieth day of May next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a man of about forty-one of the second lay of that harbor, whose name is Cobb Terrell, was hired on Friday the twenty-eighth day of May past at a box on the corner of that mole for a boat of about thirty tons with a man in her for a day, by a boat's owner of about fifty-five, on his own account and for his own purpose, and was hurt on that Friday at about half past three in the afternoon in about nine feet of water off the west quay inside that harbor's own line; and that on the said Sunday he said out loud, in about nine people's hearing at the head of a slip on that mole, the figure a bonesetter of that harbor gave him on the Saturday morning last, being two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, being four hundred and eight pence, and said out loud that he was not to put weight on the left foot for ten days and was not to be on the water for fourteen.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man in a blue coat, about fifty, carrying a book, who is not an officer and has no rank and is not named and is a working man and not a face of anything, came up that mole at about half past ten in the morning of the said day and asked one man in a boat that that yard had paid what that yard does on that harbor; and that the holder of that writ gave the standing reason out loud before the said man answered, being the rule of the first day of May past, that a man in a boat that yard has paid is a man that yard can be asked about, and that the said reason was given to the asker and not to the man asked.*

*And that the said man of about forty-one answered: *I am not going to tell you what you should have said*; and that about nine people heard it and nobody improved on it; and that this is the second occasion in this harbor on which that sentence has been said on a slip, the first having been on the sixteenth day of May past in about forty people's hearing in a market by the same man to a boat's man of about forty; and that the holder of that writ gave out loud on that mole the reason for the said second occasion, being that a man who has a sentence for a thing when it is easy and a better sentence for it when it is not has a chosen sentence, and a chosen sentence is one picked that day about a person he does not like being asked about, and that this is the thing the first day of May past is for; and that the said man of about forty-one was in a market in this harbor on the sixteenth day of May past with about forty other people and had had a fortnight to work out what he heard.*

*And that the said man in the blue coat said three things out loud in about nine people's hearing, being that he has not got a room with men in it deciding this and has got a notice and a form and a desk at Ternwatch and a line drawn from a mole-head to a can upon a stake and a master who will not tick a box and a man in an office who cannot write anything down, and that he has been given one page and a boat and has used them; and that he does not know whether that is right and does not know anybody on that coast who does, and would like to go on not knowing; and that a restriction that gets a man's paper into a room he cannot reach is a bad instrument, that he does not say it is a false one, that he says it is bad, and that he has been using it for nine days and has watched a boat of about thirty tons lie off that mole with a stove out of her wheelhouse while it was bad.*

*And that the said man in the blue coat went down that mole and off in a dinghy and was not followed and was not watched and was not asked one more question, and that no person of that yard sent for him and none thanked him; and that the said man of about forty-one said out loud afterward that he could not have told the asker what that yard does on that harbor either, and that the asker did not look at the said foot once.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the forty-first line:*

*What it is not: — that a man asked a question by a stranger is not a witness and has not become one by being asked, and is not a mouth for that yard and is not a mouth against it; that a rule that has been held once and is given a second occasion is not a rule that has been softened, and the reason for the second occasion was given out loud on a mole in nine people's hearing rather than kept; and that a bad instrument is not a false one and is not going to be settled in this harbor in this month by two men on a slip.*

---

**Nothing came into this yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and sixty pence in the bag, which is one mark, eight shillings and fourpence, and the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and eighty pence, being two marks, four shillings and eightpence. The wall did not come down and the cistern was not charged. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. The two objects of nobody's are three hundred and fifty-three days up the crown of that mole and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and nineteenth night, and neither of them has moved, and a man of about forty-one of that harbor's own second lay is not to put weight on the left foot for ten days and is not to be on the water for fourteen, and a bonesetter of about sixty has said two marks, seven shillings and fourpence, being four hundred and eight pence, and that yard has not yet paid one penny of that figure.**

**And a man in a blue coat of about fifty with a book came up this mole and asked a question this morning and was answered once and not improved on, and he said out loud that he has not got a room and has got a notice and a form and a desk, and that the thing is bad and is not false, and this yard did not answer him and did not tell the man on the capstan what to have said. Nothing in that shed was signed. Nobody in that yard was thanked. Nobody in that harbor got a penny out of it.**
