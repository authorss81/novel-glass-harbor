# Chapter 394

*Tuesday the Seventeenth of August, the Sixty-Fourth Day of the Ninety, the Forty-Third Wage Tuesday and a Wage of Four Marks Not Paid and Forty-Three Weeks Fallen Due With No Envelope Against Any of Them, a Caulker of About Twenty-Three on the Mole Head Stone at Four in the Morning and Paid One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence in Coin on the Flat of It at Four in the Afternoon, a Figure of Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence for Two Days Against a Bag of Three Hundred and Twenty-Eight Which Means One of Them Has to Be Earned, a Master of About Thirty-Three With No Papers on Him Who Says He Will Be on the Second Lay at Four o'Clock on Saturday and Is Not Going Out, and a Saturday Asked for on a Tuesday*

Tuesday the seventeenth of August, on that mole head, and in that shed, and on the second lay.

**The said Tuesday was the forty-third wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column did not move, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, so that forty-three weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard on the said day, being a day of a man of about twenty-three at that mole head, paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon, and nothing came in, and there are one hundred and sixty pence in the bag, being one mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and that yard paid a man of about twenty-three one hundred and sixty-eight pence on a morning on which it did not pay its own wage of six hundred and forty pence, and neither of those figures is anybody's debt and neither of them is a gift and the two are not to be added together.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about nine until about eleven and did not go up the hill. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-two days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-four days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

He was on the mole head at about four in the morning with a maul and a bucket of oakum and his own hands, and he had been there since about half past three, and he was not surprised to see anybody and did not say so.

He worked the day where he was told to work it, which is the whole of the day. The seven boats were lying at their own berths on the third lay and the second and a man of about thirty-one of the third lay had a boat of about nineteen tons in company with him at a berth, and Fell went round the seven of them in the order of the sheet and did not do the big things and did not say that he had done the big things. He put oakum in the seams that wanted oakum and he drove it with his own maul and he caulked about nine feet of a garboard on the second lay that had been open since June and that nobody had wanted, and he said what he would do to the other seams and did not do them, and he was on the mole head at about four in the afternoon with his hands black to the wrist.

"That is a day," Calder said.

"It is a day," said Fell. "It is a day and I have been paid for it at four o'clock, and I am not going to be told by anybody at four o'clock that it was more than that, and I am not going to be told at the end of a Saturday that it was less than that either."

---

The ship's hand priced the Saturday out loud at about ten in the morning, before anybody had agreed to anything, which is the only thing anybody in that yard is asked to do.

"Two days of that man," she said, "is three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks one shilling and fourpence. There are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in that bag, being two marks and eightpence. So the second day is eight pence more than the yard has got in it, and a yard that has not got a figure has not got a Saturday, and I am not going to write a Saturday in that column against a day it cannot pay for."

"Then I will earn it," Calder said, "and I will earn it before Friday, and it will come out of this yard's own figures the same as this one did, and not one penny of it will come out of the man with two papers in the office on the west basin, and not one penny of it will come out of the owner of any of those seven boats, and the man on the stone is not to be paid by the person who wants the thing looked at. That is standing and it is the reason the yard is short and the two are not to be added together."

"You are going to earn it at a berth," Nessa said, "because that is the only day this yard has. And I will price that too and it is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, because that is the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall and it is the same figure for a boat and a man and it is not this yard's figure, and I have not got a figure for the man who does not sleep and I am not going to invent one at half past ten in the morning on a Tuesday."

**AND THE WHOLE OF THE MONEY OF THAT YARD IN THE FIVE DAYS SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF THIS MONTH IS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY PENCE IN THE BAG, BEING ONE MARK, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE ON THE FOURTEENTH LESS THE ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE THAT WENT OUT ON THIS DAY, AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE WENT OUT ON THIS DAY AND NOTHING CAME IN ON ANY OF THE FIVE, AND THE FIGURE THAT BAG IS SHORT OF THE WAGE OF FOUR MARKS IS FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE, BEING THREE MARKS, AND NEITHER OF THOSE FIGURES IS GOING TO BE PAID AND NOBODY IS TO WRITE ONE WORD UNDER EITHER SAYING ANYBODY IS GOING TO. THE SECOND DAY OF THAT MAN IS EIGHT PENCE MORE THAN THAT BAG HAD IN IT AND IS NOT PAID FOR AND IS NOT AGREED TO AND IS NOT ASKED FOR AGAIN UNTIL THURSDAY.**

---

The master of about thirty-three of that third lay came up the hill at about two in the afternoon and stood on the step and did not come in.

"I have heard there is a Saturday," he said.

"There is a Saturday."

"Then I am not on it and I am not on the paper and I have not been asked and I am not going to be, and I have come up this hill to say that I know it, so that if a man in a room somewhere in a month asks me whether I was in company on the twenty-first of August, I can say I knew about it and I was not in it." He put his hands in his pockets and left them there. "My papers are in a locker in a house in another street with three things in it that are not paper. I said no on the eleventh and I gave a reason and you did not ask me for it twice, and I have thought about it since and I would not take it back if you did ask."

"I am not going to ask you."

"I know you are not." He looked at the shed door. "I will be on the second lay at four o'clock on Saturday at my own moorings and I am not coming out, and that is all I have come to say, and you can write nothing on that sheet about me and I would rather you wrote nothing on that sheet about me than wrote one line."

"Nothing is on that sheet about you and nothing will be."

"Then that is the end of it and I am not going to stand on a step about it," said the master of about thirty-three, and went off the hill, and was not thanked and was not asked for anything, and nobody in that harbor has ever said to him that he was right.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the caulker was paid on the flat of the bench stone, in coin, out of the bag, a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and he counted it and put it inside his coat and said nothing about the sum.

"I am going to ask you for the Saturday," Calder said.

"I have not said no to the Saturday."

"I have not said you have. I am asking you for it now, on a Tuesday, for Saturday the twenty-first of August, and it is a flood and it is a day out and a day back, and I will pay you one hundred and sixty-eight pence for it out of this yard's own figures at four o'clock in the afternoon of that day, and the figure is the figure on the slate at the end stall and it is not this yard's figure, and no penny of it comes out of the owner of any of the seven boats."

"Ask me again on Thursday," said Fell, "and I will tell you on Thursday, and I will be at the stone at half past three on the Saturday either way, because that is where I am at half past three, and you have not asked me for the Saturday and I have not said yes to the Saturday and I will be standing on a stone in the morning and neither of us will be able to say that I said yes to a thing I was not asked for."

"That is fair and I will ask you on Thursday and I will not pay you anything today for a day you have not agreed to."

"I have got no argument with that," said Fell, "and I have got a maul and a bucket and I have got nine feet of garboard to come back to on Friday, and the coal for my own fire is not going to come and that is not anybody's debt and I am not putting it in a column."

**AND A DAY WAS PAID ON A TUESDAY TO A MAN WHO HAD BEEN ASKED FOR IT BY NAME ON A MONDAY, AND NO FIGURE IS AGAINST HIM, AND A SECOND DAY WAS ASKED FOR ON A TUESDAY AND WAS NOT AGREED TO ON A TUESDAY, AND A MASTER OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WITH NO PAPERS ON HIM CAME UP A HILL ON THE SAID DAY TO SAY THAT HE KNEW ABOUT A SATURDAY HE WAS NOT ON, AND NOTHING WAS WRITTEN ON ANY SHEET ABOUT HIM AND HE WAS NOT THANKED.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the seventeenth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Tuesday was the forty-third wage Tuesday and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it and the column did not move, so that forty-three weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that the wall came down and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the cistern was not charged on the said day because the said day is not a rota day; and that a caulker of about twenty-three was on the mole head stone at about four in the morning and had been there since about half past three, and that he worked the day at seven berths in the order of the sheet and caulked about nine feet of a garboard on the second lay that had been open since June and that nobody had wanted, and that he said what he would do to the other seams and did not do them; and that he was paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence in coin on the flat of the stone at about four in the afternoon and said nothing about the sum; and that the ship's hand priced the Saturday out loud at about ten in the morning before anybody agreed to it, being that two days of that man is three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks one shilling and fourpence, against three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and that the second day is eight pence more than the yard has got and that a yard that has not got a figure has not got a Saturday; and that the holder said he would earn it before Friday out of that yard's own figures and not one penny out of the man with two papers or the owner of any of the seven boats; and that a master of about thirty-three of that third lay came up the hill at about two in the afternoon and said he knew there was a Saturday and that he was not on it and had not been asked and would not be, and that he would be on the second lay at four o'clock on Saturday at his own moorings and was not coming out, and that nothing is on that sheet about him and nothing will be, and that he was not thanked and was not asked for anything; and that the caulker was asked for the Saturday at about half past four in the afternoon and said ask me again on Thursday, and that nothing was paid to him on the said day for a day he had not agreed to, and that he will be at the stone at half past three on the Saturday either way and that neither of the two of them will be able to say he said yes to a thing he was not asked for.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that a day a man is paid for is not a wage owed to him and is not in the wage column and is not to be added to a wage column that has not moved, and that neither figure is a debt of anybody's; and that a yard that is eight pence short of a Saturday is a yard that has to earn a Saturday, and that earning it at a berth is the only day this yard has and that this is not an argument for a berth and is not an argument against one; and that a man who says ask me again on Thursday has not refused and has not agreed and has kept both, and that paying him nothing on a Tuesday for a day he has not agreed to is the only honest thing this yard can do with him; and that a master with no papers on him who comes up a hill to say he knows about a day he is not on is not a witness and is not a person in a matter and is not a man who came back, and that nobody in that harbor has said to him that he was right about anything.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and nineteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a day paid to a man standing on a stone is not a wage and is not an advance and is not a retainer and is not the first of anything, and that a second day asked for and not agreed to on the day it is asked for is not a refusal and is not a promise; and that eight pence between a bag and a Saturday is not a hardship and is not a reason and is the whole of the arithmetic of this yard on this day; and that a man who says he would rather nothing were written about him is not protecting himself and is not hiding and is a man who has read a piece of paper once and understood it; and that paying a man for a day out of a yard's own figures on a day the yard does not pay its own wage is not a kindness and is not a debt paid and the two figures are not to be added together.*
