# Chapter 391

*Saturday the Fourteenth of August, the Sixty-First Day of the Ninety, a Sheet Ruled in That Shed With a Line for Every Man in Every Boat and No Figure in It and No Name on It Before the Man Gave It, Five Masters of Two Lays Asked One at a Time in the Same Words and Three of Them Said No and Two of Them Said Yes, About Eleven Masters Asked in All With an Hour Against Every One of Them, Four of Them Said No and a Reason Was Given for Each and No Name Goes Against Any of the Four, and the Cost of Four of Them Is a Man's Day and Is Not a Figure Against Him*

Saturday the fourteenth of August, on that quay, in that shed, and on the third lay.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and about eleven masters of that harbor have now been asked one at a time, in the same words, what a company of boats out on a flood and back on a flood with a paper between them naming each master and each man aboard would be, and seven of them have said yes and four of them have said no, and every one of the eleven answers and of the four refusals is on a leaf in that shed with an hour against it, and no name is against any of the four and no figure is against any of the seven.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she did not come up that hill on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-nine days and has not been touched and has not been taken down. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-one days, the chest is shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

He ruled the sheet himself at about seven in the morning, on the bench stone, with a rule that has a nick in one edge that everybody in that shed knows about.

It is a single sheet folded once. Down the left side, a line for a master and a line for every man under that master, and a ruled column at the right-hand edge with the head of a master's hand against it and an hour against the head of it. There is a head at the top of the left side with nothing under it but a number, being one to eleven, and the eleven was the highest number he wrote on it and he wrote it before he asked anybody anything.

"There is no wage on it," Nessa said. "There is no price against a boat on it. There is no figure of any kind on it, and I have not put one there, and if anybody puts one there I shall take the sheet off the bench and I shall not be asked twice."

"That is a thing you have just said twice," Calder said.

"I have said it once and I shall say it again if it happens," she said, "and you can put that in whichever column you like."

He did not write a name on that sheet before the man gave it to him. That is the whole of the manner of it and there is no better way of doing it and he did it in that order, in the front of the shed, at about half past seven, with the door open.

---

He asked five men on the said day, one at a time, in the same words he asked six men on the Wednesday before, and he wrote each answer down as he came back from the man and he wrote the refusals in the same hand and in the same place on the leaf, and no name went against any of the three.

**A MASTER OF ABOUT FORTY-SEVEN OF THE THIRD LAY SAID NO AND GAVE A REASON AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT TWICE.** He was the man who had said a thing to the water on the eighth about a page and a list, and he said the other half of it again and it was not the same half.

"I have been in company with men off this coast three times and it went wrong twice," he said, "and both times it was a Wednesday and a man was not there, and both times the boats did not know. And I have not got a way of stopping that by writing my name on a piece of paper. And I will tell you what writing my name on it does. It puts the Wednesday in a room. In my boat it is a Wednesday and a man who was not there, and in a room it is a Wednesday in a file with my name at the top of it, and a man in that room will want to know why I was not told and I will have to answer him, and I will answer him, and then it is a fact about me and not about the Wednesday." He put his thumb on the sheet without touching it. "So I will not. That is the reason and it is a poor one and it is the one I have."

"That is a no, and it goes on the leaf with the hour, and I am not going to ask you for the reason twice."

"I know you are not. I gave it to you anyway."

**A HERRING SKIPPER OF FIFTY-ONE WITH A BOAT OF HIS OWN AND MUNICIPAL PAPERS SAID NO AND GAVE A REASON AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT TWICE.** He was the man who said on the twenty-second of July past that he was not going to use his papers for a company of boats, and nothing has moved him off that saying since.

"I have not shown them to anybody in four years," he said, "and I have not wanted to and I am not going to start with seven of me. And there is another thing and I am going to say it and you are not going to like it. A paper with eleven masters' names on it is a thing that is in a room by Christmas. Not because anybody is wicked. Because that is what a paper is. And the room has a box in it and the box has a date in it, and the date is a day, and the day is a Friday, and on the Friday somebody in an office that has never met me will decide that a sheet of eleven names is a record of something, and I will find that out from a man on a quay." He did not raise his voice. "I have got papers of my own that say I am a lawful man and I have never once needed them, and I am not going to hang them behind a sheet that is going into somebody's drawer. No."

"That is a no, and it goes on the leaf with the hour, and it is the same no you gave on the twenty-second of July and I am not going to move you off it and I am not going to argue with a man about a drawer he has not seen."

"I know you are not," said the herring skipper. "I have said no to a great many people and you are the first one who did not start at the beginning of it."

**A MASTER OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT OF THE SECOND LAY, WHO TRADES OUT OF IT AND HAS A BOY OF NINE, SAID NO AND GAVE A REASON AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT TWICE.**

"My boat is my bread and my boy's bread after me," he said, "and I have been in company twice and I will do it again if a man asks me on a quay. And that is the whole of the trouble. Every man in that harbour will do it if a man asks him on a quay. And what is different on a paper is that a man in an office does not have to ask me. He has my name and he has the hour and he has the boat, and he comes and finds me, and I am not at a quay when he comes, I am in a chair, and I have a boy of nine and a boat that wants caulking twice a year." He looked at the sheet. "You have written eleven on it. There are eleven numbers on it and no names on it, and that is the part I keep coming back to. Somebody has got a list of eleven somewhere and I would rather it was the list of things a boat wants and not a list of men."

"It is eleven blank lines and I have not written a name on it yet."

"You will by the morning," said the master of about fifty-eight, "and I will not be one of the eleven, and that is my answer and you have it and I am going up the slip."

**A MASTER OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE OF THE SECOND LAY SAID YES.** He was the man who came up the hill on foot on the sixth of August and told this yard that six masters of the third lay were lying off the point with a small boat of about twelve tons alongside the outside of them, and he came up on that day to tell and not to ask, and he came up this day to tell and not to ask, and nobody thanked him either time.

"I have a boat of about twenty-six tons and a hand lead and a line and a man in her who can use one," he said, "and a man who can read a clock and not a watch, and I have got my warp and I have got the one warp every boat in this harbor has got. I have not got a second one and I have not asked anybody for a second one. And I will say the thing I have not said to anybody. On the sixth I brought you word up that hill and I got nothing for it and I did not expect anything and I came again. And if I put my hand at the foot of that sheet then on the twenty-first of August there is a piece of paper in this harbor with my name on it and your seven boats' names on it and every man in them, and if anything is done on that water there are seven men in it who were there and cannot be talked out of it. That is worth a great deal more to me than a warp."

He put his hand flat on the bench stone beside the sheet and it was dry, and Calder wrote the hour against his answer on the leaf, which was a quarter to eleven in the morning, and the master of about twenty-nine read the hour back and said it was right, and he was told that his own hand would go at the foot of that sheet on the Friday and not before that, and he said that was the arrangement he understood.

**A MASTER OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX OF THE SECOND LAY WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-TWO TONS SAID YES, AND SAID BEFORE HE SAID IT THAT HE CANNOT READ.**

"I cannot read," he said. "I have not been able to since I was about ten and I have never been able to write my own name, and I sign a mark and I have signed a mark on about four things in my life and two of them were for other people. So before you put me down I am telling you, because a man who finds it out afterwards is a man who has been had."

"You have told me and it goes down with the rest of it and I am not going to put a mark on it now. Tell me what you want done with it."

"Read the whole of it to me," he said. "All of it, and not the part about the day. And when you have read it, read my own men back to me and let me hear whether you have got them right, because I shall know by Saturday whether you have got them right and you will not."

"That is fair and it will be done and it will be done twice, once when it is written and once when it is signed, and I am not going to improve on the second reading."

---

**ABOUT ELEVEN MASTERS HAVE NOW BEEN ASKED IN THAT HARBOR, ONE AT A TIME AND IN THE SAME WORDS, AND SEVEN OF THEM HAVE SAID YES AND FOUR OF THEM HAVE SAID NO, AND A REASON WAS GIVEN FOR EVERY ONE OF THE FOUR AND NONE OF THE FOUR REASONS IS A FIGURE AND NONE OF THE FOUR IS A NAME, AND NO MASTER WAS ASKED TWICE AND NO PAPER WAS SIGNED IN ADVANCE BY ANYBODY, AND EVERY REFUSAL IS FINAL, AND NO FIGURE IS AGAINST ANY OF THE SEVEN AND NO FIGURE IS AGAINST ANY OF THE FOUR, AND WHAT THE FOUR PAID WAS A DAY.**

"They gave me a day," Calder said, up on the second slip at about four in the afternoon, to the two men who were standing there and to nobody else, and he said it in the same tone he used for a figure. "Four men gave me a day apiece, and I know what a day is, because it is a mark and eightpence and it is the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall, and four days of that is six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks two shillings and eightpence. None of that is going against the four. None of it came out of that bag, because nobody was paid for being asked and the day is theirs and not mine and not this yard's, and there is no return in this harbor that a day like that goes into and there is no office it goes to. It came out of nobody. That is the honest figure and I would rather say it out loud than have it written anywhere."

"And the four of them will be at their own benches tomorrow," said the master of about fifty-five, who had come up about four and had not been asked anything, "and you will not see one of them on that water, and you have not asked them to be anywhere else."

"No," Calder said. "That is what it costs and I have just said it out loud and I am not going to improve on it before supper."

**AND A STANDING INVENTORY IS ENTERED IN THAT SHED ON THE SAID DAY IN FULL, ONCE, AND IS CARRIED BY A SHORT POINTER ON THE NINE DAYS THAT FOLLOW: three marks to a man of Ashtide since the eighth of August past, unsent for; seven pence to that yard's own shipwright; eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, chest shut, and not asked in ninety-one days; eight shillings and tenpence to a woman with her name on the figure; forty marks first after food to a person of no office, neither reduced nor paid; forty marks whole to a boy of twenty-two, the three nots standing unasked for a fifth week, the third column empty and unappointed, and a hand at the head of it that is not scratched out. AND STANDING WITH THOSE SIX, A PAGE OF SIX NAMES ON A MOLE WITH A BOAT AS A PRICE AND NO FIGURE AND A STONE IN ONE MAN'S OWN POCKET; A MUNICIPAL CLAIM ON ONE OF THREE THINGS IN A CIRCUIT CUSTODY ROOM THAT CANNOT BE TAKEN BACK WITHOUT A REQUEST NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO MAKE, AND THE ROOM IS NOT ENTERED; A BOX IN AN OFFICE ON THE WEST BASIN THAT IS NO LONGER EMPTY, AND ANOTHER BOX ON THE OTHER PAPER THAT IS AND THAT A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WANTS LEFT EMPTY; A MUNICIPAL PAPER OF THE EIGHTEENTH OF JULY NAMING TWO BOATS AND THREE MEN AND A TIDE WITH A DOOR OPEN FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST AND NOBODY AT IT; AND A SLIP OF PAPER WITH A FIGURE ON IT AND NO NAME ON IT IN A DRAWER WITH A SHEET IN IT, WHICH WAS NOT LOOKED AT ON ANY DAY OF THESE TEN DAYS AND IS NOT PRICED AND IS NOT HEADED AND STAYS IN THE DRAWER. NONE OF THE ABOVE IS PAID, ADVANCED, REDUCED OR IMPROVED IN ANY DAY OF THESE TEN DAYS.**

---

At about ten past five he went up to the mole head, because that is where he goes, and there was nothing up there and nobody had sent him.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it is out on the fourteenth of August as it was on the fourteenth of May, and a light costs oil, and the last figure anybody in that harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is on a board in the front street with February's above it and both dates on the board, and a figure put on a board cannot be taken off it again.

On the crown, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins and about four feet from a page of six names in a hand nobody corrected, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and twenty-nine days old, and one of them has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and it has not been lashed back. The bench under that pump in the yard behind that market is bare for the hundred and ninety-fifth night, and it adds one a night.

There were two lights out at about a mile off the point where there were two lights on the evening of the first of August, and they have not moved in thirteen days, being counted from the hour they were first seen and not from the hour they were first missed, and nobody in that harbor has hailed them and nobody knows whose they are.

*First column, entered on Saturday the fourteenth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Saturday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a single sheet folded once was ruled on the bench stone at about seven in the morning with a line for a master and a line for every man under him and a column at the right-hand edge for a master's hand and an hour, and that eleven was the highest number written on it and was written before anybody was asked anything; and that there is no figure of any kind on that sheet and none was put on it; and that five masters of the two lays were asked one at a time in the same words on the said day, and that a master of about forty-seven of the third lay said no and gave a reason about a Wednesday becoming a fact in a file and was not asked for it twice, and that a herring skipper of fifty-one with papers of his own said no and gave a reason about a sheet of eleven names being in a room by Christmas with a box and a date in it and was not asked for it twice and has not been moved off the saying of the twenty-second of July past, and that a master of about fifty-eight of the second lay with a boy of nine said no and gave a reason about a name in a file and was not asked for it twice, and that a master of about twenty-nine of the second lay said yes and said that a name on a sheet is worth more to him than a warp, and that a master of about thirty-six of the second lay with a boat of about twenty-two tons said he cannot read before he said yes and asked for the whole of the sheet to be read to him and for his own men to be read back to him; and that the answer of the master of about twenty-nine went on a leaf in that shed at a quarter to eleven in the morning with the hour against it and the hour was read back to him and he said it was right, and that no hand went at the foot of that sheet on the said day and that the hand of that man went at the foot of it on the Friday and not before; and that about eleven masters have now been asked in that harbor in all and seven have said yes and four have said no, and that every one of the eleven answers and of the four refusals is on a leaf in that shed with an hour against it, and that no name is against any of the four and no figure is against any of the seven and no figure is against any of the four; and that the holder said out loud on the second slip at about four in the afternoon that four men gave him a day apiece and that a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence and that four days of it is six hundred and seventy-two pence and that none of it goes against the four and none of it came out of that bag and that there is no return in that harbor that a day like that goes into; and that the mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past; and that on the crown of that mole the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and twenty-nine days old, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and that the bench under the pump in the yard behind that market is bare for the hundred and ninety-fifth night; and that there were two lights out at about a mile off the point on the said evening where there were two lights on the evening of the first, and that they have not moved in thirteen days counted from the hour they were first seen, and that nobody in that harbor has hailed them or knows whose they are.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the said day is not entered here again and stands; and that a sheet with a line for every man in every boat and no figure in it is not a crew and is not a muster and is not a roll and is not the beginning of one, and that eleven blank lines with no names on them before the men gave them is a sheet and not a return and belongs to nobody; and that three of the four refusals on the said day were given for reasons a man could only give once, and that a reason given once and written down with the hour against it is not an argument to be had again and is not an excuse and is not a debt; and that a day given to a man who has said no is a day and not a figure and is not against him, and that four days of nobody's labour has no return and no office and is not entered in any column of any book in that harbor as a loss, because a loss with nobody's name on it cannot be asked about by anybody; and that a master of about thirty-six of the second lay said he cannot read before he said yes, and that the day a man has to announce that is the day a sheet is a list and not a favour, and that both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other; and that the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall and her slate has a figure in chalk on a slate that was put up on the twenty-fifth of July past and cannot be taken off it, and that a slate at the end stall of a market is not a wall and is not anybody's, and that the slate did not move on the said day and no figure was said out loud in the front street of that harbor.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and sixteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a sheet with eleven blank lines and no figure in it is not a crew and is not a muster and is not a roll and is not the beginning of one, and that eleven masters asked one at a time is not a company and that seven of them saying yes is not a fleet; and that a reason given once and written down with the hour against it is not an argument and is not a lie and is not to be had a second time; and that a day a man gave to a man who asked him is not a figure against him and is not entered in any return, and that a loss with nobody's name on it cannot be asked about by anybody; and that a man who says he cannot read before he says yes has not been had and has not shamed himself and that a sheet is not the same thing as a favour.*
