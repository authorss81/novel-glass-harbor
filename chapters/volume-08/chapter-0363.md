# Chapter 363

*Saturday the Seventeenth of July, the Thirty-Third Day of the Ninety, the Terms Read Out in Full in a Room in Veyr in About Six Minutes, Seven Lines With the Fifth Requiring a Thing Ordered and Not Done, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Saturday the seventeenth of July, in Veyr in that room, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, being in a city, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was out of that harbor on the said day and in Veyr, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The man of about fifty who keeps the room read the seven lines out at about ten in the morning, standing at the end of the table, in about six minutes, and he did not read them as if they were his and he did not improve on one of them.

The first line says the room sits to look at how a harbor on that coast uses its own papers when it sends a boat out and brings one in. The second says it may write down what it is shown and nothing else. The third says nobody in the room has a title and nobody will be given one in it. The fourth says the clerk in the room off it will write the hour and what was said and will read it back and give out nothing.

The fifth line says the room requires a particular work to be ordered, being a foundation survey of that harbor's own mole where it stands in eleven feet, and that the ordering is the whole of what the room does about it, and that the work has not been done.

The sixth says no person in that harbor is to be paid by the person who wants the thing looked at. The seventh says the room convicts nobody and takes nothing and closes nothing.

**That is the whole of the terms, and there is no eighth line, and no schedule and no enclosure and nothing on the back of them, and they were read once and not twice, because twice is a conversation a man can join.**

Calder Marr sat at the table with his hands flat on it and listened to the fifth line the way a man listens to a thing he has been told not to touch. The woman of no office sat beside him and wrote nothing down, because nothing in that room is hers to write.

When the man had finished, the clerk in the room off it read back the hour and the fact that the seven lines had been read, and asked nothing, and nobody asked him anything, and the pen stopped.

On the way down the stairs the woman of no office said one sentence, and she said it to the stairwell and not to him.

"They have ordered it and they have not done it, and nobody in that harbor wants it done."

He said nothing to that, because there was nothing in it to improve on, and because a work ordered and not done is a work ordered and not done, and because no person of that yard was in any water on the said day and none went into any stone.

At about four in that shed the ship's hand entered the day against the first column in her own left hand, being that the terms were read out in full in about six minutes in a room in Veyr, and that the fifth line requires a measuring ordered and not done, and that nobody in that harbor wants it done, and that no person of that yard was in any water and none in any stone on the said day.

**The measuring is not reached, not measured, not touched, not asked about and not closed, and no chapter of this batch puts anybody in that water or in that stone, and the one instrument that carries it is the terms on the wall in that room and no other paper in this volume.**

---

At about ten past five by that city's bell they walked to the landing again, because there is no mole head in Veyr that he goes to, and they stood and looked at the water and said nothing about the fifth line, because a line read out once in a room is not a thing to carry downstairs.

A shipwright of about sixty in that street mended a thwart with a strip off nothing anybody named, in about forty minutes, and asked for nothing, and a bonesetter of about fifty came past with his bag and looked at nothing and touched nothing and said nothing, because there was no hand in that street that wanted setting.

The woman of no office said at last, with her eyes on the water, that a change of politics in an office is a paper and not a boat, and that the paper is on a wall under glass and the boat is still on the bottom off the south end with six men ashore, and that the two are not the same thing and neither of them cancels the other.

**And both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, that a room has ordered a measuring and that a harbor is poorer for it in nothing and richer in nothing.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the seventeenth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed by the ship's hand in her own left hand, the holder and the woman being in Veyr: that the said Saturday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that in a room in Veyr off a street behind the fish market there a man of about fifty with no title read out seven lines under glass in about six minutes, being the terms drawn by the Crown, and that the fifth line requires a measuring of the ground under that harbor's own mole where it stands in eleven feet to be ordered, and that the ordering is the whole of what the room does about it, and that the work has not been done and nobody in that harbor wants it done, and that no person of that yard was in any water on the said day and none in any stone; and that the clerk of about sixty in a room off it read back the hour and asked nothing and was asked nothing; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-eight weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a shipwright of about sixty mended a thwart in that street in about forty minutes and a bonesetter of about fifty went past with his bag and touched nothing; and that about eleven people of that harbor cannot read the notice, being a count of people, and that nobody in that yard is to tell any of them anything; and that nine communities hold the records one in each, and six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the eighty-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that seven lines read out in about six minutes in a room where nobody has a title are not a hearing that convicts anybody and are not a court and do not speak in anybody's mouth; and that a measuring ordered and not done is not a work begun and is not a price and is not wanted by anybody in that harbor; and that no person of that yard in any water and none in any stone on the said day is not an absence that proves anything but the day.*
