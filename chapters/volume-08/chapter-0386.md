# Chapter 386

*Monday the Ninth of August, the Fifty-Sixth Day of the Ninety, the Wall Bare for the Sixty-Sixth Time and the Cistern Charged for the Seventy-First and Walked on None, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In at a Berth, a Boat of About Thirteen Tons With No Paper of Any Kind in Her Going Out at Half Past Seven on a Breach of the First Clause, Five of Six Masters of a Lay Coming In Past the Outer Marks at Half Past Four on a Line That Never Asked Them Anything, and Nobody Thanked*

Monday the ninth of August, at the office on the west basin, at that cistern, at the third lay, and in that shed.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday and was a rota day and was a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-sixth time and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern at the back of the market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventy-first time, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, and nothing went out of it, and there are five hundred and forty pence in the bag, being three marks five shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is one hundred pence, being eight shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and that bag is a day and a piece of a day and has been less on four of the last ten days and the figure of it is on the bench stone in that shed and not on a board anywhere.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about two until about four. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-four days and has not been touched.**

---

The man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no municipal papers of any kind went out past the outer marks at about half past seven that morning on a breach of the first clause of the notice of the seventeenth of May past, and he went alone, and nobody sent him, and he was not paid and was not asked to pay and was not told the terms because he was not being paid and there were no terms.

He had a fold of paper in his inside pocket. It was a copy of the notice of the seventeenth of May, and it was not cut out of anything and it was not written on by anybody in this harbor, and it had been in that man's own coat since before the spring and he had taken it out of the drawer where his wife keeps the paper that comes in the market and folded it himself and he did not say where it came from and nobody asked him.

**A BOAT OF ABOUT THIRTEEN TONS WITH ONE MAN IN HER AND NO PAPER OF ANY KIND WENT OUT PAST THE LINE OF THAT HARBOR ON A BREACH OF THE FIRST CLAUSE ON THE SAID DAY AND CAME BACK IN PAST THE OUTER MARKS AT ABOUT SIX IN THE EVENING ON THE SIXTH CLAUSE, WHICH IS FREE, AND A FOLD OF PAPER WENT OUT IN HIS INSIDE POCKET AND CAME BACK IN HIS INSIDE POCKET, AND NOBODY HAILED HIM GOING OUT AND NOBODY HAILED HIM COMING IN AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR WHO KNEW WHICH CLAUSE HE WAS BREAKING WAS THE MAN WHO BROKE IT.**

He was alongside the outside of the six at about nine o'clock, which is a mile and a half off the point, and there was a small boat of about twelve tons lying on the outside of the group with a boat's boat in her and a man of about nineteen at her tiller.

He did not go aboard the small boat. He lay off her and put his own bow on the small boat's quarter and held on to nothing, and the man of about nineteen came across four of his own feet of water in a dinghy and put his own hand on that boat's gunwale and looked at what was in the man's hand.

"That is the notice," the man of about thirty-eight said. "The one on the wall in the front street of the harbour, and I have read it out of that wall twice. The fifth of it is out. The sixth of it is in. It does not ask you a question and it does not ask me a question and it has not asked anybody a question since the seventeenth of May past and every boat of that harbour has come in on it since without writing a word down anybody could answer for."

"You have got no papers."

"I have got no papers. I have not had any for four years and I have said so out loud in that shed and I am saying it again here." He did not move the paper. "You are asking five masters whether their papers are in order and four of them told you yes and the fifty-five told you yes and they are all five of them sitting there now waiting to find out what a piece of paper is worth. It is worth the sixth clause. It is worth the sixth clause and nothing else and there is nobody on this coast who can put a figure on that, and if you have a list then you can put six ticks on it this morning and it will be a true list, and if the man of the thirty-three has no papers on him then there is no door for him and I am not going to tell him there is, because I have been in on the sixth and the sixth never once asked me for a paper and I know what I am talking about and he does not."

The man of about nineteen looked at him for a while and then went back up his own ladder into that small boat and brought up a board off the after thwart, which was a notice, and it was the same notice and it was worn at the folds, and he held it in one hand and read the sixth line of it with his thumb on the line and not his finger, and then he put the board down and went down into the dinghy again.

He did not say anything to anybody. He went to the five boats in order, from the outside one, and he was in each of them about a minute, and he came back up his own ladder, and the five of them got under way in the next quarter of an hour without a word between any two of them that anybody heard.

**FIVE MASTERS OF THE THIRD LAY CAME PAST THE OUTER MARKS OF THAT HARBOR AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON ON THE SAID DAY UNDER THE SIXTH CLAUSE, WHICH IS FREE, AND THE SIXTH OF THAT CLAUSE ASKS NOBODY ANYTHING AND ASKED NOTHING THAT AFTERNOON, AND A LEVER OF NINETEEN READ IT OFF HIS OWN BOARD WITH HIS THUMB ON THE LINE AND PUT SIX TICKS IN A LIST AND A TICK IS NOT A FIGURE AND SIX TICKS ARE NOT A COMPANY.**

The man of about thirty-eight came in at about six with the fold of paper still in his inside pocket and came up the hill on foot because somebody's legs were better than his, and he did not go into that shed and he did not go to the end stall and he went home.

The man of about thirty-one of the third lay, who had a chart in a bag and had been up the hill on Thursday past and had not been written down, watched all five of them come up the third lay from the deck of his own boat and then went below and shut his hatch.

**AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR THANKED THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT FOR GOING OUT, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR SAID HE HAD DONE ANYTHING, AND HE WAS NOT PAID AND WAS NOT ASKED TO PAY AND WAS NOT SENT, AND WHAT HE DID WAS GIVE THE SIXTH LINE OF A NOTICE ON A WALL TO A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN WHO ALREADY HAD THE SAME NOTICE ON HIS OWN BOARD, AND THE SIXTH OF THE SIX MASTERS, WHO HAS NO PAPERS ON HIM, IS STILL LYING AT A MILE AND A HALF OFF THE POINT OF THAT HARBOR AT THE CLOSE OF THE SAID DAY, AND WHERE THERE ARE NO PAPERS THERE IS NO DOOR, AND NOBODY HAS SAID OTHERWISE.**

---

At about two in the afternoon Calder was in that shed and the man of about fifty from the office on the west basin came up the hill and stood in the doorway, and he was not asked anything and he asked for nothing, and he said he was passing and he was not passing, and he came in and stood at the bench.

"I am not here about a boat," he said.

"You never are."

"I am here because a small boat of about twelve tons lay off the point at a mile and a half on Saturday and a man of about nineteen wrote six things in a list on Monday, and there was a boat of this harbor alongside her at nine o'clock with no papers at all, and that boat's master is not any business of this office and I do not know his name and I have not asked." He put his hat in his hand. "I am telling you that I know it happened because the two of them were in the same water for a quarter of an hour and the nineteen wrote a line about it, and I am telling you that this office is not going to write about it, and that the box on my table is for days a boat belonging to this harbour has gone out past the line without a declaration, and that the twenty-seventh of July is the last day in that box and that the ninth of August is not going into it, because a boat that comes in again is not a day, and I have thought about it and I have decided it and I am not asking you whether you agree."

**AND THE ONLY MAN IN THAT HARBOR WHO HAS A BOX FOR DAYS A BOAT WENT OUT PAST THE LINE HAS DECIDED HIMSELF, ON THE SAID DAY, IN HIS OWN HEAD, THAT A BOAT WHICH CAME IN AGAIN IS NOT A DAY, AND HE TOLD A MAN WITH NO OFFICE BEHIND HIM SO THAT IT COULD NOT BE SAID LATER THAT HE HAD BEEN ASKED, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR ASKED HIM ANYTHING AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR ASKED HIM NOT TO, AND A DECISION BY A CLERK IS NOT AN INSTRUCTION AND IS NOT A RETURN AND IS NOT PAID FOR BY ANYBODY ASKING FOR IT.**

and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-sixth time and went up again at the angle since the twelfth and nobody put it back; and that a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no municipal papers of any kind went out past the outer marks of that harbor at about half past seven in the morning on a breach of the first clause of the notice of the seventeenth of May past, alone, and that nobody sent him and he was not paid and was not asked to pay and was not told terms because he was not being paid and there were no terms; and that at about two in the afternoon a man of about fifty from the office on the west basin came up the hill to that shed and said he was not there about a boat, and said that the box on his table is for days a boat belonging to that harbor has gone out past the line without a declaration, and that the twenty-seventh of July is the last day in that box and that the ninth of August is not going into it because a boat that came in again is not a day, and that he had thought about it and decided it and was not asking whether this yard agreed; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty-one weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourth is not entered here again and stands; and that a fold of paper in a man's own inside pocket is not a document and is not a copy of anything and is not an instrument, and that the day that harbor's own figures go on a wall in that harbor is the day a sheet goes up outside whatever is already there and this yard has not done it and is not going to; and that six ticks in a list in a man of about nineteen's own hand are not a figure and are not a company and are not the beginning of one, and that a list a working man keeps of what he let in is a working man's list and not an office's and is in nobody's return; and that where there are no papers there is no door, and that a man with no papers who has been in twice on a clause that never asked him for one is not an argument for the door and is not against it and has not been thanked and is not going to be.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and eleventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a boat going out with no paper of any kind on a breach of the first clause and coming in on the sixth is not a lawful thing and is not an unlawful thing and is not a precedent and is not anybody's permission; and that five masters let in on a line of a notice that asks no questions is a foot and not a victory, and that a tick is not a figure and six is not a company and nobody's gain and nobody's loss is priced against any of it; and that a clerk who decides by himself not to enter a day is not an informer and is not a friend of this yard and is not paid by this yard and is not somebody's instrument, and a decision by a clerk is not an instruction.*
