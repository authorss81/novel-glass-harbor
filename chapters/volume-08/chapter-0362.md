# Chapter 362

*Friday the Sixteenth of July, the Thirty-Second Day of the Ninety, Two People Going Up That Coast to Veyr on an Ordinary Boat and Coming into a Room Where Nobody Has a Title, a Clerk of About Sixty in a Room off It Writing and Not Asked Anything, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Friday the sixteenth of July, on the water up that coast, and in Veyr in a room off a street behind the fish market, and on that mole.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, being up that coast on the water and then in a city, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was out of that harbor on the said day and going to Veyr, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

They went up that coast on an ordinary boat that carries coal, because the boat that would wait at Ternwatch was a boat that waits and not a boat that is sent, and because a man who is asked to a room does not get to send for a boat at a price he has not got.

The woman of no office sat in the stern with her hands in her lap and looked at the water and said nothing for the first hour. Calder sat amidships with the two leaves in his coat and did not take them out, because a paper carried in a coat on a boat is a paper and not a reading.

At about two in the afternoon they came into Veyr from the sea, and the city was a noise and a smell of coal and fish and rope, and nobody met them, and nobody asked their names, and they walked up from the landing behind a boy of about seventeen who carries messages for a penny and who did not know where the street was and asked twice.

The room is off a street behind the fish market there, up one flight of stairs, with a window onto a yard where a pump stands. It is a room with a table in it and six chairs round the table and a bench against the wall, and there is nothing on the walls but a sheet with seven lines on it, which are the terms, in a clerk's hand, under glass.

There was a man in the room when they came in, of about fifty, with a leaf on the table in front of him with a day and an hour on it and nothing else, and he stood up and said he keeps the room and not the inquiry, and that he has no title in it and no seal in it, and that he will sit at the end of the table and not at the head of it, and then he sat down at the end.

There was nobody else in the room. No title was said in it on the said day. No seal line was carried in it by anybody, and nobody in it carried a seal line that was not already on the face of a document in that room, because there was no seal line on any face in it at all except the two leaves in Calder's coat, which he did not take out.

In a room off the room, with the door open, a clerk of about sixty sat at a desk with a book open and a pen in his hand, and he wrote the hour and the two names as they gave them, and he asked nothing, and nobody asked him anything, and the pen went on after they had stopped speaking.

Calder gave his name as the holder of that writ, being the words on the paper that asked him, and the woman gave hers as a woman of no office, being what she is, and the clerk wrote both down and did not ask what office she was not of.

"This is a room," the man of about fifty said. "It sits on terms drawn by the Crown. The terms are on the wall. Nobody in this room has a title. The clerk in the room off it writes and is not asked anything. That is the whole of what this room is."

Mara Venn looked at the seven lines under glass for a long time. Calder looked at them after her. Neither of them read any of them out loud, because a paper under glass in a room is read by looking and not by reading out.

At about four the man of about fifty said the room would sit again in the morning, and that they could sleep where they liked and eat where they liked and that the room pays for nothing and nobody in it is paid by anybody, and they went out down the stairs into the noise again and found a lodging behind the market for twelve pence in all, being one shilling, at six pence a night each, paid out of their own hands and not out of that yard, and no part of it came out of the bag.

**Twelve pence out of two people's own hands for a night's lodging in a city is not a figure in that yard's book, and it is entered here so that nobody says afterwards that it was, and a room that pays for nothing is a room that pays for nothing.**

---

At about ten past five by that city's own bell, Calder walked back to the landing and looked at the water, because that is what he does at ten past five, and there was no mole head there and no light to look at.

A man carrying a barrow of shavings up the steps went round him and did not stop, and a boy of about seventeen who carries messages for a penny was sitting on the bottom step with his feet in the water and did not offer to take anybody anywhere, and a shutter came down across a stall at the end of that street and the woman behind it did not look at anybody and said nothing to anybody.

**Nothing in that street was his, and the two people in his own harbor whose counters he knows were both four days behind him on the water, and a man in a city for a room does not get to ask anybody what they are selling.**

At about six the woman of no office came down to the landing and stood beside him and looked at the same water, and neither of them said anything about the room, because a room seen once is not a room understood.

*First column, entered on Friday the sixteenth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed by the ship's hand in her own left hand, the holder and the woman being in Veyr: that the said Friday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that the wall came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth; and that the holder of that writ and a woman of no office went up that coast on an ordinary boat that carries coal and came into a room in Veyr off a street behind the fish market there, being a room with a table and six chairs and a bench and seven lines under glass, and that a man of about fifty keeps the room and has no title in it and sat at the end of the table, and that nobody in the room has a title and no seal line was carried in it; and that a clerk of about sixty sits in a room off it and wrote the hour and the two names and asked nothing and was asked nothing; and that both gave their names as they are and looked at the seven lines and read none of them out loud; and that they lie that night in a lodging behind the market at twelve pence in all, being one shilling, at six pence each, paid out of their own hands and out of nothing in that yard; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-eight weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the room in Veyr is a room and not anything else, with a yard and a pump outside its window; and that nothing in that street was his and that the chandler of that harbor and the copyist of that harbor are four days behind him on the water and were not in it; and that a lantern was burning over the fish market's own door and it was not lit for anybody who came into this street; and that the standing clause entered on the fifteenth is not entered here again and stands, being the count of people who cannot read the notice and the records one in each of nine communities and the office that answers them.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the eighty-seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a room in a city with a table and six chairs and seven lines under glass is not a court and is not a hearing that convicts anybody and does not speak in anybody's mouth; and that a man with no title keeping a room is not an office and is not a face of anything; and that a clerk writing an hour and two names in a book in a room off a room is not asked anything and is not a witness to anything but the hour.*
