# Chapter 379

*Monday the Second Day of August, the Forty-Ninth Day of the Ninety, the Wall Bare for the Sixty-Fifth Time, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Ninth, a Man of About Fifty Who Says He Is Going to Write a Day Down in His Own Book and Is Not Asked to Stop, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Monday the second of August, in that shed, and in the office on the west basin, and on that mole.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, was a rota day and was a wipe, being the sixty-fifth wipe and the sixty-ninth charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-fifth time and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on none of it by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-ninth, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

He came up to that shed at about eleven in the morning without knocking, which he had not done before, and he did not take his hat off, which he had done on both previous occasions, and Calder understood from the two of those things that something had happened in that office since the Sunday.

"I have one thing to say and it is not a request," the man of about fifty said, "and I am going to say it standing up because I have said the last two things standing up and I am not going to change the habit on a Monday."

"Say it."

"On Tuesday the twenty-seventh of July past, at about eight in the morning, a boat of about eighteen tons belonging to nobody whose business it is went out past the line of this harbor without a declaration, and she was gone off the harbour mouth in sight of two men on the third lay, and she did not come in until about half past six in the evening, and she came in under the sixth clause, which is free." He was not holding anything. "At about five in the afternoon a tow of eight boats came down the fairway on the ebb and was held eleven minutes, and a lighter of about thirty tons went on the ground on the edge of the fairway in about six foot of water. That lighter put in here on Tuesday night on her own account because she was making water, and I have a return of her because a lighter that comes into a harbour on her own account is a thing this office enters, and the name on that return is the name of the man who was in her and the man who was in her gave his name to a clerk of this office without being asked to."

"You have that already," Calder said.

"I have that already," the man of about fifty agreed. "What I do not have is the other half of it. There is a box on a table in that office with a figure in it for days a boat belonging to this harbor has gone out past the line without a declaration, and that box has been empty since the fifth of July past, and it is empty on the day I am speaking to you because on Monday nothing went out." He put his hands in his pockets. "I am going to put Tuesday in it. Not this week. There is no hurry at all and I am not doing it to catch you out. I am doing it because a lighter of that tow is lying at your quay with water in her and there is a man in this town who is going to ask me where that tow came from and where it went, and I would rather have written Tuesday down myself in my own book with my own hand and my own hour than have it written for me by somebody who was not in your fairway."

Calder stood in that doorway and did not ask him a single question, and it was not a struggle, and it was the first time in a week that a refusal had not cost anything at all.

"Why are you not asking me anything," the man of about fifty said. It was not an accusation. He sounded almost disappointed.

"Because you said on Sunday that nobody had ever asked you for the fourth box and that you go to the office every day and look at it, and because a man who has said that has told me what he wants and I did not take it then either." He kept his hands down at his sides. "And because this yard has no office and no standing and no paper of any kind, and if I ask a man in that office a question about a boat, then that question is a request, and a request is a claim, and in a year somebody will read it in a return and see the name of this yard against a question about a boat that went out past the line. I am not entitled to that answer and I would have to write it down if I got it."

"Then I will write what I know," said the man of about fifty. "The boat, the hour, the tide, the eleven minutes and the lighter. I will not write who was in her, because I do not know and I have not asked, and I have not asked because I have learned one thing in the last fortnight and I have learned it from you and not from anybody in my office."

"What have you learned."

"That a person in this town who is not asked anything is a person who cannot be got to answer anything," said the man of about fifty, "and that in a return of that office there is not one box I would rather be empty than full."

He went away, and he did not come back that day, and the fourth box on the other paper on that table was not filled that day either and is not filled now.

**AND A DAY WILL GO INTO A BOX IN A MUNICIPAL OFFICE IN THAT HARBOR BECAUSE A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY DECIDED TO PUT IT THERE HIMSELF, AND THIS YARD DID NOT ASK HIM TO AND DID NOT ASK HIM NOT TO AND COULD NOT HAVE ASKED HIM EITHER WAY WITHOUT PUTTING ITS OWN NAME AGAINST A QUESTION ABOUT A BOAT, AND NEITHER OF THOSE TWO THINGS IS THE SAME THING AS THE OTHER, AND A WEEK AFTER A MAN WENT OUT PAST THE LINE THERE WILL BE AN HOUR OF HIM IN A BOOK IN A TOWN FOUR DAYS UP THIS COAST.**

---

At about two Calder went down to the west basin office himself, because there is a clerk of about sixty in that office who has been there twenty-two years and is owed a quarter and has no book and no power, and his name is at the foot of the municipal paper of the eighteenth of July, and he is a different man from the man of about fifty, and the two are told apart in every place where both of them stand.

He did not ask the clerk of about sixty anything. He stood at the counter and looked at the board on the wall, which is the harbor authority's list, and which is a list and is not a schedule, and which binds nobody, and the fire party's hose is not on it, and the tide gang is four men short, and there is nothing on that board about any man in this harbor by name.

The clerk of about sixty did not ask him what he wanted either, and wrote nothing down, and the two of them stood in that office for about a minute and a half and neither of them said a word, and the fourth box on the other paper on the man of about fifty's table was not filled, and the figure of the day boats is on a slip of paper in the man's own hand and is going to have a Tuesday in it in about a week.

---

At about half past three the day's first column was entered with the hours. At ten past five in the evening he was on the mole head, and there was nothing up there and nobody had sent him.

There were two lights out at about a mile where there had been two lights on Sunday, and they had not moved in a day, and nobody in that harbor has hailed them and nobody in that harbor knows whose they are. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-seven days and has not been touched. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eighty-third night. The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and seventeen days old. The second seam and the mark four hundred yards off it stand at a season of nine marks each, neither paid.

**AND A DOOR IS OPEN FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST TO A CONVOY UNDER MUNICIPAL PAPERS WHERE THE PAPERS ARE IN ORDER, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED THE THREE MEN IT NAMES AND NOBODY HAS WRITTEN A DAY ON IT, AND NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR KNOWS WHO LEFT THAT PAPER ON THAT TABLE AND IS NOT GOING TO STAND ON A HILL AND GUESS, AND THIS YARD HAS NOW BEEN ON THE WATER ONCE INSIDE THE LINE AND HAS GONE OUT ON A BREACH AND CAME IN FREE, AND NONE OF THOSE FOUR THINGS HAS BEEN PUT IN FRONT OF ANYBODY IN ANY ROOM, AND THAT IS THE STATE OF IT.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the second day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Monday was a rota day and a wipe day, being the sixty-fifth wipe and the sixty-ninth charge, and that the cistern was charged and walked on none and the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that a man of about fifty from the office on the west basin came to that shed at about eleven in the morning and said he was going to enter in a box in that office the day of Tuesday the twenty-seventh, being a boat of about eighteen tons of nobody's business that went out past the line at about eight without a declaration and came in at about half past six under the sixth clause, together with a tow of eight boats held eleven minutes and a lighter of about thirty tons on the ground in about six foot, and that he said he was going to put it there himself with his own hand and his own hour rather than have it written for him by somebody who was not in the fairway; and that the holder asked him nothing and told him why he was not going to ask, being that this yard has no office and no standing and no paper, and that a question from this yard about a boat would be a request and a request is a claim and this yard is not entitled to the answer; and that the said man said he would write what he knows and would not write who was in the boat because he does not know and has not asked, and said that a person in that town who is not asked anything is a person who cannot be got to answer anything, and that there is one box in that office he would rather be empty than full; and that the fourth box on the other paper on that table was not filled on the said day; and that the holder went to the office on the west basin at about two in the afternoon and stood at the counter and did not ask the clerk of about sixty anything, and that the clerk of about sixty did not ask him what he wanted and wrote nothing down, and that the two of them stood in that office for about a minute and a half and said nothing to each other, and that the list on the board in that office is a list and is not a schedule and binds nobody and the hose is not on it; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a day will go into a box in a municipal office in that harbor because a man of about fifty chose to put it there himself, and that this yard did not ask him to and did not ask him not to and could not have done either without putting its own name against a question about a boat, and that neither of those two things is the same thing as the other; and that a question from a yard with no office behind it is a request and a request is a claim; and that there is a door open four days up that coast to a convoy under municipal papers where the papers are in order, and that nobody has asked the three men it names and nobody has written a day on it, and that nobody in this harbor knows who left that paper on that table and is not going to stand on a hill and guess; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that a man entering a day in his own book is not an informer and is not a spy and is not a man doing his job against anybody, and is a man with a box on a table that has been empty since the fifth of July; and that not asking him a question is not a manoeuvre and is not a kindness and does not put this yard in his debt, because a thing this yard did not ask for cannot be owed to it; and that two hours of a man's work out of a day bought at a fair price, and eleven minutes of a tow, and a day in a municipal return, are three separate facts and are not to be added together into a shape.*
