# Chapter 375

*Thursday the Twenty-Ninth of July, the Forty-Fifth Day of the Ninety, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Eighth, a Room Off the Market With a Fair Copy on a Desk in It and One Word at the Head of a Column, a Man Who Wants to Know Who Sends Work to That Room and Does Not Ask, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Thursday the twenty-ninth of July, in that shed, and in a room off that market, and on that mole.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, was a rota day and was not a wipe, being the sixty-eighth charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on none of it by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-eighth, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day and came up the hill at about five. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

He had one question and he did not ask it, and that is the whole of the day and it took him two hours to get to the room and about ninety seconds to stand in the doorway of it.

The room off the market is up two steps and down a passage and it is nine feet by twelve, and there is one window, and there is a counter along the wall with three stools at it, and a woman of about fifty-nine sits at that counter with a pen and a copying pad and has done for twenty-two years, and she is not a witness and has never been asked to be one.

There was a fair copy on the desk in front of her. It was the fourth sheet of a run of them, and it was the cleanest hand on that coast, and it had a head ruled across the top of it and, under the head, two columns, and at the head of the left-hand column there was one word.

He was standing in the doorway. He could read it. It was the same word, in a small fair hand, under a ruled line, with a clean margin all round it, and the hand that had written it in block capitals and filled it in on a board in a boat had not written it this time; a person who copies does not fill in capitals, and a person who fills in capitals does not rule a margin.

The woman of about fifty-nine kept her pen moving and did not look up, and after a while she put her left hand flat on the sheet and slid it about half an inch towards the near edge of the desk, which is not a thing a woman does at a desk when she is copying, and then she went on copying with her right hand.

That was the whole of what she did and she never once looked at the doorway.

He stood there long enough to be sure and not long enough to be a man with an errand, and then he went back down the passage and up the two steps and out into the market.

**A FAIR COPY IN A ROOM OFF A MARKET IS MADE FROM SOMETHING SOMEBODY SENT IN, AND THE SHEET HAS A RULED HEAD AND A MARGIN AND A CLEAN HAND, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR SENT ANYTHING INTO THAT ROOM, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR KEEPS A SEALED CASE WITH A WORD AT THE TOP OF IT IN A MARKET ROOM, AND A HARBOR IN WHICH A THING HAS BEEN COPIED FAIRLY TWENTY-TWO TIMES A WEEK FOR ELEVEN WEEKS HAS HAD SOMETHING SENT IN ELEVEN TIMES A WEEK, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT HARBOR SENT ANYTHING.**

At about four that afternoon he said the whole of it in that shed in about four sentences, to the ship's hand, and the woman of no office was on the bench and he said it to the room.

"I want to know who sends work into that room and who pays for it, and I am not going to ask her, and I am not going to ask anybody else either, and there is a reason and the reason is the rule and not what I am." He put his hand flat on the bench. "A copyist of twenty-two years who has never been asked what she is copying is the only kind of person in that town who can keep doing it. The day somebody in this yard asks her one question, she has to have an answer, and the day she has an answer is the day she is a person in a matter. And I am not going to put her in a matter. So it is not asked."

"You noticed the sheet," the woman of no office said.

"I noticed the sheet."

"She moved it."

"She moved it half an inch and she did not look up, and that is a woman saying something to a man in a doorway in the only way she can say it without being a person in a matter, and I am not going to use it." He took his hand off the bench. "And I want to be exact about this, because I have been on the water two days and I know what a want looks like. That is a want. It is the same want as the man of about fifty's two boats and the same want as the man of about thirty-one's five, and it goes in the second column with the reason against the line that the holder saw it and did not act on it, and it does not go in the first column, because a man on a mole cannot see a sheet in a room off a market, and if a thing cannot be checked from a mole it is not in the first column, and that rule was made in this shed and not by me and I am not going to break it in the week that I most want to."

The ship's hand wrote it in the second column and asked him one question, which is the only question she asks him in a week.

"Would you have gone in if she had looked up?"

"No," Calder said. "There is no version of that room where I go in and come out with the same thing I went in with, and she is the one who has to sit in it afterwards."

**AND IT IS ENTERED ON THE FACE OF THAT DAY THAT A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-NINE WITH TWENTY-TWO YEARS IN A ROOM OFF THAT MARKET MOVED A SHEET HALF AN INCH TOWARDS THE EDGE OF A DESK AND DID NOT LOOK UP, AND THAT NOBODY IN THAT SHED WENT IN, AND THAT THE SLIP IN THE DRAWER HAS SEVEN THOUSAND ON IT AND NO COLUMN HEAD AND NO NAME AND IS NOT CONNECTED IN THAT BOOK TO ANYTHING THAT HAPPENED IN THAT ROOM, BECAUSE NOBODY IN THAT SHED KNOWS THAT THE TWO ARE ANYTHING TO DO WITH EACH OTHER.**

---

At about eleven in the morning the market had no flour in it, which was not a thing anybody had put on a board, and the woman of about fifty-eight at the end stall told Calder so without being asked, because he had come to her slate on the Sunday and the slate was still up with the figure on it and the date beside it.

"There was a lighter of flour for this place on the Wednesday tide," she said. "There is not one now, and there is not one coming, and I have not got a price for you because there is nothing to price. When it comes back I will put it on this slate with the day beside it and it will be a different figure from the last one, and the last one will be underneath, because I do not take a figure off my board and neither does anybody else in this market."

"You have no figure for it."

"I have no figure for it because there is no flour," she said, "and a woman who puts a figure on a board for flour that is not in the harbour is a woman who is telling lies to a market, and I have been doing this for thirty-one years and I am not going to start in July."

---

At about half past three the day's first column was entered with the hours. He went up to the mole head at ten past five, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-three days and has not been taken down. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and seventy-ninth night. The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and thirteen days old. The third channel is still nobody's measurement and the instrument it was found with went back up that coast on a Tuesday in the middle of July and has not come down, and no hand on that coast has it.

**AND A HARBOR CAN HAVE A WORD AT THE HEAD OF A COLUMN IN A ROOM OFF ITS OWN MARKET AND NO FIGURE FOR ITS OWN FLOUR, AND BOTH OF THOSE ARE TRUE, AND THE ONE IS A COPY AND THE OTHER IS A LACK, AND NEITHER OF THEM CANCELS THE OTHER, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS IN THE FOURTH COLUMN TONIGHT.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Thursday was a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that the cistern was charged and walked on none, the sixty-eighth, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that the holder went to a room off that market at about two in the afternoon and stood in the doorway of it and did not go in; and that on a fair copy on a desk in that room there was a ruled head and two columns under it and one word at the head of the left-hand column, and that the word is the same word as the word at the head of the board in a boat of the line, in a small fair hand with a ruled margin and not in block capitals filled in; and that the woman of about fifty-nine in that room, with twenty-two years in it, moved the sheet about half an inch towards the near edge of the desk with her left hand and did not look up and went on copying; and that the holder said out loud in that shed at about four that he wanted to know who sends work into that room and who pays for it and was not going to ask, the reason given being that a copyist who has never been asked what she is copying is the only kind of person in that town who can keep doing it, and that the day anybody asks her is the day she is a person in a matter, and that he noticed the sheet and the half inch and was not going to use either; and that a fair copy in that room is made from something somebody sent in, and that nobody in that harbor sent anything into that room, and that this is not entered in the first column because a man on a mole cannot see a sheet in a room off a market; and that the woman of about fifty-eight at the end stall told the holder without being asked that there is no flour in that market and none coming, and that she has no figure for it because there is nothing to price, and that when it comes it will go on the slate with the day beside it and the last figure will be underneath; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about fifty-nine with twenty-two years in a room off that market wanted something and did not say it, and that the holder saw the want and did not act on it, and that both of those are entered and not improved on; and that there is no version of that room in which the holder comes out with what he went in with, and the woman is the one who has to sit in it afterwards; and that the third channel is nobody's measurement and the borrowed instrument is up that coast and has not come down and no hand on that coast has it; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundredth line:*

*What it is not: — that one hundred lines in that column is not a hundredth year and is not anything anybody is to be impressed by; and that a word copied fairly in a municipal copying room is not a raid and is not a seizure and is not a finding against anybody, and is a piece of paper with a word on it in the cleanest hand on that coast; and that not going into a room is not caution and is not coldness and is not a thing anybody has to be thanked for, and that not asking a woman a question she was not asked before is not a refusal of hers.*
