# Chapter 399

*Sunday the Twenty-Second of August, the Sixty-Ninth Day of the Ninety, Eleven Weeks Read Out in a Public Room Over the Fish Market in the Order They Were Signed and the Shape of a Countersigned Thing Read After Them and Not Before, About Sixty People on the Flat of a Mole in the Hearing of an Admission Said Once in His Own Voice and Not Improved On by Anybody Including Himself, a Paper of Terms With a Door in It and a Man Standing at the Door and Not an Opening, a Question Asked Once in a Room With a Clerk in It That Named No Person and Was Answered Nowhere, and a Thing Said Once on a Mole Between Two People That Is Not a Marriage and Is Not a Reward and Is Not Forty Marks*

Sunday the twenty-second of August, in a room over the fish market, on that mole, and in the office on the west basin.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are one hundred and sixty pence in the bag, being one mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the caulker of about twenty-three was not asked for that day by anybody and did not offer and is not going to be asked for it again by anybody in that crew, and the three hundred and thirty-six pence that has gone out of that yard in these ten days went out on a Tuesday and on a Saturday, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence for each, and the one hundred and sixty-eight pence that came in came in on the eighteenth at a berth, and the two figures are not the same figure and neither of them is the wage of four marks, which is six hundred and forty pence and has not been paid for forty-three weeks and is not going to be paid on the forty-fourth, which is tomorrow.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in the room over that market from about eleven and on that mole at about four in the afternoon and again at about six, and she asked one question in that harbor in ten days. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-seven days and has not been touched and has not been taken down. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-nine days, the chest is shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

The room is over the fish market and it is let out on a Sunday to anybody who wants it, and the man who lets it takes the money in a bowl on the step and does not ask who is going in. There is no clerk in that room and no board on the wall of it and no list and no name over the door.

Calder went up at about eleven with a leaf and nothing else in his hands. He had ruled it that morning with a column and eleven lines in it and no name against any of the eleven and no figure against any of the eleven, and the only things on it were eleven dates in the order he had read them off a board about a yard wide under a sloping canvas on an after thwart on the twenty-seventh of July past.

He stood where a man in a chair could see him and nobody else could see the leaf.

"I am going to say four things and then I am going to read this and I am not going to say them again this month." He put his hand flat on his own hip and did not put it on the leaf. "One. I have not got the sheet. I read a board and there is no copy of it in that shed and nothing in my pocket on this earth is anybody's. Two. I am reading dates off a leaf in my own hand and I have not moved one of them to make it come out even. Three. If anybody here wants to write any of this down they may write it down and it is theirs and I am not going to know their names. Four. I am going to read the shape of a thing after the eleven and not before them, and I am going to say why afterwards and not now."

He read the eleven dates. The eighth of May, the fifteenth of May, the twenty-second of May, the twenty-ninth of May, the fifth of June, the twelfth of June, the nineteenth of June, the twenty-sixth of June, the third of July, the tenth of July, and the twenty-second of July.

"Ten of those are a week apart and the last one is twelve days after the one before it," he said. "That is what the board says and I have not tidied it. There are eleven marks against eleven dates in a column on a board about a yard wide, and the word at the head of that board is a word about what the work is, and I will come to it in a minute."

"Eleven boats," said a man at the back.

"Eleven hulls," Calder said. "Not eleven men. Not eleven families. I do not know who was aboard any of those eleven and I have not asked and I am not going to ask, and if a man in a room in a month asks me who was in any of them I am going to tell him I do not know, and that is going to be the answer whatever is in the room."

About forty people in a room will say something at some point. Nothing was said.

"A hand lead and a line is not in that column," said a man near the middle.

"No," Calder said, "and I am glad you said it, and I am not going to use it."

He read the shape of the thing then, and he read it flat and without stopping, and the room heard it in the order it stands on a board.

"There is one word at the head of it in block capitals and the word is *UNSEALED*. There are two ruled columns under it. There are the eleven marks I have just read you against the eleven dates. At the foot of it there are two impressions struck side by side and I read neither of them and I do not know whose they are and I am not going to find out. And above the head of the board there is a date cut into the timber, being the fourteenth of February, and that is ninety-two days before the notice went up on a wall in a street two doors from where I am standing now."

Then he said the fourth thing, which he had said he would say afterwards.

"I read it after the eleven and not before them because I have not got it. If I had said the word at the top first, then forty people in this room would be thinking about a word, and in a year the word would be what happened and the eleven hulls would be what was said about it. They are not. Eleven hulls is what happened. A word is what somebody wrote at the top of a board, and I cannot read the two impressions at the foot of it and the man who can is not in this room and may never be in this room."

"And is the flour coming back," said the woman of about fifty-eight from the back, and she was not asking him, she was asking the room, and he turned round and answered her anyway.

"No," he said. "And I am not going to tell you a figure for it because I have not got one, and there is no flour in that market and there is not going to be any this month. That is the whole of what I know and I have known it since Saturday at about one o'clock."

**AND THE ELEVEN DATES WERE READ OUT ALOUD IN THAT ROOM IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SIGNED AND THE SHAPE OF A COUNTERSIGNED THING WAS READ OUT AFTER THEM AND NOT BEFORE THEM, AND THE SLIP OF PAPER THAT CAME OFF THAT WATER ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF JULY PAST WAS IN A DRAWER IN THAT SHED WITH A SHEET IN IT AND DID NOT COME UP THAT STAIR AND WAS NOT PRODUCED AND WAS NOT READ AND WAS NOT PRICED AND WAS NOT NAMED AND IS STILL IN THE DRAWER, AND NOBODY WHO CAME UP THAT STAIR ON THE SAID DAY WAS ASKED FOR A NAME BY ANYBODY.**

He was on the flat of the mole at about four in the afternoon and about sixty people were on that mole and the market was emptying down onto it, which is what a Sunday does to a mole in that harbor.

The master of about fifty-five of that third lay was against the parapet with his arms on it and had not said anything for ten minutes. The master of about thirty was beside him. The master of about thirty-six of the second lay was on the third step with his hands on his knees. The man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait was on the ground with his own painter across his knees and his own painter had been across his knees all day and he had not asked anybody for anything. The man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers was at the head of the steps and had come on his own account and was not thanked.

Calder stood on the flat of the bench stone, which is the same stone a caulker of about twenty-three stands on at half past three every morning he cannot sleep, and nobody had asked that man for the Sunday and he had told them so on the Saturday.

"I have got about four minutes and I am going to use them," he said, and the front of that mole went quiet in a way that a mole does not, and about sixty people heard it and there was nobody else within forty yards who did not. "I am going to say it once. I am not going to say it a second time this month and I am not going to have anybody say it for me, and if you want to say something to me you can say it down at the shed afterwards and not up here."

He said it in his own voice, at the speed of a man reading a figure.

"Saturday the twenty-first of August. I named the day on a quay on the Friday and I said the reason was the tide, and the tide was the reason, and it was about a quarter of the reason. I chose that day. I knew it was the flood when I chose it. Nobody made me name it and nobody put it in my mouth. And I did not ask what else goes down that reach on the flood, and I did not ask because I did not want the answer, and that is the whole of it and it is not going to get better by me saying it slower."

"A transport of about a hundred and forty tons with a merchant's company aboard and about sixty tons of flour in her and three of her own boats in tow came down that reach at about half past ten," he said. "They cut her fore cable with a wire and stove her fore hatch with a maul and put a hole in her garboard with a boat's hook, and about half of what she had in her was in the water by the time she was inside the point at one o'clock. None of that flour is in this harbor. Nobody has laid a claim for a pound of it. Nobody has been made whole. Nobody was hurt. Nobody has been counted and I am not going to count anybody and I am not going to say one word about a man who was aboard that transport or a man who was in the water this morning, because I do not know and I will not guess."

"And I am going to say the three things that people are going to reach for, and I am going to reach for them first so that nobody else has to."

"There is two foot of water on a line in that reach that a man made who is dead and it is nobody's arithmetic, and I am not putting it against the flour, because it is not mine to put anywhere. And if it had been mine I would be standing here just the same, and I want that said in that order and not in the other order."

"That mole behind me is four thousand seven hundred yards of second seam off a board in the office on the west basin, and there is not one man in this harbor keeping it and there never has been one, and I am not going to stand up here in front of sixty people and tell them that a mole is long. A mole being long is a thing you all know, and it is not an answer to a boat's fore hatch, and I have watched a man get ready to say it and I have said it first."

"And this harbor is poorer tonight than it was in May and it will be poorer in September, and that is not what the flour was, and I am putting the two of them side by side because both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other."

He stopped. Nobody said anything for about four seconds, which on that mole was a long time.

Then he said the last of it and he said it at the same speed.

"That is all of it. I am not going to say it again and I am not going to have anybody in this harbor say it for me. And I am not going to be told up here that I did my best, and if anybody wants to tell me that they can tell me down at the shed and I will not thank them for it."

Nobody said anything. Nobody put a hand on him. Nobody stood at his shoulder. Nobody said that he was brave and nobody said that he had done his best and nobody said that it was not his fault and nobody said the patrol was going down that reach anyway. The master of about fifty-five took his arms off the parapet and put them down at his sides and said nothing, and the master of about thirty said nothing, and the man of about thirty-six on the third step said one thing and only one thing, to the water.

"She was not there to be caught," he said. "She was there because that is the water."

"That is right," Calder said, "and I have not got one word to add to that, and I am not going to."

**AND HE WAS NOT THANKED FOR IT, AND NOBODY STOOD WITH HIM, AND NOBODY PUT A HAND ON HIM, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS TOLD THAT HE WAS BRAVE OR THAT HE DID HIS BEST, AND NOBODY SAID THE PATROL WAS GOING DOWN THAT REACH ANYWAY, AND THE MEN WHO HAD BEEN IN THE WATER ON THE SAID MORNING DID NOT SAY OTHERWISE, AND HE DID NOT IMPROVE ON IT AT FOUR O'CLOCK AND HE DID NOT IMPROVE ON IT AFTERWARDS BY ANYBODY AND HE DID NOT IMPROVE ON IT HIMSELF, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS SAID A WORD ABOUT THE TWENTY-FIRST OF AUGUST IN ANY ROOM.**

He went down to the office on the west basin at about half past five and the man of about fifty was at his own table with his two papers on it and the fourth box on the other one was still empty.

"I have read a paper," the man said, before Calder was through the door. "I am not going to give it to you and I am not going to ask you for it back and I am not here about a boat. There is a copy of the terms of the second sitting in a drawer in this office and I have read all seven of the terms and I have read the sixth of them about nine times this month and I have read a clause that is not one of the seven."

"Read me the clause."

"That is a request," said the man of about fifty, "and I am not going to be asked for it and I am not going to give it to you. What I am going to do is tell you that there is a clause in it and what shape it is, and then I am going to go back to my table. It gives two named people a look at one thing out of three in that office's custody room. Somebody of that office stands in the room the whole time. An hour is named on it and that hour is the end of it and not the middle. And nothing goes out of that room in any hand, and it says so in those words, and it is a clause about a door and a man standing at the door and not about an opening."

"And the third of the three."

"Is not on it," said the man of about fifty, "and is not named on it, and there is a claim on it in a book in this office that has been in a book in this office since the December before last and I have never seen anybody come in here to take it back, and I am not going to be the one who starts that this month."

He went back to his table and did not look up again.

Calder stood at that counter for the length of a pipe and then some. The clerk of about sixty was in that room, twenty-two years in it and owed a quarter and with no book and no power, and he had not said anything and had not been asked anything, and at about ten to six Calder asked him one question, out loud, once.

"If the Navy cannot own the sea by force, is the Crown going to try to own history by making it legally singular?"

The clerk of about sixty put his thumb down on the edge of the board on that wall, which is a list and is not a schedule and binds nobody in that harbor, and he looked at the list for a while.

"I have not got a book for that," he said. "I want to say that straight, because you have asked it in a room and there is a man in it, and I have been in this room twenty-two years and I have never once been asked a question I could answer and I am not going to start on a Sunday. And I am not going to say yes to it and I am not going to say no to it, and I am not going to tell you what the Crown is going to do, because I have not got a book for that either and I have not got a book for what that office or any other office intends and I have never pretended to anybody that I had."

**AND THE QUESTION WAS ASKED ONCE, IN A ROOM WITH A CLERK IN IT, BY A MAN WITH NO OFFICE, AND IT NAMED NO PERSON, AND THE CLERK OF ABOUT SIXTY ANSWERED IT BY SAYING THAT HE HAD NO BOOK FOR IT, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ANSWERED IT SINCE AND IT IS NOT ANSWERED NOWHERE ELSE EITHER.**

She was on the crown of that mole at about six with the market gone off it and the light out, which it is, and has been since the seventh day of May past, and the lens has been out of it since then and oil costs what a barrel of lamp oil costs on a board in the front street and a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again.

The flat of the bench stone was bare and there was a maul on the step and a bucket with an inch of rain in it, and Calder did not stand on it and did not look at it for longer than a man looks at a step, and Mara Venn did not look at it either, and neither of them said one word about it, and it is not going into any column of any ledger in that harbor and it is not a fault and it is not going to be turned into anything today.

"Say the rest of it as a thing and not as a reason," she said. "I have had a reason off you on a mole an hour and a half ago and I am not going to have it twice, and I am not going to thank you for having it, and I am not going to forgive you for it, and the forty marks is not what I am standing here for and it is not going to be reduced by anything that happened on this mole this afternoon."

He said nothing.

"You are going to do it again," she said. "Not this. Something with a day named on a quay in it. And the day after you name it you are going to come up that hill and tell me what it is, and I am not going to ask you for it, and if you come and tell me and I do not like it then that is what happens and I will not make a scene about it and I will not stand in that shed and make a scene about it either."

"That is standing," Calder said, "and I am not going to put it in a column and I am not going to put it in a return and I am not going to say it out loud in front of anybody else."

"You are not going to put it anywhere, and I am going to be the one holding it, and that is the whole of what there is," she said. "And it is not a rescue and it is not a reward for a mole and it is not the apology. I have not got the apology yet and I am not asking you for it and if you try to give me one on this mole I will go down that slip and you will not see me for a fortnight."

"Then I will not."

"And I am not going into that shed's book and you are not going into my hand, and the two of us are not going to be a thing that a man in a room can put in one line with a figure under it, and if anybody in this harbor or any other asks me what you are to me I am going to tell them the truth, which is that you tell me before and I do not agree and neither of us is paid for the other."

"That is the whole of it," Calder said.

"That is the whole of it," said Mara Venn, "and it is not a good thing, and it is not going to be a good thing, and nobody in this harbor is going to be told about it, and I am not going to be thanked for it and you are not going to be thanked for it and if either of us starts thanking the other for it I will stop coming up that hill."
