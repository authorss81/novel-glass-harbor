# Chapter 361

*Thursday the Fifteenth of July, the Thirty-First Day of the Ninety, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Fourth Time, a Paper Carried Down That Coast in the Coal With Terms on It for a Room in Veyr, Two People Asked to Go Into It Because They Can Be Shown the Thing, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Thursday the fifteenth of July, in that shed, and at the foot of that mole, and on the crown of it.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and was a rota day, being the sixty-fourth charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on none of it by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-fourth, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, so that bag is a day and a piece of a day.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The paper came down that coast in the coal, in the inside pocket of an ordinary boat's own boy of about seventeen, who did not know what it was and was not paid and did not ask, the way the sheet of thirteen lines went up it on the Saturday.

It was folded twice and it was not sealed. On the outside, in a hand nobody in that shed knew, were six words: *for the holder of that writ*. The boy gave it to the ship's hand at about nine in the morning at the foot of the mole steps, and she carried it up the hill in her left hand and put it on the bench with nothing under it and nothing against it.

Calder Marr opened it with the woman of no office on the bench and the ship's hand at the end of it, and he read it out himself because it was addressed to him and because a paper addressed to one man ought to be read by that man before anybody else reads it for him.

It was two leaves, in a clerk's hand, and it said that an inquiry would sit in Veyr, in a room off a street behind the fish market there, on terms drawn by the Crown, and that the terms were on the second leaf, and that the two people on that coast who could be shown the thing the terms were about were himself and the woman of no office, and that they were asked to come, and that a boat would wait at Ternwatch for three days and would not wait longer.

He read the second leaf after it. The terms were seven lines, and no line in them said what anybody had done or what anybody was to answer for, and no line in them named a price and no line in them named anybody. A line somewhere in the middle of them said a clerk would sit in a room off the room and write the hour and what was said, and that what the clerk wrote would be read back and not given out.

**A paper carried down a coast in the coal is a paper carried down a coast in the coal, and it is not an order and it is not a summons, and this yard has no figure for three days of a boat waiting at Ternwatch and has not invented one.**

The woman of no office said nothing while he read. When he had finished she put her hand flat on the bench beside the two leaves and did not touch either of them.

"You are going," she said. It was not a question.

"I am going," Calder said. "The room is in Veyr. A room in Veyr is a room and not anything else, and I am not going to stand on this hill and refuse a room I have not seen."

"It asks for two," she said. "I am the other one."

"You are the other one if you go," he said. "I am not answering for you in a shed."

She looked at the two leaves for a while. The ship's hand stood at the end of the bench and said nothing, because nothing in either leaf was addressed to her and because a person who is not asked to a room does not get to say what the room is for.

"I will go," the woman of no office said at last. "Not because it asks. Because there is a thing in it that can be shown, and I am one of the two it can be shown to, and a thing that can be shown ought to be looked at by somebody who is not being paid by the person who wants it looked at."

That was all she said about going, and nobody improved on it.

---

At about eleven the man of about fifty-one of the six came down to the shed and stood in the doorway with his hat in his hand, and asked whether there was a day's work, and was told there was not.

He did not argue. He said his page was still on the crown and the wind had not taken it, and Calder said it had not, and the man nodded and went back up the mole and looked at his six names for a while and then went down to the third lay.

At about half past eleven a man of the tide gang came past with a hod and said the gang was four men short and the reeve's office had nothing on its list, and that the list was on the board in the office and was a list and not anything else, and that nobody was bound by it and nobody was going to be.

At about twelve a man of about thirty-two tried the fire party's hose at the standpipe at the end of the third lay and it was dead, and it has been dead since before the spring, and he coupled it and uncoupled it and said it was dead in the ordinary voice and went back to his work.

**The harbor authority's list is not a schedule and does not bind anybody, and the fire party's hose is dead until the spring, and the tide gang is four men short, and none of the three is a figure and none of them is entered against anybody.**

---

At about four in the afternoon the three of them sat in that shed and said what the day was, because that is the hour that shed does things, and the woman of no office said one sentence about the paper and it was not about going.

"A civilian convoy under municipal papers is not a thing this room has looked at," she said. "The notice on the wall in a different street has been there fifty-eight days yesterday and nobody in this harbor has compared it with anything. I am not comparing it now. I am saying the paper on a table in the office on the west basin is a municipal document and is not an instrument of anybody's, and a boat that goes out under it is not a vessel returning from beyond the line."

Calder wrote that down in the second column with a reason against the line and no name against it, because it was said in that shed and not in the room in Veyr and because a thing said in a shed is not a thing said in a room.

At about ten past five he went up to the mole head, because that is where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it is out on the fifteenth of July as it was on the fourteenth of May, and a light costs oil, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first day of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and both dates are on the board.

The small steam tender of about ninety tons was still at the outer water at about ten past five, with two lines out and a lamp not yet at her masthead, and nobody in that harbor had been aboard her and nobody had spoken to anybody in her, and the young man of about twenty-three was in her own wheelhouse with a telescope in his hand and had not hailed.

On the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins and about four feet from a page with six names on it in a hand nobody corrected, the two objects of nobody's are three hundred and ninety-nine days old, and one of them has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and it has not been lashed back, and neither has moved.

The mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it was not looked at on the said day.

**And both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, that a room in a city has asked for two people by name and that six men in this harbor have no boat and none of them was asked for anything on the said day.**

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the fifteenth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Thursday was a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that the cistern was charged and walked on none, the sixty-fourth, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that a paper of two leaves came down that coast in the coal in the inside pocket of an ordinary boat's own boy of about seventeen, who was not paid and did not know what it was, and that it says an inquiry will sit in Veyr in a room off a street behind the fish market there on terms drawn by the Crown, and that the terms are seven lines on the second leaf and that no line in them names anybody and that no line in them says what anybody had done or what anybody was to answer for and that a clerk would sit in a room off the room and write the hour and what was said and that it would be read back and not given out, and that the two people on that coast who can be shown the thing are the holder of that writ and a woman of no office, and that both are asked to come and a boat will wait at Ternwatch for three days; and that the holder said out loud he is going and that a room in Veyr is a room and he will not refuse one he has not seen, and that the woman of no office said she will go because a thing that can be shown ought to be looked at by somebody who is not being paid by the person who wants it looked at; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-eight weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a civilian convoy under municipal papers is not a vessel returning from beyond the line, said in that shed by a woman of no office and written down with the reason against the line that it was said in a shed and not in a room; and that the tide gang is four men short and the reeve's office has nothing on its list and the list is not a schedule and binds nobody; and that the fire party's hose was tried at the standpipe at the end of the third lay and is dead and has been dead since before the spring; and that about eleven people of that harbor cannot read the notice, being a count of people and not a figure, and that nobody in that yard is to tell any of them anything and nobody was sent and nothing was written; and that nine communities hold the records one in each, and six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the eighty-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a paper carried down a coast in the coal asking two people to come to a room in a city is not an order and is not a summons and is not a finding against anybody; and that a man saying he will go into a room he has not seen is not courage and is not a refusal and is not anything but going; and that a boat waiting three days at Ternwatch is not a figure and has no price against it here because this yard does not know what a day of that boat is worth and is not going to invent one.*
