# Chapter 381

*Wednesday the Fourth of August, the Fifty-First Day of the Ninety, a Lighter of About Thirty Tons Broken Up in Nine Foot of Water Against the Wall of That Quay With Nothing Landed Out of Her at Any Hour, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In at a Berth, Four Masters Asked One Question in One Morning and Four Answers That Were All Things and Not One of Them a Man, and a List With No Name on It*

Wednesday the fourth of August, at that quay, and on the third lay, and in that shed.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth in that harbor, and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall of that market is the same figure and was put up by a woman of about fifty-eight on Sunday the twenty-fifth of July past and is not this yard's figure and cannot be taken off it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she came up that hill at about nine in the morning and sat on the bench at the back of that shed and asked nothing at all, which is the eleventh time she has come up that hill since she came down that coast. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-nine days and the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for eighty-one days and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

The lighter of about thirty tons was broken up at that quay on the Wednesday, and she was broken up in nine foot of water against the wall, which is not the four foot at the edge of the fairway and is not the same four foot, and there was a man of about thirty on a staging against her side and a man of about twenty-five in the water to his waist and a man of about thirty-eight on the wall with a saw.

They had her frames out by about eleven in the morning. They came up out of the water one at a time, a frame at a time, and set them on the wall along her length, and the whole of what came out of her went onto that wall and nothing came off it, and nothing was landed out of her at any hour, and there was no sack and no barrel and no chest, and the two tons of flour in her bottom had gone to a paste in her bilge weeks ago and was pumped over the side and went down the harbour and nobody in that harbor saw it go and nobody in that harbor had a figure for it.

The man of about fifty-three who had been in her was on the wall at about nine in the morning and he did not help and he did not say one word to anybody, and nobody said one word to him, and he went down the quay at about eleven and did not come back that day.

**And there is no figure against that lighter in any book in that harbor, and no claim on her, and no sack of anything came ashore out of her at any hour on any day, and the man of about fifty-three in her asked for nothing and was given nothing and was not paid and was not asked to pay, and none of that is a return and none of it is anybody's loss and the wall of that quay is the only place in that harbor where the whole of what was in her can be seen, being eight frames and a keelson and nothing else.**

---

He went down the third lay at about one in the afternoon with a leaf and nothing else in his hand, and he asked four men one question, and the question was the same question in the same words four times.

He said it to a master of about fifty-five of the third lay first, at his own hatch, with his own boat's gear round him.

"What does your boat want, before she can lie in company with another boat for a day out and a day back."

The master of about fifty-five did not ask him what he was doing it for.

"A spare warp," he said. "Every boat in this harbour has the one warp she carries and no second one, and the day a boat cuts her warp is the day she is in the water with the company coming down on her, and everybody behind her knows it before she has been in the water a minute. I have had that happen once and I did not lose the boat and I have not stopped thinking about it."

That went on the leaf as an answer and not as a want.

The second was a master of about forty of the second lay, who had come down the third lay to look at a pump that was not for sale and was asked the question standing up on a stone.

"A man who can read a clock," he said. "Not a watch. A watch is a man's own and a man in the water is not going to be told the hour by his own watch, and I have watched a company come in with the tide an hour off because every man in her had a watch and no two watches in that company were the same watch. I want a clock on a post on whatever quay they are going to, and a man standing at it, and I do not care which quay."

The third answer came from a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons, who had no municipal papers of any kind and had said in the open on the twenty-eighth of July past that he came to tell and not to ask, and who had gone out on the Saturday of that week on a breach of the first clause and had come in under the sixth with no paper at all.

"Somebody ashore at the other end who is awake at the hour," he said. "That is the whole of what I have. Not a man I know and not a man I would trust with anything. Just somebody at a quay at four in the morning who is awake. A dog would do it and a dog has never once failed to be where it is supposed to be, and I have been in eleven boats in the last year and not one of them had a dog in her and one of them had a man in her who was asleep."

He said it and went up the third lay, and he did not ask to be put down for anything, and he has not asked to be put down for anything since the twenty-eighth of July past and is not going to.

The fourth answer came from a shipwright of about sixty in the front street, with a bench in his own front door and a strip of leather in his hand, and it was not an answer at all.

"No," he said. "You have come to the wrong bench with the wrong question. When I am asked what a boat wants, I am asked for a boat, and a boat is a hull, and I will tell you what a hull costs this month and you can go and be poor somewhere else with it. If what you want is a boat that can lie alongside another boat for a day and a day back, then what you want is a boat that has been kept, and I do not know how anybody prices being kept, and I have never had a customer who wanted to buy it, and I am not going to write it on a board and chalk a day beside it."

**AND A SHIPWRIGHT OF ABOUT SIXTY IN THE FRONT STREET OF THAT HARBOR TOLD A MAN HE HAD COME TO THE WRONG BENCH WITH THE WRONG QUESTION, AND HE SAID IT IN ONE SENTENCE AND WENT BACK TO HIS LEATHER, AND HE IS ONE OF ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE OF THAT TOWN WHO CANNOT READ THE NOTICE, THAT COUNT BEING TWO PEOPLE COUNTING SEPARATELY AND ADDING, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED HAS ASKED HIM ONE WORD ABOUT IT AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

---

The leaf came back to the shed at about four in the afternoon and went on the bench stone, and the ship's hand read it standing up.

"Three things," she said. "A warp I can price. A clock and a man to stand at it I can price, and I will price it out loud before anybody agrees to it and the price is a man's day and not a shilling, and the man is not yours to buy. Somebody awake at four in the morning on a quay you have not chosen yet is not a thing that can be bought at any figure, and that is not a fault in it. It is what it is."

"Read it back."

"Three things and no men. And the shipwright is right and you have got the question the wrong way round, and you know you have, because you have written down what four men said they want and not one of those four is going to be on the water when the water is not there." She put her finger on the last line. "This one is a person. A person is not a line on a leaf. If you put a person on that leaf you will have a list of men, and you have refused two lists of men inside a fortnight and you may do what you like with the reasons you gave for them, and I am not going to be the one who hands you the third."

"I am not going to put a name on it," Calder said. "I have said the reason out loud twice this month and I am not going to say it a third time on a different day and in a different voice. A convoy is no use at all except in the water somebody is looking after between it and the next tide. That is on the face of it and it is not a secret and it is not a discovery and it is not mine. It is the first thing anybody in this harbour has ever been able to say about it, and I have said it because four men answered a question I asked, and one of them told me I had come to the wrong bench."

"Then the list is three things," Nessa said, "and it is a good list, and it is on that bench stone now, and a list of things is not a price and is not a claim and does not go against anybody's name."

"And on Wednesday the next," Calder said, "I am going to ask the same question again on the same lay, and I will not write any of the answers down on the same leaf."

**AND A LIST OF THREE THINGS LIES ON A BENCH STONE IN THAT SHED WITH NO NAME ON IT AND NO FIGURE ON IT AND NO MAN ON IT, AND IT IS THE FIRST LIST ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS EVER MADE ABOUT BEING IN COMPANY WITH ANOTHER BOAT, AND A MAN WROTE IT DOWN AND A MAN PRICED TWO THIRDS OF IT OUT LOUD AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE OF A LAY SAID THAT HALF OF IT CANNOT BE BOUGHT, AND THE FIGURE THAT CAME INTO THAT YARD ON THE SAID DAY IS THE FIGURE ON A SLATE IN A MARKET AND NOT A PRICE FOR ANYTHING THAT WAS ASKED FOR.**

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a lighter broken up in nine foot of water against the wall of a quay is not a salvage and is not a claim and is not a figure anybody is owed, and that nothing landed out of her is nothing landed and not a delivery and not a refusal; and that a list of three things with no man on it is not a crew and is not a muster and is not a way of being fair to anybody, and that three things that can be bought and one that cannot is not a fleet and is not the beginning of one; and that a shipwright who tells you the question is the wrong way round is not a man refusing to help and is not a man who has no answer and is not owed anything and was not asked.*
