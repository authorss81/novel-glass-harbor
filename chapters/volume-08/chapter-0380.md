# Chapter 380

*Tuesday the Third of August, the Fiftieth Day of the Ninety, the Forty-First Week and a Wage Not Paid, a Leaf Read Back in Full With the Count on the Face of It and the Definition of Every Figure on the Face of It, a Woman of No Office Asking One Question About Seven Thousand, the Hundred and Fifth Line and the Column Not Closed, and a Mole Head at Ten Past Five in the Evening With a Light Not Lit and Two Lights Off the Point That Belong to Nobody in That Harbor*

Tuesday the third of August, in that shed at about four in the afternoon, and on that mole at about ten past five in the evening.

**The said Tuesday was the forty-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid, and the column did not move, and there are twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in it with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, so that forty-one weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that bag on the thirty-first and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it on the twenty-seventh, and the two of those are four days apart and they are not the same four hundred and thirty-six pence of anything.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that shed at about four for the reading and for nothing else, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and it is the tenth time she has come up that hill since she came down that coast, being the twenty-second of July for his leaf, the twenty-third for her answer in writing, the twenty-fourth for the first reading, the twenty-sixth, the twenty-eighth for four things said out loud, the twenty-ninth, the thirty-first, the first of August, and this one for the reading. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it and the woman of no office was on the bench at the back.

**The first column of that leaf has one hundred and twelve entries in it at the hour of the read-back and one hundred and thirteen at the close.** There were one hundred and three of them at the close of Saturday the twenty-fourth day of July past and ten have gone in since, one for every day from Sunday the twenty-fifth to Tuesday the third of August, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own entry is the tenth and makes one hundred and thirteen, and one hundred and twelve of them were in it at the hour of this reading.

**The second column has four hundred and thirty-seven lines in it at the hour of the read-back and four hundred and thirty-nine at the close.** Three hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twenty-fourth day of July past. Three of the day's entries go in before the reading of that day and two after it, and that is the shape of that shed's habit and is not a rule, and no reader in any office can check it, and that is what the second column is for. Ten days of that shape is fifty, and three hundred and eighty-nine and fifty is four hundred and thirty-nine, and three of this Tuesday's went in before the reading and make four hundred and thirty-seven at the hour of it. **The said figure is that book of that yard's own and is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's one hundred and twelve and the fourth column's one hundred and four, at the hour of this read-back, are counts of entries and of lines, and a day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count both of them. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments.**

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The three nots standing against the eighth day of June past were not asked about on any of the ten days and are not going to be asked about by anybody in that crew, and there has been a fourth week now in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them. He was not on the stone on any of these ten days and was put on nothing.**

**And the fourth column has one hundred and four lines in it at the hour of the read-back and one hundred and five at the close, and the hundred and fifth is the last line in that book, and there is nothing under it, and the column is not closed and nobody has closed it and nobody in this yard is going to.**

Then the woman of no office asked one question, and it was the only question asked in that shed that day, and it was about a piece of paper two inches by an inch.

"Seven thousand," she said. "Is it money."

"I do not know," Calder said. "Nobody on the water said. There is no name on it and nothing else on it and it is not written out in pence and it is not written out in marks and shillings and pence, and I am not going to guess at a figure in front of the two of you on the strength of a thing a boy of about twenty-six wrote on a piece of paper and then gave to a man in a boat."

"Then it is not money in this shed," she said, "and that is a decision and not a finding, and I am going to write it in the second column with the reason against the line, and the reason is that nobody said and that nobody is going to say it in a room."

"That is the whole of it," the ship's hand said.

---

At about ten past five he went up to the mole head, because that is where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it is out on the third of August as it was on the fourteenth of May, and a light costs oil, and the last figure anybody in that harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted to a woman on the first day of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and both of those dates are on a board in the front street and the February figure has not been taken off it because a figure put on that board cannot be taken off it again.

There is no tender at the outer water of that harbor. She lay there seventeen days from the tenth of July to the twenty-sixth of July with two lines out and a lamp at her masthead from the twentieth, and no officer came ashore and no paper came ashore and nobody hailed anybody and nobody in that harbor went aboard her or spoke to anybody in her on any of the seventeen days, and on the morning of the twenty-seventh she was at the head of eight boats inside the line with the young man of about twenty-three on her roof, and nobody in that harbor was told what she was for and this yard did not ask and is not going to. That is twenty-five days about that water and eight of them inside the line.

There are two lights out at about a mile. They were not there on the evening of the twenty-eighth of July and they were first seen on the evening of Sunday the first of August, and they have not moved in two days, and nobody in that harbor has hailed them and nobody in that harbor knows whose they are, and nobody in that harbor is going to write a day against them.

The notice of the seventeenth day of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-eight days and has not been taken down and has not been compared with anything by anybody in that harbor, and the fifth clause of it is a breach and the sixth clause of it is free, and the sixth clause has been the reason that harbor is open since the seventeenth of May, and that line was written by an office that did not write it for that harbor. There are two older naval notices further along the same wall and neither of them has been touched either.

On the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins and about four feet from a page of six names on it in a hand nobody corrected, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and eighteen days old, and one of them has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and it has not been lashed back, and neither has moved and nobody has asked whose either is.

The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eighty-fourth night, and it adds one a night.

The second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it was not looked at on any of the ten days, and the mark four hundred yards off it is of a make nobody in that harbor makes, and the season of both is nine marks and neither has been paid and neither is going to be, and the use of the first was taken once on the eighteenth day of June past and closed, four figures and no fifth at any hour of any day, ladder in another man's book, nobody to explain it, not Fisk.

Eight marks and a day of a boat has been owing to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May past, and the chest is shut and it has not been argued and it has not been asked in eighty days, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page in this shed or on a page anywhere.

**AND A BAG WENT OUT ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH AND CAME BACK THE SAME ON THE THIRTY-FIRST AND A TOW CARRYING FLOUR CAME DOWN A FAIRWAY ON THE SAME HOUR AND WAS PUT ACROSS IT BY A MAN WITH TWO FOOT OF WATER UNDER HIM, AND A BOARD IN A BOAT CARRIES A WORD AT THE HEAD OF IT AND ELEVEN MARKS IN A COLUMN AND A DATE CUT INTO THE TIMBER ABOVE THE HEAD OF IT, AND A LIGHTER OF ABOUT THIRTY TONS IS LYING AT THAT QUAY WITH ABOUT A FOOT AND FOUR INCHES OF WATER IN HER AND NO SACK OF ANYTHING HAS COME ASHORE OUT OF HER, AND A SLIP OF PAPER WITH SEVEN THOUSAND ON IT IS IN A DRAWER WITH A SHEET IN IT AND HAS NO COLUMN HEAD AND NO NAME, AND NONE OF THOSE IS A FIGURE THAT THIS SHED OWES ANYBODY AND NONE OF THEM CANCELS ANOTHER AND NEITHER OF THEM IS A REASON TO DO ANYTHING TOMORROW, AND ALL OF THAT IS ON A LEAF AND THE COLUMN IS NOT CLOSED.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the third day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Tuesday was the forty-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and not a wipe, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and the column did not move, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty-one weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that the whole of what came into that yard in the ten days ended on the thirty-first of July past and the whole of what went out of it went on the twenty-seventh, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence in and one hundred and sixty-eight pence out, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence is one mark and eightpence and is a boat with a man in her for a day and is the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wall came down bare twice in the ten days, being the sixty-fourth on Monday the twenty-sixth of July and the sixty-fifth on the second of August, and went up both times at the angle since the twelfth; and that the cistern was charged three times and walked on none, being the sixty-seventh on the twenty-sixth, the sixty-eighth on Thursday the twenty-ninth and the sixty-ninth on the second of August, the rota being a Monday and a Thursday, and the thirtieth of July being a Friday and the third of August being a Tuesday and neither of them being a rota day, and the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that a lighter of about thirty tons with about a foot and four inches of water in her and about two tons of wet flour in her bottom is lying at that quay and no sack has been landed out of her and no figure of anything has been written against her; and that seven boats of that tow of eight are lying off the point at about a mile with two lights, which were first seen on the evening of Sunday the first and have not moved in two days.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands; and that a day was lost on Thursday the first day of July past to a thing that has no name in it, with a reason against the line and no name in it and no figure in it, and that no day of any week before that one may be entered against the same line afterwards and none has been, and the line is not to be added to; and that the third channel found on an adrift buoy on the twelfth of July with a borrowed instrument is not measured and the instrument went back up that coast on a Tuesday and has not come down, and that nothing in the ten days ending on the said day was measured that was not measured with an instrument in a hand; and that a woman of no office asked at about four in the afternoon on the said day whether seven thousand was money and was told nobody on the water said and that nobody is going to say it in a room, and that it is entered as a decision of that shed and not as a finding, with the reason against the line that nobody said; and that a slip of paper about two inches by an inch is in a drawer in that shed with a sheet in it and has no column head and no name on it and has not been shown to any room and has not been read out of that shed; and that the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked in eighty days and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page; and that the two lights at about a mile are nobody's and nobody in that harbor is going to write a day against them; and that the tender of about ninety tons lay at the outer water seventeen days and has been at the head of eight boats inside the line eight days, and that is twenty-five days about that water, and that nobody in that harbor has been told what she is for.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and fifth line:*

*What it is not: — that eleven marks in a column in a board in a boat are eleven hulls and are not eleven men, and that two tons of wet flour in the bottom of a lighter at a quay is not a sum of money and is not anybody's claim, and that those two facts are the whole of what that shed knows about a word on a piece of paper, and both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other; and that seven thousand on a slip with no name on it is not a figure this yard can pay and is not a figure this yard has been given, and that the leaving of it in a drawer is not a decision and is not a holding and is not a custody; and that a hundred and four lines in a column that is not closed is not a case and is not an argument and is not a verdict, and that the hundred and fifth line is the last line in that book tonight and there is nothing under it and nobody in that shed is going to write under it.*
