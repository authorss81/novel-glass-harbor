# Chapter 383

*Friday the Sixth of August, the Fifty-Third Day of the Ninety, a Counter in the Front Street That This Yard Went Past and Did Not Stop At and a Bonesetter at His Own Door Who Was Not Asked a Thing, the Same Question Asked a Fifth Time and a Fifth Answer That Was a Man and Was Not Put on the Leaf, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Friday the sixth of August, in that shed, and on the third lay, and in the front street.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth in that harbor is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and this yard has not had one of those since Wednesday past and is not going to have another this week, and the cheapest thing in that harbor has not moved off its board since the first of June and the mole-head light is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about eleven until about one. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for eighty-three days, the chest is shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

The front street of that harbor is one street long and the chandler's counter is in it, and the chandler is about fifty, and his wife does the counting behind him, and he has said twice in that street that he has nothing to say about the notice and he is not going to be asked a third time by anybody, including this yard.

Calder went up that street at about half past ten in the morning and went past the counter.

He did not stop. He did not slow down at it. The chandler was behind the counter with his back half turned and a paper in his hand and his wife at the far end of the same counter with a slate, and neither of them looked up, and the man who went past was going to a door up the street and not to that counter, and the holder of that writ went to the door.

**And that is the whole of the said Friday in that street, and no figure was said out loud in that street on the said day, and the figure on the board over that counter is the figure it was on the first of June with the February figure above it and both dates on the board, and neither of them moved, and nobody in that street asked the man who sells it anything and he was not asked a third time.**

---

The bonesetter of about fifty has a door and a room behind it and a smell that goes out into the street when the door is open, and the door was open on the said Friday at about eleven in the morning because the day was warm and there was nobody in the room.

The man of about forty-four of the six was on the step of the bonesetter's door with his left hand out of the binding, and the top of two fingers of it was gone since the twelfth of July past, and the hand was stiff across them and pink and not a bonesetter's hand and never had been, and the shipwright of about sixty had set it on the Monday the twelfth with a strip off a leech and a needle in about forty minutes and the bill of five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, had been paid out of that man's own hand in coin and said twice and not a third time.

The bonesetter looked at the hand from inside his own doorway and did not come out of it and did not hold out his own.

"I have told that hand what it is," he said. "A man came on a Saturday in July and I told it, and I told it in front of two other people on a step in this street, and that is twice. I am not going to say it a third time to a person who has not asked me and to a person who is not the one I said it to."

"He is not asking you," Calder said.

"I know he is not asking me. That is why I am saying it and not waiting." The bonesetter shut the door to about a foot and then stopped and left it where it was. "You go up and down that street every day of the week and you have never once brought anybody to my door. I have noticed. I do not know what it is you are doing and I am not asking and I would rather it went on being a thing I did not ask about."

"Then it goes on being a thing you did not ask about," Calder said.

"That is all right," the bonesetter said, and shut the rest of the door.

**AND NOBODY TOOK THE HAND OF THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR TO A BONESETTER ON THE SAID DAY AND NOBODY TOOK IT ANYWHERE ELSE EITHER, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAID NOTHING TO EITHER OF THEM, AND HE IS BACK ON THE WATER AS A BOAT'S MAN AND NOT A MASTER AND WAS NOT PAID AND WAS NOT ASKED TO PAY, AND THE SIXTY-SIX PENCE HE PAID FOR IT IN JULY CAME OUT OF HIS OWN HAND AND DID NOT COME OUT OF THAT YARD AND IS NOT PAID TWICE.**

---

He went back down the third lay at about one in the afternoon and asked the question a fifth time, and he asked it in the same words, and the fifth man was a master of about forty-one of the third lay with an anchor over his own stern and two of his own crew on a coil of line.

"What does your boat want, before she can lie in company with another boat for a day out and a day back."

The master of about forty-one thought about it and then said it as one sentence and did not soften it.

"Somebody in this harbor who is there on the day."

"Go on."

"That is the answer and I have got nothing after it." He turned the anchor stock over in his hands. "I have got the spare warp you came and got off the man of fifty-five, and I have got a clock question off the man of forty, and I have got a dog question off the thirty-eight, and I have got a lead off somebody. And every one of those four is on your board. And I have been in company three times in my life and twice it went wrong, and it went wrong both times because there was a day when somebody who was going to be there was not there, and the boats did not know it and did not stop and did not turn round, and they found out at about four in the afternoon in about a mile of water where nobody had told them to look."

Calder did not write it down.

"You are not writing that one."

"No."

"Why not, when it is the best of the five."

"Because it is a person," Calder said, "and a leaf in this shed with a person's name or a person's job on it is a list of men, and I have refused two of those inside a fortnight and I gave my reasons for them out loud and I am not going to change my reasons because the fifth answer is the one I wanted. What I will do is write down that a fifth man was asked and what he said and that it is not on this leaf, and put the reason against that line, and the reason is that the day a yard writes down who turns up, that yard has a list, and a list is the boat, and everybody not on it is not asked and everybody on it has a figure."

The master of about forty-one put the anchor stock down and looked at him.

"You have understood that wrong on purpose."

"I have understood it in a shed with three hundred and seventy-two pence in a bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence," Calder said, "and I have understood it since the twenty-sixth of July, and I have been wrong about it before and I have been wrong about it in front of about forty people, and I am not going to be wrong about it again quietly in a doorway."

**AND A FIFTH MAN OF THAT THIRD LAY WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION IN THE SAME WORDS AND GAVE THE ANSWER THAT THE WHOLE OF A COMPANY OF BOATS HANGS ON, AND IT WAS NOT PUT ON A LEAF, AND NO FIGURE AND NO NAME AND NO JOB WENT AGAINST ANY PERSON IN THAT HARBOR ON THE SAID DAY, AND A LIST OF FOUR THINGS IS ON A BENCH STONE IN THAT SHED WITH A FIFTH MAN'S SENTENCE IN THE SECOND COLUMN UNDER IT AND THE REASON AGAINST THE LINE.**

---

*First column, entered on Friday the sixth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Friday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that the holder went up the front street of that harbor at about half past ten in the morning and went past the chandler's counter without stopping at it, and that the chandler of about fifty was behind that counter with his wife doing the counting and neither of them looked up, and that no figure was said out loud in that street on the said day and nobody in that street asked the man who sells it anything and he was not asked a third time, and that the figure on the board over that counter is the figure it was on the first of June with the February figure above it and both dates on the board and neither moved; and that a bonesetter of about fifty had his door open at about eleven in the morning and a man of about forty-four of the six was on his step with the top of two fingers of his left hand gone since the twelfth of July past and the hand stiff across them and not a bonesetter's hand and never had been, and that the said bonesetter looked at the hand from inside his own doorway and did not come out and did not hold out his own hand; and that the said bonesetter said he had told that hand what it is twice, once on a Saturday in July in front of two other people on a step in that street, and that he was not going to say it a third time to a person who had not asked him and was not the one he said it to; and that the holder said the man was not asking him and that that was why the bonesetter was saying it and not waiting, and that the said bonesetter said the holder goes up and down that street every day of the week and has never once brought anybody to his door, and that he did not know what the holder was doing and was not asking and would rather it went on being a thing he did not ask about, and that the holder said then it goes on being a thing he did not ask about, and that the said bonesetter shut the rest of the door; and that nobody took that hand to a bonesetter on the said day and nobody took it anywhere else either; and that the bill of five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, for setting it on the twelfth of July past was paid out of that man's own hand in coin, was said twice and not a third time, and did not come out of that yard and is not paid twice; and that the holder went down the third lay at about one in the afternoon and asked the same question in the same words a fifth time of a master of about forty-one of that lay with an anchor over his own stern; and that the said master answered somebody in this harbor who is there on the day, and said that is the answer and he had nothing after it, and said he has been in company three times in his life and that twice it went wrong and that it went wrong both times because there was a day when somebody who was going to be there was not there, and that the boats did not know it and did not stop and did not turn round, and that they found out at about four in the afternoon in about a mile of water where nobody had told them to look; and that the holder did not write that answer down, and said the reason out loud, being that the day a yard writes down who turns up that yard has a list, and a list is the boat, and everybody not on it is not asked and everybody on it has a figure; and that the said master said the holder had understood that wrong on purpose, and that the holder said he had understood it in a shed with three hundred and seventy-two pence in a bag and had been wrong about it before in front of about forty people and was not going to be wrong about it again quietly in a doorway; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty-one weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourth is not entered here again and stands; and that a chandler of about fifty who has said twice that he has nothing to say about the notice has not been asked a third time by anybody in that street or in that shed, and that a counter a man walks past is not a refusal of anybody's and is not a courtesy and is not going in anywhere as though it were one; and that a bonesetter who has said a thing twice about a hand is not a man withholding a service and is not owed a visit, and that a hand out of a binding is not an offer and is not a claim; and that the fifth answer of the five, being that somebody in that harbor has to be there on the day, is not on the list of things and is in this column with the reason against the line, and that the reason is that a yard which writes down who turns up has a list, and a list is the boat; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay keeps the whole fouling ladder of that harbor in his own book and was not asked about a hand lead on the said day, and was not asked about it on the fifth either, and is not going to be asked about it by anybody in that crew.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a counter a man goes past is not a quarrel and is not a debt and is not a figure, and that a chandler not asked a third time has not been protected and is not owed a visit; and that a bonesetter's door and a hand out of a binding are not a debt between anybody and are not anybody's business; and that a man who gives the best of five answers and is not thanked for it and is not written down has not been refused and has not been used, and that the best of five answers being the one that is not on the leaf is not a fault in the leaf.*
