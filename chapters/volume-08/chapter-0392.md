# Chapter 392

*Sunday the Fifteenth of August, the Sixty-Second Day of the Ninety, a Sheet Carried Round Seven Boats at Their Own Berths and Every Man in Them Named as the Man Who Owned Him Said It and Spelled as He Said It, One Master Who Cannot Read and Who Asked for the Whole of It and Then for His Own Men Twice, One Man in One Boat Who Would Not Have His Name Written and Whose Master Wrote It Anyway Because the Man Is Aboard, a Second Warp Bought Out of One Man's Own Pocket and Not Entered Against the Sheet, and Nobody in That Harbor Who Could Not Read Asked to Be Put on It*

Sunday the fifteenth of August, in that shed, and on the third lay, and on the second.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a sheet is going round seven boats at their own berths with a name against every man in them, and no figure is on it, and no figure went against the seven who carry it, and the three men who said no on the fourth of this month and on the day before it are not on it and are not going on it and have not been asked again.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on that hill at about eleven in the morning and looked at the sheet on the bench stone and asked one question about it and was answered and did not ask another. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-two days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

He went round the seven berths on foot with the sheet flat on his own forearm and a pencil behind his ear and no slate and no second paper, and the order of the seven was the order of the sheet, being one to eleven, and the four blanks in the numbers were the four men who had said no and they stayed blank all day.

Every name went on as the man who owned the boat said it, and where a man said a name in a way that did not sit down on the page Calder wrote it as he said it and did not ask him a second time about the spelling, and one master of about thirty of the third lay said a surname twice differently in two hours and Calder wrote it both times and left the two of them there side by side without a mark between them, and the master of about thirty looked at it and said that was right and went away.

The master's own hand went at the foot of that sheet on the Saturday and the hour went against it, and what went on the Sunday was the names and the lines, and the sheet was not folded and not rolled and was carried in a man's own inside pocket between berths with a pebble on it, and the pebble went back in the pocket of a man of about twenty-nine of the second lay at half past four and is not in this shed and was never in it.

**A MASTER OF ABOUT THIRTY OF THAT THIRD LAY READ HIS OWN MEN BACK IN ONE GO, ALL OF THEM, WITHOUT STOPPING, AND SAID THAT HE HAD NOT HAD HIS OWN CREW WRITE THEMSELVES DOWN IN NINE YEARS AND THAT HE WAS NOT AWARE IT COULD BE DONE IN AN AFTERNOON.** He is a man of about thirty and he does not read the sheet and did not pretend to; he had it read to him, all of it, and he said: "That is right, and you have got one of them wrong and it is the second one, and he is on my after thwart and he will tell you so, and I am not going to."

The second one was corrected in front of the man himself, who was a man of about twenty-two and who said his own name twice before Calder had it right and then said that was the one, and Calder wrote what he said and nothing else, and the master of about thirty said thank you to nobody in particular.

**ONE MAN IN ONE OF THE SEVEN BOATS WOULD NOT HAVE HIS NAME WRITTEN DOWN, AND THE MASTER OF THAT BOAT WROTE IT WITH HIS OWN HAND BECAUSE THE MAN IS ABOARD, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR KNOWS THAT MAN'S NAME BUT HIS MASTER.**

"He will not have it down," the master said. "He has not said why and I did not ask him and I am not going to come up here and ask him, because I have been with him nine years and I know what I get if I ask."

"Then I will leave the line blank and put his name against you and nobody will know whose it is."

"No. Write it," the master said. "He is aboard on Saturday. A line with nothing against it is a hole and a hole in a sheet like this is a question somebody will ask him in a room in a month, and he has not got the answers to that room the same as you have." Calder wrote it and the master looked at it and did not say it out loud and went back to his boat, and the man himself was not asked anything on the said day by anybody, and he does not know to this hour that his name is on a sheet in a pocket in a boat of about twenty-six tons, and nobody in that shed is going to tell him.

**A MASTER OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX OF THE SECOND LAY, WHO CANNOT READ AND SAID SO BEFORE HE SAID YES, HAD THE WHOLE OF THE SHEET READ TO HIM AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND ASKED FOR HIS OWN MEN TO BE READ BACK TO HIM ALONE, AND WAS READ BACK TO HIM ALONE, AND ASKED FOR IT AGAIN AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS READ BACK TO HIM AGAIN.**

"Again," he said, the second time, and Calder read it again, and did not improve on the first reading, and did not say that he had read it once already, and did not hurry it, and the man of about thirty-six sat on a capstan in the sun with his hand flat on his own knee and followed it as far as he could by the sound of the words going past.

"That is the same," he said, when it was done. "You have said the same thing twice, and that is the first time anybody has read me the same thing twice."

"It will be read to you a third time when it is signed on Friday, and it will be the third time and not a favour," Calder said.

"Then it will be the third time and I will be here for it," said the master of about thirty-six.

---

**THE FOUR THINGS THAT CAN BE BOUGHT ARE ON A LEAF AND THE FIFTH THING THAT CANNOT IS NOT ON IT, AND WHAT THE SEVEN BOATS HAVE AND HAVE NOT GOT WAS SAID OUT LOUD AT EACH BERTH AND WAS NOT WRITTEN ON THE SHEET AND NO FIGURE WENT AGAINST ANY BOAT.**

The master of about fifty-five of that third lay brought the second warp out of his own fore hatch in a bundle with a piece of twine round it and put it down on the gunwale of Calder's boat.

"Bought on Friday off the west quay out of my own hand and it cost me what it cost me and I have not got the figure written down anywhere and I am not going to write it down for you," he said. "You are not having that figure on your sheet and you are not having it on that leaf either, and if you put it there I will take the warp back aboard and I will not be in company with you on the Saturday."

"It will not go on either. There is no figure on that sheet at all."

"Then I will tell you what it is for and then I will stop talking about money," said the master of about fifty-five, "and it is for a Saturday, because I have been in company three times and in two of them a man cut his warp and the company came down on him and he went round in a sea with nobody's hand on him, and one warp is one warp and a second warp is the difference between a bad Saturday and a Saturday, and that is all it is and it cost me my own money and I have not got anything back for it and I am not going to."

A master of about forty of the second lay had a man in her who can read a clock and not a watch and said so. A master of about forty-one of the third lay had a hand lead and a line and somebody who can use one and said so. A master of about thirty-eight of the third lay said he had nobody awake ashore at the other end at the hour and had not had for two years, and Calder wrote that in the second column and not on the sheet, and nobody gave him anything for saying it and he was not thanked and is not going to be.

**AND ON THE SAID DAY THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-ONE OF THAT HARBOR, WHO HAS SIX NAMES AND A BOAT ON A PIECE OF PAPER ON THE CROWN OF THAT MOLE AND A STONE IN HIS OWN POCKET, WAS NOT ASKED FOR A LINE, AND NOTHING OF HIS WENT ON THAT SHEET, AND NO FIGURE OF HIS IS IN IT AND NO BOAT WAS PROMISED TO HIM AND NO WAGE WAS PROMISED TO HIM, AND HE WAS ON THAT CROWN AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND SAID NOTHING AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR ANYTHING. AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THE THIRD LAY CAME DOWN THE HILL AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR WITH HIS BAG IN HIS HAND AND WENT PAST THE SHED AND DID NOT STOP AT IT, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED FOR ANYTHING BY ANYBODY AND WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN AND WAS NOT GOING TO BE CITED ANYWHERE BY ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR OR IN ANY OTHER, AND HE DID NOT ASK TO BE.**

---

The second column of that leaf carried two things on the said day and the first column carried the rest, and one of the two was a line about a man of about twenty-two in a boat of about thirty of that lay who would not have his name written, and the reason against that line was the reason his own master gave, being that a hole in a sheet like that is a question somebody will ask him in a room in a month and he has not got the answers to that room.

"Write that in your own words," Calder said, "and not in mine."

"His words, then," said Nessa, "and mine against them, and I am going to write that a man wrote his name in because he is aboard and that is not a favour and is not a protection and is not anything except a line."

**AND A SHEET CARRIED ROUND SEVEN BOATS IN AN AFTERNOON HAS A NAME AGAINST EVERY MAN IN THEM AND NO FIGURE AGAINST ANY BOAT AND NO FIGURE AGAINST ANY MAN, AND FOUR OF THE ELEVEN LINES ON IT ARE BLANK AND STAY BLANK, AND ONE MAN IS IN A BOAT WITH HIS NAME ON IT BECAUSE HIS MASTER WROTE IT AND NOT BECAUSE HE ASKED, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX HAD THE SAME THING READ TO HIM TWICE AND A THIRD TIME IS COMING ON FRIDAY, AND A WARP WAS BOUGHT OUT OF ONE MAN'S OWN POCKET AND ITS FIGURE IS NOWHERE, AND NONE OF THAT IS A COMPANY AND NONE OF IT IS A CREW AND THE SHEET IS IN A POCKET IN A BOAT AND NOT ON A WALL.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the fifteenth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Sunday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a sheet was carried round seven berths on foot between about nine in the morning and about half past four in the afternoon with a pebble on it out of a man's own pocket, and that the four blank lines in it stayed blank all day and are the four men who said no; and that every name went on as the man who owned the boat said it and that one surname went on twice differently as it was said and both were left standing side by side with no mark between them; and that the hand of a master of about thirty of that third lay went at the foot of the sheet and the hour against it went down on the Saturday and that on the Sunday the names and the lines went on and nothing else; and that a master of about thirty read his own men back all in one go and said he had not had his own crew write themselves down in nine years and did not know it could be done in an afternoon, and that one name of his was wrong on the sheet and was corrected in front of the man himself, being a man of about twenty-two who said his own name twice and then said that was the one; and that one man in one of the seven boats would not have his name written and his master wrote it with his own hand because the man is aboard, and that the man himself was not asked anything by anybody on the said day and does not know that his name is on that sheet; and that a master of about thirty-six of the second lay, who cannot read and said so before he said yes, had the whole of the sheet read to him at about eleven in the morning, asked for his own men to be read back to him alone and was read back to him alone, asked for it again at about half past three in the afternoon and was read back to him again, and said that it was the first time anybody had read him the same thing twice; and that a master of about fifty-five of the third lay put a second warp on the gunwale of that yard's boat, bought out of his own hand off the west quay, and said what it was for and would not have its figure written down anywhere; and that a master of about forty of the second lay has a man in her who can read a clock and not a watch, and a master of about forty-one of the third lay has a hand lead and a line and somebody who can use one, and a master of about thirty-eight of that lay said he has nobody awake ashore at the other end at the hour and has not had for two years, and that this went in the second column and not on the sheet; and that the man of about fifty-one of that harbor was not asked for a line on the said day and nothing of his went on that sheet and no figure of his is in it and no boat was promised to him and no wage was promised to him; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay came down that hill at about half past four with his bag in his hand and went past that shed and did not stop at it, and was not asked for anything and was not written down.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that a line a man will not have written is a hole in a sheet, and that a hole in a sheet of names is a question somebody will ask a man of about twenty-two in a room in a month, and that the master of that boat wrote the name because the man is aboard and that this is not a favour and is not a protection and is not anything except a line; and that a warp bought out of one man's own pocket and carried in his own fore hatch is not a contribution to a company and is not entered against the sheet and is not a figure in that shed, and that a man who says he has nobody awake ashore at the other end at the hour is not a man who is being asked for a confession and is not thanked for saying it; and that a man who cannot read asking for the same thing to be read to him twice is not a man who wants it read twice and it is a man who has not had it read to him once, and that the third reading is on Friday and is not a favour; and that a pebble on a sheet in a wind is not a seal and is not an impression and is not a mark of anybody, and that the pebble went back into a man's own pocket at half past four and was never in that shed.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and seventeenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a name written as a man said it is not a spelling of him and is not a correction of him, and that two spellings of one surname standing side by side in a man's own hand are not an error and are not to be scratched out; and that a sheet with a name against every man in seven boats is not a list of what a boat wants, and that a list of what a boat wants is not a sheet, and that both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other; and that a warp bought out of a man's own pocket and not entered is not a gift and is not a share and is not a claim on anybody and is not a figure in any book in that harbor; and that a man whose name is on a sheet because his master wrote it is not a man who has been entered and is not a man who has been listed and is the only person in that harbor who is on a piece of paper and does not know it.*
