# Chapter 396

*Thursday the Nineteenth of August, the Sixty-Sixth Day of the Ninety, the Seventy-Fourth Time the Cistern Was Charged and Walked on None of It, a Route Out He Can Draw and Cannot Take Anybody Down, a Man of About Thirty-Three Asked for a Saturday on a Thursday and Said Yes, a Mole Head Stone at Four in the Morning With Nobody Standing on It Because Nobody Had Been Asked for the Thursday, and a Sheet Up in the Front Street Since Friday That Nobody in That Harbor Put Up and That Nobody in That Harbor Can Read*

Thursday the nineteenth of August, in that shed, in the fish market, and on that mole head at four in the morning.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and was the day the cistern was charged for the seventy-fourth time at about eight and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and the top name on the rota board is still off it, and the wall came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and nobody in that harbor was paid by anybody on the said day and nobody asked anybody for anything in that harbor on the said day except one man of that harbor, who was asked on a Thursday for a Saturday and said yes, and his day and his wage and his hour are the same three things they have been since the tenth of August and there is no fourth.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed at about one in the afternoon and did not come up the hill. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-four days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-six days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

He did not go out on the Thursday. He sat in that shed from about nine until about one with the leaf open on the bench stone and a chart of the reach below the foul weighted at the corners, and he got no further with it on the said day than he had got with it on the thirteenth, and what he wrote in the second column is the whole of it.

The reach below the foul comes out a mile and a half inside the point, and there is a channel in it he has never been down, and he has three soundings off a hand lead on a leaf that a stranger with a lead could go and take again, and the two places he put on the leaf are in a man's own boat lengths and not in feet, because the man who gave them gave them that way and would not be asked to change them.

"The third channel is three iron rings on a pinger on a buoy that came adrift on the twelfth of July," he said. "It is nobody's measurement. Nobody in this harbor put it there and nobody in this harbor has been down it, and the pinger went back up that coast on the Tuesday of the thirteenth and it has not come down and there is no hand on that coast that has it. So I have a route out and I cannot take anybody down it, and if I say it in one more breath as a route out then I will be saying a thing I cannot do, and I have been caught doing that once in a month already and I am not doing it twice."

"And the man of about thirty-four of the second lay," said Nessa.

"Keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and gave me three soundings on the first of August and kept one back and told me plainly that there is one thing in that book he will not give and did not name it, and that if I asked him he would put the book in his pocket and go home. He was not asked on the first of August and he has not been asked since and he is not going to be asked by anybody in that crew before Saturday or after Saturday or on any day this month, and I am not going to be the man who teaches them that a thing can be asked for twice." He squared the chart with his own hand. "I can draw you that reach on this paper. I cannot take you down it, and a thing you can draw is not a thing you have got, and I have got three soundings and a channel on a buoy and a man who will not be asked, and that is all of it and it is the same as it was a week ago and I have not added one thing to it today."

"I have written that in the second column with the reason against the line," Nessa said, "and the reason is that no sounding was taken on this reach on this day by anybody, and that a route nobody has been down is not a passage and is not being written down as one."

---

He went up to that mole head at about four in the morning on the Thursday because there was nothing else to do at that hour and because that is where he goes.

There was nobody on the flat of the bench stone. There was a maul on the step where a man of about twenty-nine had left it in the dark the evening before, and a bucket with three inches of rainwater in it, and nothing else, and the stone was wet through in one place from about nine o'clock on that same evening.

Calder stood there for about two minutes and then went down.

He did not go back up at four in the afternoon, and he did not look for the man, and he did not ask anybody in that harbor where the man was, and nobody in that harbor was told that he had gone up there and found nothing, because there was nothing to tell. The man of about twenty-three was not asked for the Thursday. He was paid for the tenth of August past and for the seventeenth of this month, and both of those days were asked for by name on the day before them, and the nineteenth and the twentieth of this month were asked of him by nobody, and he was at his own bench with his own vice on the second lay and his fire out because there is no coal in this harbor for a private fire, and there is a garboard on the second lay open since June that nobody has wanted and that he has nine feet of, and nobody has sent for him.

**AND THE STONE AT THE MOLE HEAD AT FOUR IN THE MORNING ON THE NINETEENTH OF AUGUST HAD NOBODY ON IT, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS GOING TO BE ON IT, AND IT IS NOT AN ABSENCE AND IT IS NOT A FAULT AND IT IS NOT A LESSON, AND IT WENT IN NO COLUMN OF ANY LEDGER IN THAT SHED, AND NOBODY WAS TOLD ANYTHING ABOUT IT AT ALL.**

---

The woman of about fifty-eight told him at about half past two in the afternoon at the end stall, without being asked, in the way she has told him things.

"There is a sheet up in the front street since Friday," she said. "It is on a board and it has a seal on the top of it that I cannot read, and it has about nine lines on it in a small hand, and my man cannot read it and I cannot read it and neither of us has read it and neither of us is going to. And I have looked at it and I have seen that it is not about flour and it is not about oil, because everybody on that street has been in and out of this stall for two months knowing what this stall has not got." She put a basket down. "And there is a figure on the other side of the board where the other sheet was, so it is not a thing somebody put over a notice, it is a thing somebody put over a figure, and whoever put it there did not take the figure off first."

"Have you heard anybody say what it is about?"

"Two men at the fish counter on Saturday," she said, "and both of them said the word *register* and neither of them could tell me what a register was for. And I am not going to stand in the front street and ask a sheet about itself, and neither are you, and I have said so out loud to you first so that if you do it I shall hear about it from somebody else."

He did not go up the front street and look at it on the Thursday. He went up it at about a quarter to four the same afternoon, standing, with his hat in his hand, for about as long as it takes to read nine lines twice, and nobody in that street spoke to him while he was doing it and he spoke to nobody.

What it said, in nine lines, under a seal he did not look at a second time, was that certain work is taken out of other men's hulls in this and in every other port by concerns that keep no account of it; that a concern which takes work out of a hull and keeps no account of what it has taken is a concern whose accounts no man has ever seen; and that the Crown proposes that every such concern be entered in one register, and that a concern not entered in that register is a concern whose accounts are not to be produced to anybody.

It named no person. It named no harbor. It put no figure on it anywhere, and there was no price on it at the end, and it asked for nothing of anybody standing in that street, and it did not say that the work was stolen, and it did not say that the men in it were paid, and it did not say anything at all about a date.

**AND A NINE-LINE SHEET WITH A SEAL ON THE TOP OF IT WENT UP IN THE FRONT STREET OF THAT HARBOR ON THE FOURTEENTH OF AUGUST, WHICH IS A SATURDAY, ON THE OUTSIDE OF A FIGURE THAT WAS NOT TAKEN DOWN FIRST, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR PUT IT UP AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS TAKEN IT DOWN AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ANSWERED IT, AND IT IS TRUE ENOUGH ON THE FACE OF IT THAT NOBODY WHO READS IT CAN GO UP THE STREET AND SAY IT IS A LIE, AND HE READ IT TWICE AT A QUARTER TO FOUR ON THE THURSDAY AND SAID NOTHING AT ALL ABOUT IT OUT LOUD TO THE STREET AND TO NOBODY ELSE.**

He put his hat back on and went to the quay.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon he went down to the second lay and asked the caulker for the Saturday for the third time, as he had said he would, and this time the caulker had been asked.

"Saturday the twenty-first of August," Calder said, "and it is a flood and it is a day out and a day back, and I will pay you one hundred and sixty-eight pence for it out of that yard's own figures at four o'clock in the afternoon of that day, being one mark and eightpence, and the figure is the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall and it is not this yard's figure, and not one penny of it comes out of the owner of any of those seven boats, and your name is not on the sheet and is not on the leaf and is not going on a board."

"Yes," said Fell. "I will be at the stone at half past three and I will be on it at four o'clock and if I am not on it at four o'clock then that is the day and you will not have anybody to blame and I will not have said a word about it after."

"You will be on it. And I am not going to thank you and I am not going to put you on a board, and I have said both of those once already and I am not going to say them again."

"You said them on the tenth," said Fell. "I have not forgotten it and you do not have to say it again to prove it."

**AND THE DAY OF SATURDAY THE TWENTY-FIRST OF AUGUST WAS ASKED FOR BY NAME ON A MONDAY AND ON A TUESDAY AND WAS AGREED TO ON A THURSDAY, AND IT IS PAID FOR ON THE DAY AND NOT BEFORE AND NOT AFTER, AND THE MAN IT IS FOR IS ON A DAY AND A WAGE AND AN HOUR AND ON NOTHING ELSE, AND A FIGURE HE HAS FOR THE WHOLE OF THOSE TEN DAYS IS A FIGURE NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO PAY, WHICH IS A MARK AND EIGHTPENCE FOR A DAY AND TWO MARKS AND FOURPENCE FOR TWO, AND THE BAG HAS A MARK IN IT AT THE CLOSE OF THE TENTH DAY.**

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the nineteenth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that the cistern was charged for the seventy-fourth time at about eight and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that the holder sat in that shed from about nine until about one with a leaf open and a chart of the reach below the foul weighted at the corners and got no further with it than he had got with it on the thirteenth; and that a reach below the foul comes out a mile and a half inside the point and there is a channel in it he has never been down and he has three soundings off a hand lead on a leaf a stranger could take again, and that the two places on that leaf are in a man's own boat lengths and not in feet; and that the third channel is three iron rings on a pinger on a buoy that came adrift on the twelfth of July and is nobody's measurement, and that the pinger went back up that coast on the Tuesday of the thirteenth and has not come down and no hand on that coast has it; and that the holder said he had a route out and could not take anybody down it, and that a thing a man can draw is not a thing he has got; and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and gave three soundings on the first of August and kept one back and told the holder there was one thing in that book he would not give and did not name it, and that he has not been asked since and is not going to be asked by anybody in that crew; and that the holder went up to that mole head at about four in the morning on the said Thursday and there was nobody on the flat of the bench stone and there was a maul on the step and a bucket with three inches of rainwater in it, and that he stood there about two minutes and went down and did not go up again at four in the afternoon and did not look for the man and did not ask anybody where he was and told nobody anything; and that the said man was not asked for the Thursday by anybody, and was at his own bench on the second lay with his own fire out because there is no coal in that harbor for a private fire, and has nine feet of garboard to come back to on Friday, and nobody has sent for him; and that a woman of about fifty-eight told the holder at about half past two in the afternoon at the end stall, unasked, that there is a sheet up in the front street since Friday with a seal on it and nine lines in a small hand, and that she and her man cannot read it and are not going to, and that it is not about flour and it is not about oil, and that it went up on the outside of a figure that was not taken down first; and that two men at the fish counter on Saturday said the word register and neither of them could say what a register was for; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the holder asked the caulker for the Saturday for the third time and the caulker had been asked and said yes, and that he will be at the stone at half past three and will be on it at four o'clock, and that he is not going to be thanked and is not going on a board and that both of those were said once on the tenth of August and were not said again on the said day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that no sounding was taken on that reach on that day by anybody and that a route nobody has been down is not a passage and is not written down as one; and that a stone with nobody on it at four in the morning is not an absence and is not a fault and is not a lesson and went in no column of that leaf, and that the reason it went in no column is that there is nothing in it that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could check and there is nothing in it against anybody; and that a nine-line sheet with a seal on it in a front street is a sheet and not a notice and not an order and not a threat and asks nothing of anybody standing in that street, and that it names no person and no harbor and puts no figure on it and has no price at the end of it; and that it is true enough on the face of it that nobody who reads it can go up the street and say it is a lie, and that it is not answered in this harbor and this yard is not going to answer it, and that the answer to it is not in that yard's keeping and is not going to be asked of the seven men whose hands are at the foot of a sheet in a pocket in a boat.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and twenty-first line:*

*What it is not: — that a route a man can draw is not a route he has and is not a passage and is not a thing to be taken out on, and that a man who knows where a channel is and has not been down it has not got a way out; and that a stone with nobody on it at four in the morning is not an absence and is not a fault and is not the fifth answer and is not the thing on the list, and that nobody was going to be there; and that a sheet with a seal on it asking for a register of concerns that keep no account of what they have taken is not a lie and is not a slander and is not a notice, and that nobody in that harbor can say the first of those three things out loud on the quay; and that being asked for a Saturday on a Monday and on a Tuesday and agreeing to it on a Thursday is not a negotiation and is not a hiring and is three conversations and not a contract.*
