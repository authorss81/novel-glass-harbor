# Chapter 382

*Thursday the Fifth of August, the Fifty-Second Day of the Ninety, the Seventieth Time the Cistern Was Charged and Walked on None, a Man of About Thirty-One of the Third Lay With a Chart in a Bag Who Came Up the Hill and Was Not Cited in Anything, a Hand Lead and a Hand That Cannot Use One and a Man Whose Book Is Not to Be Asked For, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Thursday the fifth of August, in that shed, and at the cistern, and on the front street.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and it was a rota day, and the cistern at the back of the market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventieth time, and the top name on the rota board is still off it and has been off it since before the spring. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the fire party's hose at the standpipe is dead and has been dead since before the spring, and the tide gang of that harbor is four men short, and the list on the board in the office on the west basin carries none of it in a way that binds anybody, because a list is a list.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty days and has not been touched.**

---

The man of about thirty-one of the third lay came up the hill at about eleven in the morning with a bag in his hand and stopped in the doorway of that shed and did not come in until he was asked, which nobody did.

He is a person of no office. He has never held one. He is not a witness to anything and he was not asked to be and he was not turned into one by anybody, and the only thing he has ever brought up that hill is a chart in a bag that he wanted looked at once in June and that nobody in that yard has looked at since and is not going to be asked about this month.

"I heard at the market that you went down a lay on the Wednesday and asked four men a question," he said. "I did not hear the question. I want to say before you tell me anything that I am not here to be put on anything, and I am not here because somebody sent me, and if I have come at a wrong hour you can tell me and I will go down again."

"I did not send for you," Calder said, "and I have not asked anybody who told you, and I am not going to ask anybody who told you, and I will tell you the reason when you have heard the question, and not before."

"Then ask it."

"What does your boat want, before she can lie in company with another boat for a day out and a day back."

The man of about thirty-one thought about it for a while, standing in the doorway with the bag against his leg.

"A hand lead," he said. "Not the boat's line and not a knotted line with a stone on it. A lead and a line, and somebody in her who can use one, and that is two things and not one, and a boat of about twenty tons in company on a flood is a boat that is being asked to find the same water three times in an hour and there is no way to do that with your eye."

"That is the fourth thing," Calder said, "and it goes on the leaf with the other three and with no name against it."

"Then you will be wanting to know which boats in this harbor have a hand lead."

"I am not going to want to know that," Calder said, "and if anybody in this crew goes near the second lay to ask it, I will stop him myself, and I will tell you why and I will tell you now, because you are the only man on this coast who has asked me a question I cannot answer by saying no."

**And he told him the reason in the same words he gave a man of about thirty-two about a hose in a return on Monday the twenty-sixth of July, and about a man's name on a leaf in a drawer on Sunday the first of August, being that a figure against a man in a return of that office or in a list in this yard follows the man, and that this harbor has one man in it who keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and has never been asked for a copy of it and said plainly on the first of August that if he were asked he would put the book in his pocket and go home, and that he was not asked then and is not going to be asked now and is not going to be asked by anybody in this crew about a lead or about anything else.**

"So there is one hand lead in this harbor that anybody knows of and one in a book," Calder said, "and I am not going to go and count them, and the count is not on the list, and the list says a hand lead in every boat and every boat that has not got one is a boat that has not got one and that is the whole of what a leaf can say about it."

"That is not a list of what a boat wants," the man of about thirty-one said. "That is a list of what is in this harbor."

"It is both," Calder said, "and I am writing down the one that can be bought and leaving the other one where it is, and if that is a fault in the list then it is a fault in the list and not in your question."

The man of about thirty-one put the bag down on the step and took it up again, and did not open it, and did not say what was in it, and nobody asked.

"You are not going to look in the bag."

"I looked in it once in June and I did not see anything in it that I could use and I did not see anything in it that I was entitled to, and I have not looked at it since and I am not going to look at it again this month, and that is not a promise about the rest of the year."

"Will you say that where I can hear you," the man said, "in that room in the front street, if anybody in this town ever asks you in there whether a man with a chart came up the hill."

"No," Calder said, "because the day anybody in this town asks me in a room where there is a clerk whether a man came up a hill, that is the day that man is a person in a matter, and you would be in it and you did not come to be in it, and the clerk of that room is about sixty and has been in it twenty-two years and is owed a quarter and has no book and no power and I am not going to be the reason a question is put to him on your account."

**AND HE WENT DOWN THE HILL AT ABOUT NOON WITH THE BAG STILL SHUT, AND HE WAS NOT PAID, HE WAS NOT ASKED TO PAY, HE WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN BY NAME IN ANY COLUMN OF ANY LEDGER IN THAT SHED, AND NO PERSON IN ANY ROOM IN THAT HARBOR WAS TOLD THAT HE HAD BEEN UP THE HILL AT ALL, AND A CHART IS A THING A MAN CAN CARRY AND CANNOT CITE, AND HE IS A PERSON OF NO OFFICE AND IS NOT A WITNESS AND WAS NOT TURNED INTO ONE.**

---

At about half past three the day's first column was entered with the hours and the four answers, and the second column carried what a man on a mole cannot check, and the third column was empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that has not been said out loud in that harbor since the thirteenth of May past and whose surname is on no page of anything.

At about half past four Calder went down to the office on the west basin and stood at the counter and did not say anything, and the clerk of about sixty did not say anything to him, and the two of them stood there for the length of a pipe and neither of them put a hand on anything, and the list on the board on that wall is a list and is not a schedule and binds nobody in that harbor, and the fire party's hose is not on it and the tide gang's want of four men is not on it and the north berth being out is on it and that is all it carries this morning.

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WITH A CHART IN A BAG CAME UP A HILL IN THAT HARBOR ON THE SAID DAY AND WENT DOWN IT AGAIN, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED ANYTHING AND NOBODY WAS TOLD ANYTHING AND NOBODY WROTE HIS NAME DOWN, AND A LIST OF WHAT A BOAT WANTS HAS FOUR THINGS ON IT AT THE CLOSE OF THE SAID DAY AND NOT ONE MAN, AND THE FOURTH OF THEM CANNOT BE BOUGHT AND THE FIFTH OF THEM CANNOT BE FOUND OUT BY ASKING, AND NEITHER OF THOSE IS IN THE FIRST COLUMN AND ONE OF THEM IS IN THE SECOND AND THE OTHER ONE IS NOWHERE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the fifth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Thursday was a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventieth time, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up on the said day at the angle it has been at since the twelfth; and that a man of about thirty-one of the third lay came up the hill at about eleven with a bag in his hand, stood in the doorway and was not asked to come in, and said he had heard at the market that the holder had gone down a lay and asked four men a question and had not heard the question, and said he was not there to be put on anything and was not sent by anybody; and that the holder said he had not sent for him and had not asked anybody who told him and was not going to ask anybody who told him; and that the question asked was the same question in the same words and that the said man answered a hand lead and not the boat's line, and a lead and a line and somebody in her who can use one, and said that is two things and not one, and said a boat of about twenty tons in company on a flood is a boat being asked to find the same water three times in an hour and there is no way to do that with the eye; and that the answer went on the leaf with the other three and with no name against it, being four things and no men; and that the holder said he was not going to want to know which boats in that harbor have a hand lead, and gave the reason out loud in the same words he gave a man of about thirty-two about a hose in a return on the twenty-sixth of July and about a man's name on a leaf in a drawer on the first of August, being that a figure against a man in a return of that office or a list in that yard follows the man, and that one man of that harbor keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and said on the first of August that if he were asked he would put the book in his pocket and go home, and that he was not asked then and is not going to be asked now and not by anybody in that crew; and that the holder said there is one hand lead in that harbor that anybody knows of and one in a man's own book, and that he is not going to count them, and that a leaf may say that a boat wants a hand lead and may say which boats have not got one and that is the whole of what a leaf can say about it; and that the said man said that is a list of what is in that harbor and the holder said it is both and that the one that can be bought is written down and the other one is left where it is; and that the said man set the bag down on the step and took it up again and did not open it and did not say what was in it and nobody asked; and that the holder said he looked in the bag once in June, did not see anything in it he could use and did not see anything in it he was entitled to, and has not looked at it since; and that the said man asked whether the holder would say so in the room in the front street if anybody in that town ever asked in there whether a man with a chart came up the hill, and that the holder said no and gave the reason, being that the day anybody in that town asks that question in a room with a clerk in it is the day that man is a person in a matter, and that the clerk of that room is about sixty and has been in it twenty-two years and is owed a quarter and has no book and no power and is not going to be the reason a question is put to him on that man's account; and that the said man went down the hill at about noon with the bag still shut and was not paid and was not written down by name in any column of that yard's leaf, and that no person in any room in that harbor was told that he had been up the hill at all; and that the holder went down to the office on the west basin at about half past four and stood at the counter and said nothing, and that the clerk of about sixty said nothing to him, and that the two of them stood there for the length of a pipe and neither put a hand on anything, and that the list on the board on that wall is a list and is not a schedule and binds nobody in that harbor; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty-one weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a chart in a bag is a thing a man can carry and cannot cite, and that a person of no office who comes up a hill on his own account and is not written down is not a witness and was not turned into one and is not to be cited in a room in that harbor or in any other; and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder of that harbor in his own book and who gave three soundings off a hand lead on the first of August was not asked about a hand lead on the said day and is not going to be asked by anybody in that crew, and that the reason was given out loud to a man who came to ask a question and had a right to the answer; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourth is not entered here again and stands; and that a boat wants a hand lead and there are two in that harbor and one of them is in a man's own book and the number of boats is not counted, and that not counting a number is a decision of that shed and not a finding, with the reason against the line that a count of hand leads is a list of boats and a list of boats is a list of men.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a man who comes up a hill with a bag and is not written down is not a hidden hand in a matter and is not a person kept out of it, and that a chart is not a paper and is not a document and is not a thing anybody may be cited on; and that a man who will not be asked for a figure he has kept since March is not withholding anything from this yard and is not protecting himself, and that the reason given out loud to a man who asked is not a confidence and is not a favour; and that a list of four things with no man on it is not a fleet and is not the beginning of one, and that a list which says what a boat wants and not which boats have not got it is a better list and not a worse one.*
