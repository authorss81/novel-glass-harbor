# Chapter 384

*Saturday the Seventh of August, the Fifty-Fourth Day of the Ninety, Six Masters of the Third Lay Lying Off the Point at About a Mile and a Half With a Small Boat of About Twelve Tons Alongside Them and a Lever of Nineteen at Her Tiller, Five of Them Who Said Their Papers Were in Order and One Who Would Answer No Question and Was Put in Last, and a Yard That Did Not Go Out*

Saturday the seventh of August, on the third lay, and at that quay, and in that shed.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the market of that harbor runs on a Saturday and the end stall of it is open and the price of a boat and a man for a day is in chalk on a slate there with the day of the month beside it, being the twenty-fifth of July, and that figure is not this yard's and cannot be taken off it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-two days and has not been touched and has not been taken down.**

---

A boat of the second lay of about twenty-six tons came in past the outer marks at about two in the afternoon on the sixth clause, which is free, and put in at the second lay and did not go alongside the third, and the man of about twenty-nine who was in her came up the hill on foot because somebody in that boat had better legs than he did and was not the man in her.

"They have six of us off the point," he said. "Not us. Six of the third lay. About a mile and a half, on the other side of the point from the flour boats, and there is a small boat of about twelve tons lying alongside the outside one of them with a boat's boat in her and a man at her tiller of about nineteen and nothing flying on her and no gun on her that I could see and I was close enough to see."

"How long."

"She was there when we went out at about ten and she was there when we came in at about two and the six of them had not moved and had not hailed and had not tried to come in." He put his hat in his hand. "She is putting them in one at a time. The outside one went to her in a dinghy at about half past ten and came back in his own boat, and then the next one went at about eleven, and that is the way it has gone all day, one at a time, and each of them about half an hour in her."

"What is she asking them."

"Papers." The man of about twenty-nine looked at the shed as though the shed had said something. "That is what the nineteen told the first one, in words, and the first one told the second one, and that is how we know. She is asking them whether their papers are in order and she is writing on something when they hand them over, and she has not asked any of them anything else that anybody has heard, and there was no officer in her that anybody could see and the nineteen at the tiller is the only man anybody has spoken to."

"Then what did the five do."

"Four of them handed them over and came back in their own boats and said nothing to anybody, and the fifth is the fifty-five of the third lay and he handed his over and sat in her for about a quarter of an hour and then came back in his own boat and came up this hill." The man of about twenty-nine did not put his hat back on. "And the sixth of them is the thirty-three of the third lay and the nineteen asked him the same question and he said he had no papers on him, and the nineteen asked him where his papers were and he said they were in a locker in a house in another street, and that he had gone out on the Saturday morning without going in first, and the nineteen wrote that down and put him in her last, at about one, and he has not come out."

---

Calder did not go out.

He stood in the doorway of that shed and worked it out in the way a man works out a thing he has already decided and wants the deciding of to be on the record.

"I am not going out," he said. "I have got one boat and one man at the tiller in her and I paid him a hundred and sixty-eight pence for a day on Tuesday the twenty-seventh out of a bag that had it in, and the money that paid him is still in that bag and there is no second such day in this month. And I went alongside a boat of theirs once, on the twenty-seventh, on a day I had paid for, and I lay there four hours and did not talk, and then I came off her into a tow of eight boats and cut my own line, and the line I lost was eight fathoms and the thing I lost that was not mine was a lighter of about thirty tons with a foot and four inches of water in her and about two tons of flour in her bottom, and she was broken up against a wall in nine foot of water on Wednesday past and nothing came ashore out of her at any hour."

"That is not the same water," the man of about twenty-nine said.

"It is not the same water and it is the same mistake, and I have not got the money to make it a third time," Calder said. "And there is a second thing and it is the one that would keep me in that shed whatever the money was."

"Go on."

"A papers question is not a thing a boat can answer." He said it flatly, with his hands down. "There is no water in this that does anything to a paper. The five that went in there handed over what they had and came back in their own boats. If I had gone out I should have lain alongside one of them in a boat of this harbor with no declaration and no paper of any kind, and either that boat of about twelve tons has a room for me in her, which nobody in that harbor has any reason to suppose, or it has not, and then I have put this yard's name against a boat of theirs at a mile and a half in front of a man of nineteen with a list, and a name against a boat is a thing that follows the boat."

**AND THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT DID NOT GO OUT, AND GAVE THE REASON OUT LOUD IN A DOORWAY IN FRONT OF A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE SAID IT WAS NOT THE SAME WATER AND WAS TOLD IT IS NOT THE SAME WATER AND IT IS THE SAME MISTAKE, AND A YARD WITH NO OFFICE BEHIND IT AND NO PAPER OF ITS OWN IS THE LAST THING ON ANY COAST THAT WANTS ITS OWN NAME IN A LIST, AND NONE OF THE SIX BOATS CAME IN ON THE SAID DAY AND NONE OF THEM WAS HAILED AND NOBODY SENT ANYTHING AND NOBODY WROTE A DAY AGAINST ANY OF IT IN ANY RETURN OF ANY OFFICE IN THAT HARBOR, BECAUSE THERE IS NO OFFICE TO WRITE IT IN AND THE ONLY MAN IN THAT HARBOR WITH A BOX FOR DAYS A BOAT WENT OUT PAST THE LINE HAS NOT BEEN ASKED ABOUT IT AND IS NOT GOING TO BE.**

---

He was on the third lay at about five in the afternoon and the six berths were empty.

That was the whole of it and it was checkable by anybody standing on that lay. Six boats of that harbor lay out past the point and were not in their berths, and their gear was where their crews had left it, and there was a coil of line on the second boat's bollard with nobody on it and a hand lead on the third boat's thwart and nobody touching either, and a lamp in the wheelhouse of the fourth with the wick trimmed and the glass down and no man in her.

The master of about fifty-five of that lay was on the third lay at about half past five with the spare warp he had told the holder about on Wednesday coiled in his own cockpit, and he was not in a hurry about anything and he did not say one word about the small boat of about twelve tons.

"You will not be going out for the thirty-three," the man of about twenty-nine said to him, not quietly.

"No," the master of about fifty-five said.

"You will not be going out for anybody."

"No," he said, and then he said one more thing, and he said it to the water and not to anybody. "There is one spare warp in six boats and it is in my cockpit and it is a spare warp for my own boat. I have not given it away and I have not promised it and nobody has asked me for it and I am not going to bring it up that ladder to a boat of about twelve tons and hand it to a man of about nineteen who will write my name on something."

Nobody said anything to that either. And the master of about fifty-five said the other half of it before he went off the lay, flatly, to that lay and not to the man of about twenty-nine who is of the second.

"The thirty-three has no papers on him and he is not lying and he is not a smuggler, and he has a boat of his own and a wife in the second lay and a locker in a house in another street with three things in it that are not paper. Where there are no papers there is no door, and that is not an opinion of mine and it is not a hard thing about him, and I am not going to say it out loud on that lay to make anybody feel better about a Saturday."

**AND SIX BERTHS ON THAT THIRD LAY WERE EMPTY AT ABOUT FIVE IN THE AFTERNOON AND SIX BOATS OF THAT HARBOR LAY OUT PAST THE POINT WITH ABOUT TWENTY-SIX TONS OF FLOUR IN SEVEN OF A TOW OF EIGHT LYING AT ABOUT A MILE WITH TWO LIGHTS, FIRST SEEN ON THE EVENING OF SUNDAY THE FIRST OF AUGUST PAST AND NOT THERE ON THE EVENING OF THE TWENTY-EIGHTH, AND UNMOVED IN SIX DAYS, BEING THE INTERVAL FROM THE HOUR THEY WERE FIRST SEEN AND NOT FROM THE HOUR THEY WERE FIRST MISSED, AND NEITHER GROUP HAS BEEN HAILED BY ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR AND NEITHER GROUP HAS BEEN ASKED FOR ANYTHING, AND A TOW THAT IS HELD ELEVEN MINUTES IS A FIGURE AND A TOW THAT LIES AT A MILE IS A POSITION, AND THE TWO ARE NOT THE SAME THING AND THE SECOND ONE HAS NO FIGURE AT ALL.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the seventh day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Saturday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a boat of the second lay of about twenty-six tons came in past the outer marks at about two in the afternoon on the sixth clause, which is free, and put in at the second lay, and that a man of about twenty-nine who was in her came up the hill on foot; and that he said six masters of the third lay are lying off the point at about a mile and a half on the other side of the point from the flour boats, and that a small boat of about twelve tons is lying alongside the outside one of them with a boat's boat in her and a man of about nineteen at her tiller and nothing flying on her and no gun on her that could be seen at close enough, and that she was there at about ten and at about two and the six had not moved and had not hailed; and that she is putting them in one at a time, about half an hour in her each, and that what she is asking them is whether their papers are in order, and that she is writing on something when they hand them over, and that no officer in her was seen and the only man anybody spoke to was the lever of about nineteen; and that four of the six handed their papers over and came back in their own boats and said nothing to anybody, and that the fifth was the master of about fifty-five of that lay, who handed his over, sat in her about a quarter of an hour and came back in his own boat; and that the sixth was a master of about thirty-three of that lay, who said he had no papers on him, and said where they were, being a locker in a house in another street, and that he had gone out on the Saturday morning without going in first, and that the lever of about nineteen wrote that down and put him in her last, at about one, and that he had not come out at the hour the boat of the second lay came in; and that the holder said he was not going out, and gave two reasons out loud in the doorway, being that this yard has one boat and one man at the tiller in her and that the hundred and sixty-eight pence that paid him on the twenty-seventh of July is still in that bag and there is no second such day in that month, and that he went alongside a boat of theirs once on the twenty-seventh, lay there four hours, said nothing, and came off into a tow of eight and cut his own line, and lost eight fathoms of his own line and a lighter of about thirty tons with a foot and four inches of water in her and about two tons of flour in her bottom, which was broken up against a wall in nine foot of water on Wednesday past with nothing landed out of her at any hour; and that the said man said it was not the same water and that the holder said it is not the same water and it is the same mistake; and that the second reason given was that a papers question is not a thing a boat can answer, and that a boat of that harbor going out alongside one of the six with no declaration and no paper of any kind would either have a room in that small boat, which nobody in that harbor has any reason to suppose, or would not, and that in the second case this yard's name would be put against a boat of theirs at a mile and a half in front of a man of about nineteen with a list, and that a name against a boat is a thing that follows the boat; and that six berths on that third lay were empty at about five in the afternoon and six boats of that harbor lay out past the point, with a coil of line on the second boat's bollard and a hand lead on the third boat's thwart and a lamp in the wheelhouse of the fourth with the wick trimmed and the glass down and no man in her; and that the master of about fifty-five was on the third lay at about half past five with the spare warp coiled in his own cockpit and said he would not be going out for the man of about thirty-three and would not be going out for anybody, and said that there is one spare warp in six boats and it is a spare warp for his own boat and that he has not given it away and has not promised it and nobody has asked him for it and that he is not going to bring it up a ladder to a boat of about twelve tons and hand it to a man of about nineteen who will write his name on something; and that nobody said anything to that either, and that the said master said the other half of it before he went off the lay, being that the thirty-three has no papers on him and is not lying and is not a smuggler, and that where there are no papers there is no door, and that that is not an opinion of his and is not a hard thing about the man; and that none of the six boats came in on the said day and none was hailed and nobody sent anything; and that no day was written against any of it in any return of any office in that harbor, and that the only man in that harbor with a box for days a boat went out past the line has not been asked about it and is not going to be; and that about twenty-six tons of flour is in seven boats of a tow of eight lying off the point at about a mile with two lights, first seen on the evening of Sunday the first of August past and not there on the evening of the twenty-eighth, and unmoved in six days, being the interval from the hour they were first seen and not from the hour they were first missed; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty-one weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourth is not entered here again and stands; and that a small boat of about twelve tons with a boat's boat in her and a lever of about nineteen at her tiller was lying off the point at about a mile and a half on the seventh of August, and that somebody sent her and this yard does not know who and is not going to stand on a hill and guess and has asked nobody and will not; and that where there are no papers there is no door, and that a man who would not answer a question about his papers is not a man who is doing something and is not a man who has done anything, and that the refusal goes on and is not to be taken back; and that five of six masters of a lay came back in their own boats and said nothing to anybody and one sat in her a quarter of an hour and one would not answer and has not come out; and that a tow of eight was held eleven minutes in that fairway on the twenty-seventh of July past and is a figure, and that seven boats of that tow lying at about a mile is a position, and that a position has no figure and is nobody's and is not to be balanced against eleven minutes or against six berths.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that six masters of a lay lying off a point with a boat alongside them is not a fleet and is not an organisation and is not the beginning of one, and that a papers question is not a man and is not a cargo and is not a cargo of anybody; and that a yard which did not go out is not a yard that would not go and is not a yard that was afraid, and that the reason it gave was a reason and not a virtue; and that a man who would not answer a question about his own papers has not been convicted of anything and has not been charged with anything and is not anybody's prisoner in anybody's mouth, and that the refusal is a fact about a Saturday and not about a policy.*
