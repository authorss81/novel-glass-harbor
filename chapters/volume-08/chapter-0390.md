# Chapter 390

*Friday the Thirteenth of August, the Sixtieth Day of the Ninety, the Forty-Third Week Not Yet Due and the Column Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and a Hundred and Four Marks, a Boat of the Third Lay In at Two With a Small Boat of About Twelve Tons Round the Point at Eleven With Two Boats in Her, a Man Who Named the Tide and Knew What He Was Naming, a Route Out He Knows and Does Not Have, a Leaf Read Back in Full With the Count on the Face of It and the Definition of Every Figure on the Face of It, the Hundred and Fifteenth Line and the Column Not Closed, and a Mole Head at Ten Past Five in the Evening With a Light Not Lit and Two Lights Off the Point That Belong to Nobody in That Harbor*

Friday the thirteenth of August, on that quay, in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the whole of what came into that yard in the last ten days is three hundred and thirty-six pence on two days, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth on the fourth of August and the same on the ninth, and the whole of what went out of it is two hundred and twelve pence on two days, being a caulker's day on the tenth and a loaf and a half and a candle and a pot on the twelfth, and the two are not the same figure and neither of them is the wage of four marks, which is six hundred and forty pence and has not been paid for forty-two weeks and is not going to be paid on the forty-third.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed at about four for the reading and for nothing else. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-eight days and has not been touched and has not been taken down and has not been compared with anything by anybody in that harbor. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety days, the chest is shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

The master of about fifty-five of the third lay came up the hill at about two in the afternoon with his own boat's painter over his shoulder and put it down on the step, because he had carried it three hundred yards and could not think what else to do with his hands.

"I have been in company with three of my own and I am not asking you for anything," he said. "I am here because a small boat of about twelve tons came round the point at about eleven this morning with two boats in her and she went down-coast, and I have seen her before and I know which one she is, and the two boats with her are new to me. And before she went round the point she came to the six of us on the water and put a man in a dinghy alongside me and he asked me one question."

"What question."

"Where the company out of this harbor sails and on what day." The master of about fifty-five set the painter down again. "And I told him. I told him the flood, because that is the tide and it is what everybody in that harbor would have told him and there is nothing else to tell a man about a day out of a place. I did not say your name. Nobody said your name. But I said the day."

"Which day."

"The flood. On the day you name." He looked at Calder and did not put the blame anywhere, which was the worst of it and was visible. "I have been four days on this and I have a spare warp in my cockpit and a second one to buy, and I said yes on Wednesday and I mean it and I am not going to take it back. And I am standing on a step telling a man what he has already worked out, because I would rather you heard it off me than off a sheet, and I have thought about that on the water for three hours and I have not found a better reason."

**AND A SMALL BOAT OF ABOUT TWELVE TONS WENT ROUND THE POINT OF THAT HARBOR AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING WITH TWO BOATS IN HER AND WENT DOWN-COAST, AND SHE WAS THREE BOATS AND NOT TWELVE AND NONE OF THEM IS A MAN-OF-WAR AND NO OFFICER IN HER WAS SEEN BY ANYBODY, AND A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN PUT A QUESTION INTO A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-EIGHT TONS AT THE OUTER WATER AND WAS TOLD THE TIDE, AND A FLOOD IS A TIDE AND IT IS THE ONLY TIDE THAT HARBOR CAN MAKE.**

---

He went into that shed and shut the door, which he does not do.

"The flood," he said. "That is what I named on Wednesday to a man on the quay in front of a master of about thirty, and I named it in front of about nine men of that third lay over four days, and I said it out loud to that master of about thirty on Tuesday past when he asked me whether I was going to take a company out on the flood and back on the flood, and I said no. And that is the day it is on, and I chose it."

"You chose it."

"I chose it because a flood is the only water this harbor can make and because those boats can do nothing else in any other water, and because the four things and a person on that leaf only work on one tide and I said so in a doorway and I was right about that." He stood with his hands on the bench stone. "And a flood is the only hour a loaded transport can come down that reach going that way. That has been true since I was a boy and it is in every man's head on this coast and it is in no book. I knew it when I named the day. Nobody made me name the day and nobody put it in my mouth and I did not ask what else goes down a reach on the flood because I did not ask."

Nessa was in the shed and she had a leaf in front of her and she did not put her pen down and she did not pick it up.

"Go on," Calder said, to himself more than to her. "There is a reach below the foul that comes out a mile and a half inside the point and a channel in it that I have never been down and a man of about thirty-four of the second lay has in his own book, and he gave me three soundings off a hand lead on the first of August and put two of them in the first column and kept the third one back because the distance between his own two places is in his own boat lengths and not in feet, and he told me plainly that there is one thing in that book he will not give and did not name it, and that if I asked him he would put the book in his pocket and go home. He was not asked. He is not going to be asked by anybody in this crew."

"And the channel."

"Three iron rings on a pinger on an adrift buoy on the twelfth of July past, and it is nobody's measurement, and the pinger went back up that coast on the Tuesday of the thirteenth and it has not come down and no hand on that coast has it." He turned round. "So I know a route out. I know where it is and I know the shape of it and I have three soundings in a leaf in this drawer that a stranger with a pencil could go and take again with a lead. And I have not got it, and the reason I have not got it is the same reason there is no spare warp in six boats and it is the same reason the man whose day it is is a caulker of twenty-three who was on a stone at four in the morning on Tuesday because he cannot sleep, and not an arrangement and not a list and not a person anybody in this harbor is paying to be there except on a Tuesday for a day at a mark and eightpence."

"Knowing where a route is and not having it," Nessa said, "those are two different things."

"Both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other," Calder said, "and I have said that shape out loud in this shed twice in a month and I am not going to improve on it now."

**AND A MAN IN THAT SHED ON THE SAID DAY SAID WHAT A THING ON A BOARD IS, AND SAID IT ABOUT A SHAPE AND NOT ABOUT A SHEET, AND THE SHAPE IS A BOARD OF ABOUT A YARD WIDE UNDER A SLOPING CANVAS ON AN AFTER THROWT, WITH ONE WORD AT THE HEAD OF IT IN BLOCK CAPTAILS, BEING *UNSEALED*, TWO RULED COLUMNS UNDER IT, ELEVEN MARKS IN THE RIGHT-HAND ONE AGAINST DATES FROM THE EIGHTH OF MAY TO THE TWENTY-SECOND OF JULY, TWO IMPRESSIONS STRUCK SIDE BY SIDE AT THE FOOT OF WHICH HE READ NEITHER, AND A DATE CUT INTO THE TIMBER ABOVE THE HEAD, BEING THE FOURTEENTH OF FEBRUARY, WHICH IS NINETY-TWO DAYS BEFORE THE NOTICE WENT UP ON A WALL IN A DIFFERENT STREET. HE HAS NOT GOT THE FORM, HE HAS NOT GOT A COPY OF IT, NO DOCUMENT OF ANYBODY'S CHANGED HANDS ON THE WATER, AND WHAT HE HAS GOT IS A SHAPE, A DATE, TWO IMPRESSIONS HE DID NOT READ AND A WORD, AND HE SAID THAT OUT LOUD ONCE AND HE IS NOT GOING TO SAY IT AGAIN IN THAT SHED, AND IT IS A SHAPE AND NOT A SHEET AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HE HAS.**

They read the leaf back at about four in the afternoon, which is the hour that shed does things, and there were three of them in it.

**The first column of that leaf has one hundred and twenty-two entries in it at the hour of the read-back and one hundred and twenty-three at the close.** There were one hundred and thirteen of them at the close of Tuesday the third of August past and ten have gone in since, one for every day from Wednesday the fourth to Friday the thirteenth, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own entry is the tenth and makes one hundred and twenty-three, and one hundred and twenty-two of them were in it at the hour of this reading.

**The second column has four hundred and eighty-seven lines in it at the hour of the read-back and four hundred and eighty-nine at the close.** Three hundred and thirty-nine of them were in it at the close of the third of August past. Three of the day's entries go in before the reading of that day and two after it, and that is the shape of that shed's habit and is not a rule, and no reader in any office can check it, and that is what the second column is for. Ten days of that shape is fifty, and three hundred and thirty-nine and fifty is four hundred and eighty-nine, and three of this Friday's went in before the reading and make four hundred and eighty-seven at the hour of it. **The said figure is that book of that yard's own and is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's one hundred and twenty-two and the fourth column's one hundred and fourteen, at the hour of this read-back, are counts of entries and of lines, and a day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count both of them. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments.**

**The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. The three nots standing against the eighth day of June past were not asked about on any of the ten days and are not going to be asked about by anybody in that crew, and there is now a fifth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them. He was not on the stone on any of these ten days and was put on nothing.**

**And the fourth column has one hundred and fourteen lines in it at the hour of the read-back and one hundred and fifteen at the close, and the hundred and fifteenth is the last line in that book, and there is nothing under it, and the column is not closed and nobody has closed it and nobody in this yard is going to.**

Then the woman of no office asked one question, and it was the only question asked in that shed that day, and it was about the day he had named on Wednesday.

"Will you take them out on it."

"No," Calder said. "Not on that day and not on any day until a caulker of about twenty-three has been asked for that day by name and has said yes to that day and has been paid for that day. And if it comes to it I will not take them out on a flood, and I will say why on the quay in front of the boats and not in this shed, and the reason will be the tide and the reason will be true and it will not be the whole of the reason."

"That is a decision and not a finding," Mara Venn said, "and I will write it in the second column with the reason against the line, and the reason is that you know the hour and you are not going to be at it."

"That is the reason," Calder said.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it is out on the thirteenth of August as it was on the fourteenth of May, and a light costs oil, and the last figure anybody in that harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is on a board in the front street with February's above it and both dates on it, and a figure put on a board cannot be taken off it again.
