# Chapter 372

*Monday the Twenty-Sixth of July, the Forty-Second Day of the Ninety, the Wall Bare for the Sixty-Fourth Time, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Seventh, a Hose That Is Dead Until the Spring, a Man Who Wants a Day's Work and a Yard That Has None, and a Day's Boat Agreed on a Bollard Inside the Line*

Monday the twenty-sixth of July, in that shed, and on that mole, and at the quay inside the line.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, was a rota day and was a wipe, being the sixty-fourth wipe and the sixty-seventh charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-fourth time and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by a man of about forty-one and walked on none of it by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-seventh, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and that is the figure in chalk on a slate at the end stall of that market and this yard did not put it there.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she came up that hill at about eleven and sat on the bench at the back of that shed and did not read anything and was not read to. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning by a man of about forty-one who came off the rota and filled her and went away, and the man of about thirty-two with the hose coupled it at half past nine and uncoupled it at half past ten and did not get a cup of water out of the run, and that was the sixty-seventh.

He came into that shed at about ten with the wet end of the hose over his shoulder and asked whether this yard would go to the reeve's office on the west basin and put the fire party's hose in a return.

"No," Calder said.

"It has been dead since before the spring."

"I know it has."

"I have asked twice. I asked the office and I asked the man who keeps the fire party's book and both of them said it was not on anything." He put the hose down across the doorway, which he had not meant to do, and left it there. "If it is in a return from this office then it is in a return from this office, and the office has to answer it, and a man from the office has to walk down to the third lay and stand at the standpipe with me and put his hand on it."

"It would be," Calder said, "against your name."

"That is what I am asking for."

"That is why it is no." Calder did not raise his voice and did not put anything on the bench to do it with. "The list on that board in that office is a list and is not a schedule and it binds nobody, and it is the only reason anything in this harbor still gets done. The moment this yard writes your name against a hose, it stops being a hose and it becomes a figure against a man, and a figure against a man in one of those returns is a thing that follows the man when he goes to a hard, and you are one of the tide gang and not one of the men who owns a boat, and a man with a figure against him in a return of that office is a man who cannot say no to anybody afterwards. I have watched it happen twice on this coast to a man with a figure and once to a woman, and none of the three times was anybody cruel. They were all arithmetic."

The man of about thirty-two stood in the doorway with a wet hose across it and looked at the floor of that shed for a while.

"Then it is nobody's," he said.

"Then it is nobody's, and it is dead, and it will be dead until the spring, and that is a bad thing to write in a book and a worse thing to leave out of one." Calder picked the end of the hose up and gave it back to him. "I will not put your name to it and I will not put anybody's name to it and I will not put a figure against that standpipe in a book in this shed, because the day somebody wants that hose there is the day a man's whole season goes on it, and that is not a trade this yard trades in."

**THE FIRE PARTY'S HOSE IS DEAD UNTIL THE SPRING AND IS DEAD AT THIS HOUR OF THIS DAY AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR IT A THIRD TIME AND NOBODY WILL, AND THE WANT WAS REFUSED AND THE REFUSAL WAS GIVEN IN A DOORWAY WITH A WET HOSE ACROSS IT AND NOBODY IMPROVED ON IT.**

---

A man of the tide gang came past about an hour later with a hod of lime on his shoulder and stopped at the step, and said the gang was four short, and that the four who had gone were gone, and that nobody had asked them for a reason and this yard was not going to.

"No," Calder said. "I have no office and I cannot ask anybody anything and if I asked them a reason they would have to give me one and then it would be a thing in a book."

"That is what I said," said the man with the hod, "and I have said it to two other doors this week and one of them told me to go to the office." He shifted the hod. "The rota is Monday and Thursday and we are charged on both and we walk on none, and the four of us that are short are short on both days, and it is not a want of a man, it is a want of four, and I am telling you because you are the only one on this quay who writes anything down and you are not going to write this one."

"Then it is entered in the first column and not in a return," Calder said, "and the first column is a leaf in a drawer in this shed and a man can read it and a man cannot answer for it, and that is the only difference and it is the whole of it."

---

At about one in the afternoon Calder went down to the quay on the inside of the line, where a vessel of perhaps eighteen tons was lying with a bight of her own line round a bollard that she had been round every night of the week, and a man of about nineteen was at her tiller with the bight in hand and not cutting it.

The harbour's list is not a schedule and does not bind anybody, and this yard did not go to the office on the west basin to ask for that boat, and the man of nineteen was not sent for and was not asked and was not thanked, and nobody in that harbor knows what he is here for except that he lies inside the line and does not cut a line.

"I want your boat for a day," Calder said.

The man of nineteen did not let go of the bight.

"What day," he said.

"Tomorrow. Out about seven and in by dark."

"Where."

"That is the part I am buying and I am not going to say it here on a quay in front of the fish store."

The man of nineteen looked at him then, and not at the fish store and not at the mole, and then he went back to the bight.

"A hundred and sixty-eight pence," he said, "being one mark and eightpence, and that is the day with a man in her and not the day without, and you are not bringing anybody else and I am not taking anybody else. In coin, at the mole head, before she sails, and not on the quay and not after. A man does not sail out of this harbour on a promise in this harbour, and I have been on the water three weeks and I have not had a bad word off anybody and I intend to keep it that way."

"That is the figure on the slate at the end stall," Calder said.

"Then the woman of about fifty-eight is the price of this harbour and I am not arguing with her about it," said the man of nineteen, "and it is her slate and she wrote it and I have read it, and I would have said the same, and you can pay me tomorrow morning at the mole head and I will not be paid twice for one day by anybody."

"You will be paid once," Calder said, "and I am not going to ask you a single question about what you do at the outer water, and I am not going to do it on the quay and I am not going to do it in the boat, and that is not rudeness and you can set it down however you like."

The man of nineteen took the bight round the bollard once more, tight, and did not cut it, and said that a man who does not ask is a man he will take out for, and that he would be at the mole head at a quarter to seven.

**AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR ASKED HIM ONE WORD ABOUT ANYTHING, AND THE SECOND COLUMN CARRIES IT WITH THE REASON AGAINST THE LINE THAT A MAN WITH A BOOK OR A MAN WITH A BIGHT OF LINE WHO TAKES A DAY'S MONEY IS NOT THE SAME THING AS A MAN WHO DOES NOT, AND THAT THIS YARD DOES NOT KNOW WHICH OF THE TWO IT IS AND IS NOT GOING TO FIND OUT.**

---

At about half past three the ship's hand entered the day in the first column with the hours and the figures, and the third column was empty and nobody has been appointed to it.

At about ten past five in the evening he was on the mole head, which is where he goes at that hour, and there was nothing up there and nobody had sent him.

The tender of about ninety tons was at the outer water with two lines out and her lamp at her masthead, and that is the seventeenth day of her and no officer has come ashore and no paper has come ashore and nobody has hailed anybody.

On the crown, about four feet from a page with six names on it, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and ten days old and one corner has been off since the seventeenth of June past. The second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May, and nobody went down to it on the said day.

**AND THE DAY AFTER TOMORROW A BOAT OF PERHAPS EIGHTEEN TONS WILL SAIL OUT OF THAT HARBOUR PAID FOR AT THE MOLE HEAD IN COIN BEFORE SHE SAILS, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED HER OWNER WHAT SHE IS FOR AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO, AND THE FIGURE FOR HER IS ON A SLATE IN CHALK AT THE END OF A MARKET STALL AND HE SAID HE WOULD BE AT THE MOLE HEAD AT A QUARTER TO SEVEN, AND A FIGURE A MAN WILL SAIL ON IS NOT A FIGURE HE WILL TAKE A RISK ON, AND NEITHER OF THOSE IS A REASON AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING IN THE FOURTH COLUMN TONIGHT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-sixth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Monday was a rota day and a wipe day, being the sixty-fourth wipe and the sixty-seventh charge, and that the cistern was charged at about eight by a man of about forty-one and walked on none by the man with the hose at about half past nine; and that a man of about thirty-two asked this yard to put the fire party's hose in a return of the office on the west basin and was refused, the reason given in the doorway being that a figure against that standpipe would be a figure against that man and a figure against a man in a return of that office follows the man to a hard, and that the hose is nobody's and is dead until the spring and no figure was put against it; and that a man of the tide gang with a hod of lime said the gang was four men short, that the four who had gone were gone, that nobody had asked them for a reason and this yard was not going to, and that the want is four and not one, and that the said want is entered in this first column and in no return of any office, and a leaf in a drawer in this shed can be read and cannot be answered for; and that the holder agreed a boat of perhaps eighteen tons and a man in her for one day out and in for the day following, at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, being the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall and not a figure of this yard's, to be paid in coin at the mole head before she sails; and that a man of nineteen said the same figure without being asked what this yard would pay and said he would not be paid twice for one day by anybody; and that the holder said he would not ask him one word about what he does at the outer water and did not ask him and is not going to; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-nine weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them, the next falling due on tomorrow, Tuesday the twenty-seventh, being the fortieth week.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man who does not ask is a man he will take out for, and that a man with a bight of line round a bollard who takes a day's money is not the same thing as a man who does not, and that this yard does not know which of the two he is and is not going to find out and did not ask him and is not going to; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands; and that the tender of about ninety tons is at the outer water for the seventeenth day with two lines out and no officer ashore and no paper ashore; and that nobody in that harbor knows and is not going to be told what that vessel is for; and that the third channel is still nobody's measurement and the borrowed instrument is still up that coast and has not come down.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a hose refused is not a hose fixed and a want refused in a doorway is not a want ended; and that four men short of a gang is not a figure against the four and is not entered in any office's return and cannot be; and that a day of a boat agreed on a bollard inside the line is not a crew and is not a standing order and is not a beginning of anything, and there is no paper of it anywhere and this yard will not want one.*
