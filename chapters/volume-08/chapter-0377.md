# Chapter 377

*Saturday the Thirty-First of July, the Forty-Seventh Day of the Ninety, a Day's Work With a Boat and Two Men at the Third Lay Paid in Coin at Four in the Afternoon, a Lighter of About Thirty Tons Lying at That Quay With Water in Her and Wet Flour in Her Bottom, a Figure of Four Hundred and Fifty Pence Said by the Man Who Sells the Oil and Not by a Yard, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Saturday the thirty-first of July, in that shed, and in the front street, and at the quay, and on that mole.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into it, being a day's work with a boat and two men at the third lay, and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the figure that came in is the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall, and this yard charged the herring skipper of fifty-one nothing over it and nothing under it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was at the shed at about four when the money was counted out. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The third day of caulking and painting on a boat of about thirty-one tons lying at the third lay began at about seven in the morning and was finished at about half past three, and the work was done by the holder and a man of about twenty-nine with a caulking iron who is not on the stone and is not owed a penny by anybody and asked for nothing and was not asked for anything, and the two of them were in that boat nine hours and the second man had his own bread and his own water and it came out of his own hand and not out of that shed.

The herring skipper of fifty-one came down at about four in the afternoon and put a hundred and sixty-eight pence on the flat of the bench stone in coin and counted it and said the figure was the figure on the slate and he was not going to argue with a woman of about fifty-eight about a day of a boat, and the day's hand went in the bag and the bag was shut.

Then he said one thing before he went, and he said it standing, and he did not sit down for it.

"If your yard is going to be busy in the autumn," he said, "I have a second boat and I would send work to whoever is not looking for it. I am not asking you to hold anything and I am not booking anything and I am not going to come and ask you about it. If you are busy, I will come, and if you are not busy I will go up the street, and that is the whole of what I came to say."

"I will not be busy in the autumn," Calder said.

"Then I will ask you in the spring, and I am not asking you to remember that I said it."

**AND HE WENT AWAY, AND HE HAS MUNICIPAL PAPERS OF HIS OWN, AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF JULY PAST HE SAID IN THE OPEN THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO USE THEM FOR A CONVOY, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED HAS SAID ONE WORD TO HIM ABOUT EIGHT BOATS OR A TOW OR A BOARD SINCE THE FIFTEENTH OF JULY, AND HE PAID FOR A DAY'S CAULKING AND WENT AWAY, AND NEITHER OF THOSE THINGS CANCELS THE OTHER.**

---

At about eleven in the morning the holder was in the front street when a man he did not know came out of the chandler's counter and stood in the road and looked at the board over it, and the chandler of about fifty was behind that counter with his wife doing the counting behind him, and nobody in that harbor has said one word to that chandler about the notice and he has said twice that he has nothing to say about it and he is not going to be asked a third time by anybody, including this yard.

The man asked the price of a barrel of lamp oil out loud, in the road, to anybody.

The chandler of about fifty said it to him the way he would have said it to anybody.

"Four hundred and fifty pence," he said. "That's thirty-seven shillings and sixpence. That is the figure I quoted to a woman on the first of June and it is the figure I have quoted since. The three hundred and seventy-eight pence you can read on the board above it was thirty-one shillings and sixpence and it is the price of February and I have not taken it off, because a figure put on this board cannot be taken off it again, and if it comes down I will chalk the new one underneath it with the day beside it and the old one will stay where it is for as long as I trade in this street."

The man looked at the board for a while and then went away without arguing and did not buy anything.

**AND THAT FIGURE IS THE FIGURE OF A BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR AND IT DID NOT MOVE ON THE SAID DAY, AND IT WAS SAID BY THE MAN WHO SELLS THE OIL AND NOT BY A YARD, AND THAT IS WHY IT IS ON THE FACE OF THIS CHAPTER AND NOT ON THE FACE OF ANY OTHER OF THE TEN DAYS, AND THE MOLE-HEAD LIGHT IS OUT AND ITS LENS HAS BEEN OUT SINCE THE SEVENTH DAY OF MAY PAST AND NOTHING HAS COME OFF THAT FIGURE SINCE THE FIRST OF JUNE.**

---

The lighter of about thirty tons was lying at that quay with her port side to the stone and about a foot and four inches of water in her, and she had come in on Tuesday night on her own account because she was making water at her seams, and there was a man of about fifty-three in her with a pump and a lantern and about two tons of flour in her bottom that was wet through the boards and could not be landed.

She was not the flour this harbor was waiting for. About twenty-six tons of that flour was in seven boats of a tow of eight, and the seven were still lying off the point at about a mile on the night of the thirty-first, and they had not come in and nobody had hailed them, and the eighth was somewhere inside the line and had not been seen from that quay.

Nobody landed a sack off that lighter. Nobody in that harbor had a price for what was in her, and the harbor authority's list is not a schedule and binds nobody, and the clerk of the office on the west basin was not sent for and was not asked anything, and the flour in the bottom of her went on getting wet and nobody wrote a figure against it and nobody claimed it.

Calder was on that quay at about two in the afternoon and the man of about fifty-three was on her hatch with the pump handle in his hand.

"You were in the fairway on Tuesday," the man said. He did not say it as an accusation and he did not say it as a favour; he said it the way a man says a thing he has worked out for himself and is checking.

"I was," Calder said. "My boat came up two foot on the edge of it at about ten to five. The figure in my book for that reach is eleven and she found nine. I had not sounded it and there was a tow coming down on the ebb. I cut my own stern line, and the tow took it, and the painter of yours parted, and you were held eleven minutes and you went on in about six foot."

The man of about fifty-three pumped twice and looked at the water that came and put it over the side.

"That is what happened," he said. "I have not said it to anybody else and I am not going to, because there is nobody in this harbor to say it to and because you have told me before I have told you, and a man who has told you before you have told him has not taken anything off you." He set the pump down. "I am not going to say it was not your fault. It was your boat in my tow and you cut your own line, and if you had not cut it you would have gone across me with a case in my arms and I would have had a man in the water. So it was a thing done by a man and I do not know what else to call it, and I am not going to call it anything else at eleven o'clock on a Saturday with a pump handle in my hand."

He said no more about it then or at any hour after, and he did not ask for anything, and nothing was given to him and nothing was promised to him, and he pumped that lighter out over the following three days and she was broken up on that quay on the fourth of August in nine foot of water against the wall, which is a different water from the edge of the fairway and not the same four foot, with her frames cut out and nothing landed out of her at any hour.

**AND HE WAS NOT COMFORTED AND WAS NOT THANKED AND WAS NOT FORGIVEN, BECAUSE THERE WAS NOTHING TO ASK FOR AND NOBODY ASKED FOR ANYTHING, AND A MAN WHO SAYS *THAT IS WHAT HAPPENED* ON A QUAY IN A HAZARD JACKET IS NOT ABSOLVING ANYBODY AND IS NOT CONDEMNING ANYBODY AND IS THE ONLY THING HE HAD.**

---

At about half past three the day's first column was entered with the hours and the figure. At ten past five the holder was on the mole head, and there was nothing up there and nobody had sent him.

The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-five days. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eighty-first night. The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and fifteen days old. The second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May, and nobody went down to it on the said day.

**AND A HARBOR IS TWO HUNDRED AND FOUR PENCE RICHER ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF JULY THAN IT WAS ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH, BEING A DAY OF CAULKING PAID BY A HERRING SKIPPER WHO IS NOT USING ANY PAPERS FOR ANYTHING, AND THE CHEAPEST THING IN THAT HARBOR IS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE MOLES OF THAT FIGURE AND THAT ONE ARE FURTHER APART THAN THEY WERE A WEEK AGO, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING ANYWHERE ON ITS OWN.**

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirty-first day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Saturday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a day of caulking and painting was done on a boat of about thirty-one tons at the third lay from about seven in the morning until about half past three, being nine hours by the holder and a man of about twenty-nine with a caulking iron who is not on the stone and is not owed a penny by anybody and who brought his own bread and his own water out of his own hand; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one put one hundred and sixty-eight pence on the bench stone in coin at about four in the afternoon, being the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall, and nothing went over that figure and nothing under it; and that the said skipper said that if the yard were busy in the autumn he would send work to whoever was not looking for it, and that he was not asking for anything to be held and nothing was booked, and that the holder said he would not be busy in the autumn, and that the said skipper said he would ask again in the spring; and that a lighter of about thirty tons lay at that quay with about a foot and four inches of water in her, having come in on the night of the twenty-seventh on her own account because she was making water at her seams, with about two tons of flour in her bottom wet through the boards; and that a man of about fifty-three in that lighter said to the holder on the said day at about two in the afternoon that he was in the fairway on Tuesday and that his boat came up two foot where the book said eleven and that he cut his own line and the tow took it and the painter parted, and that the said man said that is what happened and said he was not going to call it anything else, and asked for nothing and was given nothing and was promised nothing; and that about twenty-six tons of that flour is in seven boats of that tow of eight which are lying off the point at about a mile and have not come in and have not been hailed, and that nobody landed a sack off that lighter and no figure of any kind was written against her and no claim was made on her and the clerk of the office on the west basin was not sent for; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into it and nothing went out, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the figure of four hundred and fifty pence for a barrel of lamp oil, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, was said out loud in the road by the chandler of about fifty to a man who did not buy, with the figure of three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, left visible above it on the board and both dates on it, and that the said figure did not move on the said day, and that a figure put on that board cannot be taken off it; and that a man who says a thing happened is not forgiving anybody and is not condemning anybody and is not being kind; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and second line:*

*What it is not: — that a day of caulking paid by a man with papers of his own who is not using them for a convoy is not a harbour's recovery and is not anybody's apology; and that a bag two hundred and four pence heavier is not a harbour better off and the cheapest thing in it has not moved and the light at the mole head is out; and that a lighter with wet flour in her bottom is not the flour and is not a delivery and is not a thing landed, and no sack of it has come ashore and nobody is going to say that any has.*
