# Chapter 365

*Monday the Nineteenth of July, the Thirty-Fifth Day of the Ninety, the Wall Bare for the Sixty-Third Time and Put Up Again at the Angle Since the Twelfth, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Fifth Time, a Woman of No Office Asking for the Cost to Be Said Out Loud in a Room With a Clerk in It, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Monday the nineteenth of July, in Veyr in that room, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, and was a rota day and a wipe, being the sixty-third wipe and the sixty-fifth charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-third time and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on none of it by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-fifth, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, being in a city, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was out of that harbor on the said day and in Veyr, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

In that room at about ten in the morning the man of about fifty put the municipal paper back on the table beside the eight lines, and said the room had written yesterday what the sixth line does for a convoy with papers, and that today it would write nothing unless it was shown something.

The woman of no office sat at the table with her hands in her lap and looked at the man and not at the papers.

"Then show it what a refusal costs," she said. "Not in a shed. Here. In a room with a clerk in it. Say it out loud."

Calder Marr looked at her, because she had not told him she was going to say it, and because a thing asked for in a room is a thing that has to be answered in that room or not at all.

"I will say it tomorrow," he said. "I will not say it today without having thought about it, because a man who says what a thing costs without thinking is a man inventing a figure, and I have no figure."

"You have no figure," she said. "I have no figure and I will not invent one. Say what it is and not what it is worth."

The man of about fifty said the room would hear it tomorrow at ten, and the clerk in the room off it wrote the hour for tomorrow and asked nothing, and nobody asked him anything.

That was the whole of the morning, and they went out down the stairs into the noise, and neither of them said anything about tomorrow, because a thing promised for tomorrow in a room is not a thing to talk about on stairs.

---

At about twelve in that shed the ship's hand put the wall up at the angle since the twelfth, because she was the only one of the yard in it, and she entered the sixty-third wipe and the sixty-fifth charge in the first column in her own left hand, with the figures and the hours.

A man of about forty-four of the six came past the shed at about two with his left hand in a sling and his two fingers bound, being the seventh day off the water of his nine, and he asked for nothing and was asked for nothing, and the ship's hand gave him water at the door and he drank it and went back up the mole to look at the page with six names on it.

**He is off the water nine days from the twelfth and is one of about eleven people of that harbor who cannot read the notice, and he was not added to the count because nobody asked, and no part of his bill of five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, came out of that yard.**

At about three a copyist of about fifty-nine in a room off that market sat at her counter copying what she has never asked about, with twenty-two years of it behind her, and nobody in that yard asked her what she was copying, because a copyist who is not asked is a copyist who can be asked nothing.

At about ten past five by that city's bell Calder and the woman stood at the landing and looked at the water, and she said one sentence about tomorrow and it was not about him.

"A refusal has a cost whether it is said out loud or not," she said. "Tomorrow it will be said out loud. That does not make it larger and does not make it smaller."

He nodded, because there was nothing in it to improve on.

*First column, entered on Monday the nineteenth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed by the ship's hand in her own left hand, the holder and the woman being in Veyr: that the said Monday was a wipe day and a rota day, being the sixty-third wipe and the sixty-fifth charge, and that the wall came down bare for the sixty-third time and went up again at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back, and that the cistern was charged and walked on none, the sixty-fifth, and the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that in a room in Veyr a woman of no office asked that what a refusal costs be said out loud in a room with a clerk in it, and that the holder said he will say it tomorrow at ten and will not invent a figure; and that the man of about fifty said the room would hear it tomorrow, and that the clerk of about sixty in a room off it wrote the hour and asked nothing and was asked nothing; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-eight weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them, the next falling due on Tuesday the twentieth.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about forty-four is on the seventh day off the water of his nine with his hand bound and asked for nothing; and that a copyist of about fifty-nine copies what she has never asked about; and that the tender of about ninety tons is still at the outer water with two lines out and no officer ashore and no paper ashore; and that a page of forty lines in a town four days up that coast is on a wall there and is not this yard's and has never been copied by anybody here; and that the standing clause entered on the fifteenth is not entered here again and stands, being the count of people who cannot read the notice and the records one in each of nine communities and the office that answers them.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninetieth line:*

*What it is not: — that asking for a cost to be said out loud in a room is not asking for a figure and is not asking for forgiveness and closes nothing else in that room and nothing else in that shed; and that saying it will be said tomorrow is not saying it today and is not refusing to say it; and that a man with his hand bound on the seventh day off the water is not this yard's injury and is not discharged by anybody's.*
