# Chapter 354

*Thursday the Eighth of July, the Twenty-Fourth Day of the Ninety, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Second Time, Six Names and a Boat on a Page on the Crown of That Mole in a Hand Nobody Corrected, a Boy of About Seventeen Carrying a Word Ashore That Nobody Wrote and Did Not Sign, One Sentence from a Man of Sixty-One Who Was Not Asked for Any, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Thursday the eighth of July, on that mole, and in that shed, and at the cistern.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a wipe and was a rota day, and the cistern was charged on it at about eight in the morning and walked on by the man with the hose at about half past nine the same morning, the sixty-second, and the next charge falls on Monday the twelfth of July, being the sixty-third, and the next wipe falls on the same Monday, being the sixty-second. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing new in the bag, which holds seventy-six pence, being six shillings and fourpence, of which sixty-eight pence came in on the Wednesday and eight pence of it is the last of what came in on the day before that and there is no mark in the bag at all, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and sixty-four pence, being three marks and seven shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The cistern is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and there is no figure against it anywhere in this harbor and the rota is a piece of board with four names on it and a man of about thirty-two has had the top name off it since the spring and nobody has put it back.

They charged it at eight and walked on it at half past nine, and the walking-on is a thing that has happened at every charge since the first of March and is a custom of this harbor and not a decision anybody takes, and the reason for it has never been written down and is not going to be, and the two men who walked on it were a man of about thirty-two with a hose and a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime, and the second of those is the man who came into the shed on the Wednesday and asked whether he was the eleventh, and neither of them said anything about the instrument of the seventh and neither of them was asked.

---

The page went up on the crown of that mole at about eleven in the morning, and it went up on the stone under the third tarpaulin, which is the dry stone, and it is the same stone the two objects of nobody's have been sitting on since the spring, nine feet apart, and it is four feet from the nearer of them and nobody has moved either of them and nobody has said whether that is on purpose.

The man who put it there is about fifty-one and he is one of the six who are ashore with no boat, and he is not a man of no standing whatever, and he is not a man who is going to be called anything by anybody.

He wrote it himself. He wrote it with a stub of pencil on the back of a reeve's office docket, and it took him about half an hour, and his hand is the hand of a man who learned to write late and wrote a great deal of shipping in it for thirty years, and there is no spelling in it that anybody in that harbor would have written differently and there are two that somebody in that harbor would have written differently, and **no person in this yard corrected either of them and no person in that yard has a copy of that page and no person in that yard was asked to keep one.**

What is on it, and it is entered here as it stands and not as anybody would write it:

> The six of us. The 19th of June, twenty minutes to six in the evening, off the south end.
> A boat. That is the price. We have no figure and we are not putting one on.
> Nobody asked us. We were not there to be asked.
> — Six names, and the six names are in their own hands and four of them are in the same hand and two of them are in a different and worse one.

And at the bottom, in the same hand and about a foot below the six names, because he thought of it after he had written the rest and did not want to be asked whether he had:

> Written by me because it is my paper. Not asked in. The mole is not his and not hers and it is the Harbor's.

Nobody in that shed read that page on the Thursday and nobody was given it and nobody copied it. The reeve's office was not told about it. The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall walked past it at about one o'clock and looked at it for about half a minute and went on. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay read it standing and gave no opinion and went back to work.

**THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT WENT UP THAT MOLE AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN, STOOD IN FRONT OF THAT PAGE FOR ABOUT TWO MINUTES, AND WENT BACK DOWN, AND DID NOT PUT A HAND ON IT, AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT BY ANYBODY, AND SAID ONE SENTENCE TO NOBODY, AND THE SENTENCE WAS THAT IT IS HIS OWN MISTAKE THAT IT IS ON THE HARBOR'S STONE AND NOT ON THE SHED WALL AND HE IS NOT GOING TO MOVE IT.**

It stayed up through the rest of that day and through the eighth and it is up now, and nobody has taken it down and nobody in this yard will, and the notice of the seventeenth day of May past is on a different wall in a different street and is not next to it and has not been compared with it by anybody in that harbor.

---

The boy came ashore off a lighter at about half past three in the afternoon and he is about seventeen and he is not on the stone of any yard and has not been since the fifteenth of May past and is not to be put on it, and he carried a word and not a paper.

**The word was this, and it is in his mouth and nobody wrote it for him and it is not signed and it did not come off an office's table, and the boy said so before anybody asked him anything: a steamer is lying at the moorings at Ternwatch, and there is a second vessel with her, and a man of about thirty-four came off the second one at four o'clock on the Monday and walked up the yard and went into a building with a door that has no glass in it, and that is the whole of what the boy had.**

He got it from a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime, who got it from a lighterman without a road, who got it from a coal boat that goes up that coast twice a month. None of them has an office. None of them has a reason to be careful. **And nobody in that harbor has asked the boy where he got it and the boy has not been asked by anybody and he is not a courier and he was not paid and he took himself off down to the south end before anybody thought to say anything about it.**

What is in the second column against that word is only this: that a steamer at the moorings is a fact a lighterman saw and not an instrument, and that the second vessel is a fact a boy carried and not a census, and that a door with no glass in it is a door.

---

At about ten past four on that crown of that mole, Tobias Wren was taking the turn of the eleven pieces of a boat he has been building since the spring, and the holder of that writ came up about four and a half and stood about six feet off and did not go over and did not say anything for about two minutes, and then said, not to the man of sixty-one, but out loud, in the ordinary way a person says a thing they have been carrying:

"**Twenty-six years ago they put a paper on a boat of mine at Veyr. The paper was for leaving and the boat went on being theirs about two years after the paper was taken off her, and I have never found out by what right, and I did not ask, because I was thirty-five and I had a winter's work in hand and I wanted the winter's work.**"

And the man of sixty-one did not answer it and did not look up, and the holder of that writ did not ask him a second question and did not say what he thought about it, and it is entered with the hour and it is not improved on by anybody, and neither of them has said since what he makes of it.

**HE WAS NOT SENT FOR. HE WAS NOT ASKED FOR ANYTHING. HE IS NOT A TEACHER AND NOT A DOOR AND NOT A SACRIFICE AND HE IS NOT THIS YARD'S INJURY. A man of sixty-one on that crown who had something in his hand on a Thursday in July is not Alden Marr, and the man on the far bench across that crown is not Tobias Wren, and the two are not one man and no chapter has put them in one sentence.**

---

At about six the shed was shut and the north berth chain was still lying in a heap in the dark where it was put down on the Wednesday afternoon, and the reeve's office had nothing else on the list that week, and nobody in that harbor has been paid for anything since the Wednesday, and the price of a barrel of lamp oil is four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, and the figure quoted on the first day of June past is still the figure and February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, is still visible above it with both dates on the board, and the mole-head light is not lit.

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the eighth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the cistern was charged at about eight in the morning of the said day and walked on by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-second, and that the top name on the rota board has been off it since the spring and has not been put back and no figure stands against that cistern in any book in this harbor; and that a page was put up on the dry stone on the crown of that mole at about eleven in the morning, on the back of a reeve's office docket, in the hand of a man of about fifty-one of the six who are ashore with no boat, and that it carries the six of them, the nineteenth day of June, twenty minutes to six in the evening, off the south end, and the price, being a boat, and that no figure is on it and none is going on, and that the six names are in their own hands and that no person in that yard corrected either of the two spellings in it and that no person in that yard has a copy of it and was not asked for one; and that it is on the harbor's stone and not on this yard's wall and that the holder of that writ went up that mole at about half past eleven, stood in front of it about two minutes, did not put a hand on it, was not asked for it, said one sentence to nobody, and went back down; and that a boy of about seventeen came ashore off a lighter at about half past three in the afternoon carrying a word and not a paper, being that a steamer is lying at the moorings at Ternwatch with a second vessel with her, and that a man of about thirty-four came off the second one at four o'clock on the Monday and walked up that yard and went into a building with a door that has no glass in it, and that the word came from a man with a hod of lime off a lighterman without a road off a coal boat and is not signed and came off no office's table, and that nobody in that harbor asked the boy where he got it and he is not a courier and was not paid; and that at about ten past four in the afternoon the man of sixty-one on that crown said twenty-six words standing at a boat he is building, and that he was not asked for anything and that nothing of it was improved on.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the page on that mole is on the same stone as the two objects of nobody's and about four feet from the nearer of them, and that neither of those two objects has moved and nobody has moved them and nobody has said whether the four feet is on purpose; and that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past is on a different wall in a different street and has not been compared with that page by anybody in that harbor; and that the chain raised at the north berth on the Wednesday was still lying in a heap in the dark on the Thursday evening and that the office of the harbor authority had nothing else on its list that week and that nobody in that harbor has been paid for anything since the Wednesday; and that a lighterman saw a steamer and a boy carried a word and a door has no glass in it, and that none of the three is an instrument, and that this yard is not going to write up that coast to find out more, and did not.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that a page with six names on it is not a claim and is not a demand and is not an instrument and has no jurisdiction whatever, and that the mole is the harbor's and not this yard's, and that the man who wrote it said so on the bottom of it in his own hand and got a sentence wrong and nobody here corrected him; and that a boy who carries a word ashore is not a courier and is not a face of anything and the door he described is a door; and that twenty-six words said by a man of sixty-one about a paper on a boat of his in a city four days from here twenty-six years ago is not an explanation of the instrument of the seventh and is not a warning and is not advice, and he was not asked and will not be, and the man on the far bench is not him.*
