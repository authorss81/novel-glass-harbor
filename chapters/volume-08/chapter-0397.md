# Chapter 397

*Friday the Twentieth of August, the Sixty-Seventh Day of the Ninety, Seven Hands at the Foot of a Sheet With an Hour Against Each of Them and No Figure and No Wage and Nothing in It for Anybody and Nothing in It Against Anybody, the Whole of a Sheet Read Aloud to a Man Who Cannot Read for the Third Time and a Mark at the Foot of It in His Own Hand, a Tender of About Ninety Tons at the Head of a Line of Boats for the Twenty-Sixth Day and No Reason Given for Anything, and the Name of a Day Given Aloud on a Quay in Front of the Boats With the Tide as the Reason and the Reason Not the Whole of the Reason*

Friday the twentieth of August, on that quay, in that shed, and at the third lay.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and seven hands went at the foot of a sheet in the front room of that shed between about nine in the morning and about half past two in the afternoon, and no figure went against any of them and no wage and no price and no share, and there is nothing in that sheet for anybody and nothing in it against anybody, and the four lines that are blank are the four men who said no and they are blank and they are going to stay blank.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she stood at the back of that front room from about ten until about one and did not ask one question and did not read that sheet. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-five days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-seven days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

The sheet went round the front room on the bench stone and nobody read it out to anybody who could read it, because there was nothing on it that a man who could read wanted.

The master of about fifty-five of that third lay put his hand at the foot of it at about nine in the morning and gave the hour himself, and it was a quarter to nine, and Calder wrote the hour and not the name of the hour, and the master read the hour back and said it was right. The master of about thirty of that third lay put his hand down at about half past nine and gave the hour and did not read it back because he cannot read and had not offered to and nobody asked him to. The master of about forty of the second lay, and the master of about forty-one of that lay, and the master of about thirty-eight of that lay, and the master of about twenty-nine of that second lay, in that order, and the hours against them were eleven o'clock, twenty past eleven, twenty-five minutes to twelve, and half past twelve.

That is seven hands and seven hours and the sheet does not say what any of them is paid and does not say what any of them owes and does not say what day it is.

The seventh of the seven was the master of about thirty-six of the second lay, and he cannot read and he has never been able to write his own name, and the whole of the sheet was read out to him a third time in that front room at about a quarter past one in the afternoon, and Calder read it from the top and did not skip a line and did not read the hours out of an order of his own choosing, and when he came to the end of the left side the man put his own thumb on the bench stone and stopped him.

"Read me the two at the top again," he said. "The first man in the first boat and the second man in the first boat. I have been sitting here for an hour and I want to hear those two and I am not going to tell you why."

Calder read the two again and the man of about thirty-six said that was right and nothing else, and Calder did not ask him why, and the master of about thirty-six then put a mark at the foot of the sheet with the blunt end of a pencil that was not sharpened for it, and the mark was a cross with one arm longer than the other, and he gave the hour, which was twenty-five minutes to two, and Calder wrote the hour and wrote the word *mark* against the hand and not the word *signature*, because the man could not sign and had not pretended to.

**AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TO SIGN THAT SHEET IN ADVANCE AND EVERY ONE OF THE SEVEN CAME AND PUT HIS OWN HAND AT THE FOOT OF IT ON THE DAY, AND ONE OF THE SEVEN PUT A MARK AT THE FOOT OF IT AND IT WAS CALLED A MARK, AND NO MASTER WAS ASKED TWICE, AND EVERY ONE OF THE SEVEN WAS TOLD WHAT WAS IN IT AND WHAT WAS NOT IN IT BEFORE HE PUT ANYTHING AT THE FOOT OF IT, AND THE FOUR WHO SAID NO ARE NOT ON IT AND ARE NOT GOING ON IT.**

---

At about two in the afternoon Calder went down to the head of the third lay and said the thing out loud, in front of the boats, because he had said on the tenth and again on the thirteenth that he would say it on a quay and not in a shed.

"Saturday the twenty-first of August. Out on the flood at about half past seven and in on the evening's flood at about half past five. Seven of you and a sheet between you and a man on that mole head at four in the morning and at four in the afternoon, and he has been asked for that day by name and he has said yes to that day and I will pay him for it at four o'clock on the day and not before and not after, and it comes out of my own figures and not one penny of it comes out of the owner of any boat on this quay."

About nine men of that lay and the two lays were on the quay or on the steps. Nobody said anything.

"The reason is the tide," Calder said. "A flood is the only water this harbor can make and a flood is the only hour a loaded transport can come down that reach going that way, and that is the reason and that reason is true. It is not the whole of the reason."

"Then say the rest of it," said the master of about thirty of that third lay.

"No."

"You stood on this quay on the tenth and told me you would say why here and not in the shed, and you have said a quarter of it."

"I have said a quarter of it and the quarter I have said is true and the three quarters I have not said are mine and I am not going to lay them on a quay in front of nine men on a Friday afternoon, because if I say them now then in a week somebody will quote me and I will have said it in the wrong order to the wrong men." Calder did not move his hands. "You will hear the rest of it from me and it will not be from me to a man on a quay asking me a question, and I am telling you now that I am not going to pretend otherwise, and I would rather you were angry with me on a Friday than disappointed on a Monday."

"I am not angry," said the master of about thirty. "I am going to be in that water in a day and I have a right to know what the rest of it is, and you have told me twice now that you will not say it, and that is a thing at least."

**AND THE NAME OF A DAY WAS SAID ALOUD ON A QUAY IN FRONT OF THE BOATS ON THE TWENTIETH OF AUGUST, AND THE REASON GIVEN WAS THE TIDE, AND THE REASON WAS TRUE AND WAS NOT THE WHOLE OF IT, AND THE REST OF IT WAS NOT SAID BY ANYBODY ON THAT QUAY ON THAT DAY, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS TOLD THAT A TRANSPORT WAS DUE ON THAT REACH, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAD BEEN ASKED ABOUT A TRANSPORT BY ANYBODY, AND THIS YARD DID NOT ASK AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

---

The tender of about ninety tons was at the head of eight boats inside the line of that harbor on the said day, and that was her twenty-sixth day, and nobody in that harbor was told what she was waiting for and nobody hailed her and nobody went near her and no man was put aboard her and no man of this yard had been in her at any hour of this month.

She was not there on the Saturday morning, and this yard did not see her go and has not asked anybody where she went and is not going to, and the eight boats she had been at the head of were not there on the Saturday morning either, and nobody in that harbor has written a day against either of those two facts and neither of them is in any return.

**AND A TENDER OF ABOUT NINETY TONS WAS AT THE HEAD OF EIGHT BOATS INSIDE THE LINE FOR TWENTY-SIX DAYS AND WAS NOT THERE ON THE TWENTY-FIRST, AND A TOW OF SEVEN BOATS WITH ABOUT TWENTY-TONS OF FLOUR IN THEM HAS BEEN LYING AT ABOUT A MILE OFF THAT POINT SINCE THE EVENING OF THE FIRST OF AUGUST, AND THE TWO LIGHTS OF THEM HAVE NOT MOVED IN NINETEEN DAYS, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS HAILED THEM AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR KNOWS WHOSE THEY ARE.**

---

The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall came down the front street at about four in the afternoon with her slate under her arm and stood in the front room of that shed and put the slate on the bench stone face up, which she does not do.

"There is a figure on that slate," she said, "in chalk, with the day of the month beside it, and I put it there on the twenty-fifth of July and it cannot be taken off it again and this yard did not put it there and cannot take it off it. And on Saturday morning, when those boats of yours are on the water and I have not got a thing to sell anybody who is not going out, there will be nine men in this market who have not got a boat and who will ask me what a mark and eightpence buys. And I will tell them what it buys. And I am telling you now, on a Friday, that I am not going to tell them one word about a sheet in a boat or a man on a stone or anything else, and I would rather you knew that before Saturday than after it."

"That is your stall and your figure and your answer," Calder said, "and there is nothing in that I could have stopped and I am not going to stand here and thank you for it, because you have not done it for me."

"I have not," said the woman of about fifty-eight. "I have done it for the nine."

*First column, entered on Friday the twentieth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Friday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that seven hands went at the foot of a sheet in the front room of that shed between about nine in the morning and about half past two in the afternoon with an hour against each of them, being a quarter to nine, half past nine, eleven o'clock, twenty past eleven, twenty-five minutes to twelve and half past twelve for six of them, and twenty-five minutes to two for the seventh, who put a mark at the foot of it with a pencil that was not sharpened for it, the mark being a cross with one arm longer than the other; and that the seventh of the seven cannot read and the whole of the sheet was read out to him for the third time at about a quarter past one and he asked for two names at the top of the first boat to be read again and they were read again and he was not asked why; and that every one of the seven was told what was in that sheet and what was not in it before he put anything at the foot of it, and that no master was asked twice and no paper was signed in advance by anybody; and that the four lines that are blank are the four men who said no and they stayed blank; and that the holder went down to the head of the third lay at about two in the afternoon and said the name of the day out loud in front of about nine men of two lays, being Saturday the twenty-first of August, out on the flood at about half past seven and in on the evening's flood at about half past five, and that he said the reason was the tide and that a flood is the only water this harbor can make and the only hour a loaded transport can come down that reach going that way, and that the reason is true and is not the whole of the reason; and that a master of about thirty of that third lay asked him to say the rest of it and that he said no, and that he said he would rather a man be angry with him on a Friday than disappointed on a Monday; and that nobody in that harbor was told that a transport was due on that reach and this yard did not ask; and that a tender of about ninety tons was at the head of eight boats inside the line on the said day for her twenty-sixth day and was not there on the Saturday morning and that this yard did not see her go and has not asked where she went; and that a tow of seven boats with about twenty tons of flour in them has been lying at about a mile off that point since the evening of the first of August and the two lights of them have not moved in nineteen days counted from the hour they were first seen; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall put her slate on the bench stone face up at about four in the afternoon and said the figure on it is in chalk with the day of the month beside it, put there on the twenty-fifth of July past, and that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off it again and this yard did not put it there and cannot take it off it, and that there will be nine men in that market on Saturday morning who have not got a boat and who will ask her what a mark and eightpence buys; and that she said she would tell them what it buys and would not say one word about a sheet in a boat or a man on a stone, and that she said she had not done it for him.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that a hand at the foot of a sheet and an hour against it is not a signature where a man cannot sign and is not a return and is not a contract, and that a mark made with a pencil that was not sharpened for it is a mark and is the only mark in that harbor that is on a piece of paper and was not put there by a man who is paid to put things there; and that a name read out a third time to a man who cannot read is not a favour and is not a kindness and is the third of three and the third is not owed to him by anybody; and that a day named out loud on a quay with the tide as the reason and the reason not the whole of the reason is a decision and not a finding, and that the part of the reason that was not said is not held back from the men who are going out and is not held back from anybody, and that it is the holder's own and he has not given it to a room and is not going to; and that a woman with an end stall and a slate deciding what she will say on a market morning is not an ally and is not a witness and has told this yard in advance so that it could not be said afterwards that it was sprung on her, and that she has done nothing for that yard.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and twenty-second line:*

*What it is not: — that seven hands and seven hours at the foot of a sheet is not a crew and is not a muster and is not a roll and is not a company and is not a fleet and is not the beginning of one, and that a paper with names in it and no figure in it is not a paper anybody owns; and that a mark made with a pencil that was not sharpened for it is not a signature and is not a forgery and is not a lesser kind of anything; and that a day named on a quay with the tide as the reason is not a reason and the reason that was given is not the whole of the reason and the part that was not given is not a lie; and that a figure in chalk on a slate at the end stall of a market cannot be taken off and was not put there by that yard and is not that yard's and a slate is not a wall.*
