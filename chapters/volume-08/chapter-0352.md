# Chapter 352

*Tuesday the Sixth of July, the Twenty-Second Day of the Ninety, the Thirty-Seventh Week and a Wage Not Paid, a Woman of About Sixty-One in a Fish Market with a Paper in a Box She Cannot Read and Eleven Days Old, a Chandler's Counter Shut to One Man and a Sentence Recorded a Second Time, the Notice on a Wall Not Touched, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Tuesday the sixth of July, in that fish market, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-seventh week, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column did not move, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and thirty-seven weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that fish market on the said morning and heard what is entered below and did not hear it from him. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The eleventh man was found in about nine people's hearing and it took about forty minutes, and nothing was said at him for any of them.

She is sixty-one or near it and she is of the fish market and she has a stall two in from the end, which is not the end stall, and the woman who holds the end stall is of about fifty-eight and has a slate and did not come over. The sixty-one-year-old was folding a herring and she had a paper in the flat of her left hand the whole time and she put it down on the board when she was asked to and she did not open it.

The yard's own man asked her the question, and he asked it in the way the question is asked on that coast, which is that he said what he wanted, which was the four things, and then he said what the four things were, and then he said what they were for, and then he stopped.

What he said in the fourth part, standing in front of her board, was: *they want the paper people can check, and they want the eleven leaves that are in five towns, and they want the last one which is a man signing a piece of writing that says a thing about a ferry is true as it has already been written, and I am not able to hand any of it over and I am not able to answer for you.*

That is on the page and it is in his own mouth and he said it and it was four sentences and it took about half a minute and eleven people at that market heard the whole of it.

**And then he did not say the next part, and the next part was the reason, and the reason was that she cannot read.**

It is not that she is slow. It is that in this harbor there are about eleven people who cannot read the notice and the number is a number of people and not a figure and it was arrived at by two people counting separately and adding and it has not been printed in any office in this country. Nobody in this yard is to tell any of them anything. That is not a kindness and it is not a rule of this yard's own making and it is written nowhere; it came about because in May a boy of about seventeen carried a word up a hill in front of a woman and the carrying of it was the telling, and the telling of it was how a thing got to be known in a market before the person it was about could say he did not want it known.

So the holder of that writ stood in front of a woman of about sixty-one with a paper in her hand and was not able to say one word about what was on it, because saying a word about it was the telling, and he could not ask her for a signature on a line of writing she had not read, and he could not ask her whether she wanted the line of writing to be kept or given away, and he could not ask her anything at all.

She looked at him for about as long as it takes to fold a herring.

"**You have read me the whole of that twice, you. I don't know what a leaf is. I have had this eleven days.**"

She put her hand on the paper on the board without opening it.

"**A man gave me this at the back of the fish market and he said it was about the boats and I said thank you and I put it in my box and I have not opened it and I am not going to bother anybody about it, and you can tell whoever wants it that it is in my box and it has been there eleven days and I am not taking it to anybody to be got rid of.**"

And she picked the herring up and went on selling it, and about six people were still standing there, and the yard's own man said the thing that goes in the second column, which is that he had come to that market with a question and had not asked it and that the not asking was in the yard's own rule and that he had made the rule himself.

**AND HE DID NOT SAY WHETHER HE WOULD HAVE ASKED IT. He has been asked about that by nobody and the yard has entered that he was not asked and that he did not say.**

The sixth of the eleven is a man of about forty-four on a hard with a boat on it and a line about her moulds that is staying on, and he is the one whose refusal is the first line on a sheet of forty lines in a drawer in that shed, and he cannot read either, and he was told about the offer by a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime who told him it was a great dole and that he should have it, and he has come to the shed on his own account on the said day at about two in the afternoon and has said one thing.

"**I have had a paper off the moon with my own name at the top of it and somebody says I was one of eleven that said no, and I said no, and if somebody wants me to say something else now that would be a second one and I have only got the one.**"

And he went back down to his hard. He is not the man of about fifty-five and he is not a refusal in any other sense and he is not on the sheet again and nobody asked him anything else and the sheet was not taken out of that drawer on the said day.

**AND THAT IS THE POINT OF THE WHOLE DAY, AND IT IS WRITTEN DOWN IN THE YARD'S OWN SECOND COLUMN WITH A REASON AGAINST IT AND NO NAME AGAINST IT: an instrument that wants eleven people to sign one sentence cannot be answered honestly by anybody in this harbor, because one of the eleven cannot read the sentence, and nobody in this yard is permitted to read it to her, and a signature obtained from a person who does not know what she is signing is not a signature and is worse than no signature, because it can be printed.**

---

The chandler is of about fifty and his wife does the counting and she was at the counter on the said day and he was behind it, and the counter has been shut to one man since the twenty-ninth day of June and has not been open to anybody since, which is a thing that has cost a chandler's shop about nine pounds a week in the ordinary way and which nobody in that building has said out loud.

He asked, in the middle of the morning, in about four people's hearing, that it be recorded that he still had nothing to say about it.

**It was recorded a second time, once, on the said day, in the shed, in the second column, and the recording says that he asked for it to be recorded again and that it was recorded again and that this yard is not going to keep asking him, because a man who has said twice that he has nothing to say has said it twice and the third time is a question dressed as a courtesy.**

The clerk of the office off the west basin is about sixty and has been twenty-two years in it and is owed a quarter, and he has no book and no power, and nobody in this yard has asked him one question about the second paper that went onto his table on the Monday, and he was not asked one on the said day either, and there is no line in any of the four columns of that leaf that is against that man and there is not going to be one.

And the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was not touched on the said day. Nothing was put on that market wall and nothing came off it, and the two older naval notices further along the same wall are still on it where they have been, and the fourth return of that yard is still on that wall under one sheet and is still the only paper on it that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and the sixth clause of that notice was not read aloud on the said day in that market or anywhere else in that harbor.

**The man of about thirty who is two days down that coast was not sent for and was not written to and nobody in this yard went up that road on the said day, and he does not know that there is an instrument, and he is not going to be told by anybody in this yard, and the yard entered that on the said day at about half past nine in the evening and did not name him.**

---

The woman of no office heard the whole of the market part of it at the end of the second aisle and she said four things and two of them were about him and neither of the two was kind.

"**You stood in front of her and you read her the prices of four leaves out loud in a market at about half past eleven on a Tuesday morning, in front of nine people, and that is the thing you have spent a year learning not to do, and you did it in a good cause.**"

"**And you did not ask her one question, and I want to know which of those two you did on purpose.**"

And then she said the other two, and both of them were work, and both of them were in the ordinary voice she uses for figures.

"**A signature off a woman who cannot read is not a signature and is not worth the paper it is on, and there are about eleven of them, and the eleventh of them has said out loud in a market that it is in her box and she is not taking it to anybody, and that is the strongest thing anybody said in that market today and it is hers and not yours.**"

"**And the four owners of the four copies are not going to be in this harbor this week, and one of them is a man of about thirty-six with a coaster and eleven weeks of a season and his father's book in his house, and you are not going to ask him either, and that is right, and it means the answer takes until the middle of next week at the earliest, and the Navy will not wait until the middle of next week.**"

And she was not asked about any of it and none of it was improved on and she went down the hill at about one and did not come back up it that day.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the sixth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-seventh week, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and that the column did not move, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and that thirty-seven weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and that the next wage Tuesday falls on Tuesday the thirteenth of July, being the thirty-eighth week, and that the column will not move on it; and that in the fish market of that harbor at about eleven in the morning of the said day, in about eleven people's hearing and not in a room, the holder of that writ said four sentences to a woman of about sixty-one standing at a stall two in from the end, being the four prices of the instrument of the seventh day of July read out and what they were for and that he was not able to hand any of it over and was not able to answer for her, and that about six people were still standing when he stopped; and that the said woman is one of about eleven people of that harbor who cannot read, the said number being two people's separate counts added and not a figure printed in any office, and that he asked her nothing and told her nothing and said why in a market and not in a shed; and that she is the eleventh of the said eleven that this batch has entered and that the count of the said eleven is the same as the count carried in the second column of this yard's own book and no more of them were found on the said day; and that the sixth of them is a man of about forty-four with a boat on a hard and a line about her moulds that is staying on, and that he is the man whose refusal is the first line on the sheet of forty lines in a drawer in that shed, and that he came to that shed on his own account at about two in the afternoon and said one sentence and went back down to his hard, and that the said sheet was not taken out of that drawer on the said day and he is not on it again.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that an instrument that wants eleven people to sign one sentence cannot be answered honestly by anybody in that harbor, because one of the eleven cannot read the sentence, and that nobody in this yard is permitted to read it to her, and that a signature obtained from a person who does not know what she is signing is not a signature and is worse than no signature because it can be printed; and that a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime had told that man of about forty-four that the instrument was a great dole and that he should have it, and that the said man came and said he had only got the one no and that a second one would be a second one, and that nobody improved on it and nobody asked him anything else; and that the man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch was not asked a question and said nothing and was not thanked; that a chandler of about fifty asked for a second time in about four people's hearing that it be recorded that he had nothing to say about it, and that it was recorded a second time, once, and that this yard is not going to keep asking him, and that a man who has said twice that he has nothing to say has said it twice; and that a paper went onto a table in an office off the west basin on the Monday with the fourth box not filled and the clerk of that office was not asked one word about it on the said day and is not going to be, and that no line of any column of this leaf stands against that clerk; and that the man of about thirty who is two days down that coast was not sent for and was not written to and that nobody in this yard went up that road on the said day; and that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was not touched and the sixth clause of it was not read aloud on the said day in that market or anywhere else in that harbor; and that the woman of no office was in that market on the said morning and heard all of it and did not hear it from him, and that she said two things about him and neither of the two was kind and both of the two were true, and that she was not asked about any of it and that none of it was improved on.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-seventh line:*

*What it is not: — that a man standing in a market and not asking a question is not a man who was afraid to ask it and is not a man who had no question and is not a man who was silent for want of a witness, and that the rule that stopped him is one he made himself in May after a boy of seventeen carried a word up a hill in front of a woman; and that a signature that cannot be read is not a consent and is not a refusal and is not a signature, and that the woman of about sixty-one who has had a paper in a box eleven days and has not opened it and is not going to bother anybody about it has done the only thing in this batch that was done entirely on her own account; and that a man with a boat on a hard and a mould line staying on, who has one no and has been told there were eleven, is not a person who has been asked twice, and that a document is not made heavier by having the same name on it twice, and that a chandler's counter being shut to one man is a cost and not a sentence and has been entered once and will not be entered a third time.*
