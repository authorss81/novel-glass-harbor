# Chapter 359

*Tuesday the Thirteenth of July, the Twenty-Ninth Day of the Ninety, the Thirty-Eighth Week and a Wage Not Paid and the Column Not Moved, Two Fingers off a Left Hand at Half Past Two in the Afternoon off the Foul and Five Shillings and Sixpence out of the Same Man's Own Hand, an Answer Coming Down That Coast Road in the Coal at About Six in the Morning and One Line Filled In and the Sheet Not Recopied, a Man of About Thirty-Four of a Packet at the Mole Head at Ten Past Five Saying Nine Words, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Tuesday the thirteenth of July, in that shed, and on that mole, and off the foul.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-eighth week, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column did not move, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and thirty-eight weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and the bag holds two hundred and four pence, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and forty marks whole stand against a boy of twenty-two who was not on the stone on the said day and was not put on it and the three nots of the eighth day of June stand unasked. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The man of about forty-four had a hand in that water on the Monday because he wanted the day, the same as the other one did, and he was not on the crew and he was not on the list and he was not on the wage, and the one hundred and sixty-eight pence went to the other man and the whole of it, and this one was told the terms before he went out and said he knew them, which is a thing about this harbor and not about him.

**He worked a day for nothing on the Monday and was hurt on it and paid five shillings and sixpence for the hurt, and that is in the second column of that yard's own book with the reason against the line, and the reason is that he was not on the list, and nobody in that yard put him on it and nobody in that yard has thought about it since.**

At about half past two in the afternoon the buoy came up on the hand-rope and the shackle came with her, and the shackle caught him, and the top of two fingers of his left hand went off in about the time it takes to say it, and he was not in the water and he did not go in and there was no blood in that boat to speak of.

**There is no bonesetter on that coast on a Monday afternoon. There is a shipwright of about sixty with a bench in the front street and he set it at about four o'clock in the afternoon with a strip off a leech and a needle and about forty minutes, and a bonesetter of about fifty came on the Saturday and looked at it and said it was not a bonesetter's work and had not done it and was not going to touch it, and the first man to be seen by the second was a shipwright and that is the whole of it.**

The bill was five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, and it was paid by the man out of his own hand in coin, and it was said twice — once by the shipwright and once by the man — and neither of them said it a third time, and no part of it came out of that yard and no part of it came out of the list of the harbor authority and nobody in that office put it on anything.

**He is off the water nine days from the thirteenth and there is no boat in that harbor that is carrying him and he is one of about eleven people of that town who cannot read the notice, which is a count of people and not a figure and which this yard has never added him to before and has not added him to now, because nobody asked and the count is two people counting separately and adding and it is not a roll.** That last was the shipwright's wife, at the door, and she said it to nobody in particular and it was heard by two people and one of them wrote it down.

He is not the man at the far bench and he is not the man on the crown of that mole and neither of those two men lost anything on the thirteenth day of July.

---

The answer came down the coast road in the coal at about six in the morning, and it was folded once and it was in the inside pocket of the same boat's own boy, who took it out of the pocket and gave it to the man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and who did not know what it was either.

**It is the answer of a man of about thirty-six with a coaster of about thirty tons and eleven weeks of a season, and it is one sentence: no. And then: *My father wrote the spelling and not me, and a man does not give up his father's book because a piece of paper came ashore in the coal.* And the man's own copy is in a coal locker in a town four days up that coast and it is the same coal locker and it was not taken out to be carried and nobody went in that coal locker and nobody in that town knew there was a coal locker with anything in it that mattered.**

Then the ship's hand filled in the empty line on the sheet of thirteen lines — the line that had been empty on the Saturday and had not been a hole — with his name and the hour of the answer, being about six in the morning on the said day, and did not touch either of the two lines that carry a place and no price and no payer.

**AND THE SHEET WAS NOT RECOPIED, and none of the five places of that sheet has a fresh copy on it because of anything that happened on the said day, and nobody in that yard thinks it should, and the woman of no office said one sentence about it at about nine in the morning, which was that a sheet with a line filled in on it thirteen days after it went up a coast is a sheet and not a record, and that if this yard cannot leave a line empty on a paper for four days then it should not be putting papers up coast roads.**

---

At about half past nine in the morning at the end of the third lay, a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons said one thing to a man of about thirty-one of the third lay and it was about eleven feet of water and a chart in a bag.

They have said this to each other before, in a market in the last month, and neither of them has said it since, and the whole of what is entered is that the man of about thirty-eight said he would rather that chart was read out in that market in front of nine people than carried about in a bag for another nine years, and that the man of about thirty-one said he knew that, and that he had had it in a bag for nine years and looked at it himself about once a season and he was the person who wanted it looked at, and that he had not changed his mind and was not going to, and that neither of them said anything else and nobody improved on it and nobody else heard it.

**THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT SAID IT OUT LOUD A SECOND TIME ON THAT DAY, AT THE END OF THE THIRD LAY, TO A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THAT SAME LAY, AND IT WAS HEARD BY TWO PEOPLE, AND ONE OF THEM WROTE IT DOWN AND PUT NO NAME AGAINST IT. HE IS A PERSON OF NO OFFICE AND HE IS NOT A WITNESS AND HE WAS NOT TURNED INTO ONE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED HIM FOR ONE THING SINCE HE CAME DOWN THAT COAST, AND A STONE WITH A BOAT'S HOLE IN IT IS ON NO PAGE OF THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK.**

---

At about ten past five in the evening, on the third step from the bottom of that mole, a man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait stood with a line in his hand that is not his, being a copy of the sixth clause of a notice made out in his own hand on the twenty-seventh of May past.

He had come down that coast on the Friday and he had been in that harbor for four days and had not gone up to the crown and had not gone into the shed and had not asked anybody anything, and he had not slept more than about two hours a night, and he was not paid for any of the four days and nobody in that harbor thought of paying him and nobody has entered a figure for four days of a man's time on that coast because this yard does not know what a day of his is worth and is not going to invent one, and **he works for nobody and is not a courier and is not a servant and is not a face of anything, and he is a person who came down a coast to look at a boat and is going back in the morning.**

He said nine words, to nobody, and they were about the tender, and they are entered with the hour and they are not improved on and he was not asked where the nine words came from and did not say.

"**She asks at Ternwatch whether a boat has a man in her.**"

And he went down the steps and got into his packet and nobody watched him go and he was not thanked and he will not be, and the sixth clause of the notice of the seventeenth day of May past was on that mole head in a man's coat for the fifty-seventh day and nobody read it out loud on the said day and nobody stood in front of the wall it is written on.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the thirteenth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-eighth week, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and that the column did not move, and that the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and that thirty-eight weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that a man of about forty-four, being the second of the six men of that harbor who are ashore with no boat, who had the ground chain up off the north berth on the seventh and who went into the water off the foul on the Monday on his own account, not on the list and not on the crew and not on the wage, had the top of two fingers of his left hand taken off by the shackle of an adrift mooring buoy at about half past two in the afternoon of the said Monday, and that he was not paid that day and was not asked to pay and was told the terms before he went out and said he knew them, and that he was not in the water and did not go in; and that there is no bonesetter on that coast on a Monday afternoon, and that a shipwright of about sixty set it at about four o'clock with a strip off a leech and a needle in about forty minutes, and that a bonesetter of about fifty came on the Saturday, said it was not a bonesetter's work, had not done it and would not touch it, and that the first man to be seen by the second was a shipwright; and that the bill was five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, paid by the man out of his own hand in coin and said twice and not a third time, and that no part of it came out of that yard and none out of the list of the harbor authority and nothing in that office put it on anything; and that he is off the water nine days from the said Monday; and that the wife of that shipwright said at her door that he is one of about eleven people of that town who cannot read the notice, and that two people heard it and one of them wrote it down and that he was not added to that count because nobody asked and the count is two people counting separately and adding.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the answer of a man of about thirty-six with a coaster of about thirty tons and eleven weeks of a season came down that coast road in the coal at about six in the morning of the said day and is one sentence and then one more, being no, and that his father wrote the spelling and not him and a man does not give up his father's book because a piece of paper came ashore in the coal, and that his own copy is in that coal locker and was not taken out to be carried and nobody went into that locker and nobody in that town knew what was in it; and that the empty line on the sheet of thirteen lines was filled in with his name and the hour of about six in the morning and that neither of the two lines carrying a place and no price and no payer was touched, and that the sheet was not recopied and none of its five places has a fresh copy on it because of anything that happened on the said day; and that a man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait was in that harbor for four days and slept about two hours a night and was not paid for any of the four and that no figure was entered for four days of a man's time on that coast because this yard does not know what a day of his is worth, and that he said nine words at about ten past five in the afternoon on the third step from the bottom of that mole, being that she asks at Ternwatch whether a boat has a man in her, and that he was not asked where the nine words came from and did not say.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the eighty-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that two fingers of a working man's hand and a bill of five shillings and sixpence out of his own hand and nine days off the water are not this yard's injury and are not discharged by anybody's, and that the man they came off belongs to is a man of that harbor with no boat who worked the day for nothing and was not on the list, and that the first man to set it was a shipwright and that the second man looked at it and would not touch it; that a sentence carried down a coast road in the coal thirteen days after a sheet went up that road is not a record and is not a copy and does not make the sheet a record, and the woman of no office said that in that shed and it was not improved on; that a man who would rather a chart were read out in a market than carried in a bag for nine years is not asking anybody for anything and the man with the bag has not changed his mind and neither of them is going to be cited in a room; and that nine words said by a packet man on a mole step about what a boat of about ninety tons does at Ternwatch is not an intelligence and is not a report and is not from anybody's office, and he is not a courier and is not a face of anything and nobody watched him go.*
