# Chapter 358

*Monday the Twelfth of July, the Twenty-Eighth Day of the Ninety, the Wall Bare for the Sixty-Second Time and Put Up Again at a Different Angle, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Third Time, One Day's Work off the Foul with a Boat and Two Men and a Man of About Fifty-One of the Six on a Wage for the First Time in About Two Months at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Forty Pence of Shackle and Line Gone Out the Same Afternoon, Three Channels on a Borrowed Pinger and Nothing Measured and Nobody Left in the Water, and Two Hundred and Four Pence in the Bag at the End of It*

Monday the twelfth of July, off the foul, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Monday was a wipe day and a rota day, being the sixty-second wipe and the sixty-third charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-second time and went up again at a different angle from the one it has been at since March, and one man of about forty-four said so out loud in the front street and nobody argued with him and nobody changed it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-third, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day and forty pence went out of it, and the bag held seventy-six pence at six o'clock on the morning of the said day and holds two hundred and four pence at the end of it, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and there is one mark in it, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day and came up the hill at about half past four and was told the two figures and said they were right. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The work off the foul was a mooring buoy that had gone adrift in the spring and had been found in the first week of July by a man of about thirty-four and had not been got up because the reeve's office had nothing on its list, and the list on the said morning had two items on it and this was the second.

They were out from about half past ten until about three. The fouling on that buoy was not the fouling anybody expects off that ground, and the man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book and is not Fisk and has never been Fisk was in the boat because he asked to be, and nobody asked him, and he was not thanked for asking.

**HE CAME ASHORE WITH A BORROWED PINGER IN HIS HAND, WHICH IS AN INSTRUMENT AND NOT A PERSON, AND IT IS BORROWED FROM A PRESSURE HOUSE AT TERNWATCH AND THE MAN IT IS BORROWED FROM HAS NOT BEEN ASKED ABOUT IT BY ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR AND IS NOT GOING TO BE, AND THE INSTRUMENT WENT BACK up that coast on the Tuesday in the ordinary way.**

And he said what he had seen, and it took him about a minute, and nobody improved on it and nobody asked him a second question.

"**A pinger puts down two channels on a thing that has been in the water a long while. It is not reading anything. It is counting what the iron on the other side of the hull is doing to the note, and if you have put the instrument on the wrong side of the plate you get a figure and not a truth, and everybody who has ever done this has got a figure and not a truth.**"

"**There are three.**"

"**The third one is not fouling. Fouling grows the way the water goes round a thing. That thing has been in this water since March and the water goes one way round that ground and the fouling on her cable is the other way, and I did not measure it and I did not time it and I did not go back a second day and I am not going to, and I am telling you at about three in the afternoon with a borrowed instrument in my hand because I have not got anything else to do with it and because a third channel is not a thing to sit on.**"

Then he gave the instrument back inside his coat and got back in the boat and they finished the buoy and the buoy went up the north side on a hand-rope with a great deal of water coming off her, and nobody measured the water either.

**NOTHING WAS MEASURED. NO INSTRUMENT WAS LEFT IN THAT WATER. NO PERSON OF THAT YARD WAS IN ANY WATER ON THAT DAY AFTER ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON, and the third channel is in the second column of that yard's own book with a reason against the line and no name against the reason, and the reason is that the only instrument that saw it is borrowed and goes up that coast on the Tuesday and that nobody in that harbor has a second one.**

---

The man who took the day's wage with the boat was one of the six, and there was one other man of the six in that boat who was not on the wage and not on the list and not paid, and that is entered below and it is not a favour.

He is about fifty-one and he is one of the six whose only boat went down by the head on the foul off the south end of that mole on the nineteenth of June past, and his name is on a page on the crown of that mole in his own hand with a boat as the price and no figure, and he came down to the shed at about nine on the said morning and asked for a day's work and was given one, and he was paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, out of a tin at about half past three in the afternoon in coin, and he counted it on the bollard and did not say thank you and was not thanked.

**A BOAT WITH A MAN IN HER FOR A DAY IS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE. That is what the reeve's office pays and it is what the harbor authority's list pays, for the day with the boat and the two men together, and it is not a wage and it is not a rate, and it came to one man and not to the two of them, and the second of the six was in that water for nothing and did not ask for anything and was not asked to pay for it. It is the first money that man of about fifty-one has had since the nineteenth day of June past and it is two months and it is about a fifth of what a boat of about thirty tons costs, and there is no figure for what a boat costs in this harbor because no board in this harbor has one on it and the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall has not been asked and is not going to be, and this yard did not put a figure on the page on that mole when he wrote it and is not going to put one on now.**

He put the coin in his own pocket and went back up the mole and looked at his own six names on his own page for a while and then went down to the third lay and had a quart of something, and the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said the price of a quart out loud and he paid it and it was on her board with the date on it, and nobody has taken that figure off and nobody is going to.

The other four men of the six were not in that boat and were not asked and were not told there was work, and the man of about forty-four, who is the second of the six, was in that boat on his own account and was not paid and was not asked to pay and was told the terms before he went out and said he knew them, and the man of about thirty-four of the second lay, who asked to be in the boat, was in it on his own account and was not paid and was not asked to pay.

---

At about half past four, the woman of no office came up the hill and the ship's hand gave her the two figures and she checked them, and she said one thing about them that was not about them.

"**That bag is now bigger than it has been since the eighteenth of May and it is one mark and three shillings and eightpence and it is about two days of itself, and on the first of July it was nothing, and nothing was the correct figure then, and this is not an improvement in this harbor, it is two days of one man and one set of new chain, and the harbor has not been fed by any of it and the price of oil has not moved and the mole-head light is not lit and there are six men of this town with no boat and five of them had no work today and were not told there was any.**"

And then she said the other thing, which was about a man with a borrowed instrument.

"**A third channel is not a thing. Whatever that is, it is a man of about thirty-four with a thing borrowed off a pressure house four days up this coast, telling three people on a boat at about three in the afternoon. If it is anything it is in the second column and it is not in anybody's mouth, and if it is nothing then we have spent a day of a boat and forty pence of line finding out nothing, which is what most days are, and the man who did it was not asked to be a teacher and was not made one.**"

---

At about half past five, a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime came past the shed with his hod on his shoulder and stopped and said that he had read the notice out to his wife on the Sunday, in the kitchen, in his own words, and that she had asked him what a *breach* was, and that he had said he did not know, and that they had both decided it did not matter on a Sunday.

**Nobody in that harbor said anything about that to either of them, and the woman of no office was not told about it until the Wednesday, and when she was told she said one sentence, which was that a man who says I do not know to his wife on a Sunday has done more with that notice than anybody in this town has done with it since the seventeenth of May, and that it is not going on a slate and there is no figure for it and she is not going to ask him for one.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twelfth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-second time and went up again at a different angle from the one it had been at since March, and that one man of about forty-four said so in the front street and nobody argued with him and nobody put it back; and that the cistern was charged and walked on none, the sixty-third, and that the top name on the rota board has been off it since the spring; and that a mooring buoy adrift since the spring and found in the first week of July was got up off the foul on the said day from about half past ten in the morning until about three in the afternoon, being the second of two items on the list of the harbor authority, and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book asked to be in the boat and was not asked and was not thanked for asking; and that a man of about fifty-one of the six whose boat went down by the head off the south end of that mole on the nineteenth day of June past took the whole of the said day's wage, and that he was paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, out of a tin at about half past three in the afternoon in coin, and that he counted it on a bollard and did not say thank you and was not thanked; and that a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence and that is what the harbor authority's list pays for the day with the boat and the two men together and is not a wage and is not a rate; and that the man of about forty-four, being the second of the six, was in that boat on his own account and was not paid and was not asked to pay and was told the terms before he went out and said he knew them; and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay was in it on his own account and was not paid and was not asked to pay; and that the other four men of the six were not in that boat and were not asked and were not told there was work; and that the man's own page on the crown of that mole was not altered on the said day and carries a boat as a price and no figure, and that no figure for what a boat costs exists on any board in that harbor and none was invented.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a pinger puts down two channels on a thing that has been in the water a long while, and that it is not reading anything and it is counting what the iron on the other side of a hull is doing to a note, and that a man who has the instrument on the wrong side of a plate gets a figure and not a truth; and that there are three channels on the said buoy, and that the third is not fouling, and that fouling grows the way the water goes round a thing and that the fouling on her cable goes the other way, and that it was said at about three in the afternoon by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay with a borrowed instrument in his hand and was not measured, not timed and not gone back to, and that he said he was not going to; and that the said instrument is borrowed from a pressure house at Ternwatch and went back up that coast on the Tuesday in the ordinary way, and that the man it is borrowed from has not been asked about it by anybody in that harbor and is not going to be, and that nothing was measured, no instrument was left in that water, and no person of that yard was in any water on the said day after about three in the afternoon; and that a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime read the notice out to his wife in a kitchen on the Sunday in his own words and that she asked him what a *breach* was and that he said he did not know.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the eighty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a bag of one mark and three shillings and eightpence is not an advance and is not a recovery and is not a good month, and the harbor has not been fed by any of it and the cheapest thing in it went up six shillings on the first of June and has not come down; that one of six men with no boat having a day's wage is not a resolution and is not a beginning of one, and five of them had no work and were not told there was any, and the page on that mole still says a boat and still says no figure; that a third channel on a borrowed instrument is not a discovery, is not in anybody's mouth, and is not going to be in this volume's first column, and a man who asked to be in a boat and was not asked to be anything else is not a teacher and was not made one; and that a man saying I do not know to his wife on a Sunday is not a public act and is not on any slate and has no figure against it and is the whole of what he did.*
