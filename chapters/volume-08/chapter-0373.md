# Chapter 373

*Tuesday the Twenty-Seventh of July, the Forty-Third Day of the Ninety, the Fortieth Week and a Wage Not Paid, a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Gone Out of That Bag in Coin at the Mole Head Before She Sailed, a Convoy of Eight Boats and a Tender at the Head of Them, a Board With One Word at the Head of It and Eleven Marks in a Column, a Man Who Refused Him and Then Put Him to Work, Two Foot of Water on a Line Made Years Ago, and a Tow Cut by the Man Whose Boat It Was*

Tuesday the twenty-seventh of July, at the mole head, and on the water inside the line, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Tuesday was the fortieth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and the column did not move, so that forty weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard on the said day, being a boat of perhaps eighteen tons and a man in her for a day at the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall, paid in coin at the mole head at about a quarter to seven in the morning before she sailed, and nothing came in, and there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, being on the water, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she did not go up the hill on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

He paid the man of nineteen at the mole head at about a quarter to seven in the morning, out of the bag and in coin, a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, counted onto the flat of the bench stone because a man will not take money standing up in a wind if there is a stone to put it on. The man of nineteen counted it and put it inside his coat and said nothing about the sum and nothing about the day, and they let go from the quay inside the line at about ten past seven.

**A BOAT WITH A MAN IN HER FOR A DAY IS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AND THAT IS WHAT WENT OUT, AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT IS ONE MARK AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SLATE AT THE END STALL IS THE SAME FIGURE AND WAS PUT UP BY A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT AND NOT BY THIS YARD, AND A DAY'S WORK WITH A BOAT AND TWO MEN AT A BERTH IN THIS HARBOR IS ALSO ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AND HAS BEEN SINCE THE SEVENTH OF JULY PAST, AND NEITHER PRICE IS A PRICE OF ANYTHING THAT HAPPENED ON THE WATER TODAY.**

They went out past the line at about eight with no declaration, which is in breach of the first clause of the notice of the seventeenth of May past and was in breach of the same clause on the night of the twenty-sixth of June past when ten boats of this coast did the same thing and came back in under the sixth. The man of nineteen at the tiller did not speak about it, and Calder did not explain it, and there was nobody on that quay to explain it to except a man of about nineteen who had been left with a line round a bollard for three weeks.

At about half past ten, with the flood still making and about two miles inside the line, Calder saw the tender of about ninety tons.

She was not at the outer water. She was up-coast at the head of eight boats, and there was no tender at the outer water where she had lain for seventeen days, and there was no lamp at a masthead there, and no officer had come ashore and no paper had come ashore in seventeen days, and now she was at the head of a line of eight work boats and the young man of about twenty-three was out on her roof with a telescope.

**NOBODY TOLD HIM WHAT SHE WAS FOR AND HE DID NOT ASK AND HE IS NOT GOING TO, AND THE SEVENTEEN DAYS SHE SPENT AT THAT HARBOR'S OUTER WATER ARE NOT ON ANY PAGE OF THIS SHED AGAINST ANY PERSON, AND A THING THAT WAITS AT A HARBOUR'S MOUTH AND THEN GOES TO THE HEAD OF A LINE IS A THING SOMEBODY SENT, AND THIS YARD HAS NOT SENT IT AND IS NOT GOING TO BE TOLD WHO DID.**

He let the boat fall astern of the line and worked down it. The eight boats were not in company with a man-of-war. They were work boats, some of them old, and they were not flying anything, and there was no gun on any of them that he could see, and the only thing that made them a line was that they were all doing the same thing at the same hour of the same tide.

The third boat had a man of about forty-one in her that afternoon, on her after thwart with a line across his shoulders, and he put his hand up before Calder had got the boat's side alongside, and it was not a greeting.

"You are not going to be aboard this boat."

"No."

"I did not ask you a question. I told you." The man did not stop working the line. "There is no room in her and you are not on her list, and a boat of ours does not take a man off the water without papers of her own, and there is no paper in this harbour that puts you on this boat."

"There is no paper in this harbour that puts anybody on any boat," Calder said. "I am not asking to board. I am asking to lie alongside for as long as you have got work."

"Then lie alongside and do not talk."

Calder lay alongside for about half an hour and did not talk. There were two boats in company with a lighter between them and eight of the men going down into the lighter's bottom and coming up with something in their arms, and the something was in cases, and the cases were going up into a boat with a hatch abaft and the hatch was going down again, and no case was carried past anybody and nothing was asked of anybody and there was nobody standing over the lighter with a book.

At about noon, on the second boat of the line, a young man of about twenty-six was sitting on the after thwart with a slate on his knees and a pencil behind his ear, and he looked at Calder's boat the way a man looks at a boat that has come alongside and has not gone away.

"You are the one who has been going down the line since ten," he said.

"I have."

"You are not from this coast." He turned the slate over and turned it back. "Right. Can I ask you something and you can say no."

"You can ask."

"If a hull is lifted twice, is that one hull or two."

Calder looked at the slate and did not go closer to it. On it there were eleven short strokes in a column and four columns of figures beside them, and the figures were in pounds and shillings and the strokes were not.

"I do not know," he said. "I have not counted, I have not been in any of them, and if I said one or two I would be saying it in front of you and you would write it down, and then there would be a figure on your slate with a man from off this coast behind it and neither of us would ever be able to take it off again."

The young man laughed, once, without much in it.

"Nobody does," he said. "That is the whole of my morning. I have been told to put a number in the second column for a hull that came alongside on Friday and is coming alongside again on Wednesday, and I have asked three people, and the first one said it was a different hull because it was a different day, and the second one said it was the same hull because it was the same boat, and the third one told me to come and see him in a room, and I am not going to see him in a room." He wrote one stroke on the slate and did not look up. "One. I have put one. If it is wrong it is my hand on it and not yours, and I know whose hand it is."

"You did ask," Calder said. "You asked a man in a boat instead of asking nobody, and that is a different thing from what I do, and I am not going to improve on it by saying so."

"I know what it is," said the young man. "I am not asking you to be careful. I am asking you to be somewhere I can put the question."

Calder did not answer that, because there was nothing to answer that was not going to be worse than what the young man had already got.

The board was on the after thwart of that same boat, under a sloping piece of canvas, and it was about a yard wide and a couple of feet long, and it was a board, and it had been painted and ruled and had been kept by hand for a long time, and it was lying where anybody in that boat could read it and where a man in the next boat along could read it too if he did not mind standing up in his own bow.

Across the head of it, in block capitals that had been filled in and not ruled, there was one word.

**UNSEALED.**

Under the head there were two columns ruled the whole length of the board, and the left-hand one was empty, and at the head of the right-hand one there was a date in May, and under the date there were eleven marks going down the column at even spaces, and against the last of the eleven there was another date, being the twenty-second of July. At the foot of the board, side by side and the same age, there were two impressions struck in something dark, the first of them round and about two inches across and the second of them smaller and struck close up against the first, and Calder was eight feet off the board and did not get closer to it, and did not ask the young man what was on either of them, and will not.

Above the head, on the bare timber of the board where there was no ruling at all, there was a date cut into the wood and gone white in the grain of it.

**The fourteenth of February.**

That is three months and three days before the seventeenth of May past, which is the day the notice went up in a different street in that harbor, and the fifth clause of that notice describes the line that Calder went out past at eight o'clock that morning without a declaration, and the sixth clause of that notice is free and is the reason he was able to come back in.

"A standing thing," the young man said, following where he was looking. "It is not mine. It came with the boat in April and it is kept in the boat and every man in her can write on it, and the word at the top of it is the word that is written at the top of the sheet, and the sheet is in a box in the forepeak and I have not been in the box."

"What goes in the column."

"A hull."

"How many."

"Eleven, since the eighth of May, and it is not one a week to the day, because a hull is a hull when it is alongside and it does not care what day that is." He put the pencil behind his ear again. "And I will tell you what nobody in that harbour has told me, and you may do what you like with it. Nobody in my boat has taken a thing out of anybody's boat by force. There is no man in my boat with a weapon on him that I have seen, and I have been in her two years. What we do is this. A man in a boat goes alongside a hull and he asks the master for a thing, and the master gives it, because there is a form with a word at the top of it, and the word is the word at the top of that board, and a master who has been asked for a thing under that word gives it and goes on fishing. And what comes out of the hull goes into a case and the case goes into a lighter and the lighter goes somewhere, and I have not been told where, and I have not asked, and I would not go and see."

He looked at Calder properly for the first time.

"That is not raiding," he said. "I want to say that and I want to say it to somebody. Everybody ashore thinks it is raiding. It is not raiding. It is a box with a date on it."

**AND NOBODY HAS EVER GIVEN A MASTER IN THIS COUNTRY A FORM SAYING WHAT A MAN OUTSIDE AN OFFICER'S COMPANY MAY BE ASKED FOR, AND THE ABSENCE OF THAT PAPER IS NOT A FAULT OF ANYBODY AND IS NOT A MORAL OF ANYBODY, IT IS A FACT ABOUT A WALL IN A DIFFERENT STREET IN A HARBOR, AND THE WALL HAS BEEN UP SINCE THE SEVENTEENTH OF MAY PAST AND HAS NOT BEEN COMPARED WITH ANYTHING BY ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR.**

---

At about one the man of about forty-one on the third boat leaned out and said four words.

"Hold that."

He threw a line and Calder caught it, and the man of about forty-one came down his own ladder into the water to chest depth and took the other bight and they brought eight cases up out of the lighter and set them down on the lighter's hatch, and then eight more, and then they shifted the empty cases back down into the lighter because the lighter was going away in an hour and the boat was not.

There were four men in that work and there was work for six. Calder asked nothing about it and was told nothing and did the half of it. The man of about forty-one had not said *will you* and had not said *I want*, and had said *hold that* the way a man says a thing that is true at that moment, and Calder had taken the line because a line with nobody on it is a line that goes in the water.

They were in the water to the chest for about two hours and twenty minutes and the tide turned under them at about half past two and the work got harder and neither of them said so. At about half past three Calder got back into his own boat and the man of about forty-one stood on the ladder of his own and looked at him.

"You will not come in with us," he said.

"No."

"You knew that at ten o'clock."

"I knew it at ten o'clock."

"That is not a thing a man says."

"It is a thing a man says when he has been told it once by a man who is not going to be asked twice," said Calder, "and I was told it at ten and I have not asked you a thing since, and I am not going to now, and you have had two hours and a half of me for nothing, and I am not going to say a word to you about what you did with them."

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE WENT BACK UP HIS LADDER AND DID NOT ANSWER THAT, AND HE DID NOT REPORT THAT A BOAT HAD COME ALONGSIDE, AND A MAN WHO DOES NOT REPORT A THING IS NOT THE SAME THING AS A MAN WHO CANNOT REPORT IT, AND THIS YARD DOES NOT KNOW WHICH OF THE TWO HE IS AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

Calder let go from the line and put the boat's head round for the harbour side, and the flood was still on her and running hard, and he came down the fairway at about ten to five with the man of nineteen at the tiller and neither of them said anything for four minutes.

Then the boat came up all of a sudden under him.

Not much. Two foot of it. She came up with her forefoot on something and stopped, and the water came in over the mast and over the rail forward and stood about a foot and two inches on the boards, and Calder got the oar down and pushed her off and she slid off whatever it was and came free, and the whole of it took about forty seconds.

**THE FIGURE HE CARRIED FOR THAT REACH WAS ELEVEN FOOT AND THE FIGURE SHE FOUND WAS NINE, AND HE HAD NOT SOUNDED IT, BECAUSE HE HAD BEEN IN COMPANY WITH EIGHT BOATS FOR FOUR HOURS AND HAD NO TIME AND THERE WAS A TOW COMING DOWN THE FAIRWAY, AND THE FIGURE HE CARRIED FOR THAT REACH WAS ENTERED IN A BOOK IN THIS SHED BY A MAN WHO WAS IN A BOAT AND WAS PAID IN FISH AND IS DEAD, AND HE WROTE IT HONESTLY, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN HIS HONEST FIGURE AND THE BOTTOM OF THAT FAIRWAY IS TWO FOOT, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS NOBODY'S, BECAUSE A COAST DOES NOT STAND STILL AND A BOOK OF SOUNDINGS IS A BOOK OF SOUNDINGS AND NOT A PROMISE.**

The tow came down the fairway at about five on the ebb, eight boats and about forty feet of line between them, and the tow came across.

The tow was already inside him and the lighter was inside him too when Calder understood what was going to happen, and he understood it in the way a man understands a thing about half a second before his hands do anything about it, and what his hands did was find the stern line where it went aft to the third boat of the line he had been lying alongside for an hour and a half, and cut it.

He cut his own line. The cut went under and across the tow because his stern line was already under it, and the tow had his line in it and not the other way round, and the tow's weight closed on a length of eight-point line that was never made for it and took it.

The lighter went up broadside and hung there, and the tow came on, and the painter parted at the ring bolt, and the lighter went on and set her down on the edge of the fairway in about six foot of water and stopped.

**ELEVEN MINUTES**, said a man of about fifty-six at a tiller in the towing boat afterwards, twice, to two different people, and neither of them contradicted him.

Calder got his boat clear of the tow by the strength of the flood and went in past the outer marks at about half past six on the sixth clause, which is free, and which says in and does not say in what.

The boat was not holed and nothing was broken in her. He had lost about eight fathoms of line, and he had lost a boat's length of light, and he had lost about four hours of a day he had paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence for, and the man of nineteen at the tiller looked at the gear and then at him.

"You have lost something," the man said.

"A line."

"That is the cheapest thing a man can lose out there." He made the bight fast on its own bollard and did not look up at it. "I am not going to ask what the other one was, and if you want to tell me a figure for it I will not put the figure anywhere. That is the whole of what I have to say about it and I am saying it now so that you know I have thought about it and decided not to ask."

"You have decided right," Calder said.

**AND THE MOUTH OF THE HARBOUR HAD A LINE OF LAMPS ON IT AFTER DARK, AND HE COUNTED THEM FROM THE QUAY, AND EIGHT OF THEM WERE A TOW, AND A TOW DOES NOT LIE AT A HARBOUR'S MOUTH UNLESS SOMEBODY HAS PUT IT THERE, AND NOBODY SAID SO TO HIM AND HE SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY AND HE WENT UP THE HILL AND DID NOT LOOK AT THEM AGAIN.**

---

At about half past three the ship's hand entered the day in the first column with the hours and the figure that went out, and the second column carries what could not be written in a column a man can check, and the third column was empty and nobody has been appointed to it.

He was on the mole head at about ten past five, where he goes every evening of this harbor, and there was nothing up there and nobody had sent him, and the tow's lamps were still on the water at the outer marks.

**AND A TENDER OF ABOUT NINETY TONS THAT HAD SAT AT THAT HARBOUR'S OUTER WATER FOR SEVENTEEN DAYS WENT TO THE HEAD OF EIGHT BOATS INSIDE THE LINE ON THE SAID DAY, AND A TOW CARRYING FLOUR CAME DOWN THE SAME FAIRWAY ON THE SAME HOUR AND WAS PUT ACROSS IT BY A MAN WHO CUT HIS OWN LINE BECAUSE HE HAD TWO FOOT OF WATER UNDER HIM AND NO TIME TO SOUND, AND THE FLOUR IS NOT IN THAT HARBOR, AND NONE OF THE EIGHT CAME IN, AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT CAME INTO THAT YARD ON THE SAID DAY WAS NOTHING AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT WENT OUT OF IT WAS ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE.**

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Tuesday was the fortieth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and not a wipe, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid and the column did not move, so that forty weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that the holder paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, in coin on the flat of the mole head stone at about a quarter to seven in the morning to a man of about nineteen for a boat of perhaps eighteen tons and a day, being the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall and not a figure of this yard's; and that the boat went out past the line at about eight with no declaration, being in breach of the first clause of the notice of the seventeenth of May past, and came in at about half past six on the sixth clause, and no other boat of that harbor went out on the said day and nobody was sent and nothing was written; and that at about half past ten the tender of about ninety tons was seen at the head of eight boats inside the line and not at the outer water where she had lain seventeen days, and nobody told the holder what she was for and he did not ask and is not going to; and that eight boats were in company without a man-of-war and without anything flying and were doing one thing at one hour on one tide; and that a man of about forty-one on the third boat refused him the boat at about half past nine and gave the reason, being that there is no room in her and no paper in that harbor that puts him on her, and did not report that a boat had come alongside; and that a man of about twenty-six with a slate asked whether a hull lifted twice is one hull or two, and was told no, and put one stroke on the slate, and asked it of a man in a boat rather than of nobody; and that eight cases were lifted out of a lighter and into a boat and down again into the lighter, and no case was carried past anybody and nothing was asked of anybody; and that from about one until about half past three the holder shifted eight cases up and eight empty cases down in the chest of the water with the man of about forty-one, being half a day's work for four men where there is work for six, and the man did not ask him and he did not offer and neither of them said a word about it and he was not paid and was not asked to pay; and that at about ten to five on the flood his boat came up two foot on the edge of the fairway, the figure he carried for that reach being eleven and the figure she found being nine, and he had not sounded it and there was a tow coming; and that he cut his own stern line and the tow took it and the painter of a lighter of about thirty tons parted and the lighter was held eleven minutes and took the ground in about six foot; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a board about a yard wide under a sloping canvas in a boat of that line carries one word at the head of it in block capitals and two ruled columns under it, and that the word is UNSEALED, and that there are eleven marks in the right-hand column against dates beginning on the eighth of May and ending on the twenty-second of July, and that a mark in that column is a hull and is not a man, and that this yard has not counted anything in it and is not going to; and that there are two impressions struck at the foot of that board side by side, the first round and about two inches across and the second smaller and struck close against it, and that the holder did not go closer to them and did not ask what was on them and is not going to; and that a date cut into the timber above the head of that board reads the fourteenth of February, and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past went up on a wall in a different street in this harbor ninety-two days after that date and seventy-one days before the said day, and has not been touched and has not been compared with anything by anybody in that harbor; and that no master in this country has been given a form saying what a man outside an officer's company may be asked for, and that the absence of that paper is a fact about a wall and is not a fault of anybody; and that a tow carrying flour lay at the outer marks of this harbor all night and eight lamps were on the water and none of the eight came in; and that a lighter of about thirty tons took the ground in about six foot on the edge of the fairway and a man of about fifty-six at a tiller said eleven minutes and said it twice; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands; and that the third channel is still nobody's measurement and the borrowed instrument is still up that coast and has not come down.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that a board with one word at the head of it is not the form and is not a copy of the form and is not anybody's document, and that a word on a board is a word and not a verdict on anybody; and that eleven marks in a column are eleven hulls and are not eleven men and not eleven families and are not to be counted as anything a man can be sorry for; and that two foot of water under a boat on a coast that has moved is nobody's arithmetic and is not a fault of anybody now living, and is not to be balanced against anything; and that a tow held eleven minutes is not a thing this yard did and is not a thing this yard is going to call the price of what it did.*
