# Chapter 369

*Friday the Twenty-Third of July, the Thirty-Ninth Day of the Ninety, an Answer in Writing That Is Not Forgiveness, a Harbor Poorer for a Door Found Open and Not Richer by One Penny, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Friday the twenty-third of July, in that shed, and on that mole, and at the outer water.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

At about nine in the morning the woman of no office put a leaf on the bench in that shed in her own hand, being her answer under the procedure, and she did not read it out.

The leaf says she has read his leaf of yesterday, and that no paper using her hand will be shown in any room on the strength of it this week, and that she will keep the five leaves where they are, and that is the whole of the answer.

Calder read it where he sat and put it in the drawer with the sheet in it, and said he has it, and that a day waited is a day waited, and that is the whole of what the procedure has done: it has made him wait a day and write a leaf and not ask out loud, and it has not given him anything.

She did not forgive him on the leaf and she did not thank him on it, and what is on it is a procedure and not a pardon, and nobody in that shed improved on it.

**He does not get to hold the hiding of another year as an excuse and he did not, and he does not get to say he was protecting her and he did not, and what she asked for instead of forgiveness is what is on the leaf and nothing else.**

At about eleven the ship's hand priced nothing, because there was nothing to price, and said one sentence about the week, being that a door found open in a city has not fed this harbor by one penny and has not brought down the cheapest oil by one penny and has not lit the mole-head light, and that is not a defeat and is not a failure and is a harbor in July.

The cheapest thing in that town is still four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first day of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, left visible above it and both dates on the board, and the mole-head light is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May.

At about two a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons mended a warp on the third lay and said he has no municipal papers and wants none, and nobody said he was wrong, because a man with no papers is not a man with papers unused.

At about ten past five Calder went up to the mole head, because that is where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

The tender of about ninety tons was still at the outer water with two lines out, and the young man of about twenty-three was in her own wheelhouse with a telescope in his hand, and a lever of nineteen on a vessel of perhaps eighteen tons was patient with a bight of line and did not cut it, and no officer came ashore and no paper came ashore and nobody hailed anybody.

On the crown, about four feet from a page with six names on it, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and seven days old, and one corner has been off since the seventeenth of June past and has not been lashed back.

The mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it was not looked at on the said day.

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-third day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Friday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a woman of no office put an answer in writing on a leaf under the procedure, being that no paper using her hand will be shown in any room this week and she keeps the five where they are, with no forgiveness and no thanks on it; and that the holder put it in the drawer and said a day waited is a day waited; and that the cheapest oil stays at four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, above it and both dates on the board, and the light is out; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-nine weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about thirty-eight with no municipal papers wants none and nobody said he was wrong; and that the tender is still at the outer water with two lines out and no hail; and that about eleven people of that harbor cannot read the notice, being a count of people, and that nobody in that yard is to tell any of them anything; and that nine communities hold the records one in each, and six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-fourth line:*

*What it is not: — that an answer in writing keeping five leaves where they are is not forgiveness and is not thanks and is not a resolution of any debt; and that a door open in a city that has not fed a harbor by one penny is not a victory and is not a defeat; and that a man with no papers wanting none is not in the wrong and no room has said he is.*
