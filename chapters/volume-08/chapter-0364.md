# Chapter 364

*Sunday the Eighteenth of July, the Thirty-Fourth Day of the Ninety, a Municipal Paper on a Table in Veyr and a Civilian Convoy Under It That Is Not a Vessel Returning From Beyond the Line, the Sixth Clause's Door Open to It as Well as to the Others, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Sunday the eighteenth of July, in Veyr in that room, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, being in a city, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was out of that harbor on the said day and in Veyr, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

On the table in that room on the said morning there were two papers, and neither of them was the terms under glass.

The first was the notice of the seventeenth of May past, copied out in a clerk's hand, all eight lines of it, with the fifth line and the sixth line marked at the side with a stroke of the pen. Nobody read it out loud. It lay there so that anybody at the table could look at it, because a notice about a vessel is a notice and a schedule is a list and a sheet is a sheet, and nothing in that room knew anything more than that.

The second was a municipal paper from the office on the west basin, brought down that coast in a coat by a man off the west quay who had been given it to carry and not to read, and who had carried it to Veyr on the ordinary boat and asked for nothing and was thanked for nothing. It names two boats and three men and a tide, and the name at the foot of it is that of the clerk of that office, being the clerk of about sixty who has been twenty-two years in it and is owed a quarter and has no book and no power, and it says the two boats may go out together under it and come in under it, and it is a municipal document and not an instrument of anybody's.

The man of about fifty who keeps the room put his hand on the second paper and then on the first, and said that any person might put a paper in front of the room and that the question on the second one was whether it made any difference to the first, and that the room would answer only what the lines said, and that the man who left that paper on that table was not in the room and had given no name and would not be asked for one in it.

Calder Marr looked at the eight lines for a long time. The woman of no office looked at the municipal paper for longer, because it was the plainer of the two.

"What the sixth line says," Calder said at last, "is that in is free. It does not say in what. It says in."

The man of about fifty nodded, because a man keeping a room may nod at a sentence without improving on it.

"What the municipal paper says," Mara Venn said, "is that two boats under it are a civilian convoy going out together on a tide and coming in on a tide. A civilian convoy under municipal papers is not a vessel returning from beyond the line. The sixth line's door is open to it as well as to the others."

The clerk in the room off it wrote that down, and read back the hour and the two sentences, and asked nothing, and nobody asked him anything.

**It is the first thing anybody has come out of a room with that is a door and not a refusal, and it is a foot and not a fleet, and it is not an instrument of anybody's, and it binds nobody who does not use it, and nobody in that room said anybody was in the wrong for not using it.**

The man of about fifty said the room would write that the door is open to such a convoy where the papers are in order, and that where there are no papers there is no door, and that a paper on a table is a paper on a table and nothing more, and the clerk wrote that and read it back.

Neither Calder nor the woman asked for anything more on the said day, because a foot is a foot and a room that gives a foot has given what it can, and because the clause that has never been tested has been tested once and does not need to be tested twice in one afternoon.

---

In that shed at about four the ship's hand entered the day, being that a municipal paper naming two boats and three men was put on the table in that room, and that the sixth line was looked at beside it, and that the room wrote that the door is open to a civilian convoy under such papers, and that it binds nobody who does not use it.

On the crown of that mole at about ten past five the small steam tender of about ninety tons was still at the outer water with two lines out, and the young man of about twenty-three was in her own wheelhouse with a telescope, and a lever of nineteen on a vessel of perhaps eighteen tons was patient with a bight of line round a bollard and did not cut it, and no officer came ashore and no paper came ashore and nobody hailed anybody.

The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and two days old on the said day, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins, one corner off since the seventeenth of June past and not lashed back, and neither has moved, and about four feet from a page with six names on it in a hand nobody corrected.

The mark on the face of the second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it was not looked at on the said day.

**And both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, that a door has been found open in a room in a city and that a light is still out on a mole head in a harbor.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the eighteenth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed by the ship's hand in her own left hand, the holder and the woman being in Veyr: that the said Sunday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a municipal paper from the office on the west basin naming two boats and three men and a tide was put on the table in that room in Veyr, carried there in a coat by a man off the west quay who was asked to carry it and not to read it, and that the man who left that paper in that room was not in it and had given no name and was not asked for one there; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past in eight lines was on the same table with the fifth line and the sixth line marked; and that the holder said the sixth line says in is free and does not say in what, and that a woman of no office said a civilian convoy under municipal papers is not a vessel returning from beyond the line so the sixth line's door is open to it as well as to the others; and that the room wrote that the door is open to such a convoy where the papers are in order and that it binds nobody who does not use it and nobody was said to be in the wrong for not using it; and that the clerk of about sixty in a room off it wrote the hour and read it back and asked nothing and was asked nothing; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-eight weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the municipal paper is a municipal document and not an instrument of anybody's; and that the tender of about ninety tons is still at the outer water with two lines out and no officer ashore and no paper ashore and nobody hailed; and that the man of about thirty-two two days down that coast was not sent for and was not written to and does not know there is a room; and that about eleven people of that harbor cannot read the notice, being a count of people, and that nobody in that yard is to tell any of them anything; and that nine communities hold the records one in each, and six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the eighty-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that a civilian convoy under municipal papers going out together on a tide is not a vessel returning from beyond the line and is not anything else either; and that a door found open in a room is not a price solved and is not a boat paid for by the person who wants the thing looked at; and that a foot won in a room in a city is not a victory and does not make a harbor richer by one penny.*
