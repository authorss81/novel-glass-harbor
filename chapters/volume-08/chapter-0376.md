# Chapter 376

*Friday the Thirtieth of July, the Forty-Sixth Day of the Ninety, a Man of About Fifty-One of Six Who Has Not Asked for Anything Since the Fifteenth and Who Asked Again on the Thirtieth, Thirty-Six Pence in a Bag and a Figure of One Hundred and Sixty-Eight on a Slate, a Boy of About Seventeen With a Word in His Pocket, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Friday the thirtieth of July, in that shed, and on that mole, and in the front street.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth in this harbor is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and that bag is not a fifth of one.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

He came down to that shed at about eleven in the morning and stood in the doorway in the way a man stands in a doorway who has decided in the street which sentence he is going to use.

"I am not going to ask for a page of anything," he said. "I am going to ask for a day."

"There is no day's work."

"I know there is no day's work." He put his hat in his hand, which he had not done in July before. "I asked you on the fifteenth whether there was a day's work and you said there was not, and I went back up the mole and I have not asked anybody for anything since, and I would like it on your own head that this is the first time in a fortnight I have had to come and ask twice, and that I have come twice, and that the second time is because of what I saw on Tuesday."

"What did you see on Tuesday."

"A boat of eighteen tons went out of this harbour at seven in the morning with one man in her and you were in her, and I stood on the crown and I watched her go past the outer marks at about eight, and I know which way she was pointing and I know what is up-coast, and I have been in that water since I was nineteen." He did not come into that shed. "You went to look at them. And I sat up there for two hours and a bit after that and I did not have anybody I could ask about it, because there is nobody in this harbor I could ask about it and not have it be a thing I have said out loud, and my page is on that mole with six names on it and I am one of the six, and I have been one of the six since the nineteenth of June."

Calder came out from behind the bench and stood where the man could see him and not much else.

"I did not go to ask them anything," he said. "I went to lie alongside eight boats for a day because I had a hundred and sixty-eight pence in this yard and a boat at a fair price, and a man of nineteen who would take the money, and I got two hours and twenty minutes of a man's work out of the day for nothing and came back with eight fathoms of line gone and a tow of eight boats held eleven minutes in the fairway and a lighter with water in her. And I have not improved on that and I am not going to."

"You are not going to take me out."

"I am not going to take you out."

"Then say the reason, because you have just said everything else."

"Two reasons and the first one is the money and I will not dress it up. There is a boat of eighteen tons with one man in her and I paid that man a hundred and sixty-eight pence out of this bag on Tuesday at a quarter to seven, and there is thirty-six pence in the bag now, and thirty-six pence is three shillings, and if I put a second man in that boat I have not got his day and he would not be owed it and would not be told that he was not owed it, because I would have to say it to him and I have not got the nerve to say it to a man of about fifty-one with two fingers and a page on a mole." He turned his hands over. "And the second one is worse and it is not about money. That page of yours has six names on it. If I take one of you out on a thing I have not asked the other five about, then in a week there is one of the six on a boat and five of the six on a stone, and in a month somebody writes a sentence about it. I have seen a harbor do that. They all say the word *crew* and they all say *six* and the six becomes a one and then the one is a decision somebody made and nobody can find out who made it, because I made it in a shed with thirty-six pence in a bag and I did not write it down."

The man of about fifty-one of the six stood in the doorway and took that without any difficulty at all.

"Then ask me," he said.

"I will."

"Not in a fortnight. Not when you have a day. Ask me when you have nothing to spend and no boat, on a day like this one, and ask me then, and if I say yes I will come on the day you say and I will take what there is, and if there is nothing there will be nothing and I will not have been promised it."

"That is the terms."

"That is the terms and I have thought about them for two days on a mole and you have had them in about ninety seconds." He put his hat back on. "You went to look at them and you have not told anybody in this harbor what you saw and I have not asked you, and I want to say one thing about that and then I will go up the hill. I have a page on that mole saying a boat and no figure. I have had it there since the eighth of July. If what you saw is what I think you saw, then in a month there will be a figure against me on somebody's return and against a boat, and there is no way on this coast to have been in a boat that somebody wanted and not have a figure about it, and the only thing this page of mine has ever been for is to say that six men were here before anybody wrote a number."

"That is what it is for," Calder said.

"So is it worth anything to you that I said that."

"It is worth something to me," Calder said, "and I am not going to put it in the first column, because a man on a mole cannot check it, and I am not going to put it in the fourth column either, because the fourth column says what a thing is not, and what you have just said is a thing that is."

**AND HE WENT UP THE MOLE AFTERWARDS AND THE STONE WAS STILL ON THE CORNER OF HIS PAGE AND HE TOOK IT OFF AND PUT IT IN HIS OWN POCKET, WHICH HE HAD NOT DONE BEFORE, AND NOBODY ON THAT CROWN ASKED HIM WHY AND THE PAGE IS ON THE CROWN OF THAT MOLE AT TEN PAST FIVE AND NOBODY HAS ASKED.**

---

A boy of about seventeen came into that shed at about one in the afternoon with a word in his pocket and gave it to the ship's hand and was not paid and did not know what it was and did not ask, and the word was that a herring skipper of fifty-one wanted a day of a man and a boat on the Saturday for caulking and painting and would pay the figure on the slate, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.

The boy's name was not written down and his age was not written down and this yard does not write boys down.

**A HERRING SKIPPER OF FIFTY-ONE WITH MUNICIPAL PAPERS OF HIS OWN WHO SAID IN THE OPEN ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF JULY PAST THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO USE THEM FOR A CONVOY, IS NOT USING THEM FOR ANYTHING ON SATURDAY, AND IS PAYING ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE FOR A DAY OF A MAN AND A BOAT AND NOTHING ELSE, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED HAS SAID A WORD ABOUT THE CONVOY TO HIM AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.**

At about half past three the day's first column was entered with the hours and the figures, and the third column was empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the man of about fifty-one of the six was not entered in it and was not entered against anybody.

At about ten past five in the evening he was on the mole head, in the wind, with the page of six names four feet off his boot.

The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-four days. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eightieth night. The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and fourteen days old and one corner has been off since the seventeenth of June past. The second seam and the mark four hundred yards off it stand at a season of nine marks each, neither paid, neither going to be.

**AND A HARBOR HAS SIX MEN ASHORE WITH NO BOAT AND A PAGE SAYING SO AND NO FIGURE, AND A BOAT OF EIGHTEEN TONS WITH ONE MAN IN HER, AND A BAG WITH THIRTY-SIX PENCE IN IT, AND A FIGURE OF ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE IN CHALK ON A SLATE, AND EVERY ONE OF THOSE FOUR IS AN HONEST FIGURE AND NONE OF THE FOUR WILL BE PAID ON ANY ACCOUNT BY ANYBODY.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Friday the thirtieth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Friday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a man of about fifty-one of the six came down to that shed at about eleven and asked for a day, having asked on the fifteenth of July past and not asked since, and that the holder told him there was no day's work and that the boat which went out on the twenty-seventh was a boat of eighteen tons with one man in her paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence, and that there are thirty-six pence in the bag, and that a second man in that boat could not be paid and could not be told he could not be paid; and that the holder also refused to take one of the six out on a thing he had not asked the other five about, the reason given being that one of six on a boat and five of six on a stone becomes a word and a figure in a month and that the figure will be in a return and there will be no way of finding out who made it; and that the said man said ask me when you have nothing to spend and no boat and I will take what there is, and that those are the terms and that the holder said he would ask him and did not ask him on the said day; and that the said man said he would not have been promised anything; and that a boy of about seventeen carried a word into that shed at about one in the afternoon, was not paid, did not know what it was and did not ask, and the word was that a herring skipper of fifty-one wanted a day of a man and a boat on the Saturday for caulking and painting at the figure on the slate, and that no name and no age of the said boy is written down in this yard; and that after the said man went up the mole he took the stone off the corner of his own page and put it in his own pocket, which he had not done before, and nobody on that crown asked him why; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that six men of that harbor have been ashore with no boat since the nineteenth of June past, of whom one had a day's wage on the twelfth of July, one worked that day for nothing and lost the top of two fingers of his left hand, and four have had nothing and were not told there was anything, and that on the said day one of the four came and asked a second time; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one with papers of his own who said on the twenty-second of July past that he would not use them for a convoy is paying one hundred and sixty-eight pence for a day of a man and a boat for caulking and painting and nothing else, and that no word about the convoy was said to him in that shed and none is going to be; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and first line:*

*What it is not: — that a man coming twice is not a man owed a second time, and that a bag with thirty-six pence in it is not a refusal of a man's worth; and that one man of six on a boat is not a crew and is not the beginning of one and is not a decision this yard is going to let be made in a shed without a day written against it; and that a page on a mole saying a boat and no figure is not a claim and is not a price and is not settled by anybody and is not going into the first column tonight.*
