# Chapter 395

*Wednesday the Eighteenth of August, the Sixty-Fifth Day of the Ninety, a Day's Work With a Boat and Two Men at a Berth and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In at the Figure in Chalk on a Slate at the End Stall Which Is Not This Yard's Figure, a Herring Skipper of Fifty-One With Papers of His Own Who Has Been Asked by Somebody Whether He Wants to Take His No Back and Did Not Say Who Asked Him, and Two Men at the Foot of That Mole of Whom Neither Was Asked for Anything*

Wednesday the eighteenth of August, on that quay, at a berth, and on the crown of that mole.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, at the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall, and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and that one day at a berth is the whole of what came into this yard in ten days, and three hundred and thirty-six pence is the whole of what went out of it, on two days, being a Tuesday and a Saturday of a man of about twenty-three, and the two are not the same figure and neither of them is the wage of four marks, which is six hundred and forty pence and has not been paid for forty-three weeks and is not going to be paid on the forty-fourth.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she did not come up that hill on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-three days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-five days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

The berth was a boat of about nineteen tons belonging to a man of about forty of the second lay who wanted a seam opened and caulked and wanted it done in one day and had said so at the price in chalk and had got it, and Calder was in her with the man of about nineteen at the tiller and one other man of about twenty-six with a bucket and a maul.

They were on the berth two tides and not one. The tide gang's want of four men is on no board in that harbor and is in the first column of that leaf and in no return of any office, and there were three men on that work and not four, and Calder did the fourth thing himself, which was the caulking of about eleven feet of a seam along the port side, and he was slow at it and the man of about twenty-six said so once and then did not say it again.

The man of about nineteen had the tiller from half past six in the morning until about six in the afternoon and said the figure first, before anybody had said anything about money at all.

"One hundred and sixty-eight pence," he said. "That is what it is and I have not asked you what you would pay and I am not going to ask you. And I am not going to be paid twice for one day by anybody, and if anybody on that quay offers me a second mark and eightpence for this day for carrying a sheet of paper in my pocket I am going to hand it back and you can have your sheet."

"You will not be paid twice for that day by anybody in this harbor or out of it, and I will say so on the quay on Friday in front of the boats if you want it said out loud."

"No," said the man of about nineteen. "I do not want it said out loud. I want it not to happen, and those are two different things and the second one is the one I am paid for."

At about two in the afternoon there was a pause in the caulking and the man of about nineteen, who had the tiller and was watching the water and not the town, said that there was a boat going down the reach at about a mile and a half with two boats in company and no lights showing and that she was not one of that harbor's and that he had seen her on the ninth of this month at the other end of the tow of flour boats, and that he had not written it down then and was not writing it down now.

"She is a boat," he said. "That is all she is and that is all I have got and I am not going to have anybody make a thing out of my having seen a boat."

---

The herring skipper of fifty-one came up that hill at about four in the afternoon with his hands in his pockets and did not take them out.

"I have been asked this week by somebody whether I want to take my no back," he said, before he had sat down. "That is all I have come to say and I am not going to tell you who asked me, and you are not going to ask me, and I am not going to tell you what I said to him either, and if I did tell you it would be worth nothing to you, because I have said the same words to about four men in eleven years and it has never once been taken off me."

"I am not going to ask you who, and I am not going to ask you what you said."

"Then here is the rest of it and then I am going home." He stood in the middle of that shed and did not sit down. "I said no on the fourteenth and I gave you a reason about a room and a box and a date, and you wrote it on a leaf with the hour against it and no name against it, and you did not ask me for it twice, and that is the whole of what I have got out of this. And on Friday seven men are going to put their hands at the foot of a sheet in front of me and go out on a flood. And I am not going to do it. And I have thought about it for four days at sea and I have got a new reason and the new reason is worse than the old one, which is that when it happens there will be a record that a man sailed and a record that a man did not, and both of them will be in the same room, and the one that says a man did not will be the one somebody asks about."

He stopped there and did not improve on it and did not say a word about the twenty-first of August beyond that, and Calder did not improve on it either, and did not say that he was proud of him, and did not say that he understood, and did not put it in a return, and the herring skipper of fifty-one went off the hill at about ten past four and got into his own boat and untied her and went down the third lay under the sixth clause, which is free, and which has not asked anybody anything since the seventeenth of May, and which nobody in that harbor has ever been thanked for.

**AND A HERRING SKIPPER OF FIFTY-ONE WITH MUNICIPAL PAPERS OF HIS OWN HAS SAID NO TWICE TO THE SAME THING IN FOUR DAYS, HAS BEEN ASKED BY SOMEBODY ELSE WHETHER HE WANTS TO TAKE IT BACK, HAS TOLD THIS YARD HE WAS ASKED AND HAS NOT SAID BY WHOM AND HAS NOT BEEN ASKED, AND HIS BOAT WENT DOWN THE THIRD LAY UNDER THE SIXTH CLAUSE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS SAID HE WAS RIGHT AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS SAID HE WAS WRONG, AND NO FIGURE IS AGAINST HIM AND NO FIGURE WILL BE.**

---

At about half past five Calder went up to the mole and stopped at the foot of it, and there were two men at the bottom of the steps and neither of them was waiting for him.

The man of about fifty-one of that harbor was on the crown with his own page and his own stone, where he has put it himself since the thirtieth of July past, and he has six names on that page and a boat and no figure, and he has not said a word about it to anybody for thirty-five days and he was not asked for a word about it on the said day, and nothing of his went on a sheet in a pocket in a boat and no figure of his is in one and no boat was promised to him and no wage was promised to him and his page is not going into any return and is not going to be taken down by anybody.

The man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers of any kind stood four feet below him at the foot of the steps with his own painter over his shoulder and looked up at the page for about as long as it takes to read a thing, and did not go up, and did not speak to either of them, and neither of them spoke to him.

He went off down the slip at about ten past six with a fold of a notice of the seventeenth of May past in his own inside pocket, which nobody in that harbor wrote and which he has had in that coat since before the spring, and he is not going out on the Saturday and nobody asked him whether he was and he did not offer.

**AND ON THE CROWN OF THAT MOLE THERE IS A PAGE OF SIX NAMES WITH A BOAT AS A PRICE AND NO FIGURE, AND THE OWNER OF IT IS GIVEN NOTHING BY ANYBODY IN THIS BATCH, AND A MAN WITH NO PAPERS OF ANY KIND STOOD AT THE FOOT OF THE STEPS AND LOOKED UP AT IT AND WENT AWAY AGAIN, AND NEITHER OF THEM WAS ASKED FOR ANYTHING BY ANYBODY ON THE SAID DAY.**

**AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE CAME INTO THAT YARD ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF AUGUST AND WAS THE ONLY FIGURE THAT CAME INTO IT IN TEN DAYS, AND IT WAS EARNED AT A BERTH WITH THREE MEN ON THE WORK WHERE THE WANT IS FOUR, AND THE FOURTH WAS DONE BY THE MAN WHO OWNS THE LEAF AND BY NOBODY'S FIGURE, AND THE BAG IS BACK ON THE FIGURE IT WAS ON THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST AND THE FIGURE IT IS SHORT OF THE WAGE IS THE FIGURE IT WAS SHORT OF ON THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO PAY IT.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the eighteenth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Wednesday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, at the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall; and that the work was done on two tides and not one by a boat of about nineteen tons of a man of about forty of the second lay; and that the man of about nineteen had the tiller from half past six in the morning until about six in the afternoon and said the figure first before anybody had said anything about money and said he was not going to ask what this yard would pay, and that he is not going to be paid twice for one day by anybody and that if anybody on that quay offers him a second mark and eightpence for carrying a sheet in his pocket he will hand it back; and that there were three men on that work and the want is four, and the fourth was done by the holder of that writ and no figure is against him for it; and that a man of about twenty-six with a bucket and a maul said once that the holder was slow at caulking and did not say it again; and that a man of about nineteen with the tiller said at about two in the afternoon that there was a boat going down the reach at a mile and a half with two boats in company and no lights showing, and that she is not one of that harbor's and that he had seen her on the ninth of August at the other end of the tow of flour boats, and that he wrote nothing down then and wrote nothing down now and is not going to have anybody make a thing of it; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one came up that hill at about four in the afternoon and said he had been asked by somebody whether he wanted to take his no back, and that he would not say by whom and was not asked, and that he said no on the fourteenth and gave a reason about a room and a box and a date and it was written on a leaf with the hour against it and no name against it; and that he said a second reason and that the second reason is worse than the first, being that there will be a record that a man sailed and a record that a man did not and both will be in the same room; and that he was not improved on and was not thanked and was not told he was understood and nothing was put in a return, and that his boat went down the third lay under the sixth clause; and that at about half past five the holder went up the mole and two men were at the foot of the steps and neither was asked for anything, and that the man of about fifty-one was on the crown with his own page and his own stone and said nothing and was not asked, and that the man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers looked up at the page for about as long as it takes to read a thing and did not go up and did not speak to either of them and is not going out on the Saturday and was not asked whether he was.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that a man who is asked by somebody whether he wants to take a no back and tells this yard he was asked and does not say by whom has not been turned into anything and is not a witness and is not an informer and is not a man in a matter, and that this yard did not ask and is not going to; and that a man who is right about a room and a box and a date in four years and wrong about it tomorrow is a man with a reason and not a prophet, and that nobody in that harbor has told him he was right about either; and that a man of about nineteen with a tiller who says a boat is a boat is not a failure and is not an omission and is not to be improved on by anybody, and that a thing seen from a tiller and not written down is not a record and is not the beginning of one; and that two men at the foot of a mole on a Wednesday evening, one of them with a page and a stone and one of them with no papers at all, are not a scene and are not a symbol and neither of them was there for anybody else.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and twentieth line:*

*What it is not: — that a man with papers of his own who will not put them behind a paper is not a coward and is not a refuser and is not a man to be moved and is not a man to be argued with, and that a second reason worse than a first one is not a change of heart and is not a man coming round; and that a day at a berth earned by three men where the want is four is not an improvement in that harbor and is not a beginning of anything and is not a figure against the man who did the fourth thing; and that two men standing at the foot of a mole looking at a page neither of whom is owed anything by anybody are not a complaint and are not a claim and are not a return; and that a man saying a boat is a boat is not a man who has failed to see a thing.*
