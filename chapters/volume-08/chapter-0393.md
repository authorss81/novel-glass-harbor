# Chapter 393

*Monday the Sixteenth of August, the Sixty-Third Day of the Ninety, the Sixty-Seventh Time the Wall Came Down Bare and the Seventy-Third Time the Cistern Was Charged and Walked on None of It, a Master of About Thirty of the Third Lay Who Says Out Loud That a Paper With Every Man's Name on It Is a List and the Reason This Yard Gave on the Fourth for Leaving the Fifth Man Off a Leaf, and a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Asked on a Monday for a Tuesday and Not Asked Twice and Not Put on Any Sheet*

Monday the sixteenth of August, on that quay, in that shed, and on the second lay.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday and was the day the wall came down bare for the sixty-seventh time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth with nobody putting it back, and the cistern was charged for the seventy-third time at about eight and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the wage column stands where it stood, being twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and forty-two weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and the forty-third falls tomorrow.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she did not come up that hill on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-one days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-three days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

The master of about thirty of that third lay was at that shed door at about nine in the morning and he was not the first one there and he was not the last.

He put his hand down flat on the bench stone, not on the sheet, about a foot from it.

"Say the words again," he said. "I want to hear the words again and then I will say mine and then I will go down and get my warp mended, and I have not got a great deal of time and neither have you."

"I said, on the fourth of this month, to about forty people on that quay: what does your boat want before she can lie in company with another boat for a day out and a day back. And four men of that lay gave me four things, and then a fifth man gave me a fifth thing that is a man and is not a thing, and I did not write the fifth one on the leaf. And you know the reason I gave for that, because you were in the first hour."

"You said the day a yard writes down who turns up, that yard has a list, and a list is the boat."

"That is what I said and I am not going to improve on it and I have said it once in this shed and I am not going to say it a second time because you are asking me to, which is a thing I have noticed men do." Calder did not move the sheet. "Ask your own question."

"My own question is that sheet," said the master of about thirty. "That is four things I can hold in my head. That is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper is a different animal, and I have a boat of thirty tons and about nine men in her and every one of their names is on it, and there is my name at the foot of it in my own hand, and on Friday I am going to sail in company with six other boats out of this harbor on a flood because of a piece of paper. And I have worked for men all my life on pieces of paper and I know what a piece of paper is, and what a piece of paper is, is a way of asking a man a question in three months in a town he has not been to."

He put his finger on the sheet, on a name, and did not move it.

"That is a list," he said. "You have told me for twelve days that a list is the boat. And I have come up this hill to tell you that the thing you have just told me not to do, you have done, and you have done it with a rule instead of a pen, and I did not come up here to be clever about it. I came up here because I am going to be in that water on Saturday and I would rather say it on a Monday."

"It is a list," Calder said.

"Thank you," said the master of about thirty, in the voice of a man who has been braced for the opposite.

"It is a list, and I am not going to tell you it is not, and I am not going to stand in this doorway and argue the word with you." He put two fingers on the sheet and did not lift it. "Now here is what I have got to say and I am going to say it once and you can do what you like with it afterwards. That paper says who is going. It does not say who is going to be there. It is the difference between those two, and it is the whole of what a day out and a day back is, and if I had got a second sheet with the men who are going to be on the mole head at four in the morning in it, this one would be a hand's breadth wider and there would be two of them and the second one would have a name against a man who cannot sleep and it would be a name against a man who is owed forty marks in this harbor and I am not going to write it and I have not written it and I am not going to."

"That does not make the paper clean."

"I have not said it does. I have said it is the whole of what I have got against it, and that it is one sheet and not two, and that the thing I have not got is the thing you refused to write down on the fourth, and I have not got it, and there is no way to get it onto paper without making the paper worse." He took his hand off the sheet. "And I am going to be in that water on Saturday with my name at the foot of it. If you think I have not worked that out by the time I am halfway down the slip, you have not been in a boat in your life."

---

Nessa put two things in the second column that afternoon and read the first one out before she wrote it, which she does not do.

"I am going to write this as a man said it and not as you would have said it," she said, "and then I am going to write what it does to the yard, and the two of them are not going to be joined up with a colon."

She wrote. What she wrote in the second column, with the reason against the line, was that a paper which names who is going is not a paper which names who will be there, and that the difference between the two is a day's labour and is not a defence of the paper, and that the reason a man of about thirty of that third lay said so on the sixteenth of August is that he has worked for men on papers and knows what a paper is for; and Calder told her to put down that the man of about thirty had come up the hill on purpose and had not been sent for and had not been thanked and was not going to be, and she wrote that in too.

"The refusal of the fifth man is the best answer this yard has got and I have written it where a stranger can find it," Calder said, "and it is also the reason there is one sheet and not two, and those two things are not a triumph and I am not going to let anybody in this shed call them one."

---

He went down to the second lay at about four in the afternoon and found the caulker at his own bench, which is a bench and not an office and has a vice on the end of it and a fire that goes out in the afternoon because there is no coal in this harbor for a private fire.

"Isaac Fell. I am going to ask you for a day by name and I am going to pay you for it, and the day is tomorrow, the seventeenth, and it is a Tuesday, and I am asking you now, on the Monday, and not tomorrow at four in the morning, so that you can say no to it while there is still a Monday left."

Fell put the caulking iron down in the vice and turned round on the stool.

"You did that on purpose," he said.

"I did that on purpose because I have been up that hill four days and every one of them I have been the man who wants something by the time the other man hears about it."

"Then say what you are asking for."

"Eight hours of a Tuesday, in that harbor, wherever you say I want you, and I will pay you one hundred and sixty-eight pence for it out of that yard's own figures at four o'clock in the afternoon, being one mark and eightpence, and the figure is the figure in chalk on the slate at the end stall and it is not this yard's figure, and no penny of it comes out of the owner of any of those seven boats or out of the man with two papers in the office on the west basin, and nobody in that harbor is going to be paid by the person who wants the thing looked at."

"And your name is not going on the sheet."

"Your name is not on the sheet, and your name is not on the leaf of what is wanted, and you are not on a board and you are not on a list and nobody is going to tell me that you are to be put on one."

"I have told you that twice."

"You have told me that twice and I have written it down both times, and I am not going to say it a third time because I would be saying it to be reassuring and there is no use in that." Calder did not move. "Do you want the day or do you not want the day."

"I want the day," said Fell, "and I will be at the stone at half past three, because that is where I am at half past three, and you will not have to come and get me."

**AND ON THE SAID DAY A CAULKER OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WAS ASKED FOR A TUESDAY BY NAME, ON A MONDAY, AND SAID YES, AND WAS PAID FOR IT ON THAT TUESDAY AND ON NO OTHER DAY, AND HE IS ON A DAY AND A WAGE AND AN HOUR AND ON NOTHING ELSE, AND HIS NAME IS ON NO SHEET IN THAT HARBOR AND ON NO LEAF IN THAT SHED, AND THE ONE THING HE SAID ON THE TENTH OF AUGUST WAS NOT SAID AGAIN BY ANYBODY ON THE SIXTEENTH AND WAS NOT IMPROVED ON BY ANYBODY INCLUDING THE MAN WHO HEARD IT.**

**AND A MASTER OF ABOUT THIRTY OF THAT THIRD LAY CAME UP A HILL ON THE SIXTEENTH TO SAY THAT A SHEET WITH EVERY MAN'S NAME ON IT IS A LIST, AND WAS TOLD HE WAS RIGHT, AND WAS NOT THANKED, AND SAID HE WOULD RATHER SAY IT ON A MONDAY THAN FIND OUT ON A SATURDAY, AND CARRYED A PIECE OF PAPER ON SATURDAY ANYWAY.**

*First column, entered on Monday the sixteenth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Monday was not a wage Tuesday and was the sixty-seventh time the wall came down bare and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth with nobody putting it back; and that the cistern was charged for the seventy-third time at about eight and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it; and that a master of about thirty of the third lay was at the door of that shed at about nine in the morning of his own account and was not sent for and put his hand flat on the bench stone about a foot from the sheet and asked for the words of the fourth of August to be said again; and that the holder said them again and then said he was not going to say them a second time in that doorway and asked the man his own question; and that the man said that a paper with every man's name on it is a list, and that the holder said it is a list and was not going to argue the word; and that the holder said that the paper names who is going and does not name who is going to be there, and that the difference between those two is the whole of a day's work, and that if there were a second sheet it would have a name against a man who cannot sleep on the mole head and a name against a man of twenty-two who is owed forty marks in that harbor and neither of those names has been written down and neither is going to be; and that the man said that did not make the paper clean and the holder said he had not said it did; and that the man said he would rather say it on a Monday than find out on a Saturday, and that he is carrying the paper on Saturday anyway; and that the ship's hand read one line of the second column out loud before she wrote it and wrote the man's own words and the yard's own words on the same line and would not join them with a colon; and that at about four in the afternoon the holder went down to the second lay and asked a caulker of about twenty-three for the day of Tuesday the seventeenth by name, on the Monday, and gave the reason out loud, being that he had been the man who wanted something by the time the other man heard about it for four days; and that the said man said yes and said he would be at the stone at half past three, and that his name is not on the sheet and not on the leaf of what is wanted and not on a board and not on a list, and that the one thing he said on the tenth of August was not said again by anybody on the said day and was not improved on.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that a paper which names who is going is not a paper which names who will be there, and that the difference between the two is a day's labour and is not a defence of the paper and does not clean it; and that the reason a master of about thirty of that third lay said so on the sixteenth of August is that he has worked for men on papers and knows what a paper is for, and that he came up the hill on his own account on a Monday because he was going to be in that water on Saturday and would rather say it with a day in front of him; and that the refusal to write a fifth man on a leaf on the fourth of August is the best answer this yard has and is also the reason there is one sheet and not two, and that those two are not a triumph and were not called one in that shed; and that asking a man for a day on the day before the day is a different act from asking him for it on the day, and that it is a different act because it leaves him a day in which to refuse, and that nobody in that harbor is owed a day by anybody and no figure is against any man for refusing one.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and eighteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a paper naming who is going is not a paper naming who will be there, and that the difference between those two is not a defence of the paper and does not make it clean; and that a man's own argument against a sheet and that man's hand at the foot of the same sheet are not two different men and are not a kind of man and are only one man being told something a Monday in advance; and that a day asked for on the day before it is not a favour and is not a favour asked backwards, and that a man who is left a day in which to refuse has not been given anything; and that the fifth thing being a man and not being on the sheet is not a certificate and is not a virtue and is not put on the sheet by anybody in that harbor.*
