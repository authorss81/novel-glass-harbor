# Chapter 351

*Monday the Fifth of July, the Twenty-First Day of the Ninety, the Wall Bare for the Sixty-First Time and the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-First, a Second Paper Going onto One Table in an Office off the West Basin with the Fourth Box Not Filled, a Man of About Thirty-Four of a Pressure House at Ternwatch Ashore off a Lighter at Ten in the Morning With a Sealed Instrument in a Leather Pocket He Did Not Want to Carry, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Monday the fifth of July, in that shed, and in an office off the west basin, and on that mole.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the sixty-first time on it and was put up again in the same hour at the same angle. The cistern was charged on it and walked on none of it, the sixty-first, and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and the next of them is Thursday. Nothing came into this yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there is nothing in the bag, which is no pence and no shilling and no mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, being four marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, so that bag cannot buy a day.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and at no other hour of it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of it and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand and nothing on that hand now but weather, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The second paper went onto the table in the office on the west basin at about a quarter past nine in the morning, which is earlier than it has ever gone on, and it went on with the fourth box not filled, and nobody in that room said anything about the fourth box and nobody filled it.

The man who put it down is about fifty and he has a boat. The man already at that table when he came in, with a paper of his own and a hat on his knee, is a man off the west quay, and he is not this man, and the two of them did not speak, and the clerk of that office — who is about sixty and has been twenty-two years in it and is owed a quarter and has no book and no power — wrote both names in the same register and neither man looked at the other's line.

**A paper on a table in that office is a paper on a table. It is not a claim and it is not a licence and it is not an instrument of anybody's, and no office in this harbor has ever read one of them aloud, and nobody read either of these two aloud on the said day, and the fourth box stayed empty on both of them or on one of them, and the yard does not know which and did not ask.**

Then at about ten in the morning a lighter came up to the south end with the flood and put a man ashore off her that she was not carrying at six.

He is about thirty-four and he is of a pressure house at Ternwatch, and the pressure house is a trade and not an office and not a navy and not a fleet, and he works in it. His name is Garrow Fisk and nobody in this harbor has said it in a month and he is not a villain and he is not a teacher and he was not asked to be either of those things today or on any day of this month. He came off the lighter with a sealed instrument in a leather pocket and the leather was wet through on one side and dry on the other, and the shape of it had been against somebody's ribs for a good part of the way, and when the yard's own man asked him at the top of the steps whose instrument that was, he said it belonged to nobody he had met.

**HE DID NOT SAY THAT HE DID NOT WANT TO CARRY IT, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED AGAIN, AND WHAT IS ENTERED AGAINST THAT HOUR IS WHAT HE SAID AND NO MORE.**

It went onto the bench in that shed with a bulkhead brace against one corner of it, because the floor of that shed is wet under that bench and has been wet since the spring and a sheet of paper laid flat on it comes up with a corner of itself in its own printing. It is a single leaf. It has two seals on it. It has one signature on it, and the signature is *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*, which is the first of those words Calder has ever seen in that order with that name under it, and he had known the name for a long time and had never known the office it belonged to.

Nessa Pike read it out. She is the only person in that shed who reads anything out loud, and she was asked to because her hand is steadier left-handed than it is right, and she did not want to and did it, and the whole of it is here and it took her about four minutes and she read every word of it and did not leave out a word of the small print and nobody stopped her.

**The first thing on it is the word AMNESTY and the second is the word PROTECTION and both of them are spelled out at the bottom in a line of type no larger than the rest, and the bottom of the sheet is where the four things are, and the four things are not on the front.**

Then the seal at the foot of it, second of the two, and it carries a name that is not the first name: *Admiral Soren Vey, fifty-two, of the closed-sea squadron*. It is printed small. It is on the face of that one instrument and it is the only place in this harbor where that name is on any paper, and the yard entered that against the hour and did not read anything into it, because a name printed small at the foot of an instrument is a name printed small at the foot of an instrument and this yard has been wrong about small print before.

Then Nessa said the four things, and she said them the way she says a price, which is that she says the thing and then she says what it is made of.

"**One. The public provenance record, being five leaves, made in her own hand when the two glass objects left this town's own deck under one witnessed surrender, and there are two of those records and this is the one that is not in the circuit office.**"

"**Two. A sheet of forty lines, being one leaf, of which the forty lines carry a name and an hour on all forty and a price and a payer on thirty-seven, and the wrong figure stands beside its own correction and the wrong figure was not scratched out, and the five places of every copy of it are written on the outside of the fold.**"

"**Three. Four copies of that same sheet, being four leaves, in four hands, being one in a room off that fish market and one on a boat in this harbor and one in a coal locker in a town four days up that coast and one in this harbor in a man's own lockers.**"

"**Four. A leaf on which there is one correction and one date against it and nothing else on the leaf.**"

That is eleven leaves in six hands and it is the whole of what the yard knows as the public archive, and the yard did not write that list and has not had it written for it since the twenty-seventh of May, and the woman of no office wrote it and gave it to him and has not been asked for it since.

And then the eighth thing, which is the fourth price, and which was not on the sheet at all in those words. It was in the last paragraph and it was one sentence long and it read: *that the parties subscribing confirm the account of the seizure of the ferry Aster Gannet as the same account set out in the salvage report and the seizure report, and no correction of that account is to be offered or held.*

And Nessa Pike put her hand flat on the bench on either side of the leaf and did not say anything for a moment, and then she said the thing that is in the second column of this yard's own book for the seventh day of July, being the answer, and she said it in her own mouth and not in Calder's.

"**That is not a thing. You cannot hand a person over in a bag and you cannot hand a line of writing over either, and a man who signs that sentence has not got the truth in a box, he has got his own name written under somebody else's account, and then the only difference between him and the man who wrote the account is that he wrote it a week later.**"

Calder Marr did not answer it that day. He did not answer it in that shed, and he did not answer it that week, and there is a rule in that yard that a thing offered to eleven people is not answered by one of them, and he made the rule himself on the twenty-fifth day of June past in about nine people's hearing, and he did not break it on the seventh day of July and he was not asked to.

What he did do, at about half past four in the afternoon, with the leaf still lying on the bench with the brace against it, was write the four prices out on the back of the leaf in his own hand and put it in front of the woman of no office, who had come up the hill at about three and had sat down without being asked, and say that those were the four things and that they were not his to give and that he was not going to answer for them in a shed with two people in it, and that every one of the four had an owner and that the owners were eleven people in five towns and that he was going to say so out loud where anybody could hear it and that he was not going to be the one who said it in a shed.

The woman of no office read the four prices on the back of that leaf. She did not say no. She said a different thing first, and she said it in the ordinary voice she uses for figures.

"**Eleven. You have counted the leaves again and got eleven. I want the owners counted. A leaf is a thing and a person is a person and if a person cannot read, that is not the same as a person who has said yes.**"

And she put her finger on the fourth price and left it there, and Calder Marr looked at the place where her finger was, and the sentence on that leaf is about a man signing a line of writing, and the finger was on the words *no correction of that account is to be offered or held*, and neither of them said the thing that was under the finger, and it was written down the same night with a reason against the line and no name against it.

---

At about four o'clock the man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch came back down to the south end to wait for the lighter, and the yard's own man walked him as far as the top of the steps, which is two hundred yards, and neither of them said anything on the walk out.

At the top of the steps the man of about thirty-four stopped and said one sentence and it was not about the instrument.

"**I have carried paper in a pocket before and I have never once had to think about the weight of it.**"

And he went down the steps and got onto the lighter and the lighter went out on the ebb, and he was not thanked, and he is not going to be, and nobody in that shed has asked him one word about what he said or what he did not say, and there is no office behind him and he is not a face of anything and he does not know what is on the fourth of that sheet and was not shown it.

*First column, entered on Monday the fifth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a second paper went onto the table in the office of the Morrow Quay harbor authority on the west basin at about a quarter past nine in the morning of the said day with the fourth box not filled, and that the man who put it down is about fifty and has a boat, and that the man already at that table with a paper of his own and a hat on his knee is a man off the west quay and is not this man, and that the two of them did not speak and that the clerk of that office wrote both names in the same register and neither man looked at the other's line, and that a paper on a table in that office is a paper on a table and is not a claim and not a licence and not an instrument of anybody's, and that the fourth box was not filled and the yard does not know on which of the two papers it was not filled and did not ask; and that the wall came down bare for the sixty-first time and went up again in the same hour at the same angle; and that the cistern was charged and walked on none, the sixty-first, and that the next charge on the rota falls on Thursday the eighth of July; and that a man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch, being Garrow Fisk, of no office and not a courier and not a servant and not a face of anything, came ashore off a lighter at about ten in the morning with a sealed instrument in a wet leather pocket, and that it was put on the bench in that shed with a bulkhead brace against one corner of it because the floor of that shed is wet, and that it is a single leaf with two seals on it and one signature, the signature being *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*, and that the second seal carries the name *Admiral Soren Vey, fifty-two, of the closed-sea squadron* printed small at the foot of the said instrument, and that the said name is on the face of that one instrument and on no other paper in this harbor, and that no meaning was read into it; and that the said instrument was read out in full in about four minutes by the ship's hand in her left hand, and that its four prices are the public provenance record, being five leaves, the sheet of forty lines itself, being one leaf with the five places written on the outside of the fold, four copies of that sheet, being four leaves in four hands, and a leaf carrying one correction and one date, being eleven leaves in six hands in all, and that the fourth price is not a thing and is one sentence reading that the parties subscribing confirm the account of the seizure of the ferry Aster Gannet as the same account set out in the salvage report and the seizure report, and that no correction of that account is to be offered or held.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the fourth price is not a thing that can be handed over, being that a man who signs that sentence has not the truth in a box but his own name written under somebody else's account, and that this was said in that shed by the ship's hand and not by the holder of that writ and was not improved on by anybody; and that the holder of that writ did not answer the said instrument in that shed and did not answer it in that week, on the rule made by himself in about nine people's hearing on the twenty-fifth day of June past, that a thing offered to eleven people is not answered by one of them; and that the four prices were written out on the back of the said leaf in his own hand at about half past four in the afternoon and put in front of the woman of no office, who had come up the hill at about three and sat down without being asked, and that she did not say no, and that she said that a leaf is a thing and a person is a person and that if a person cannot read that is not the same as a person who has said yes, and that she put her finger on the fourth price and left it there, and that neither of them said the thing that was under the finger, and that nothing was said under it and no name is against that line; and that the man of about thirty-four of a pressure house at Ternwatch said one sentence at the top of the south-end steps at about four in the afternoon, being that he had carried paper in a pocket before and had never once had to think about the weight of it, and that he was not thanked and is not going to be and was asked nothing and was shown nothing of the fourth of that sheet, and that the two hundred yards of walk to the top of those steps is not a figure and none was invented for it; and that the woman of no office has come up that hill on every day of this month and has not been reduced to four marks and has not been paid the forty and is not going to be paid the forty either, and that neither of them has said why she is here.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a sealed instrument with two seals on it and four things named as a price is not an offer made to a man, and that the eleven leaves are not in his keeping and he is not one of the eleven owners, and that he put the four prices in front of a woman and not into a drawer; and that a man who wrote down the four prices on the back of a Crown leaf and got no answer is not a man who has been given anything and is not a man who has refused anything, because there was nothing to refuse until the eleven people he does not own are asked; and that a working man of about thirty-four who carried a paper he did not want is not an officer and not a courier and not a face of anything, and a name printed small at the foot of an instrument is not a visit; and that amnesty and protection are two words and neither of them is a boat and neither of them is food, and this yard has not been told what either of them is worth and has not asked.*
