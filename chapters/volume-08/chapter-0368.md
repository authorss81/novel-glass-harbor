# Chapter 368

*Thursday the Twenty-Second of July, the Thirty-Eighth Day of the Ninety, the Cistern Charged and Walked on None for the Sixty-Sixth Time, a Leaf Written Under the Procedure Costing a Day, a Man of About Forty-Four Back on the Water, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Thursday the twenty-second of July, in that shed, and on that mole, and off the foul.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday, and was a rota day and not a wipe, being the sixty-sixth charge. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on none of it by the man with the hose at about half past nine, the sixty-sixth, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from ten in the morning until four in the afternoon and the holder of that writ did not go over to it, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. On the said day a man of about fifty-one of the six read his own six names on the crown of that mole at about four and put a stone on the corner of the page, and he had not asked anybody for anything since the fifteenth and said he was not going to. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and at about nine in the morning she had a leaf of his in her hand and read it where she sat. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not the man on the far bench.**

---

At about nine in the morning in that shed Calder Marr took a leaf out of the drawer and wrote on it in his own hand, because the procedure asks for writing and not for talking.

The leaf says what a room in Veyr wrote about a convoy with papers, and that he wants to show one of the five leaves in that room if the room asks for it again, and that he is asking a day before as the procedure says, and it gives the day and the hour. He put it on the bench in front of the woman of no office and did not read it out, because there is no asking out loud.

She read it where she sat, and said she had it, and that she will answer in writing or not, and that tomorrow is soon enough, and that is the whole of what the procedure cost that day: a leaf out of a drawer and a day of waiting and the asking not done out loud.

**He wrote it in his own hand and not in hers, because the procedure is his and not hers, and he put nothing against her name on it, and he did not ask her whether the day was enough because a man who asks a woman whether a day is enough has asked her out loud. He put the leaf face down on the bench when he had read where she had read it, and she turned it back up, and neither of them said why.**

**It cost him a day he has not got and a leaf he has got, and it is entered here and is not a resolution of anything else, and nobody in that room in Veyr asked for anything on the said day and nobody in that shed asked for anything more.**

At about eleven a man of about forty-four of the six went back on the water with his left hand still bound at the top, being the day after his nine days, and he went as a boat's man and not as a master, and he was not paid for going and was not asked to pay, and the terms were told him before he went and he said he knew them.

He came past that shed at about two with a coil of line over his shoulder and put it down on the bench and stood there, and did not ask for anything, and was not asked for anything, and said that he had been nine days on the stone and had not been asked by anybody to do anything on any of the nine, and that the ninth had not been the worst of them.

A shipwright of about sixty looked at the binding at the foot of the steps and said it would hold for a boat's work and not for a buoy's, and a bonesetter of about fifty came past on the front street and looked at it from three paces off and said it was not a bonesetter's work and had not done it and would not touch it, and neither of them asked for anything and neither was asked.

At about two a herring skipper of fifty-one with a boat of his own came up the mole and looked at the page with six names on it and then at the tender at the outer water, and said to nobody in particular that he has municipal papers of his own and is not going to use them for a convoy, because his boat goes out alone and comes in alone, and nobody said he was wrong, because a door open to those who use it binds nobody who does not use it.

**And nobody in that harbor was shown to be in the wrong for not using it, and the room in Veyr wrote nothing about him and nobody asked it to.**

At about half past two a chandler of about fifty was behind his counter in the front street with his wife doing the counting behind him and the counter shut to one man, and a man came to it and looked at what was in the window and went away without arguing, and nobody said anything to the chandler, because a man who has said twice that he has nothing to say has said it twice and this yard is not going to make it a third.

At about four the man of about fifty-one of the six was on the crown of that mole with his page, and the wind had not taken it, and he said he had not asked anybody for anything since the fifteenth and was not going to, and he read his own six names over and did not say to anybody what they were for. He put a stone on the corner of it, which he had not done before, and the stone was a flat one off that mole and about the size of his hand, and he turned up the corner, put the stone on, and turned the corner back and took his hand away and looked at whether the page was still going to be there in the morning. Then he went down to the third lay, and nobody in that yard went up after him, and a page with six names on it and a boat for a price is a thing a man puts a stone on, because a man has to be able to tell in the morning that he put something there.

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-second day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Thursday was a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that the cistern was charged and walked on none, the sixty-sixth; and that the holder put in writing on a leaf what a room wants a paper for and gave it a day before under the procedure, costing him a day and a leaf and the asking not done out loud, and that the woman of no office said she has it and will answer or not tomorrow; and that a man of about forty-four went back on the water the day after his nine with his hand bound, not paid and not asked to pay, and came past that shed at about two with a coil of line and said he had been nine days on the stone and had not been asked by anybody to do anything on any of the nine; and that a shipwright of about sixty said the binding would hold for a boat's work and a bonesetter of about fifty said it was not his work and would not touch it; and that a herring skipper of fifty-one said he will not use his papers for a convoy and nobody said he was wrong; and that a chandler of about fifty had his counter shut to one man in the front street and a man came to it and went away without arguing and nobody said anything to him, and that he has said twice that he has nothing to say and this yard is not going to make it a third; and that a man of about fifty-one of the six read his own six names on the crown of that mole at about four and put a stone on the corner of the page, which he had not done before, and had not asked anybody for anything since the fifteenth; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-nine weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a chandler of about fifty has his counter shut to one man in the front street with his wife doing the counting behind him and was not asked and is not going to be asked a third time; and that a man of about fifty-one of the six put a stone on the corner of his own page on the crown of that mole, which he had not done before, and had not asked anybody for anything since the fifteenth; and that a shipwright of about sixty said of the binding that it would hold for a boat's work and not for a buoy's, which is a judgement and not a figure; and that the tender of about ninety tons is still at the outer water with two lines out and no officer ashore and no paper ashore; and that the standing clause entered on the fifteenth is not entered here again and stands, being the count of people who cannot read the notice and the records one in each of nine communities and the office that answers them.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-third line:*

*What it is not: — that a leaf in writing a day before is not a paper shown in a room and is not an answer and does not close anything; and that a man going back on the water with his hand bound is not this yard's recovery and is not discharged by anybody's; and that a man not using papers he has got is not in the wrong and no room has said he is.*
