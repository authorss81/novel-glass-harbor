# Chapter 389

*Thursday the Twelfth of August, the Fifty-Ninth Day of the Ninety, the Cistern Charged for the Seventy-Second Time and Walked on None, a Man of About Forty-Four of the Six Ashore With the Top of Two Fingers of His Left Hand Gone Since the Twelfth of July Past Back on the Water as a Boat's Man and Not a Master and Not Paid and Not Asked to Pay, Forty-Four Pence Out of That Yard for a Loaf and a Half and a Candle, and the Name He Asked for on the First of August Still Refused*

Thursday the twelfth of August, at that cistern, on the third lay, and in that shed.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and was a rota day and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of the market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventy-second time, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and forty-four pence went out of it, being three shillings and eightpence, for a loaf and a half and a candle and a pot for a man who was out all day and came back at about six, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and that yard has not had a hundred and sixty-eight pence come in since the ninth of August and has not had one hundred and sixty-eight pence go out since the tenth, and both of those figures are in this yard's bag and neither of them is anybody's debt.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed on the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-seven days and has not been touched.**

---

He went out on the Wednesday of that week in a boat of about twenty-one tons of the third lay with a master of about thirty in her, and he went out because he asked, and he was asked, and neither of those is a favour and neither of them is a debt.

He is the second of the six men of that harbor who have been ashore with no boat since the nineteenth of June past. He is about forty-four. He lost the top of two fingers of his left hand off the shackle of an adrift mooring buoy off the foul at about half past two in the afternoon of Monday the twelfth of July past, and there is no bonesetter on that coast on a Monday afternoon, and a shipwright of about sixty set it at about four with a strip off a leech and a needle in about forty minutes, and the bill of five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, was paid out of that man's own hand in coin and said twice and not a third time, and a bonesetter of about fifty came on the Saturday and said it was not a bonesetter's work and had not done it and was not going to touch it, and on the sixth of August past stood in his own doorway and said he was not going to say it a third time.

The binding came off his hand for the first time on the first of August past. It is stiff across the two stumps and it will not close on a maul and it will not hold a caulking iron. On the twelfth of August past he stood at the tiller of a boat of about twenty-one tons from about half past five in the morning until about six in the afternoon, and he was not paid and was not asked to pay, and the master of about thirty said the terms out loud before she sailed and the man of about forty-four said he knew them.

**He is a boat's man and not a master and he was put to a tiller and not to a caulking iron, and the reason was given out loud on the quay at about half past five in the morning by the master of about thirty and not by this yard, and the reason was that a man who can steer with two fingers gone is worth more on that boat than a man who can caulk with two fingers gone, and that the caulking on that boat is not caulked and has not been since the spring and will not be done in this month.**

He was fed before he went and the pot came back empty and clean at about six in the evening, and the man of about forty-four brought the pot back to that shed himself and stood in the doorway with it and did not come in.

"That is forty-four pence," he said, "and it came out of that bag and I know what is in that bag because I have read it off the bench stone like everybody in this harbor does, and there is no figure against me and I am not going to ask what is left in it."

"There is three hundred and twenty-eight pence in it," Calder said, "being two marks and eightpence, and the figure it is short of a wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and none of that is your business and all of it is on a board in this shed that is not the market wall."

"I know it is not the market wall. You have not put anything of yours on a wall in this town in three months and everybody has noticed and nobody has said so."

"Nobody has said so because nobody in this town is going to do a thing for nothing and a thing that is not asked for is a thing that is not owed." Calder did not take the pot. "That forty-four pence is not a wage and it is not against your name and it is not going in a return and there is no return. I fed a man who was out in my yard's day and I am telling you what it cost because you brought the pot back and I would not have told you otherwise."

"You would have told me if I had not brought the pot back?"

"No. I would not have told you at all and I would not have written it, and you would have gone home not knowing, and that is how this shed has worked for three months and it is why nobody in this town can say one true thing about it."

The man of about forty-four put the pot down on the step and looked at it and then at the doorway.

"You still have not written my name on a leaf in a drawer," he said. "I asked you for that on the first of August and you said no and you were right and I have thought about it for eleven days on the water and on a stone and I have come to tell you that you were right and that I have come to tell you and not to ask you again."

"I said no and I am going to keep saying no and the reason has not changed and I have not improved on it," Calder said, "and I am not going to thank you for coming up a hill to tell me I was right, and if there is a day and a boat and a wage one day then I am going to come and find you and you are going to say no, and that will be the third time and it will be a no like the other two."

"That is fair," the man of about forty-four said, and picked the pot up, and went down the hill.

**AND A NAME WAS ASKED FOR ON A LEAF IN A DRAWER ON THE FIRST OF AUGUST PAST AND WAS REFUSED AGAINST THE YARD'S OWN INTEREST AND THE REFUSAL STANDS AND THE SAME WORDS WERE USED ON THE SAID DAY, AND A MAN CAME UP A HILL ON THE TWELFTH OF AUGUST TO TELL THE YARD IT HAD BEEN RIGHT AND WAS NOT THANKED FOR IT AND WAS TOLD HE WOULD BE ASKED AGAIN ONE DAY AND SAID THAT WOULD BE FAIR, AND FORTY-FOUR PENCE WENT OUT OF THAT YARD FOR A LOAF AND A HALF AND A CANDLE AND IS NOT A WAGE AND IS NOT AGAINST ANY MAN'S NAME AND IS NOT A DEBT AND IS NOT TO BE CALLED ONE, AND A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-ONE TONS WENT OUT ON THE FIRST CLAUSE AT HALF PAST FIVE IN THE MORNING WITH A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR AT HER TILLER WHO IS ONE OF SIX MEN ASHORE WITH NO BOAT AND CAME IN ON THE SIXTH AT SIX IN THE EVENING WITH A CLEAN POT.**

At about half past three the day's first column was entered with the hours and the one figure, and the second column carried what a man on a mole cannot check, and the third column was empty and nobody has been appointed to it.

There were two lights out at about a mile off the point on the twelfth of August where they were on the first, and they have not moved in eleven days, being counted from the hour they were first seen, and nobody in that harbor has hailed them and nobody in that harbor knows whose they are, and about twenty-six tons of flour is in seven boats of a tow of eight that has not come in and has not been hailed once in eleven days, and no sack of it is in that harbor and no figure has been written against it and no claim has been made on it by anybody.

**AND A HARBOR HAS A TIDE GANG FOUR MEN SHORT AND A HOSE DEAD UNTIL THE SPRING AND A NORTH BERTH OUT AND A MOLE-HEAD LIGHT OUT AND A CISTERN CHARGED SEVENTY-TWO TIMES AND WALKED ON NONE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE CAME IN THAT MORNING ON A LINE THAT ASKED HIM NOTHING AND NOBODY ASKED HIM FOR HIS PAPERS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WENT OUT ON MONDAY ON A BREACH OF THE FIRST CLAUSE WITH NO PAPER OF ANY KIND IN HIS POCKET AND CAME BACK IN ON THE SIXTH AND WAS NOT THANKED, AND A CAULKER OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WAS PAID ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE ON TUESDAY FOR A DAY HE DID NOT HAVE TO ASK FOR, AND A SPARE WARP IN SIX BOATS IS ONE SPARE WARP AND THE MAN WHO HAS IT HAS NOT GIVEN IT AWAY, AND NONE OF THOSE SIX THINGS IS A FIGURE ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS OWED AND NONE OF THEM CANCELS ANOTHER.**

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and fourteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that forty-four pence for a loaf and a half and a candle is not a wage and is not a day and is not a settlement and is not a kindness and does not go against a man who has been refused twice; and that a man back on the water with two fingers gone is not given a boat and is not owed a day and is not a hero and is not a lesson and is not anybody's return; and that a refusal of a name on a leaf is not a judgment on the man and is not a favor to him and is not a rule of that shed, and it is the same three refusals and the same three reasons and neither of them is about the man being refused.*
