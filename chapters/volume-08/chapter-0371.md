# Chapter 371

*Sunday the Twenty-Fifth of July, the Forty-First Day of the Ninety, a Figure Put on a Slate in a Market That Cannot Be Taken Off Again, a Man With Two Papers on One Table Who Wants Two Boats, a Refusal Given Without Improving on It, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Sunday the twenty-fifth of July, in that shed, and in that market, and in the office on the west basin, and on that mole.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, so that bag is a day and a piece of a day of one boat, and this yard has no other figure for a boat than that one and has not got a second one.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not on the quay on the said day and did not come up the hill. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

**And the whole standing inventory of that harbor and that yard is entered once on the said Sunday and is not entered again in the ten days after it, being carried by a short pointer: three marks to a man of Ashtide since the eighth of August past, unsent for; seven pence to that yard's own shipwright; eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, chest shut, not asked in seventy-one days, no digit of anything he ever said printed; eight shillings and tenpence to a woman with her name on the figure; forty marks first after food to a person of no office; forty marks whole to a boy of twenty-two off the stone since the fifteenth of May, the three nots of the eighth of June standing and unasked; a page of six names on a mole with a boat as a price and no figure; a municipal claim on one of the three things in the circuit custody room that cannot be taken back without a request nobody in that harbor is going to make; the chandler's note at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven, unadvanced and not re-written; the delayed note at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, unmeasured; four returns and no fifth; the first echo's witness where he is; the second seam fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth of May, use closed at four figures with no fifth at any hour of any day, its ladder in another man's book, nobody to explain it, not Fisk, and the mark four hundred yards off it of a make nobody in that harbor makes, both seasons nine marks, neither paid and neither going to be; the can upon a stake, not a route. And about eleven people of that harbor cannot read the notice, being a count of people and not a figure arrived at by two people counting separately and adding, and nobody in that yard is to tell any of them anything, and nobody was sent and nothing was written, and the man of about thirty two days down that coast does not know there is a room and is not going to be told by anybody in that yard; and nine communities hold the records one in each, and they have held them six centuries, and the office that answers them is three hundred years old, and the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities.**

---

The market was open and the woman of about fifty-eight had her end stall and her slate out on a crate, the way she has had it out since the spring, and on the slate there was a figure in chalk with the day of the month beside it.

It was the price of a boat with a man in her for a day, and it was one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and it was the third time that week three different men had come to that stall and asked her what a boat and a man came to for a day, and she had said it out loud to each of them and it had not been written anywhere, and so on Sunday she wrote it on the slate.

"You have to put it up," she said, before Calder had said anything at all. "Three times is three times. The next one is going to ask me on a Friday and I am not saying it on a Friday twice."

That was her whole reason and she did not improve on it. The rule she holds is that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again, and she had not taken it off, and it was going to stay on that slate through the winter whether the price came down or not.

The ship's hand read the slate and copied the figure and the date of it into the yard's own book with the reason against the line, being that the figure was said out loud in a market by the woman who owns the stall and is not a figure this yard made up.

Then she priced the day before anybody agreed to it, which is her office and which nobody has ever asked her to do twice.

"A boat and a man, a day," she said. "Eight hours of a man, and he is nineteen and he is worth his day and I would not put him on for less and I would not put him on for more. A fathom of line, and you have got line. A night's food for two, because a man does not work a day on that quay without eating and he will not eat at his own expense twice in one week. Two tides and not one, and if the tide is wrong you are there until it is right and nobody pays a man for standing about. And the figure is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and it is the figure on that slate, and if the slate is wrong then the slate is wrong and not the day."

Calder said he had not got any of it in coin yet and the figure was the figure and he would pay it or he would not go.

**A day's work with a boat and two men at a berth in this harbor is the same figure and has been since the seventh day of July past, and this yard has one price for a boat and a man and it is written in chalk on a slate at the end stall in that market by a woman of about fifty-eight, and this yard did not put it there and cannot take it off.**

---

The man of about fifty came up to that shed at about two in the afternoon with two papers on one table behind him in his own office on the west basin, and the fourth box on the other one of them was not filled, and it had not been filled since the fifth of July past and nobody in that harbor has asked him about it.

He did not come in out of the rain because there was no rain. He stood in the doorway with his hat in his hand and asked whether the holder of that writ would give him ten minutes, and got ten minutes.

"Three things," he said. "I am not asking you for the leaves and I am not asking her for anything at all, and you may tell her I said so. I want to say one of them and it is the only one I have come about."

"Go on."

"The paper of the eighteenth of July names two boats and three men and a tide. It came off my table in a coat, by a man off the west quay who was given it to carry and not to read, and it is a municipal document and I signed the foot of it as a clerk of that office and not as anything else. Two boats. Three men. A tide." He turned the paper over once and put it back the way it was. "If two boats go out under it together and come in under it together, that paper is a thing that happened. If they do not, that paper is a paper on a table, and I have a form in this office with a box in it for days boats went out under municipal papers, and the box is empty, and the empty of it has been in a return of this office since the ninth of November past when a hard at Ashtide was left unanswered and has been in nine returns since."

"That is a figure against a hard at Ashtide," Calder said.

"It is a figure against a hard at Ashtide," the man of about fifty said, "and I have had nine returns with it in and nobody has ever asked me to put it out. I am not asking you to put it out. I am asking you for two boats and three men and a tide, and I will pay for them, and I will sign for them, and it will be in a return of this office before the month is out."

Calder did not answer for a while. The light in that shed comes in over the door and at about two in the afternoon on a Sunday it comes in low and lands on the bench and on the floor and not on the man in the doorway.

"No," he said.

"I have not finished."

"You have not, and no is the whole of it and I will say the rest so you do not have to come back. I will not put two other men's boats on your paper to make a piece of paper true. The three men your paper names have not been asked, and I am not going to ask three men in the last four days of a month to go out on a tide somebody else wrote, and then go back on a return and say we did it. And if I asked them and one of them said no, and I took him anyway because the paper wanted him, then the paper is a thing that happened and it is not worth the having, and you would have it in a return and I would have it on my own page, and one of those two is a lie and I know which one it is before I start."

The man of about fifty stood in the doorway and did not argue, which is not a thing anybody in this harbor expected him to do, and which is the reason the refusal is on this page and not improved on afterwards.

"That is a refusal on a matter of a form," he said at last.

"It is a refusal on a matter of three men."

"It is the same matter. I have never seen a refusal in this harbor that was not about a form in the end." He put his hat on. "I will say one thing and then I will go, and I am not asking you for anything, and I am telling you it because there is nobody else in this harbor to tell. Nobody has ever asked me for it either. The fourth box on the other paper has been empty since the fifth of July and no one has come to this office and looked at it, and I go to the office every day and I look at it, and that is all I have got to say and I am saying it to you because you are the only man on this quay who has ever come into that office and he came for a different thing and did not look at the table."

**AND HE WENT AWAY AND NOBODY IMPROVED ON IT, AND THE WANT WAS NOT SAID AS A WANT AND WAS HEARD AS ONE, AND THE SHIP'S HAND WROTE IT IN THE SECOND COLUMN WITH THE REASON AGAINST THE LINE THAT IT WAS SAID IN A DOORWAY OF THAT SHED AND NOT IN THE OFFICE ON THE WEST BASIN.**

---

At about half past three the ship's hand wrote the day into the first column and the standing inventory into the second, and the fourth column got a line, and the third column was empty for the eleventh consecutive week of this harbor being poor, and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand at the head of it is a boy of twenty-two's and the name beside it is not that boy's and was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past.

At about ten past five he went up to the mole head, because that is where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it is out on the twenty-fifth of July as it was on the fourteenth of May, and the last figure anybody in this harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted to a woman on the first day of June, which was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and both dates are on the board at a counter in the front street.

The small steam tender of about ninety tons was at the outer water with two lines out and her lamp at her masthead, and that is the sixteenth day of her, and nobody in that harbor has been aboard her and nobody has spoken to anybody in her, and the young man of about twenty-three was in her own wheelhouse with a telescope and had not been on her roof since the twentieth.

On the crown, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins and about four feet from a page with six names on it in a hand nobody corrected, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and nine days old, and one of them has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and it has not been lashed back, and the bench under the pump in the yard behind that market is bare for the hundred and seventy-fifth night.

The notice of the seventeenth day of May past is on its wall in a different street for sixty-nine days and has not been touched and has not been taken down and has not been compared with anything by anybody in that harbor.

**AND A FIGURE WENT UP ON A SLATE IN THAT MARKET ON THE SAID SUNDAY AND IT IS A FIGURE ABOUT A BOAT AND A MAN AND NOT A FIGURE ABOUT ANY PERSON, AND NOBODY IN THIS COUNTRY HAS SAID A WORD ABOUT IT, AND THE SLATE IS THE ONLY PLACE IN EIGHT HARBORS ON THIS COAST WHERE THE PRICE OF A DAY'S BOAT IS IN A HAND A STRANGER CAN READ, AND NO OFFICE IN ANY CITY IS OBLIGED TO LOOK AT A SLATE, AND THE FIGURE IS THE SAME ONE THIS YARD HAS BEEN PAYING SINCE THE SEVENTH OF JULY PAST, AND NEITHER OF THOSE THINGS CANCELS THE OTHER AND BOTH OF THEM ARE TRUE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Sunday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall put a figure in chalk on her slate in that market and gave the reason herself, being that three men had asked her the price of a boat and a man for a day in one week and she was not saying it a fourth time out loud, and that the figure is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again and this one is not going to be; and that the ship's hand priced the day before anybody agreed to it, being eight hours of a man of nineteen, a fathom of line, a night's food for two, and two tides and not one; and that a man of about fifty from the office on the west basin came to that shed at about two and said he wanted two boats and three men and a tide out under the municipal paper of the eighteenth of July, and that the holder refused him, and the reason given was that the three men have not been asked and that a paper which needs a refusal to be true is a paper and not a return; and that the said man said he would sign and pay for them and it would be in a return of that office before the month is out, and that the said refusal was given without improving on it; and that the said man said one thing on going, being that nobody has ever asked him for the fourth box on the other paper and that it has been empty since the fifth of July, and that he said it because there is nobody else in that harbor to tell, and that the woman of no office was not asked and was not in that shed; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-nine weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them, the next falling due on Tuesday the twenty-seventh being the fortieth week.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory of that harbor and that yard is entered in full once here and is not entered again in the ten days after this day and is carried by a short pointer, being three marks to a man of Ashtide unsent for, seven pence to that yard's own shipwright, eight marks and a day of a boat to that harbor's own bellwright not asked in seventy-one days with no digit printed, eight shillings and tenpence to a woman with her name on the figure, forty marks first after food to a person of no office, forty marks whole to a boy of twenty-two with the three nots of the eighth of June standing and unasked, a page of six names on a mole with a boat and no figure, a municipal claim on one of the three things in the circuit custody room, the chandler's note at thirty-one with its three counts, the delayed note at eleven and two, four returns and no fifth, the first echo's witness, the second seam closed at four figures with its ladder in another man's book and not Fisk, the mark four hundred yards off it, and the can upon a stake; and that about eleven people of that harbor cannot read the notice, being a count of people and not a figure, that nobody in that yard is to tell any of them anything, that nobody was sent and nothing was written, and that the man of about thirty two days down that coast does not know there is a room; and that nine communities hold the records one in each, and six centuries of that practice, and three hundred years of the office that answers it, and the office's three hundred years was not handed to the communities; and that a third channel found on an adrift buoy on the twelfth of July is not measured and the borrowed instrument went back up that coast on the Tuesday of the thirteenth and has not come down; and that the tender of about ninety tons is at the outer water for the sixteenth day with two lines out, no officer ashore, no paper ashore, no hail and nobody aboard her, and that nobody in that harbor has been told what she is for and this yard is not going to ask.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-sixth line:*

*What it is not: — that a figure in chalk on a slate at the end of a market stall is not this yard's price list and cannot be taken off and is not put up by anybody in this shed; and that a man wanting two boats out and in under a paper and being refused is not an enemy of anybody and is not a friend of this yard, and that a want said in a doorway on a Sunday and heard is a want and nothing came of it; and that a refusal of three men who have not been asked is not a refusal of the Crown and is not a refusal of anything at all except three men's names on somebody else's return.*
