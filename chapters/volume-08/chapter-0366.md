# Chapter 366

*Tuesday the Twentieth of July, the Thirty-Sixth Day of the Ninety, the Thirty-Ninth Week and a Wage Not Paid and the Column Not Moved, Four Things Said Out Loud in a Room in Veyr With a Clerk in It About What a Refusal Costs, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Tuesday the twentieth of July, in Veyr in that room, and in that shed, and on that mole.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-ninth week, and the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and the column did not move, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and thirty-nine weeks now stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and the bag holds two hundred and four pence, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, being in a city, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was out of that harbor on the said day and in Veyr, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

At about ten in the morning in that room the man of about fifty sat at the end of the table and said the room would hear what a refusal costs, and the clerk in the room off it took up his pen.

Calder Marr stood at the table on his own feet, because a thing said out loud in a room is said standing, and he said the four things and did not improve on them, and the woman of no office sat on the bench against the wall and listened and did not write.

"One," he said. "Eleven leaves in six hands stay where they are. Five of them are her hand and they are not given up and they are not protected, and they can be asked for again."

"Two," he said. "A sheet of thirteen lines stays with a name and an hour on all thirteen and a price and a payer on eleven, and two places and no price and no payer, and no fresh copy on any of its five places because of anything said in this room."

"Three," he said. "Six men with no boat get nothing out of a refusal. One of them had a day's wage and one of them lost two fingers off his left hand and paid five shillings and sixpence, being sixty-six pence, out of his own hand for it, and the other four had nothing and were not told there was anything, and none of that is changed by saying no."

"Four," he said. "This room writes down that the door is open to a convoy with papers and binds nobody who does not use it, and nobody in this harbor is paid by the person who wants the thing looked at, and the cheapest oil stays at four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, and the mole-head light stays out."

He sat down. Nobody in the room said anything for a while, because four things said without improvement are four things and not a speech.

The woman of no office said one sentence from the bench, and she said it to the room and not to him.

"I have no figure for what a refusal costs," she said. "I am not inventing one."

The man of about fifty nodded, because a woman saying she has no figure is not a thing to improve on. The clerk in the room off it read back the hour and the four things and her sentence, and asked nothing, and nobody asked him anything, and the pen stopped.

**A woman of no office is in that room and is not on the crew and is not restored and is not forgiven and her forty marks first after food are neither reduced nor paid, and she has no figure for what a refusal costs and does not invent one, and he said the four things and did not improve on them.**

---

At about four in that shed the ship's hand entered the wage Tuesday and the four things in the first column in her own left hand, with the figures and the hours, and the column not moved.

A man of about forty-four of the six came past at about five with his hand still bound, being the eighth day off the water of his nine, and he stood on the crown and looked at the tender at the outer water for a while and said nothing, because a man off the water has nothing to say about a boat he cannot go to.

The tender of about ninety tons lay at the outer water with two lines out, and no officer came ashore that day and no paper came ashore and nobody hailed anybody. She had a lamp at her masthead where she had not had one on the fifteenth, and the young man of about twenty-three stood in her own wheelhouse with a telescope in his hand and did not say anything to anybody about the lamp, and nobody in that harbor walked out to the end of that quay to look at it. A lever of nineteen on a vessel of perhaps eighteen tons was inside the line that evening, made fast on a bollard on the quay, and did not cut anything.

The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and four days old on the said day, and the bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and seventieth night, and neither has moved.

Nothing has been done to the second seam. The fouling on its weather side is about a foot and seven inches and the fouling on the other side is nothing, as it was on the nineteenth day of May past, and it was not looked at on the said day.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twentieth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed by the ship's hand in her own left hand, the holder and the woman being in Veyr: that the said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday, being the thirty-ninth week, and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on it, and that the column did not move and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and thirty-nine weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that in a room in Veyr at about ten in the morning the holder stood on his own feet and said out loud what a refusal costs, being four things and no more, that eleven leaves stay where they are and five of them are her hand, that a sheet of thirteen lines stays with two places and no fresh copy, that six men with no boat get nothing out of it, and that the room binds nobody who does not use the door and nobody is paid by the person who wants the thing looked at and the cheapest oil stays at four hundred and fifty pence and the light stays out, and he did not improve on them; and that a woman of no office in that room said she has no figure for what it costs and will not invent one, being of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food neither reduced nor paid; and that the clerk of about sixty in a room off it read back the hour and asked nothing and was asked nothing; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about forty-four is on the eighth day off the water of his nine; and that the tender is still at the outer water with two lines out and no hail; and that a man of sixty-one is on the crown of that mole with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and is not sent for and not asked and not thanked; and that the standing clause entered on the fifteenth is not entered here again and stands, being the count of people who cannot read the notice and the records one in each of nine communities and the office that answers them.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-first line:*

*What it is not: — that four things said out loud standing in a room with a clerk in it are not a figure and are not an improvement and settle nothing outside that room; and that a woman with no figure saying she will not invent one is not refusing to answer and is not forgiving anybody; and that a wage Tuesday with a column not moved is not a payment and is not a promise and has nothing under it.*
