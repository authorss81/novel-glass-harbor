# Chapter 353

*Wednesday the Seventh of July, the Twenty-Third Day of the Ninety, a Sealed Instrument Read Out in Full in About Four Minutes and Its Four Prices Priced in Hours and Material Before Anybody Was Asked Anything, One Day's Work at the North Berth with a Boat and Two Men at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Ninety-Two Pence of New Stud Chain Gone Out the Same Morning, and the Difference Between What Comes In and What Goes Out Written Down at the End of It*

Wednesday the seventh of July, in that shed, and at the north berth, and on that mole.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole. The next charge on the cistern rota falls on Thursday the eighth of July, being the sixty-second, and the next wipe falls on Monday the twelfth, being the sixty-second, and both of those are inside this week. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard on the said day and ninety-two pence went out of it, being one mark and eightpence in and seven shillings and eightpence out, and the twenty pence that was in the bag on the seventh of this month is not the twenty pence that was in it on the last day of June, and the bag held nothing at all at six o'clock on the morning of the said day and holds seventy-six pence at the end of it, being six shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is five hundred and sixty-four pence, being three marks and seven shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The instrument was read out in full on the Wednesday morning at about ten o'clock, and it was read by the ship's hand in her left hand, and this time there were five people in that shed and the five were told in advance that it was going to be read and were not asked to come and were not asked to stay.

**THE WHOLE OF IT, and it is a single leaf and it is here. First line, the word AMNESTY, and the second line, the word PROTECTION, and then the four things, and then the last paragraph, and then two seals and one signature, and the signature being *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*, and the second seal carrying *Admiral Soren Vey, fifty-two, of the closed-sea squadron* printed small at the foot of it, and that is the whole of the text and there is no fifth thing on it and no schedule and no enclosure and nothing on the back.**

Nessa Pike read it and then she did what she has done since the twenty-fourth day of April past, which is that she priced it, and this time she priced it in hours and in material because nobody in that shed could price it in anything else.

"**The first thing, the five leaves. That took a woman about two months to make and it took her nothing to buy. It is worth exactly what it is worth, which is two months of one woman, and no office in this country can put a figure on that and I am not going to invent one.**"

"**The second thing is the sheet itself, one leaf, and it is in the drawer in this shed with the five places written on the outside of the fold, and the five places are that drawer, a room off that fish market, a boat in this harbor, a coal locker in a town four days up that coast, and a man's own lockers in this harbor.**"

"**The third thing is four copies, in four hands: a copyist of about fifty-nine at a counter off that fish market who has been copying in a room for twenty-two years and has never once asked what she is copying; a herring skipper of fifty-one, on a thwart, in this harbor; a man of about thirty-six out of his father's book in his father's house, a day up that coast, who wrote his copy into a coal locker there, and a locker is not a room anybody can be asked about; and a man of about thirty-eight of no standing whatever with a boat of about thirteen tons, who wrote his in about two hours sitting on a coil of line.**"

"**The fourth thing is a leaf with one correction and one date on it and nothing else on it, and that one is mine. It is in my hand. It is in this shed, in the drawer with the sheet in it, and it is the only one of the eleven that is in a building anybody can walk into for nothing.**"

And then she priced the last paragraph, and she did it in about a minute, and she did it in front of the five of them and she did not soften it.

"**The last paragraph wants eleven people to put a name under a sentence. It is not four things. It is four things and then it is eleven people, and each one of the eleven has to be asked, and each one has to be told what the sentence says, and about one of the eleven cannot be read to by anybody in this yard.**"

"**So the price of the last paragraph is eleven conversations and about forty days and one of them that cannot happen at all, and that is not a figure of money and I have not got one, and I will not put a mark against it, and if anybody wants to tell me this is a great dole I will read them that sentence again.**"

Nobody improved on it. The man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in his own book was not in that shed and was not asked. The woman of no office was in it and said one thing and it was that the eleven conversations were the price of the last paragraph and that the last paragraph was the only part of that instrument anybody would remember in a year, and that it should have been the first line and not the last, and that it is being the last line that it is an offer and not a demand.

---

The north berth had been out since the spring and the reeve's office had it on a list, and the list is not a schedule and has no dates on it and does not bind anybody, and a man of about forty-four came down to the shed at about eleven and said that the ground chain on the north berth was fouled and had been fouled since the spring and that he wanted it up and did not care what it cost and was not going to argue about it.

That is not a salvage and it is not a claim and it is not a charter and it is not an instrument of anybody's. It is a day's work with a boat and two men on the harbor's own ground, and the harbor authority pays for it out of a berth fund and the price is the price, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and it is a mark and eightpence, and it is the first money that has come into that yard since the twentieth of June past.

They were on that ground from about half past eleven until about four in the afternoon, in eleven feet at the lowest, and the chain came up with nineteen feet of it and a shackle that was cut, not parted, and nobody in that harbor has said the word *cut* out loud about it and one man in that boat said it once and nobody took it up and it is in the second column with a reason.

**Nine feet twelve inches of new stud chain went out of that yard before noon, at ninety-two pence, being seven shillings and eightpence, and it went to a chandler's counter in the front street and it was the whole of the day's buying and there was nothing else, and the figure is in pence first because that is the rule of this yard and then in shillings because there are no marks in it.**

And at about four in the afternoon the boat came back up on the north side and the chain went down on the north berth in a heap where it will be picked up when somebody picks it up, and the man of about forty-four looked at it and said that was nineteen feet of a thing he had been putting off since March, and did not thank anybody, and neither did the yard ask him to, and neither of them mentioned the instrument of the morning at any point in the whole of the day.

**THE DAY'S TWO PRICES, WHICH ARE THE WHOLE OF THE MONEY THAT MOVED IN THIS HARBOR ON THE SAID DAY, and they are entered here in the order they went: one hundred and sixty-eight pence in, being one mark and eightpence, for one day's work with a boat and two men at the north berth; ninety-two pence out, being seven shillings and eightpence, for nine feet twelve inches of new stud chain. One hundred and sixty-eight and ninety-two is seventy-six pence, and seventy-six pence is six shillings and fourpence, and there is no mark in it. A boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, so that bag buys rather less than half a day of itself and it cannot buy oil and it cannot buy food and it cannot buy a signature.**

---

At about two in the afternoon a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime came into the shed and asked whether he was the eleventh.

He was told he was not, and he was told that there were about eleven people in that harbor who could not read the notice, and he was told that being the eleventh was not an office and had no duty in it whatever, and that the number was two people counting separately and adding and had not been printed anywhere, and that the only thing anybody was doing with the count was asking whether a man who could read was going to read it to the people who could not.

He said that he had been going to read it to them and had not done it, and that his wife could not read and had not asked him to, and that he did not know what he would have said if she had, and that he had put the notice off until tomorrow.

**And nobody in that shed told him what to do about it and nobody in that shed told him not to, and the woman of no office said one sentence to him at the door, which was that a man who can read is not a public letter office and never has been, and he said he had not thought of it that way, and she said that was all right and that he should go home.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the seventh day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the instrument of the said day is a single leaf and was read out in full in about four minutes in the said shed at about ten in the morning by the ship's hand in her left hand, in the presence of five people who were told in advance that it was going to be read and were not asked to come and were not asked to stay, and that the whole of its text is the first line, the second line, the four things, the last paragraph, two seals and one signature, and that there is no fifth thing on it and no schedule and no enclosure and nothing on the back of it; and that the four things are the public provenance record, being five leaves, the sheet of forty lines itself, being one leaf, four copies of that sheet, being four leaves in four hands, and a leaf carrying one correction and one date, being eleven leaves in six hands in all; and that the four things were priced in hours and material before anybody was asked anything, being two months of one woman for the five leaves, a drawer with the five places written on the outside of the fold for the second thing, twenty-two years of a room off that fish market for one copy, a coal locker in a town four days up that coast for another, a thwart in this harbor for a third and a coil of line for a fourth, and a drawer in that shed for the fourth thing; and that the last paragraph of the said instrument wants eleven people to put a name under a sentence, and that its price is eleven conversations and about forty days and one of the eleven that cannot happen at all, and that no figure of money was put against it and none was invented.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that at about four in the afternoon of the said day a man of about forty-four had a boat alongside the north berth in eleven feet at the lowest and the ground chain came up with nineteen feet of it and a shackle that was cut and not parted, and that one man in that boat said the word *cut* once and nobody took it up and nobody in that harbor has said it out loud about it since; and that nineteen feet of chain went down in a heap on that berth at about four in the afternoon and will be picked up when somebody picks it up; and that a man of about twenty-six with a hod of lime came into that shed at about two in the afternoon and asked whether he was the eleventh and was told he was not, and that he had intended to read the notice to the people who cannot read and had not done it, and that the woman of no office said one sentence to him at the door and that he said he had not thought of it that way.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the seventy-eighth line:*

*What it is not: — that an amnesty and a protection are not two payments and are not two things this yard can spend, and that four leaves and eleven leaves are not the same quantity and the instrument is careful about the difference and this yard was not; and that pricing an instrument in hours is not the same as refusing it, and that the ship's hand priced it and did not refuse it and has not said she would refuse it; and that a day's work at the north berth at one hundred and sixty-eight pence and nine feet twelve inches of chain at ninety-two pence is not a business and is not a recovery and is not a salvage and brought up nineteen feet of a thing that was on the harbor's own ground since the spring; and that a man who carries a hod of lime is not a clerk of a court and a woman who can read is not a public letter office, and that the count of about eleven is a count of people and not a figure and is not on any board in this harbor.*
