# Chapter 374

*Wednesday the Twenty-Eighth Day of July, the Forty-Fourth Day of the Ninety, a Slip of Paper Two Inches by One in the Drawer With the Sheet in It, Four Things Said Out Loud in a Shed With a Woman of No Office on the Bench, a Man of About Thirty-Eight Who Wants to Go and Look at a Tow and Is Told Not To, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Wednesday the twenty-eighth day of July, in that shed, and on that mole, and at the foot of the quay.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a boat with a man in her for a day is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, which is a day and a piece of a day more than that bag holds.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that shed at about nine in the morning and was on the bench at the back of it and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

He wrote a leaf at about nine in the morning and put it on the bench in front of the woman of no office and did not read it out, because there is no asking out loud.

It said four things, and they were the four of yesterday's water, and the woman of no office read it where she sat and gave it back and did not ask him a question about any of them, which is what she has been doing for a week and which costs him more than a question would.

**THE FOUR THINGS, IN HIS OWN HAND, ON ONE LEAF, ON A WEDNESDAY IN JULY: that a tow of eight boats and about forty feet of line came down the fairway on the ebb at about five and came across his boat; that his boat came up two foot on the edge of that fairway, the figure he carried for that reach being eleven and the figure she found being nine, and that he had not sounded it and had no time; that he cut his own stern line, and the tow took his line and not the other way round, and the painter of a lighter of about thirty tons parted; that the lighter was held eleven minutes and took the ground in about six foot and is still lying there or was at dark, and that the eight boats of the tow have not come into this harbor and did not come in last night and are not at the outer water of this harbor this morning, and the flour that was in that lighter is not in this harbor, and there is water in that lighter over the flour in it. And at the foot of the leaf, in the same hand: *there is no figure for what a lighter of flour is worth and this yard is not going to invent one.*

He put the leaf in the drawer with the sheet in it, and the slip of paper went in beside it, and the drawer was shut.

---

The slip of paper had come off a pad about two inches by one, and it had been in the inside pocket of his coat since the middle of the day yesterday, and it had seven thousand on it in pencil in a hand that is not the hand on the board in the boat, and there is no name on it and nothing else on it, and there is no mark against it, and it is not in the currency that this harbor writes money in.

Nessa Pike read it where it lay on the bench and turned it over and put it down again.

"Two inches by an inch," she said. "A quarter of a sheet cut down, and cut with a knife and not off a fold. Pencil, and the pencil has been sharpened with a blade and not a knife, which is a thing a man does who is not writing and is only putting a number down so that a person can see it has been put down."

"What is it."

"It is seven thousand and it is not a sum." She said it as a person correcting a figure and not as a person making a point. "If that were money, this shed would write it out in pence and then in marks and shillings and pence, the way it has written every figure that has ever come into it since the twenty-seventh day of March past. It is not written out in any hand on the boat it came off and it is not written out here, and a figure that is not written out in pence is not a sum of money to anybody who has to keep a book, and I am not going to enter it in this book as a sum of money because I do not know what it is a sum of."

"Then what do you enter it as."

"As a slip of paper with a figure on it and no name on it," the ship's hand said, "which is what it is, and I will put it in the second column with the reason against the line that no mouth on the water said what it was, and I will not put a mark against it and I will not put a column head on it and I will not give it a name, because the day this yard gives a piece of paper a name is the day this yard has an opinion about it, and this yard has not got one."

**AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT WAS DONE WITH SEVEN THOUSAND IN THAT SHED AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT IS GOING TO BE DONE WITH IT, AND A FIGURE WITH NO NAME ON IT CANNOT BE PAID, CANNOT BE INVOICED, CANNOT BE ENTERED AS A WAGE AGAINST A MAN OR AS A PRICE AGAINST A BOAT, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO GIVE IT ONE.**

At about half past ten Calder said the four things out loud in that shed with the woman of no office on the bench and the ship's hand at the end of it, because a thing written on a leaf and put in a drawer is a thing nobody has to stand behind, and he had said in a city in the previous week that he would say things out loud or not at all.

He said it in the order it happened and he did not improve on it and he did not put a reason under any part of it.

The tow was eight boats and about forty feet of line. The ebb was at five. His boat came up two foot because the figure in the book was eleven and the figure under her was nine, and the book was made by a man who was paid in fish and is dead. He cut his own line because the alternative was being put across a lighter of about thirty tons with a case in her arms, and because a line is a thing a man may cut and a lighter is not. The tow took his line. The painter parted. Eleven minutes. Six foot. The tow did not come in and the flour is not in this harbor and there is water in that lighter over the flour in it. He had paid a hundred and sixty-eight pence for the day and had got two hours and twenty minutes of a man's work out of it and a boat with no line on her.

That was all of it. He stopped there and he did not say that it had been worth it, and he did not say that it had not, and he did not offer it against the board in the boat, and nobody in that shed offered it against anything.

The ship's hand put it in the first column with the hours, because a man standing at the foot of that quay in the dark could have counted the lamps on the water and counted them again in the morning.

Mara Venn said nothing about it at all, then and afterwards, and the next time she came up that hill she asked him about a procedure and not about a tow, and nobody in that shed improved on what he had said.

---

At about two a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons came down to that shed and stood in the doorway with a warp over his shoulder and said his boat was going out on Saturday for a trip that would bring her over the line on the way home, and that he has no municipal papers and has never had any, and that he has come in on the sixth line for two years without a paper of any kind, and that on Saturday morning he means to lie off the point and look at the thing that is lying out there.

"No," Calder said.

"You have not asked me what I want to go and look at."

"You have told me what you want to go and look at and it is eight boats and a tow and a lighter with water in her, and I am not going to let you do it." He came out from behind the bench. "Listen to what I am saying. You have a boat of thirteen tons and you sail her with yourself in her, and you have no papers, and you have never wanted any, and there is not a man in this harbor who has ever said you were in the wrong for that, and I am not going to be the first. If you lie off that point on Saturday morning in a boat with one man in her, then for one hour there is a boat with no papers lying off a line of eight boats inside a naval line, and it does not matter in the slightest what you were doing. It is not what you were doing. It is a boat with no papers, next to a line of eight boats, and a lighter with water in her, and the two of them together are one line in a return, and I am telling you that the return would be made out of the two of them and not out of either."

The man of about thirty-eight stood in the doorway and did not argue and did not go away.

"I have not asked you for anything," he said.

"No."

"I came down here to tell you that I was going to and not to ask you whether I might." He shifted the warp on his shoulder. "I have wanted to look at one since I was thirty and there has never been one, and there is one out there now, and it is a Saturday and it is a fair tide, and I have told you instead of going and saying nothing to anybody, and I would do the same again."

"Go on Saturday to the south end and look at the fog come off the water," Calder said, "and if I ever want to stop a man of thirty-eight from looking at a thing, I will think about this morning, and I will not do it again."

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WENT AWAY WITH HIS WARP AND HIS BOAT WENT OUT ON SATURDAY AND CAME IN UNDER THE SIXTH CLAUSE WITH NO PAPER OF ANY KIND, AS SHE HAS DONE FOR TWO YEARS, AND NOBODY SAID HE WAS WRONG AND NOBODY THANKED HIM FOR TELLING ANYBODY ANYTHING.**

---

At ten past five that evening he walked up to the mole head and stood on it, because that is where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

There is no tender at the outer water of that harbor and there has not been one since the morning of the twenty-seventh, and she lay there seventeen days and in seventeen days no officer came ashore and no paper came ashore and nobody hailed anybody and nobody in that harbor went aboard her or spoke to anybody in her, and she was at the head of eight boats inside the line on the morning of the twenty-seventh and nobody in that harbor has been told where she went and is not going to be.

On the crown, about four feet from a page with six names on it, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and twelve days old and one corner has been off since the seventeenth of June past and has not been lashed back. The second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth day of May, and nobody went down to it on the said day and nobody said they meant to.

**AND THE BENCH UNDER THAT PUMP IS BARE FOR THE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIXTH NIGHT, AND THE NOTICE ON ITS WALL IN A DIFFERENT STREET IS ON ITS SEVENTY-SECOND DAY OF BEING UP AND HAS NOT BEEN TOUCHED, AND THE FIGURES OF THE SECOND SEAM AND THE MARK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS OFF IT STAND AT NINE MARKS EACH AND NEITHER IS PAID, AND NONE OF THESE FIGURES IS A FIGURE ABOUT WHAT HAPPENED ON THE WATER YESTERDAY AND NONE OF THEM IS BALANCED AGAINST IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-eighth day of July next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Wednesday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that the holder put four things in writing on a leaf in his own hand at about nine in the morning and gave it to the woman of no office to read where she sat and did not read it out, being the four of the water of the day before; and that at about half past ten he said the same four things out loud in that shed with a woman of no office on the bench and the ship's hand at the end of it, and did not improve on them and did not put a reason under any part of them, and that a man standing at the foot of that quay could have counted the lamps on the water in the dark and could count them again in the morning, and that the said four things are in this first column with the hours and not in any return of any office; and that a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no municipal papers told this yard at about two in the afternoon that he meant to lie off the point on Saturday morning and look at the line lying out there, and was told no, the reason given being that a boat of thirteen tons with one man in her and no paper is a boat with no papers, and that a boat with no papers is the first thing any office would put a name to; and that the said man said he had come to tell and not to ask and that he would do the same again, and that his boat went out on Saturday and came in under the sixth clause with no paper of any kind; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are thirty-six pence in the bag, being three shillings, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and four pence, being three marks and ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a slip of paper about two inches by an inch has seven thousand on it in pencil in a hand that is not the hand on the board in the boat, and no name on it and nothing else on it, and no mouth on the water said what it was, and that a figure not written out in pence and in marks and shillings and pence is not a sum of money to a shed that keeps a book, and that the said slip is entered as a slip of paper with a figure on it and no name on it and is not given a column head and is not given a name, and is not paid and is not invoiced and is not entered as a wage against a man or a price against a boat; and that the said slip went into the drawer with the sheet in it and the drawer was shut; and that there is no tender at the outer water of that harbor and there has not been one since the morning of the twenty-seventh, and she lay there seventeen days, and nobody in that harbor has been told where she went and is not going to be; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the ninety-ninth line:*

*What it is not: — that four things said out loud in a shed with a woman of no office on the bench is not a confession and is not an account and is not a thing anybody is to answer for, and that saying a thing out loud a second time in a room where a clerk of about sixty is not sitting is not a rehearsal; and that a slip of paper with seven thousand on it and no name on it is not a figure this yard can price and is not to be weighed against a lighter of flour with water in her; and that a man of about thirty-eight told this yard what he meant to do instead of doing it, and that telling is not asking, and that being told no is not a thing anybody has been shown to be in the wrong for.*
