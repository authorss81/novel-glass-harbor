# Chapter 388

*Wednesday the Eleventh of August, the Fifty-Eighth Day of the Ninety, a Man of About Fifty With Two Papers on One Table Saying That the Twenty-Seventh of July Is in the Box and That the Fourth Box on the Other Paper Is Still Empty, Six Masters of a Third Lay Asked One at a Time and Five Who Said Yes and One Who Said No and the No Is on the Leaf, a Master of About Thirty-Three With No Papers on Him Coming In on a Line That Did Not Ask Him, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Wednesday the eleventh of August, in that shed, in the office on the west basin, and on the third lay.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the wage column did not move on the Tuesday past and stands where it stood, being twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty-two weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she came up that hill at about eleven in the morning and stood in the doorway of that shed and listened to what a man of about fifty said and asked him nothing and asked the holder nothing. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-six days and the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for eighty-eight days and the chest is shut and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

He came up the hill at about eleven and did not knock and did not take his hat off, which he had not done on the twenty-eighth of July past and had done on every other occasion.

"It is in," he said.

Calder said nothing.

"In the box, in my own hand, with my own hour against it, on Monday morning, at about nine, and I wrote what I know and I did not write who was in the boat because I do not know and I have not asked, and the boat, the hour, the tide, the eleven minutes and the lighter are all in it, and it is the twenty-seventh of July and it is the last day in that box since the fifth of July past and the box is no longer empty." He put his hands in his pockets. "I have not come up here to be told anything and there is nothing in this you are entitled to. I am telling you because in about a month somebody in a room is going to read it and I would rather that you had heard the number off me than off a sheet."

"I have heard it," Calder said.

"That is all right. I have told you and you have not asked me a single question about it and I have been waiting eleven days to find out whether you would and you have not." He took his hat off then and looked at the bench stone and not at Calder. "The other paper on that table has a box in it and the box is still empty and it has been empty since the fifth of July past and it is the only box in that office I have never once wanted to fill and I want to say that out loud in a doorway to one person, and I have said it, and it does not go any further than this doorway."

"It does not go any further than this doorway," Calder said, "and I am not going to put it in any column, because I could not check a hair of it and I would have to write down a thing I cannot check with somebody's hand on it."

"Then do not write it down," the man of about fifty said, and put his hat on and went off the hill.

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WROTE THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF JULY INTO A BOX IN AN OFFICE ON THE WEST BASIN IN HIS OWN HAND WITH HIS OWN HOUR ON MONDAY MORNING AT ABOUT NINE, AND HE TOLD THIS YARD ON THE SAID DAY AND WAS NOT ASKED ONE QUESTION AND ASKED FOR NOTHING, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR ASKED HIM TO AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR COULD HAVE STOPPED HIM, AND IT IS NOT PAID FOR BY ANYBODY ASKING FOR IT AND IT IS NOT TO BE REVERSED, AND THE FOURTH BOX ON THE OTHER PAPER ON THAT TABLE IS STILL EMPTY AND HE WANTS IT EMPTY AND SAID SO IN A DOORWAY AND IT GOES NO FURTHER THAN THAT DOORWAY.**

---

At about two in the afternoon Calder went down to the office on the west basin and stood at the counter and said nothing, and the clerk of about sixty, who has been in that room twenty-two years and is owed a quarter and has no book and no power, said nothing to him, and neither of them put a hand on anything and neither of them asked the other for anything.

The list on the board on that wall is a list. It is not a schedule. It binds nobody in that harbor, and this is said about it once and is not said again this week. The fire party's hose is not on it and is dead and has been dead since before the spring. The tide gang's want of four men is not on it. The north berth being out is on it, and the cistern's rota is on it, and the top name on the rota board is off it, and there is not one name of any man in that harbor on that board.

**AND A LIST ON A WALL IN A HARBOUR OFFICE IS A LIST AND NOT A SCHEDULE AND BINDS NOBODY, AND A HOSE THAT IS DEAD UNTIL THE SPRING IS NOT ON IT, AND FOUR MEN SHORT IN A TIDE GANG IS NOT ON IT, AND NOTHING IN THAT ROOM THIS AFTERNOON CAME FROM A PERSON WHO WAS ASKED ANYTHING, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS SENT TO THAT OFFICE ON ANY DAY OF THIS MONTH TO ASK FOR ANYTHING.**

---

He went down the third lay at about half past three and asked six men, one at a time, and he asked them the same thing in the same words and he wrote the answers down as he came back from each of them, and he wrote the refusals in the same hand and in the same place.

A company of boats, out on a flood and back on a flood, with the same men in them going out and coming in, and a paper between them with each master's name and each man's name on it, and a man on a mole head at four in the morning and at four in the afternoon, and nothing in it for anybody and nothing in it against anybody and a day and a wage for the man on the stone.

Four of them said yes in the first hour.

The master of about fifty-five of that lay said yes and then said the second half of it, which is that a spare warp in six boats is one spare warp and he would want a second one before the day and had not got one and would have to buy one, and that he was not going to be the man who said no to a day he was asked for and then went and did not come.

The master of about forty of the second lay said yes.

The master of about thirty of that lay said yes.

The master of about forty-one of that lay, who had given the best of five answers on Friday last and had not been thanked for it and had not been written down, said yes.

The master of about thirty-three of that lay, who has no papers on him and whose papers are in a locker in a house in another street, said no.

"Not because of the day or the wage," he said. "I have a boat of my own and a wife in the second lay and a locker in a house in another street with three things in it that are not paper, and I have not had my papers in my hand since a Tuesday in the spring. Put my name on a piece of paper and the paper says a master and everybody in that room then wants to know what a master with a boat of his own is doing in a company of eleven, and then somebody wants to know where my papers are, and I have not got them, and I am not going to stand on a quay and say I left them at home so that a man of about nineteen can write it on a board. No. And the two of us are not a person and I am not your man and I am not anybody's."

"That is a no," Calder said, "and it is on the leaf, and I am not going to ask you for the reason twice."

"I know you are not. I gave it to you anyway." The master of about thirty-three looked at the third lay. "What is on your leaf is not a company then. Five of us, if the other four hold, and you have not asked the other four of my lay and you are not going to get me to say you ought to. Five is not a company and I hope you know that, because I have been in five-boat companies off this coast and they are five boats."

"Five is a foot," Calder said, "and I have been told that by a man of about thirty-three with no papers on him on a Wednesday, and I am not going to improve on it."

**AND SIX MASTERS OF THAT THIRD LAY WERE ASKED ON THE SAID DAY, ONE AT A TIME, IN THE SAME WORDS, AND FOUR SAID YES IN THE FIRST HOUR AND A FIFTH SAID YES WITH THE SECOND HALF OF IT AND A SIXTH SAID NO AND GAVE A REASON AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR THE REASON TWICE, AND EVERY ONE OF THE SIX ANSWERS AND OF THE ONE REFUSAL IS ON A LEAF IN THAT SHED WITH A HOUR AGAINST IT, AND NO NAME IS ON THE LEAF AGAINST ANY OF THEM, AND NO FIGURE IS, AND THE SIX OF THEM ARE NOT A COMPANY AND NOT A CREW AND NOT A FLEET AND NOT THE BEGINNING OF ONE, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED WHETHER HE WOULD LIKE IT.**

---

The master of about thirty-three of that lay came past the outer marks of that harbor at about half past four in the afternoon on the sixth clause, which is free, and he came in alone in his own boat, and he did not hail anybody and nobody hailed him.

The small boat of about twelve tons was at that hour at the other end of the tow of seven flour boats a mile off the point, and there was nobody at that harbor's outer water at all, and the sixth clause does not ask a question whether there is anybody there or not.

He put his boat on the third lay at about five and did not go up the hill and did not go into the shed and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him where his papers were, because there was nobody in that harbor who had been asked to ask him.

**AND SIX MASTERS OF THAT THIRD LAY ARE ALL IN THAT HARBOR AT THE CLOSE OF THE SAID DAY, BEING FIVE WHO SAID THEIR PAPERS WERE IN ORDER AND ONE WHO HAD NONE ON HIM, AND THE SIXTH CAME IN ON A LINE OF A NOTICE THAT HAS NEVER ASKED ANYBODY ANYTHING AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR A SINGLE THING WHEN HE DID, AND NOT ONE PENNY OF ANY OF IT CAME OUT OF THAT YARD AND NOT ONE HOUR OF ANYBODY'S CAME INTO IT AND NOBODY'S OWE, LOSS OR GAIN IS TO BE PRICED AGAINST IT, AND WHERE THERE ARE NO PAPERS THERE IS NO DOOR AND THE ONLY LINE THAT LET HIM IN WAS THE ONE THAT DID NOT CARE.**

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and thirteenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a day in a box in a municipal office is not a claim and not a return and not a victory and is not paid for by anybody asking for it, and that a man with two papers on one table and a box that is no longer empty is not an officer and is not a face and is nobody's instrument; and that six masters asked one at a time is not a muster and is not a roll and is not a crew, and that five of them saying yes and one of them saying no is not a company and is not the beginning of one; and that a man who comes in on a line that never asked him a question is not a smuggler and is not a man with nothing to declare and is not anybody's problem, and that nobody asking him where his papers are is not a favour and is not an oversight and is the only thing that was done for him.*
