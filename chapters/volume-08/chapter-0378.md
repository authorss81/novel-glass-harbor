# Chapter 378

*Sunday the First Day of August, the Forty-Eighth Day of the Ninety, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay With a Hand Lead and Three Figures Out of His Own Book and One of Them That Cannot Be Checked, a Man of About Forty-Four Who Asked to Be Put Down for the First Boat and Was Given the Same Answer the Hose Was Given on Monday, and Nothing at All Coming into That Yard or Going Out of It*

Sunday the first day of August, in that shed, and at the edge of that quay, and on that mole.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth in this harbor is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and this yard is now four days from having paid one of those and is not going to have another before the month is out.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about ten. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

---

The man of about thirty-four of the second lay came down the third lay at about eleven in the morning with a hand lead under his arm and a book under the other arm, and he is not Fisk, and the fouling ladder of that harbor is in his own book and in nobody else's, and there has never been a copy of it and nobody has asked him for one.

"I have three," he said. "That is what I have come about and I am not asking for anything. I sounded three places at the edge of the fairway at about seven this morning with this, and there is a light going."

"Read them."

"Four foot and no more at the edge of the stone." He turned the book open on his own arm. "Six foot and three inches a boat's length inside that. Eight foot and nine inches at the third place, and that third place is where your lighter went on Tuesday evening, and I have had eight foot and nine inches in this book at that place since a Tuesday in the fourth of March and nobody has ever asked me for it, and I have never had a reason to give it to anybody."

The ship's hand took the book and copied the first and the third of them into the first column with the hours, and put the second of them in the second column with the reason against the line that the distance between the first place and the second place is in that man's own boat lengths and not in feet, and that a figure a stranger cannot walk out with a lead and a line is not in the first column, and the first and the third of them are in the first column because a man with a lead can go and get them again.

"You have not asked me about anything else," said the man of about thirty-four, "and I want to tell you what happens if you do."

"Go on."

"I will give you the whole fairway, and I will keep a book of it, and I will not give you the other thing, and I am not going to tell you what the other thing is by naming it." He shut the book on his arm. "The other thing is a fouling that has been in that book since before I was in the second lay, and I keep it because I keep everything, and the reason I keep it is that the harbour has never once asked me for a figure, and the day it asks me for one is the day somebody in an office has decided what the answer is for. And if you ask me for it, I will put this book in my pocket and go home, and I will not be angry, and I will not do it again, and I will not read the book to anybody in this harbor again while I am standing up."

Calder looked at him for a while and then looked at the water.

"I am not going to ask you," he said. "I did not ask you on the twenty-fourth of May past and I did not ask you in June and I am not going to ask you now, and I am not going to be the man in this harbor who finally asks, because there has been a lot of things in this harbor that were going to be all right and were not, and I have not got one more of those in me."

"Good," said the man of about thirty-four, and went back up the third lay, and did not say anything else, and nobody followed him.

**THE FOULING FIGURE AT THE THIRD PLACE OF THE THREE IS IN A MAN'S OWN BOOK SINCE A TUESDAY IN THE FOURTH OF MARCH AND IS NOW IN THAT SHED'S FIRST COLUMN WITH THE HOUR ON IT, AND THE SEA AT THE FIFTH SEAM IS FOULED ABOUT A FOOT AND SEVEN INCHES ON THE WEATHER SIDE AND NOTHING ON THE OTHER, WHERE IT WAS ON THE NINETEENTH OF MAY PAST, AND IT WAS NOT LOOKED AT ON THE SAID DAY AND WAS NOT ASKED ABOUT AND WAS NOT MEASURED AND WAS NOT TOUCHED AND WAS NOT NAMED, AND NO PERSON OF THAT YARD WAS IN ANY WATER ON THE SAID DAY.**

---

The man of about forty-four of the six came past that shed at about four in the afternoon with a coil of line over his shoulder and put the coil on the bench, and the top of his left hand was out of the binding for the first time since the twelfth of July, and it was stiff across the two fingers and it was pink and it was not a bonesetter's hand and never had been.

"I want to be put down for the first boat," he said.

Calder did not move.

"I am not asking you for a day and I know there is not one. I am asking you to write a name on a piece of paper and put it in a drawer, so that when there is a boat and a day and a wage of four marks or a hundred and sixty-eight pence, whichever it is, I am the one you ask first and not the one you ask last." He turned his hand over and looked at it. "I will take a week's wage for a week. I am not asking for the day on the twelfth of July, and I never asked for that, and I paid the five shillings and sixpence out of my own hand and nobody in that shed has offered me a penny of it and I did not expect one."

"There is no list in this shed," Calder said.

"I know there is no list in this shed. That is why I am asking you for one, and I have thought about it, and I have got it wrong and I have come anyway."

**"On Monday I told a man of about thirty-two that I would not put his name against a hose in a book, because a figure against a man in a return of that office follows the man to a hard, and I gave him the reason and he went away with it." Calder put his hand flat on the bench, on the coil, not on the man's hand. "It is the same answer and it is the same reason and it goes against me and I am giving it to you the same way, and the reason is that the day there is a list, the list is the boat, and everybody who is not on it is not asked, and everybody who is on it has a figure, and the six of you have a page on a mole that says a boat and no figure and the whole reason that page has been any use is that there is no figure on it. A list would finish it."**

The man of about forty-four stood there and nodded before Calder had finished, which told him that the man had known the answer before he came, and that he had come to be told it and not to argue it, and that a man of forty-four with two fingers gone on a job he worked for nothing for nine days is not a man who argues with a rule he has heard the reason for.

"That is the answer you gave the hose man," he said. "I heard about it on Wednesday from a man who was there. I will take it the same way and I will not come and ask you again this month." He picked the coil up. "And you are not to tell me I could not have been a name on a piece of paper in June. I know. I have thought about that one too, on a mole, for nine days."

---

At about half past four the day's first column was entered with the hours and the three figures and the reason against the second of them, and the third column was empty and nobody has been appointed to it.

At about ten past five he was on the mole head, where he goes, and there is nothing up there and nobody sent him.

There were two lights out at about a mile where on Wednesday evening there had been none, and they were not the tender's, and they had not moved in an hour, and nobody in that harbor has hailed them and nobody in that harbor knows whose they are. On Wednesday there had been one line of eight boats inside the line with a tender at the head of them, and on the said Sunday there was a line of eight with a tender at the head of them, and seven boats at about a mile, and one lighter at a quay with water in her, and that is four things and the four of them were one thing on Tuesday afternoon.

The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for seventy-six days. The bench under that pump is bare for the hundred and eighty-second night. The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and sixteen days old and one corner has been off since the seventeenth of June past.

**AND A HARBOR HAS THREE FIGURES IN A COLUMN THAT A MAN WITH A LEAD CAN GO AND GET AGAIN AND A FOURTH FIGURE IN THE SAME WEEK THAT IT CANNOT, AND HAS A FIGURE IN CHALK THAT WILL NOT COME OFF A BOARD, AND HAS A FIGURE ON A LEAF IN A DRAWER WITH NO NAME ON IT, AND HAS NOT GOT A FIGURE OF ANY KIND FOR WHAT CAME ASHORE OUT OF A LIGHTER, AND EVERY ONE OF THOSE IS TRUE AND NONE OF THEM IS THE SAME THING AS ANOTHER, AND THIS YARD DOES NOT PUT THEM IN THE SAME COLUMN AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the first day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the said Sunday was not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay, who is not Fisk and who keeps the whole fouling ladder of that harbor in his own book and has never been asked for a copy of it, sounded three places at the edge of the fairway at about seven in the morning on the said day with a hand lead, and that the figures are four foot and no more at the edge of the stone, six foot and three inches a boat's length inside that, and eight foot and nine inches at the third place where the lighter went on the evening of the twenty-seventh, and that the said man has had eight foot and nine inches at the third place in his own book since a Tuesday in the fourth of March and has never had a reason to give it to anybody; and that the first and the third of the three figures are in this first column with the hours because a man with a lead can go and get them again, and that the second of the three is in the second column because the distance between the first place and the second place is in that man's own boat lengths and not in feet; and that the said man said he would give the whole fairway and would not give the other thing and did not name it, and that if he was asked about it he would put his book in his pocket and go home, and that the holder did not ask him and has not asked him and is not going to; and that a man of about forty-four of the six came past that shed at about four in the afternoon with the top of his left hand out of the binding for the first time since the twelfth of July, and asked to have a name written on a leaf in a drawer so that he would be the first asked and not the last, and offered to take a week's wage and said he was not asking for the day on the twelfth of July, and was refused with the same reason and the same words as the hose was refused on the twenty-sixth, and that the reason is that a list becomes the boat and everybody not on it is not asked and everybody on it has a figure, and that the page of six names on the mole has said a boat and no figure since the eighth of July and a list would finish it; and that the said man said he had known the answer before he came and that he had come to be told it and not to argue it, and that he will not come and ask again this month; and that at about ten past five on the said day there were two lights out at about a mile which were not there on Wednesday evening and which had not moved in an hour, and that nobody in that harbor has hailed them; and that nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and that there are two hundred and four pence in the bag, being one mark and three shillings and eightpence, and that the figure short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks and nine shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid; and that nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past; and that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest and forty weeks fallen due with no envelope against any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that six foot and three inches a boat's length inside the edge of the stone, the distance being in that man's own boat lengths and not in feet, and a figure a stranger cannot walk out with a lead and a line is not in the first column; and that a fouling which has been in that man's book since before he was in the second lay is not in that shed's book and is not going to be asked for by anybody in that shed, and that the day a harbour asks a man for that figure is the day somebody in an office has decided what the answer is for; and that the sea at the fifth seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth of May past, and it was not looked at on the said day and was not asked about and was not measured and was not touched and was not named, and no person of that yard was in any water on the said day; and that a third channel was found on an adrift buoy on the twelfth of July with a borrowed instrument which went back up that coast on a Tuesday and has not come down, that a pinger puts down two channels on a thing that has been in the water a long while and is not reading anything but counting what the iron on the other side of a hull is doing to a note, that a man with the instrument on the wrong side of a plate gets a figure and not a truth, that the holder does not have the instrument and had three soundings off a hand lead in one morning and did not put the two in the same column; and that the standing inventory entered in full on the twenty-fifth is not entered here again and stands.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and third line:*

*What it is not: — that a man offering to take a week's wage and asking for a name in a drawer is not a man asking for a figure and is not owed one; and that a list is not a crew and is not a muster and is not a way of being fair to anybody, and a list made out of six men who have a page and no figure is a list made out of a page; and that not asking a man for a figure he has kept since March is not a thing anybody has to be thanked for, and is not caution, and is not going in this column as though it were a virtue.*
