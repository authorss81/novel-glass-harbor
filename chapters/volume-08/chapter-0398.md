# Chapter 398

*Saturday the Twenty-First of August, the Sixty-Eighth Day of the Ninety, Seven Boats Out Past the Outer Marks at Half Past Seven in the Morning on a Breach of the First Clause With a Sheet in a Master's Own Pocket, a Body of Named Men in the Same Water at the Hour, a Transport of About a Hundred and Forty Tons Coming Down the Reach on the Same Flood With About Sixty Tons of Flour in Her and a Boat's Boat Alongside Her, Eleven Marks and a Word and a Date Cut Into Timber Read Aloud in a Boat, About Nine Boats of a Squadron Who Stopped, a Man of About Thirty-One With a Board Under His Arm Who Stepped Into a Boat and Was Not Followed, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Paid on the Flat of a Stone at Four o'Clock, and a Man of About Thirty-Four of a Screw-Steam Packet Out of Bramble Strait Who Was on That Reach and Says What He Was In*

Saturday the twenty-first of August, on that mole head, on the third lay, on that reach, and in that shed.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard on the said day, being a Saturday of a man of about twenty-three at that mole head, paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon, and nothing came in, and there are one hundred and sixty pence in the bag, being one mark, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and eighty pence, being three marks, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the whole of what came into that yard in the last ten days is one hundred and sixty-eight pence on one day, being the eighteenth, and the whole of what went out of it is three hundred and thirty-six pence on two days, being the seventeenth and the said day, and the two are not the same figure and neither of them is the wage of four marks, which is six hundred and forty pence and has not been paid for forty-three weeks and is not going to be paid on the forty-fourth.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed at about eight in the morning and did not go up the hill and did not see the sheet go into a pocket. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for ninety-six days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for ninety-eight days, the chest is shut, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

He was on the flat of the bench stone at about half past three in the morning with a maul and a bucket of oakum and his own hands and he had been there since about three, and he had been asked for that day by name three times.

At half past six the seven were lying off the point at about a mile, and the man of about fifty-five of that third lay had the sheet folded in his own inside pocket with the seven hands and the seven hours on the outside of the fold where any man in any of the seven boats could put his thumb and feel them.

Calder went out in his own boat of about nineteen tons with the man of about nineteen at the tiller, past the outer marks at about half past seven in the morning, on a breach of the first clause of a notice of the seventeenth of May past, which is the way it is done. No declaration went with her. No paper of that yard's went in her. The man of about nineteen said the time and did not say anything else about it, and nobody hailed them going out and nobody hailed them coming in and no man of that yard was put aboard any of the seven and not one penny of any of that came out of the owner of any boat.

They did not do anything for the first two hours, and that was the work.

---

**THE WORK ON THE SAID DAY WAS BEING THERE, IN COMPANY, WITH A PIECE OF PAPER IN A MAN'S POCKET, AND IT WAS THE WHOLE OF IT, AND A PIECE OF PAPER DOES NOT KEEP A MAN AT A TILLER AND A TIDE DECIDES THAT.**

Calder got his boat alongside the second of the seven at about ten and put himself in a position where the seven of them could hear one voice, and he said it out loud, once, in a boat, on the water.

"I am going to say the same thing in seven boats and it takes about nine minutes in each and you have all heard it from me in this shed, and I am saying it now because a thing said in a shed in a town is a thing a man in a room can write down as a thing somebody said in a shed, and this is not that." He did not raise his voice and about thirty men could hear him. "Each of you will say what your boat has and what your boat has not got, and you will say the hour, and I shall write it on this leaf in the wet, and the leaf is in my own pocket and it goes back in the shed tonight and it is not going anywhere near any office. A spare warp. A man who can read a clock and not a watch. Somebody awake ashore at the other end at the hour. A hand lead and a line and somebody who can use one. And the fifth thing is a man on a mole head at four in the morning and at four in the afternoon and he is not on that sheet and he is not on this leaf and he is not on a board and he is not on a list, and he is on a day and a wage and an hour, and the wage is a mark and eightpence and it came out of my own figures and not one penny of it came out of the owner of any of your boats, and I am saying that out loud on the water so that there is a man on this water who has heard it said."

They said it. Seven boats and about thirty men, and two of them had nothing and said so, and one of the seven said that he had a spare warp and a second warp he had bought out of his own pocket on the fifteenth of August, and the figure for that warp is not on any paper in this harbor and was not said out loud on the water, and nobody asked for it.

Then, at about twenty past ten, a boat of about thirty tons came down the reach.

---

She was a transport of about a hundred and forty tons with a merchant's company aboard and about sixty tons of flour in her and three boats of her own in tow, and she was coming down that reach on the flood going the way a loaded transport comes down that reach going that way, which is the only way she can come, and which is the reason that harbor can make.

At about half past ten the small boat of about twelve tons, which has a boat's boat in her and a lever of about nineteen at her tiller and which was on the water off that point on the seventh, the ninth and the thirteenth of August, came out from the deep water with two of hers, and a boat's boat went along the transport's port side.

They cut her fore cable with a wire and stove her fore hatch with a maul and put a hole in her garboard with a boat's hook, and the three of her own boats in tow cut their own painters and went their own ways, and the transport came on down the reach with her fore hatch open and about half of what she had in her in the water by the time she was inside the point, at about one in the afternoon.

Nobody was hurt. Nobody was named. Nobody was counted, and no figure of anybody came ashore with her, and nobody in that harbor has laid a claim for anything and nobody has asked for a thing. The flour of that transport did not all come ashore and did not come ashore at all into that harbor, and nobody has been made whole, and the paper of her own company says nothing about the four hours between the half past ten and the one o'clock and nobody has asked for a copy of it.

**AND ALL SEVEN BOATS SAW IT, EVERY ONE OF THEM, AND EVERY MAN IN THEM, AND THE ONLY THING THAT ANY OF THEM HAD TO MAKE IT A RECORD WAS A SHEET OF PAPER WITH THEIR OWN NAMES ON IT IN A MASTER'S POCKET, AND THE MASTER OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE PUT HIS THUMB ON THE FOLD OF THAT SHEET AND SAID ONE WORD OUT LOUD, AND THE WORD WAS *AULD* SCOTTISH FOR THE PAST TENSE OF TO SEE, AND HE SAID IT IN HIS OWN MOUTH AND NOBODY TOOK IT DOWN.**

---

The nine boats of the closed-sea squadron were in company inside the line at about a mile and a half at the time, at the same hour, working from civilian hulls the way they had been working since the eighth of May.

Calder saw them first and did not say anything about them for about twenty minutes, and what he did at about eleven was put his own boat across the reach into the middle of the seven and say, in the same voice he had used for nine minutes in seven boats, one more thing.

"I am going to say a thing that I have not said in a room and I am not going to say it in a room tonight either, so that nobody in this harbor can tell me I said it in a room first and then in a boat." He put his hand flat on his own thwart. "On the twenty-seventh of July past I lay alongside a boat in a line inside the line and I read a board off an after thwart under a sloping canvas and I did not get it and I did not get near it and I did not ask. It is about a yard wide. There is one word at the head of it in block capitals and the word is *UNSEALED*. There are two ruled columns under it. In the right-hand one there are eleven marks against dates and the first of the dates is the eighth of May and the last of them is the twenty-second of July. At the foot of it there are two impressions struck side by side and I read neither of them, and I do not know whose they are, and I am not going to find out this morning. And above the head of the board there is a date cut into the timber, and the date is the fourteenth of February."

Nobody in the seven boats said anything and the water did not say anything.

"That is a shape and it is not a sheet," Calder said, "and I have not got the sheet and there is no copy of it in that shed and no document of yours has changed hands with me at any hour since the spring, and nothing in my pocket is yours. And I am not going to stand in this boat and say a word about what was in any of those eleven hulls, because I do not know and I am not going to guess, and I am not going to say a word about any man who was aboard any of them, because I do not know that either and a man in a room will ask me for it and I will not have it."

The work on that reach stopped at about eleven and did not start again that day, and it stopped the way work stops, which is that a man put a maul down and then eleven other men put theirs down over about a quarter of an hour, and not one of them was brave and not one of them was brave in the sense that anybody in this series has ever been brave.

It stopped because a thing done in front of about thirty men in seven boats with a sheet of paper naming every one of them in a master's pocket is a thing somebody will write down, and because the word at the head of the board is a word about what the work is, and thirty men on that water had that word in their ears at the same minute and in the same words from one mouth.

---

The man with the board under his arm was in the third of the nine boats, and he had a satchel across him and he was about thirty-one and he was not an officer and nobody in that harbor had ever seen his face before.

He stepped from the third boat into the boat's boat of the second of the nine at about twenty past eleven and the boat's boat went away up the reach at ten knots or thereabouts, and the master of about forty-one of that third lay had a boat at the quarter of his own with the man of about twenty-two in her, and a boat at the quarter is forty minutes behind a boat's boat and is a great deal more than forty minutes ahead of a man in another man's hull with a lead line and no water in his own boat.

Calder did not ask him.

The master of about forty-one looked at him. Calder did not ask him and the master of about forty-one did not go, and his man of about twenty-two did not take the painter out, and the boat's boat was inside the point at about noon and was not seen again that day by anybody in that harbor, and nobody stopped the man who was in it and nobody asked him where he was going and nobody thanked him for anything and he is not mentioned again in a room by anybody in this series.

"You had him," said the master of about fifty-five, at about two in the afternoon, in a low voice, across about four feet of water.

"I had him," said Calder.

Nobody said anything else about it, and Calder did not improve on it and did not explain it and did not say what the other thing was, and there is one line in the second column of that leaf and the second column is where a man puts a thing he cannot check with a pencil, and it is not in the first column and it is not in any return and it is not in any office and it was not entered in the wage column and it cost nothing and it is not nothing.

**AND THE MAN WITH THE BOARD UNDER HIS ARM WALKED AWAY FROM IT, AND HE WAS NOT STOPPED, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED WHERE HE WAS GOING, AND HE WAS NOT THANKED, AND HE WAS NOT MENTIONED AGAIN IN A ROOM, AND THE COST OF THAT IS A SHEET THAT STILL EXISTS SOMEWHERE AND IS NOT IN THAT HARBOR AND IS NOT IN THAT SHED, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT THIS VOLUME COST AND IT IS NOT A WOUND AND IT IS NOT A DEATH.**

---

He paid the man on the stone at about four in the afternoon, on the flat of the bench stone, in coin, out of the bag, one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and Fell counted it and put it inside his coat.

"I was on here at half past three," he said, "and I have been on here all day at both ends of it, and I was not asked for the Sunday and I will not be on here on the Sunday, and I am telling you that now so that you do not come up on the Sunday and find nobody and stand there and make something of it."

"I was up here on the Thursday morning at four and there was nobody on it," Calder said, "and I did not stand there and make something of it and I have not said one word to anybody in this harbor about that stone being empty on the Thursday morning and I am not going to, and you have just told me the Sunday and I have taken it."

"Then that is two," said Fell, "and I am not going to charge you for the two."

---

They came in past the outer marks at about half past five in the evening on the sixth clause of the notice of the seventeenth of May past, which is free, and which has not asked anybody anything since the day it went up, and which was written by an office that did not write it for that harbor. The man of about nineteen at the tiller said the hour and the clause and nothing else, and the seven came in and the sheet went back into a man's own pocket on the mole and no man of that yard said a word about it at any time.

**AND A TENDER OF ABOUT NINETY TONS THAT HAD BEEN AT THE HEAD OF EIGHT BOATS INSIDE THE LINE FOR TWENTY-SIX DAYS WAS NOT THERE ON THE SAID MORNING AND WAS NOT SEEN AGAIN ON THAT REACH, AND THE WORK OF A SQUADRON ON THAT REACH STOPPED AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND DID NOT START AGAIN THAT DAY, AND A SHIP OF ABOUT SIXTY TONS WITH THE SQUADRON'S OWN BOATS IN COMPANY WITH HER WENT UP-COAST IN THE NIGHT, AND THE SMALL BOAT OF ABOUT TWELVE TONS WENT UP-COAST IN FRONT OF HER AND DID NOT COME BACK DOWN, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS ON ANY OF IT AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS TOLD OF IT UNTIL A MAN WHO HAD BEEN ON IT CAME UP A QUAY AND SAID SO IN FRONT OF WHOMEVER WAS STANDING THERE.**

The man of about thirty-four of a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait was on that quay at about ten past six in the evening with his own painter over his shoulder.

"There was a ship of about sixty tons going up-coast in the night with three of their boats in company with her," he said. "She was not a man-of-war and she had a company aboard and a master's company, and I have been up and down that reach for eleven years and I know what a man-of-war's lights look like and that was not one. And the little one of about twelve tons went up-coast in front of her and she is not on that reach and I have been past that reach twice since and she is not on it." He put the painter down. "And their work on that reach stopped at about eleven and did not start again that day, and there was nothing on the water at all after about twelve. That is what I saw and I am not here to tell you what it means and I have not come up here to be thanked and you are not going to thank me."

"Nobody in this harbor is going to thank you," Calder said, "and nobody in it is going to say what it means either, and I have a leaf in that shed and I am not going to put a word about a ship in it, because I could not check one hair of it and it is not mine."

In that shed at about half past nine that night, in one sentence, and not improved on by anybody afterwards, Calder said that the squadron had lost the ship and that the man who had it had gone up-coast, and that not one penny of it had come into that yard and that there was nothing in that yard's leaf that came of it and that it was not a victory and there was nobody in that harbor to whom it was one.

**AND A SQUADRON HAS LOST A SHIP AND THE MAN WHO HAD IT, AND NOBODY WHO WAS IN A ROOM HAS SAID SO, AND THE ONLY MAN WHO SAID ANYTHING ABOUT IT WAS A MAN WHO WAS ON THE REACH AND HE SAID WHAT HE SAW AND SAID HE WAS NOT HERE TO SAY WHAT IT MEANT, AND IT WAS NOT A LESSON AND IT WAS NOT A FALL AND NOBODY DUCKED AND NOBODY CHEERED AND THERE IS NO FIGURE AGAINST IT AND NO FIGURE FOR IT.**

and that a caulker of about twenty-three was on the flat of the bench stone at the mole head at about half past three in the morning and had been there since about three; and that seven boats of that harbor lay off the point at about a mile at half past six in the morning and that the sheet was folded in the inside pocket of a master of about fifty-five of that third lay with the seven hands and the seven hours on the outside of the fold; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourteenth is not entered here again and stands; and that being in company with a piece of paper in a man's pocket is not a record and is not a defence and is not a fleet and is not the beginning of one, and that the whole of what thirty men did on that water on that day was be there, and that what they were for was to be there afterwards; and that a thing read out in a boat on the water is not a thing said in a room and is not a paper and does not become a record because a boat heard it, and that a shape of a board is not a sheet and eleven marks in a column are eleven hulls and not eleven men and not eleven families, and that nobody in this yard knows who was aboard any of the eleven and is not going to find out; and that a man with a board under his arm walking away from it is not a man got away with anything and is not a defeat and is not a victory, and that the holder had a route out and did not have it and did not ask a man with a boat at his quarter to go, and that the cost of that is a sheet that still exists and is not in that harbor, and it is one line in this column and it is not in the first column and it is in no return and it cost nothing and it is not nothing; and that a squadron losing a ship and the man who had it is not a lesson and is not a fall and is not a thing this yard has won, and that not one penny of it came into that yard and nothing in that yard's leaf came of it; and that nobody in that harbor counted anybody that day and no casualty figure exists on any page and nobody is named and no body is named, and that what is on that reach this volume is a loss of flour and a loss of time and a thing done to a hull.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and twenty-third line:*

*What it is not: — that seven boats in company on a flood with a sheet in a master's pocket is not a fleet and is not a company and is not a crew and is not a record and is not the beginning of one; and that a word read out in a boat on the water is not a paper and does not become a record because thirty men heard it, and that a shape of a board is not a sheet; and that a man with a board under his arm who walks away from it is not a man who has been let go and is not a man who has got away with a thing and is not a defeat and is not a victory; and that a loss of flour and a loss of time and a thing done to a hull is not a death and is not a casualty and is not a figure and nobody was counted; and that a squadron without a ship is not a fall and is not a lesson and is not a thing anybody in that harbor is to be pleased about.*
