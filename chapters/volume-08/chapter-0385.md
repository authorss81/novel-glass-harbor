# Chapter 385

*Sunday the Eighth of August, the Fifty-Fifth Day of the Ninety, a Page of Six Names on a Mole and a Man of About Fifty-One Who Did Not Say the Thing About It and Three Other Men Who Did, a Man of Sixty-One With a Boat's Hook Who Said One True Sentence and Was Not Asked a Second, and a Boat of About Thirteen Tons With No Paper of Any Kind in It at All*

Sunday the eighth of August, on the crown of that mole, and on the third lay, and in that shed.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday, was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and seventy-two pence in the bag, being two marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and six masters of that harbor are still lying off the point at about a mile and a half this evening with five of them who said their papers were in order and one of them who has none on him, and nobody in that harbor has written a day against any of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she came up that hill at about four in the afternoon, sat on the bench at the back, and asked nothing, which is what she has done every time she has come up it since the twenty-eighth of July. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for eighty-three days.**

---

He went up to the mole head at about ten past five in the evening, because that is where he goes, and there was nothing up there and nobody had sent him.

The mole-head light is not lit. Its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past and it was out on the eighth of August as it was on the fourteenth of May, and a light costs oil, and the last figure anybody in that harbor has for a barrel of lamp oil is on a board in the front street with February's above it and both dates on the board, and a figure put on that board cannot be taken off it again.

On the crown, about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins and about four feet from a page of six names on it, the two objects of nobody's are four hundred and twenty-three days old, and one of them has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June past and it has not been lashed back, and neither has moved and nobody has asked whose either is. The bench under that pump in the yard behind that market is bare for the hundred and eighty-ninth night.

There were two lights out at about a mile off the point on the evening of Sunday the eighth of August where they were on the evening of the first, and they have not moved in seven days, being counted from the hour they were first seen and not from the hour they were first missed, and nobody in that harbor has hailed them and nobody in that harbor knows whose they are and nobody in that harbor is going to write a day against them. A mile and a half on the other side of the same point, six more boats were lying with a small boat of about twelve tons alongside the outside one of them, and nobody in that harbor has been told what any of that is for.

The second seam is fouled about a foot and seven inches on the weather side and nothing on the other, where it was on the nineteenth of May past, and it was not looked at on the said day and was not asked about and was not named.

---

It had been said on the crown that afternoon, and not by him.

The man of about fifty-one of that harbor had a page on that crown since the eighth of July past with six names on it in his own hand and no figure, and he had put a stone on the corner of it on the thirtieth and had taken the stone off and put it in his own pocket on the same day, and the page has been weighted with nothing and has not blown away, and there are about nine feet between that page and the two tarpaulins.

At about four in the afternoon three men of the third lay came up the crown and stood in front of it, and none of them was asked to and none of them came for it.

The master of about fifty-five, who is one of the five whose papers are in order and whose boat is lying off the point at about a mile and a half, put his hand flat on the page without touching it.

"That is a good page," he said. "I have always said that."

He turned round then and put his own hand flat on the inside of the wheelhouse door of his own boat's head, which is not on that mole, and which is nine feet away and is not a page.

"I have got a board in that boat," he said. "Same thing as that page and the other way up. It is what she wants and what I have not got and what I have got twice of. It has been in that boat eleven years. It is painted and it is nearly all gone and I have never shown it to a living soul and it is the one thing in that boat I would be sorry to lose, and I have never once been asked for it, and I am not going to be." He took his hand off the door. "And I have never once had six men and no boat, and I am not going to stand on this crown and say the two are the same, because they are not, and a man of fifty-one would not thank me for saying they were."

A master of about forty-seven of that lay said the other half of it, to the water.

"You cannot read that page," he said. "There is nothing on it. And I have got a list of what I owe in my own head and I have had it in my own head since I was nineteen, and it has never been on anything, and there is nobody on this coast who can check one hair of it. Both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, and I have had thirty years to want a page of my own and I have not put one up, and I am not going to tell him he should have."

The man of about fifty-one of the six was on the crown the whole of that and heard all of it and said none of it, and he did not explain his page to anybody, and nobody asked him to, and he is not one of the six masters off the point and owns five sixths of a boat on the foul off the south end of that mole and has not had a boat to put it in since the nineteenth of June past.

**AND A PAGE OF SIX NAMES AND A BOAT AND NO FIGURE HAS STOOD ON THE CROWN OF THAT MOLE SINCE THE EIGHTH OF JULY PAST, AND ON THE EIGHTH OF AUGUST THREE MEN OF THAT LAY LOOKED AT IT AND SAID WHAT IT IS, AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE SAID IT ABOUT HIM, AND HE SAID NOTHING, AND THE PAGE IS NOT A LIST OF WHAT A BOAT WANTS AND A LIST OF WHAT A BOAT WANTS IS NOT A PAGE, AND BOTH OF THOSE ARE TRUE AND NEITHER OF THEM CANCELS THE OTHER, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN PUT AGAINST THAT MAN BY ANYBODY AND NO BOAT HAS BEEN GIVEN TO HIM AND NO WAGE HAS BEEN PROMISED TO HIM AND THE PAGE IS NOT GOING INTO ANY RETURN.**

Tobias Wren worked the hook into a seam of one of his eleven pieces at the other end of that crown and said one sentence, to nobody, without stopping.

"The one that was kept was kept by somebody who came on the days he was not needed."

Nobody asked him which one, and nobody said anything to him about it, and he did not say it again, and he is not a teacher and not a door and he is not the man on the far bench, and the holder of that writ did not improve on it and did not repeat it and did not write it down.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no municipal papers of any kind was at the shed door again, and he came in without being asked this time.

"You told me on the twenty-eighth of July not to lie off the point and look at the line," he said. "I did not do it and I want to say that I did not do it, and I am not here about the line."

"Then what are you here about."

"Six masters of the third lay are lying off the point at a mile and a half with a small boat alongside them and she is asking them whether their papers are in order." He put his hat down on the bench stone. "I have no papers. I have not had any for four years and I have not wanted any and I have said that out loud in this shed before. There is a clause on the notice on the wall in the other street and the fifth of it is out and the sixth of it is in, and the sixth of it does not ask anybody a question. I have been in on it twice and I came in on it on the Saturday of the week before with no paper of any kind and the man on the quay wrote nothing down and said nothing to me."

"I know what the sixth clause says," Calder said. "I have been in on it twice as well."

"Then you know it is the only line on that notice that never asked anybody anything," the man of about thirty-eight said. "I am not asking you for anything. I am telling you because you have a leaf in that shed with four things on it and no men, and the man of about thirty-three has no papers on him at this moment and I have been in on a line that did not need any, and one of us is going to be standing off that point tomorrow telling a man with a list about a line he has never read."

**AND THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT ASKED HIM FOR NOTHING AND SENT HIM FOR NOTHING AND PAID HIM FOR NOTHING, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT SAID HE CAME TO TELL AND NOT TO ASK ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF JULY PAST AND HE CAME TO TELL AND NOT TO ASK ON THE EIGHTH OF AUGUST, AND WHAT HE TOLD WAS A LINE OF A NOTICE ON A WALL IN A DIFFERENT STREET IN THAT HARBOR THAT HE HAS READ HIMSELF OUT OF IT TWICE, AND NOBODY HAS GIVEN HIM ANYTHING FOR IT AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the standing inventory entered in full on the fourth is not entered here again and stands; and that a page of six names with a boat and no figure is not a maintenance record and is not a list of what a boat wants and is not a price, and that a list of what a boat wants kept in a man's own head is not a page and is not checkable, and that both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other; and that the sixth clause of the notice of the seventeenth of May past is the only line on that notice that has never asked anybody anything, and that a man of about thirty-eight with no papers has been in on it twice and knows it from the inside of his own boat and not out of a room, and that he was not asked for anything and was not paid for telling; and that a man of sixty-one said one true thing about a boat being kept and it was not written down and it was not asked after, and that paying the cost of a thing is not naming the thing and that saying a true thing about a boat is not teaching anybody.*

*And in the fourth column of the leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past, that night, in the ship's left hand, the hundred and tenth line:*

*What it is not: — that a page of six names is not a list of what a boat wants and a board in a boat is not a page, and that a man who owns five sixths of a boat and has no boat is not a comment on anybody's keeping; and that a page that has been standing since the eighth of July past with a boat as a price and no figure is not a claim and is not a return and is not going into any return and is not a price anybody is going to be charged; and that three men of a lay saying a thing about their own boats on a mole and not about a man who is standing next to them is not a kindness and is not a courtesy and is not a thing anybody did for him.*
