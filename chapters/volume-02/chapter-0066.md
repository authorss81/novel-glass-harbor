# Chapter 66

*The Man Who Is Not Allowed*

The glass had been falling since the fourteenth and on the morning of Friday the seventeenth of February a wind came up the harbor from the south-east that put a chop on the outer water and made the mole's face run, and Elias Voke came down to the quay before the office was open and said that a man does not dive the ninth seam on a day like this one, and Calder Marr said that a man does not dive the ninth seam on any day this winter, because the winter is the only part of the year left.

The reason went into the harbor authority's book at eight o'clock in the morning of the seventeenth, at the reeve's table, and Calder Marr wrote it in his own left hand with the clerk standing there, and it was Elias Voke's reason and not his own, because Elias Voke cannot write a sentence with a clause in it and had said so in the road in about as many words.

Voke had not asked him to. Voke had said, on the quay, standing in the rain, that the account was his and that if the yard was going to write his reason he would put his own name at the end of it and that he would not pretend he had written it, and Calder Marr had said that was the correct arrangement and that the yard's own reason of the nineteenth of January had a hole in it that a boy had entered, and had said both of those things in the road where the wind was taking them.

*That on the seventeenth instant, at the ninth seam of this office's mole, at the top of the flood, upon a ground belonging to this office and covered by the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first, Elias Voke of the herring boat Anna Ray, master, being the party upon whose account the work is to be done and this office paying the diver and the labor out of its own repair account, proposes to shift a stone lying upon a shackle bedded in the foundation of this office's mole and to bring that shackle and the fouling upon it up, and to make up upon the new ring of the fourth set a mooring of this office which has been out of service since the first of December; that a diver is proposed and is named, being Tam Slee of the Grey Widow, hired out of that crew's locker for the day; that a limit of twenty-five minutes is fixed on the slate by the diver at the boat; that the tender named is the foreman of this office's tide gang and the cutoff is his to pull without asking anybody; that no member of the Free Keel yard is proposed to be in the water or upon the face, and that the purpose of the work is stated in full in this paragraph and is the making good of a mooring of this office out of service, and that the fouling upon the ground line between the third and the ninth seams is not in the scope of this day's work and is entered here as a fact of this office's own account and not of this office's work.*

"Read the last sentence back," Kest said.

"Read the whole of the fourth paragraph back," Nessa Pike said, and Bevin Rook read it back, twice, and then a third time, and then he read the sentence about the fouling back a fourth time on his own, and nobody had asked him to.

"That is the first reason this office has had from a party in four months that has not had a hole in it," Kest said, "and I want the room to hear me say that it was written for the office and not for the yard, and that the party could not write it, and that the man who did write it has spent a month learning that a reason is a thing you put your hand on and that when he finally put his hand on one for somebody else the thing he wanted to say out loud in it was the fouling, and he put it in the fourth paragraph where a clerk would copy it out and not skip it."

Then the reeve took the pen and put his own line under it, and it was the fourth one since the twelfth of January and it was the shortest of the four.

*The first clause of a notice this office did not write closes the toe and inner face of this mole from the third seam to the eleventh to all work and to all approach by all persons, which includes this office. This office names ground inside that closure, engages a diver inside it, and enters the fact on this page. This office has no power to lift that notice and is not lifting it. The party has not asked for it to be lifted. The party is not on the face and will not be on the face today, and this office has told the party so in its own voice and the party has not argued with this office, which is the first time in four months that has happened in this room.*

---

They went down to the mole at half past nine in the rain and stopped at the crown, above the marker at the eleventh, because the marker was a cut in the mole's stone with a ring of paint round it, and Calder Marr had stood at that marker with the reeve's man in January and had been told the words of the first clause by the man who had to write them down, and he read them now without being told.

*...to all work and to all approach by all persons, which includes this office, this yard, and any other crew in this harbor.*

"So you stand here," Nessa Pike said, "and not one step further, and that is not a thing anybody is deciding, it is a thing a notice said in October, and Calder, I want you to hear me say it out loud in front of Rook so that it is in his book and not only in mine. You have been the best line hand on this coast since October and there is a clause with your name in it that says you may not walk down a mole, and I am not going to have you stand on this crown being brave about it."

"I am not being brave about it. I am being careful about it, which I would like the record to say as a different word."

"Enter it as a different word," Nessa Pike said, and the boy entered it, and the difference between careful and brave is a line in a book, and a line in a book is the entire difference between the two, and it is not much, and it is all a man has.

Below them, on the face, the tide gang was already on it: Neale at the ring bolt with the fall, Wil Slee on the guide, and Petch and Rowlandike on the face with a hand crane out of the net loft that the harbor authority had hired at two marks a day out of its own repair account, and the harbor's pump was standing on the mole in the rain pumping a seam of dirty water out of a stone cradle that was not doing anything except letting a man in and out of the foundation, and neither the crane nor the pump was the yard's and the yard's book would not carry either of them.

Tam Slee went over at eleven minutes past ten in about four and a half feet with a half of a hand's breadth over the stone, and the stone came up in nine minutes, and then the shackle would not move.

---

It took until half past one.

The first hour of it was a fouled shackle in a mole's own foundation, and the fouling on it was the same hard grey slate-skin with the worms still in it that a bellwright had put a wet thumb over on the eleventh of January, and the water was running down the face in sheets with the wind on it, and Calder Marr could hear his own name being called up the line in another man's voice and could not tell whether the words were going to be about the depth or about the air.

At eighteen minutes the shackle came a quarter of a turn and stopped.

"He says it is a quarter of a turn and it is stopped," the boy called down, "and he says he wants a bigger spanner and the office has not got one, and he says—"

"Read what he said and not what you think he meant," Calder said, and had it out of him in about four seconds, and the boy put his right hand flat on the wall for a moment and then wrote it.

"He said, *Tell your foreman to come down here and look at it and then tell me whether that is iron or stone*, and I am writing that in the second column because I cannot check it from here and a man on a mole cannot check it from here either, and the answer is going to be in the first column when Neale says it in his own voice on the record, and both of them are going in and neither of them is going to be scratched out."

Neale went down. It was stone. It was a stone sleeve cast round the shackle by forty years of the mole's own foundation work and it would have to come away in pieces, and it would take a second go, and the second go would have to be on a falling tide in the same hour because the clerk's slip for the day said the day.

The boy read the clerk's slip out loud before anybody said anything about it, which was not required of anybody.

"Day," he said. "Six in the morning to six in the evening. And it is four in the afternoon."

"Then he has two hours," Kest said, from behind them, and he had come up the mole steps in the rain without a coat and without saying why, "and the top of the flood is at twenty past four, and the tide will be making hard on the face by half past three, and this office is not going to send a man down on a making tide in a swell to save a mooring it bought in January."

"Then the mooring is not worked today," Nessa Pike said.

"The mooring is not worked today unless this office says it is, and this office is going to say it is not, and this office is going to write that down in its own book with the hour on it, and the reason is not the weather and the reason is not the tide, and I am going to write the real reason underneath and the real reason is that I will not send a man down there on a making tide for a shackle."

"You will send him down twice in one day on a slack," Voke said.

"I will send him down twice in one day on a slack if the slack comes, and the slack comes at half past one, and it is half past one now." Kest turned round and looked at Calder Marr, who had been standing at the crown of the mole since half past nine in the rain with his hands in his pockets and had not gone down the face and had not offered to and had not said one word about the water. "Say it to him yourself, Marr, because you are not going to be able to work on Friday or Saturday either and I would like you to hear the reason from the man who took it and not to work it out."

"I have been standing at this marker for four hours," Calder Marr said, "and I have not gone down the face, and you told me in January that the first clause includes this yard, and you were right and I have read it three times since and I am not going to argue it with you on a wet mole in February. And a diver is a man you cannot replace, and I would have been the diver, and the thing that has stopped me from being the diver is a piece of paper about a seam in a mole that I am not allowed to look at, and I do not know whether that is justice or a waste, and I have not got the right to an opinion about it in front of your clerk, and I would like it entered that I did not have one."

"Enter it," Kest said, and the clerk entered it, and the boy read it back, and the line went into the harbor authority's book in Calder Marr's own left hand, in Calder Marr's own words, with the hour, and it is the only line in four months that Calder Marr has entered in a municipal book that says he did not have a right to an opinion.

---

The slack came at half past one and they went twice, and the second time the shackle came up in nine minutes with a sleeve of the mole's own stone about a foot long still round it, and the fouling came off it in one piece and the whole of it went into a basket and up the face and onto the mole steps, and a boy looked at it for about four seconds and then went and stood at the far end of the mole for a while.

Then the ring of the fourth set went on, and the mooring was made up at twenty past three in the rain, and Neale wrote the time on a slate of his own, and the harbor's clerk entered it in the authority's book, and Bevin Rook read the whole of the afternoon back twice, and then did the thing that none of the four men who stood on that mole had thought of, which was to ask the clerk for the book.

---

"I do not fill it in," the clerk said. "I fill in a box and I say what a box does, and this afternoon I have filled in a box four times and said what a box does four times, and what you are asking me for is a man from outside this office writing in this office's book."

"Yes."

"No."

"Then read it to me and I will write it and you will initial it, and the initial is the office's and the hand is mine, and that is a thing this office has done before, on the seventeenth of January, when a party wrote a reason and a clerk stamped nothing because nothing about it is stampable."

The clerk looked at the reeve, and the reeve said nothing at all for about eleven seconds, which everybody who has ever been in that room understood to be a decision.

"Write it in your own right hand," the clerk said, "and I will initial the foot of it, and I will enter the hour, and the office does not adopt it and the office will keep it, and if anybody in Veyr ever asks me what a box is for I shall be able to say I kept one."

Bevin Rook wrote the afternoon of the seventeenth of February on the last leaf of the authority's book in his own right hand, in two columns, and he wrote it while the rain came off the mole steps and the harbor's own tide gang stood about and did not interrupt him.

*The seventeenth of February. The ninth seam. The mooring out of service since the first of December is made up and in service from four o'clock. Ground: the mole's own toe, at and below four and a half feet, in the closure of the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first, which this office has not lifted. A stone of the foundation, about a foot long, was in pieces on the steps, with the fouling upon it in one piece, and is in the authority's store and not in any shed in this harbor, and the fouling is hard grey slate-skin with the worms in it, and I do not know its age and nobody asked me.*

*First column, checkable from the mole: the diver, Tam Slee, hired out of the Grey Widow on Elias Voke's account and at this office's cost; the tender, Neale; the air read at the boat by the diver himself and by nobody else; the diver in the water at ten minutes past ten and again at thirty-two minutes past one, each time of twenty-five minutes; the slack at half past one; the top of the flood; the state of the water, a south-easter and a chop and a half of rain. Second column, not checkable from a mole: the state of the shackle before the stone came off, which was a quarter of a turn and stopped; the quarter of a turn; what was under the stone; whether the sleeve of stone round the shackle is in one piece or two; the diver's own figure for the depth of the ring bolt, which is nine, and is his, and goes in as his.*

*And this is in the same entry and in my own hand: I was not in the water and I was not on the face. The first clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October names me, and names Calder Marr, and names Nessa Pike, and this office has told us so and we have not argued, and I have written this account from the crown of the mole at a hundred yards, and every figure in the first column of it is a figure I have taken off a man's slate or off a man's mouth and not one of them is a figure I saw with my own eyes, and I would like a man in Veyr in four years to be able to read that sentence and know exactly how far away from the water this record was made.*

The clerk read it and initialled the foot of it and entered the hour, and did not amend one word, and Bevin Rook read it back twice afterwards to four men on a wet mole, and Alden Marr was not asked for anything and was not there, and the notice of the thirty-first was on a market wall four hundred yards behind them with a circuit's seal over the nail and was not the harbor authority's to lift, and had not been lifted, and the mooring of the ninth seam was in service from four o'clock in the afternoon of Friday the seventeenth of February by a man out of another crew's locker on a herring skipper's account, and the only thing the Free Keel yard had to do with the whole of it was write it down, which it had never in five months been asked to do and had never thought of asking to do.
