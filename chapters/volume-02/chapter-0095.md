# Chapter 95

*The Accountants*

There is a second shackle on the ring of a block that is made for one, and the block was a mooring block of the ninth seam's own cut out of old weighhouse gear, and it took the shackle and about an inch and a half of good iron on the next link, and the ring of a block is not a ring a man can get his fingers into, and a boy of twenty-two with a bad hand went at it with a knife in a boat until somebody in her told him he was in the wrong place, and he was, and everybody in that mole's shadow was in the wrong place for the first four minutes.

That is what Calder Marr says afterwards, and it is the only thing he says about it, and he says it four times on three different days.

The mole's shadow is a mile and a half long and a boat in a half sea under a mole is a boat in a wall of green with nowhere in it to look, and the handbell had been rung from the north wall, and every man in this harbor who has ever been in a boat in weather has looked for a lost man in the shadow of the wall he knows, and the yard looked there, and the boy looked there, and Dace's man of fifty-one with the bad wheel was in the barge on the other side of the mole and had the wrong idea too and said so.

"Four minutes," he said. "I have been in this harbor forty-nine years and I would have looked in the shadow. There is nothing wrong with any of us. It is simply the wrong four minutes and there is no instrument in this harbor that would have taken them off us."

Then Calder Marr put his hand flat on the mole's own stone and said: "He was not in the shade. He was in the light, and he is nine hundred yards inshore of the third lay and two hundred south, and the *Grey Widow*'s men have a name for that water, and I am going to say it and then I am going to go and get him."

"The Accountants," said the man of fifty-one. "Five fathoms on the ebb, and a man in a working night in there is in the mud to the armpits before anybody knows he has gone. There is no stone for them and there is no list and there are about eleven families and one of them is mine. **Do not tell me there is a bell out there.**"

---

The reeve's barge came off the west basin with the office's own hand at the wheel and Neale in the bow and the harbor authority's clerk on the ledge with his board on his knees, and Calder Marr, Nessa Pike, Bevin Rook and the two Slees went into her with the yard's hand lead, fifteen fathoms of line, the gland, and the pot of air bought on the seventh of April. Kest stood on the mole's steps in a reeve's coat in the last of the light and did not get into the boat, and said one thing to the office's hand at the wheel, which the hand repeated to Neale on the way out because he thought it was the sort of thing a man says once.

"I am committing the only boat this office has into a half sea in the dark with a man in the water off the inner shoals," he said. "I have not got another one. Tell them at the inner face, if anybody is standing there, that this office is in the water and that the reeve knows what it has cost."

They found the shackle on the second sweep in about four fathoms of water on the face of the shoal, and the boy got it up on a boat-hook in three tries with a bad hand and did not let go of it, and then they went inshore and south to the place where the water goes pale because it is shallow and not because it is kind, and the boy put the lead over the side himself, standing on the fender, and it came up five.

Five fathoms, and soft, and the ebb making across the shoal at about a knot and a half, and the light going.

The account was read out on the fender in about twenty seconds with the pot hissing and the sea going over the mole.

"Ground, the inner face of the inner shoals, nine hundred yards inshore of the third lay and two hundred south, in five fathoms on the ebb, and the bottom is soft and the ebb is making across it and the boy sounded it twice and the tide-gang hand sounded it once. Purpose, a boy of twenty-two with a hand that will not close is in that water and a boat was seen empty at half past five and a handbell was rung twice from the north wall at four o'clock and one man has been missing since four. The diver is Calder Marr, of this party. The tender is Neale, of this harbor's tide gang, and he is not the diver, and he has a line and he has not got a boat of his own and the line is not made fast to anything in this harbor. The limit is twenty-two minutes, and I fixed it at the boat and no man in this harbor may put a hand on it. The cutoff is his to pull without asking anybody and I am not going to be able to stop him if he pulls it. I am going down now. I am not entering anything and nobody is entering anything with me. There is no air left in the pot and there is no other air in this harbor and the eighth of that pot is going on a hand lead in my hand and the rest of it is on two men in a boat."

"I have not got to be asked twice and I am not going to be asked twice," Neale said, to nobody, in the bow of a reeve's barge.

---

It took four minutes to find him in five fathoms of water with half a knot and a half of ebb across a shoal at the end of a gale, and the yard's whole life in the year to come is a consequence of four of them.

He was in a pocket of the bottom, half on his side, with a stone under his cheek and the shackle in a left hand that would not open, and when Calder Marr got the line to him and put his hand on the cuff of the coat the man said — a word, a piece of a word, a man saying the first syllable of somebody's name, three times, and on the third time it came out of the port quarter of the barge, about four hundred yards away, about three fathoms above the water, flat, unhurried, and not out loud.

It was the *Grey Widow*'s own hand on the fender calling a man who was not in her.

It was the only thing in that water for eleven minutes that Calder Marr could not put in the first column, and it is in the second column of the yard's own book with the hour and the words, and the reason given against it is that no man on a mole in this harbor could check where a man's voice goes in a half sea, and there is nothing in this yard's book that explains it and the yard does not intend to invent one.

Then the fourth minute of the search, and the last minute of it, was this: a small cold thing in the leg of Calder Marr's right arm, the same shape as the burn that comes back hours after a rope has been off a man's hand, and a man who has been in eleven places knows what that is and can act on it without being told, and it is in the second column with a reason against it, and the reason is that no man could have said it out loud in time and that the man who had it was the man who could act on it and not the man who could speak.

At ten minutes Calder Marr had the man's collar and the man had the shackle still in his hand, and at eleven minutes Neale pulled the line.

The tender's stop is not a request. Calder Marr came up through four fathoms of water and up the boat's side and up onto the gunwale of a reeve's barge with a man of sixty-one holding a line and not looking down, and he was furious, and he put both hands flat on the stern seat and said, in a voice that carried about two feet, that he had four minutes.

"You had four minutes," said Neale, "and I am a man of fifty-one and I have read a notice in my life and I have read yours, and you put the limit on the slate at twenty-two and you were at eleven, and the third man in that water has been in a half sea since four o'clock and there is a boy of twenty-two at the bottom of it and the only other air in this harbor is in a pot that has been standing three weeks."

"Then I would have had him at thirteen and I would have had him up at seventeen with air in me and the *Grey Widow*'s hand in there after me, and the two of them would be off that shoal and I would be in the mud with a cold arm and no pot, and I would have got him and I would have got nobody, and that is a good answer and it is the wrong one."

"It is the only answer there is tonight," Neale said, and he did not apologize, and he did not look away, and he put the line down and coiled it, and nobody in that boat said one word about it.

---

It was twenty past seven when the air ran out in the boy's hand and two Slees came to the buoy in a dinghy they had taken off the third lay's wharf without asking, because there was a boy in the water at the Accountants and a boat was the fastest thing in Morrow Quay, and they were in it before anybody had said a word to anybody, and they will not be asked about it and should not be.

All three were up. The little one was cold to the bone and conscious and had the shackle in his hand and would not let go of it, and the cloth that came off the ninth seam's mooring pole went round him on the mole steps at about half past eight, and it was somebody's good canvas and the yard did not know whose until the following day.

Two things went that the yard will not be getting back. The pot of air was done. The compressor's gland was not: a man in a boat in a half sea put a shackle on a coil of line to save a man in a hurry, and it went with about ten feet of the yard's own delivery hose and the gland with it, and there is no hose in this harbor to hire and none to buy. The hand lead's line went at the same moment, at the same hand, and that is a thing the yard can make and cannot make before Thursday.

The third lay's boat was still against the second lay's wharf at half past eight with her chain round a pole that is not a bollard, and the harbor authority's fire party went out in her at about a quarter to ten, in the dark, in an easing wind, with a chain and a pole and four men, and the tide had an hour and a half to run.

---

It was the boy who found the name, at about nine, on the top of the mole steps with a slate on his knees. He had two names and a list, and he read the list out first, because he had been told to read a list before an opinion and it was in the book that way.

The two: Neave Tarrant, of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found. And Ivo Trethewey, of the second lay, thirty-one, at the top of the make on the twenty-ninth of March, not found.

**It was not Dace's man.** It was not a man off the *Grey Widow* and not a man anybody in that boat had ever seen, and Dace's man of fifty-one with the bad wheel got down two steps and stood with his back to the water and put his hand over his face for about four seconds, and a man of forty-eight who has lost two of other men's on a coast with a proper chart and a light said the only sentence he said that night: "That is not my boat."

"I know it is not," said the man of fifty-one, "and I have not said a name and I am not going to. I want to go down to my house and I want somebody to walk with me and not to talk."

Nessa Pike walked with him, and the yard's book says that she did, and does not say why, because she said nothing about it and nobody asked.

The man was Ivo Trethewey, thirty-one, of the second lay, top of the make on the twenty-ninth of March. He had a shackle of the ninth seam's own mooring in his hand because he had gone down to that mooring on Friday afternoon for six marks, off his own account, with no instrument and no permit, to re-rig a chain that had been found round a pole that is not a bollard. The ninth seam is ground the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first closes, and he was not lawful there, and a boat found him in a green sea on a falling glass with the shackle still in his hand. There is a notch cut in a cast face on the ninth seam's own stone where his shackle had been, and the mooring is out of service from the first of May, and the notice is not lifted, and the yard has not asked for it to be lifted and will not, and none of those three things has anything to do with the fact that he is alive.

He lived. The clerk wrote that he lived, and that is the whole of the official shape of it, and it took two men of the tide gang and a boy's bad hand and eleven minutes and a compressor's gland and a hand lead's line and about three hours of an easing north-northeast, and it took the yard something it cannot put a figure on, which was a claim window of one date, two days' work, an air pot three weeks standing, and about ten feet of hose.

---

The read-back on the same night, Friday the twenty-eighth of April, at about nine o'clock, at the top of this mole, with the light gone and the wind easing and nineteen men of the third lay asleep on the boards at the top of the second lay's wharf:

*First column, checkable by any man who can get to a mole in this harbor: the tide, half past ten past six; the call, two blows of a handbell on the north wall at four o'clock; the place, nine hundred yards inshore of the third lay and two hundred south, the inner face of the inner shoals, five fathoms on the ebb, soft bottom, ebb making across it at about a knot and a half; the man, in the water at twenty-one minutes past six, recovered at eleven minutes, alive, cold to the bone, with the shackle in his hand, and he is in this harbor's water and in no boat in it; the diver, Calder Marr, of this party; the tender, Neale, of this harbor's tide gang, who is not the diver; the limit, twenty-two minutes, fixed by the diver at the boat; the cutoff, the tender's, pulled at eleven minutes, and that a tender's decision is not a request; the air, out of a pot bought on the seventh of April and standing three weeks in the party's shed, the whole of it used, and no other air in this harbor; the loss, a compressor gland, about ten feet of the yard's own delivery hose, and the yard's hand lead's line; the man found, Ivo Trethewey, of the second lay, thirty-one, and that he came out of the mole's shadow at about four o'clock on the twenty-eighth of April.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that a man heard a call out of the wrong part of a boat, and the call was a copy of a call he could hear, and the delay on it is not given; that a man in five fathoms of water could not speak at the moment he needed to and acted instead; what the four minutes of looking in the wrong place were, being the shade of the wall a man knows; and that a hand held a shackle in a half sea and would not open, and that the hand is a known thing in this yard and has a book of its own and no man has asked about it and none is going to.*

And under the two columns, in Calder Marr's own hand, three lines, because there is no column on this earth for a thing that is both a fault and a life, and this yard keeps them in a line and not in a box:

*That the ninth seam's mooring is ground the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first closes, that the notice has not been lifted, that this yard has not asked for it to be lifted and will not, and that a man without an instrument and without a permit worked on that mooring on Friday the twenty-eighth of April on his own account and for six marks, and was not lawful there, and is alive.*

*That the notice of the thirty-first did not save him, and that the yard will not say in any book that it did, and will not say in any book that the notice is not worth the paper it is on, and that both of those are in one page and neither is a reason to touch the closure.*

*That the yard put a compressor gland and a pot of air and a hand lead's line into five fathoms of water on Friday the twenty-eighth to get a man out of ground this office's own notice closes, and that this yard does not intend to write that sentence twice, and that a man in a city in four years who wants to know what a notice is for will have to ask a man who was in the water, and there is one, and he is not going to be asked.*

Three men read that back — the boy, the yard, and the shipwright — and the fourth was Harl Dace, who came up the mole at about a quarter to twelve on the Friday, read it standing at the top of the steps, said the two words that Edric Pringle and Sella Brigg have both said and that nobody has ever been paid for saying, and then said the thing about the claim window, in his own mouth, and had it entered.

"The claim window of the twenty-eighth is spent," Dace said. "One date, and the date is the twenty-eighth, and it was worked and it is logged in the harbor authority's own book in the clerk's own hand, and there is not one pound of iron off the bedded material in it and there is going to be none, because the sea was on the ground and the men were needed elsewhere. In five weeks a man in a city is going to read that entry and call it a window that was missed, because his box cannot hold the difference and neither can mine. **You went into the water in front of witnesses and came back with a man's life and nothing else, and a thing like that has no paper at the end of it in this harbor, and I have been here thirty-two years and I have never seen one.**

"And the second half, and I am not a clerk and I am not a reeve and nobody has asked me to. There is a piece of glass the size of a folded chart with a false seal on it, in my own boat, in my own keeping since the autumn, and on the seventh of April a claim window put a date and a place on it and a woman of no office and no fee told me that a boat cannot be asked anything and that my seal is false in the one clause that would let anybody use it. I have had that answer since the tenth of April and I have not been able to do one thing with it.

"**This morning a man with a bad hand went into five fathoms to get a man off a bar of gravel, and the reason he is alive is that somebody who has never met him, on the strength of a piece of paper with a date and a name on it, put a boat and a bellwright and two Slees and a boy with a bad hand into the water.** The paper is what a man reads in a city. In this harbor it is what a man with a bad hand put his name against at seven in the morning, and that is the whole of what a piece of paper has ever been for, and I am forty-eight and I have not said that out loud before and I am not going to say it twice."

He read it back in the book himself and would not let the boy read it for him, and it went in in his own words and in his own right hand, and it is the first thing anybody in this harbor has said to the yard about a writ that the yard is going to use.

And the position in the yard's book at the end of Friday the twenty-eighth of April is **thirteen marks in a bag, thirty-three on the note, nothing in a tin that had two crowns in it on the first of February, no instrument, no claim, and a claim window of the twenty-eighth that was worked and logged and raised nothing**, and the third lay's boat was off the second lay's wharf before midnight with her chain on a bollard and a fire party asleep on her boards, and the north wall took the sea all night with fifty fathoms of winter warp lying on it where ninety were to lie.
