# Chapter 100

*The Tenth Thing on the Wall*

The market on Wednesday the third of May was about a third of a Saturday, and a man who wants a market wall to be read by people has to be on the boards at seven in the morning, because after nine the boards are a market and a wall with a thing on it is a thing you stop in front of on your way past.

Calder Marr was on the boards at seven with a nail in his hand and a sheet under his arm, and the tenth thing went up at ten past seven, and it went up under the naval paper that has been on that wall since the eighteenth of March, which is the only way anybody in this harbor can put a sheet up, because the wall belongs to nobody and the paper has been on it for six weeks and no man has ever been asked to move it and no man ever will be.

It is a longer sheet than the ninth thing. It has eleven lines at the head, and then the ninth thing entire — both columns, four read-backs, a conservator's finding with her name at her asking and *unsigned* under it, and two men who have spoken about it, one of whom said it was a tale and has not been proved wrong either — and then three more things under that, and then the head of the whole thing again at the bottom, and then a list of six hands and a mouth.

The four men read it back at about ten to eight with the market coming in behind them and about forty people in the street, and the fifth read it back at half past eight, and nobody in that market had to be asked.

---

The fifth was Edric Pringle, of the *Anna Beth*, fifty-four, of the second lay, and she came down the boards in her oilskins and read the whole of it standing back from the wall with her thumb going down it, and it took her about eight minutes, and there is no third time a page like that has been read in this market and there will not be a fourth.

She stopped at the fifth of the eleven lines at the head and stood there.

"That is the wrong way round," she said.

"It is."

"**Four hundred feet of my own warp came off that wharf and it was my own account, and I came up this hill and said the cause and I put six marks in and you have put the cause on a wall, and you have got it the right way up for the first time in my life, and I have been in this harbor fifty-one years and I have never had a cause of mine put right way up in a book.** I am not going to say thank you for it, because you are on a market wall at seven in the morning and there are forty people here and I am fifty-four. But I am going to read the rest of it out and I am going to read it out and I am going to read the part about the man off the Accounts twice."

And she read the rest of it out, to the boards, in the voice of a woman who has spent her life reading out a boat's figures to a man who could not read them, and when she came to the second column of the twenty-eighth of April she read the two sentences about the four minutes in the shade of a wall a man knows, and then the sentence about a hand that would not open, twice, as she said she would, and then she said the thing that made about nine people in that market look at the ground.

"And that is the first time in my life that anybody has written down that a man held on to a thing he could not put down, and I have been married to a man with a bad thumb for thirty years, and the fire party took my warp and half this market knows it, and there is a line on this wall about a hand and it is in a column with a reason against it, and the reason is that nobody is going to be asked about it." She gave the wall the two words back. "That is a thing to put on a wall and I have not got anything to say against one line of it."

---

The sixth thing at the head is a question, and it was on the wall at ten past seven on Wednesday the third of May, and it is the only line in eleven lines that is a question, and it is in the yard's own hand and it does not explain anything and it is not going to.

*That a bar of gravel comes up to two and three-quarters of a fathom in the middle of a spring tide at twenty past two on the flood, five miles north of this harbor's headland, inside the water and inshore of the shoals, and that it is on this harbor's own chart as a blank, and that the blank is not a wrong figure and cannot be argued with, and that there is nothing inshore of the headland for five miles north on any chart made by any office this coast has, because there is nothing there to moor on and nobody to moor, and that a man of forty-eight has worked that water for thirty-one years and has a slate and is not a writer and is a witness and not a party and is not paid and has not been asked to be right.*

*And that the party does not know who takes a road off a chart, and does not intend to find out in a hurry, and is not going to say the word for what a road with no chart on it is, because the party has been wrong in public twice this month in two columns with a reason against every line of the second, and a party that has been wrong twice has no business putting a name on a thing.*

Noll Tarner read that one twice at about ten past nine with his hands behind his back, the way he read everything on that wall, and then he said the word.

"That is a network," he said, "and you have got three boats on it and a cairn and a creek and a bar of gravel and a man in a panel, and and a man in a market at the beginning of April put a seal with a boat's name on a sheet of glass, and I have been in this harbor thirty-one years and I have not got the picture and I am going to say the word out loud because nobody else in this market is going to, and if that is a network then somebody has taken it off a chart on purpose, and if that is not a network then there is a man of forty-eight who is going to be a fool in front of forty people in ten years' time."

"No," Calder Marr said.

"You do not know that it is not."

"I know that I have been wrong in public twice in this market this month and both times the wrong thing was written down in my own hand with a reason against it and the reason was the only part of it that was any use," Calder Marr said. "And I am not going to put a name on a road of gravel and a cairn of ballast stones that a man of fifty-eight has called a tale in a market and has not been proved wrong either, and I am not going to say the word you have just said, because the day that word is on a wall in my hand is the day every man in this market has a thing to be frightened of, and there is nothing on that wall now to be frightened of except me.

"You have a better answer than that and you gave it in April and I did not take it. Say the other one."

Tarner stood in front of a market wall for about half a minute.

"Then the question is who edits the charts," he said, "and it is not a question about a network, it is a question about a man with a pen, and the answer is that there are about forty offices on this coast that draw water and every one of them draws what it has sounded and not one of them has ever sounded five miles of a shore that nobody wanted to moor on, and the day a man did want to moor on it, somebody is going to find out what his office has got, and I do not care whose name is on the sea."

"That goes in the second column," the boy said, "and the reason is that a man in a market cannot be checked."

"It goes on the wall in his own mouth in the first column," Calder Marr said, "because a man in a market can be asked again tomorrow, and if he says it twice it is a thing he means."

---

A man of the second lay came down the boards at about eleven and asked the boy, in front of about nine people, for the seventh copy, and the boy said there was no money in this yard to print a sheet of paper and there never had been since the eighteenth of March, and the man asked what a copy of the tenth thing was going to cost him.

"Nothing," the boy said. "There is no charge. I will write it out this evening and you can have it in an hour, and the copy will have your name on it, because that is a thing that is not on the wall and cannot be on a wall, and it is the only thing on this sheet that binds anybody and it is a piece of paper and no more."

The yard's own copy is on the market wall and the authority's copy is in the clerk's book, and the six copies went out on Tuesday in six hands, and one of the six is on a table in the south lay this morning with a line in it that the wall has not got.

And the count of the copies of the public sheet of the fourteenth of October is eleven, and a slip of paper went up under the tenth thing with the eleven names on it and four of them named as not reached and not asked, and it says in its own line that nothing on that wall changes the count and that no holder of any of them has been asked by this yard for any of them and will not be. A shilling changed hands at the foot of the dock at about a quarter to twelve on the Wednesday the third of May, for that slip, and the man who paid it is a coaster's master and not Amos Pryde, and he was not asked to pay it and he would not have been given it, and the shilling is not the yard's income and is not in the bag and will not be.

---

The boy read the position out at half past four in the afternoon, at the top of the dock, with the market gone and about eleven people in the street and the wind in the south and flat water and the glass neither falling nor rising.

"Seven marks in a bag. Twenty-three on the chandler's note, and the three counts of the weeks are twenty-two in the creditor's book, thirteen in Calder Marr's head, and eleven as the last figure ever written in that page, and none of them has moved since the fourth of January and none of them is going to be moved tonight by anything I do with a pencil, and the note was written up on the fourth of January at the yard's asking and it has not been re-written since, and nobody in this yard is going to ask for it to be re-written this year.

"Four lines and sixteen marks in the wage column, and the oldest line in it is the fifth of March, and I left it there on purpose and I have said why and it is in my own hand. Forty marks of claim standing whole, not reduced, not asked to be reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she is not of the writ and the writ cannot reach her, and on the twenty-second of March she said out loud that if a mark of it is offered she will walk it down the dock and put it on the wet stones, and there has not been an offer and there is not going to be one out of that money. Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg in her own name, undiminished, unpaid since the sixth of February, and about four hundred feet of her warp at the bottom of this harbor. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, PAID on Thursday the twenty-ninth of April out of four hands in a market and not one mark of it out of this yard's work, and there has not been a going out since the twenty-seventh of February before the second of May and there is one booked for the second.

"Twenty marks of iron on the yard's own ground, unsold, at a fair sale to a clerk's thirteen, with a saw kerf in one crown shackle that nobody has accounted for, and on Monday the second of May a man of the south lay offered nineteen marks for the stack and a shilling for the shackle, separately, and the yard has neither taken the nineteen nor taken the shilling, and the reason is entered and the reason is that a yard that has declared a thing about its own gear in a book cannot then sell that gear without a buyer reading the declaration first, and that is what the declaration is for and it was not put there to make the iron worth more.

"No compressor gland and no delivery hose in this harbor, and a hand lead's line made on Monday night in a net loft, badly, once. The ninth seam's mooring out of service from the first of May with no end of it entered, and Petch stood off the wall to mind the cart in front of the last lay's wharf until the tenth, and the tide gang is four men short. The mole-head light not on and not to be, and the repair account empty since the middle of February. The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle, and the harbor's carpenters are free and have not been to her since the twenty-eighth of November. The hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters of dues disputed and unpaid. A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found. A man of the second lay found on Friday the twenty-eighth at the top of the tide, alive, in eleven feet of water off the inner shoals.

"A writ on the bench that runs to the first of March next with a reporting clause on it and four names on it, and the clause is a longer leash than anything this yard has ever held, and the yard took it because a yard with no paper cannot work. And a notice of the thirty-first of October that is not lifted and that this yard has not asked to have lifted and will not, whose second clause has not been argued by anybody in this yard in eight months, and whose third clause names three people in this yard who have not been on the authority's work since the day it was served.

"And a bell on this town's own ground in eight and a half feet off the south end, with a fouled waist, a frame cut through the casting on one side, two parts of a chain in a lead plug, a new rope on it bought on the eleventh of January, and a man of fifty who says that a bell only speaks while somebody keeps the weather working on it, and that the price of a person in a boat in the weather is eight marks and a day of that boat, and that it will be the same in a year and the year after that."

"Read the delay back," said the shipwright.

The boy put his hand flat on the wall.

"The harbor's three strokes at about two o'clock on Tuesday the second of May, and the answer out of the water off the south end, a hand's width of a second, and it is the same figure as the sixth of December and the eleventh and nineteenth of January and the second of May, and nobody has converted a hand's width on a lead line into a fraction of a second and nobody is going to, because a hand's width on a lead line is not a fraction of a second and I am not going to invent a conversion for a book.

"**No source. Not written as a voice.** That is the form of that line and it has been the form of that line since the twenty-sixth of December and it is the only honest form of it and there is nothing else to put. And I am not entering an occasion, and the count of occasions since the twenty-sixth of October stands at eleven where it stood on the thirty-first of March, and there is a line on this wall that says that, and I have said it out loud in a market and a man has heard me say it and I do not care."

He read it back twice, the way the fifth of April and the seventeenth of April and the twenty-eighth of April were read back, and then he took his hand off the wall, and about nine people in the street at the top of the dock, and one of them was a man with a slate of his own that was not on a yard's wall, and one of them was a man of fifty-eight out of the south lay who had walked down to look at a line he had written in pencil, and one of them was a man of fifty who had come down the hill to hear whether a chain in a lead plug was on a market wall in a town of four hundred people, and it was not on the wall, and nobody in the street knew it was in a chest in a shed with a lock on it that had a boy's writing on the lid.

---

"There is one more thing and it is the only one I have ever put on that wall that I have not read back to anybody," Calder Marr said, at about five o'clock, "and it is a question and it is about a place, and it is not on the sheet, and I am going to say it once in this street and no man is to write it down.

"There is a place west-southwest of the headland of this harbor at seventy fathoms under this water, and it is a drowned city, and a man of fifty-eight used to tell tales about it in this market when he was thirty and never one of them was any good. I have been eleven years in this harbor trying to find out what happened to my brother and I have got as far as a cairn of ballast stones and a stem in the sand and a bar of gravel and a man in a panel who looked out of a window at a shop in Ashtide, and every one of those things is about five miles north of this mole and about eleven feet deep and there is not a foot of it in that direction, and the seventy fathoms is west-southwest, and **I cannot put the two in the same sentence, and there is a woman in this town who was asked in April to have the two put in the same book and she said no, and she was right, and I am not going to say her name and I am not going to say it in a market.**

"So that is the whole of what a man of this yard has on the third of May, and there is a man in a city who is going to read a writ with a reporting clause on it and a coast road that no chart carries under a naval claim, and he is not going to get an answer out of any of it, and he is going to ask who edits the charts, and I do not know, and I have said so on a wall, and it is a question about a man with a pen and there is no road in it and no city in it and no nine of anything in it.

"**And a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years, and the rope on it is a year old in January, and it will be eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the whole of the craft is the going out, and nobody in this harbor wants it, and I will go, and I am telling the four of you now so that in four years there is no argument about it.**"

The market had gone in. The boards were wet, the tide was making, the tenth thing was on the wall under a naval paper that had been there six weeks, the six copies were in six boats, the writ was on a bench in a shed, and the bell was in eight and a half feet of water off the south end of a mole in a harbor with one berth out of service and a mooring out of service and nineteen winters of dues that had not been paid since anybody could remember, and the boy put the pencil on top of the book and the glass stood still.
