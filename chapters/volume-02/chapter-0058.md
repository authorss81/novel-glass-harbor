# Chapter 58

*What the Chart Costs*

The reeve's barge was on the west basin at a quarter to nine on Tuesday the seventeenth of January with a stove going in the after part and the chart of the inner ground in a tin under a tarpaulin in a locked box that the harbor's own clerk opens with a key the harbor's clerk owns, and Calder Marr had three things to ask for and had decided in the road which of the three he was going to say first, and it was not the one he had come for.

"You have had the cost of the twelfth for two days," he said, "and you have done nothing about it in public, and I am going to let you have the first five minutes to say why, and then I am going to ask you for three things and I have written them down in the order I am going to say them, which is a thing I learned from a hearing room and not from a man."

"I have not done nothing," Kest said. "I have done two things and neither of them can be seen from a quay. Come aboard. Sit down. Your hands are cold and there is no reason for them to be."

"I will stand."

"Then stand, and take the rail, because the rail is warm, and I am not going to have a man telling me he is cold on my own barge as though I were a landlord."

---

He had paid the harbor's part of the twelfth, and he said so in his own voice, and he read it out of his own book because he wanted it in the room in a form a man could check.

"Entered, this office, the thirteenth of January. That on the twelfth instant the work at the third seam of the mole's toe was private work on the ground of a herring skipper and not this office's work, and that the crew which stood on the wall and did not go in consisted of three men named in a notice of the thirty-first of October, and that this office's own tide gang stood on the wall in two places and did no work, and that the ground tackle of the ninth seam belongs to this office and the fouling on it belongs to this office and the stone in the foundation above it belongs to this office, and that the fouling was not shifted on the twelfth and the mooring is not worked and this office is out one mooring which it owns.

"And I have paid for two things out of this office's own repair account, and I have entered that I paid them, and I have entered the hour. Neale's tide gang, one day on the wall on the twelfth, at this office's cost, three marks. A hand crane out of the net loft for two days, at this office's cost, four marks, and not the yard's. That is seven marks this office has paid for a day of work it did not get done, and I would like a clerk to write down that a harbor authority has paid seven marks to stand at the wall and look at three men it cannot use, because in three years that is the fact about this harbor that a man in Veyr will find hardest to believe and it will be true."

The harbor's clerk wrote it down and read it back.

"That is the harbor's part," Kest said. "It is seven marks and it is the whole of what I have, and the part I cannot pay is the boy's wrist, and I am not going to tell you I am going to pay it, because I cannot, and because the boy is owed forty marks by your yard and is owed none of them, and if this office paid that boy a mark today it would be the fourth office in four months to put itself between him and a figure, and the fourth one is the one that will not let him stand on his own."

"That is the right answer and I did not expect it and I want to say so in your own hearing room and not in this boat."

"You can say it wherever you like, and I will not repeat it."

---

"Then the price of the other thing, before I ask for it, because you told me on the twenty-eighth of November that you would tell me prices before you took them and I have held you to that once and I intend to hold you to it until one of us dies."

Kest took his hands off the chart box.

"You are going to ask me for the nineteenth of January," he said. "And you are going to ask me for a ground named in the paper, and I have already written the ground down and I have not sent for you, and I am going to say the price before I grant it, and the price is not the nineteenth of January.

"A fourth set of moorings. Not the first three, which came back into service on the first of December with the narrowing. The fourth. The ninth seam's. And the herring fleet's winter warp, bought out of this office's own account, laid along the north wall, this season, and cut and laid by Neale's gang in the fortnight before the first of March, and the gang has three men stood by on a Friday and a Monday and nowhere to put the days.

"And I will tell you what that costs and who it costs, because I told a room on the twenty-eighth of November that the thing the yard wanted and the thing the town paid for are not the same thing, and I am saying it a second time about a smaller paper and it is worse the second time and not better.

"It costs this office money that was going somewhere else. It does not touch the nineteen winters of dues. Those stay disputed and unpaid and the money that has just gone on four sets of mooring gear and a season of warp is money that was not going to be spent on them, and Mr Voke can have his statement of the authority's capacity and I will have his statement of what the authority has spent, and a man in a coast office is going to read both and is going to work out that in the winter of this harbor's worst year the harbor authority bought forty boats their moorings and did not buy a herring skipper nineteen winters of peace, and he will be right, and I will have said it first.

"It costs Neale three men in the last week of February, which is the week those men would be on their own gear, and it costs them at the end of a winter with nothing in it. It costs Voke his claim in practice and not on paper, which is the worst kind, because he cannot show anybody. And it costs forty boats a berth they were not going to get, one north berth short, on the first of March, in a northeast blow." He put the flat of his hand on the box. "There. That is the price and I have not granted it and you may take it back and I will not hold it against you."

"You have told me the price of a thing I have not asked for," Calder Marr said, "which is a thing you did in November and which I have not stopped noticing."

"Who does the work," Calder said. "That is the fourth question I did not ask and it is the only one that matters."

"That is a thing I have not put in the book and you may put it in yours or not. Neale's gang cannot do it either, for the reason I gave in a room on the twenty-eighth: a crew that wanted that mooring worked would want it worked by men in the water on this office's work, and the fouling is on this office's own face and the notice closes the face. So the work goes to a man out of another crew's locker, at this office's cost, hired the same way Tam Slee was hired on the twelfth, on a herring skipper's account, with a clerk's slip for the day, because that is the only shape of it that is lawful in this harbor this winter.

"And I will tell you the rest of it, because you would have found it out in a month. I have not engaged the diver. If I do, the man who goes down is very likely the man who went down on the twelfth, because there are four divers on this coast who will take this office's money and one of them is free in the third week of February. And the stone over that shackle was shifted four inches on the twelfth and not freed, and in a hard month it will have to be shifted properly by a hired man, with a boy's wrist standing against the last attempt at it.

"I am not asking you to talk me out of it. I have said the price and I am going to take it, and the price is that the harbor authority of Morrow Quay buys four moorings and a season of warp with a boy's wrist in the account, and every one of those four nouns is true and none of them cancels another."

"I have, because a reeve who tells the price of a thing before a man asks is a reeve who is managing him, and a reeve who tells it after is a reeve who took it out of his pocket in front of a witness. I have been doing the second one since October and I intend to go on doing it."

"Grant it," Calder said. "The nineteenth, and name the ground."

---

He wrote it himself, in his own hand, on the authority's own paper, and read the naming back, and the naming was four things and the third of them was the one the boy had found the purpose of the twenty-first lacked.

*The nineteenth of January, a Thursday. The top of the flood. The mole's toe, the third and fourth seams, and the bedded seam in this office's own foundation running north under the rubble, of which the position used by the party since the twenty-eighth of October is the south end. The water inside the harbor line. A hand lead, a slate, and the core hung in eight and a half feet off the south end. And no diver, unless the reason entered in this office's book at eight in the morning on that day names one.*

"That is the ground named in the paper," Kest said, "and the naming is the part you asked for and the reason you asked for it is in a shed in Morrow Quay and I have read a copy of it, and I am not going to discuss it, and I want you to understand that I read it and did not ask, and that is twice in five weeks. And there is a thing in that naming that is not in the paper in those words, and I will say it out loud instead. The first clause of the notice of the thirty-first closes the mole's toe and inner face from the third seam to the eleventh to all work and all approach by all persons, including this office. I have named ground my own notice closes. I did the same on the twelfth and entered it in the same book, and I have done it twice, and I am telling you now so that you can tell the yard and the yard can tell its boy."

"You want the yard holding it as well as you."

"I want the yard holding it as well as me," Kest said. "I have been the only man in this harbor holding things since the thirty-first of October and it has made me a man who decides things alone and writes them down afterward."

"And the price is entered in the hearing record and in your own book."

"It is entered in the hearing record of this office and in my own book, and the hearing record has no sitting attached to it, and the clerk will read it out at the next sitting anybody calls, and the next sitting anybody calls is the first of March if nothing goes wrong, and on the first of March a man will stand in a room and hear that a harbor authority spent its own money on four sets of moorings and a season of warp in the fortnight before he promised it in a room, and he will hear it on the day he is asking me a question about capacity, and that is where I have put it, deliberately, because I am not going to be a man whose generosity is discovered."

He put a line in at the bottom of the entry and turned the book so Calder Marr could read it upside down.

*And it is entered here that the day of the nineteenth of January is paid as a day, at the rate of eighteen marks for a crew of three and a boat, out of this office's own account and not out of the holder's, and that the harbor's eighth and the harbor's share come off anything raised and that nothing is to be raised, and that the authority's bills in this quarter go out on the first of the month, and that the said sum will be in the holder's hands on the first of February and not before.*

"Eighteen," Calder said.

"Eighteen, and the eighth and the share off the top of it if you raise anything, and you are not going to raise anything on that ground and I have written *nothing is to be raised* in my own book so that nobody can come back to me in a year and say the authority took a share of a raising it had told a crew not to make. That gives you two and two off the top and fourteen in your hand on the first of February."

"On the first of February," Calder said, "which is a Wednesday, which is the day a boy of twenty-two has been entered in a book as a hand at four a week, and the fourteen is not four weeks of him, and I would like the reeve to know that I have not told the boy the fourteen is coming and that I am not going to, because a boy who is told his wage is coming on the first of February is a boy who has been given a date, and I have spent four months taking dates out of this yard and I am not putting one back in for money."

"Then he will find out on the first of February," Kest said, "like a man, which is worse and better and is the only way I know of that has ever worked."

---

The yard lodged a plan that afternoon, in the correct form, on its own paper, in Calder Marr's own left hand, with no harbor authority in it at all, and it went up to the circuit office with a fisher's hand on the delivery, because a plan of this kind has to be delivered by somebody nobody in the building has met, and Elias Voke was the somebody.

It was for one supervised look at the fifth seam, at eleven feet, in a bell, with a counter above and a man from the harbor's own staff in it, on a date to be named by the office, on the authority's own bond, and it was eight clauses long and every clause of it was an answer to one of the four grounds on which the same plan had been refused in November, and it was correct in all eight.

Calder Marr did not get an answer in that week.

"That is the plan lodged on the seventeenth and there will be no answer to it in January," he said, to the yard, on the Wednesday morning, "and I am going to tell you the rest of it now so that nobody in this shed builds a week on a hope. It will be refused. It will be refused again and it will be refused correctly, and the second refusal will be better written than the first, because this one is eight clauses instead of six and it has a fisher's hand on the delivery and it has a thing in it about a bond that this office can draw and a bond that another office cannot.

"And the reason it will be refused is not a matter of a better argument. I have got the better argument. I have had it since the sixth of December and I have written it down and it is in a book in this shed. The reason it will be refused is that the office that would have to countersign it is the office that wrote the notice, and the office that wrote the notice is not going to be the office that says the notice is wrong, and no letter I write in a shed in February is going to change the mind of a man who is not going to read it as a matter of course.

"The mechanism for that plate is a change of politics in an office. It is not an argument. I have been trying to make it an argument for four months and it is not one, and I would rather say that out loud in this shed on a Wednesday morning than have four of you go on thinking that a good sentence on a sheet of paper is going to get a man into eleven feet of water under this mole."

"Then what is in this shed in February," Nessa Pike said, "besides a plan that will be refused and a note that will be paid and a pump that will not be fitted."

"A bell that has to be looked at in March," Calder Marr said, "and a day on the nineteenth, and a wall, and a boy with a wage that starts on the first of February, and a net loft where a man is asking a question I cannot answer. And I have asked for the specific thing now and I have been given the smaller thing and I have not argued, and I would like it noted that that is the first time in my life that has happened and I am not certain it was the right way to do it and I am going to find out in about a year."

They read the plan out in the shed on the Wednesday, all eight clauses, twice, and it went into the book with a date, and Elias Voke's name went on the delivery because he put it there and because a man who is owed nineteen winters is allowed to carry another man's paper up a hill.

And Calder Marr asked about the people afterwards, on the quay, in the cold, by name, because a man who takes a price out of a harbor's money and does not know a single name in it is a man who has taken it out of nobody.

"Neale," he said. "The three men. And Voke. And the boy whose arm went in, and his mother's name is not in any of your books and I am not going to ask you for it, and you are not going to tell me, and I have asked you for the three names you have got and I would like them now."

"Neale has three of them," Kest said. "A man called Rowlandike, a man called Petch, and a third who is the brother of Tam Slee, the diver who went down out of the *Grey Widow* on the twelfth and came up with a whole arm and nothing else anybody has found since. Two brothers on this coast, one on this office's gang and one diving for Harl Dace, and I entered that on the fifteenth when I found out, because a harbor that has put a family on both sides of a line it drew itself is a harbor I would rather wrote down than had described to me in Veyr. And Neale will tell you all three himself and be glad to, because Neale has been waiting five weeks for somebody to ask him a question about his own gang instead of about a boat."

"And Voke."

"Elias Voke of the *Anna Ray*, fifty-one, nineteen winters, and he has a brother-in-law in the third lay who has nine winters of his own, and the brother's-in-law is the man who will lose a boat in a northeast blow on the first of March and not Voke, and I did not know that on the twenty-eighth of November and I know it now, and it is in my book with the date on which I found it out, which is the fifteenth, and the finding out is the only thing I have done for the fleet in five weeks that I am not ashamed of in a room."
