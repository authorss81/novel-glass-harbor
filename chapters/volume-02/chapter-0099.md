# Chapter 99

*Three Columns, and the Sixth Hand*

The tide was at half past one on Tuesday the second of May and the wind had come round into the south-west on Monday afternoon and gone into the south, and the glass had stopped falling at about eleven o'clock on Monday night and was not rising, and the harbor was flat, and the flat was a thing that four men in a boat did not believe until they were in it.

Harl Dace put the *Grey Widow* alongside the mole's steps at twenty to one and did not take any money for the boat, and the four of them went out: Corvin Sarn, with his own chest and his own hand-lead and a lantern; the yard, with a slate, a pencil, a stopwatch on a string and no air; Dace at the wheel; and Nessa Pike, who went because the going out is a person in a boat in the weather and a person in a boat in the weather is a thing with a man in it, and because she said so out loud in the shed at seven in the morning and nobody argued.

The boy did not go, and did not ask to, and had a hand that had been going since the fourth of April, and he stood on the mole's steps at the top of the flood with a slate against the rail and a pencil that he had to change every ten minutes.

---

The water came up over the fouling about twenty past one and the core came out of it in about a minute and a half, because eight and a half feet is a thing you can stand a man on the bottom of this harbor in, and Dace put the boat's bow over the fouling and Sarn went down the ladder with a line and a lamp and came up off it in four minutes.

Calder Marr read the position off the slate across eight feet of water.

"The fouling is about the same," Sarn called. "It is about the same and I will not give you a figure and I will not have one given. The mark the flood has left in it is the same mark. **The mass is the same and I have not dropped it a hand's width and neither have I lifted it, and that is the whole of what a man can say about a mass in nine weeks.**

"And the ground line is fouled exactly as it was and I have not touched it and I am not going to, and there is forty feet of the harbor's own marline lying at the bottom of it that came off this office's own moorings gear in February and I am not touching that either, and if this office ever wants it out it can have a diver and a bond and I will not be the one to argue with it."

The tide bell went at about two o'clock, three strokes, out of the third lay's wall, and the water off the south end of the mole gave it back a hand's width of a second later, and Calder Marr turned his head for it out of thirty-one years of doing it, and the boy on the mole's steps wrote four figures and the hour, and Sarn said one thing from the ladder that was not a finding and not an opinion and went in the second column because the man who said it was in a boat.

"A hand's width of a second, and that is the same as it was on the sixth of December and the same as it was in January and the same as it was in February, and I have been in this harbor since the twentieth of October and I have never known that thing to move, and I am not going to stand in a boat and tell you why it has not moved, because I do not know, and if I told you I did I would be selling a thing I have not got, and I have had a man in this trade try to sell me that once and he was doing it in a shed and he was doing it to make a point about a bell he had not been in the water for."

---

The going out was done at about ten past three and they were on the steps at twenty to four, and the four men wrote the entry at the top of the mole's steps in a flat calm on the second of May, and the boy was the fifth and the fifth is a thing this book has not had in five months, and the reason is the writ and the reason was given out loud on the steps in front of the authority's own hand who came out in a punt with the ledger on a board.

"It is the writ," the boy said. "The writ says that a report of a finding of worked glass is to go up the coast within four days with the place, the hour, the person in whose keeping it is and the limits, and it says that the holder is to produce it on demand to any officer of this office. And I have been the yard's book for two hundred days and I have been four days late in three weeks and I have a hand that has been going since the fourth of April, and the day I cannot hold a pencil is the day a man in a city holds this yard's record in his own hand and writes whatever he likes into a space on a form.

"So there is a column now for what the boy said, and he is not in the boat, and the four men in the boat cannot check it, and it goes in whatever order the boy can say it and it goes in late, and the line at the top of it says the time it was read on the steps and the time it was written in the book, and the difference between the two is the whole of what is being fixed and I am not going to pretend the two times are the same time."

The clerk entered the third column in his own hand as being of the yard's own book and not of his office's, and said what a box can hold, and it is on the record in both books.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: the tide, the top of the flood, half past one on the second of May; the water, eight and a half feet by the pole, the pole being the one with the mark cut in it in February; the fouling, being about the same, and about the same not being a figure; the mass, being the same and not dropped and not lifted; the ground line, being fouled as it was and not touched, and forty feet of the harbor's own marline lying in it; the three strokes of the harbor's tide bell at about two o'clock and the delay of the answer out of the water off the south end, being a hand's width of a second, the figure being the same as the sixth of December and the same as the eleventh and nineteenth of January; the bellwright, Corvin Sarn, and that he is not of this party and was not asked anything; the four men in the boat, being Sarn, the holder, Dace of the *Grey Widow* and Pike, of the same.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: what the water has been doing to that thing for three months and a half, and that this is not a figure a man can take off a pole; the scour on the seaward face, which is not what it was in February and which the man who knows cannot say is different better or different worse; what is under the fouling, which has not been looked at and will not be looked at from a boat by a man with his hands full; and the frame, and the two parts of a chain in a lead plug, and the party is not going to describe the rest of it and is not going to be asked.*

*Third column, said by a man who was not in the boat and read at the top of the mole's steps at about ten past three on the second of May and written in this book at about half past five the same afternoon, the two times being entered and the difference between them being the whole of the thing and not a fault: the boy read the position off the slate and the four men corrected it and the four men are the ones who were there, and the boy took the delay himself at ten minutes past two on Monday the first of May on the flood off a flat calm and read it back twice and entered it and did not turn a hand's width on a lead line into a fraction of a second, because a hand's width on a lead line is not a fraction of a second and he is not going to invent a conversion for a book; and there is one other thing in the water on the first of May and the fourth of April and the eleventh and nineteenth of January and this yard is not going to write it down; and no occasion is entered in the delayed note and the count of occasions since the twenty-sixth of October stands at eleven where it stood on the thirty-first of March, because a figure taken on purpose on a flat calm with a slate and a stopwatch is not a note heard out of the weather, and a boy who has counted eleven of them is not going to make it twelve by writing a number.*

The five of them read it back, and the sixth man was the clerk and the seventh was Harl Dace, who had not been asked, and the fourth was the holder and he read it after his own crew for the second time in two days.

---

The sheets went out on the Tuesday afternoon and it took the rest of the day and it is the first time in five months that this yard has had anything printed, and it was not printed, and that is the whole of the point of it.

The ninth thing on the market wall, which went up on Saturday the fifteenth of April under a naval paper that has been on that wall since the eighteenth of March, was a sheet about one day with a boy's names on it and a finding in it that no person with an office in this harbor may enter. It was right. It was two columns and four read-backs and it could be copied by any man who can read. And it was eleven feet of paper about four hours of the twenty-eighth of April, and it was in a market, and it was nailed up, and it was gone the way every other thing on that wall is gone.

So it was copied, by hand, on the second of May, by six people, and no two of them is of this yard.

**Sella Brigg's hand is round and fair and she wrote it out in an hour and a half and did it at her own table and would not be paid for it.** Harl Dace's is the hand of a man of forty-eight who keeps a master's slate and who wrote it out twice because he did not like the first. Edric Pringle's is very small and very fair and she copied it in a hand that could have been a clerk's, having spent thirty years writing out the figures of a boat's own accounts in a hand her husband could not read, and she said that out loud, and it is in the first column, because a man in this market could check it by asking her.

**Coll Ferrow's is the hand of a man of thirty-one with a bad wheel and nine days in this harbor because of it**, and he wrote his own copy in pencil, in a shed, with a slate under it, and it took him all of the third of May and he would not have anybody help him with the figures. **Oswin Trask's is the hand of a man of fifty-eight who is in the south lay and has not written anything down in forty years**, and he took the copy away and did it at his own table and brought it back on the Thursday of the third of May and would not say how long it had taken him, and when Calder Marr read it and compared it against the wall it was correct in every figure and had one line in it that was not on the wall, and the line was: *and I have not been to it since the fifteenth of April and I am not going and that is mine and not yours.*

And the sixth hand was the boy, and it was not a hand.

"I did not write any of them and I am not one of the six writers and I want it said on the wall that I am not one of the six writers," he said, "because in four years there is going to be a man in a city who reads a list of six hands and one of the names on it is mine, and there is not a mark on this earth that I have ever made and six men are going to say that I did."

"So the list says six hands and a mouth," Calder Marr said. "And the mouth read every one of them back, and the mouth is not a hand, and the wall is not a seal and is not a claim and is not a survey, and binds nobody and consents for nobody, and that is the ninth thing's own four lines and they are the head of this one as well, and the head of this one is eleven lines and the eleventh is about a chart and I will read it out and then the whole of it goes on the wall tomorrow and the copies go out tonight."

*That this sheet has been copied by hand six times by six people of six different boats and one of the six wrote it in pencil on a slate, and that the list of the six is on it, and that the boy who reads the book did not write any of them and is not one of the six and is a mouth and not a hand, and that no copy of it is in this yard's keeping and one is on this yard's market wall and one is in the harbor authority's ledger in the clerk's own hand, and that the party asks no person to do anything with it and will not be asked.*

Four men read the head back. The four were the boy, the yard, the shipwright, and the holder of the writ, who has now read back after his own crew three times in two days, and the clerk read it after all of them and entered it in his own book and said the wall was good until the eighth of May.

---

Harl Dace carried two of them to the *Anna Ray* and to the *Low Lantern* in his own boat in the evening, and Edric Pringle carried two down the hill in her own hands on the bus, which is a thing she said out loud in a market on the Monday, and neither of them was asked to carry anything and neither of them was paid for carrying anything and both of them said the same sentence in different words, which is that a sheet of paper in a man's own boat is a thing in more than one place and that is the only arithmetic any of them has ever been good at.

And on the second of May, at about six in the evening, the yard went up to the net loft and made a hand lead's line out of the flax and did it wrong once and did it again, and Nessa Pike said the line was a boat's line and not a diver's and would be right in a fortnight or not at all, and the boy wrote a line in the book about a man making a line at night, and the reason he gave was that no man on a mole could check how a line was made and that in four years somebody might want to know.

And the position in the yard's own book at the end of Tuesday the second of May is **seven marks in a bag, twenty-three on the chandler's note, nothing in a tin, a writ on the bench that runs to the first of March next with a reporting clause on it and four names on it, twenty marks of unsold iron on the yard's own ground with a saw kerf in one crown shackle that a man of the south lay has offered a shilling for on its own, four lines and sixteen marks in a wage column whose oldest line is the fifth of March, forty marks of claim standing whole, forty marks to a conservator with no office standing and not reached by the writ, eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg standing, no compressor gland and no delivery hose in this harbor, the ninth seam's mooring out of service with no end of it entered, the tide gang four men short from the first of May, the mole-head light not on and not to be, the *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle, the hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November, nineteen winters of dues disputed and unpaid, and a boy of twenty-two who is to be off the stone on the fifteenth of May whatever his own arithmetic says.**
