# Chapter 76

*The Officer's Figure*

The slate wall behind the reeve's bench was blank on the morning of Wednesday the fifteenth of March and it was blank on purpose, and Ors Kest said so out loud before anybody sat down, which nobody in that room had ever heard a reeve do about a wall.

"It was wiped on Monday the thirteenth at eight and I have put nothing on it since," he said, "and there will be a great deal on it after this morning, and I want the room to know that I chose. The ledger is the record and the wall is a convenience, and in about an hour there are going to be two slates in this room and one of them is mine and one of them belongs to the vessel on the pile, and I want anybody who writes on either of them to be able to see the other one, and I want a man who came up here to read a figure off a wall to be told to his face that a figure on a wall is a figure for a week."

Then he called the sitting, and the clerk opened the ledger at a leaf with a rule on it, and the officer of the cutter came in with his notebook open for the first time since anybody in Morrow Quay had seen it open.

The master of the *Vigil* had signed a paper on the eighth of November which was nailed over four other sheets on the fish market wall, and the request of the officer was dated the twelfth of March and had come up from the tender in a sealed jacket on the morning of the Tuesday, and it asked for five things, and the first of them was a slate.

---

"Ten," the officer said, and put a slate on the table, face up.

It was a naval slate and it was not new, and the figures on it were in a white chalk that had gone soft in the wet. The upper line was a date, the eighteenth of December, and against it the word *sheets* and the figure ten. There was a second line under it and a third, and the third was in a different chalk and in a different hand, and the officer put his finger on it and stopped there.

"That upper figure is my vessel's count of the printed sheets of the fourteenth of October, made at the mole head on the eighteenth of December by a man in my tender, and I am told it is the number that was published on this harbor's market wall on the twenty-third of November. It is entered here as the vessel's figure and as the vessel's figure only, and this office is not asked to adopt it, and I am not asking this office to adopt it. I am asking that it be noted beside whatever else is entered this morning, with its date, because the yard's count and my count differ by one and a man in a city will be shown both."

Kest had the yard's figure in front of him in the ledger and it was the count of the twenty-third of November with ten, and the second line under it, in a different pen, the eighteen of January, with eleven, and a seventh line that read that the number of copies was therefore eleven and had been ten and both numbers are true of their own dates.

"Entered," the clerk said. "The vessel's figure, the eighteenth of December, ten. The party's figure, the twenty-third of November, ten, and the eighteenth of January, eleven. Both stand and neither is scratched out, and the difference of one is entered as a difference and not as an error, and I am writing that because the office does not know which of the two is the right number and has no instrument for finding out."

"Thank you," said the officer. "That is the first of the five."

---

The second was the Registry's request of the thirtieth of December, and the officer put it on the table himself, and it was four paragraphs in nineteen words and nineteen words.

"The vessel does not ask this office to order a party to deliver anything," he said. "A harbor authority has no such power and I have not come up here to waste a morning pretending it does. What the vessel asks is that this office note, as a fact of the record, that a document produced for a contested material, which the producing party amends in public, and carries the amendment on the same wall on the same day, and publishes a fresh count of its own copies while the first count is still standing above it, is not a record that is single; and that the request of the circuit office of the thirtieth of December ought to be granted, and this office is of a mind to communicate that to that office."

"You have put four things in one sentence and two of them are an argument," Kest said, "and I am going to take them in the order they are in and not in the order they are comfortable in. Yes: I will note that the sheet is amended in public. It is amended in public and it is dated and the amendment says the difference is withdrawn by the yard and names the sheet inside which says why, and a man in a market can read both, and I have wanted it on the market wall since the fourth of January and it is the reeve's own rule that the record is the ledger and not the paper. No: this office has no power to compel a party to deliver a paper, and I will not order it, and I will not be asked twice. And the second half — that the sheet is not a record that is single — I will note as the vessel's opinion, in the vessel's words, and not as a finding of this office, because it is an opinion and a good one, and because I have no way of testing it in this room and neither has the vessel and neither has the party."

"Will you communicate it."

"I will send the vessel's paper to the circuit office with this office's note on it and the date on it, and I will do that today, and I am telling the room that I am doing it and not asking anybody's leave, because a request of a vessel is a thing a harbor authority forwards and a party is told about and not a thing a harbor authority refuses on the party's behalf." Kest shut the folio and opened the ledger. "Second thing noted. Move on."

---

The third was the one that cost something, and it was the third because the officer was a man who worked down a list in order.

"That on the fourth of March, at the request of the vessel, the working boat *Sea Wren* was held in this harbor with her gear and her papers, upon the ground of entries concerning a contested material entered in the fish market of this town. That the party holds a book in which the day of the twenty-sixth of January is entered in the party's own hand in two columns, and that the entry was made at the vessel's own asking and on the twenty-eighth of January. And that this office is asked to note that the party permitted a crew not of the party's association to have its day entered in the party's own book, and that the party has declined to strike the entry out upon that crew's asking, and that the crew's master has asked for and received a copy of the leaf, countersigned by the holder."

"That is four facts and every one of them is true," Calder Marr said, "and I am not going to argue a word of it, and I would like the fourth one entered in a slightly different form, which is that a crew not of this association asked to have its day entered and the party put it on its own wall in two hands four days later without being asked to."

"The difference is not material."

"It is the whole of the difference and it is four days long," Calder Marr said, "and the party has spent five months being careful about exactly that and it is not going to be careless about it in a hearing room because the man reading it out has a form in a file in Veyr."

The clerk entered it as Calder Marr had it, in the party's words, and the officer did not object, and the third thing went into the ledger of the harbor authority in a form in which the Free Keel yard's book, and a leaf of it, and a date on a wall, and a crew that is not a party, are all on the page together for the first time in five months, and none of them is struck out, and they will all be copied.

---

The fourth was the one Kest had to speak slowly, and he did, and he had the cost of it in front of him before he began, and he has said since that he did it in the only order available to him.

"This office is asked whether the request of the vessel for the production of the party's book, being dated the twelfth instant, is an inspection of the holder within the meaning of the holder's charter, and whether this office is of a mind to enter it as one." He put both hands flat on the ledger. "No. It is not. An inspection under that instrument is an inspection of ground and gear, and a harbor authority can walk a man over a mole and look at a pump and write it down, and a request for a piece of paper is a request for a piece of paper, and if this office entered a request for paper as an inspection then in two years a party in this harbor could hold up every sheet of paper it has by walking a naval officer round a shed. That is not a protection of anybody. It is a licence."

He stopped, and the officer's pencil was moving, and Kest looked at it.

"I am going to say the next part slowly and I am saying it because I am not going to have a word of that charter found out about me in four years. I have just used the word *inspection* nine times in a room in which a naval officer is writing, and the word *inspection* is in that charter, and so is the word *suspend*, and the word *suspend* is in that instrument because somebody in an office wrote it in November and put it in front of me and I put it in front of the yard. I have said it out loud. I would have said it out loud in any case, because this office does not have secrets about its own instruments, and a thing said in a room cannot afterwards be found to have been thought up." He squared the folio. "I am not entering the vessel's request as an inspection, the vessel may inspect the holder's ground and gear at any hour of any day without asking this office, and this office will not enter a request for paper as one. Fourth thing answered, and the word is in his book and he may make what he likes of it, and that is the price of saying a thing where four people can hear it."

---

The fifth was Mara Venn, and she said it before the officer had finished the sentence introducing it.

"No."

The room did not go quiet, because it was not a room that went quiet, but four men stopped writing.

"I will say it fully and I will say it in my own words so that it is in the ledger as mine and not as a summary." She did not stand up. "There is no office in this coast in which I can hold anything any more, and I have held nothing since the fourth of January, and I have no standing in this office and none in the circuit office and none in the office that would be asked to test a document. I am named in a copy of the request of the thirtieth of December as a person who has already declined a related request in writing on four grounds, and that paper is in a file in the west of this city, and the office which wrote it has told the party in its own type that I am not a person through whom it can be answered, and it was right then and it is right now.

"You are going to ask me whether a sheet of paper in the fish market is a copy of a sheet in a custody room and whether a conservator could say so. I could probably say something about paper. I could probably say something about ink, about a fair hand, about a press that takes forty sheets at a time. And I would be saying it without standing, without a right of reply, and without anything behind me to be punished by, and in about two years one of those sentences would be copied out of this ledger into a hearing in Veyr and would be the only sentence of mine in it. I have watched four months of this yard make every person in it useful. Do not make me useful. I am a trade. I am expensive or I am cheap and I am on a shelf, and you have not bought anything off that shelf and you are not going to today."

"Nobody is going to buy anything off it," Kest said, "and the clerk will enter what she said in her own words and unshortened, and I am going to add one sentence of my own underneath it, which is that a ground stated by a person with no standing is still a ground, and that this office has been putting grounds from people with no standing into its own book since the seventeenth of February and intends to go on doing it, and that in about two years somebody is going to find four such entries in one ledger and draw a conclusion, and the conclusion will be correct, and there will be nothing on the page to stop it."

---

Elias Voke was on the back bench and he had not said a word for an hour and a quarter, and he said two, and both of them were said to the officer and not to the reeve.

"First. I wrote the statement of the capacity of this harbor at my own table on the Saturday night before the first of March and I put that yard's book in it and I put a boy's name in it, and I did both on purpose, and I would do them again. You are going to read that document in a city you have not been inside and you are going to read it against that book, and you are perfectly entitled to, and when you do, the thing you are reading is not a thing that was put in a document to be checked. It is a thing a herring skipper put in a document so that a coast office would know whose figures were being kept about the ground of his own harbor. That is not a weakness in it. That is the only reason it is worth reading.

"Second." He put his hand on the back of the bench in front of him. "If that document is not good enough because a yard's figures change, then in about a year every skipper on this coast will learn that the way to be safe is to write nothing, and the coast that does that is a coast with no capacity and no hard at Ashtide and no answer in it, and you may put my name against that as well."

Kest entered both sentences and did not shorten them, and the officer wrote four lines in his own book and did not read them out, which is the correct thing to do with four lines in a room where four men are reading back.

---

The last thing was the only thing the officer had come up for that the reeve could not answer in a sentence, and he said it at the end because he had been in the room for an hour and a quarter and had earned the last of it.

"I do not know whether there is a second season," Captain Veyra Holt's officer said, "and I am not going to pretend to this office that my vessel's position is any part of that. I have read the charter of the twenty-eighth of November and I know what day it runs out on, and it runs out on Friday the thirty-first of March, and after that day the holder is a man with a public record and no instrument, and that is the vessel's business and not the office's, and the vessel has taken no step to bring it about and will take none."

"That is the first thing said in this room in five months that this office has believed without checking," Kest said, "and I am going to enter it as heard, and I am going to add the rest of it, which is that whatever comes back from Veyr about a second season is a date this yard does not control and a date this office does not control, and that this office has no power to renew that charter and holds nothing that renews it, and that the last of it is in sixteen days and is a Friday, and that on Friday the thirty-first of March the holder of a one-season provisional working charter in Morrow Quay will be a man with a book and a wall and no paper, and that this office will have watched it happen and will have had no part in it."

The clerk entered that too, in his own hand, at the foot of the page, and then stopped and looked at what he had written, which was two sides, and said, without being asked, that he had been keeping a page since the seventeenth of February and that this morning was the first morning it had needed two, and that nobody had told him to keep it and that he was going to keep it whether anybody told him to or not.

And Bevin Rook was not in that room, because he is not a party, and the officer asked after him once, in the ordinary way, at the door; and Kest said that he was not a party and had not been asked for anything and was not to be sent for; and Calder Marr, who had come out of the hearing room onto the mole steps at half past eleven, stood on the steps in the wind for a moment with a ledger shut behind a door he had been inside all morning, and did not go up the hill to the shed to tell anybody anything, and went instead to the fish market and stood in front of a wall with seven things on it and one of them with a naval paper nailed over it and a four-inch gap.
