# Chapter 72

*A Form and a Signature Line*

The form was on the boards of the *Sea Wren* before the market had finished opening, and it was done in daylight, in about two hours, and nobody on the fish dock laid a hand on a man.

The officer of the cutter came up on the tender at half past nine and came ashore with a notebook shut under his arm and a leading hand behind him carrying a slate, and he asked for the master by name, which was *Mr Tarner*, and Tarner came out of the wheelhouse with the pump's discharge pipe in his hand and put it down, and that was the whole of the greeting.

"Wend Crale is on the fish dock with a broken finger," Tarner said, before anybody had said anything at all. "Piet Marrow is fifteen and cannot read. If this is about the crew I would like it said in front of the crew and not on the quay."

"Mr Tarner." The officer turned the notebook so that it was shut and visible, which was a courtesy and also a thing to point at. "This is not a boarding and it is not an arrest and nobody is charged. It is a form. May I read it to you at the bow."

---

He read it standing on the boards with the sheet in one hand, and the leading hand read the hour out of the slate at the top of it, and the hour was twenty-four minutes past ten in the morning of Saturday the fourth of March, and Bevin Rook, who was on the fish dock with a slate of his own and no reason to be there except that the market is where a man finds out things, wrote the hour down before anybody had said a word about anything else.

*Second sheet. Held in this harbor pending verification of entries concerning a contested material entered in the fish market of Morrow Quay. The vessel, her gear, and her papers are held.*

*That the vessel so held is not claimed to contain any material whatever.*

*That no person is charged and that the master is requested to sign below.*

That was the whole of the reading. The officer read it twice, because the yard's own rule about a document being read twice in a room with the party present had been in the fish market since November and a naval officer is not above learning a thing off a wall.

Nessa Pike, who had come down with the book in a satchel because a man does not leave a book in a shed when something is about to happen on a dock, said one word, out loud, to nobody: "Read."

"It has been read."

"Read the endorsement," she said, "because that is the box, and a box is where a thing gets said that the first sheet does not say, and I would like it read before there is a signature on it, and not after."

---

Tarner asked for the endorsement to be written out, and the leading hand wrote it out on the slate and the officer read it aloud and the leading hand entered it in the form, and it read, in the office's own type, that the vessel so held is a working salvage boat of no crew of any association, that she carries no transparent material and none is alleged, that her master is a member of the Free Keel association and has been off its slate since November, and that no person of the Free Keel yard of Morrow Quay has been served with this instrument or named as a party to it.

"That is four things and they are all true and I did not write any of them and none of them is against me," Tarner said. "Now put the fifth one in and let me see you write it."

The leading hand looked at the officer.

"Put it in," the officer said.

"That the twenty-sixth of January is a day in the Free Keel yard's book and on the Free Keel yard's market wall, and that this vessel's owner did not write the entry and was not asked to write it and cannot strike it out, and that if this vessel is held because of an entry then the entry is in the office's own market and can be read by any man in this harbor who can stand up."

Nobody in that street said anything for a moment, and the market went on being a market behind them, and a woman put a crate of fish down about thirty feet away and made a noise getting it off her shoulder.

"That is a good sentence and it is not yours," Tarner said. "Write it as his."

"It is the office's type," the officer said. "The office's type is the only type this instrument has."

"My name is on the paper as a person making an assertion about my own boat in an endorsement to a form that is holding her, and the assertion is in the office's type, and in four years a man in Veyr will read it as though the office had written it and the office will have a copy of my hand on the bottom of it. Write that the assertion is the master's and was made at the master's request, in the master's words, and put the master's name against it, and if the office will not do that then the office has put a man's own words in its own mouth on its own paper and I would like that done in front of the fish market and not on the mole."

It was done. The office's type carried four lines and the master's words carried five, and the master's name went against the five, and the leading hand initialled the corner, and the whole of that took eleven minutes and cost the office nothing at all.

Then Tarner signed the signature line, and he signed it the way a man signs a thing he has made somebody else read out loud: slowly, in the middle of the line, and without any remark.

---

What went into the lighter was the reason the yard had spent five months learning to write things down about, and it was not a fight and it was not a struggle, and there was nowhere in it for anybody to put a hand.

The pump came out first, because the pump was the thing the boat was for. It went down onto the boards and up the side into the tender's boat and across into the lighter, and it is a two-man pump with a cracked housing in it that was bought in November and has never been hired out and is worth four marks a tide to a man who has no pump, and the man who owns the *Sea Wren* is not a man who has no pump, and the *Sea Wren*'s pump was the only working pump in this harbor that did not belong to the harbor authority, and now it was in a lighter with a line through it.

Then the leads, the two warps coiled, the hand lead with its line, a shackle off the twelve-pound iron, an oar and a spare, the pump's hose, the tool box, and the gear, all of it gone up the side and across and down in about twenty minutes by four men working in daylight on a fish dock where anybody could see them and nobody did.

And then the papers.

"That is the log," Tarner said, and he had it in his own hand, and he did not hand it over, and the officer did not ask for it twice.

"Right," Tarner said. "I am going to read it out, and you are going to write that I read it out, and then you are going to have it. It is my book and it is the only thing aboard that is mine and not the boat's, and I have heard what your office does with a book it is given, and I would rather a man on this dock heard what is in it while I have still got it."

He read it out standing on the boards. He read the twenty-eighth of January, the fouled ring on a herring man's warp at the twelfth seam, four and a half feet, twenty-one minutes by his own count and nineteen by the boat's, and the stone, and the shackle, and the four feet of warp.

And he read it out in two columns, and he said where he had got the two columns from, in the middle of a market, with his voice up, so that eleven or twelve people heard it.

"I keep it like that because a yard on this dock told me to, in January, and I want that written down too, because in four years somebody is going to find out that a working crew in this harbor keeps two columns and the first thing they will say is that it is a naval form, and it is not, and it is theirs, and they put it up on a wall for nothing."

"That is not in the form," the officer said.

"No," Tarner said. "It is not in the form. Write it on the endorsement anyway, because you have about four minutes before you have to be somewhere else and because I would like one thing I have said in this street to be on a piece of paper that goes out of it."

It went on the endorsement. The officer read the second sheet back in full afterwards, the type and the master's words and the signature line, and the leading hand entered the hour again, and the officer signed at the foot of the first sheet, which is the only place on the paper where anybody's name is in his own office's act.

---

Somebody went up the west basin for the reeve at about half past ten and came back at about eleven with a sentence, and the sentence was that this office has no power over an instrument of a vessel of the Crown Navy and will not pretend to any, and the officer said that he had not expected otherwise and had not asked, and the man who brought the sentence said it out loud on the dock in front of about forty people because he had been told to, and the office of the harbor authority was in it and was not in it, and the clerk who brought it looked as though he would have preferred to have been carrying something with a box on it.

Then the gear went across and the lighter took it out past the mole and the *Sea Wren* was moved at about two in the afternoon, unlit, with her pump in a lighter ahead of her and her warps in a lighter behind her, to a mooring at the pile under the *Vigil*, where there is a line through the ring and a board on the timber with an hour chalked on it and nothing else.

The *Sea Wren*'s crew went aboard and stayed aboard, and the crew may go ashore twice a day, which is not a punishment and is not a mercy, and the boy wrote both of those words in his own right hand with the hour on them because he did not know yet which of the two it was and said so.

---

Four men had come down the dock between ten and two asking after a crew to hire in a blow, and every one of them had asked after the *Sea Wren* by name, and every one of them was told the same thing in about four words, and two of them had money.

"I want that in the book this afternoon," Nessa Pike said, before they were up the steps. "The two of them had money and I do not know how much and I am not going to ask, and a man with a hand lead and a slate and a good ear was standing on a dock all morning and was not hired, and that is not a fact about the wind. That is a fact about a form."

"Enter it in the third column," Bevin Rook said, and then stopped, because there is no third column, and because the two-column convention is the yard's own and it is a record of a day's work and not a column of grievances.

"Enter it under the day with the hour and no adjective," Nessa Pike said, "and put my name on it, and Calder is going to say that a man is not an entry."

---

Calder Marr said nothing at all until the lighter was round the mole, and then he said one thing to Noll Tarner, who was standing on the dock with an empty boat behind him and a book in his hand that he had read out in a market and did not have any more.

"I cannot argue with you and I am not going to try," he said. "The day is in our book and it is on our wall and you asked for it there and we put it there, and I have said for five months that a record is worth having because a man can read it and check it, and I have said it in a market with your mug in your hand, and I meant it, and it is the reason there is a form on your deck this morning and I am not going to stand here and tell you it is not."

"I know it is not," Tarner said. "That is the part I cannot get round. If you had kept it in a drawer I would be afloat tonight and I would hate you, and I would be afloat, and in three weeks I would be doing a job off the north wall and I would have heard about it afterwards, which is the part I would have minded. As it is I have read it in a street in front of a market and I know exactly what it says and I cannot get at it, and that is a different thing and I have not decided which one it is yet and I am not going to decide it in front of these people."

He got into the boat that took him out to the pile, and the boat went out, and the fish dock was a fish dock again, and the *Kittiwake* was still on the hard with her bilge blocked in and no cradle, and the ninth seam's mooring was in service, and the north berth was out of service and was the one that took a north-easter, and it was the fourth of March and there were twenty-seven days to the last of the month and a charter that had the same twenty-seven left in it.
