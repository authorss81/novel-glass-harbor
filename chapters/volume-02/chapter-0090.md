# Chapter 90

*Two Facts in One Book*

The slate wall behind the reeve's bench was wiped on Monday the seventeenth of April at eight, whether there was a sitting or not, and nothing had been put on it since, and the clerk said on the dock at about nine that his own page had wanted a side and a half that morning and that he was going to keep it, and that the wall was a convenience and the ledger was the record and everybody in Morrow Quay now knew the difference between the two of them and had not needed to be told.

The whole of the last fourteen days went into the yard's own book at about four in the afternoon on the seventeenth, in two columns, and it is the longest entry in that book and it is the one that is going to be read.

*First column, checkable by any man in this harbor or any man who can sail: the cairn on the Old Custom House, eleven feet by pole at half-tide, the stones ballast and not one man's work, the weed green on the bottom course and the top three black; the stem on the Nine Foot called the Accountant, nine fathoms, four casts; the bar at twenty past two on the flood off a spring, two and three-quarters; the fouling at four fathoms and a hand's width, being a knuckle of kelp, a shoe, and four feet of herring warp gone hard; the hour of every cast, the lead, the slate, the man who swung it, the breeze, the sea; and the name of the man who gave the account, which is Harl Dace, master, of the *Grey Widow*, of no crew of this association, not paid, not engaged, not asked to check it first; and the names of the two men who have spoken about it since, one of whom said in a market that it was a tale and has not been proved wrong either, and one of whom said the two figures were his own out of a slate he has carried since November.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that the third mark, the Kettle, was not looked at, because it dries three days out of a month and this was not one of them, and the reason for going back for it; that the bar's figure at half past was two and a quarter on the ebb, which is the shoulder and not the bar; what the shelf under the stem is doing; the fouling itself; and the fact that five miles of that coast was not looked at and cannot be, and that eleven miles of water was run to look at two stones and a bar, and that no survey of anything exists and no instrument was used, and that the account is a master's account and a day's work and not a chart.*

And under the two columns, in Calder Marr's own hand, four lines that the four men read back, and that the clerk of the harbor authority has a copy of it in his own hand, read back and not shortened, and that he is keeping it whether the wall is up or not.

*That the seal on the face of the object described above is false, and that the finding is a conservator's, in her own words and her own hand, at her own asking, and is unsigned, because no person in this harbor with an office has entered it and the reason why is the fourth of January.*

*That the finding is about a seal and not about the sea, and that the account is about the sea and not about the seal, and that neither of the two is a reason to believe the other, and that they are in the same book in the same week because both of them are true and neither of them helps anybody.*

*That the object is in the keeping of a man and never was in the keeping of this yard, and that the party made no claim to the water, and that this yard holds no instrument, no claim, and no paper of any kind at the hour of writing, and that this is entered as a fact and not as an opinion.*

*That this yard does not know who held the tool, does not know who made the glass, does not know why a man wrote the name of his own boat down as the thing that owns a record of the sea, and does not intend to find out in a hurry, and that a man in a city who wants all three of those things is going to have to ask somebody who is not this yard.*

"That is the whole of it," Calder Marr said, "and there is not a line of it that I could say twice."

"You will be able to say it four times in four years," the boy said, "and it will be four men reading it back, and I have counted the read-backs on this page and there are nine, and I have not been told to count them and I have not asked anybody to be the fourth."

---

The money was read out at five and the boy read it twice and then read the position, and this time the position had a line in it that had not been there at the end of March.

"Gross in the last fourteen days, twenty, and every mark of it on the fifth of April, from Iven Toft, a herring boat's own ring and about four feet of her own warp at the second seam, on her own account, the pump hired and not lent. The eighth two, the share two, air two, rope a mark, a hand two out of the *Grey Widow*, and the pump four. That is thirteen out of twenty and the yard's was seven, and there was nothing on the sixth and there has been nothing since the fifth, and there was a day when two men came up this hill and one of them was answered.

"The bag was five on the thirty-first of March and it is three. The note is at thirty and it went up three on the eleventh with a fortnight of bread, oil and candles in the creditor's own hand, paid out of this bag the same afternoon, and it is the biggest that page has been since the eighth of March. The three counts of the weeks are twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written, and none of them has moved since the fourth of January and none of them is going to be moved by anything in this book. The tin has two crowns in it, and they went in on the first of February and have not been out of it since.

"And the wage. Ten weeks entered, the first four paid, and the fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth and tenth unpaid, and that is twenty-four marks in a column with no adjectives in it, and the eleventh falls on Tuesday the eighteenth and will be entered the same way, and I have not asked and nobody has told me a date, and nobody in this crew is going to ask me about it."

"Read the owing back," Nessa Pike said, and it is the part of the book that no employer on this coast would have printed.

"Forty marks to the boy for the reading and the hand, standing whole, not reduced, not asked to be reduced, and not paid. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not paid, not reduced, not asked for, and on the twelfth of April she was refused four marks by a shop for an opinion she gave in a market and put that in her own column, and her forty is not one mark smaller for it. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, owed since the twenty-seventh of February, and there is no eight marks in this harbor before October and there is not going to be. And eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg in her own name, undiminished, unpaid, entered on the sixth of February, and the stack of iron it came off is still unsold and is still worth twenty marks at a fair sale to a clerk's thirteen and there is a saw kerf in one crown shackle that nobody has accounted for."

The book went on the bench and the pencil went on the shelf, and the stove was not lit and the wind was still off the water and the glass had been falling for fourteen days and had not made up into a rain.

---

The rest of it went in as the yard writes the rest of it, which is as a set of dated positions with no adjectives, and a man in four years can take them one at a time.

The one-season provisional working charter ended at midnight on Friday the thirty-first of March and there is no second season and no instrument of any kind in this yard's keeping. The working boat *Sea Wren* is at the pile under the *Vigil*, with her gear and her pump in a lighter and her master's own logbook in a naval file, and a copy of a leaf of this yard's own book is in his coat, and the crew may go ashore twice a day, and that is not a punishment and has never been a mercy. The circuit office's fourth request, dated the sixteenth of March, is unanswered and unrefused, and the count of the copies of the public sheet in that office's own type is eleven, and the *Vigil*'s slate still says ten as of the eighteenth of December, and both stand, and eleven copies are on a wall or in a chest or in another crew's keeping or in a man's stall, and this yard has asked no holder of any of them for any of them, and will not.

The notice of the thirty-first stands whole. Its first clause closes the mole's toe and inner face from the third seam to the eleventh to all work and to all approach by all persons, which includes this office, and this office has no power to lift it; the harbor authority has now entered that fact five times in its own book, and the fifth of them was on Friday the seventh of April at the asking of this yard, and the cost of it belongs to the reeve. The second clause keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in this harbor, and no person in this yard has argued it, and on the seventh it was the reason a sheet of worked glass was looked at in daylight on a mole's crown and not indoors anywhere. The third clause names Calder Marr, Nessa Pike and Bevin Rook for the authority's work, and no one of them has been on the authority's work since it was served.

The north berth is out of service and is the only wall in this harbor that takes a north-easter. The ninth seam's mooring is in service and is a mooring and not a berth. Fifty fathoms of the herring fleet's winter warp lie on the north wall where ninety were to lie, and forty feet of the harbor authority's own marline is in the sea, and three men of that authority's tide gang are off again from the first of April, and the mole-head light went on at dusk on the eighteenth of March and was taken in on the twentieth and has not been lit since, because a light on the mole head costs oil and the repair account has been empty since the middle of February. The third lay is full. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered and has been since the ninth of November. The *Kittiwake* is on the hard with her bilge blocked in and no cradle under her, and the harbor's carpenters are free and have not been to her.

A man of the third lay with nine winters of his own went out in a northeast on the twenty-eighth of February and has not come back, and there is not one line about him in this yard's book, and the boy entered on the twenty-first of March that he had looked twice, and nothing has changed on that page since, and nobody in this yard has been asked anything about him by anybody, and the yard has not gone.

The bell is on the harbor's own ground in eight and a half feet off the south end with its waist fouled and its tongue half worn through, and the going out costs eight marks and a day of a boat, and the eight marks are in the column above and are not paid, and there has not been a going out since the twenty-seventh of February, and the rope on it was bought on the eleventh of January and a rope is a year, and nobody in this yard has said out loud what happens in June.

Alden Marr is at the far bench and is not asked for anything and did not read the two columns and Calder Marr did not go over to him.

The one false line on the unverified slate is still false, and the slate was not moved, and two words would make it true, and a true slate with a boy's name in it is a document in a yard any man may read. The boy has not asked and has not been asked. The conservator with no office knows what is in the anchor and has still never been asked to say it, and on the ninth of April a man in a shed entered that he was not going to ask her, and she did not ask him, and there is a page that says so.

---

At about half past five a coasting master came up the hill and stood in the doorway and asked whether this harbor had a room for a man off a boat.

"That is not a question I can answer," Calder Marr said.

"He has come in out of a weather that was not on the glass yesterday," the man said, "and he is not hurt, and he is asleep in my wheelhouse, and I am asking because there is nowhere in this town to put a man at nine in the evening and the tide-gang store is shut and the shed is shut and the man would be sat on the mole steps all night."

"Neale has a stove," Nessa Pike said. "I will not put him in a yard that owes a man eight marks and a boy's four a week, and that is the honest answer and it is my own, and I will say it again in the road if anybody asks me."

The man went down the hill to the tide-gang store and the nine things on the market wall were still up in the dark with a nail through the corner of the newest one, and the eighth of them had been standing for thirty days with a naval paper nailed over one of the sheets beside it, and the copy of the ninth went into the hands of a coaster's master at the foot of the dock on Monday evening at the price of a shilling, which he paid, and he would not have taken it for nothing.
