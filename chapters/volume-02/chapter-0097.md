# Chapter 97

*A Longer Leash With a Name on It*

The hearing room over the tide-gang store had a stove in it and a bench for the reeve and a bench for the clerk and four benches for people who had come to be in a room, and the slate wall behind the reeve's bench was bare at eight o'clock on Monday the first of May because it is wiped every Monday at eight whether there is a sitting or not, and for the first time since the fifteenth of March Ors Kest put something on it.

He put up one figure. The wall had been bare for six weeks and everybody in that harbor had learned in that time that a figure on that wall is a figure for a week and the ledger is the record, and he wrote on it in chalk, in a hand that is trained to look neutral:

**FIVE OFF THIS MOLE, FRIDAY. NONE OF THEM ON THIS OFFICE. ONE MAN OUT. NOTHING RAISED.**

"That is on there on purpose," he said, before he called the sitting, "and it is the first thing I have put on that wall since the fifteenth of March, and it is there for a week and it is not the record. The record has the claim window and the reason and the two columns and the tender's cutoff and the whole of it in a ledger with a date on it, and if any man in this room thinks the wall is enough, he should say so now and I will wipe it and not write it again."

Nobody said so.

"There were five men off this mole on Friday the twenty-eighth of April," Kest said. "Two of them were on this office's tide gang and hired out of a working boat, one was this office's foreman as a tender, and two were of the yard that is not on this office's work and has not been since the thirty-first of October. **And the man they brought out was not one of the five.** He was a man of thirty-one of the second lay, at the top of the make since the twenty-ninth of March, who had gone down to the ninth seam's own mooring on Friday afternoon on his own account and for six marks with no instrument and no permit, and there was a shackle of that mooring in a hand that would not open, and he was on no list of this office and in no boat of this office and this office enters him as a man found and not as a man worked. **Nothing was raised.** A claim window of one date was worked and logged and raised nothing and it is gone, and I entered it and did not shorten it, and in five weeks a man in a city is going to call it a window that was missed, and no instrument in this coast holds the difference between the two, and in nine years as reeve I have watched four men be saved and one of them was saved by a thing that had no form at all."

---

The yard sat on the second bench and not the first, and the four of them were Calder Marr, Nessa Pike, Bevin Rook and Elias Voke of the *Anna Ray*, who had come in his own coat and had not offered to speak, and Edric Pringle sat at the end of the second bench and had a slate on her knees that she did not read from, and Mara Venn was at the back on a bench with no name on it, and Ambrose Sell of the circuit office of this coast sat at a table of his own with two papers and a pen and no inkstand, because inkstains are a thing a clerk has opinions about.

Kest called the sitting and the clerk opened the ledger at a leaf with a rule on it, and read the application, which was in Calder Marr's own left hand and unedited and which the boy read out first in his own voice and then the clerk read out in his own, and both of them read the same words, because the boy does not trust a reading and a clerk does not trust a hand.

*That the Free Keel yard of Morrow Quay applies for a provisional writ of salvage working; that the persons in the writ are Calder Marr, Nessa Pike and Bevin Rook of that yard, and Tam Slee, of the *Grey Widow* of Morrow Quay, hired and not of this association; that the ground is the mole from the first seam to the twelfth, the dump, the north wall, the first and second seams, all ground landward of the third seam, and private ground within the harbor line; that the fourth-hundred-yard bar off the outer marks stands and is not narrowed by this writ; that the harbor's eighth and the harbor's share follow the holder upon anything raised; and that the party does not ask this office for a bond, and that a bond is pledge of the party's own, and that the party's own is six weeks of air, a compressor gland, a hand lead's line, twenty marks of unsold iron with a saw kerf in a crown shackle, and a shed.*

Kest read that last line twice himself before he let the clerk have it.

"That is your own arithmetic and I am not going to have it as a valuation, because a court of wreck claims in Veyr is not going to accept a yard's list of gear as a fact about a yard. Enter it as a statement by the holder and not as an inventory." He turned round. "Read the entries of the twenty-eighth."

---

The clerk read the entries of the twenty-eighth: the claim window, the reason in the party's own left hand with the clerk present and unedited, the sixth of the reeve's acknowledgements, the two divers not of the party each fixing his own limit at the boat, the tender named and not a diver, the cutoff his, the pot of air three weeks standing and read out on a mole before anybody was wet, the man found, the handbell at four, the two blows, the half-tide, the place, the five fathoms, the eleven minutes, the raising of nothing, and the losing of a gland and about ten feet of the yard's own delivery hose and the yard's hand lead's line.

Then he read the cause of the warp, which is at the top of the second lay's wharf on the twenty-fourth of April, in the words of the woman who lost it, said first by her, in a market, not softened anywhere, and entered in no office's book until the twenty-ninth.

"This office took a fire party to the second lay on the twenty-fourth of April on this office's own motion and its own order, and the party went in the water about four hundred feet of herring warp off a wharf because the hose cart could not get round the third lay's boat, and that boat was lying against that wharf with forty of her men in a bucket chain at the time. Those two facts are the cause, and neither of them is a fault of the holder's yard, which was not there and sent nobody.

"And I am entering the fourth figure with it: the north wall took a sea on the twenty-eighth of April with fifty fathoms of the herring fleet's winter warp on it where ninety were to lie, and the money that would replace the other forty fathoms went on four sets of moorings and a season of warp in the fortnight before the first of March, on this office's account, and this office has not bought a fathom since. That is a fact about this office and it is not entered as a favour and it is not entered as an apology."

He read it back. Edric Pringle said the two words she has said at a mole and on a wall and in a market, and then said one more: that she would have said the cause in the same words on the day and would say it again on the day the seam is mended, and that the yard is not to put the date of a fire in a wage column.

"Then the price of this writ is the ninth seam," Kest said, "and it goes in the ledger today and on the wall in a week, and nobody is going to thank me for it.

"**The ninth seam's mooring is out of service from the first of May with no end of it entered, and the stone is not to be taken up and the fouled shackle is not to be freed, and the mooring is a mooring and not a berth and it is the only one this harbor has that is not on a bollard.** The ground line between the third and the ninth seams is untouched, as it has been since the seventeenth of February, and the fouling on it is not to be cleared by anybody under this writ or any other paper I have ever signed. And a man was in five fathoms off the inner shoals on Friday night because a man without an instrument was working on that mooring in a half sea, and the notice of the thirty-first closes the ninth seam and did not save him, and this office will not pretend to anybody that it did, and will not pretend that the notice was not worth the paper it is on, because this office is the one that put a foot inside that notice five times in four months and asked for the sixth.

"And there is a second price and it is a man. Petch is stood off the wall from the first of May to the tenth to mind the chain and the hose cart in front of the last lay's wharf, because that wharf is this town's fire front and a cart standing in front of it has to be moved when a boat comes in. This office's tide gang is three men short since the first of April and is now four, and I am not going to have anybody tell me in June that the mole steps are four feet higher than they were in April because the men who clear them were stood off to mind a cart."

---

Then the clause.

Ambrose Sell of the circuit office put two papers on the table and said that he had one to attach and one to read, and that the attachment was not his to make, and read it in a voice taught to be bored without being slow, and the room heard it twice, once in his voice and once in the clerk's.

*That the holder shall report to the circuit office of this coast, and to the officer of any vessel holding a claim in this harbor, every finding of worked glass, whole or in part, and every raising of material from ground which any other party claims, within four days of the finding or the raising, with a copy to this office; that such report shall state the place, the hour, the person in whose keeping the material is, and the limits stated on it; and that where a finding is disputed between persons this office may seat one of them and one other and take such finding as it is given, without prejudice to any claim; and that the holder shall produce this writ upon demand to any officer of this office or of the harbor authority.*

The sheet carried three names in three places and no face in the room. It was requested in writing by the magistrate of that circuit, signed by the master of a cutter holding a claim in this harbor, and sealed by Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner, whose office had wanted this yard's book since the thirtieth of December and had wanted it four times.

Kest stopped at *upon demand* and read it out by himself, twice. "Nobody in this room may interpret that clause. I have no power to enlarge it or to narrow it and I am not going to try in a room with a clerk taking it down. What I will do is enter the holder's objection in full and unshortened, and then grant the writ, and both of those are within my hand this morning."

The objection was in Calder Marr's own left hand and the clerk read it and did not shorten it, and it is in this office's ledger in two hands and went up the coast with the writ in a sealed jacket on the second of May:

*That the clause is capable of being read to reach a bar of gravel and a man's account of a coast, and that the holder has made no claim to the water, to the marks, to the bar or to any use of them, and has asked for no instrument in respect of them and holds none; and that if any office wishes to read it that way, the holder will say so in writing on the day the office asks and not before, and will hand over what it has.

*That the clause asks for findings, and a finding is not a conclusion; and that the holder reported two findings in public in the month of April, one against the holder's own interest and one the work of a person with no office and no fee, and will go on reporting findings in that manner and will not go on reporting conclusions, and that if this office wants conclusions it will have to get them from somebody it has a power over.

*That the holder does not object to the clause and takes the writ with it, and takes it because a yard with no paper cannot work, and a yard with a reporting clause knows what it has agreed to, and a yard that has agreed to something in writing can be asked about it in a room by a man with a book, and that is better than a yard that has agreed to nothing in writing and can be asked about it in a market by anybody at all.*

---

There is a finding on the clerk's page that the harbor authority of this harbor cannot enter, and it was put there by the clerk and not by the yard, and it is the reason the office's book is one page longer than it would otherwise be.

"This office cannot enter an examination of an object," the clerk said. "It entered on the seventh of April that a sheet of worked glass was on this office's own ground between the sixth and ninth seams on that date and that nobody went in the water, and that is the whole of what this office has. A conservator of no office came up that mole on the tenth of April with her own book and her own calipers and put *unsigned* under her own finding, and there is nowhere in this office's book that a finding goes. I said so on the tenth and I am saying it again today, and this is the fourth time in a year this office has discovered a thing it cannot hold, and I am keeping all four of them in this book on purpose."

**And the writ did not reach her and cannot.** "I have been in a Registry house," Mara Venn said, from the back, before anybody asked her anything, which is the fourth time in five months that she has said a thing before anybody asked it. "I have been a door that was tried in December and I have said so out loud in a room on the first of March. That writ asks a holder to report every finding of worked glass within four days. I have no office and I am not a holder and I am not of that yard, and there is no line in it that reaches me, and I want that said in this room and entered, because I have been refused four marks in writing for an opinion I gave in a market and I am not going to be brought into a man's paper by a clause written by a man who has never met me.

"Forty marks, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and I am not asking for them this morning and I am not going to be thanked for not asking, because not asking is not a gift to anybody. **I have a paper of my own going up the coast on the second of May and it is not this yard's and it is not a copy of anything of theirs, and it has three clauses on it about a false seal and a boat, and my name is at the foot of it in my own hand, and I have not told this yard what it says, because it is not this yard's and I am not an assistant and I am not going to be one this afternoon.** It asks three questions about a chart. I am not going to tell you what they are, and if you ask me in a market in June I will tell you that I have not looked at the sheet of that coast and could not tell you."

The clerk entered it and read it back and Mara Venn read it after him and said one word, and the word was *entered*.

---

The writ was made out on a sheet of the harbor authority's own and signed by its reeve, and the seal at the foot of it is the harbor authority's and not the Registry's and not a naval one, and the first thing in it is a name and the fourth thing in it is a name and both of them are in the room.

Kest read out the fourth name twice and then stopped, and looked at the bench, and the room was quiet in the way a room is quiet when every man in it knows what is going to happen and none of them is going to say it.

"That is a man who is not in this room," he said. "A man who has been in this yard's water twice this month and is not of this yard. This office can put his name on that sheet. This office cannot ask him whether he is content to have it there, and a name on a writ is a leash, and a lease on a man who has not agreed to it is the thing I have spent nine years of this office refusing to do."

**"Then read it to him and ask him," Calder Marr said. "And do not send the boy, because his hand is going and I have been watching it go since the fourth of April. I will go and ask him myself, this afternoon, and I will bring the sheet up the hill, and I will put it in front of him on the bench in this shed, and he will say what he says in front of three men, and it goes in the book with his words and not mine."**

"That is the first sensible thing anybody has said in this room in three months," Kest said, "and it is said by a man who has spent five months deciding things alone, and I am entering it as a remark of the reeve and not as a finding. If anybody in a city wants to know when a yard of Morrow Quay started to behave like a crew, it is in this ledger on the first of May, and it is a page about a man going up a hill on his own two feet to ask a question."

He entered it. Nobody argued, because there was nothing to argue about; a man had offered to walk up a hill, and everybody in that room understood what it had cost him to offer it in front of Ambrose Sell.

---

The grant is in the ledger in the reeve's own hand and it went up the coast in a sealed jacket on the second of May with a reporting clause on it and a name on the fourth line, and it runs to the first of March next, and it is inside the harbor line, and it is not the Second Bell license and does not lead to one, and it may be suspended after an inspection for cause by one man with a key and a book.

And the last line of the grant is not Kest's, and it is the only sentence in the whole of it that a man can hear an echo of a first season in, and he wrote it himself and the clerk read it back and it is in the record.

*"And this office is not the office that can renew this writ, and the second season is not this office's to give, and nothing in this instrument says that there is a second season, and if there is one it will arrive out of a city from a man who has never been inside this harbor, and the holder will be told about it when it arrives and not before."*

**"That is the leash, then," said Elias Voke from the second bench, in his own voice, and not as a joke.** "It is a longer one. It has our names on it. Nineteen winters and a hard at Ashtide and a man with a slate in a market asking after a woman in January, and every one of those is going up the coast on Monday with a writ in a jacket. I said in a hearing room on the first of March that whatever comes back decides whether there is a second season and the yard's part in it is nothing, and I have been right about that for two months and I would have given a good deal to be wrong."

"You are right about it," Calder Marr said. "And the yard has a writ, and the yard has a name on it four times, and on Friday the seventh of April we gave back the only claim any yard on this dock has had to a real object in five months, in a market, in front of the man it came from, because a boat cannot be asked anything. **We are the most reliable crew in this harbor and it has cost us a hand lead, a hose and a claim window, and the writ we got for it is a leash with our names on it, and I am going to take it, and I am not going to stand in a room and tell anybody that the two things are a bargain, because they are not a bargain, and both of them are true, and neither of them cancels the other.**"

Ambrose Sell said, "The circuit office will read that in June and not the words before it," and Calder Marr said, "Then the words before it are in my own book and four men read them back, and the writ is in your file, and the record of this harbor is longer than any one of us."

And the sitting was over at about twenty past twelve and the wall behind the reeve's bench carried one figure in chalk that was good for a week, and the ledger carried the rest.
