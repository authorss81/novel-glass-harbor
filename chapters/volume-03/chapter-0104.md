# Chapter 104

*The Gland and the Hose*

The second return went up the coast on Wednesday the tenth of May in a sealed jacket with a copy for the harbor authority's ledger in the clerk's own hand, and it went up on the day it was due, and that was the first time this yard has ever sent one of anything on the day.

It is one page and it is shorter than the first and it is in Calder Marr's own left hand and the boy read it out first and the clerk read it after him, and both read the same words.

*Return the second, made by the holder of the provisional writ of the first of May, on Wednesday the tenth of May.*

*The finding: on Saturday the sixth of May last, at about eleven in the morning, on the crown of this mole between the third and ninth seams, in the open air and in daylight, a lump of green glass about a hand's length, with a crust of this mole's own mortar on two sides of it, and a scalloped edge of eleven scallops in about four inches, which is not a fracture and not a mould line.*

*The place: this mole. The hour: about eleven in the morning of the sixth of May. The keeper: the holder, and the object is on this mole's own ground in the open air under a tarpaulin and is not in any shed and is not in any boat, and it is entered here because this form asks where it is. The limits: that the object is worth nothing to anybody the party has been able to find out about; that it carries no claim seal and is in no office's book and has no number anywhere; that it is not a panel, and is not the ferry plate, and is not the worked panel given back in a market on the seventh of April and is not connected with that object; that the party does not know its age, does not know who worked it, and does not know whether it was ever attached to anything, and declines to give a figure for its age and declines to give a reason for declining, and will answer in writing on the day any office asks; and that the scalloped edge is entered in the party's first column because a man on a mole can look at an edge and the party's second column is for things a man on a mole cannot check, and the party's own sentence on the edge is that the party does not know what it means and a clause asks for a finding and not a meaning.*

*And the finding was the sixth of May, the day of the finding is not counted, and this return is the fourth day after it and is made on that day and not late.*

The clerk read it and did not shorten it and then said the sentence he says.

"This office can enter that a return was made on the tenth of May and that it was due on the tenth. It cannot enter that a lump is worth nothing, and I am not going to try, and it is the sixth thing this office has discovered that it cannot hold." He put the jacket down. "**And I am going to write on my own page that the first of these two returns was two days late and the second was on the day, and both are the same instrument, and the box has no room for the difference and this book has.**"

---

The chandler at the sign of the Drowned Bell put the page under his arm and the pen behind his ear and listened to the whole of it, and Calder Marr said it out loud, which is the standing practice of this yard and which the man at the sign of the Drowned Bell heard him say at the head of this yard's own dock on the thirty-first of March in front of about forty people and has not forgotten.

"A mark is a hundred and sixty pence and a hundred and sixty is not divisible by three, and that is a thing you taught this yard in a market in February, and I am not going to argue it with you and I am not going to have a figure invented for it. **A compressor's gland is iron. Iron comes up this coast on a handcart, a day and a half, at a rate I do not control and you do not control and neither of us can name.** Put it on the page at your own figure and write it in your own hand, and the carting is yours, and I am not going to ask you for a line of credit, because a line of credit is a man writing down that he will sell this yard bread in June, and this yard has decided what it wants to be able to ask for and it is not that."

The chandler wrote **five marks** on the page in his own hand, under the figure of twenty-three, and did not write the carting, and said why without being asked.

"I am not putting a shilling and threepence on that page," he said. "A man who is owed twenty-three marks by a yard with a mark in the bag does not add a shilling and threepence to a page for the sake of being paid it in a year. **The carting is two miles and a day of a man and it is yours and it is not mine.**"

"A fortnight of bread while you have the book open," said Nessa Pike.

"Three, and the same as every other fortnight, and the oil and the candles are in it." He wrote it and the page went from twenty-three to twenty-eight and then to thirty-one, and the three counts of the weeks on that page did not move and were not moved, because they have not moved since the fourth of January and nothing a yard does with a pencil is going to move them.

---

**The pump went out of the shed at about eleven on Wednesday the tenth of May and it has not been out of this shed since Iven Toft hired it for the day at four marks on Wednesday the fifth of April, and a man with money asked for it for two days on Saturday the twenty-second of April and did not get it because the pump was on the mole and this yard was in a coal store, and that refusal is entered against this yard in this yard's own book with no reason against it, because there is nothing to put a reason against.**

The man was in the shed at a quarter past eleven and it was the man from the second lay who had been in the market on the twenty-second of April, and he had come up the hill at eight that morning to ask again and had not gone away, and Nessa Pike had made him a pot of tea and had not charged him for it and was going to mention that in the book whether he liked it or not.

"Two days," he said. "Wednesday and Thursday. Four marks a day, which is the figure off the board and not a figure off a hat. I have been in and out of this harbor on my own account for nineteen years and I have never in my life paid more than four a day for that pump and I am not going to start today, and if you want five I will go up the hill and get a lighterman's and you may have the pump back on Friday."

"Four a day and two days in advance, and the second day is on the Thursday, and the pump comes back on the Friday morning and not before, and if it is not back on the Friday morning I will have it back on the Friday morning," said Nessa Pike.

**"Then I am signing against that," she said, "because somebody has to. This yard is hiring out the only working pump between here and the second lay for two days and it is doing it on a Wednesday in May with a gland on a page and no hose in this harbor, and if we want it back in June we will not have it, and I am putting my name to losing it and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be told later that I did not."**

He counted eight marks onto the bench in four silver and four in the small coin, and said the thing that nobody in that shed wanted said.

"I asked for it on the twenty-second of April and I did not get it, and I did not think about it again until Wednesday morning at eight o'clock, and I want that said on your page and I want it said in the form I am saying it and not in a kind way. **You were in a coal store. That is the whole of it and there is no reason against it and I am not going to be one of four men in this shed who have been let down by this yard, because I have hired that pump four times since the twenty-first of March and three of them went well.** I am not a customer of this yard and I am not going to become one, and the day I am a customer of this yard is the day somebody in this harbor starts being one, and there is a great deal of work in between. **Four marks a day is a fair figure and I would have paid four and a quarter, and I should not have been made to come up this hill twice for it, and you did not do it on purpose and I am not going to pretend you did.**"

The yard entered the whole of it in his own words and read it back to him and he read it after them and did not ask for a mark of it to be taken out.

Eight marks in on the Wednesday and four more on the Thursday, and two days lost, and the yard's whole year is in the fact that the most important object in it came up this coast on somebody else's cart.

---

On Thursday the eleventh the yard's own man walked two miles up the coast road with a handcart and came back in the evening with a casting about the size of a doubled fist in oilcloth, and the creditor's figure of five marks is on the page and the carting is not, and the air in this harbor is a pot that was filled on Monday at a compressor that is not the yard's and carried on a hose that is not the yard's, and about ten feet of the yard's own delivery hose is at the bottom of four fathoms off the inner shoals where it went on the twenty-eighth of April, and there is no hose in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms and a man of this yard wrote that list out at a bench in April and would not put his wage on it.

**Nessa Pike put the casting on the bench and looked at it and did not fit it, and said so.**

"That is the right casting and it is not going on that pump this week, because that pump is on a lighterman's wharf at four marks a day until Friday morning and on Friday morning it comes back and it is going on it then, and the yard is going to be without air for nine days and everybody in this shed already knows that and I have said it out loud twice, so it is in the book twice."

The figures were read back on the Thursday evening at about six, at the head of the dock, and the boy read them out and the yard read them after him and the shipwright read them after that, and the fourth was the chandler's own boy, who had come down the hill for nothing and had not been asked.

*Thirteen marks in a bag on Tuesday the ninth of May at the hour of the wage, and thirty-one on the chandler's note after a casting of five marks in the creditor's own hand on Wednesday the tenth and a fortnight of bread at three the same afternoon, the carting not on the page, and the three counts of the weeks at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and none of them moved and none of them to be moved by anything in this book. Nothing in a tin. **Twelve marks in on Monday the eighth and four straight out on Tuesday the ninth, so thirteen and thirty-five.** Five lines and twenty marks in the wage column, the thirteenth entered on the fourth of May and the fifth of March still the oldest and staying where it is. The fourteenth week paid on the ninth and the fifteenth to be paid on the fifteenth and the sixteenth to fall on the Tuesday. Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office standing, and not reachable by this writ. Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg standing, and four hundred feet of her own warp on the bottom. Twenty marks of iron unsold with a saw kerf in a crown shackle, and four hundred and forty pounds of old iron in three pieces in the same heap behind the shed with a bell-metal gun-port among them, and the heap is not to be sold one mark's worth of without a buyer reading the declaration first, and the gun-port is not weighed and not priced and not offered to anybody.*

**"And the boy has four days left on the stone," the shipwright said, "and the third column has two times at the top of it and one of them is a hand that is going, and I am going to say one thing about that and then I am going to the net loft."**

In the net loft at half past eight on the Thursday night a woman of about fifty was mending a warp of her own and did not put down the needle, and Calder Marr stood in the doorway with a length of the yard's own flax in his hand that he had made badly on the second of May and had made again and had not asked anybody about since, and she looked at it and then went back to the warp.

"I said in a fortnight or not at all," she said, "and it has been nine days, and I am not going to say anything about it tonight, and I have four of your knots in that line and every one of them is a knot I would not have tied, and I will tell you in about a week, and I am not going to be thanked for it either." She went back to the warp. "And the twenty pounds of iron behind your shed with the bell-metal in it has been sitting under a tarpaulin since April, and the man who would know that casting in about four seconds is a hundred yards down this road, and you have not asked him, and I am not going to tell you to ask him."
