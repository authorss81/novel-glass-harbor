# Chapter 120

*The Signature*

He came up the hill on Wednesday the fourteenth of June at about eleven in the morning with a paper in an oilcloth case, and he was fifty-nine or thereabouts and he did not come up the hill out of breath, which told Calder Marr at the door that he had walked it from the bottom of the harbor road and not taken a cart, and that he had wanted to arrive on his own feet.

**He was not from an office. He was not from a court. He was not from the Crown and not from the harbor authority and not a clerk of anything, and he said so in the doorway before he was in, because he had read a form with a clause on it and had learned what a doorway is for.**

"My name is Torren Skell. I have two boats and I trade past this harbor four or five times a year and I have a berth at your north end that I do not use and a boy of my own who knows your shoal. **I am not anybody's clerk and I have not come about the writ and I have not come about the notice and I have not come to complain of anything, and I want you to hear that from me in the doorway before you decide what I am.**"

"Then come in," Calder Marr said, "and I will put the kettle on and I am not going to be able to give you much of an answer, and I would rather you knew that in the doorway as well."

He came in and put the oilcloth case on the bench and took a paper out of it, and the paper was one sheet, written on one side, in a hand that was better than anybody in this harbor's.

**He had read three things and he had put them together correctly, and he said so himself, which was the first thing that made Calder Marr sit down.**

"I have read a fisher's document going up this coast about what this harbor can moor, and a sheet on your market wall with a woman's name and a figure on it, and a page out of this yard's own book with a clause on it that says a finding of worked glass goes up the coast inside four days whether anybody likes it or not. **Three documents, three offices, none of them mine, and every one of them says the same thing in a different hand: this coast is run by about forty people who are not paid and a hundred boats that are.** That is not a scandal. I have seen a hundred coasts like it. What I have not seen is a yard that writes all of it down and puts it on a wall with a figure against it."

"And you have come to buy it," Nessa Pike said.

"I have come to put four things on this bench and I am going to say what I want for them before I say what they are, because I have been told by a man on this coast that a man who wants a thing says what it is for."

---

**"I want the right to use the road. Not the paper. Not a seal. I want to be able to send a man of mine to go and look at those figures, twice a year, with a boat, on your own sheet, and to have a copy of the sheet to do it off, and I do not want your name on mine and I do not want you to know what I find on the third trip and not on the first two.**"

"That is a license," Nessa Pike said.

"That is a license and I will have it written and I will pay for the writing and I will pay for the copy and I will put my name on it and I will not try to hide it."

**He put the four things down one at a time and he did it slowly, the way a man does a thing he has rehearsed in a boat for three days.**

"**Thirty marks.** Not a draft on a bank and not a note. Counted out of a hand in this shed in front of your shipwright and your book, and I have carried it up this hill in a bag and it is in the bag now. **That is about three times everything this yard has earned since the first of February and I know that, because I have read the figures on the same page you have read them on.**"

"**A mooring of mine.** There is one inshore above the north wall that I pay for and do not use, and it is yours for the season, and the north berth is out in your harbor and the ninth seam's mooring has been out of service since the first of May with no end of it entered, and if a man of yours wants to lay a boat alongside at night in a weather that is what it is for. It is a mooring, and if you are not there in a year I will take it back and I will not ask you for anything first."

"**My gear.** A spare set of ground warps, a capstan bar, a hand lead and forty fathoms of line, on my own account, for the season, and I do not want a figure for them, because I have had this conversation with two yards in four years and it goes wrong at the figure and not before it."

"And a line in a book that is not yours." In his own engagement book, in his own hand, in the ordinary way, as a contractor's name against work he has had done — the yard's name, the day, what the work was. **A man in a city can look at my book and see a chain of work with a name on it that is not a name on a wall that anybody could write.** That is all that is. It is not a partnership and it is not a share and it is not a say in anything. It is one line in a book I keep whether you are in it or not."

He put his hand flat on the paper.

"**And that is four things, and the four of them are worth more than the thirty marks and I would do it for two of them.** I am not trying to buy a secret. I am trying to stop being the only person I know who cannot get anybody to go and look."

---

**"You are right about the arithmetic," Calder Marr said. "And I am not going to argue it with you, and I am going to tell you why in nine sentences, and then I am going to give your paper back."**

"Take as long as you like."

"**A license to use a road cannot be given by the people who found it, because the people who found it are not the people who keep it, and this yard has never once been there.**" He put his hand flat on the table on either side of the paper. "**You have done that arithmetic better than I have been doing it since the third of May and I am not going to pretend otherwise, and six men copied that sheet by hand in six boats on the second of May and not one of the six is mine and not one of the six was paid, and that is the only reason there is anything on that wall for you to license.**

"**If your name goes on it, then the road is kept by a man with two boats and a following, and the day you want a second thing out of it you will take it, and I would rather have nothing than that. I am not saying you would; I am saying that the thing you would be buying does not exist yet and cannot be sold, and that if it did exist you would be the wrong man to hold it and I have not got the right man.**

"**I do not know who keeps it, and nobody in this shed knows, and the only person I know of who could answer your question is a man of fifty-four out of a storm belt who came into this harbor on Sunday four days in a weather and told me on my own mole steps that the thing that keeps a channel is a person who will go out and look at it twice a year for nothing, and he has gone home and he is coming back in the autumn.**

"So the answer to your question is that this yard does not keep it, cannot give it away, and does not know who does, and that is the shape of the thing and not a way of saying no. **What I would take instead is a person who will go and look at it, twice a year, for nothing, and does not have to be told why, and if you want to be that person, you may write to me and I will answer on the day it comes, and I am not going to pretend I could pay you for it.**

"**I am not going to thank you for the offer and I am not going to call it a foolish one. And if you come up this hill in a month I will give you the same answer in the same words, and you may write it down.**"

That is nine, and neither of the other two in that shed counted them, and neither of them was asked to.

Skell looked at him for a long moment and then he said the thing that was going to be in this yard's book for the rest of the volume.

**"That is a thing a man says when he has nothing."**

"It is," Calder Marr said.

"You are a line diver with a bag with eight marks in it and a boy off the stone since the fifteenth of May and a note at thirty-one, and you have just turned down thirty marks and a mooring and a man's gear, and you are telling me the alternative is a favor. **I have run two boats for twenty-six years and I have never had a favor off a man with eight marks in a bag in my life. What I have had is men like you telling me what they would take instead, and then not taking it, and then the thing going to somebody who pays money for it.**"

"**I am not going to argue that with you either, because you have been in twenty-six years of it and I have been in nineteen years of it and yours is the longer column.**"

"Right." He put the case under his arm. "I am not going to come up this hill again this month and I am not going to send anybody, and I am not going to write to any office about this, because there is nobody in an office on this coast I would write to. **And I am not going to put a mark against your name anywhere, because I have been on the other side of that and I know what it costs the man who is on it.**"

"You are not going to have one put against yours here either," Nessa Pike said. "There is a book on this bench and everything you have said is going in it in your own words and in ours, and if I read that back to you now you may tell me I have left anything out."

"Read it back," he said. "I have been in this harbor once and I would like to be in it twice."

She read it back, and Calder Marr read it after her, and at the end of it he wrote the refusal on the back of Skell's own paper in his own left hand, in nine sentences, and gave the paper back, and Skell folded it into the oilcloth case at the door and went down the hill on his own feet.

---

**And nothing came in and nothing went out, and the refusal cost the town a recovery, and the recovery was not the yard's.**

Thirty marks would have paid for the warp twice over. It would have paid for a second day of it and a boat's day to do the second day on and the victuals and a hired man and a hired hand and a second pot of air, and it would have paid for the yard's eighth and the harbor's share and left the yard's own twelve on a job that nobody was ever going to sell.

**It is on the page in the yard's own hand and it is the only line in that book this month that is written about a thing that did not happen.**

*That on Wednesday the fourteenth of June instant a man offered this party thirty marks in coin, a mooring above the north wall, his own ground warps, capstan bar, hand lead and line for the season at no figure, and an entry in a book of his own that is not this party's, in exchange for a license to go and look at the road and a copy of the sheet; that this party refused it and gave the paper back with the refusal written on the back of it in nine sentences in the holder's own left hand; and that the reason the refusal costs this harbor a third recovery is that the thirty marks would have paid for about four hundred feet of a woman's own warp on the bottom of this harbor, which is on a market wall with her name and a figure and a date on it since the seventh of June; and that the party entered that it had been offered money for the first time since the first of February and that it is not able to replace that money, and that a yard that cannot replace an offer is not a yard that gets to turn one down out of a principle, and that this yard has turned one down out of a principle and knows exactly what that is worth.*

Nessa Pike read that back and then she put her hand flat on the page, which she does not do.

"**I want it in that last clause that we know what it is worth,**" she said. "**Not because I want to be hard on him. Because if this yard ever says that it chose a principle over a woman's rope, it will be wrong, and it will have been wrong by about a hundred and thirty yards of somebody's best tarred stuff.**"

"It is in it."

"Then I will not say anything else about it and I would like nobody in this shed to say anything else about it either, and I would like us to go and stand somewhere else."

---

Calder Marr went out and stood at the top of the mole's steps above the third seam's mark at about half past four in the afternoon with the wind in the south and about an hour and a half of it, and there was a crate under a tarpaulin nine feet to his left and a bucket under a tarpaulin nine feet to his right, and both of them were weighed down now with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope that had been lent at nothing and entered at nothing.

**He was not thinking about the thirty marks. He was thinking that a man had come up a hill in June and offered him four good things and had been refused for a reason that is true, and that the reason is going to be on a piece of paper in an oilcloth case going somewhere down this coast, and that in four years somebody will read it, and that the woman it is about has got her name and a figure and a date on a wall and four hundred feet of her own rope at the bottom of this harbor, and nobody has told her, and she is not going to be told this week.**

He went down at about six. The shed was shut and the lamp in the net loft was lit and there was nothing to be done, and he did it anyway.

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about seven on the Thursday the fifteenth, and the tide was making and the water off the south end gave the bell its answer a hand's width of a second late, and nobody in that street could place the sound, and it was not taken and it was not written down.

**Eight marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January. Nothing in a tin. **Eight lines and thirty-two marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and three envelopes on a bench in the chest and not among the five rolled in oilcloth, and no man in this yard is going to be the one who carries one.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful. **Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg, undiminished, unpaid, four hundred feet of her own warp still on the bottom of this harbor, and an offer of thirty marks refused in a shed on Wednesday, and she has not been told and is not going to be told this week.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued. **And eight marks and a day of a boat is a craft that recurs, and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years, and it will be eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it.**

The cistern charged three times this month by people nobody pays. The ninth seam out, the north berth out, the third lay full, the tide gang four men short, the light not on, the repair account empty, no boat of this office's own, the *Kittiwake* on the hard, the hard at Ashtide, nineteen winters, the *Sea Wren* at the pile, eleven copies and four unreached holders not asked, a man of the third lay nine winters not found.

A lump of green glass in a bucket and a piece of worked glass of a foot and a half in a crate, nine feet apart on the crown of this mole, in the open air, because the second clause of a notice neither of us wrote will not let them be indoors, and **nine scallops in about three inches seen for the second time and accounted for neither time, and a mark on one face that nobody in that boat identified and that this party is not going to identify.**

**A crew of two and a half and a third column that gets filled late and is not renamed and is not scratched out and is not going to be quietly stopped, and a first of July, a Saturday, four weeks and a day off the first of June, and a mooring with a lease on nothing, and four hundred feet of a woman's own warp at the bottom of this harbor that is nobody's to raise, and on Saturday the seventeenth of June a return falls due about a shard of worked glass that belongs to nobody, in a crate under a tarpaulin on a mole's crown, in the open air, because the law of a town this yard did not write will not let it be in a building.**

**And the yard has three days to write it, and there is no boy in this shed to read it back, and there is no boy in this harbor who reads anything back, and there is not going to be one.**
