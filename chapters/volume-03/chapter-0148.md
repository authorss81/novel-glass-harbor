# Chapter 148

*What a Bell-Archive Is, and the Limit a Person in a Doorway Is Wearing*

Thursday the tenth of August, at about ten in the morning, at the top of the mole's steps above the third seam's mark, in about forty people's hearing, in daylight, in the open air, and the wind in the northwest making about three hours of it.

Four of them came up the steps and stopped and did not go any further, and forty people on a mole's crown in a market let them stand there.

The woman was thirty-eight or thereabouts and had salt in the seams of a coat that was somebody's coat and not hers, and she spoke first, and she said who they were without being asked.

"**I am Nessa Vale of the Sill, and this is my brother-in-law Awn Proude, and this is Tom Kittow, and this boy is Ewan Kittow and he is seventeen and he went into the water on Tuesday and a man of your yard came after him, and I am going to say that at the top of these steps in front of all of you and I am not going to say it again and I am not going to say it to him.

"**The Sill is the weir at the south end of this mole, and the sill stone of it is under four feet of stone at the south end of this mole, and every one of you has walked over it, and a man in this harbor cut his first hundred feet of a mole face and put his name in a stone at the head of it and wrote down where he worked and never once asked what the joints at the foot of the wall were for.

"**And we have been in this harbor since the second of June. We came in on the flood with eleven days' water in us and we put her over on the shoal inside the four hundred yards because we had to, and we have been pumping her ever since, and we did not put a lamp up and we did not fire anything and we did not go to the officer of this harbor and we did not go to that man of a vessel and we did not send a letter, and I am not going to explain why and I am not going to as though there were a reason anybody here would have a right to.**"

---

**And she was asked one question, and it was asked by Nessa Pike, and it was a good question, and the answer took eleven minutes.**

"**What is in the case.**"

"**Five bells,**" said Nessa Vale. "**In a case under the after hatch with a grating over it, and the grating was pinned and the pins were seized, and one of us went down there in June with a hammer to get at them and could not, and that is the last time, and it was in June of this year, and nobody of ours has been in that locker since.**

"**And I am going to tell you what a bell-archive is because a man in a coat has nailed a paper on your market wall with an officer's name at the foot of it that says every part of her is Crown material, and you have all read it, and half of you are standing here working out what is in the boat.**

"**It is not a book. A book is a thing you open. That is five bells with a line cut into the lip of each one, and the line is not a letter in any hand that anybody in this country has ever used, and it is read out — not written down, read out — by whoever has the turn, on a fixed day, in the hall, in front of the whole community, and what it says is what we did, and when, and how many, and who was in it, and what it cost.

"**The oldest of those five is about a hundred and forty years old by the sound of it and it is the only one of the five with a night in it that I was not told as a child. There are four others. The other four are the ones we could not leave behind. We have the rest of them. The rest of them went in a fire in a year I am not going to name in a market, and one of our own was a witness to that fire and she is dead nine years and there is nobody left who heard that fire and one of the bells that went into it.

"**And that is the whole of what is in that boat that is worth anything, and it is worth everything we have, and I am not going to pretend to you this morning that it is worth more than the four of us. It is worth more than the four of us to us, and it is worth nothing whatever to anybody standing on this mole, and both of those are true and I have said them in the same breath and I am not taking either back.**"

---

**And then she took a sheet out of the inside of somebody else's coat and put it in the shipwright's hand, and the shipwright read it out in the open air in about forty people's hearing, and it is in the first column and it is in their own words and it is signed by four people.**

> *We will say what a bell-archive is and who may strike one and what one is for and who keeps it. We will say it in our own words. We will say it once.
>
> *We will not say it in a shed. We will not say it into a book that goes out of this harbor, and we will not say it to a man with a seal, and we will not say it again in a market. A bell-archive is not a thing that is read out. It is a thing that is struck, in a hall, in front of the people it is about, and there is nobody in this town it is about. That is the whole of the limit and we have written it ourselves and we are not going to be asked to take it off.
>
> *We will not say one word about who took her off us. If you ask us that we will answer the way we have answered everybody since the fourth of August, which is that we do not know and we were not there.
>
> *We will not give our names to any office, in any city, in any year. The names are on the sheet for you because you are nine people on a mole and you are not a city. If a name goes up this coast it goes in a file and a file does not strike anything.*

---

Calder Marr read it twice and stood with it in his hand for about a minute and a half and then said the thing that is entered against him.

"**I am going to say this in the open air because I am not going to be the man who says it in a shed.

"**I wanted the second half of it lifted. I wanted it lifted from the first hour I read it and I have wanted it every hour since. I wanted a record of a chain of vessels kept by nine communities with a date against each and a place against each, and I wanted it on a market wall, and I wanted a man in a city to be obliged to look at it.

"**And I am not going to ask them to take it off, and I am not going to try, and the reason is that it is not mine to take off. It is theirs and it says so in the first line and the yard is going to enter that the holder asked for it to be lifted and could not lift it and did not ask again, and that the wanting is in this yard's own book with a date on it, and that in about four years a man is going to come up this hill and ask this party whether it had the whole of it, and the answer is going to be that it asked, and that it was told no by four people on a mole's steps, and that it wrote down that it had asked.**

"**And both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, and I am not going to be the one who says that sentence for the first time, because a woman said it in front of me a quarter of an hour ago on this mole's crown with forty people on it, and she said it first, and she said it better than I could have said it. I said the same shape of a sentence in a room in April about a lead and a hose and a claim window, and I have said it twice since, and I never had anybody write it down, and I did not write hers down either and I am not going to now.

"**And it is going to be said in this harbor by somebody who is not me, and it is not going to be said by me in a market about four people who have lost a boat.**"

---

**And on Friday the eleventh of August, at about ten in the morning, in the market office of Morrow Quay, with the press unlocked under its stone slab because the counting was going on and about eleven people in the room, the page was written out.**

Nobody arranged that either. The woman of the second lay who had the four of them in a room on the Thursday night had asked them, and they had asked her, and the office is open to anybody on a Tuesday and a Friday morning and there is no fee and no clerk and nobody has to ask anybody's permission.

**And Mara Venn was at the long table with a book of her own and a pencil and her calipers on the table beside her, and she was not asked for anything by anybody, and this party did not ask her for anything, and she has entered that in her own book, and that entry is hers and not ours.**

"**Six things,**" said Mara Venn, "**and I am going to say them to this room and not to the four of them, because the four of them did not ask me and are not going to, and if I say them to the four of them it will look as though I had been sent.

"**A cast bell of that size is not cast flat and it is not cast in one piece. There is a maker's mark on the sound bow of each of them and there is a line on the sound bow and the line and the lip are cut with the same tool, which means the same hand, or the same shop, and I cannot tell you which and I am not going to.

"**The two shallow cuts inside the lip of the largest of them are of different ages, and they are of the same order as the two cuts at the second seam of this mole which a conservator of no office put in a book on the sixteenth of July, and I have read that book and I have not put the two together and I am not going to and I would be obliged if nobody in this room put them together either, because I do not have a cast, and two lines on two objects is twice and not a pattern, and a yard that counts a row of nine once is right and a woman with a pencil who counts it twice is a woman with a pencil.

"**The rest of that is a thing I cannot do and it is six marks and two days to have done by somebody who can, and it is not on that table, and it is not in this harbor, and neither is the money.**"

She put her pencil down.

"**And I am going to say the thing that is on the table, and it is the only thing I have got, and it is not worth the six marks. Five bells in a case under an after hatch at about five and a half fathoms, inside the four hundred yards, off a mole nobody in this harbor is allowed to go past on a writ, on a vessel the Crown has claimed whole. There is no instrument in this harbor that reaches that water. There is no money in this harbor that reaches that water. There is no trade on this coast whose work that is.

"**And there is no office in this harbor that will send anybody, because the officer of this harbor authority entered on Sunday the sixth in his own book that there is nothing in his book that will put a boat of his past the outer marks, and he said it out loud on a mole, and he was right.

"**So there are five bells about a mile and a half off your harbor and there is nobody going, and I am not going, and I am not asking anybody to send me, and I am thirty, and I am owed forty marks since the fifteenth of October, and the cost of not being able to do this is not the six marks. It is that I know what they are and I cannot get at them, and that is a thing I have now said out loud in a room in this harbor and I cannot unsay it, and I would like somebody in this room to understand that the not-saying costs me and not the boat.**"

---

**And the page went out of the market office at about half past eleven with a woman of the second lay carrying forty copies of it in a bundle, and there is no price on it and there is no scale on it and there is nowhere on it to write a letter to, and a shilling changed hands at the foot of the dock for a copy of the *Sallow Reach's* own printed sheet, and that shilling is not this yard's income and is not in the bag and will not be.**

**Nothing came in or out of that yard on Thursday the tenth or on Friday the eleventh, and the reason is on the page and it is a working reason: a yard of two and a half people that spent three days in a boat past the outer marks and three days writing down what it did there has not sold a day at the second seam's own face in ten days, and the men who hire a day want it on a Monday or a Tuesday, and there has been no Monday and no Tuesday in the ten days.**

**Nothing in a bag. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved since the fourth of January, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid and of no use because the thing after the gland is the hose, and the tin empty since the twenty-eighth of April. Sixteen lines and sixty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and eleven envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the fifteenth of August. And three marks owing to the master of the *Sallow Reach* for a day of the eighth, and three marks is not nothing and has a date on it and is in the first column.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole to a boy of twenty-two who cannot do the thing he was kept for, and the first column says so in this party's own hand with a date on it, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand. Forty marks to the woman sitting at the long table, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not paid this week and not asked for. Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, in no sentence together in this book and not in one anywhere. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, unpaid since the twenty-third of May, unargued, not paid, not asked about, and no second going out bought and none to be bought inside four years. **And four people of the Sill who have no boat, no wages and about nine marks between them, and who have not asked this yard for anything, and whom this yard is not going to give anything to, and both of those are correct, and the yard has entered that it would have liked to and had nothing.**

**And a case of five bells about a mile and a half off the outer marks in about five and a half fathoms of water, with a grating lid pinned over it and the pins seized, and no instrument in this harbor that reaches that water and no money in this harbor that reaches that water and no trade on this coast whose work it is, and a limit written by four people on a mole's steps in their own words with four names on it and the word *Harbors* not on it anywhere.**
