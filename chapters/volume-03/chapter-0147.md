# Chapter 147

*What Was on the Gunwale, and What Was in the Water*

Tuesday the eighth of August, at about eleven in the morning, in the shed, the wage went in first.

Four marks, in Calder Marr's own left hand, the week written on the outside of the envelope in the same hand, and the envelope into the bench chest, and it was not among the six rolled in oilcloth and it was not going anywhere this week and nothing was going to carry it. **Sixteen lines and sixty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and eleven envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere in eleven weeks, and the reason is the reason it has been since the second of May: a wage is not a reason to go to a door.**

Then the air went out of the bag, two pots at the harbor authority's own compressor on that office's own hose at this yard's own two-mark rate, four marks, and they were filled at about a quarter past twelve and they were in the boat by one.

**And at the mole, at about ten past one, Perry Ollett came alongside the *Sallow Reach* and took his figure off the board.**

Six marks. The boat, the day, and two of his men, and the air out of his own compressor at no figure, and no victuals on it. The wage had gone at about eleven and the air had gone at about a quarter past twelve and **there were seven marks in the bag at the mole at ten past one on the eighth of August**, and the yard paid three of the six at the mole in front of the man who hired the day, the way it pays every other man at the mole, and **three marks did not go, and is a debt to a man of Ashtide with a date on it, and is in the first column, and this party is not going to pretend that three marks is nothing.**

"**That is the first time in four years I have taken part payment for a day's work and been glad of it,**" Ollett said. "**And I want two things said and one of them is not about money. I want it said that I said six and you paid three and both of those are the arrangement. And I want it said that the six did not include the air because the air was mine and it was in her already.**

"**And I am going to say the third thing now so that I do not have to say it at six o'clock. That is a boy off her foredeck and he is going into the water on the seaward side, and if he does I am going in after him with a line off my own bow and not off yours, and I am not going to be the man who has taken a line from a boat with a pot in it, and you are going to want my line and I am going to say no.**

"**And if it comes to it I am going to have a boat of mine alongside a boat of yours with two of my men on her deck and one of them on her water, and that is a thing that has gone wrong in other waters and everybody who has been in a boat for thirty years knows exactly what it is, and I have thought about it all morning and I cannot make it better by saying anything else.**"

"**It cannot,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to argue with a man who has thought about it longer than I have.**"

---

**The six were read out in order at the boat, in daylight, off the north end of the mole, at about twenty past one, with the shipwright holding the line, and they go in the same words every time this yard goes in the water, and the reason for that is in the yard's own book and is that a man who reads his own six differently has a reason for it and does not know what it is.**

Named tender who is not the diver. Independent line, on the boat's own bow. Fixed air limit on the slate, set at the boat by the diver and entered as the diver's, and it was seventeen and he set it himself and would not let the holder raise it. Seated named weight. A cutoff the tender can pull without asking anybody, and it is hers, and the word is *Enough*. And a stated reason, entered in the yard's own book before anybody went in the water, in the holder's own left hand, unedited, **with the clerk not present, and the absence entered rather than filled in, and with the further fact entered that there is no clerk to be present because there is no instrument to be present for: the writ of the first of May is bounded by the outer marks and does not reach the ground this party is going to, and the reporting clause is on the writ, and no office in this harbor or any other has issued anything for that ground, and the party is going on its own account and has entered that.**

*That on Tuesday the eighth of August instant this party goes on its own account, and not on the account of the harbor authority of this harbor or of any other office, to a vessel named HEARTHWAKE lying in about seven fathoms inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks of this harbor, being about a mile and a half off, with four persons in her; that the purpose stated is to get four persons off her and to nothing else, and that no part of that vessel is to be raised, worked, taken, entered or removed, and that the party is going there to take people off and is going to leave with people off and is going to take nothing; that one man of this party goes in the water and the tender is the shipwright of this party, who has never been in the water; that the limit is the holder's own and is seventeen minutes and he set it himself at the boat; that a boat of another crew is alongside on her own account and on her own master's account at a figure named out loud and part paid, and the reason that is entered is that a crew of this harbor cannot put air on that water in any other way, and that the reason it is entered is not to make it a partnership and this party has entered that it is not a partnership; and that the sixth day, the third, the fourth and the fifth of August are shut on the third lay and are not a reason and are not used as one.*

---

**And the tide was right at about two and they were on the water at about four minutes past two, and the outer marks went by on the port bow at about a quarter past, and the four-hundred-yard line was not marked on anything, and they went past it anyway and the shipwright said the time out loud, and the two men of the *Sallow Reach* said nothing at all, and the third column was not filled that night, and the reason it was not filled is entered in this yard's own book on the Wednesday night and not here.**

The imperial line was about three miles further on and nobody went near it and nobody looked for it.

---

He went down at about ten past two with the pot and the slate and a lamp on his shoulder, and the water over the after hatch was about five and a half feet at the top of the tide and the hatch was a grating under a tarpaulin and the tarpaulin had a corner gone off it, **and he thought about a mole's crown in this harbor with a corner gone off a tarpaulin since the seventeenth of June, and he did not write that down then and has not written it down now and it is entered in the second column with a reason against it and the reason is that a man who starts noticing a coincidence in the middle of six feet of water over a wreck is a man who has stopped working.**

What came up under his hand inside the case was not a bell and it was not a join. It came into his palm as a break, and the break was in the garboard of the *Hearthwake*, three feet forward of the case, where a plank had been cut and let go and mended over, and the mending was on the mending, and the difference between the first cut and the cut that came on top of it came into his hand the way a change of angle comes into a hand, which is without any picture at all and is simply *that way and not this way*.

**And it told him there were mendings under mendings. And it told him nothing whatever about when, or in what order, or by whose hand, or why a boat of a community of this coast was lying in seven fathoms with a case of five bells in her, and this time he did not supply any of it, and he has entered that he did not supply any of it, and the reason he did not is in the second column and the reason is that a man who has been wrong in public in a market once about a word like *chain* does not get to be the man who supplies the rest of a sentence about a boat with people in her.**

He came up on the first call and said one thing, and the thing was that the hatch was good and the case was under it and it was heavy, and the shipwright wrote it on the slate and did not improve on it.

---

**Three calls. Seventeen minutes each. The limit was his and he kept it three times out of three, and the pot was at about a third at the second call, and the *Sallow Reach's* compressor put a hundred and forty pounds of air into it from about thirty feet away and it made a noise like a man clearing his throat.**

The case was about four feet by two and the lid was a grating and the grating was pinned and the pins were seized and he got two of them with the bar and the third one with the flat of a spanner, and the lid came off, and there were five bells in it.

The smallest was about the size of a fist. The largest was about a foot across the mouth. **They were not a set. There was nothing about them that belonged to a music and every one of them had a line cut into the lip of it that was not a letter in any alphabet that anybody on this coast uses, and he put his hand on the cold metal of the big one and it was cold and it was wet and it said nothing, and he did not strike it, and he has entered that he did not strike it and that there is no instrument in this harbor and none in this boat and the reason he did not strike it is that a bell is not a thing you find out what by making a noise on.**

**And on the lip of the largest of the five, on the inside, where a man's thumb goes when he holds it, there were two shallow cuts across the metal and they were of different ages, and he entered the two cuts and did not enter what they meant.**

They got four of the five in a bight of the *Sallow Reach's* own warp on the third call and the line came up to the gunwale of the yard's own boat and the two men of the *Sallow Reach* had it on the stern and were hauling, and the case stood on its end in about three feet of water with the lid against the *Hearthwake's* own rail, and the four bells in it were half out of the water and the fifth was under, and it was the heaviest single thing any of them had ever had hold of, and it was not coming up in one haul and they all three of them knew it.

---

**And at about twenty past three the grapnel on the *Vigil's* tender came tight, and the *Hearthwake* went over another three foot to starboard, and a boy of about seventeen with an anchor chain in his hands went off her foredeck on the seaward side into about seven fathoms.**

He shouted. It was a shout and not words, and it came from the water and it came off the deck of the *Hearthwake* at the same time, and for about a second Calder Marr could not tell which of the two places it had come from, and that second and a half is the whole of what this party paid for the four of them and it is not a price anybody chose to pay in advance.

He had a bight of warp round his forearm with the case on the end of it and the two men of the *Sallow Reach* were hauling on it and the case was a foot above the water.

He let it go.

**It did not swing away. It went down the way a thing goes down when the thing under it goes down, which is straight, and it went down about four feet and the grating lid was the last of it, and one of the men of the *Sallow Reach* said a word in his own language and the other one did not say anything at all, and Calder Marr was in the water in about a second and a half and had the boy by the collar of an oilskin with a chain in his other hand and the chain went down and kept going.**

---

It took nineteen minutes and nobody in that water was in any danger of drowning, and the thing that nearly finished it was not the water. It was that the *Hearthwake* was rolling about a foot and a half on a swell with a seven foot list and a three foot roll on top of it, and twice the boy went under the *Sallow Reach's* counter, and once Calder Marr lost the boy's collar and had him again by the chain, and the third time the woman came off her after the boy with a boathook in her hand and did not go in but stood in her own doorway and hooked the boy and held him on while the man of the *Sallow Reach* took his line off his own bow and got a bight round the boy's chest from the outside, exactly as he said he would on the mole at ten past one.

**And the fourth one, the other man, got off her on his own at about ten past four, over the seaward rail, on his own account, with a line of his own round his own waist, and he had it because the boy of the seventeenth had put it there before he went over the rail, and that is a thing a crew does.**

Nessa Pike never said the word. **That is on the page and it is entered, because a tender's stop is not a request and a tender's stop is not a decision either, and she had her hand on the cutoff the whole time and she has entered that she did not pull it and that the reason she did not pull it is that a man of thirty-two going to a boy of seventeen was not going to be stopped by a woman in a boat in about nineteen minutes, and if it had been twenty-five she would have pulled it and she has entered that too.**

---

**And at about two o'clock on Wednesday the ninth of August the *Sallow Reach* came alongside the *Hearthwake* under her own steam and took four people off her in about twenty minutes, and one of them would not go until she had taken the pump handle off its shaft and left it in the pump, and one of them went across and got the boy first, and they came up the mole's steps at about six in the afternoon in front of about forty people, and the mole head's light was not on behind them, and four people from the *Sallow Reach* carried a blanket and somebody's mother.**

Nell Ardery was on the steps. She had not come up the hill. She came down to the bottom of them and she did not say one word to any of the four and she went and got a woman of the second lay who had a room, and the four of them went up the boards and into that room and the door was shut.

**And the *Vigil*'s tender lay at about two hundred yards off the whole time with a grapnel on a cable and did not take a hand off a line, and the *Vigil* herself stood off two and a half miles all of Wednesday with her boats, and the notice of the fourth of August was still on the market wall of this harbor with the yard's own fourth return under it, and the whole of that is entered and none of it is a finding.**

---

**And on the Wednesday night the second column was filled in the holder's own left hand and read back, and the word is not in it.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered in the holder's own left hand on Wednesday the ninth of August instant at about nine at night in this shed, and the word that is in the fifth line of that form is in a line above this one and is not put in this one and the four above it have no name in them and are not struck out: that on the eighth of August instant at about ten past two, in about six feet of water over a wreck with a pot and a lamp and the tender in the boat, the holder could not for the whole of the first call down tell which of two sounds was the boat's; and that at about twenty past three on the same day, with a case of five bells half out of the water on a line round his own forearm and a boy of seventeen in seven fathoms off his own gunwale, the holder could not tell for about a second and a half whether a shout came from the water or from the deck; and that the second of those is the reason a case of five bells is at the bottom of the sea about a mile and a half off this harbor inside the four hundred yards, and that the party is not going to soften that and is not going to put it in a second column twice.*

*And the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that no man on a mole can check which of his own ears a sound came in through, and the party has made this line twice now for that reason and is not going to make it a third time for the same thing and is not going to stop making it.*

---

**And nothing was raised on the ground of the outer marks and no finding of worked glass was made on either day, and a basket that is half out of a hatch in a boat is not a raising, and this party has entered that it raised nothing and is not going to let anybody call a thing a raising that came up out of a hatch in a boat and went back down into a boat, and the reporting clause of the first of May does not reach that ground, and there is no fifth return and there is not going to be one, and a dated line is in this book for the eighth and the ninth of August saying that this party was past the outer marks on its own account and raised nothing and told no office and will tell no office, and that line is a fact about this yard's book and is in no office's book and is not sent to anybody.**

**And the third column was not filled on Tuesday the eighth of August, and this is the first time since the second of May that it has not been filled on a day, and it is entered here in the first column with the reason, which is that a day with a boat in the water off the outer marks is not a day this yard sits down in a shed, and the party has entered the further fact that it does not know who will fill that column next and has not appointed anybody and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Wednesday night, and a column that nobody fills is a column somebody else fills, and the party has entered that it knows that and is doing it anyway.**
