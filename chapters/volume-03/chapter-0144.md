# Chapter 144

*Seven Foot of List, and a Tender That Came Alongside to Hold Her Down*

Wednesday the second of August, at seven in the morning, the moorings of the third lay were shut, and the shut went up on a board on the head of the third lay's own pier in a hand that was not a fisher's, and about thirty men read it standing up, and one of them said the date out loud, which was the date they had all been told in a market on Sunday the twenty-sixth of July by a man of their own lay who had not asked anybody for anything.

**A day on the dump, on a hirer's own account, being ground the writ of the first of May names in its own ground, four casts of the hand lead's line and a barrow of the dump's own stone moved six feet.**

Gross on the board thirteen. The eighth two. The share two. A hired hand three. The victuals of the day one and rope one, the two of them together two. The three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being nine out of thirteen, and the four is in the bag and the nine never was. **Seven marks in a bag.**

The six were not read out because nobody went in the water, and the yard has entered that they were not read out and that the reason is that the six are the method of going in the water and the two of them were not going in.

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon, from the crown of the mole above the third seam's mark, with a glass, the whole of the third lay and the inner shoals and a mile and a half of water outside the outer marks, the two of them watched a boat with people in her go down by the head and take the ground, and neither of them could do one single thing about it, and it took about four minutes.**

She had been lying with a list of four foot to starboard. She came up about half a fathom on the flood — that is what a boat on a shoal does at the top of a tide — and the lift came under her holed bow, and she went down by the head, and the list went to seven foot in about two minutes and the sea came over her foredeck and stopped there.

"**A tender,**" said the shipwright.

The tender was lying inside the four hundred yards, about two hundred yards off the *Hearthwake*'s port quarter, and she had a line out. Calder Marr put the glass up and looked at the line and then looked at the tender's bow and then at the water between them.

"**That is a grapnel,**" he said. "**That is a grapnel on her own anchor cable and a tender with a winch, and he has put it on because she was pumping nine days and he wanted her held, and he has just held her.**"

"**Then he has killed her.**"

"**Then he has killed her,**" said Calder Marr, "**and he has done it on a Crown claim, in sight of a working harbor, with a paper on the market wall that says no assistance will be rendered, and the paper is nailed over a return of ours with a place and an hour and a keeper and limits on it, and a man in a city can put the two side by side whenever he wants, and I am not going to pretend I cannot see them side by side from here.**"

The tender's line came tight. The *Hearthwake* did not move again. Somebody in that glass was still on her pump, because the pump handle kept coming up and going down at the same rate it had been coming up and going down for nine days, and the two of them watched it for about four minutes without saying anything.

---

**And nothing was entered in the first column about the tender and the grapnel for about an hour and a half, and then it was, and the reason for the hour and a half is on the page.**

*That at about half past two in the afternoon on Wednesday the second of August instant, from the crown of this mole in about nine people's hearing on a mole's crown, being about a mile and a half off — which number is entered separately and is not a part of this line — a vessel of about forty tons lying inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks with a list of four foot to starboard came up about half a fathom on the flood and took the ground by the head and settled with about seven foot of list to starboard, the sea being over her foredeck; and that a small vessel of about two tons which had lain at about two hundred yards off her port quarter since the twenty-eighth of July instant had a line out to her and the line came tight and she did not move again; and that this party has entered the tender, the line and the hour because the tender's line and the tender's order came from a vessel of the Crown and the tender's master is not a person in this harbor; and that this party has entered that it did not go to the officer of this harbor authority on that day and did not ask that office to do anything and has entered the reason, which is that an instrument of that office which would put a boat of that office past the outer marks would carry that officer's acknowledgement at that officer's cost, and there are eight of those and both of this year's are spent and this party did not ask for a ninth and is not going to; and that this party did not report the grounding to any office and is not going to; and that the reason is that this party raised nothing on that day and no finding of worked glass was made and there is no return to make and a thing said to a clerk is a thing that goes on a page, and this party has entered that it made no report on the second of August and knows exactly what a reader of a page in five years is going to make of it.*

**And the head-count is entered separately, because this yard does not put a number of people in the middle of a finding: that there were nine, or there were ten, and the yard has not checked and is not going to.**

---

**And the *Vigil* herself was lying two and a half miles off the point, at anchor, bow to the weather, and Calder Marr looked at her for about four minutes through a glass at fifty feet and could make out one thing about her, and entered it, and it is in the first column and it is the only sentence this yard has written about the *Vigil* in five months.**

*That on Wednesday the second of August instant, from the crown of this mole, at about three in the afternoon, a vessel of the Crown of about two hundred tons and a three-masted schooner in the rig of about thirty years ago was observed lying at anchor off this coast about two and a half miles off the point and beyond the outer marks; and that she was hove to and had not been seen to move between the time she was first made out on the twenty-eighth of July instant and the third of August instant; and that this party has entered no other observation of that vessel and has not asked for one and is not going to, and the reason is that a man who writes down what he has seen of a vessel of the Crown from a mile and a half with a glass is a man who has begun to keep a watch, and a yard that keeps a watch is a yard that has stopped being a yard and become something else, and this yard entered on the fifth of July that it is not going to be a party to the naming of a road and it is not going to be a party to the keeping of a watch either, and the second of those was not in that entry and is now.*

**And the tide gang came past at about four with the line-marking pot and the four of them, and there should be eight, and the two of them stopped and looked at the two of them and the two of them looked at the two of them, and about nine people were on that crown and nobody said one word to anybody, and it went in the first column in the shipwright's hand as a fact about a crown and not as a conversation.**

---

**And on Thursday the third of August the cistern was charged for the fourteenth time, on the rota, at no figure, by this party, and the gate took three men from ten until about two, and the cistern has held since the nineteenth of June and this party is not going to be thanked for it.**

Nothing came in or out of that yard on Thursday, and thirty-one stands on the chandler's note and has not been re-written since the fourth of January, and the three counts of that page are twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and none of them has moved, and the tin is empty and the tin has been empty since the twenty-eighth of April, and **fifteen lines and sixty marks stand in the wage column with the fifth of March the oldest and ten envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere.** No delivery hose. The longest thing of hose in this harbor about nine fathoms. Two pots of air this month at this yard's own two-mark rate and they are the whole of the air in this harbor, and the two of them are in a locker in a shed and have been since the twenty-second of July, and the compressor's gland on this yard's own pump is fitted and unpaid at five marks and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose. Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she is in this harbor on Friday mornings at about ten in a room open to anybody, and the party did not arrange that and has not asked her for anything since the twenty-third of July and is not going to. Eight shillings and tenpence to each of two women, in no sentence together. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued. The ninth seam out with an end entered for the first of September. The north berth out. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The mole-head light not on and the repair account empty since the middle of February. No boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. The hard at Ashtide, unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters in a naval file, in a letter, and in a list. The *Sea Wren* at the pile, and this party has not thanked him. **And four days of the third lay shut from yesterday, and the price of them is on the third lay and not on this party, and the man who pays it said the number out loud in a market in forty people before this party had heard of any of it.**

---

**And at about five in the afternoon on the Thursday a woman came up the hill who had not been up it in a fortnight and who was sixty-odd and whose mother was on the Sill, and she did not come into the shed, she stood in the doorway with her basket.**

"**I have been up the coast road and back,**" Nell Ardery said, "**and I have been to the two men of the third lay who are on that boat's own work and I have asked them two questions and I have not offered either of them anything and they have not asked me for anything.

"**The first is how many are in her. There are four. A woman, two men, and a boy of about seventeen, and the four of them have been in her since she came on the ground in June and they pump in watches and they eat and they sleep and they have not been off her.

"**The second is how long. And this is the part I came up here for, and I am going to say the figure and then I am going to stop talking and let you have it.

"**They can pump her with what is in her until about the fourth day of August. After that the water comes in faster than two men and a boy and a woman can shift it with a pump and buckets, and that is not a figure they made up. It is a figure they have, because they have been doing it nine days and they can see what is left in the pump's own well.**"

---

Nessa Pike wrote it in the first column in her own hand with the hour and the name and read it back, and Calder Marr read it back after her, and then she wrote the second line under it, which is the line that cost this yard something to write.

*That on Thursday the third of August instant, at about five in the afternoon, a woman of the second lay gave this party a figure of the fourth day of August in respect of a vessel with four persons in her lying inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks; and that the party has entered the figure, the hour, the name and the two men of the third lay it came from, without alteration and without shortening; and that the party has entered that it did not ask that woman one question about the four persons, their names, their trade, their ages, where they came from or what they were carrying; and that the reason is that a party which has said out loud in a market that it is not going to ask a man a single question about a boat has got to be able to say it about a woman as well, and it said it about a woman on the twenty-sixth of July and it is saying it now, and the three reasons are the same three reasons and the first of them is that the answer to every one of those questions is a thing that could be used against the people in the boat, and this party has learned what a form is in the last three days and it is not going to become one.*

**And the yard has a day on Friday the seventh of August booked on the second seam's own face on a hirer's own account and it was not cancelled, and the party went and did it, and the reason it was not cancelled is on the page and it is that a day on the second seam at about three marks is three of the ten, and there is a tide at the second seam's own face at about ten past one on Friday the seventh and a man has to be on the water at ten past one on Friday the seventh to be of any use to a boat at two o'clock off the outer marks four days later, and the tide does not care what is on the crown of the mole at the time.**
