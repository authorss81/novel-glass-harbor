# Chapter 143

*Thirteen Mondays, and the Thing He Was Kept For*

Monday the thirty-first of July. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was thirteen Mondays, and there was no sitting and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it.

**And the cistern was charged for the thirteenth time, on the rota, at no figure, and not by this party.** Two men of the second lay came past at about ten in the morning with a barrow of stone in it and put it down and gave about fifty minutes, and a man of the third lay who is not a member of any office in this harbor gave about forty, and none of the three would take anything and none of them were thanked and this party was not there. **Thirteen walks now stand on that office's own page at no figure and the cistern behind the engine house has not gone dry since the nineteenth of June.**

A day at the second seam's own face on a hirer's own account, six casts of the hand lead's line and a line laid out with two pins and a rule, and nobody in the water.

Gross on the board thirteen. The eighth two. The share two. A hired hand three. The victuals of the day one and rope one. All three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being nine out of thirteen and the yard's being four. **Seven marks in a bag**, and they are the first money to come into that bag since Friday the twenty-second of July.

---

**And on Tuesday the first of August the wage went in at about eleven in the morning, four marks, in Calder Marr's own left hand, because there is nobody else in that shed who writes, and the week was written on the outside of the envelope in the same hand, and the envelope went into the bench chest and is not among the six rolled in oilcloth and is not going anywhere this week and nothing is going to carry it, and the reason is on the page and it is the same reason it has been since the second of May: a wage is not a reason to go to a door.**

**Three marks in a bag. Fifteen lines and sixty marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and ten envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere in ten weeks.**

---

**And at about half past eleven on the Tuesday morning, with the market not in yet, Calder Marr took the pencil and put a line in the first column, and he read it back and the shipwright read it back after him, and there was nobody else in that shed.**

*That the thing Bevin Rook was kept for in this yard is reading a position back out of a book and putting it into a slate for a man in a boat, and that he did it for two hundred days, and that on Thursday the fourth of May he read back four figures and got all four right, and that on Thursday the eleventh of May he read back two and got one of the two wrong, and that on the fifteenth of May he was put off the stone at a date this party named in a room in front of a clerk of the circuit office before anybody asked this party for a date, and the reason entered on that day was that his hand has been going since the fourth of April and a bonesetter's book says a boat hand in June.*

*And that he has not read a position back since the fifteenth of May and is not going to and cannot, and that this party has entered that he cannot and has entered the date and has entered that it did not ask him, and is not going to, and is not going to hire a boy to do it and call that the same thing, and is not going to put it in the second column where a reason against it would turn it into a complaint.*

*And that he is twenty-two years old; that the forty marks of his claim stand whole and are not reduced and are not asked to be reduced; that a column in this book is not a wage and this party is not going to pretend that keeping a column is keeping a man; and that the head of the third column carries two times in his hand and this party has not scraped them out and is not going to and a request to scrape them out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

Nobody said anything about it for about a minute, and the shipwright put her hand flat on the page the way she does when she is not going to improve on a thing, and Calder Marr said that was the whole of it and there was nothing to add to it and that was right, and they did not go on to anything else, and that is entered.

---

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Monday and on the Tuesday, from about eight until about six on the Monday and from about nine until about four on the Tuesday, with his back to the room and a cough that came every forty minutes, and he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing, and neither brother went over to him, and it is a single line in the first column and either brother can check it.

---

**And the third column was filled on the Tuesday evening, with the two times at the head of it and the lateness against them, and it is the ninth entry in that column since the second of May and the ninth since the boy went off the stone, and the party is not going to draw anything out of nine figures, for the reason it gave when there were four of them: a yard which begins to keep a figure on its own lateness is a yard that has stopped keeping a figure on its work.**

*That the entry was read on the steps of the mole at about seven in the evening on Tuesday the first of August and was written in this book at about eleven the same night, the difference being about four hours, which is the longest difference this column has run since the fourth of April, and which the party has entered and is not going to account for, and whose account, if there is one, belongs to a man who is not on this stone.*

---

**And the argument on the Monday evening was not about money and was not about air and it was the only one of the four arguments this month that Calder Marr lost, and it went like this.**

"**We should say her name in a market,**" the shipwright said, "**and we should say that we know she is pumping, and we should say it on the boards on Saturday, and we should say that this yard has no instrument out there and has ten marks short of the day.**

"**And I am telling you I have thought about that for a full day and I have got to the end of it and the end of it is a form. That is what it is. It is a form about a boat with people in her, made out in a street, by a yard with a writ and a Crown attachment on it and no air. In four years there is a page in a file in a city that says this yard stood on the boards of Morrow Quay on the second of August and made an announcement about a vessel it had not been to.**"

"**Then what do you want to do.**"

"**I want to do the work and I want to be quiet about it, and I want the quietness to be entered with a reason against it, and the reason is that a thing said in a street about people in a boat is a thing that can be used against the people in the boat, and I have thought about that for a full day and it is the only opinion I have got this month that I have not changed once.**"

"**Agreed,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I am going to write the date on it, because in about four years somebody is going to find out that this yard said one thing about a boat in a market on the twenty-sixth of July and nothing at all about her on the second of August while it was going, and the nothing is going to look like concealment, and it is not concealment, it is a rule, and the rule needs a date on it.**"

---

**And that evening the two columns were read out at the head of the dock with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, and the wind was up behind them and the mole head's light was not on, and it is the whole of the three days.**

Three marks in a bag. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose, and the tin empty since the twenty-eighth of April. **Fifteen lines and sixty marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, ten envelopes on a bench and not one of them has been anywhere, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the eighth of August.** **No money has come into this yard out of any office since the eighth of May and no office in this harbor is going to pay this yard a mark.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, unpaid, not reduced, not asked to be reduced, **and the first column now says in this party's own hand and with a date on it that he cannot do the thing he was kept for and that this party is not going to ask him whether he could, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand and are not being taken out this month or any month.** Forty marks to a conservator of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful, and not one mark of it paid out of anything that has come into this yard since the eighth of May. Eight shillings and tenpence to each of two women, undiminished, unpaid, **in no sentence together in this book, and the woman whose warp came up on Friday has been paid by a man of Ashtide and is owed eight shillings and tenpence by this yard and the two figures are not one figure and neither of them has moved.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it, and that standing sentence is unreversed.

**And the harbor's own gear is on the harbor's own account and has had no boat of its own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April, and there is no boat of this office's own that could have been on that water in any of the last nine days, and this party has entered that in the first column and has entered that it did not go to the officer of this harbor authority and ask for one, and the reason is that an instrument that would put a boat of this office's past the outer marks would carry the reeve's acknowledgement at the reeve's cost, and there are eight of those and both of this year's are spent and there is no ninth and this party has entered that it did not ask for one and is not going to.**

The ninth seam out of service with an end of it entered for the first of September. The north berth out. The third lay full, and a full is not a repair, **and the moorings of the third lay are shut for four days from tomorrow for the examination of this mole's footing, and the man who pays for that is the man of the third lay who said the number out loud in a market in forty people on the twenty-sixth, and this party did not ask for it and is not going to be thanked for it and is not going to use it.** The tide gang four men short, and a man of that gang on Saturday the twenty-ninth of July gave this party a list and a half of it nine days late, in the open air, and gave his name, and is in the first column. The cistern charged for the thirteenth time at no figure, and this party did not walk it, and the reason is on the page. The mole-head light is not on, and has not been on since the third lay went dark in March, and the repair account of the office that owns it has been empty since the middle of February and is not going to be filled this year. The fire party's hose will not be rolled until the autumn. The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle under her. The hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters in a naval file, in four lines of a letter, and in a list in a city. The *Sea Wren* at the pile, and this party has not thanked him and has not been asked to. A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found, one line in this book, and this yard has not gone.

**And a Crown notice nailed over this yard's own fourth return on Friday the twenty-eighth of July, over a printed sheet that a man of Ashtide put up on Monday the twenty-seventh in front of about thirty people and did not know was going to be covered, and the notice has four days in it and the same hand on it as the reporting clause, and it says that no assistance will be rendered to a vessel with people in her, and it is on a market wall in a working harbor, and it is a nail and a sheet and it came up this coast before the tide did.**

**And there is no writ past the outer marks and no clause past the outer marks and no instrument of any office past the outer marks, and the party has entered that it knows the imperial line is about three miles beyond that boat and that no person, no boat and no lead of this yard is going anywhere near it, and that the full Second Bell license is not granted to anybody in this harbor and is not approached and is not going to be approached by this yard in this season, and that the party has entered that it would not know what to do with it if it were.**

**And ten marks is the cost of one day at the outer marks and this yard has three, and the next of the four is Tuesday, and the four days the third lay is shut are the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth of August, and nobody in this yard has said any of those numbers out loud in a market and every one of them is in this book.**
