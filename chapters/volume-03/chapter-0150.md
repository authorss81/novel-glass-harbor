# Chapter 150

*Under a Naval Paper, and the Wage of the Fifteenth*

Monday the fourteenth of August, at eight in the morning, the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and it came down bare, and that was fifteen Mondays, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and there has not been a sitting in that room since Saturday the twelfth.

**And the cistern was not charged, and the reason is on the page in the yard's own hand, and it is a working reason and not a virtue.**

*That the rota falls on a Monday and a Thursday and that this party has walked it thirteen times since the end of May at no figure; that on Thursday the tenth instant both persons of this party were at the top of this mole's steps from ten in the morning with forty people on the crown, and on Monday the fourteenth instant they were in the press of the market office from nine until about two with a sheet; and that the gate takes four hours with three men and this party has two and a half, and that this party has not walked that gate on either of those days and has entered that it did not; and that the cistern has held since the nineteenth of June and this party is not going to be thanked for that and is not going to use it for anything.*

---

**And the sheet was finished at about two in the afternoon on the fourteenth. It is one sheet in the harbor authority's own paper with no price on it and no scale on it and nowhere on it to write a letter to, and it carries three things and a limit, and this is what is on it.**

> *THE PLACE OF EVERY COPY OF THIS SHEET, as far as this yard is able to state it. It is copied at the foot of this dock, on the day it goes up, at no price, by anybody who asks. Forty went out of the market office press on Friday the eleventh and one went up this coast in a bundle with a woman of the second lay, and copies are in about sixty boats of this harbor and of the first, second and third lays and of Ashtide, going up and down this coast as ordinary cargo. One copy is in a city going to press, and this yard was told that in advance in the ordinary post at no cost and cannot stop it. Four of the eleven copies of the public sheet of the fourteenth of October are not reached and have never been reached, this yard has not asked and is not going to, and the list of the eleven is on a wall in this harbor since the third of May with a shilling against it.*
>
> *THE NINE PLACES, AS THIS YARD IS ABLE TO STATE THEM, WITH A DATE AGAINST EACH. This sheet does not say what they are. It does not call them a chain and it does not call them a compact, and the word Harbors is not on this sheet anywhere.*
>
> *Six of the nine are places on this harbor's own second seam, in six entries of a builder's day-book of the port of Ashtide in the press of the market office, in a builder's hand and not a clerk's, kept by a man who was paid by the foot and wrote down where he worked and nothing else. They are: forty feet; seventy-three feet; one hundred and forty-one feet; two hundred and ten feet; two hundred and seventy-nine feet; three hundred and forty-eight feet; each of them at the second seam. The builder's name is cut into a stone at the mole head. The book gives a day against each entry and no year in any hand this yard can read, and this sheet gives the day and says the year is not on it.*
>
> *Three of the nine are in books and have no joint under them. They are this harbor's own weir, called the Sill, in a weir-book in the same press, in a fisher's hand, with a night in a westerly and eleven hundred and forty fish and a lost boat, and a repair to that weir's sill stone in the same hand four years after, and the third of the six places above lying a foot and four inches below that sill stone. They are a village not on this coast and not on any chart anybody in this harbor has seen, in a poor-book, with twenty-two children put out of it and nine of them named. They are a sand and shell lay of this coast that is not on any chart in this harbor, in a tide-gang's step book, in a column after the figures for the foot of this mole's face stop.*
>
> *This yard has entered that it does not know why the three are there and is not going to guess. One of the nine is under the south end of this mole and four of that nine were living in this town on Friday last, and this sheet says that and says nothing else about them.*
>
> *THE TWO COLUMNS, as this yard keeps them, with a reason against every line of the second. First column: what a man on a mole or in a boat can check. Second column: what he cannot. Both stand, neither is scratched out, and a request to strike one out is refused. Nine lines of the first and five of the second are on it, each of the five carrying its reason in full.*
>
> *A LIMIT IS SET AT THE HEAD OF THE TESTIMONY BELOW BY FOUR PEOPLE OF THIS HARBOR IN THEIR OWN WORDS and it has not been lifted by this party. The holder of the writ asked for it to be lifted on the tenth of August, was not asked twice, did not ask again, and the asking is on the page with a date on it.*
>
> *A case of five bells lies in about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards under the after hatch of a vessel claimed whole by an office in a city. It is the only local copy of a community's account of itself, it is not on this sheet, it is not for sale, and it went back into the water out of a man's hands on the eighth of August and this yard has entered that and has not softened it.*
>
> *THIS PARTY WENT PAST THE OUTER MARKS on the eighth of August on its own account, with no instrument of any office, and broke a sentence it entered on its own account on the fifth of July and repeated in a market on the twenty-sixth. The breaking was entered in advance in this yard's own book. The cost is in the first column of that book and anybody may come up this hill and ask what it was.*

---

**And on Tuesday the fifteenth of August, at about eleven in the morning, in a market, in daylight, the sheet went up on the market wall of Morrow Quay, on the same nails everything goes up on, on the outside of whatever was there.**

**And what was there was the naval paper of the fourth of August, with the Crown stamp off by about a sixteenth of an inch to the right of where it should be. It was over the *Sallow Reach's* printed sheet, which was over the sheet of the seventh of June, which was over the yard's tenth thing, and the yard's own fourth return of the twenty-second of July was under the whole of it, and it has been under four papers since the twenty-eighth of July and is still on that wall, and a man can read it if he stands at the side of it in the right wind.**

"**It is under the naval paper,**" said Nessa Pike at the foot of the boards, in about forty people's hearing, "**and it went up on the outside of it, and a sheet that goes up goes up on the outside of whatever is there, and that has been the arrangement since the ninth of November.

"**And I am not going to stand here and pretend that having a sheet under a naval paper is the same as having a sheet on its own. It is not. It is a sheet on a wall that four other sheets are standing on, and a man can read all five and cannot un-read any of them, and the yard is not the party that decided any of it.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A shilling changed hands at the foot of the dock for a copy of the *Sallow Reach's* sheet, and it is not this yard's income and is not in the bag and will not be.

---

**And the wage of that week fell on the same morning at about eleven, and the bag was empty, and it was not paid.**

Four marks was due at about eleven in the morning in Calder Marr's own left hand with the week written on the outside of the envelope in the same hand, and the bag was nothing and had been nothing since the evening of Tuesday the eighth of August. **And it was not paid, and it is entered in the first column at about ten past eleven in the morning, in the ship's own hand, and the envelope was not made.**

*That the wage of the week ending Tuesday the fifteenth of August instant, at this yard's own rate of four marks a week, is not paid; that the figure is four marks and this yard is four marks short; that the money which would have paid it was spent on the eighth of August on the office's pot of air and on the master of the Sallow Reach at a figure he named out loud and was part paid; that the party did not borrow it, did not ask the harbor authority or any office for it, did not pay it in part and did not put it in an envelope; and that the reason it is in the first column and not the second is that a man can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask whether the wage of the fifteenth of August was paid, and the answer is no, and the reason is money spent on a boat past the outer marks on a Tuesday morning, and that both halves of that are on this page and neither of them is a complaint.*

**Sixteen lines and sixty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there since the fifth of March, and eleven envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere in twelve weeks. Nothing in a bag. Thirty-one on the chandler's note. Three marks owing to the master of the *Sallow Reach* for a day of the eighth, with a date on it. Both of those are debts and neither of them is going to be paid this month.**

---

**And the third column was filled at about four in the afternoon on the fifteenth, with the two times at the head of it and the lateness against them, and the two times at the head of it are in a boy's hand and have not been scraped out, and a request to scrape them out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.**

*That the entry was read on the steps of the mole at about ten past three in the afternoon and written in this book at about ten past four the same afternoon, the difference being about an hour; and that this is the tenth entry in that column since the second of May and the fifth since the boy went off the stone; and that the head of that column carries two times in the hand of a boy of twenty-two who is not on this stone and whom this party does not write to, does not go to see, and does not ask anybody who has seen him what he said, and those three nots went in on the eighth of June and are not being taken out; and that this party cannot read a position back, has not been able to since the fifteenth of May, and is not going to hire a boy to do it and call that the same thing; and that the lateness is about an hour, the shortest since the fourth of April, and the party is not going to make anything of that either way.*

Nobody read that one back to anybody, and it is the only entry in nineteen days that was not read back twice, and the entry above it says why, and the reason is hers and was given in advance.

---

**And the two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about five in the afternoon on Tuesday the fifteenth with the market gone in and about nine people in the street, and the wind was up behind them and the mole head's light was not on, and it is the whole of the nineteen days since the twenty-seventh of July, and it goes in the same words every time because the words are the practice.**

**Nothing in a bag. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and this party has said out loud at the head of this dock on this afternoon, in nine people's hearing, that the note is not re-written and is going to stay un-re-written; that its three counts stand at twenty-two weeks, thirteen weeks and eleven weeks as last written on the fourth of January and none of them has moved and none of them is moved by anything a yard does with a pencil; that the refusal of the thirty-first of March stands unreversed; and that re-writing a note with nothing in the bag is not a payment, and this yard said so in a market on the third of May and is saying it again today. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page unpaid and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for a day of work. Six pots of air bought this season at this yard's own two-mark rate, two of them in a boat past the outer marks on the eighth of August. No delivery hose, and the longest thing of hose in this harbor about nine fathoms.**

**Four wage weeks of the last five months not paid, and the most recent of them not paid this morning at about ten past eleven for want of four marks that were spent on a boat.** Forty marks of claim standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, unpaid, not reduced, not asked to be reduced, **and the first column of that book says in this party's own hand and with a date on it that he cannot do the thing he was kept for and that this party is not going to ask him whether he could.** Forty marks to a woman of thirty of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful, not one mark of it paid out of anything that has come into this yard since the eighth of May; and she was in the market office on Friday morning at about ten because that office is open to anybody, and this party did not arrange it. Eight shillings and tenpence to each of two women, undiminished, unpaid, **in no sentence together in this book; and one of them was paid by a man of Ashtide on the twenty-eighth of July for about four hundred feet of her own warp off the bottom of this harbor, and is owed the eight shillings and tenpence by this yard, and the two figures are not one figure and neither of them has moved.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid on the twenty-ninth of April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, **not paid this month, not argued this month and not asked about this month; and the chest in a shed in this yard was not opened and was not brought down the hill, and no figure out of it is on any wall in this harbor and none is in this book.**

**One herring berth short. The ninth seam's mooring out of service since the first of May with an end of it entered against it, being the first of September, which is seventeen days from now. The third lay full, and a full is not a repair, and its moorings were shut four days in the first week of this month for an examination of this mole's footing that cost a third lay four days of the best of its season and cost the office which entered it nothing, because that office's repair account has been empty since the middle of February. The tide gang four men short, and a man of that gang gave this yard nine days of his own watching on the twenty-ninth of July and gave his name; the cistern charged fifteen times on that office's own page at no figure, and this party walked it once in those three weeks, on the third, and not on the tenth or the fourteenth, and nobody was paid for any of them and nobody was thanked. The mole-head light not on, because a light costs oil. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. No boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April, and nothing in that office's book on Sunday the sixth that would have put one past the outer marks, and the officer of it said so out loud on a mole's steps in eleven people's hearing and entered it at his own cost and is not a friend of this party. The *Kittiwake* on the hard and no cradle under her. The hard at Ashtide, unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters of disputed and unpaid dues, in a naval file and in four lines of a letter in a herring skipper's coat and in a list in a city, and a herring skipper said a sentence about it out loud in a market on Sunday last and would not say it twice. The *Sea Wren* at the pile with her papers and her master's own logbook in a file, and this party has not thanked him and has not been asked to. A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found, one line in this yard's book, and this yard has not gone. Twenty marks of this yard's own unsold iron and four hundred and forty pounds of old iron with a bell-metal gun-port in it, in a heap behind the shed, unweighed, unpriced, not looked at in nineteen days, and sellable by nobody without a buyer reading our declaration first. Four of the eleven copies of a public sheet not reached and not going to be. A man of fifty-four out of a storm belt is still coming in the autumn to look at five miles of that coast for nothing, and no instrument here can call him and none can stop him, and this party did not go and look this batch.**

**A bell on the town's own ground in eight and a half feet off the south end, fouled, a frame cut through the casting on one side, no tongue on the frame at all and two parts of a chain in a lead plug where the tongue was, and a going out on the second of May that is done and is spent and was not repeated and is not going to be repeated inside four years. And in about four years a bell is going to be out in a boat at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the whole of the craft is the going out, and the four of them were told that on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it, and it stands unreversed.**

**Two things of nobody's still live in the open air on this mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither object has moved in ninety days, and the marking on one of them is not identified and is not going to be. Six joints four fathoms under that same stone, nine fastenings in about seven feet of each, three thicknesses of fouling, a chip of worked glass put into a packing on purpose, and a line of six places three hundred and fifty-one feet long which this party has declined to call a chain, in a market, in eleven people's hearing, in the words of a man who was wrong in three parts out of four of the same sentence and has not taken it back. A crew of Ashtide lies at the outer end of the second lay with a compressor of her own and forty fathoms of her own hose, and she is the reason the air on the eighth of August was not bought off the office's compressor, and this yard told a woman on the seventh of July to go to him and is not going in the way of it.**

**Four returns on the road to a city, being the fourth of May two days late, the tenth of May on the day, the seventeenth of June on the day, and the fourteenth of July on the day, and no fifth. Dated lines in this yard's own book for seventeen days of July and nine days of August saying that no finding of worked glass was made and nothing was raised and no return goes up on any of them; and a dated line for the eighth and ninth of August saying that this party was past the outer marks on its own account, raised nothing, told no office and will tell no office, and that line is a fact about this yard's book and is in no office's book and is not sent to anybody.**

**Nothing has been entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, neither figure moved in nine weeks and neither moved by a measurement. The delay is a hand's width of a second and it has not moved and was not taken and was not written down, and nobody in this yard has said why, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why, and this party is not going to be the first. A woman of sixty-eight in this county was asked once in April and refused and has said she will say no one more time in about two years, and her name is not in this book and is not going to be.**

**A crew of two and a half, a wage of four a week going into an envelope on a bench when there is four marks to put in it, eleven envelopes and not one of them anywhere, and a third column this yard intends to keep and cannot fill twice in a week, priced at four marks a week for a hand that will last a week, and nobody in this harbor is hiring at that figure to read a slate back.**

**And the boy who used to read the day back is not on the stone, and has not been on the stone since the fifteenth of May, and there is a pencil in this yard tonight and it is in somebody else's hand.**
