# Chapter 149

*A Man in a Coat With a Paper, and a Question in a Market Nobody Answers*

Saturday the twelfth of August, at about two in the afternoon, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, with the clerk and his own page under his arm and the ledger open on the table.

The wall behind the reeve's bench had been wiped on Monday at eight and had stood bare and clean for four days and nobody had put anything on it, and nobody put anything on it that day either.

**A man came in who had not been in this room before and who is not going to be in it again, and he gave his name as Amos Trewhella, of an office in a city whose name the clerk wrote down on his own page and which this yard has not repeated and is not going to repeat, and he had a paper in his hand and he did not sit down for the first ten minutes.**

"**I am going to read a paper and then I am going to ask for four things and I am going to write down what you say, and I have not come to be pleasant and I have not come to be unpleasant, and I would like those two things written down as the first two things I said.

"**One. The vessel of the Crown named *Vigil* is not to be employed upon this coast from the date of this paper.

"**Two. Captain Veyra Holt is relieved of the command of this coast and of that vessel, from the fifteenth day of August instant, and is posted from the first day of September instant to the making up of the coast's wreck lists, in the city named in the schedule, at a table, with every vessel reported, claimed, entered and closed upon this coast for the year, and with the returns of the officers employed upon it. She does not come to this harbor and she has not been in it and she will not be.

"**Three. The second season of the writ of salvage working granted at Morrow Quay on the first day of May instant is not granted, refused, or dealt with by this paper, and is not before the office from which this paper comes, and the holder will be told when it has been decided and not before, which is the last line of the writ itself and is in his own hand on his own bench.**

"**Four. I ask for the production of the writ, of the crew's book kept under the reporting clause upon it, and of the four returns made under it.**"

---

**And the yard produced all four things inside eleven minutes, whole, unshortened, and said out loud before it opened the book why it was opening the book, and the reason is the clause's own construction and has been the reason since the second of May.**

The writ went first, one sheet in the harbor authority's own paper and seal, with the reporting clause attached and three names on the clause and no face in the room. The crew's book went second, and the shipwright did not open it, and Calder Marr opened it, and the two of them turned it so the man in the coat could read it standing up.

"**The book has three columns,**" said Calder Marr, "**and the first two are ours and have a reason against every line of the second, and the third was made on the second of May by a boy of twenty-two and is filled late and the lateness is entered, and none of the three is in any office's book and none of the three is a defense of anything, and there is a line at the head of the third column saying that a number in a lead plug in a chest in that shed is not where that goes, and I am not going to say one word about that number to you or to anybody and I never have.**

"**And you will find on the ninth of August a line in the first column that says this party was past the outer marks on its own account, raised nothing, and told no office and will tell no office. That is the only thing in that book about the tenth day of August and it is a fact about this yard's book and it is in no office's book and it was not sent to anybody, and I am not going to soften it for you and you are not entitled to anything but that.**

"**And you will find on the seventh of August a line in the first column that says this party broke a sentence it had entered on its own account on the fifth of July and repeated in a market on the twenty-sixth of July, and that the sentence was that this party is not going past the outer marks this season, and that the breaking is entered in advance and not afterwards.**"

The man in the coat read the line of the seventh of August twice and did not comment on it.

"**Then I will ask you the four things and I will write them down as you say them.**

"**One. What did the party go out for on the eighth of August.**"

"**Four persons out of a boat with a hole in her, and nothing else, and we have entered that on the Monday before, and the case of five bells went back into her and is at the bottom of the sea about five and a half fathoms down and we did not raise it and we are not going to say we nearly had it and we are not going to give a figure for what it is worth, because four people told us what it is worth on a mole's steps on Thursday and one of the four is its own community and the other three are standing on the same mole.**"

"**Two. Will you claim it.**"

"**No.**

"**Three. If it were raised, to whom would it go.**"

"**To four people of the Sill, and it would go to them by their own hand and not by mine, and there is a written limit on it that they put on it themselves on Thursday last and the party has not lifted it and is not going to lift it and has entered that the holder asked for it to be lifted.**"

"**Four. Then produce the writ.**"

Calder Marr put it on the table face down, which is the way a thing is put down that is going to be picked up again.

"**You have it in front of you and it is not the Second Bell license and it is not a charter and it renews nothing, and its ground is the mole from the first seam to the twelfth, the dump, the north wall, the first and second seams, all ground landward of the third seam, and private ground within the line, and it is bounded by the outer marks, and that is why there was nothing in it on the eighth of August and that is why there is no clause out there and no keeper and no place and no hour and no limits and no return, and I would like all of that in your paper in those words, because I am not going to be the only one who has said it.**

"**And here is the last of it and you will have it out loud and you can write it down and I will not complain about the words.

"**Whatever any office in any city does with any man in any coat, this party has not been told, has not asked, does not want to know this month, and will find out when it finds out. And if a paper comes up this hill about the second season of the writ of the first of May, this party will read it, will answer it on the day it comes, and will not treat the man who brings it as a friend or as an enemy.

"**You are a man in a coat and you have come up a hill on a Saturday with a paper and you have read what I have given you and you have asked me four questions and I have answered all four, and I do not know you and I am not going to find out this month, and that is not an insult to you. It is the standing arrangement and it is in our book since the fifth of July and it is the reason you were let up the stairs.**"

---

**And the clerk entered all of it on his own page, and said what a box can hold, and the seventh time this year said a thing he could not enter.**

"**I can enter that a man in a coat came into this room at two o'clock on Saturday the twelfth of August and read a paper and asked four questions and was answered four times, and that a party produced a writ and a book and four returns whole and unshortened, and that the writ of the first of May is bounded by the outer marks and no part of it reached the water they were on. That I can enter and I have entered it.

"**And I cannot enter what a person of no office has, in a case with five bells in it at five and a half fathoms, inside the four hundred yards, on a vessel the Crown has claimed whole, when the officer of this harbor authority has entered in his own book on Sunday last that he has nothing that will put a boat of his past those marks. I have said a thing like that seven times this year and I have kept all seven and I have stopped mentioning the wall.**"

He put the page under his arm and looked at the bare wall behind the reeve's bench for about a second and then went out.

---

**And this party entered the posting in its own book on Saturday the twelfth of August, in the shipwright's hand, and checked it against the fourth thing it entered on the fifth of July, and the check is on the page.**

*That on Saturday the twelfth of August instant a paper was read in the hearing room over the tide-gang store by a man in a coat of an office in a city, and that the paper stated that the vessel of the Crown named Vigil is not to be employed upon this coast; and that Captain Veyra Holt is relieved of that vessel and of the command of this coast from the fifteenth of August instant and is posted from the first of September instant to the making up of the coast's wreck lists in a city at a table; and that the said officer does not come to this harbor and has not been in it and is not going to be; and that the party has entered the date, and the party has entered that neither the relief nor the posting puts any person, no boat and no lead inside the closure of the notice of the thirty-first of October; that the posting is not a command in this harbor; that the posting is not an inspection of, or over, the harbor authority of this harbor; that the posting is not a commission of any kind; that the posting puts nobody past the outer marks and nobody past the imperial line; and that the party has made that check against the fourth thing it entered on its own account on Wednesday the fifth of July and has entered that the check holds.

*And that the second season of the writ of the first of May is not granted, refused, or dealt with by that paper, and is not before the office from which it came, and has not been given, and the writ runs to the first of March next, and its ground is not widened and is not to be widened, and its four names are four and no fifth has been added; and that the party would not have widened it if it could and is not going to ask; and that the man who brought that paper was read the writ, was answered on the day it came, and was not treated as a friend or as an enemy, and the party does not know him and has not asked and is not going to.*

---

**And on Sunday the thirteenth of August, at about eleven in the morning, in daylight, in a market, on the boards, in about forty people's hearing, a man of a boat of the second lay who is not a member of any office in this harbor and who has no reason to be on those boards said the question.**

He was about sixty. He had been in the market office on Friday morning and had read the page.

"**That is a chain of places,**" he said. "**Six of them are on this mole under our own feet and three of them are in books in that office, and one of the nine is a weir under the south end of this mole and a man came up these steps on Thursday and told us whose it was.

"**And I am a boat and I have been on this water forty-one years, and I am going to ask one question and I am going to ask it in a market and not in a room, and I would like the answer in a market and not in a letter, and if there is not one then I would like it said out loud in a market that there is not one.

"**Who changed the rule.**"

---

Nobody answered him for about half a minute, and the mole head's light was not on behind them and the boards were wet.

"**I do not know,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Well. Then say the other thing, which is the thing I actually want, which is that you do not know in a way that means you have thought about it. Because a man who has never heard the question says *what rule* and a man who has heard it says *I do not know*.**"

"**I have thought about it for five days,**" Calder Marr said, "**and I am not going to tell you what I have got, because what I have got is a page with a limit on it that four people wrote themselves, and the limit says they will not say one word about who took her off them, and I am not going to go round the side of a limit four people put on their own account in the open air in front of forty witnesses.

"**And the rule itself. This yard has spent five months refusing to put a name on anything it cannot check. I cannot tell you a rule because I do not know what the rule was called, and I cannot tell you who changed it because I do not know what it changed from, and if I gave you a name you would have it in your hand in a week and you would have it in a city in a month, and in four years somebody would be standing on a mole in another town with the name in his hand and no lead in his water, and that is a thing this harbor has already had done to it once and I have read what it cost and I have not got a page for it.**"

"**Then you are not going to answer.**"

"**No. And I am going to be the one who says that out loud in this market, and I am going to say it in the yard's own hand with a date on it, so that a man can come up this hill in four years and ask whether the question was put and whether it was answered, and the answer will be that it was put on the thirteenth of August in about forty people's hearing and that it was not answered and that the not-answering is on the page with the reason against it, and the reason is that this party does not put a name on a thing it cannot check, and that has been the decision of this yard since the fifth of July and it is the only thing this yard has had this year that nobody can take off it.**"

---

**And then Elias Voke, fifty-one, of the *Anna Ray*, of the first lay, who has nineteen winters of disputed and unpaid dues in a naval file and in four lines of a letter in his own coat and in a list in a city, and who carried a plan up this coast for nothing in January and a statement of this harbor's capacity for nothing in March, said one thing and would not say it twice.**

"**I have got nineteen winters in a naval file,**" he said, "**because a reeve of this harbor sold a chart of this harbor for them in a room in June, and he did not have to sell it and he sold it, and he told me afterwards in a letter of four lines that he would have asked and I would have said no and he would have done it anyway.

"**And that is one of them true, and it is not that the other one is not true. Both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other. I have been saying that shape of sentence to people for forty years and I have never once had anybody write it down, because you do not write down a thing like that, you just say it and it goes.

"**Write it down. I am not going to say it again.**"

Calder Marr wrote it in the first column, in his own left hand, with the date and the hour, and read it back, and Elias Voke made him read the last sentence back twice and then said that was enough and went down the boards.

**And the entry says, in the ship's own hand at the end of it, that the sentence is not new, that it is in this yard's own book out of a room in April and out of a market in July, and that it has now been said in a market in this harbor by a herring skipper who is owed nineteen winters and was not thanked, and that the party has entered that the two halves of it are not going to be settled this year and are not going to be settled by anybody in this market, and that a man who is owed nineteen winters is not a man who has been given anything.**

**And nothing in that market answered the question, and the entry says so, and the not-answering is on the page.**
