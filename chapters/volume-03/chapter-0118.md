# Chapter 118

*The Work That Cannot Be Sold*

The reason went in at seven in the morning in Calder Marr's own left hand, on the yard's own paper, in the yard's own book, with the clerk not present because the clerk was not here and is not going to be asked to come, and it is entered that the clerk is not here and that the ground is a man's own account landward of the third seam's mark where no instrument of any office is required, and that this yard is keeping the practice anyway and is not going to have the two confused.

*Ground, the second seam's own face on this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, at the top of the flood. Nothing above that mark. Nothing under the bedded material. No ground any other party claims. The work is the *Anna Ray's* own ring and shackle and swivel and about four feet of her own warp come on the ring, on her own account, and nothing is being claimed by this party and nothing is being claimed against her, and what comes up goes onto her wharf in her own hands and not into this yard's ground.*

*And the person in the water is one man, and he is not one of the four persons upon the writ of the first of May, and he is Rolf Cardew, twenty-nine, of the second lay, hired for the day at three marks, and he is not of this party and is not of any association, and the limit of his air is his own and is fixed by him at the boat before anybody is wet and no man in this harbor may put a hand on it; and the tender is Nessa Pike, who is a shipwright and has never been in the water in her life and is not a diver, and the cutoff is hers to pull without asking anybody, and the word is Enough.*

*And that on Saturday the tenth of June instant the air in this boat is a pot of this harbor authority's own, bought of that office at this yard's own two-mark rate, filled at that office's compressor and carried on that office's own hose, and that it is the whole of the air in this harbor this morning, and that the compressor's gland on this yard's own pump has been on since the nineteenth of May and is not paid for and has not been used, because the thing after the gland is the hose and there is not one in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms.*

**And the six were read out in order at the boat, in daylight, with Rolf Cardew standing in the water on the step and the shipwright holding the line, and Calder Marr read them and Cardew said he had them.**

Named tender who is not the diver. Independent line, on the boat's own bow and not on the mole. Fixed air limit on the slate, set at the boat by the diver and entered as the diver's, and it was **twenty-two** and Cardew set it himself and would not let the holder raise it. Seated named weight — the holder, at the after thwart, and a stone in the boot of the boat besides. Cutoff the tender can pull without asking anybody, and it is hers, and the word is *Enough*. And a stated reason, entered in the office's book before anybody goes in the water, in the holder's own left hand, with the clerk present, unedited — **and the clerk is not present and the reason is in the yard's own book and the yard has entered the difference rather than filling it in.**

"A tender's stop is not a request," Nessa Pike said, "and I have said that in this shed four times since the eighth of May and I am going to say it on a boat now, and if any man in this harbor thinks a ship's work can be stopped by a man in a boat, he can come and find out at about half past ten and I will not be slow about it."

"Twenty-two minutes," Cardew said, from the step. "I will be under and I will be up and I will not argue with anybody about the minute hand."

---

They were on the second seam at about ten past nine and it went well for an hour and a half, which is the whole of what a good day at a seam is in this harbor.

The ring came up at about eleven on a quarter of a fathom of rise and about four feet of warp wound twice, hard and green, with a skin on it, and it went onto the *Anna Ray's* own deck in Elias Voke's own hands at noon and nobody weighed it and nobody priced it and the eighth and the share were off the top of the day and had been since about eight o'clock.

**And at about twenty past twelve, with the flood still making and Cardew nine minutes from his own limit, the line came up slack and then hard, and the shipwright felt it in her hand, and she said one word, and everybody in that boat stopped.**

"Cutoff," she said, and pulled.

**Cardew was up in about ninety seconds and he was on the step and he was not in any difficulty and he was not hurt and he was also not willing, and everybody in that boat knew the second of those, and he came up the ladder and sat on the gunwale and said nothing for about a minute and a half.**

"You had him at a comfortable twenty-two and he had himself at twenty-two and the two of those are the same number and they are not the same thing," he said, when he could.

"You were at twenty-nine minutes off a flat bottom on a line that had gone slack and then gone hard, and the line is how I know where you are, and it stopped telling me."

"That is fair," Cardew said, "and I did not like it, and I am not going to say I did not like it, and I am going to take my three marks and I am going to say that no man in this crew said one word to me about the twenty-nine minutes when I came up and I have been in the water in this harbor four times."

Nobody said a word to him about the twenty-nine minutes.

---

**The net had about six fathoms of line on it and about a stone in the bottom of it, and it came up with a stone and a lump of the second seam's own rubble in it and something else in it, and the something else is a foot and a half long.**

It is green through the body and cloudy, and thick at one end and thin at the other, thick at the thick end being about two and a half inches and not tapering down to nothing but stopping at about a quarter of an inch, and it has the second seam's own green stone dust ground into one face and a clean broken face at the thin end that is dull and gritty and is not a face anybody worked.

And along one of the long edges, for about three inches, there is a scalloped edge. **About nine of them. All of them the same hand. None of them running out of anything.**

"That is the same as the one off the toe," Nessa Pike said, and Calder Marr did not answer her, and she looked at him.

"You asked."

"I am not going to say what it is," Calder Marr said. "**I have had a hand on a great deal that came off this mole since I was fourteen years old and there is no name in me for that edge and I am not going to make one up on a boat in front of a hired man.** Eleven scallops in about four inches on the sixth of May on a lump out of the rubble between the third and fourth seams, and about nine in three inches on a piece of a herring fleet's own ground at the second seam, and that is twice and it is in the same hand both times and I cannot account for it once. Write it in that form and let a man in a city be the one who decides whether two is a pattern or two is twice."

**And on the flat face there is a mark.**

It is not a seal. It is not a letter. It is a group of short strokes cut or pressed into the glass, in which a number of them are joined at one end, and one of them is not joined to anything, and there is no figure in it and there is no number in it and there is nothing in it that anybody in that boat can read out loud.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get onto a mole in this harbor: that the object is about a foot and a half long, thick at one end and thin at the other, green through the body and cloudy, with this mole's own stone dust ground into one face and a broken face at the thin end which is dull and gritty; that it has a scalloped edge of about nine in about three inches on one long edge, which is not a fracture and is not a mould line, and that this yard has seen the same kind of edge once before, on the sixth of May, on a different object out of different ground, and can account for it neither time; and that it carries a mark on one face which is not a seal and is not a letter and is not a figure, and that no person in that boat has identified it, and that the party is not going to identify it, and that if it means a thing to somebody then that somebody was not in that boat.*

*And that the party does not know what it is, does not know its age, does not know who worked it, and does not know whether it was ever attached to anything, and that the age of it goes nowhere at all, on a line of its own, with the reason that this party declines to give a figure for it and declines to give a reason for declining, and that the circuit office may write for a reason and the party will answer on the day it asks.*

"It is not a panel," Nessa Pike said. "**Say the rest of it. Say what it is not, all of it, on the page, in one line, because in four years somebody is going to bring this object up next to four other objects and ask whether they are the same.**"

"It is not a panel, and it is not a plate, and it is not the ferry plate, and it is not the worked panel this yard gave back in a market on the seventh of April, and it is not the lump of the sixth of May, and it is not in any office's book and has no number anywhere and carries no claim seal, and there is no claimant and no keeper but this party and there is no office with a copy of any of it." He stopped for a second. "**And it is not connected to a lead plug in a chest in this shed, or to a mark on a stone, or to a ferry that went down thirteen years ago, or to anything under this town, and nobody is to write any two of those together.**"

"Write it down," she said, "and then tell me what happens to it, because it is in a net on the bottom of this boat and it cannot stay in a net and it cannot go into that shed."

---

**A net brings up what is in it. That is the whole of the sentence and it is why the yard had a problem on the Saturday afternoon of the tenth of June and solved it badly and quickly and in the only direction available.**

It could not go into the shed, because the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in this harbor and this yard has not argued that clause since the thirty-first of October and is not going to argue it for the sake of a piece of glass.

It could not go into a boat in this harbor, because a boat in this harbor with a thing like that in her is a boat in a shed with a lid on.

So it went back out onto the mole's crown above the third seam's mark, in a crate, under a tarpaulin weighted with a stone and a spare fender, about nine feet south of the bucket with the lump of green glass in it that has been on that crown since the sixth of May and is wetter than it was on the sixth.

"**That is a mile of this harbor's own ground with two things of nobody's on it under two tarpaulins, and there is no shed in this harbor a man can put either of them in, and it is the second clause of a notice that neither of us wrote and neither of us can lift, and I am going to say the sentence out loud once so that it is on the page in my own mouth: we are putting it back out in the weather because the law of a town we did not write will not let us put it in a building.**"

Nobody said anything to that, and the crate was carried up the steps by three men in about four minutes, and it was put down and covered and weighted, and the place and the hour and the three men were written down, and the two things on that crown were about nine feet apart and neither of them belongs to anybody.

---

**And at about ten past nine that night, on the Sunday the eleventh, Calder Marr sat down on the mole's crown between them with a lamp and looked at the thing for about twenty minutes, and did not touch it, and nobody asked him to, and the looking was the whole of what he did.**

Then he came up the hill and wrote the entry, and the two of them read it back, and there is nobody else in that shed to read it after them.

*That on Saturday the tenth of June instant, at about twenty minutes past twelve in the noon, on the second seam's own face of this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, on the *Anna Ray's* own account and with her owner in her own boat, one man being in the water at about twenty-nine minutes of a limit of twenty-two that he had set himself at the boat, and the tender having called the cutoff and pulled it, a piece of worked glass about a foot and a half long was found in a net and came up in that net; that the day of a finding is not counted; that the fourth day after it is the day it falls due; and that a return in respect of it falls due on Saturday the seventeenth of June instant and is not made tonight and is not made this month by anybody in this party who has not written it.*

*And the reason the object is on this mole's crown in a crate under a tarpaulin and is not in any shed and is not in any boat is the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October, and the party has not argued that clause and is not going to, and the party is now the keeper of a second thing it cannot sell, cannot give away, cannot put indoors, and must describe to two offices inside four days, and it took that clause on the first of May because a yard with no paper cannot work.*

*And that the party has no boy.*

---

The columns were read out at the head of the dock at about seven on the Sunday evening.

**Twelve marks in a bag, the gross of the day sixteen at the yard's own rate on the *Anna Ray's* own account, the eighth two and the share two off the top of it and gone to the harbor authority and not argued, the victuals two and a hired hand three paid at the mole, that being nine out of sixteen, and the seven is in the bag, and the pot of air and the hundred fathoms of hemp went out of it on the Friday and are not coming back, so that the whole of the job left this yard four marks and not seven, which is the figure the yard wrote on the mole's steps on the thirty-first of May before anybody knew which of those two numbers was going to be the one.** Thirty-one on the note and not re-written and the three counts of that page unmoved. Nothing in a tin. Seven lines and twenty-eight marks in the wage column and the fifth of March the oldest and three envelopes on a bench.

**And a piece of worked glass about a foot and a half long, green and cloudy, with nine scallops in three inches and a mark on one face that nobody in that boat identified, on the crown of this mole above the third seam's mark, in a crate, under a tarpaulin, in the open air, worth nothing to anybody this party has been able to find out about, and not one mark of it entered in any office's book and no number anywhere and no seal and no claimant, and a return about it falls due on Saturday the seventeenth of June instant.**
