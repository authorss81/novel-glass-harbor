# Chapter 133

*The Count of Four, and the Figure of a Hand*

The wage went in on Tuesday the eleventh of July at about eleven in the morning, in Calder Marr's own left hand, because there is nobody else in that shed who writes, and it was a wage week and it was four marks at the rate from the first of February, and the week written on the outside of it in the same hand, and the envelope is in the bench chest and is not among the five rolled in oilcloth, and it is not going anywhere this week and nothing is going to carry it, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a wage is not a reason to go to a door.

**Two marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written. Nothing in a tin. **Twelve lines and forty-eight marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and seven envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them anywhere.**

---

**And at about half past eleven on the Tuesday morning, with the market not in yet, the shipwright took the pencil and wrote in the first column of this yard's own book a thing that had been owed to that column since the third of July.**

"That is the count of four," she said. "**The twenty-eighth of May, in a westerly, at the head of the mole. The second of June, in a boat at the second seam. The fifteenth of June, at the head of this dock, which was not taken and was not written down. And the third of July, on the crown, at about eleven in the morning, at the top of the flood. Four.**"

"**And it goes in the first column and not the second, and I want that read out loud, because the second column is a page of things this yard cannot check and a count is a thing this yard can check. I can stand on a stone tomorrow and ask the man of the second lay what he saw on the second of June, and he will tell me or he will tell me he does not remember, and one of those is worth more than the four lines would be.**"

"**Read it back,**" Calder Marr said.

She read it back.

"And the reason I am not putting a name against it," she said, "**is the one I gave you on the third of July and I have not improved on it and I am not going to. The day a man in this yard writes *I could not tell which of two sounds it was* with his name on it, in about two years there is a page in this book that says what a man of this yard can and cannot hear. That page is going to be read by somebody in Veyr who is looking for exactly that, and he is going to get it out of me in a room with a seal on the table, and I am not going to hand him the key to that by writing your name on a line about a bell.**

"**And I am not writing my name against it either, and if you put one there I will take the pencil.**"

---

**And then she priced something out loud, which she had not been asked to do, and which she had told the shed on the twenty-sixth of June that she would not do a third time.**

"**I am not going to say it a third time,**" she said. "**A thing said three times in a shed is a complaint and I do not make complaints, I make entries. So I am going to say it once in a different sentence and it is going to mean the same thing and you are all going to know it means the same thing, and I am not going to have anybody in this shed call it a complaint afterward.**

"**A hand that will last a week costs four marks a week. That is the figure, because that is the wage in this yard and it is the lowest figure a working hand is held at anywhere on this coast. There is nobody in this harbor hiring at four marks a week to read a slate back. There has not been anybody in this harbor hiring at four marks a week to read a slate back since the boy came in February of last year, and he was twenty-one and he was not very good and he was cheap, and he took a herring boat in June and he is not on this stone and this yard is not writing to him and has not gone to see him and has not asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and those three nots went in on the eighth of June and they are not being taken out this month or any month.**

"**And I am not sorry that we put him on the stone on the fifteenth of May. His hand had been going since the fourth of April. It was the right thing and I would do it again and I have not said so out loud before and I am saying it now, and it is in the first column because I said it in a shed and a man in a shed can be asked about it tomorrow.**"

Nobody said anything to that, and it went in, and she read it back and Calder Marr read it back after her, and the shed held the two of them and a lamp and nobody else.

---

**On the Wednesday the twelfth they wrote to a printer in a city, and it cost nothing, and it went in the ordinary post.**

Three lines, in Calder Marr's own left hand, on the yard's own paper.

*Sir. On Monday last we made a finding and on Friday next a return upon the writ of the first of May goes up this coast in a sealed jacket with a copy into the harbor authority's ledger. The return states a place, an hour, a keeper and limits, and it states a fact and not a conclusion, and the fact is a piece of glass and a lump of packing and where they came out of the ground. We are telling you it is coming because you asked us four questions in June and we answered three of them, and because a man who marks what he has not seen ought to be told in advance which papers are about to be printed.*

*It is not for sale and there is no price on it and it is not a sheet. If you print it, print what it says and not what it is about.*

*Your servant, Calder Marr, holder of the writ.*

"**No price on it,**" said the shipwright.

"**No price on it, and no scale, and no eleven names at the head of it, and it is going on a market wall on Saturday the twenty-second if we are going to do this at all, and he is going to see it in Veyr in a fortnight and he is going to print it because he prints things.**

"**I have thought about it for a day and a half and I have gone back and forth and I am going to say out loud what changed my mind, and it was not a principle. It was that a man with a press who has already written to us once is the only person in nine hundred miles who is going to be obliged to look at a paper of ours, and a return is the only paper in this harbor that any office in any city is obliged to look at. That is the whole of it.**"

"**And a man with a press is not obliged to look at anything. He is obliged to look at what he has paid four marks for.**"

"**He did not pay four marks for it. He is going to get it for nothing, off a market wall, like everybody else, and that is the difference between the two and it is the only difference there is.**"

---

**And at about half past three on the Wednesday afternoon Edric Pringle came up the hill with a slate under her arm and said the cause out loud before she said the figure, which is how she has always done it and which nobody in this harbor has ever asked her to stop.**

"**I have heard there is a thing at the second seam, and I have heard it from four people and none of them were you, and I have come up here to say that if the face is shut for looking at it I am not going to book a day, and if it is not shut I am going to book a day for the seventeenth whatever else it is.**

"**And I have not said my figure and I am going to say it now and I am not asking you for it. Eight shillings and tenpence, since the sixth of February, undiminished, and you have not paid it and you have not offered it and I have not asked you for it in a street, and I have not asked anybody else for it either and I am not going to.**

"**And I know you have a woman owed about four hundred feet of her own warp on the bottom of this harbor and I know you have told her to go to a man of Ashtide, and I am not that woman and that is not why I am up here, and if anybody in this harbor puts those two debts in one sentence I will hear about it and I will come back up this hill.**"

"**They are in no sentence together in this book,**" said the shipwright, "**and they are not going to be in one anywhere, and that is entered and you can come and check it on any day of a week without asking.**"

"**I will check it on Thursday,**" said Edric Pringle, "**and I will tell you what I find, and if I find it I will say so in a market and not in here.**"

She went down the boards.

"**Seventeenth,**" said Calder Marr. "**The face is not shut. It is going to be the busiest three days this yard has had since the first of May and it is going to be done on a Monday and a Tuesday and a Wednesday, and one of those days is going to go wrong, and I am telling you now so that if it goes wrong you already knew it might.**"

---

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Tuesday and on the Wednesday with his back to the room and a cough that came every forty minutes, and he was sixty-one, and the return that is being written for the Friday was on the near end of the bench under a weight, and neither brother went over to him, and he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing of anybody on either day.

*That on Tuesday the eleventh and Wednesday the twelfth of July instant, Alden Marr, sixty-one, was at the far bench of this shed from about eight in the morning until about six on the Tuesday and from about nine until about four on the Wednesday, and was not asked for anything by any person of this party and asked nothing of any person of this party, and neither brother went over to him; and that this line is in the first column and can be checked by either brother on any day of a week by asking the other one.*

**And that evening the two columns were read out at the head of the dock and the money half of it is one sentence.**

Two marks in a bag and it has been two marks since Tuesday morning. **Four marks in on the Monday, four marks out on the Tuesday, and the whole of the week's money is gone by the middle of the second day of the week and there are three weeks of it to go before the twenty-fifth of July.** Thirty-one on the note and its three counts unmoved. **Twelve lines and forty-eight marks, the fifth of March the oldest, seven envelopes on a bench and none of them anywhere, and the reason is on the page and it is the same reason it has been since the second of May.** Nothing in a tin. Forty marks standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, not one mark of it paid. Eight shillings and tenpence to each of two women, and the two of them are in no sentence together in this book. Eight marks and a day of a boat to a bellwright, unpaid since the twenty-third of May, unargued.

**And a day at the second seam on Monday the seventeenth on a woman's own account that this yard has been told about and has not quoted, and a figure of eight marks a week for a hand that will last a week, which is a wage and is not a fee and is not a boat's share and is not in any column in this book, and a boy's berth is not a fee.**

**And a woman of the second lay coming up this hill on the Wednesday to say that a day of looking at a thing is not a day of work and is going to be booked anyway, and to say eight shillings and tenpence in the same breath, and to say that if the yard is going to shut the face for three days she would rather know on the Thursday than the sixteenth.**

**And on Friday a return goes up the coast, and on Saturday the twenty-second a piece of paper that has no price on it is going on a market wall, and a printer in a city is going to read it in a fortnight and print it, and this yard cannot stop him and is not going to try.**
