# Chapter 129

*Five Mondays, and a Wage*

The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday the third of July and it came down bare, and that was five Mondays, and there was no sitting and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it.

**At about ten the cistern was charged for the seventh time, on the rota, at no figure, by three people of this yard and two men of the third lay who came past and helped and would not take anything and were not thanked.**

At about half past ten, Calder Marr was on the crown above the third seam's mark, at the top of the flood, with the tarpaulins about nine feet apart on either side of him and about four hours of it left, and the mole head's handbell went at about eleven, for no reason, because it is rung on a falling tide by a man of fifty-one of another boat who has a bad wheel of his own and who was not on that mole and was not asked to be and did not know that anybody had noticed the hour.

It went out flat over the water and came back off the crown.

**On the twenty-seventh of May, in a westerly, with the lamp on his seaward side, he was certain for a second and a half that it had come off the water and then certain for a second and a half that it had come out of the man's mouth. On the second of June, on a boat at the second seam, he could not say which of the two sounds he had been given, and he did not write it down. On the fifteenth of June, at the head of this dock, the tide bell went off the south end and nobody in that street could place the sound, and it was not taken and it was not written down.**

**And on the third of July, on this crown, in the fourth time, he could not say which of the two, and he did not write it down either, and he told a shipwright about it at the top of the steps at about two in the afternoon, and that is the first time in five months that he has said it out loud to anybody, and she wrote it in her own hand and he did not tell her what to write.**

"Write what I have said and no more than it."

"**That is the second column and it is a fact and not a name for it, and the reason against the line is that no man in a shed can check where another man's sound went, and I am writing the reason and not you.**"

"And put your own name against the line and not mine, because it is your ear and not my trade."

"**I am not putting my name against it, and I am telling you why, and then you can decide. I am not putting my name against it because the day a man in this yard writes *I could not tell which of two sounds it was* with his name on it, then in about two years there is a page in this book that says what a man of this yard can and cannot hear, and that page is going to be read by somebody who is looking for exactly that, and he is going to get it out of me in a room in Veyr with a seal, and I am not going to hand him the key to that by writing my name on a line about a bell.**

"**And I am not writing my name on it and I am not asking you to write yours, and if you put a name on that line I will take the pencil.**"

She did not put a name on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that on Monday the third of July instant, at about eleven in the morning, on the crown of this mole above the third seam's mark, at the top of the flood, a person of this party could not say in which of two directions the sound of this mole head's handbell had come; and that the party has entered it as a fact and not as a name for it, and the reason is that no man on a mole can check where another man's sound went; and that this is the fourth entry of this form in this book since the twenty-sixth of December and the party has entered the count and has entered that it has entered the count and has entered no cause against any of the four; and that no person in this party has been asked about it by anybody, and this party is not going to ask anybody, and no person outside this party has been asked about it either and is not going to be.*

Nessa Pike read it back at about ten past six and Calder Marr read it back after her and there was nobody else in the shed.

"**Then that is four,**" she said. "**And I want the four entered somewhere that is not the second column, because the second column is a page of things this yard cannot check and a count is a thing this yard can check, and I want it in the first column in my hand on Tuesday the eleventh, which is a wage day and a market day and the only day in the next week a man in this harbor can ask me a question and get it read back to him.**"

---

**On Tuesday the fourth of July the wage went in at about eleven in the morning, and it was a wage week, and it came out of the ten marks that have come into this yard since the twenty-fourth of June, and the four of them it came out of have been in the bag since Friday.**

Four marks, a week at the rate from the first of February, for the week of Tuesday the fourth of July to Monday the tenth inclusive, in Calder Marr's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it in the same hand. The envelope is in the bench chest and is not among the five rolled in oilcloth, and it is not going anywhere this week and nothing is going to carry it, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a wage is not a reason to go to a door.

**Eleven lines and forty-four marks in the column, and the fifth of March the oldest, and six envelopes on a bench in a chest.**

---

**And two things came into the harbor's own book that week that had nothing to do with the yard and were entered by other people, and the yard heard about both of them on the boards and had no part in either.**

On the Monday morning, in the office's own book, in the reeve's own hand: **that the mooring at the ninth seam, out of service since the first of May with no end of it entered in any book in this harbor, is entered now with an end of it, being the first of September, and that the end is entered because this office's instruments are not being contested and this office has never before been able to enter one.**

"**And I want the other half of it said out loud in a market by somebody who is not of this office,**" Kest is said to have said, "**and I would rather it was said by a man of the third lay than by my clerk. An end of September on the ninth seam is the first thing this office has entered in five months that a person in this harbor can use, and it is entered because an officer of a vessel went up this coast with a piece of paper in his hand, and I am not going to pretend otherwise and I am not going to pretend it cost nothing.**"

And on the Tuesday, from a wharfinger of the second lay, in about nine people, on the boards, at about eleven, and the yard standing at the back and not coming forward, **that the cistern behind the engine house has not gone dry since the nineteenth of June, and that it is charged on a rota on a Monday and a Thursday by people the office cannot pay, and that the office has put seven of those walks on its own page at no figure and against its own interest, and that the wharfinger has said out loud that a cistern holding for a fortnight is not a repair account and a light is still not on and a hard at Ashtide is still unanswered and nineteen winters are in a naval file and a man's own hand, and that he said it in that order and would not put it in any other order.**

---

**And on the Tuesday evening the third column was filled for the fifth time since the second of May, and it is the longest this column has run since the boy created it, and the party has entered that and has entered that it is not going to make a practice of it.**

*That the entry in the third column was read on the steps at about seven in the evening on Tuesday the fourth of July and was written in this book at about half past eight in the morning on Wednesday the fifth, which is a lateness of about a day and a half, and which is the longest this column has run since it was created; and that the party has entered the figure and has entered that it is the longest and has entered that the party is not going to make a practice of it, and the reason is that a column that is always late is a column that has stopped expecting anybody.*

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Tuesday evening with the market coming in behind them and about nine people in the street.

**Four marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid. Nothing in a tin. **Eleven lines and forty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and six envelopes on a bench in the chest, and none of them has been anywhere, and the reason is on the page in the yard's own hand and it is the same reason it has been since the second of May.**

**And that is the whole of the money of this yard since the fifteenth of June last, in one line, in this yard's own hand, and it is on the page and nobody has read it back but the man who wrote it and the woman who is going to have to hand it to somebody in September.**

Four marks in a bag. The eighth of the day and the harbor's share off the top of everything raised, off a day rate as well, and never argued. The wage at four a week going into an envelope. A note at thirty-one that is not re-written and three counts of weeks on it that do not move. A compressor's gland on that note at five marks and unpaid and of no use, because the thing after the gland is the hose.

Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced and not asked to be reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful, **and not one mark of it paid out of any money that has come into this yard since the eighth of May, and no office in this harbor has paid this yard a mark since the eighth of May and there is no office in this harbor that is going to.**

**Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, Sella Brigg and Edric Pringle, undiminished and unpaid and not paid in three weeks; about four hundred feet of one of them's own warp on the bottom of this harbor for a hundred and forty-eight days; her name and a figure and a date on a market wall since the seventh of June; a printed sheet over the top of that wall since the twenty-fourth; and a woman saying in a market on the twenty-fifth that she is going to ask a man of another port, which this yard has no standing to stop and did not try to.**

**Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid on the twenty-ninth of April, owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid through the whole of this month, unargued, and not paid in three weeks; and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it, and that standing sentence is unreversed and is not going to be broken by anything that happened in this month.**

No air in this harbor and no delivery hose and the longest thing of hose in it about nine fathoms, and a crew of Ashtide lying at the outer end of the second lay with a compressor of her own and forty fathoms of her own hose, and neither of those facts is anybody's fault.

A cistern charged on Monday the third of July, the seventh time, at no figure, by people this office cannot pay, and entered on the office's own page at no figure and against the office's own interest, **and a cistern holding for a fortnight is not a repair account.** The ninth seam out of service since the first of May **with an end of it entered on Monday the third of July, being the first of September, and the first thing this office has entered in five months that a person can use, and entered for the reason it was entered.** The north berth out. The third lay full, which is a full and not a repair. **The north berth out and the ninth seam out and the third lay full, and those three lines out of a document this harbor's own reeve sent up this coast in May for nothing, are now in a list in a city being made up this week, and this yard published them and is entered as the commonest cause.** The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The mole-head light not on and a light costs oil. The repair account empty since the middle of February. No boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle. The hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters, in a naval file since Thursday the twenty-second and in four lines of a letter in a herring skipper's coat. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. Eleven copies and a list of eleven on a wall and a printed sheet in a market and a printed sheet going to press in a city in July. **A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found, one line in this book, and this yard has not gone and has asked nobody anything.**

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope lent at nothing and entered at nothing, and they are not in a shed and they are not in a boat and they are not going to be in either.

**Three returns on the road to a city: the fourth of May, two days late, about a lump in a bucket and a shed for six days; the tenth, on the day, about a lump worth nothing; and the seventeenth of June, on the day, about a foot and a half of worked glass that raised nothing, with nine scallops in about three inches accounted for neither time, and a mark on one face that no person in a boat identified and that this party is not going to identify.** A question written back out of a city on the twenty-ninth of June, four questions, three answered out of this yard's own book in the yard's own left hand and one not answered, and the reason given in the first line of the answer and not the last. A coast office that has had a fisher's document since the first of March and has said two sentences about it this week and put three of this harbor's own lines into a list that is being made up in a city.

**No occasion in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions since the twenty-sixth of October, and two of them with no call in them, and neither figure is moved by anything in this month and neither is moved by a measurement. The delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved.**

**And a crew of two and a half, and four marks, and a third column read on Tuesday evening and written on Wednesday morning, and a boy of twenty-two who is owed forty marks and is owed none of it and is somewhere on a herring boat with a bunk and a share and a hand that has been going since the fourth of April, and this yard has not written to him and has not gone to see him and has not asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and those three nots went in on the eighth of June in the first column with a reason against them, and they are not going to be taken out.**

**And on Wednesday the fifth there is a decision to be made about a wall, and on Thursday the sixth there is a cistern.**
