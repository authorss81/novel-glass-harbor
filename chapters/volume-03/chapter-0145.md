# Chapter 145

*Six Marks and Two Days, Asked By a Woman in a Room, and a Second Nail*

Friday the fourth of August, at about ten in the morning, in the market office of Morrow Quay, with the press unlocked under its stone slab because the counting was going on and about nine people in the room.

**The party was not there and had not arranged that anybody would be there, and the whole of what happened in that room for an hour happened because a woman of the second lay asked a question out loud in a room that is open to anybody.**

Mara Venn was at the long table with a book of her own and a pencil and her own brass calipers on the table beside her, in a cold room, in a plain coat, thirty years old, and she had a sheet of paper in front of her that was a copy of a Crown notice made in a market office press by a man who copies things for a shilling.

The woman was sixty-odd and she had a basket at her feet and she had a mother's name and she was not a member of any office in this harbor.

"**You are the one who did the glass,**" she said. "**I have heard. Will you tell me what that paper on the wall is.**"

"**No,**" said Mara Venn.

"**That is a plain answer and I have not got a plain reason for it yet.**"

"**Then here it is, and I am going to say the figure first because you have not asked me for anything and I would rather you had both together than one and then the other. Six marks and two days. That is my price, it is the same on every coast, it does not come down, and nobody in this room has six marks and two days to give me.**

"**And I am going to answer you anyway, and I am going to say the rest of it before I do, and the rest of it is this. I am not doing it for the yard. The yard is not in this room and did not ask me and would not have asked me, and if it had asked me I would have said the same figure. I have said I would not do this at nothing again and I have not said it this morning, and I am going to do this at nothing once, and I am telling everybody in this room that it is once.**

"**If anybody in this harbor wants to say that Mara Venn went to that wall for the yard, they may come and ask me and I will say what I have just said, and I would rather say it here in front of nine people than have it said for me in a market.**"

"**Ask your question,**" said the woman, "**and I will not say who asked you.**"

---

Mara Venn turned the copy over twice, and once the other way, and put her thumb on the corner of the third paragraph, and then said it out loud, and it went into her own book in her own hand with the date, unsigned, and she said why it was unsigned, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a finding of no office carries no seal and cannot be produced on demand by anybody.

"**Six things and no seventh, and you are welcome to all six and none of them is what you are asking for.**

"**One. It is not a copy of anything. I have compared it with the notice that was nailed up on Friday the twenty-eighth, which is on this table because a man copied it for a shilling on Saturday, and this is not a copy of that. It is a second original, set the same week, in the same office, from the same order, and it is longer, and the new part is in the fourth and fifth lines.

"**Two. The paper is office paper. The size is the size the harbor authority uses and it does not match the harbor authority's, and it is very slightly better stock, and the difference is a foolscap leaf that has been cut on a guillotine and not with a knife, and a man who guillotines paper is a man in an office that prints.

"**Three. The first and second paragraphs are one hand and the third and the fourth are another. The first hand is a clerk's hand, quick, and does not lift. The second hand is a naval officer's hand, which is a slow hand that has been taught not to hurry and has been taught to write on a moving deck. Those are two people. That is all I can give you and it is a fact about writing and not about the sea.

"**Four. The first notice said *any part of her*. This one says *any part of her and everything in her*. That is one clause longer and it is in the officer's own hand and it is the only line in either paper that reads like a man writing to himself and not to a wall. I do not know why a man writes a longer word in a longer notice. I have entered that I do not know and I am not going to guess.

"**Five. The fold on this one is a naval fold, folded twice and once again, and the third crease is a wet crease, made within about a day of the writing, and paper that has been wet at sea and dried in a coat is paper that has been in a pocket on a boat. That is a fact about a crease and it is the only one of the six that I would put in front of a man with a seal, and I am putting it in front of you, and you are not a man with a seal.

"**Six. The seal is not a seal. It is a stamp, and it is struck with ink and not with wax, and it is off by about a sixteenth of an inch to the right of where it should be, and that is a person working at a table in a hurry and not a person keeping a record.

"**And the seventh thing, which is the one you are going to be disappointed by and which I am not going to pretend to have. I cannot tell you what is in that boat. Nobody in this harbor can. The trade that can is this trade, and it is six marks and two days, and I have not got six marks either, and I have not got two days, and I have forty marks standing in this harbor since the fifteenth of October which nobody has paid and which I have written on the mole's steps in my own hand, and I am owed it and I am not going to do a second thing at nothing to get closer to it.**"

Somebody at the back of that room said something about bells.

She did not turn round.

"**No,**" she said.

---

**And at about two in the afternoon on Friday the fourth of August a tender came inside the line and a man in a coat came up the boards with a mallet and took the first notice off the market wall, and stood looking at the wall for about a minute, and then nailed the second one up on the same nails.**

> *To all masters, owners and persons having or claiming vessels in the roads and inshore of Morrow Quay. The notice of the twenty-eighth instant is superseded, is withdrawn from this wall, and is not to be kept or copied.*
>
> *The vessel named HEARTHWAKE, of about forty tons, lying with a list of about seven foot to starboard inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks of this harbor, is claimed by the Crown as Crown material, the said vessel being one of a formation of vessels described in a schedule held elsewhere and not produced to this harbor, and the said vessel and every part of her contents being thereby Crown material.*
>
> *Any person who shall board, enter, work upon, take from, or otherwise render assistance to the said vessel, otherwise than by the order of the officer commanding the said vessel, or shall remove from her any part of her, or any part of her contents, is required to report the fact in writing to the harbor authority of this harbor within four days, and no report made under the notice of the twenty-eighth instant is to be relied on.*
>
> *The officer commanding will render no assistance to the said vessel or to any person in her. The said vessel is Crown material and her people are aboard her at their own risk and in defiance of an order given to them in the month of June and not obeyed.*
>
> *Given at Morrow Quay on the fourth day of August instant. Veyra Holt, Master, commanding.*

**The crew of this yard did not go and read it. Two people of the third lay brought a copy up the hill at about four in the afternoon and put it on the bench, and the shipwright read it out in the shed with the door shut, and Calder Marr wrote the difference into the first column and nothing else.**

*That on Friday the fourth of August instant a notice in the same form and with the same signature was nailed upon the market wall of this harbor on the same nails, in the place of the notice of the twenty-eighth, and that the said notice is longer by about five lines; and that the said notice states that the vessel and every part of her contents are Crown material, which the notice of the twenty-eighth did not state; and that this party has entered the difference and has entered that it does not know why a longer notice contains a longer word, and that it has not asked and is not going to; and that this party has not said one word to any person in this harbor about what is in that boat, or that there is anything in that boat, or that anybody has told anybody that there is anything in that boat, and that it has not said so and will not say so; and that the two clauses in that notice which this party cannot answer are the second and the third, and that the second is a rule about what may not be touched and the third is a rule about what will not be helped, and that this party has spent three days working out how a rule is stopped and has worked out that a rule is stopped by nobody going.*

---

**And on Saturday the fifth of August nothing came in or out of that yard, and at about eleven in the morning a man of the third lay who was not the man of the twenty-ninth of July came up the hill and said one thing and went straight back down.**

"**Her pump stopped,**" he said. "**About ten. It stopped for about an hour and then it started again and it is not going as fast as it was. And a lamp went up in her rigging about twenty past ten and somebody in the boat of the *Kestrel's Sister* on the inner shoal said three short and one long, and the *Kestrel's Sister* put a lamp up in her own rigging and gave back two short and one long and a long, and then both lamps came down.

"**I do not know what any of that is and I am not going to find out and I am not going to tell anybody I do know, because I have got a boat of my own to go out on at twelve and I have not got the tide for it if I stand on this hill talking to a salvage crew about lamps.**"

---

**And in the shed that evening the shipwright wrote the whole of it in the first column, and there is one line in it that is the reason this yard is going to be poorer on Tuesday than it was on Saturday.**

*That on Saturday the fifth of August instant, at about twenty past ten in the morning, a lamp was hoisted in the rigging of a vessel with four persons in her lying inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks, and answered from a boat on the inner shoals; and that this party has entered the fact, the hour, the two boats and the three signals in the first column because a man said them in a shed and any man in this harbor can ask him about them; and that this party does not know what three short and one long means and does not know what two short and one long and a long means; and that this party has entered that it does not know, and has entered the reason, which is that a code is a practice and a practice is the only thing a community keeps that a stranger cannot read off a book, and this party has been in this harbor five months and has learned nine places out of four books in a press and has learned not one practice of anybody, and if it starts learning one now because a boat is going down, it will be learning one because a boat is going down; and that is refused, and the refusal is entered.*

**And the shipwright read it back and Calder Marr read it back after her and the shed was shut, and there were seven marks in that bag and it had been seven since Wednesday, and a wage of four was falling on Tuesday the eighth, and the fourth of August was a Friday, and it was already gone.**
