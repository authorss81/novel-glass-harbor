# Chapter 140

*Four Days of the Third Lay, and Nothing in a Bag*

The wage went in on Tuesday the twenty-fifth of July at about eleven in the morning, four marks, in Calder Marr's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it in the same hand, and the envelope went into the bench chest and is not going anywhere this week and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a wage is not a reason to go to a door.

**And the bag was four marks and the wage was four marks, and the bag is nothing, and it is nothing at about eleven in the morning on the twenty-fifth of July.**

**Fourteen lines and fifty-six marks in the wage column. The fifth of March the oldest. Nine envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere in nine weeks.**

The two of them stood in the shed and looked at the middle drawer for a while.

"**Write it down and put it on the page and do not do anything else with it,**" said the shipwright. "**A bag at nothing is not a story and it is not a low point. It is a figure, and the figure is that everything that comes into this yard is a man's wage or a man's day on a man's own account, and everything that goes out of it is a man's wage or a pot of air at two marks, and a wage that is exactly paid is not a week.**

"**And put the reason next to it, and the reason is not the reason it was at nothing for five days in April. In April this yard was living on a creditor's page. In July this yard is living on a wage, and a wage is not a page, a wage is a man's four marks for six days' work that he has not been told about and that has not been carried to him, and there is no man in this harbor who can be asked about it because he is on a herring boat.**"

It went in the first column and she read it back and he read it back after her and there was nobody else in the shed to read it back after that.

**Alden Marr was at the far bench from about eight until about six with his back to the room and a cough that came every forty minutes, and he was sixty-one, and he was not asked for anything on either day of this week and asked nothing, and neither brother went over to him, and that is a single line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week by asking the other one.**

---

**And the reeve entered something in his own book on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of July, in his own hand, at about eleven in the morning, and he told nobody until about two, and this party heard about it at about two and was not in the room and is not a party to it.**

> *That the footing of this mole from the third seam to the twelfth is to be examined; that the examination is at the cost of this office; that it is set for the second of August and the moorings of the third lay are to be closed for the four days of it; that the repair account of this office has been empty since the middle of February and the money is not coming; and that the entry is made nevertheless, at the cost of this officer, and the reason is that an office whose instruments nobody contests can enter an instrument with nothing behind it, and that is worse than no instrument, and this office has an end of the first of September on the ninth seam's mooring and has had nothing else entered in five months that a person in this harbor can use.*

**Nobody thanked him and he did not ask to be thanked and there was nothing in that room to thank him with.**

**And the price landed on the third lay, on the Wednesday afternoon, in a market, in about forty people, out of the mouth of a man of the third lay who had no office and had not been asked.**

"**Four days. The second, the third, the fourth and the fifth of August, and the tide is right for us on the second and the third and wrong on the fourth and worse on the fifth, and a lay shut for four days is a lay shut for the best two of them.**

"**And the north berth is out and the third lay is full, so a boat of ours comes back into a full harbor into no lay at all, and goes out again, or lies in the roads four days paying nothing and losing four days.**

"**And I want to say the rest in this order because the order matters. A cistern behind the engine house has held since the nineteenth of June. A mole's footing is going to be looked at. Both are true, and one of them is a lid and one of them is a hole, and I said the word *town* out loud in this market on the sixth of July and I am not going to say it again, and I am not angry, and I am not asking anybody for anything.**"

Nobody asked him for anything.

**And a woman of no office said the one thing about the four days that none of the twenty people in that market could have said, and she said it on the boards and she did not come up the hill.**

"**Four days with a lay shut is four days with a hull's bottom out of the water and nobody on it,**" Mara Venn said. "**That is a trade fact and not a complaint. If the weather comes in on the fifth of August there is a boat in this harbor with four days of fouling on her bottom and nobody able to scrub her, and that happened in this harbor in the year before I was born and it happened twice, and I know it because the men who were in it are still alive and are standing in this market.**"

---

**And at about four in the afternoon on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of July the holder of the writ went down to the mole's steps and asked the reeve one question in the open air, in about nine people's hearing, and the reeve answered it in nine words, and the nine words are in a record with a date on it.**

"**Is any of this about the plate at the fifth seam.**

"**It is not and I will not tell you what it is about.**"

**And that is the whole of what either of them got, and neither is pretending otherwise, and it is entered in the yard's own book in the first column because a man said it on a mole's steps in nine people's hearing.**

*That on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of July instant the holder asked the officer of this harbor authority, at the top of the steps, in about nine people's hearing, whether the entry of the twenty-sixth is about the plate at the fifth seam, and the officer said that it is not and that he will not tell him what it is about; and that the party has entered both halves of that and does not know what the entry is about and has not asked and is not going to; and that the plan of the seventeenth of January is refused, is in a record with a date on it, and is not reopened, and no person of this party has said a word about that plate since the seventeenth of May, which is seventy days, except a man in a room in June who said he was not going to say one word about it and has not; **and that the notice of the thirty-first of October is not lifted, is not narrowed and is not altered, and there are eight acknowledgements in that office's ledger and both of this year's are spent and this party entered none this month and asked for none and is not going to ask for one, and that the third of those joints, and the fourth, and the fifth, and the sixth, are landward of the third seam's mark, and that this party entered the ground of every day of work it did this month in the first column, and that a man in a city can check every one of them against a notice, and the notice does not reach them, and the party has said that out loud four times this month and is not going to stop saying it.***

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Wednesday evening, in the wind, with the market gone in and about nine people in the street and the mole head's light not on behind them.

**Nothing in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid and of no use whatever, because the thing after the gland is the hose, and there is not one in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms. **Fourteen lines and fifty-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and nine envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the first of August.** The tin empty and empty since the twenty-eighth of April.

**And the whole of the money that has come into this yard since the twenty-fourth of June is twenty-two marks, and the eight marks that were in this bag on the fifteenth of June went out in June as three weeks of a wage and a pot of air, and this month four weeks of a wage went out and two pots of air went out, and there is nothing in the bag, and no office in this harbor has paid this yard a mark since the eighth of May and no office in this harbor is going to.**

Forty marks of claim to a boy of twenty-two, standing whole, unpaid, not reduced, not asked to be reduced, and a wage of four a week going into an envelope, and **nobody in this crew writes to him, nobody goes to see him, and nobody asks anybody who has seen him what he said, and those three nots went in on the eighth of June and are not being taken out this month or any month.** Forty marks to a conservator of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, **and on Sunday she said out loud at the top of this mole's steps that a trade that says yes to nothing is not a trade, and the party entered it whole and disputed no word of it, and the party will keep asking her and will not ask her to be useful, and those are two different things.** Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, undiminished, unpaid, not paid in two months, **in no sentence together, and on the twenty-eighth of July a crew of Ashtide is going to take about four hundred feet of one of them's own warp off the bottom of this harbor for twenty-six marks and a day, and this yard told her to go to him and is not going in the way of it, and that man has a compressor and forty fathoms of his own hose because of a decision this yard published on the seventh of June.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid on the twenty-ninth of April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, not paid in two months, **and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it, and that standing sentence is unreversed.** Twenty marks of unsold iron and four hundred and forty pounds of old iron in three pieces with a bell-metal gun-port among them, in a heap behind the shed, unsold, unweighed, unpriced, **not looked at this month**, and the man who would know bell metal at a glance is of another boat and is not asked.

A cistern charged on Monday the twenty-fourth, the twelfth time, at no figure, and twelve walks on that office's own page since the end of May, and **a dated line in the yard's own book for Monday the seventeenth saying that this party was on the second seam from seven in the morning until about two and could not walk a four-hour gate with the same three men, and that the party has entered that it did not walk it.** The ninth seam out of service since the first of May with an end of it entered on the third of July, being the first of September. **And the examination of the mole's footing from the third seam to the twelfth entered on the twenty-sixth for the second of August, at the cost of an office whose repair account is empty, closing the third lay for four days, and the price is not going to fall on this party.** The north berth out. The third lay full, and a full is not a repair. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose will not be rolled until the autumn. The mole-head light not on. No boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April and none since. The *Kittiwake* on the hard, and no cradle under her. The hard at Ashtide, and it has been unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters in a naval file and in four lines of a letter and in a list in a city. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. **Eleven copies, a list of eleven on a wall since the third of May, four of them not reached and not asked, a printed sheet over the top of it since the twenty-fourth of June with the yard's own figures and a scale and a price, and a sheet of the yard's own on top of that since the twenty-second of July with no scale and no price and a Crown attachment on it that a man in a city is obliged to look at, and a printer in that city told in advance who cannot be stopped and will print it.** A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, one line in this book, and this yard has not gone and has asked nobody anything. **And a man of fifty-four out of a storm belt who keeps a channel nine miles off the point with two other men and has kept it nineteen years, who has a figure of his own for the bar of two and a half on the flood off a spring and two on the ebb, who offered to come up this coast in the autumn and look at five miles of that coast for nothing, and who is still coming, and that there is no instrument in this harbor that can call him and none that can stop him.**

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope lent at nothing and entered at nothing, and one of the tarpaulins has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June. **And six joints four fathoms under that stone, and the crown is nine feet above them, and the whole business of this month has happened underneath a tarpaulin this yard has walked past four times a day since the twenty-eighth of April.**

**Four returns on the road to a city and a fifth not made.** The fourth of May, two days late. The tenth of May, on the day. The seventeenth of June, on the day. The fourteenth of July, on the day, about a thumbnail, in a sealed jacket with a copy into the harbor authority's ledger, and the clerk said on the steps that he cannot enter what a thumbnail of glass is and has said so seven times this year. **And dated lines in this yard's own book for the seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth, twentieth and twenty-fourth of July saying that no finding of worked glass was made and nothing was raised and no return goes up on any of those days, and that line is a fact about this yard's book and is not in any office's book and is not sent to anybody, and there is no fifth return and there is not going to be one out of a day that produced nothing.**

A question written back out of a city on the twenty-ninth of June, four questions, three answered out of this yard's own book in its own left hand and one not answered, the reason in the first line, and a letter of three lines to the same printer on the twelfth of July at no cost. A coast office with two sentences and a list.

**Nothing has been entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and neither figure has moved in six weeks and neither is moved by a measurement. The delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and was not taken. The mole head's handbell went on the third of July, the count of four is in the first column in a shipwright's hand with no name against it, and the fourth of those four has a word in it now, entered on the twentieth, in the second column, with a name and a reason, and the four above it have no name and have not been struck out, and a request to strike a line out of a column is refused.**

**And a crew of two and a half, and a third column filled twice this month — on the tenth at about two hours, and on the twenty-second at about three hours — and the party has entered that the two figures are not a trend and is not going to draw anything out of them, and the head of that column has two times on it that a boy of twenty-two put there on the second of May, and a line under the head in the holder's own left hand saying that the lead plug is not where that goes.**

---

**And then, at about half past six, a man of the third lay who had stood at the back of that read-out for twenty minutes asked a question in the street, in nine people's hearing, and the yard did not have an answer ready, and that is entered as a fact about the party and not as anything else.**

"**There is a boat lying off the outer marks with people in her,**" he said. "**She is not a wreck. She is a working boat with a crew on her and she has been lying there since before the hard at Ashtide was shut, and the tide gang saw her on the ninth and has walked past her every day since and nobody has told them anything about her.**

"**Her name is the *Hearthwake*. And I am told by a man of a boat of mine that she is one of the nine, and I do not know what that means and neither do you, and I have said her name out loud in a market because if a man in this harbor has a boat off the outer marks with people in her, then a man in this harbor should be the one who says her name and not a man in a city.**"

Nobody in that street said anything for about half a minute, and the mole head's light was not on behind them, and the wind was up.

"**The outer marks stand where the writ of the first of May says they are,**" Calder Marr said, "**and the bar lies four hundred yards off them, and this party is not going past them this season and has not tried and is not going to, and that is on the page since the fifth of July and it is the fourth of four things this party entered on its own account without being asked.**

"**And I am going to say the rest of it, because that man has just handed us something in a market and the yard is going to be careful in front of nine people about what it does with it. There is a boat with people in her off the outer marks. There is a thing in this harbor that says the fleet's formation is legible and that it was kept by hand, in several places, over a long time, and by people who wrote down where they worked. And neither of those is a reason to go and look, and this party is not going to go and look, and it is not going to send anybody, and it is not going to ask that man a single question about her.**"

"**Then what are you going to do with it.**"

"**Write it down where anybody can check it,**" said Nessa Pike, "**which is a page in a book in a shed on a hill, and that is all, and it is not nothing, and it is not enough, and both of those are on the page.**"

**And that is where the twenty-sixth of July ends, and the bag is empty, and the column is fourteen lines, and nine envelopes are on a bench, and a man in a city with a press is going to print a Crown attachment off a market wall in about three weeks and cannot be stopped, and a boat with people in her is lying off the outer marks that this party is not going to look at, and six joints are four fathoms under a stone with nine fastenings in about seven feet of them and a chip of glass put in the packing on purpose by somebody who expected somebody to come.**
