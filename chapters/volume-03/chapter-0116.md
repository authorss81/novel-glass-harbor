# Chapter 116

*Four Hundred Feet of Warp*

They put it in front of her on the Tuesday the sixth of June at about eleven in the morning on the second lay's own boards, in daylight, in the wind, with the herring fleet lying against the wall above her and about thirty people within hearing and not one of them asked to move.

Nessa Pike read the column out. Calder Marr wrote it out beforehand in his own left hand and it went into the yard's own book at eight that morning with the hour, and it is the longest single column this yard has entered since the first of May and it is the only one that has been read out to a woman standing in front of it.

*The whole of the cost, in a column a man can check: that about four hundred feet of a woman's own warp — a hundred and thirty yards and more of the best tarred stuff on this coast — is lying along the second lay's wharf of the third lay under about six feet, and has been since the sixth of February, and is hers; that a whole boat's day at this yard's own rate for a day's work at a seam on a wall, one man in the water and a tender on the lead line, is twenty-six gross; that off the top of it the eighth is two and the share is two, and the yard has never argued either; that out of this yard's own book the office's pot of air at this yard's own two-mark rate is two, a hired hand is three, the victuals of the day are two and rope is one; that that is twelve out of twenty-six and the yard's own column of one day is twelve; and that the job is not a one-day job, and that no man in this harbor can say in figures what a second day is because nobody has ever done a second day of it, and this yard is not going to invent a figure for a thing it has not done.*

*And the yard's bag on the morning of the sixth of June is eight.*

"Eight," Sella Brigg said.

"**Eight. And that is the whole of it, and I have put the number in front of you in the same breath as the job and not after it, because a yard that tells a woman what a thing costs and then tells her what it would cost if it were dearer is doing a piece of work with the second number and I am not going to do it.**"

"You have not told me what it would cost if it were dearer," Sella Brigg said. "You have told me it is not a one-day job and that nobody knows what a second day is. **That is the first time in forty-one years that anybody in this harbor has put a figure in front of me and then stopped at the figure.**"

"It is money," Calder Marr said. "**It is not the notice and it is not the closure and it is not a court and it is not a clerk and I am not going to stand here and let a thing be talked about as though it were any of those four, because the last time a person in this yard put a rule in front of a woman instead of a figure she went away and had to think about it for a week.**"

"I know what the notice is," Sella Brigg said. "**I have a warp at the bottom of this harbor that came off in a fire and the yard that could have had it up had a rule about glass and a boy with a bad hand and I have not blamed anybody for a week that has not been six months and I am not blaming anybody for this one.**"

---

**Elias Voke came down to the second lay at about one in the afternoon because his own boat's day was booked for Saturday and because there was a thing he wanted said out loud in a place where a book was open.**

He is fifty-one and he has been in this harbor since he was a boy and he has nineteen winters of dues disputed and unpaid, and the dues are going up a coast in a file with the harbor authority's own account of what the notice of the thirty-first of October has cost it, and that account was entered on the eighteenth of May and made those dues harder to dispute and not easier, and he put his own name and his own boat's name and the nineteen winters in it in his own hand in a room in front of a clerk.

"**Two warps, two grounds, two owners, one answer,**" he said, "and I want the one answer written down as mine and not as yours, because I have said it three times this year in three places and I would like it on a page in a market."

"Go on."

"The warp of the twenty-eighth of February, which is a Tuesday, off this mole's own face between the first and second seams, on the boat's own account, and that warp belongs to a boat of the third lay and that man is a fool for having let it go and he knows it. **The warp of Friday the sixteenth of April, at the second seam, about four feet of it come on a ring, and that warp belongs to a different boat of a different lay and that man is not a fool at all and has never once been in this harbor in a state of mind to be a fool in.**"

"They are two warps."

"They are two warps and two grounds and two owners, and the answer you are going to give both of them today is the same answer, and that is fair, and I want it written as mine. **I am not asking you to find one warp. I am asking you to stop the two of them being in the same place, because the day they are in the same place on a page it is a page about a harbor that cannot afford rope, and in four years somebody will read it as a page about a harbor where rope goes.**"

"It goes on the page in your own words," Nessa Pike said, "and the answer goes on it too, and the answer is that both of them are in the same place and the answer is true, and the reason is that this yard is two and a half men with eight marks in a bag and the truth about rope and the truth about a yard are the same truth this month and I am not going to pretend to a herring skipper that they are two things."

"That is the first time anybody has refused to separate two things for my own good," Voke said, "and I am going to say the other half of it, because I have been owed for nineteen winters and I have got used to being owed. **The answer is true and it is about a yard and not about a warp, and I would rather have a yard that gives me the true answer than a yard that gives me a kind one, and I am not going to let anybody put this on a wall and call it a fact about me.**"

---

The column was read at the head of this yard's own dock at about six on the Tuesday evening with about nine people in the street.

**Eight marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the note and not re-written, and the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and none of them moved. Nothing in a tin. **Seven lines and twenty-eight marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and the fourth mark of the week of Tuesday the sixth to Monday the twelfth went into an envelope on the bench this afternoon with the week written on it in the same hand, and the envelope is not among the five rolled in oilcloth and is not going anywhere this week.**

And then, on the Wednesday the seventh of June, at about ten past seven in the morning, with the market coming in behind them and about forty people in the street, a sheet went up on the market wall under the naval paper that has been on that wall since the eighteenth of March, which is the only way anybody in this harbor can put a sheet up, and no man has ever been asked to move that paper and no man ever will be.

It is not a map. It has no water on it and no figures about water and no word on it for what a thing with a cairn in it is.

*That on Wednesday the seventh of June instant, at the head of the boards, this party entered the whole of the cost of raising about four hundred feet of a woman's own warp from the second lay's wharf of the third lay, being a whole boat's day at this yard's own rate, twenty-six gross, the eighth two, the share two, the office's pot of air two, a hired hand three, the victuals two and rope one, that being twelve out of twenty-six and the yard's own column of one day twelve, and the job not being a one-day job and no figure for a second day being given because this yard has never done a second day of it; and that the party did not do the work and is not going to do it this year; and that the reason is money, and is not the notice of the thirty-first of October, and is not any closure, and is not a court and not a clerk; and that about four hundred feet of that warp has been on the bottom of this harbor since the sixth of February, which is a hundred and twenty-one days, and that it is the property of Sella Brigg of the third lay and of nobody else; and that the party is owed that woman eight shillings and tenpence, undiminished and unpaid since the sixth of February, and did not use that debt against her and would not and entered that she asked them to enter that she could see them not using it.*

*And that on the sixth of June instant, in a market, at the head of the boards, in about thirty people's hearing, the owner of that warp was told the whole of the cost in this yard's own words and refused it, and said that she would say no at four marks as well as at twelve, and that no rule of any office was the reason and none was offered to her as the reason; and that the yard is of the view that a refusal which is not written down is a different object from a refusal which is, and that the second one is harder to keep and is the one anybody can use in a court in Veyr or in a market in this town, and that this yard has therefore put it on a wall where anybody may take it down again and copy it and hold it up in front of the party that wrote it.*

*And that nothing on this sheet is a claim, and nothing on it binds anybody, and nothing on it is a seal and nothing on it is a survey, and that this yard has declared a thing about its own gear in a book and that a buyer of anything of ours may read that declaration first, and that this sheet is that declaration applied to one refusal.*

**Sella Brigg read it out herself, at about half past eight, standing back from the wall with her thumb going down it, and she did not read it twice, and she was the seventh person in this harbor to read back a page off that wall and the first one who was owed something on it.**

"You have put my name on a market wall," she said, when she had finished.

"Your name and the date it was not raised and the figure."

"**Then it is a debt and not a map, and I want everybody in this street to know which it is, because in four years there is going to be a man come up here with a chart in his hand and he is going to look at this wall and say to somebody, that is them, that is the yard that has a road.**" She took her hand off her name. "**It is not a road and it is not a map and it is four hundred feet of my own rope and a figure I could not pay, and I would rather it was on this wall than in a file. And I am not going to thank you, because you have told me a price out loud in a market and then left me with it, and that is not a kindness and it is the first honest thing anybody has done about that rope in four months.**"

Nessa Pike said, "It goes in the yard's own book with her words and I am not reading it back to her because she has told me she does not read back a thing another person wrote about her, and I am going to honor that on the first occasion I have had to."

A man with a slate of his own, with a string round it, was in the market at about nine and read the whole of it standing in front of it and did not offer to buy a copy and was not asked to and did not ask to be told anything, and he wrote about nine lines on his own slate afterward and did not show them to anybody and went down the boards.

**And that is the thing that changed in that street, and it is not a small thing, and nobody in it enjoyed it.** Two returns and a refusal in a yard's own book is a page in a shed. A refusal with a woman's name on it and a figure on it and a date on it, nailed up where the market goes past, is a thing that can be carried to a different town by a man with a slate and read out there, and it cannot be taken back, and it cannot be put in a margin, and the party that wrote it has to go on being the party that wrote it. Sella Brigg has made herself, in writing, in a public place, a creditor of this yard's **work**, and that is not the same thing as a creditor of this yard's **money**, and it is a worse thing, because a creditor of money is paid and a creditor of work has to be answered.

And the harbor authority's repair account is still empty and the mole-head light is still not on and the ninth seam is still out of service with no end of it entered, and nothing on that wall fixed any of it and nothing on that wall was meant to.

On the Wednesday afternoon the yard carried the sheet's figure into the yard's own book and copied it out for the position, and at about seven the columns were read out at the head of the dock.

**Eight marks in a bag. Thirty-one on the note. Nothing in a tin. Seven lines and twenty-eight marks in the wage column and the fifth of March the oldest and three envelopes on a bench.** Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she has not been asked to be useful and is not an assistant and the writ cannot reach her. **Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg, undiminished, unpaid, with her name and a figure and a date on a market wall since the seventh of June.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, and the same again owing since the twenty-third of May.

No air in this harbor and no delivery hose and a gland fitted and not paid for. A hand lead's line that is right. The ninth seam out, the north berth out, the third lay full, the tide gang four men short, a cistern charged twice this week by people nobody pays, the fire party's hose dead, the light not on, this office with no boat of its own, the *Kittiwake* on the hard, the hard at Ashtide, nineteen winters, the *Sea Wren* at the pile, eleven copies and four unreached holders not asked, a man of the third lay nine winters not found. A lump of green glass in a bucket in a boat on the mole's crown with a finger's depth of water in it. Two lines in a second column on two pages and no figure in either. Eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved.

**A man of fifty-four out of a storm belt who is coming back in the autumn, and who made his figure two and a half against this yard's two and three-quarters and will not be told which is right, and whose boat has one of the six hand-copied sheets in her and the count of the six does not move because a count of hands is not a count of whereabouts.**

**And on Thursday the eighth of June a herring skipper is going to offer a berth and a season's hand in it to anybody in this harbor who wants them, and there is a bonesetter's book in this town with a boat hand in June in it, and a boy of twenty-two who is not on this yard's stone and has not been since the fifteenth of May, and nobody in this shed is going to write to him and nobody is going to go and see him and nobody is going to ask anybody who has seen him what he said, and all three of those go in this yard's own book tonight with a reason against each one.**
