# Chapter 132

*Nine in About Seven Feet*

On Sunday the ninth of July the two of them had the piece of metal on a cloth on the bench with the lamp on it and they did not agree about what it was made of, and they were both of them half right and neither of them knew it.

"**Cast iron,**" said the shipwright. "**It has been in salt water a hundred and forty days and it has not gone, and cast iron does not go like that, and a wrought nail in that water would be a wire by now.**"

"**It is not cast, because it has a chisel on the head of it and you do not chisel a casting,**" Calder Marr said. "**And I know that and you know that and neither of us knows anything about metal, and that is the whole of the difference between us.**"

"**Then we do not know what it is made of, and that goes in the second column, and the reason is that two people in a shed are not a foundry.**"

"And we are not paying a foundry," Calder Marr said.

"**Six marks and two days to have anything entered at a counter on this coast,**" said the shipwright, "**and that is a figure I have had off a conservator of no office in a market at seven in the morning on the nineteenth of May and it is the same figure in Veyr and in Ashtide and in this harbor, and it has not gone down and it is not going to go down because the person is difficult. Six marks is not in this bag. Two days is two days of the two of us.**"

"So we go and get the packing off it," Calder Marr said, "**and we take the packing to somebody and not the metal, because a packing is a thing you can put in front of anybody and a piece of metal about the length of my hand is a thing you have to know metal for, and I do not know metal and neither do you.**"

---

**And at about half past three on the Sunday afternoon they went up on the crown above the third seam's mark and stood on it, both of them, for about ten minutes, in the wind.**

The two tarpaulins were about nine feet apart where they have been since the twenty-eighth of April, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope lent at nothing and entered at nothing, and the one over the crate had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June, not lashed back because lashing a corner back on a stone crown in a westerly takes three men and a morning.

**The whole business is nine feet under their boots and has been all summer and neither of them had thought about it once.**

"**I have stood on this stone four months,**" the shipwright said, "**and I looked at that tarpaulin every day and did not once think about what is under it.**"

"**Neither did I. Put that in. Not in the second column. In the first, because a woman stood on a stone in the open air in a westerly and said it, and any man in this harbor can come up this hill tomorrow and ask her whether she said it.**"

---

The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday the tenth of July and it came down bare, and that was ten Mondays.

**And the cistern was charged at about ten, on the rota, the ninth time, at no figure, and the gate took three of them from ten until about two, and nobody was paid for it and nobody was thanked and nobody asked for anything.**

A day at the second seam on a hirer's own account. The hirer was a master of the second lay who wanted a day on the face with a ring and a shackle of his own, and he paid at the mole, which is what a hirer is for.

Gross on the board thirteen. The eighth two. The share two. A hired hand three. The victuals of the day one and rope one. All three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being nine out of thirteen and the yard's being four. The office's pot of air is not on this day, because it went out of the bag on Friday the seventh and was spent in the water on the Saturday.

**Two marks in a bag on Monday morning, and what went out of it on Friday was the pot, and four marks came into it at about two in the afternoon off a man of the second lay, and there is six in it, and it is the first money in the bag since the first of July.**

The six were read out in order at the boat before anybody was wet, in daylight, on the second seam's own face landward of the third seam's mark, with the shipwright holding the line. Named tender who is not the diver. Independent line, on the boat's own bow. Fixed air limit on the slate, set at the boat by the diver and entered as the diver's, and it was seventeen and he set it himself and would not let the holder raise it. Seated named weight. A cutoff the tender can pull without asking anybody, and it is hers, and the word is *Enough*. And a stated reason, entered in the yard's own book before anybody went in the water, in the holder's own left hand, unedited, with the clerk absent and the absence on the page.

**Nobody was this party. Both men in the water were hired and both were paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, and this party stood on the mole's steps and did not go down and was not asked to.**

---

They cleared the foot of the hull from about nine.

**The hull lying against the second seam's face is not one hull and has never been on the page as one, and there is no finding in this yard's book about it, and nobody in this harbor has claimed it, and it has been lying against that face since before there was a mole head.**

At about half past eleven the hired hand brought up on the ring a lump about the size of two fists.

It was not a stone. It was not oakum. It was not lime, and it was not pitch, and it was not any of the four things a boat in this harbor is packed with, and it came up in one piece and it came off the ring in one piece and it did not break and it did not crumble, and it had a skin on the outside of it about a quarter of an inch thick and the inside of it was a different color from the outside of it by about that much again.

"**That is lime and something,**" said the shipwright. "**And I have said *something*, and I have not said what, and I am not going to say what, and that goes in the second column.**"

---

**And at about ten past four in the afternoon, on the ring, out of the tallow of the lead, with the lead itself not touched, a chip of green glass about the length of a thumbnail came up.**

It is sea-worn to a frosted face on one side and it is green through the body and cloudy, and it is thicker at one end than the other by about a sixteenth, and it has a scalloped edge of about three in about half an inch, all the same hand, and none of them running out of a point.

The shipwright did not touch it. She stood over it on the deck of the boat with her hands behind her back for about a minute and a half.

"**Do not touch that,**" she said. "**Not because it is glass. Because it is in the tallow of a lead and it came off a working surface, and the moment one of us puts a thumb on it a person in a city is going to be told that a party handled its own finding, and that is the whole of what is left to check on it.**"

"**Then it stays where it is and the ring comes up with it on it and it goes on the cloth on the bench and nobody touches it again.**"

"**And the compound goes on a different cloth,**" said the shipwright, "**and that is not sentiment, that is two objects and one page and a man in a city who is going to lay them side by side.**"

---

The reason went in at ten past six in the yard's own book, in Calder Marr's own left hand, unedited, with the clerk not present and the absence entered rather than filled in.

*That on Monday the tenth of July instant, at about ten past four in the afternoon, on the second seam's own face of this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account and with both men in the water hired and paid at the mole by the man who hired the day and neither of them of this party, a lump about the size of two fists and a chip of green glass about the length of a thumbnail came up on the ring and in the tallow of the lead; that this ground is ground no party claims and ground the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first does not reach, and that this party has entered that the first limb of the reporting clause catches the chip by itself and that this party has not needed the second limb and does not know that it would; and that the day of the finding is not counted and a return upon the writ of the first of May falls due on Friday the fourteenth of July instant, being the fourth day after it; and that this party has entered that date and has entered that it is not going to try to move it and has entered that nobody in this yard is to tell anybody in a market that a date is not a date.*

---

**First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor:** that the lump is about the size of two fists and does not crumble and does not break and has a skin on the outside of it about a quarter of an inch thick and the inside of it a different color from the outside of it by about that much again; that it is not stone, not oakum, not lime and not pitch; that the chip is green through the body and cloudy, thicker at one end than the other by about a sixteenth, sea-worn to a frosted face on one side, with a scalloped edge of about three in about half an inch, all the same hand, none of them running out of a point; **and that the party has seen a scalloped edge of the same hand before, twice, on two different objects out of two different grounds, on the sixth of May and the tenth of June, and can account for it neither time and is not going to be asked about it and is not going to say whether it is three times or not, and the count is going on no wall and in no column.**

**And the line of what it is not, in full, because in four years somebody is going to bring this chip up next to four other objects and ask whether they are the same.** That it is not the lump of the sixth of May and not the shard of the tenth of June and not the panel this party gave back in a market on the seventh of April and not a plate and not the ferry plate and not the plate at the fifth seam; **that it is not connected to the lead plug in the chest in this shed, or to a mark on a stone, or to the compartment, whose finding stands as it has stood in three steps and is not restated here, or to the run under this town, or to the coast road, or to a bar of gravel, or to a cairn of ballast stones, or to a stem on the Nine Foot;** that it is not a mark of nine joined lines and the party is not going to write one, **and that if nine of anything is a row of nine then a man can count a row of nine and this party has counted one and is not going to count it twice;** that neither object is in any office's book and neither carries a seal and neither has a number anywhere; and **that nobody is to write any two of those things together in any book in this harbor.**

**Second column, not checkable from a mole:** the material of the lump, and the reason is that two people in a shed are not a foundry and that a person of no office in this harbor who could name it has said out loud in a market that she costs six marks and two days and that is the same figure on every coast and is not going down; the material of the piece of metal of Saturday, which is on a line of its own, with the reason against it that this party does not know metal and is not going to find out this week; **and what is in the chip, which goes nowhere at all, on a line of its own, with the reason that this party cannot read what a thing carries and is not going to try and is not going to pay six marks and two days to have it read and has not got six marks.**

---

**And the third column was filled on the Monday evening, with the two times at the head of it and the lateness against them, and it is the sixth entry in that column since the second of May and the sixth since the boy went off the stone on the fifteenth, and the party is not going to draw anything out of the six figures, for the reason it gave when there were four of them: a yard which begins to keep a figure on its own lateness is a yard that has stopped keeping a figure on its work.**

*That the entry was read on the steps at about seven in the evening on Monday the tenth of July and was written in this book at about nine the same evening, the difference being about two hours, which is the same difference the boy of twenty-two used to put at the head of it, and which this party has noticed and has entered that it has noticed.*

**And at about half past five the shipwright asked the question that had been standing in the shed since the Saturday, and she asked it out loud, and there was nobody else in the shed to be asked.**

"**Then what is it joined to.**"

"**A man in a city printed a sheet about a bar of gravel in June and we could not answer a quarter of it,**" said the shipwright, "**and this is the same shape and it is worse, because he asked us a question about a number and this is a question about a joint, and we do not have a joint, we have a lump of something and a nail.**"

"**We have a place it came out of,**" Calder Marr said, "**and the place is on the chart of this harbor in my own hand and it is the second seam's own face, and the water over it is ten, and there is a hull lying against that face that has been there longer than this mole head.**"

"**That is a place,**" said the shipwright. "**And a place is what a man goes and looks at, and that is the first thing anybody in this yard has been able to say out loud since the third of May.**"

---

**And that evening, with about nine people in the street and the market gone in, the two columns were read out at the head of the dock, and the money part is short and it is this.**

Two marks in a bag on Monday morning. **Six marks in a bag at about two in the afternoon, and nothing had come into it between the first of July and this afternoon, and the four is out of a day at the second seam on a hirer's own account, gross thirteen, the eighth two and the share two and a hired hand three and the victuals one and rope one, all three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being nine out of thirteen.** The note is at thirty-one and has not been re-written and its three counts have not moved, and five marks of compressor's gland stand on it unpaid. **Eleven lines and forty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, and six envelopes on a bench and not one of them anywhere, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the eleventh of July.** Nothing in a tin.

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not asked to be useful, **and not one mark of it paid out of anything that has come into this yard since the eighth of May.** Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, undiminished, unpaid, not paid in a month. **Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, and not going to be paid this month and the standing sentence said in a street on the third of May stands.** A woman going to a man of Ashtide on the twenty-eighth of July for twenty-six marks and a day for about four hundred feet of her own warp, and this yard told her it was her business and is not going in the way of it, and her own words at the foot of the dock this morning were that the yard's own sheet is not on the wall any more and there is a man from Ashtide over the top of it with four marks at the head of it.

**And on Friday the fourteenth of July a return goes up the coast, and it is the fourth, and the day of the finding is not counted, and the yard has entered the date and has entered that it is not going to try to move it.**
