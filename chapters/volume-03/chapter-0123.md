# Chapter 123

*The Price in the Room*

The hearing room is over the tide-gang store on the west basin and it has a slate wall behind the reeve's bench, and the wall was wiped bare at eight that morning and there was no sitting, and the room was used anyway because a man with a book of his own asked a holder to produce a writ on demand and a demand is a thing that gets produced whether anybody has called a sitting or not.

There were six people in it. The reeve. The clerk, with his own page and the office ledger. The holder of the writ and the shipwright, who was four feet away from him for the whole of it. The officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor, with a notebook and a paper. And a boy of the office's tide gang who was told to be there and stood by the door and was not asked anything and wrote nothing down.

**Nothing went on the wall, because the wall is a figure for a week and there was no figure to put up, and the clerk had said in May that he had stopped mentioning the wall and had not started again.**

---

The writ was produced at ten in the morning. One sheet, in the harbor authority's own paper, in the harbor authority's seal and not a naval one, with the reporting clause attached and three names on the clause and no face in the room: requested in writing by the magistrate of that circuit, signed by the master of a cutter holding a claim in this harbor, sealed by *Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner*. It was read out by the clerk in his own voice and the fourth line of it was read out twice, because the fourth name is a hired man of another boat and both men in that room have read it twice before.

**And the crew's book was produced with it, because the clause's own construction carries it, and the yard said so out loud before it opened it, as it said in May, and the officer said he had not asked for it and the yard said he had not.**

Both columns entire. The third column entire, with the head on it and the two times and the line a man wrote under the head in his own left hand on the twenty-first of May saying that no man in that party is filling it any more, and four entries under that line since, in the same hand, each with two times at the head of it and a lateness against it, and one of them written on Sunday the eighteenth of June at about eleven in the morning about a thing read on the steps at about seven the same evening.

**All three returns.** The first, made on the fourth of May, two days late, unshortened, with the two days between the finding and the return on the page and not in a margin, and with the line that says the object was in a boat in a shed in this harbor from the twenty-eighth of April to the fourth of May, entered in the yard's own hand without anybody asking. The second, made on the tenth, on the day. The third, made on Saturday the seventeenth, on the day, about a foot and a half of worked glass that raised nothing and is worth nothing, with nine scallops in about three inches, accounted for neither time, and a mark on one face that no person in that boat identified and that the party is not going to identify.

And a dated line in the yard's own book saying that on Tuesday the thirteenth of May no finding was made and nothing was raised and no return went up, which is a fact about that book and is not in any office's book and is not sent to anybody.

"Is there a fourth," said the officer.

"**No," said Calder Marr. "**And I said that on a mole in May and I am saying it again in a room because the room is where a man writes things down, and there is no fourth, and if a fourth is asked for this party will say it has made three, and one of the three is about a thing in a shed and one is about a lump worth nothing and one is about a foot and a half in a crate in the open air, and that is all this party has found this year that is made of glass, and I would rather a man in a city had that sentence in a clerk's hand than a page of it.**"

The clerk read the hour in and read it back and said that was the sixth time this year he had been told a thing he could not enter, and he had kept all six.

---

**Then the officer put the paper on the table, and it was the same paper that had been on the mole on Saturday, with the same date on it, and the master of the cutter's signature was at the foot of it, and the name is Veyra Holt and she is not in this harbor and did not come here and is not going to.**

Kest read it. He read it twice, in his own room, at a table, with a clerk sitting down and a boy by the door.

Then he took his coat off and hung it on the back of the bench and said, "No," and then said the rest of it, and the rest of it took eleven minutes, and the clerk wrote all of it.

**"First, what this is not, and I am going to say all three of these before anybody in this room can say them for me.**

"**It is not a claim window. I want that said because a man will hear the words *claim window* in a room and go away with a thing in his head that is not on the paper. There is no claim window on that paper and I am not going to grant one and nobody has asked me for one.**

"**It is not an acknowledgement. It is not the ninth of them. There are eight in that ledger and the last two of this year are spent, the seventh on the sixth of May and the eighth on the twelfth of June, and there is no ninth and there is not going to be one in June, and no instrument of this office has put a person, a boat or a lead inside that closure since the eighth of June and is not going to. If anything ever does, it will carry my acknowledgement on the page and in that ledger at my cost, and there is nothing left in that ledger to pay for it and I would like that written down in the same breath.**

"**And it is not a work permit and it is not a charter and it does not renew anything.** The writ of the first of May runs to the first of March next and this office cannot renew it and has never said it could. The second season is not this office's to give. It will arrive out of a city from a man who has never been inside this harbor, and whoever he is, and whatever coat he is wearing, and whoever sent him, the holder will be told about it when it arrives and not before."

He put both hands flat on the table.

"**That is the whole of what is not on that paper. Now I am going to say what is on it, and what it costs, and I am going to say the cost before I pay it, because that is what this office does and it has done it eight times.**"

---

**"The vessel has asked this office twice for this office's own chart. I refused the first one in about eleven seconds in December and I have not regretted it and I am not going to pretend I did not enjoy it, and the officer of that vessel was in this room in May and knows that I enjoy saying no to a man who has a notebook.**

"**The chart is a piece of paper on which this office's own markings stand. It shows the basin, the inner ground, the mole from the first seam to the twelfth, the harbor line, the outer marks, and the bar lying four hundred yards off those outer marks, which is a bar this office put there out of its own survey and which is not on any other paper on this coast.**

"**And it carries one notation that is mine, in my own hand, from the twenty-fifth of December last, and I would like everybody in this room to know what that notation is before they decide anything, because it is the whole of the price and nobody is going to find it out by accident.**

"The bell lies in eight and a half feet off the south end on harbor gear, fouled, with a frame cut through the casting on one side and no tongue on the frame at all and two parts of a chain in a lead plug where the tongue was. And on this office's chart, in my own hand, at the south end, in the notation a chart takes, there is a line that says that the ground gear and the mooring of that bell are not to be worked, recovered, lifted or moved by any person other than this office, and that any person proposing to do so is to be given the account of the herring fleet's dues before he is given a line and a date.

"**I wrote that in December because I wanted a man in Veyr to have to read nineteen winters of herring dues before he could take a bell off a mole. That is the only piece of chart work I have done in nine years that I expect anybody ever to read, and I spent a harbor season on it.**

"**And there is no second copy of it. The notation is not in a book. The notation is on the chart, and the chart is the paper. If that chart goes into the keeping of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor, then nineteen winters of dues go out of this office's hand and into a naval file, in my own handwriting, where I cannot hold them in front of anybody and cannot take them back.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about half a minute.

"That is the price," Kest said. "**I am naming it in a room with a clerk in it because the man it is paid out of is not in this room. Mr Voke of the *Anna Ray* went up the coast yesterday afternoon and he is one day out and he does not know. Nineteen winters is not my money and it is not the yard's money and it is not this office's money. It is a herring skipper's, and he is a man of fifty-one, and he carried a plan up this coast for nothing in January and a statement of this harbor's capacity for nothing in March and took the cost of a correct refusal in this room in May without raising his voice.**

"**So I am going to write it out this afternoon in my own hand and put it in his own hand and send it up the coast by a boat that is going on Friday, and it is going to be four lines and it is not going to be a demand and it is not going to be an order and it is going to name the price, and he is going to have the price in his own hand in six days and not out of a ledger in a city in a month.**"

---

**And then he said the other thing, and it was the last thing, and he said it in nine words and he did not improve on it.**

"**I am not going to say one word about the plate at the fifth seam.** I have been told what a thing costs in this harbor this morning. I intend to find out what the price of that is before I offer anything again."

Nobody mentioned the plate again on that day.

---

**What the reeve got for it, and he said that out loud too, and this is the part that will be read about in a year.**

"**The vessel will not contest a claim window of this office, on any ground whatever, within the harbor line, until the first of March next. Every instrument this office has issued since the fourteenth of October has been contested in a room or on a mole by a man with a notebook, and I have never once been able to find out whether any of them work, because the only way to find out is to have somebody object to one that should have stood.**

"**And I have bought that. And I am telling this room what I bought it with, because a reeve who buys an uncontested instrument has bought a reeve who can no longer find out whether his own instruments work, and that is not a small thing and it is not going to look small in four years.**

"**And the chart goes into that officer's hand at ten o'clock on Thursday the twenty-second, and it is going as this office's chart and not as a copy, because the vessel asked for the paper and not for the figures, and I have declined to make a copy of a thing I am giving away.**"

The clerk read the whole of it back. It took about as long as the writing. Kest read it after his own clerk, which he does not do.

*That on Wednesday the twenty-first of June instant, at ten in the morning, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store, the officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor produced a paper signed by the master of that vessel and dated the nineteenth instant, and the officer of this office entered in this office's own book with witnesses named that the chart of this harbor, being the basin, the inner ground, the mole from the first seam to the twelfth, the harbor line, the outer marks, the bar of four hundred yards off those outer marks, and the notation in this officer's own hand of the twenty-fifth of December last upon the ground gear and mooring of the bell at the south end, is to be delivered into the keeping of that vessel on Thursday the twenty-second instant; that in exchange the vessel has withdrawn its objection to the exercise by this office of claim windows within the harbor line for the remainder of the term of the writ of the first of May; that the price is nineteen winters of disputed and unpaid moorage dues upon the *Anna Ray* of Elias Voke, which are not this office's money and are not the yard's money and are one man's, and that the owner of that boat was not in this room and was four days out; that there is no claim window in this transaction and there is no acknowledgement in it, and that both of the two acknowledgements of this year are spent and that no instrument of this office has put a person, a boat or a lead inside the closure of the notice of the thirty-first of October since the eighth of June and is not going to; and that the cost of all of it is this officer's and is not the yard's and is not nobody's.*

Nobody thanked the reeve. He did not ask to be thanked and there was nothing in that room to thank him with.

**What Calder Marr said, and he said it once, and he said it to the clerk and not to Kest, because the clerk keeps a page.**

"That entry is the ninth thing this office has done in five months and the first one that was bought. I am not going to thank you for it and I am not going to argue with it and I am not a party to it. **I want one line in my own hand and I want it in the first column, and it is that this party was in that room at ten this morning at the request of an officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor, produced the writ and the book and three returns whole and unshortened, was asked for nothing, offered nothing, and entered nothing into this office's book, and did not know at the time what was on the second sheet of that paper and would not have cared if it had.**"

"Entered," the clerk said.

**And Nessa Pike's one sentence, at the door, to nobody in particular, and the clerk heard it and wrote it on his own page and gave it to Calder Marr afterwards, and it went into the yard's second column with a reason against it.**

"**Eight times this reeve has spent himself and every one of the eight times the price landed on somebody who was not in the room. This is the first time he has spent himself on something that was his to sell, and the price still landed on somebody who was not in the room, and I would like somebody in this harbor to work out what that makes him, because I have not got it and I have been thinking about it since eleven o'clock.**"

---

On Thursday the twenty-second at ten in the morning the chart went into the officer's hand in that room, with the clerk there, and it went as a paper and not as a copy, rolled, and the officer wrote the hour on his own page and did not say anything and went down the steps.

**And at about eleven that morning the reeve wrote four lines in his own hand and gave them to a boy of the third lay who was going up the coast on Friday with fish in boxes, and paid him nothing, because the boy was going anyway and asked for nothing and did not know what he was carrying.**

*Mr Voke. The chart of this harbor goes to a vessel on Thursday the twenty-second and it goes with a line in my own hand about the bell at the south end, and that line is worth nineteen winters to you and it is the only copy and I cannot hold it up to anybody now. I am telling you in six days instead of in a file in a month. I have paid with your winter and I did not have your leave and I would have asked and you would have said no and I would have done it anyway and this letter is so that you do not hear it from a clerk. O.K.*

**He read it back to himself once, which is not a read-back, and entered that he had done it and why, and the why is that a reeve who has just sold a man's nineteen winters owes that man the price in that man's own hand and not in an office's.**

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Thursday evening with about eleven people in the street, two of them of this yard and one of them not.

**Two marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the note and not re-written and the three counts of that page unmoved, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid. Nothing in a tin. **Nine lines and thirty-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, and four envelopes on a bench.**

**A chart of this harbor, in a locked box under a tarpaulin at the bottom of the reeve's steps since before any man in this shed was in it, went into the keeping of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor on Thursday the twenty-second of June, and with it the only copy of a line in a reeve's own hand that nineteen winters of a herring fleet's dues were written into, and there is not one mark of that in this yard's book and there never will be, and this yard did not ask to see the chart and was not asked and was not given it and is not a party to it.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not reachable by the writ. **Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg, undiminished, and about four hundred feet of her own warp on the bottom of this harbor, and thirty marks refused in this shed on Wednesday the fourteenth, and she has not been told and is not going to be told this week.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away, **and unchanged by anything that happened in that room this week, because the bell is on harbor ground and the harbor ground is not the harbor's money and eight marks and a day of a boat does not come out of a chart.**

A cistern charged on Monday the nineteenth by people this office cannot pay. No end of it entered anywhere for the ninth seam's mooring. The north berth out. The third lay full. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead. The light not on. The repair account empty since the middle of February and no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. The hard at Ashtide. Nineteen winters, unpaid, disputed, and now standing in a naval file. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. Eleven copies and four unreached holders not asked, and a list of eleven read out on a mole on Saturday. A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found, and this yard has not gone.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, and a return about the second of them made on Saturday the seventeenth and gone up the coast.

**And on Friday the twenty-third a boat of a crew out of Ashtide came into the roads with a lighter alongside her, and she was not here on the seventeenth and she was not here on the nineteenth, and this yard had not been told she was coming and did not go down to look at her and did not ask.**
