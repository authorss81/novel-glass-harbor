# Chapter 121

*The Third Return*

The reason went in at ten past six in the morning on Saturday the seventeenth of June, in Calder Marr's own left hand, in the yard's own book, on a page with a rule at the top of it, and it is the shortest entry this yard has written in five months.

*That on Saturday the seventeenth of June instant this party makes a return under the reporting clause upon the writ of the first of May, in respect of a finding of Saturday the tenth of June instant, the day of the finding not being counted and the return falling due on the fourth day after it; that no person of this party is in the water today and nothing is raised and nothing under the bedded material is touched; that this ground is a man's own account landward of the third seam's mark, that no instrument of any office is required for it, and that this party keeps the practice in its own book anyway and is not going to have the two confused; and that the clerk is not present and the reason is entered rather than filled in.*

**"We are writing a return about a thing that raised nothing," Nessa Pike said, from the bench, "and I want that in the room before anybody starts, because it is the third one and a man in a city is going to lay the three of them side by side and count how many pages of them are about money.**

**"And the answer is that two of the three are, and the third is about a foot and a half of green glass in a crate on a mole's crown, and it raised nothing, and it is worth nothing to anybody we have been able to find out about, and the clause does not say a return is only made about a thing that pays. The clause says every finding of worked glass, whole or in part. That is a foot and a half of worked glass, and it was found, and we are reporting it on the day, and I will read that line out loud to a clerk in a month if anybody in this harbor wants to hear me not read it.**"

---

The clerk came up the hill at about half past seven with his own page under his arm and a sealed jacket in his hand, and he did not go up to the shed.

In May the boy had walked down to the head of the dock with him. There was nobody to walk with him. Calder Marr walked with him as far as the steps and stopped there, and the clerk went down on his own feet with the jacket under his arm, and neither of them said anything about that, and the yard entered the fact and not the feeling.

**What was in the jacket.** One sheet, in the harbor authority's own paper, in Calder Marr's own left hand, unshortened, with the place and the hour and the keeper and the limits against the head of it, and a copy for the harbor authority's ledger in the clerk's own hand, and the clerk's own page with the date and the hour it was written in it and not the hour it was done in it, because that is a clerk's practice and has been one since April.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get onto a mole in this harbor: that on Saturday the tenth of June instant, at about twenty minutes past twelve in the noon, on the second seam's own face of this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, on the *Anna Ray's* own account and with her owner in her own boat, a piece of worked glass about a foot and a half long was found in a net and came up in that net; that the object is green through the body and cloudy, thick at one end at about two and a half inches and thin at the other, not tapering down to nothing and stopping at about a quarter of an inch, with this mole's own stone dust ground into one face and a broken face at the thin end which is dull and gritty and is not a face anybody worked; that it carries a scalloped edge of about nine in about three inches on one long edge, which is not a fracture, because a fracture in glass runs out of a point and these do not run out of anything, and which is not a mould line, because a mould line is one line and these are nine and they are all the same hand; that this party has seen the same kind of edge once before, on the sixth of May, on a different object out of different ground, and can account for it neither time; and that it carries a mark on one face which is not a seal and is not a letter and is not a figure, and which no person in that boat identified, and which this party is not going to identify, and that if it means a thing to somebody then that somebody was not in that boat.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: the mass of it, and the reason is that no man on a crown can weigh a thing in his hand and call the figure a mass. The age of it, which goes nowhere at all, on a line of its own, with the reason that this party declines to give a figure for it and declines to give a reason for declining, and the circuit office may write for a reason and the party will answer on the day it asks. And the fact that a person of this party could not say in which of two directions a sound had come from, entered on Sunday the eighteenth of June and twice before that, on the twenty-eighth of May and the second of June, in three months, in three different weathers, with a reason against each line and no cause against any of them, and no name in any of them, and nothing in any of them that knows anything.*

*And the line of what it is not, in one line, in full, because in four years somebody is going to bring this object up next to four other objects and ask whether they are the same: that it is not a panel and it is not a plate and it is not the ferry plate and it is not the worked panel this party gave back in a market on the seventh of April and it is not the lump of the sixth of May, and it is not in any office's book and has no number anywhere and carries no claim seal, and there is no claimant and no keeper but this party and there is no office with a copy of any of it; and that it is not connected to a lead plug in a chest in this shed, or to a mark on a stone, or to a ferry that went down thirteen years ago, or to anything under this town, and that nobody is to write any two of those together.*

*And the reason it is on this mole's crown in a crate under a tarpaulin and is not in any shed and is not in any boat in this harbor is the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October, and this party has not argued that clause and is not going to, and the object is a foot and a half long and about two and a half inches at the thickest and weighs what a foot and a half of glass weighs, and there is no crane of any office in this harbor that could lift it out of the crate and there is no place in this harbor it could be put.*

"Read it back," Calder Marr said.

**Nessa Pike read the whole of it back from the top to the bottom, in the shed, at about eight in the morning, and Calder Marr read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed to read it after that, and he entered that as a fact and not as an omission, and the entry is the same one that has gone in three times since the fifteenth of May: *that this column was read back in this yard's own hand by the man who wrote it, and that a column read back by the man who wrote it is not a column read back, and the reason is that the hand that used to do it is off the stone and is not coming back on a Tuesday.***

The clerk read the copy against the original in his own hand and found nothing in the one that was not in the other, and said so, because he says what a box holds and this is what the box held.

"**That is a third return and it is on the day, and I am going to write on my own page that a party with no boy in it has put three returns up this coast in six weeks and every one of them has been on the day except the first, which was two days late, and that this office has not had to ask for one of them.**" He put the page under his arm. "**And I will say the other half, because you have been decent about this for five months and this office has not deserved it. I cannot enter what a foot and a half of glass is. I can enter that a sheet came down this hill on a Saturday morning and went into a jacket and the jacket left on the same morning. That is the whole of what I have, and I have said this six times this year and I have stopped mentioning the wall.**"

---

**And nothing else happened on the seventeenth, and that is what a return day is.**

The boat was still on the haul-off above the third seam's mark with the tarpaulin over her, and both tarpaulins were still on the crown, and the crate and the bucket were about nine feet apart and about six weeks old, and the fender and the forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope were on them and had not moved.

They put the two tarpaulins back on at about ten because the wind had got into the west-southwest overnight and taken a corner off the one over the bucket, and that took twenty minutes and two men and no money, and nobody asked for anything, and the reason is in the book at no figure.

**And a return went up the coast that is about an object worth nothing, and nobody in this harbor will ever know who reads it, and the clerk said on the steps that a thing going up the coast is the only thing this yard makes that anybody in a city is obliged to look at.**

Nobody in this shed had said that before. Calder Marr stood on the crown for a while after the clerk had gone and thought about the shape of it, because a return is a paper with a date and an hour and a keeper on it going into a file in a city where nobody in this harbor has ever been inside a room, and it is the only thing this yard produces that is not a thing somebody has to want.

---

**On Sunday the eighteenth, at about eleven in the morning, the wind was in the north-east and about two hours of it, and the tide was falling, and the yard was on the crown above the third seam's mark because the crown is the only place in this harbor a man can stand in the open and be heard over a wind.**

He had a slate in his hand and no reason to have it in his hand except that it was on the bench and he picked it up.

Two men of the second lay were working a lighter off the north wall and one of them called across to the other one and the wind took about a third of it, and Calder Marr was certain, for about a second and a half, that the voice had come off the water below the wall rather than out of the man's mouth, and then certain for about a second and a half that it had come out of the man's mouth and not off the water at all.

**He had been like that since the thirteenth of October and worse since the eighth of December, and the sentence about it is a man of fifty's, said on the water off the south end on the twenty-third of May, and it has gone into this yard's second column twice and has not been improved on and is not going to be.**

He wrote it down that afternoon, in his own hand, in the second column, with the reason against the line, and read the reason out to the shipwright before he put it in.

*That on Sunday the eighteenth of June instant, on the crown of this mole above the third seam's mark, at about eleven in the morning in a north-easterly of about two hours, a person of this party could not say in which of two directions a sound had come from; and that the party has entered it as a fact and not as a name for it, and the reason is that no man in a boat can check where another man's sound went and this party is not going to put a name in this book for a thing it cannot check; and that this is the third time this party has entered the same form of line since the twenty-sixth of December, and that the party has entered the fact that it is the third time, and has not entered a cause, and no person in this party has been asked about it by anybody and this party is not going to ask anybody.*

**"You have written that three times now and you have never once written down what it is," Nessa Pike said.

"No."

"**And I have read the reason three times and the reason is better each time and the reason gets shorter each time, and in about a year it is going to be four words long and then it is going to be a habit instead of a reason.**"

"Then it will be a habit with a date on it, which is more than anybody else in this harbor has got."

The third column was filled on the Sunday as well, and the two times went in at the head of it and the lateness went in with them.

*That the entry in the third column was read on the steps at about seven in the evening on Sunday the eighteenth of June and was written in this book at about eleven the same morning, which is a lateness of about eight hours and which is the shortest this column has run since the twenty-eighth of May; and that the column is not renamed and is not scratched out and is not stopped, and that the party entered the twenty-eighth of May at about ten hours and the eighth of June at about an hour, and that the three figures do not make a trend and are not going to be made into one, and that a man who made a trend out of three figures of his own lateness would be a man doing arithmetic on a thing arithmetic is not for.*

---

Alden Marr was at the far bench with his back to the room and a cough that came every forty minutes, and he was sixty-one, and the return was on the bench at the near end with the jacket's copy under the clerk's slate, and neither brother went over to him, and he was not asked for anything on the seventeenth or on the eighteenth or on any day this month.

**He did not ask either.** That is not on the page as a virtue and it is not on the page as anything else. It is on the page because the book has a place for what a man in a room did not do, and this yard keeps that place, and the entry is a single line in the first column and it can be checked by either brother on any day of a week by asking the other one.

*That on Saturday the seventeenth and Sunday the eighteenth of June instant, Alden Marr, sixty-one, was at the far bench of this shed from about seven in the morning until about six in the evening on the Saturday and from about nine until about four on the Sunday, and was not asked for anything by any person of this party, and asked nothing of any person of this party, and neither brother went over to him.*

Calder Marr read that back to himself in the empty shed at about four on the Sunday, which is not a read-back, and entered that he had done it and why, and the why is the same one as ever: **a man who has been asked nothing for two hundred and one days and has asked nothing is two men in a room and not one, and the difference is not a thing anybody is to be thanked for.**

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about seven on the Sunday evening, with the market gone in and about nine people in the street.

**Eight marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January. Nothing in a tin. **Eight lines and thirty-two marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and three envelopes on a bench in the chest and not among the five rolled in oilcloth, and no man in this yard is going to be the one who carries one, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the twentieth and it is not going anywhere that week either.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful. **Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg, undiminished, unpaid, about four hundred feet of her own warp still on the bottom of this harbor, her name and a figure and a date on a market wall since the seventh of June, and thirty marks that would have paid for it refused in this shed on Wednesday the fourteenth, and she has not been told and is not going to be told this week.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away, and **not paid in this month and not paid in this year at this yard's present rate, and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years, and it will be eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it.**

No air in this harbor and no delivery hose and a compressor's gland fitted to this yard's own pump since Monday the nineteenth of May, unpaid, on a creditor's page at five marks, and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose. A hand lead's line made in the net loft on the second of May, badly once and made again, and said on the twenty-fourth in about nine words to be right, and used on the tenth.

**A third return made on the day, out of a shard of a foot and a half that raised nothing, and it has gone up the coast in a sealed jacket, and the clerk says on the steps that it is the only thing this yard makes that anybody in a city is obliged to look at, and there is nobody in this harbor who will ever know who reads it, and this party has not asked and is not going to and has entered that it is not going to.**

The ninth seam out with no end of it entered. The north berth out. The third lay full. The tide gang four men short. **A cistern behind an engine house charged on a rota on a Monday and a Thursday by people this office cannot pay, last charged on Monday the twelfth of June, and to be charged on Monday the nineteenth, and there is a Thursday in between that is a rota day and this yard is not going to walk it twice in one week, and the reason is that the gate takes four hours with three men and one of the three is a man who has not been paid for five pay periods.** The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The mole-head light not on and the repair account empty since the middle of February. The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle. The hard at Ashtide unanswered. Nineteen winters. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. Eleven copies and four unreached holders not asked. A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found, one line in this book, and this yard has not gone and has asked nobody anything.

A lump of green glass in a bucket and a piece of worked glass of a foot and a half in a crate, about nine feet apart on the crown of this mole above the third seam's mark, in the open air, because the second clause of a notice neither of us wrote will not let them be indoors, and **nine scallops in about three inches seen for the second time and accounted for neither time, and a mark on one face that nobody in that boat identified and that this party is not going to identify, and in six weeks there will be a third time or there will not, and the count of those is not going on any wall and is not going in any column and this party is not going to count them.**

**And a crew of two and a half, and a third column filled three times since the second of May and read back by the man who wrote it, and no occasion entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June, and eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved, and the delay a hand's width of a second and not taken and not written down.**

And on Monday morning a man in a coat is coming up this hill again with a paper in it, and this yard is not going to be told what is in it before it is in his hand.
