# Chapter 124

*The Better Sheet*

She came into the roads on Friday the twenty-third of June at about two in the afternoon with a lighter alongside, and the lighter had a compressor in it and about forty fathoms of hose coiled on top of it, and the hose was the first thing anybody on the mole's steps saw and the second thing anybody thought about, and this yard has not had a delivery hose since the twenty-eighth of April and there is not one in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms.

Her master came up the hill at half past four. He was thirty-nine, in a good coat, with a printed sheet rolled under his arm, and he was in the doorway before he was in, the way a man who has read a form does it.

"**Perry Ollett, of the *Sallow Reach*, of Ashtonide — Ashtide, the cannery port. I have six men and I have my own gear and I am not asking you for anything, and I have been up and down this coast for five weeks and I have not been in this harbor before today.**"

"Then come in," Calder Marr said. "**And I will tell you in the doorway what this yard is, because you have not asked and I would rather you had it than guessed it. Two and a half men. A bag. A note at thirty-one. No air and no hose, and the longest thing of hose in this harbor is about nine fathoms, and the reason that is so is that there is not the money in this town to buy one.**"

"That's not what I came about," Ollett said. "**I'm here about a sheet of paper.**"

---

He put the rolled sheet on the bench and unrolled it, and it was four feet by three, and it was printed, and it had a scale on it.

**It was better than the yard's. Not because it knew more. Because it could be bought.**

Everything on it was on the yard's wall already and had been since the third of May. The cairn on the Old Custom House at eleven feet by pole at half-tide, the stones ballast and not one man's work. The stem on the Nine Foot called the Accountant, nine fathoms, the middle cast of four. The bar at twenty past two on the flood off a spring, two and three-quarters, and two and a quarter on the ebb. The fouling at four fathoms and a hand's width, being a knuckle of kelp, a shoe, and four feet of herring warp gone hard.

And against every one of those, in a smaller hand, printed: **whose figure, on what day, off what ground, how many times.**

**And at the head of it, in eleven lines, the eleven places the copies of the public sheet of the fourteenth of October are held, all eleven, in order, with the name of the boat each went out in where the yard knew it.**

Nessa Pike read that part twice and put her thumb on it.

"**Where did you get that.**"

"Off the slip. It has been on your market wall since the third of May with the eleven names on it, under a shilling at the foot of the dock."

"That slip is ours."

"**It is nailed to a wall that belongs to nobody, over a naval paper that has been there since the eighteenth of March, and I did not have to steal it and I did not have to ask anybody for it, and a man at the foot of your own dock took my shilling and handed it to me and did not know what was on it.**"

Nobody in that shed said anything for a moment. The slip had been on the wall for seventy-eight days. It said eleven names and said that four of them were not reached and not asked, and that nothing on that wall changes the count and that no holder of any of them had been asked by that yard for any of them and would not be.

**It was a list of where the yard's own public record could be found, and it had been free to read for ten weeks, and the yard could not take it off the wall and could not have refused it, because a slip with a list on it that the yard keeps in its own shed is a list a man in this harbor can read out of a window, and the only thing worse than a published list is a secret one.**

---

**"I am not going to take that slip down," Calder Marr said, "and I am not going to ask you to tell me not to, and I want that in the book in those words, because a man who takes a thing down a wall and a man who says *don't* are the same act with more shame in it.**

"Now. You want a copy of the tenth thing. I know you want a copy of the tenth thing because you have just told me you have been up and down this coast for five weeks and everybody who has been up and down this coast since the second of May has asked me for a copy of the tenth thing, and I have said no to five of them and I am going to say no to you and I am going to say why in about nine sentences, and then I am going to offer you something else, and you are not to be insulted by it.**

"**One: a copy of that sheet is a copy of a copy. Two: the figure that is worth having on it is a man's own figure given out of a slate before a boat came out, and that man is not of this yard and is not a writer and is not a party and has not been asked to be right, and I cannot sell another man's slate and I would not if I could. Three: this yard has been wrong in public three times this year with a reason against every line of the second column, and a party that has been wrong three times has no business selling a map.**"

"And the reason you will not sell it is not one of those three," Ollett said.

"No," Calder Marr said. "**It is that I do not have four marks either, and I would have to take yours, and a man who sells a thing because he cannot pay his own note is a man who is selling it cheaper than it is worth, and I would rather you had it at four marks and knew what it was.**"

---

Then he said the other thing, and he said it plainly, and it is the sentence this movement turns on.

"**I am not going to ask you where you found the eleven, and you are not going to tell me, and neither of us is going to be any use to the other one about it. But I am going to say the half of it out loud because it is the true half and not the half that is about me.**

"**You did not get a road out of that slip. You got a list of eleven boats. And what you can do with a list of eleven boats is put the place of every copy of a man's public record on a printed sheet and sell it for four marks, and a man who buys it can write to any of the eleven and ask them to keep quiet, or ask them what they saw, or ask them for the sheet, and every one of those eleven people can be found by a stranger because a yard nailed up where its own paper is.**

"**And we did that. On the third of May, for a shilling, under our own hand, because we were frightened of the count. And I would do it again and I am telling you exactly what it cost and I am not asking you to give it back.**"

Ollett looked at him for a while.

"**A yard with a slip on a wall and a crew with a box in a shed. I have been up this coast five weeks and I have heard your sheet spoken of in four harbors and I have not once heard anybody say where a copy of it was. That slip of yours is the only reason I could print a sheet anybody could write to.**" He rolled his own up. "And I'll tell you what I think of that, since you have told me what you think of mine. **I think the eleven is the best thing you have done this year and I have printed it, and I would not have found four of them without it.**"

"Four," Nessa Pike said.

"**Four of the eleven I have written to and had answers from by the ordinary way, over five weeks, and they were all extremely polite and all of them asked me what I wanted it for and two of them wanted to know whether you knew I was asking.**"

"And you told them."

"**I told the first one the truth, which is that I wanted to print a sheet, and I told the second one the same, and they are the only two of the four who have stopped writing to me.**"

**And the yard did not ask which four, and did not ask the other seven either, and entered that it did not ask, and entered the reason, and the reason is the same reason it has given since the third of May and it is not going to change because a man from Ashtonide has been more useful with it than this yard has been.**

---

**"What I will take instead," Calder Marr said, "is the thing you said at the top of this hill before you were in, and I am going to say it back to you and you may write it on your own sheet.**

"**That you are a crew of six with your own compressor and forty fathoms of your own hose, and that you are not of this harbor and you are not of the Free Keel and you are not a party to anything of mine, and that you are working landward of the third seam's mark where this yard has been working since the first of May, and that neither of those facts is a fault of yours and both of them are the reason your sheet is better than mine.**"

"The hose," Ollett said. "Obviously the hose."

"**The hose and the six men and the four marks. And I am not going to pretend to a man from Ashtonide that a sheet nailed to a wall under a naval paper is a rival to a printed sheet with a scale on it, because it is not, and I have spent a hundred days this year being wrong in public and I am not going to do it again in a shed with the man holding the better paper.**"

---

**On Saturday the twenty-fourth, at about eleven in the morning, in a market, in daylight, a printed sheet went up on the market wall of Morrow Quay.**

It went up on the same nails the yard's own sheets go up on, because those are the only nails, and it went in under the naval paper of the eighteenth of March and over the sheet of the seventh of June and over the tenth thing, because a sheet that goes up goes up on the outside of whatever is already there, and that is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement since the ninth of November.

Four feet by three, a scale on it, a date on it, a price on it, and at the head of it in eleven lines the eleven places the copies of a public sheet of the fourteenth of October are held.

**A shilling changed hands at the foot of the dock for a copy of it at about half past eleven, and the shilling is not this yard's income and is not in the bag and will not be, and about nine people in that street who had read the yard's own wall in the last ten weeks stood in front of the new one for a good while without saying anything at all.**

Nobody asked the yard to move anything. Nobody has ever asked the yard to move anything and nobody ever will.

---

**And the yard worked the second seam in the morning, on the *Anna Beth's* own account, and the owner was in her own boat.**

Edric Pringle is fifty-four and of the second lay and has been in this harbor since she was eleven, and she said the cause out loud before she said the figure, which is how she has always done it and which nobody in this harbor has ever asked her to stop.

"**My own ring and my own shackle and about four feet of my own warp come on the ring, and it is under six feet off the second seam, and it is mine, and nobody has ever claimed it, and I have been in nineteen years and back, and I will not argue the eighth or the share and I have never argued either.**"

"Sixteen at the yard's own rate, the victuals and the hired hand paid at the mole by you, which is what a hirer is for."

"**Pay them at the mole and do not tell me what came out of it, because I have been in nineteen years and I know what came out of it.**"

The six were read out in order at the boat before anybody was wet, in daylight, with the hired man standing on the step and the shipwright holding the line, and the reason went in at seven in the morning in Calder Marr's own left hand with the clerk not present and the absence entered rather than filled in, because this is a man's own account landward of the third seam's mark where no instrument of any office is required and the yard keeps the practice anyway and will not have the two confused.

Named tender who is not the diver. Independent line, on the boat's own bow. Fixed air limit on the slate, set at the boat by the diver and entered as the diver's, and it was **twenty-two** and the man set it himself and would not let the holder raise it. Seated named weight. A cutoff the tender can pull without asking anybody, and it is hers, and the word is *Enough*. And a stated reason, entered in the yard's own book before anybody went in the water, in the holder's own left hand, unedited, with the clerk absent and the absence on the page.

**And a pot of this harbor authority's own, bought on the Friday at this yard's own two-mark rate, filled at that office's compressor and carried on that office's own hose, and it is the whole of the air in this harbor this morning, and the compressor's gland on this yard's own pump is on a creditor's page at five marks and is not paid for and has not been used, because the thing after the gland is the hose.**

The ring and the shackle and the swivel and about four feet of warp came up at about eleven on a quarter of a fathom of rise and went onto the *Anna Beth's* own deck in Edric Pringle's own hands, and nobody weighed it and nobody priced it, and the eighth and the share were off the top of the day and had been since about eight o'clock.

**And at about half past twelve, in the market, with the sheet still up, a man of the second lay asked a shipwright, in front of about nine people, how much the yard's hose line was, and she told him, and he said that was a great deal of money for a length of rope, and she said it was, and he went away.**

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Saturday evening with the market still in.

**Nine marks in a bag**, the seven of them out of a day at the second seam on the *Anna Beth's* own account at this yard's own rate, gross sixteen, the eighth two and the share two off the top, the victuals two and a hired hand three paid at the mole by the woman who hired the day, that being nine out of sixteen, and the pot of air having gone out of the bag on the Friday and is not coming back, so that the whole of the job left this yard four marks and not seven, which is the figure the yard wrote on the mole's steps on the thirty-first of May before anybody knew which of those two numbers was going to be the one. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved. Nothing in a tin. **Nine lines and thirty-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, four envelopes on a bench.**

**And a printed sheet went up on the market wall of this harbor on Saturday the twenty-fourth of June over the yard's own, with a scale on it and a date on it and a price on it and the place of every copy of a public sheet of the fourteenth of October at the head of it in eleven lines, and a shilling changed hands at the foot of the dock for a copy, and the yard did not ask for the shilling back and has entered that it did not ask.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, and not asked to be useful. **Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, Sella Brigg and Edric Pringle, undiminished and unpaid, and about four hundred feet of one of them's own warp on the bottom of this harbor, and thirty marks that would have paid for it refused in this shed on Wednesday the fourteenth, and she has not been told and is not going to be told this week.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away.

A cistern charged on Monday the nineteenth, on the rota, at no figure, and the fourth time in three weeks. The ninth seam out of service with no end of it entered anywhere. The north berth out. The third lay full. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The mole-head light not on, and the repair account empty since the middle of February, and no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle. The hard at Ashtide, unanswered. Nineteen winters, in a naval file since Thursday, and in four lines of a letter in a coat. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. **Eleven copies, and a list of the eleven nailed to a wall in this harbor since the third of May for a shilling, and four of them answered a stranger and two of those have stopped writing, and this yard did not ask which four and is not going to.** A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, and this yard has not gone.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins. Three returns on the seaward road to a city, and nothing entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June, and eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved.

**And the *Anna Ray* was still out. She had gone up the coast on Tuesday the twentieth with a letter in her owner's coat that a reeve had written in his own hand, and nobody in this harbor knew when she was due back and this yard did not ask, and did not send anything, and did not go down to the wall to look at her moorings while she was gone.**
