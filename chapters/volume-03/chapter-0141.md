# Chapter 141

*Four Days in Somebody Else's Hand, and a Sentence in Our Own*

Thursday the twenty-seventh of July, a day at the second seam's own face on a hirer's own account, four casts of the hand lead's line and nothing in the water.

Gross on the board fifteen. The eighth two. The share two. A hired hand three. A second hired hand three. The victuals of the day one and rope one. All four of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being twelve out of fifteen and the yard's being three. **Three marks in a bag, and the office's pot of air is not charged to this day for the plain reason that nobody went in the water, and the last pot of it went out of the bag on Friday the seventh of this month and is spent and is not coming back.**

**And the cistern was not charged, and the reason is on the page and it is a working reason and it is in this yard's own hand.**

*That the rota falls on a Monday and a Thursday; that this party has walked it twelve times since the end of May at no figure and that the gate takes four hours with three men; that on Thursday the twenty-seventh instant this party was on the second seam's own face from seven in the morning until about half past two on a hirer's own account; and that the party could not walk a four-hour gate and do a day's reading at the same time with the same three men, and that the party has not walked that gate and has entered that it has not walked it, and that the cistern has held since the nineteenth of June and this party is not going to be thanked for that and is not going to use it for anything.*

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about eight until about six with his back to the room and a cough that came every forty minutes, and he was sixty-one, and he was not asked for anything on that day and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and that is a single line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week by asking the other one.

---

**And on Friday the twenty-eighth of July, at about ten in the morning, a boat came inside the line and lay off the north wall for three hours, and a man in a coat came up the boards with a sheet under his arm and a mallet in his other hand, and the yard did not send anybody and went down and looked.**

The boat was a tender and she was not the *Vigil*, and the man in the coat was not in a uniform, and about forty people stood and watched him nail it to the market wall of Morrow Quay, on the same nails everything goes up on, on the outside of whatever was there, which has been the arrangement since the ninth of November.

**It went up over the yard's own fourth return, and the yard's own fourth return is under it, and a sheet that goes up goes up on the outside of whatever is there.**

> *To all masters, owners and persons having or claiming vessels in the roads and inshore of Morrow Quay.*
>
> *The vessel named HEARTHWAKE, of about forty tons, lying with a list to starboard inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks of this harbor, is claimed by the Crown as Crown material, the said vessel being one of a formation of vessels described in a schedule held elsewhere and not produced to this harbor.*
>
> *Any person who shall board, enter, work upon, take from, or otherwise render assistance to the said vessel, otherwise than by the order of the officer commanding the said vessel, or shall remove from her any part of her, is required to report the fact in writing to the harbor authority of this harbor within four days.*
>
> *No assistance will be rendered by the officer commanding. The said vessel is Crown material and her people are aboard her at their own risk and in defiance of an order to them.*
>
> *Given at Morrow Quay on the twenty-eighth day of July instant. Veyra Holt, Master, commanding.*

---

They stood and read it three times, which is a thing people do, and then went and got the sheet of the twenty-second of July off the wall behind it — off the wall is a figure of speech; it is still on the wall, it is under a naval paper now — and read that too, and looked at both, and about nine people said nothing at all.

**"**Four days,**" said a man of the third lay. "**It has got four days in it. That is the same four days the yard has got in the other thing.**"

"**It has,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Whose hand wrote that.**"

"**The same hand,**" said the shipwright, "**that signed the clause on a party's own writ in a room in June, and we have got the copy of that clause in a book in a shed on this hill, and a man in a city can put the two pieces of paper side by side whenever he likes, and that is not an opinion. I have looked at both and I would not have to be a magistrate to see it.**"

"**Then it is the same hand,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to say the next part out loud on this quay in about nine people's hearing, and I am going to say it because it is about me and not about her, and I would rather say it here than have it said for me in a room in Veyr in a month.**

"**On Monday the fifth of July this party entered in its own book, on its own account and without being asked, that no posting, appointment, commission, transport or instruction out of any city has put anybody past this harbor line, and that the outer marks and the bar of four hundred yards off them stand where the writ of the first of May says they are and are not nearer, and that this party is not going past them this season and has not tried and is not going to.**

"**That sentence is the reason we cannot help that boat, and it is our own, and it is in a book in a shed and anybody can come up the hill and read it, and I have just read it back to a quay at about eleven o'clock in the morning with the man who wrote it standing on the same boards.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A gull went over. The tender lay off the north wall and did nothing at all for another two hours, which is what a tender does.

---

**And the four days on that notice are a form and a form is worth what the paper it is printed on is worth, and Calder Marr said that in about nine words and it is on the page because a man said it in a market.**

"**A claim is not stopped by a form on a wall. It is stopped by nobody going, and by everybody knowing that nobody went, and the form only tells you what somebody intends to write down about it.**"

"**And it is four days,**" said the shipwright, "**and the day of the act is not counted in ours and I do not know that it is not counted in theirs, and I am not going to assume that a man has written a form in a hurry.**"

"**Then we do not need the form to be fair to be careful, and that is a worse reason and it is the one we have got.**"

---

**And at about half past one in the afternoon a woman of the second lay, sixty-odd, who has been standing at the head of the boards since about eleven, said the thing that the rest of that quay had not thought of, and she said it to nobody in particular and she said it twice.**

"**She is pumping. She has been pumping since about the time the tide gang started walking her, and I know it because I have been watching her instead of my own gear and I have been watching her for nine days and I have not told anybody and I am telling you now and I have not come up your hill to be thanked for it.**

"**A boat that has been pumping nine days in this water has not got nine days left in her, and there are people in her, and that is not a thing a man writes a form about, and I know it is not a thing a man writes a form about because I have been in a boat with a hole in it and I did not have a form, I had a man on a pump and a boy on a bucket and I did not have four days.**"

She put her basket down on the boards and then picked it up again.

"**I am not asking you for anything. I have not got anybody to ask. I have said it out loud on a quay and that is all I have and I would like it to be on somebody's page.**"

"**It is going in the first column, in the shipwright's hand, with the hour, and your name against it if you will give it, and if you will not give it then it goes in without a name the way the count of four went in,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and either way anybody can stand on this quay tomorrow and ask you whether you said it.**"

"**I will give it,**" the woman said. "**And it is Nell Ardery, and my mother was on the Sill, and that is the whole of what I know about that boat and it is more than any of you have.**"

---

**And a crew of Ashtide came out of the north wall at about two in the afternoon and took up four hundred feet of a woman's own warp off the bottom of this harbor, for twenty-six marks and a day, and Sella Brigg watched them do it from the head of the boards and did not say one word to anybody, and then said one word to Calder Marr, which was *well*, and went home.**

**She has been paid by him and is owed eight shillings and tenpence by this yard, and the two figures are not one figure, and neither of them has moved.**

---

**And that evening, with the market gone in and about nine people in the street, the two columns were read out at the head of the dock, and the money part of it is short and it is the whole of the two days.**

Three marks in a bag and nothing has come in or gone out since Thursday morning. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose. **Fourteen lines and fifty-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and nine envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere in nine weeks, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the first of August.** Nothing in a tin, and the tin has been empty since the twenty-eighth of April.

**And that sheet on the market wall is the second thing this month that has been put in front of this harbor by a man of a vessel, and the first of the two was a clause on a party's own writ, and the second of them is a paper with the same hand on it, and the yard has lost the difference between the two in about three seconds and has entered that it has lost it.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, unpaid, not reduced, not asked to be reduced, and **nobody in this crew writes to him, nobody goes to see him, and nobody asks anybody who has seen him what he said, and those three nots went in on the eighth of June and are not being taken out this month or any month.** Forty marks to a conservator of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful, and not one mark of it paid out of anything that has come into this yard since the eighth of May. Eight shillings and tenpence to each of two women, undiminished, unpaid, **and in no sentence together in this book, and a woman of the third lay checked it on Thursday the twenty-third of July and said it was right.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, and not paid this month. No delivery hose and the longest thing of hose in this harbor about nine fathoms. Two pots of air this month at this yard's own two-mark rate, filled at the office's own compressor on the office's own hose, and they are the whole of the air in this harbor.

The ninth seam out of service with an end of it entered for the first of September. **A cistern charged twelve times on that office's own page at no figure, holding since the nineteenth of June, and one rota day in this fortnight deliberately not walked with the reason on the page in the yard's own hand.** The north berth out. The third lay full. The mole-head light not on. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn, and the harbor authority's own gear is on the harbor authority's own account and has had no boat of its own at any time since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard and no cradle under her. The hard at Ashtide, unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters in a naval file and in four lines of a letter in a herring skipper's coat. The *Sea Wren* at the naval pile. A man of the third lay, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, not found, one line in this book, and this yard has not gone and has asked nobody anything.

**And a woman of the second lay named Nell Ardery said on a quay at about half past one in the afternoon on Friday the twenty-eighth of July that a boat with people in her has been pumping nine days, and gave her name, and said her mother was on the Sill, and asked for nothing, and is in the first column of this yard's own book in the shipwright's hand with the hour against it.**

**And a Crown notice with the same hand on it as the reporting clause is nailed over this yard's own fourth return, and it says that no assistance will be rendered, and it says four days, and this party is not going, and the sentence it is not going on is its own and is on the page since the fifth of July and was read out loud on a quay in nine people's hearing this morning by the man who wrote it.**
