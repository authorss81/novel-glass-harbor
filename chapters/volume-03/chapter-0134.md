# Chapter 134

*The Fourth Return, and a Clerk Who Comes Up the Hill*

The cistern was charged on Thursday the thirteenth of July at about ten, on the rota, the tenth time, at no figure, by three people of this yard and two men of the third lay who came past and helped for about fifty minutes and would not take anything and were not thanked, and the gate took the three of them from ten until about two, and the cistern behind the engine house has not gone dry since the nineteenth of June.

---

**And the rest of the Thursday was spent on one sheet of paper, and it took two people the whole of the afternoon, and that is entered because a yard of two and a half people spent a day on a document and did not earn a mark by it and could not have done otherwise, because the day of the finding is not counted and the return falls due tomorrow.**

The argument about it went on for about two hours and it was the longest argument in this shed this year and neither of them raised her voice once.

"**Then put the joint in,**" said the shipwright. "**There is a lump of packing at the foot of a hull and the packing came out of a hole and the hole is in a joint, and a man on a mole can see a joint, and if we do not say it then a man in a city who reads this sheet is going to think we found a lump lying on the bottom and that is a different thing and it is a smaller thing, and we will have made it smaller by saying less.**"

"**We will have made it smaller by saying a thing we cannot check.**"

"**A joint is checkable. Any shipwright in this harbor can go and look at it in ten minutes with a rule.**"

"**Then you go and look at it in ten minutes with a rule and come back and tell me, and until you do it is a thing two men in a shed agree is probably there,**" said Calder Marr, "**and this return is not the place for probably.**"

"**Then it is not a conclusion. A conclusion is *a chain*. A joint is a joint.**"

"**A joint is what you get when two things are made to be one thing, and I cannot stand on a mole and tell you two things were made to be one thing, because I have never in my life seen one that anybody could tell me about, and if I put that word on this sheet then in four years there is a page in this book with my hand on it saying the two were joined, and the two are what is under this mole, and I have told you since the third of May that I am not going to put a name on anything I cannot check.**"

They went at it for another twenty minutes and then she stopped it herself.

"**Then leave it out and put the leaving out,**" she said. "**Not a line saying we thought about it. A line that says what the party is not able to say, and the reason, in the second column, where a reason is required. That is what the second column is for and it is the only place in this book a man is allowed to say *I do not know* and mean it.**"

"**That is better than what I wanted,**" said Calder Marr. "**And it is worse for me, and that is how I know it is right.**"

---

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that the place is the second seam's own face of this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, being ground no party claims and ground the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first does not reach; the hour, ten past four in the afternoon of Monday the tenth of July instant; the keeper, this party, being Calder Marr, Nessa Pike and the hired men, of whom the keeper of the water is the holder; the limits, being the holder's own, seventeen minutes, set by him at the boat and entered as his, with a named tender who is not the diver, an independent line on the boat's own bow, a seated named weight and a cutoff the tender can pull without asking anybody, the word being *Enough*.*

*And the object: a lump about the size of two fists, not stone, not oakum, not lime and not pitch, with a skin on the outside of it about a quarter of an inch thick; and a chip of green glass about the length of a thumbnail, green through the body and cloudy, thicker at one end than the other by about a sixteenth, sea-worn to a frosted face on one side, with a scalloped edge of about three in about half an inch, all the same hand, none of them running out of a point.*

*And the line of what it is not, in full: not the lump of the sixth of May, not the shard of the tenth of June, not the worked panel given back in a market on the seventh of April, not a plate, not the ferry plate, not the plate at the fifth seam, not the lead plug in the chest in this shed, not a mark on a stone, not the compartment, whose finding stands as it has stood in three steps and is not restated here, not the run under this town, not the coast road, not a bar of gravel, not a cairn of ballast stones, not a stem on the Nine Foot, and not a mark of nine joined lines, and the party is not going to write one; and that neither object is in any office's book and neither carries a seal and neither has a number anywhere; and that nobody is to write any two of those things together in any book in this harbor, and that this party has entered that instruction against itself and not against anybody else.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: the material of the lump, with the reason that two people in a shed are not a foundry; the material of a piece of metal brought up on the Saturday before, on a line of its own, with the reason that this party does not know metal; **what the packing is in and who put it there, which is a line of its own, and the reason is that no man on a mole can say whose hand cut anything, and that this party declines to give a name and declines to give a trade, and that anybody who comes to this sheet in four years is going to want a name and is not going to get one from this party and will get one from a person who was not in this boat if he gets one at all;** and what is in the chip, which goes nowhere at all, on a line of its own, with the reason that this party cannot read what a thing carries and is not going to try and is not going to pay six marks and two days to have it read and has not got six marks.*

*And the third line of the second column, in this party's own hand: that on the eighth of July instant, at about ten past eleven in the morning, on a westerly of about four hours, the person in the water could not for about four minutes tell which of his two hands was holding the line, and that the reason it is entered is that no man in a boat can check which of his own hands he was using at a given second, and that the party is not going to make a second-column line of every ordinary thing a man cannot check and is making this one because it went with a day that lost an hour.*

**And Nessa Pike read the whole of it back from the top to the bottom at about seven in the evening, in the shed, and Calder Marr read it back after her, and the two of them were alone in the room, and the entry that says so is the same entry that has gone in four times since the fifteenth of May and is the same entry and has not been improved on.**

---

**The clerk came up the hill on Friday the fourteenth at about half past seven with his own page under his arm and a sealed jacket in his hand, and he did not go up to the shed.**

In April the boy had walked down to the head of the dock with him. There was nobody to walk with him. Calder Marr walked him to the steps and stopped there and the clerk went down on his own feet with the jacket under his arm and neither of them said anything about that, and the yard entered the fact and not the feeling.

**What was in the jacket.** One sheet, in the harbor authority's own paper, in Calder Marr's own left hand, unshortened, with the place and the hour and the keeper and the limits against the head of it, and a copy for the harbor authority's ledger in the clerk's own hand, and the clerk's own page with the date and the hour it was written in it and not the hour it was done in it, because that is a clerk's practice and has been one since April.

The clerk read the copy against the original and found nothing in the one that was not in the other, and said so, because he says what a box holds and this is what the box held.

"**That is a fourth return and it is on the day,**" he said, "**and I am going to write on my own page that a party with no boy in it has put four returns up this coast in ten weeks and every one of them has been on the day except the first, which was two days late, and that this office has not had to ask for one of them, and that three of the four are about a lump, a foot and a half, and a piece of the length of a thumbnail, and that if a man in a city has been waiting for a season of wrecks he has not got one and I have not been asked whether he has and I am not going to ask.**"

He put the page under his arm.

"**And I will say the other half, because you have been decent about this for five months and this office has not deserved it. I cannot enter what a thumbnail of glass is. I can enter that a sheet came down this hill on a Friday morning and went into a jacket and the jacket left on the same morning. That is the whole of what I have, and I have said this seven times this year and I have stopped mentioning the wall.**"

---

**And then he said the thing nobody in that shed had thought to say, and he said it on the steps without being asked, and he said it in one go and without stopping anywhere in it.**

"**The vessel has withdrawn its objection to a claim window of this office inside the harbor line until the first of March next. If anybody in this harbor wanted to dispute what is in that jacket they would have to go about it as a finding and not as a claim, and the clause lets this office seat one of them and take the finding as it stands, and there is nobody to seat, and there has not been anybody to seat since the twenty-first of June.**

"**I am not saying this because it flatters you. I am saying it because you are about to find out what that is worth, and it is worth a fisherman's winter that a reeve of this office sold on his own motion in a room with a clerk in it in June, and you had no part in that and neither did I, and in four years that is going to be the only reason anybody could not take the thing out of that mole, and it is going to be a reeve's price and not a party's.**"

Nobody said anything to that on the steps.

---

**And that evening, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, the two columns were read out at the head of the dock, and the clerk was not there and did not come up the hill again, and the read-out is the whole of the twenty days this party has had since the sixth of July.**

**Two marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven as last written and unmoved since the fourth of January, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid, and the tin empty since the twenty-eighth of April. **Twelve lines and forty-eight marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and seven envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the eighteenth, and the bag is two, and a wage that is exactly paid is not a week and not good news.**

**And the whole of the money that has come into this yard since the twenty-fourth of June is fourteen marks, and fourteen marks have gone out of it in the same time, as three weeks of a wage and one pot of air, and there are two marks in the bag, and the note has not moved and the three counts on the note have not moved, and no office in this harbor has paid this yard a mark since the eighth of May and no office in this harbor is going to.**

Forty marks of claim to a boy of twenty-two, standing whole, unpaid, not reduced, not asked to be reduced, and a wage of four a week going into an envelope, and **nobody in this crew writes to him, nobody goes to see him, and nobody asks anybody who has seen him what he said.** Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful, **and not one mark of it paid out of anything that has come into this yard since the eighth of May, and a man of no office in this harbor has been in this harbor since about the thirtieth of June and this party has not known it and has not asked.**

Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, undiminished, unpaid, not paid in a month, **in no sentence together in this book, and a woman of the second lay came up this hill on Wednesday last and said so out loud, and she said she would come back on Thursday to check it, and she did check it on Thursday and she came up and said it was right.** **Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, not paid in a month, and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it, and that standing sentence is unreversed.**

No air in this harbor except two pots bought out of this yard's own bag at two marks apiece, no delivery hose, the longest thing of hose in it about nine fathoms, **and a crew of Ashtide lying at the outer end of the second lay with a compressor of her own and forty fathoms of her own hose, and that man has taken a woman's warp for twenty-six marks and a day on the twenty-eighth and this yard told her to go to him and is not going in the way of it.**

A cistern charged twice this week and ten times since the end of May at no figure and against the office's own interest, holding since the nineteenth of June, **and a wharfinger of this harbor said the word *town* out loud in a market on the sixth of July and has not taken it back, and this party is not going to argue with him about it and is not going to use it for anything.** The ninth seam out of service since the first of May with an end of it entered on the third of July, being the first of September, and the first thing that office has entered in five months a person in this harbor can use. The north berth out. The third lay full, which is a full and not a repair. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The mole-head light not on. The repair account empty since the middle of February.

No boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard with no cradle. The hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters, in a naval file and in four lines of a letter in a herring skipper's coat and in a list in a city. The *Sea Wren* at the pile.

**Eleven copies, a list of eleven on a wall since the third of May, a printed sheet over the top of it since the twenty-fourth of June, and a man of Ashtide who has had four of the eleven answered him and two of those have stopped writing, and this yard did not ask which four and is not going to.** A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, one line in this book, and this yard has not gone.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of which has had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June. **And the whole of what this party is now doing is nine feet underneath that stone, and neither brother has thought about it once in four months, and that is entered as a fact about this party and not as anything else.**

**Four returns on the road to a city: the fourth of May, two days late, about a lump in a bucket and six days in a shed; the tenth of May, on the day, about that lump worth nothing; the seventeenth of June, on the day, about a foot and a half of worked glass that raised nothing; and this one, on the day, about a thumbnail.** A question written back out of a city on the twenty-ninth of June, four questions, three answered out of this yard's own book in this yard's own left hand and one not answered, the reason in the first line. A letter of three lines gone to the same printer on the twelfth of July at no cost telling him a paper is coming.

**No occasion entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and neither figure has moved in a fortnight and neither is moved by a measurement, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved.**

**And a crew of two and a half, and four entries in a second column in five months in the form of a man not being able to say in which of two directions a sound had come, with a reason against each and no cause against any of the four and nobody asked, and a count of the four in the first column in the shipwright's hand on Tuesday the eleventh with no name against it and the reason she gave for that.**

**And on Saturday, at about eleven, a woman is going to be at the foot of that face with a lens and a pair of brass calipers of her own, and this party has not asked her to come and is not going to, and she has not said she is coming, and she has been in this harbor since about the thirtieth of June.**
