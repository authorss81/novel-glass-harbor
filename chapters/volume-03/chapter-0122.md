# Chapter 122

*Asked a Second Time*

The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday the nineteenth of June and it came down bare, and that was three Mondays, and there was no sitting and nothing went up in its place, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it.

**At about ten that morning, because it is a rota day and the tank was down to about a third, three people of this yard walked out to the sluice behind the engine house with a hose and a bucket, and it took them from ten until about two, and nobody paid any part of it and nobody claimed any part of it and no part of it went into a wage column.** The gate was as bad as it has ever been. Two men of the third lay came past and helped for about an hour and would not take anything and were not thanked and did not want to be.

At about eleven, on the steps, a man came up who had a book of his own under his arm.

---

He was the officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor. He had been on the top of these steps at ten in the morning on Monday the twenty-second of May with a clerk of the circuit office and had read a third column off a book, and he was the same man and he did not give a name and was not asked for one, and the yard entered the fact that he was the same man in the first column, because a man on a mole can be asked about it tomorrow.

Nessa Pike was four feet away for the whole of it, which is what she does.

**The paper was one sheet, and it was signed at the foot by the master of the cutter, whose name is Veyra Holt and who is not in this harbor and did not come up this hill and is not going to.**

*To the harbor authority of Morrow Quay. The vessel holding a claim in this harbor has already asked, of this office, for this office's own chart of the basin and the inner ground, and that request has been refused. It is made a second time, and it is made in the same terms and without any alteration of any kind, and the vessel does not contend that the first refusal was wrong and does not ask this office to reverse it.*

*It asks this office for a copy, or an extract, or a tracing, of the chart of this harbor, of the mole, of the harbor line, of the outer marks, and of the bar lying four hundred yards off those marks; and it asks that the outer marks and the bar be shown as this office holds them and not as any other office or person holds them.*

*And it states, so that this office is not left in doubt of its own position, that the vessel will not contest a claim window of this office, upon any ground whatever within the harbor line, for the remainder of the term of the writ of the first of May; that is, until the first of March next.*

*And it asks for the whole of it by the first of July.*

"Read it out again," Nessa Pike said, from four feet away, "and this time read the date as a date and not as a courtesy, because a man who writes a month in a request and a month in a threat has written one thing and meant the other and I would like him to say which."

He read it again, and the date was a date.

"**It is not a courtesy," he said. "**And I will say the rest of it, because I have been in this harbor three times and every one of them has been in a room or on a mole with a clerk in it, and I would rather a man knew what I am.** I have no authority over this office and this office has none over me. I have a paper signed by a woman who is not here. If that paper is not answered by the first of July, it goes into a file in a city in July, with a date against it, in a hand that is not mine."

---

**"And the rider," Calder Marr said. "There is a rider on the bottom of it in your own hand and you have not read it out."**

"There is no rider."

"There is a line under the signature and it is not a signature, and you have had your thumb on it for four minutes. Read it out or say there is nothing under your thumb."

He read it out.

*And it asks this office, or the holder of a copy of any public sheet of the fourteenth of October last, for a list of the places the copies of that sheet are held.*

"That is a different paper," Calder Marr said, "and it has a different name on it, and we will deal with it separately and I am not going to let you fold it into a thing with a date on it."

"**It is a rider and it goes in the same file.**"

"Then it goes in the same file and it is answered on a different day, and you may write that down."

---

**What this yard can do about a chart.** Not very much, and it said so on the steps in about nine sentences, and then it did the two things it could do.

"**We cannot produce it.** The harbor authority's own chart of the basin and the inner ground is not on a wall in this harbor and is not in this yard's keeping and is not in this shed and never has been, and a man in this harbor who has been in that office can tell you where it lies and it is in a locked box under a tarpaulin at the bottom of the reeve's steps, and this yard has never had a key to it and would not use one if it had.

"**And we cannot refuse it for you.** That is the half that matters and I am going to say it slowly. This office's chart is not this yard's, and the yard is not the office, and a yard that refused a request for another party's property would be a yard saying it knows what is owed to whom. So we cannot say no. What we can do is tell you where the answer has to be given and in whose hand, and that is a room on the west basin with a clerk in it, and the answer is not ours and cannot be made ours by anybody standing on these steps.

"**And I am not asking for a date and I am not asking to be told before the date, and this party is not going to be a party to that paper at all.** We have four names on a writ of the first of May and the clause on it lets you ask any holder of it for the writ and such documents as the holder is required to produce, and we will produce them, and you have had them once already and there is a ledger in a circuit office with the hour of it in it. If you want the writ again, ask for it in a form with a day on it and it will come up these steps or go into that room, and either way it will be produced whole and unshortened, and that is the whole of what this yard has to do with a request for a chart."

He put his hand on the book under his arm and did not open it.

"**And I am going to say the half of it that nobody in this harbor will like, and I would rather say it on your own steps than have it said about me in a city. When that paper is answered, one way or the other, in a room, by a reeve, with a clerk, and not by a man with a book on a mole — and that is the only way it can be answered, and I know it, and you know it — then somebody in this harbor is going to say that the party with a writ on it knew the whole of it eleven months ago and did not say so.** And I am saying so now, on your own steps, at eleven in the morning, to about nine people, before you go down them. Write the hour down."

---

**And the rider was answered on the same morning, because the yard had it in a book and had no reason to keep it.**

The list of the eleven holders of the copies of the public sheet of the fourteenth of October came out of the yard's own book in Calder Marr's own left hand, and it was read out in full, and the four of them that had never been reached were named as not reached and not asked, and the yard said out loud that it was not going to ask them and had not asked them and would not, and that the count of eleven does not move because a count of hands is not a count of whereabouts, and that the reason those four are named on a wall in this harbor as not reached is that the yard would rather a man in a city read the whole of it than the part of it that flatters it.

Then the yard said the thing that is the actual answer to a rider of that kind, and it is nine words and it is in the first column because a man on a mole can be asked about it tomorrow.

"**We are not going to give that list to a vessel. We are giving it to you now, in the open air, with nine people standing here, and if you write it down you write down all of it, and if you write down a part of it you will be asked about the rest of it in a market in a fortnight and I will be the one asking.**"

He did not write it down. He said he would take it to his own office and enter it there and that he had no intention of giving it to a third party, and nobody believed him and nobody said so, and the yard entered that it did not believe him and did not say so and put the reason against the line.

---

**On Tuesday the twentieth the wage went into an envelope on the bench.**

Four marks, a week at the rate from the first of February, for the week of Tuesday the twentieth to Monday the twenty-sixth inclusive, on Tuesday the twentieth, in Calder Marr's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it in the same hand. The envelope is in the bench chest and is not among the five rolled in oilcloth. It is not going anywhere this week and nothing is going to carry it, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a wage is not a reason to go to a door.

Nine lines and thirty-six marks in the column, and the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and four envelopes on a bench.

**And the two facts of the twentieth of June, in Calder Marr's own hand, both of them checkable and neither of them in an office.**

*That a vessel holding a claim in this harbor has asked this harbor authority, for the second time, for the harbor authority's own chart, on Monday the nineteenth instant at about eleven in the morning, at the top of this mole's steps, in the open air, with a paper signed by the master of that vessel and with a date on it, the first of July; and that the first request was made in a room in this harbor in December last and was refused in about eleven seconds and this party was not in that room and did not hear it and has it from a man who was, and that the yard has been able to establish no date for the first request and has not invented one.*

*And that the chart is not on a wall in this harbor and is not in this yard's keeping and this party cannot produce it and cannot refuse it on its own account, because it is not this party's; and that this party has told the officer so in those words, in the open air, at about eleven in the morning on Monday the nineteenth, in about nine people's hearing, and has entered that it told him so before he went down the steps and not after; and that the answer to that request, whichever way it goes, will be given in a room on the west basin by the reeve, with the clerk, and not by this party and not on a mole; and that this party is not a party to that paper, has not asked to be, and is not going to be.*

"And I want one more line," Nessa Pike said, "and I want it in the second column and I want the reason against it, and I want your hand on it and not mine."

"Go on."

"**That on Monday the nineteenth of June instant a vessel asked for a chart and this party put the whole of its position into the open air on a mole in about nine sentences in front of nine people, and that there is no part of that paper this yard can refuse, and that a yard with nothing to refuse is a yard that has to be honest about a thing it has not decided, and I do not know yet whether being honest about it in advance is worth anything or is the cheapest thing a yard can do with a week it has not got work for.**" She put the pencil down. "**That is the first time I have written a line into this book that is about whether we are any good at this, and it is in the second column because two of us cannot check it, and I am not going to take it out and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Tuesday evening with no market in and about nine people in the street.

**Two marks in a bag**, the two of them for a pot of air bought of this harbor authority on Friday the nineteenth at this yard's own two-mark rate, filled at the office's compressor and carried on the office's own hose, and the pot is the whole of the air in this harbor this morning and the thing after the pot is a hose and there is not one in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms. Thirty-one on the note and not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved, and a compressor's gland of five marks still on that page and still not paid. Nothing in a tin.

**Nine lines and thirty-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, and four envelopes on a bench and no man carrying one.** Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, and not one mark of it paid out of any money that has come into this yard since the eighth of May. **Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg, undiminished, with her name and a figure and a date on a market wall since the seventh of June and about four hundred feet of her own warp on the bottom of this harbor, and thirty marks refused in this shed on Wednesday the fourteenth, and she has not been told.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April and owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away.

A cistern charged on Monday the nineteenth, the fourth time, by people this office cannot pay, at no figure, on a rota. The ninth seam out with no end of it entered. The north berth out. The third lay full. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead. The light not on and the repair account empty since the middle of February and no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. The hard at Ashtide. Nineteen winters. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. Eleven copies and four unreached holders not asked, and now a list of eleven read out on a mole to a man with a book, and the four not asked are still not asked. A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope, lent at nothing and entered at nothing, and both tarpaulins put back on on Saturday the seventeenth after a westerly had taken a corner off one, at no figure, with two men and twenty minutes.

**A piece of worked glass of a foot and a half with nine scallops in three inches and a mark on one face that nobody identified, in a crate, on a mole's crown, worth nothing, with a return about it made on the seventeenth and gone up the coast in a sealed jacket, and a clump of stone dust in one face that this party has not brushed off and is not going to brush off, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a thing that has been in the weather for five weeks is what it is.**

**And on Wednesday the twenty-third at ten in the morning, in a room on the west basin with the clerk in it, a man with a book of his own is going to ask for that writ again, and this party will produce it whole and unshortened, and the holder has said out loud on the steps that he is not going to be a party to the chart, and the answer to the chart is not his, and the first of July is a date and not a courtesy.**
