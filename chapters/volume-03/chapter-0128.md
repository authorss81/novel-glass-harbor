# Chapter 128

*The Number in a Room*

They worked the second seam on Saturday the first of July on a hirer's own account, and nobody went in the water, and the day was four casts of the hand lead off the boat at the top of the flood with the yard's own line made in the net loft in May, and the six conditions were read out in order in the shed at seven with the shipwright and the entry says the one that is the office's is not needed and was not done.

**And three of the four casts were on the mark and one was forty feet south of it, and the three on the mark were eleven and a half, and the one forty feet south was eleven and three-eighths.**

**That is the same as the second of June, in the same hour, off the same boat, on the same mark, and the yard checked it and it held.**

*That on Saturday the first of July instant, at the top of the flood, four casts of the hand lead were made on the second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark, being eleven and a half three times on the mark and eleven and three-eighths at forty feet south of it; and that this party entered on the second of June the figure at forty feet south as a figure that no man in this harbor can say what it would have read at ten o'clock instead of one; and that this party has now made the same four casts on the same mark in the same hour of the same tide five weeks afterwards and the two sets agree; and that this party has entered the agreement as a fact and not as a proof, because two sets of four casts is eight casts and this coast has men in it who have kept a bar for nineteen years on four times a year, and eight is not four times, and a yard that begins to treat eight casts as a figure is a yard that is about to put a number on a wall.*

Gross on the board fourteen. The eighth two, the share two, the office's pot of air two, a hired hand three, the victuals of the day one and rope one, all three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, which is what a hirer is for. That is eleven out of fourteen and the yard's is three, and the three is in the bag and the eleven never was.

---

**And at about four in the afternoon Calder Marr went down to the second lay on his own feet and put it in a doorway, and it was a doorway and not a room, and he did not ask the question in it.**

The forge was open and the tide was making and Corvin Sarn was sitting on an upturned crate at the back of it with his forearms black to the elbow and a hammer in his hand and no work in front of him, because there has been no work in front of that man since the first of May.

"**I have come about the plug,**" Calder Marr said.

"**I know what you have come about and I have had four months to work out how I would say no and I have not worked it out, because there is only one form it takes and you know what it is.**"

"**I have not asked you for it and I am not going to ask you for it here. I am going to ask you to come to the *Anna Ray* tomorrow morning at ten, and I am going to ask you in Mr Voke's cabin, with the door shut and the hatch open and three of us in it, and I am going to ask you in a room.**"

Sarn looked at him for a while.

"**I said in May that I would say it in a room if somebody asked me in a room and not before. That is the whole of what I have said about it and I have not said it since and I have not moved.**

"**A cabin is a room. A shed is a shed. Go and ask the boat.**"

"I have asked the boat," Calder Marr said. "**He says yes and he says what it is worth and he said it in about nine words, and I am not going to repeat them here because you are not the only man in this trade and this forge has a door on two sides.**"

"Then ask me tomorrow at ten," Sarn said, "**and shut the door.**"

---

**On Sunday the second of July, at ten in the morning, in the cabin of the *Anna Ray* at the north end, with the tide falling, the door shut and the hatch open, there were four men.**

Elias Voke, fifty-one, at the table, who had offered the room and had not left it. Corvin Sarn, fifty, at the other side of it, who had not been paid to come and was not paid to sit down and was not going to be paid for either. Calder Marr. And Nessa Pike, who read back and who was the reason there was a reader.

**Nobody in that cabin had come from an office and there was no seal in the room and there was no ledger in it and no box, and the chest in the shed on the hill was not brought down the hill and was not opened and is not going to be opened by anybody in this batch.**

"**I am going to ask you in a room,**" Calder Marr said, "**and you said you would answer it, and I am asking it once, and you are not going to be asked it again by anybody from this cabin, and whatever you say in the next five minutes is not going into any book in this harbor in figures. That is said first because you said it in May and I am holding to your form and not mine.**

"**What is the number in the plug.**"

Sarn put the hammer down on the deck. He did it the way a man puts a thing down when his hands are not free for it.

"**Right. Once.**

"**It is not a bell number. I have cut bell marks on iron for thirty-one years and it is not one, and it is not a boat number, and it is not a date, and it is not anything this harbor has ever cut, and I have been in two yards in Ternwatch in my time and it is not anything they cut either, and I am saying that as a man who could be wrong and not as a man who is right.**

"**It has more marks in it than it has figures. Two of the strokes are joined at one end and one is not joined to anything. There is a two, and there is a two and a bit of another one, and there is a fourth that is not a two, and the rest of it is not figures at all, it is tooling, and there is more tooling on that face than there is number, and I have looked at it four months and I cannot make it be one thing and I am not going to pretend to a cabin that I can.**

"**That is all of it. That is the whole of what a man of fifty knows about a piece of lead the size of his thumb, and I have been asked in a room and I have said it, and I am not saying it again, not here and not in a shed and not in nine years.**"

Nobody in that cabin said anything for about a minute.

"**Now the price,**" Sarn said, "**and then I am going up the steps to see about a shackle that has been in my head since Tuesday.**

"**Three things. You do not write it down. Nobody comes about it again. And that chest stays shut.**

"**I am not asking for money and I want you to write that down, because if you write down that I did not ask for money then a man in a city can see a man of fifty was asked in a room and said no to being paid for it, and that is the only thing I get out of this and I would like somebody to have it.**"

"**Agreed,"** Calder Marr said. "**All three, and I will write down that you did not ask for money, and I will write down that I asked you in a room and not in a shed, and I will not write down one stroke of what you have said, and I will enter that this party has undertaken not to ask you again on this and that the undertaking is in this yard's own book and binds this party and not the office and not the market.**"

"**And the boy is not to be asked either.**"

"**The boy is not to be asked, and the boy is not to be written to, and I have not written to him since the fifteenth of May and I am not going to and the three nots are on the page with a reason against them.**"

"Then I have said it," Sarn said, and picked the hammer up.

---

**They came up the mole's steps at about a quarter to eleven and the four of them stood at the top of them in the wind, and nobody said anything for a minute, and then Voke said the thing that had been sitting in him since about ten.**

"**I carried a man's paper up this coast for four months and I am going to be asked, in about four years, what it cost the harbor authority to be honest in public, and I am going to say: a man of fifty said a number out loud in my cabin and did not take a shilling for it. And I am fifty-one and I have three men and a boy and nineteen winters, and that is going to be the answer, and I did not choose it, and I am not complaining.**"

"You did not have to have a cabin in it," Nessa Pike said.

"**No," said Voke. "I did not.**"

---

**And the yard did not write it down, and Calder Marr gave the reason in about nine words on the steps and then gave it again at greater length at the bench that evening, and neither version was improved on.**

"**A figure we cannot check is a figure a man in a city can be lied to in without anybody lying, and the day we put one in this book everything else in this book is read as being of the same kind.**

"**And there is a second reason and it is better. A boy of twenty-two wrote on the lid of that chest that there is nothing in it that anybody in this harbor can check. He was twenty-two and he had a hand that was going and he was right, and I have had four months to find a way round that sentence and there is not one.**"

---

*First column, checkable by any of four men: that on Sunday the second of July instant, at about ten in the morning, in the cabin of the *Anna Ray* of Elias Voke at the north end of this harbor, with the door shut and the hatch open, a man of fifty of this harbor was asked in a room by this party what is the number in a piece of lead, and that he said it once, and that this party has entered that he said it and has not entered what he said, and that this party has undertaken in this book not to ask him about it again, and that he did not ask for money and was not offered any and would not have taken any.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: the number itself, which is not in this book and is not in any book in this harbor and is not going to be written in any office and is not going to be printed; and the reason it is in neither column, which is not a rule of this yard but a decision of it on the second of July, and the reason is that a figure this party cannot check is a figure a man in a city can be lied to in without anybody lying.*

*And the third column is not where it goes and has not been written at the head of it and is not going to be, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a thing a man said once in a room cannot be read back by a column and can only be read back by a person, and this yard has not got the person, and had one on Monday the first of May and put him off a stone on Monday the fifteenth because his hand was going, and does not write to him, and is not going to.*

**And the line at the head of the third column, in Calder Marr's own left hand, and nothing under it, and the head of the column has two times on it that a boy put there on the second of May.**

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Sunday evening with no market in and about nine people in the street.

**Eight marks in a bag**, the three of them out of a day at the second seam on a hirer's own account at this yard's own rate, gross fourteen, the eighth two and the share two, the office's pot of air two, a hired hand three, the victuals one and rope one, all three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being eleven out of fourteen. Thirty-one on the chandler's note and not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid. Nothing in a tin. **Ten lines and forty marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and five envelopes on a bench, and the last of them is four marks on Tuesday the fourth of July and it is not going anywhere that week either.**

**Eight marks and a day of a boat to the same man, owing since the twenty-third of May and unpaid and unargued, and the third time this year this yard has been in the same room as him, and the second time is the first time it has got anything out of him, and none of it is money, and none of it is written in figures, and the reason it is not written in figures is on the page in his own words and in the shipwright's and in the holder's, and the reason the eight marks and a day of a boat is not paid is that this yard has no instrument for paying a man who is not on its work and has not invented one and is not going to.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not asked to be useful. **Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, and about four hundred feet of one of their own warps at the bottom of this harbor, and a woman said in a market that she was going to ask a man of another port and this yard has no standing with her.** A cistern charged on Thursday the twenty-ninth, six times in three weeks, at no figure, and nothing yet entered for the ninth seam's mooring. The north berth out and the third lay full and both of them in a list being made up in a city this week. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The light not on and the repair account empty since the middle of February and no boat of this office of its own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. The hard at Ashtide. Nineteen winters in a naval file and in a coat. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. Eleven copies and a printed sheet in a market and a printed sheet in a city in July. A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, and this yard has not gone.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope lent at nothing and entered at nothing. Three returns on the road to a city, and a fourth question asked by a man in one of them and three quarters of it answered, and the fourth not answered and the reason given in the first line of the answer and not the last. And no occasion entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved.

**And a lead plug about the size of a thumb with a piece of chain in it and a clean half-inch drilled hole, eleven inches under a bell in eight and a half feet off the south end, taken out on the twenty-seventh of February, carried in a bellwright's coat for four months, put on a bench on the first of May on purpose, described four times by four men who do not agree, sitting in a chest in a shed on this hill with a lock the yard owns, and the lid has a boy's writing on it, and the number in it was said out loud once in a room on Sunday the second of July and is not in any book in this harbor and is not going to be, and this party has undertaken not to ask about it again, and has entered that it has undertaken it, and the undertaking is binding on this party only and not on the office and not on the market and not on the boy, who was not asked and is not to be.**
