# Chapter 109

*The Writ on the Mole*

The demand came up the hill on Saturday the twentieth of May at about eleven in the morning in the hand of Ambrose Sell, a clerk of the circuit office of this coast, who is the mouth of a clause and not an antagonist and did not raise his voice once.

It was four lines and it named a day.

*The provisional writ of the first of May is to be produced, on Monday the twenty-second of May instant, at ten of the clock in the morning, at the top of the steps of the mole at Morrow Quay, landward of the third seam's mark upon the stone, to an officer of the circuit office of this coast and to an officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor, upon demand, as the reporting clause provides. The writ and such other documents as the holder is required to produce are to be produced. This is a demand and it is not a complaint and no answer is asked for.*

"It is not a complaint," Ambrose Sell said, "and I have been told to say that in the doorway, and the person who told me to say it does not want you to spend a night deciding whether to come. **The clause says *the writ* and the office's own form asks for the writ, and I am the clerk of an office and I know what a box holds.**"

"And who is the second man," Calder Marr said.

"An officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor, and he is not me, and he is not from my office, and he is not from this coast, and he will be on those steps at ten on Monday and he will have a book of his own, and I have read this demand three times and there is no line in it that puts the two of us alone with you."

"Then this yard is not going to be alone with either of you, and it is not going to be alone with both of you either," Nessa Pike said. "I will be on that mole at ten and I will not be three feet away from him, and if either of you wants a word said in a place where the wind is not going to take it you will say so on the Friday."

**And then Calder Marr asked the question nobody had told him to ask, and it is the only thing in that morning that is about the office and not about the yard.**

"Mr Sell. If this yard were to stand one foot below the top of those steps on Monday morning — on the crown, between the sixth and ninth seams, where this office has entered seven times that it treats the whole of the mole from the third to the eleventh as inside the first clause of a notice the office did not write — what would have to happen first."

Sell looked at him for a moment and then said it in the voice of a man reading a box.

"An instrument of this office putting a person upon ground the first clause closes. The reeve's acknowledgement, in his own hand, in this office's ledger, with the cost named as his. **And the reeve is not in this harbor. The reeve entered the eighth of those on Wednesday the seventeenth and the eighth is three days old, and the eighth is in a ledger at the top of that hill, and a ninth would have to be asked of a man who is at a sitting out of this harbor and cannot be fetched by a clerk on a Saturday, and there is no instrument of this office that will do it without him.** So: the answer is that a ninth would have to be asked for, that it could not be asked for before Monday, and that the cost of it would fall on one man and would never be free. And I am not telling you that it will be refused. I am telling you that it cannot be got, and that I have looked, and that the office does not issue instruments of this office in this harbor."

"Then this yard will stand on the top of the steps," Calder Marr said, "**on Monday the twenty-second, at ten, landward of the third seam's mark upon the stone, where the ground the notice closes begins below, and I am saying that out loud now, in front of two men, because it is the first time a holder has told an officer of a circuit office where it would stand and why, and I would like it written down.**"

Ambrose Sell wrote it down. He wrote the words and the hour and the reason, and he read it back, and then he said the other thing.

"And I will write one line under it and read that too, because you have been decent about it and this office is not as careful as it would like to be. **I am entering on my own page that the ground the notice of the thirty-first of October closes begins below the top of the mole's steps at Morrow Quay, and that this is my own reading and not a ruling, and that this office has no power to narrow that notice and has not attempted to narrow it this morning.** Whoever wrote that line may disagree with me in a court of wreck claims in Veyr and I would rather be wrong on a page than quiet in a street."

---

On Sunday the twenty-first they did what a yard does the day before it has to produce a thing, which is to find out what it is going to produce and what it is not.

The book came off the bench and went onto a flat board with two battens under it, because a book that has to be carried forty yards in a wind wants a board, and Nessa Pike cut the battens on the Sunday morning out of a seat that was not going to be used again this season and entered the seat in the book as a cost, at no figure, in the first column, with her name against it.

**And at about four in the afternoon Calder Marr opened the third column and looked at it for a while and then wrote a line at the head of it, under the two times, in his own left hand.**

*That the third column was created on the second of May for what a man said when he was not in the boat, and carries the time a thing was read on the steps and the time it was written in this book, and that from Monday the fifteenth of May no man in this party is filling it, and that no man in this party is now able to say whether the two times at the head of it are the same time, and that the party is going to keep the column and is not going to scratch out the lines in it and is not going to pretend it is current, and that the reason is that a column with a head on it and nothing under it is the honest shape of this yard on the twenty-first of May and a man in a city is owed that shape and not a better one.*

"I am going to put that in the third column," he said, "and I want it noticed that I have put it in the third column and not in a second one, and the reason is that the reason for it is a thing a man in this shed can check, which is that there is nobody here to fill it."

He sat with the book on the board after the others had gone down the hill. What he did not want, and had no name for, was to carry it down a mole on Monday in two men's hands and watch a man with a claim in this harbor read it. It was the only thing in this harbor that had been true every single day since the fourteenth of October, and there was no clause in the writ that could stop it, and there would not have been one even if he had wanted one, because on Saturday he had been the man who said out loud that the clause's own construction carries the book with the writ.

He shut the book, put the board under his arm and went down the hill, and he entered none of that in the second column. The second column is for things a man in a shed can check, and he could not check one part of it, and the whole of this yard's method is that a thing which cannot be checked goes in anyway with a reason against the line. It was the first time he had wanted to leave one out.

---

**On Monday the twenty-second the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight, because it is wiped every Monday at eight whatever is or is not on it, and the clerk kept his own page, and there was no sitting, and at ten o'clock two men came out onto the top of the mole's steps.**

The tide was making. The wind was in the south-east and about two hours of it. The sun was out, which is the reason that the ground the notice of the thirty-first closes begins below the top of those steps could be seen to be below the top of those steps, and could be seen not to have been argued with by anybody standing on them.

Ambrose Sell came up the steps with a book of his own under his arm. The other man came up behind him and was an officer of a vessel holding a claim in this harbor and had a book of his own under his other arm and did not give a name and was not asked for one, and Nessa Pike was four feet away from the holder of the writ for the whole of it and said afterwards that she had not moved and had not been asked to.

**This is what was produced, and it is the whole of it.**

**The writ.** Produced in Calder Marr's own hand, in the harbor authority's own sheet, in the harbor authority's seal and not a naval one, with the reporting clause attached and the three names on the clause and no face in the room: requested in writing by the magistrate of that circuit, signed by the master of a cutter holding a claim in this harbor, sealed by Magister Ilya Serr, senior Registry examiner. It was read out by the clerk of the circuit office in his own voice and the fourth line of it was read out twice because the fourth name on the writ is a hired man of another boat and both men on those steps read it twice.

**The crew's book.** Produced because the clause's own construction carries it with the writ, and not because anybody asked for it, and the yard said so out loud before it opened it.

"Nobody asked for this," Calder Marr said. "**The office's form asks for the writ. The clause says *produce this writ upon demand*, and a writ is a piece of paper about a holder, and the piece of paper that describes the holder is in here, and I am producing it because the clause is the thing that was asked for and this book is inside it, and I am saying that out loud so that nobody in a city can read this down as an office that opened a yard's book without asking.**

"And I am producing the whole of it. Both columns entire. The third column entire, with the head on it and the two times and the line a man wrote under the head on Sunday saying that no man in this party is filling it any more. And the first column, and the second column, and the reason against every line of the second."

**Both returns.** The first, made on the fourth of May, two days late, unshortened, with the two days between the finding and the return on the page and not in a margin, and with the line that says the object was in a boat in a shed in this harbor from the twenty-eighth of April to the fourth of May, entered in the yard's own hand without anybody asking. And the second, made on the tenth, on the day, unshortened, saying the lump is worth nothing to anybody the party has been able to find out about, and carrying the scalloped edge in the first column with the party's own sentence that it does not know what the edge means and a clause asks for a finding and not a meaning.

**And two other things, because they were already public and refusing them would look like a man with something:** the tenth thing, off the market wall, in the yard's own hand, with the count of the copies of the public sheet of the fourteenth of October at its head and the four unreached holders named as not reached and not asked; and the slip with the eleven names on it, which has been on that wall since the third of May and for which a shilling changed hands at the foot of this dock and was not the yard's income.

**And then the sentence that is the best thing that happened on that mole, and it was said by a man in a coat and not by a clerk in a room.**

"Is there a third return?"

**"No," said Calder Marr. "There is no third return and there is no fourth, and I am going to say the whole of that in the open air on a mole, because I would like there to be a man in a circuit office who has been handed three of them and can remember. This yard has made two returns under that clause. The first was made on the fourth of May and it is two days late and it says that we have not looked at a lump of glass in a bucket and will not describe it, and it also says where the thing was for six days, which was in a shed, which is a thing we had to tell you about ourselves. The second was made on the tenth, on the day it was due, and it says the lump was looked at on the sixth on a mole's crown in daylight and is worth nothing. And there is a line in our own book dated Saturday the thirteenth of May that says no return went up on that day and why, and that line has not been sent to anybody and it is not going to be, and I am telling you it exists because I would rather you heard it from me than found it in a file. There is no third. If a third is asked for, this yard will say that it has made two."**

"Did you look at it," said the man in the coat.

**"We looked at it. On Saturday the sixth of May, on the crown of that mole between the third and ninth seams, in the open air and in daylight, at about eleven in the morning, because the second clause of the notice of the thirty-first keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed in this harbor and this yard has not argued that clause since the thirty-first of October and is not going to argue it on a Monday in May. Here is that return. It is worth nothing. It is on the page, with the place and the hour and the keeper and the limits, and the scalloped edge is in the first column because a man on a mole can look at an edge. And this yard is not going to say the scalloped edge means anything, because it does not know what it means, and a clause asks for a finding and a finding is not a meaning, and this yard put that sentence in a written objection on the second of May and sent it up the coast in a jacket with the writ and you are holding the writ."**

And then the man in the coat turned to the book, and went to the third column, and read the head of it, and then read the line a man had written under the head on Sunday afternoon, and then looked up.

"Who fills that column."

**"We do," Calder Marr said. "That column is of this yard's book and of no office's and it is the only instrument in it that this yard invented for itself, and it was created on the second of May by a man of twenty-two who could not hold a pencil for more than ten minutes at a time, and the reason it exists is that four men in a boat cannot check what a man said on a mole's steps, and he was four days late in three weeks, and the day he cannot hold a pencil is the day a man in a city holds this yard's record in his own hand and writes what he likes into a space on a form. That is what the head of that column says and it is in his own writing, and you are looking at it.**

"That man went off the stone on Monday the fifteenth. **This yard is one man lighter than it was on the fifteenth, and that column is going to be filled late for a while, and where it is going to be filled late is in the second column, with a reason against the line, and the reason is that there is nobody here to fill it, and I am going to be filling it myself and I am not fast.**"

He did not offer a hand and nobody asked him for one. He wrote the hour against it in his own book and the clerk of the circuit office entered the hour and the place and the fact that the ground the notice of the thirty-first closes begins below the top of the steps at which the production was made, and read all of it back.

**Nobody in that crew defended the boy's handwriting, and nobody in that crew was asked about it, and the one thing read out of the book on Monday the twenty-second of May was a line a man of twenty-two had written in his own right hand on the second of May, and it said, in his words, that a figure taken on purpose on a flat calm is a figure and not a note heard out of the weather, that the count of occasions stands at eleven where it stood on the thirty-first of March, and that he was not going to make it twelve by writing a number.**

There is no diver in that. Nothing went into the water on Monday the twenty-second and nothing came out of it. A day on a mole with the writ in a man's hand and a bucket and a tarpaulin and two stones on the crown above it raises nothing and produces no return, and the last return this yard has made is the second. No ninth acknowledgement was entered, and the yard said out loud that it did not ask for one and could not have had one, and the clerk of the circuit office wrote that down too. The two men went down the steps at twenty past ten with a writ and a book and two returns and a market wall's worth of paper between them, and the ground the notice of the thirty-first of October closes began below the top of them the whole time and was not touched by anybody.
