# Chapter 127

*A Question About a Bar of Gravel*

It came up the coast on Thursday the twenty-ninth of June in the ordinary post with the yard's name on it and no seal on it of any kind, and it was one sheet and it was four questions long, and it is the first paper that has come out of a city to this yard since the first of May.

Nessa Pike read it out in the shed at about ten in the morning.

---

*Sir. I am in Veyr and I print, at my own cost and for a boat's money, a yearly return of the coasting small craft of this coast, of which yours is a stretch of about five miles. I have printed it nineteen years and I have never had it wrong that anybody could prove, because I print only what I have seen or what a man has given me in writing, and I mark the two differently.*

*I have in hand a copy of a sheet that was nailed up in your market on the third of May last and is not mine. I have had it about three weeks. It came to me from a master of a boat of your second lay who copied it out in pencil at the foot of your own dock, in about an hour, for nothing, and would not take anything for it. So it is a copy of a copy and in a hand, and the hand is legible, and I have no complaint of anybody and this is not a complaint.*

*In it there is a line about a bar of gravel which comes up to two and three-quarters of a fathom in the middle of a spring tide at twenty past two on the flood, five miles north of your headland, and the sheet says it is on your own harbor authority's chart as a blank.*

*I have a figure of my own for that bar, which is two and a half on the same tide, made in May by a man in a boat of mine, and I have not printed either number, because I do not print a number I cannot account for and mine is one I cannot account for.*

*Therefore four questions, and I will be grateful for an answer to any of them and I will print an answer to any of them as it stands.*

*First, on what ground and how many times. Second, at what hour by whose clock. Third, off what — that is, from what tide, in what state of moon, in what strength and direction of wind. Fourth, by whose hand the lead was cast, and whether that hand had been in that water before.*

*I am not asking you what a bar is. I am not asking you for anything to sell and I am not asking you to give me a copy of your sheet, though I will buy one at four marks if you ever put one out. I have told you what I have and what I have not, and I mark the two differently, and that is the whole of my trade.*

*And one more thing, which I put last because I do not want it to be mistaken for a question. I am not of any office. I hold no seal and I have not asked for one and there is nobody in this city who could give me one for this. I print what I am given and I mark what I have not seen, and that is all.*

*Your servant, J. Hesketh.*

---

**They read it twice and then they sat with it for an hour and a half and neither of them said anything for the first twenty minutes.**

"He wants four answers and three of them are in that book," Nessa Pike said. "**Ground, hour, tide, the lead, the man who swung it, the number of casts. All of it is in the first column of the second of June and in the first column of the twenty-third of May and it is all checkable by any man who can sail to that coast. That is three quarters of his letter answered out of a page we wrote for a different reason.**

"And the fourth one is the whole of it. He wants to know whether the hand had been in that water before."

"**Thirty-one years," Calder Marr said.**

"**Thirty-one years and a slate and he is not a writer, and he is a witness and not a party, and he is not paid, and he has not been asked to be right. And that is all on your own wall in your own hand since the third of May, and it is the reason we can answer three quarters of his letter and not the last quarter, and I want that on the page before anybody decides what to do.**

"**And I am going to say the thing I have been sitting here for an hour working out how to say, and then I am going to sit down.**

"**He is going to print a stranger's number. He has told us so in the second line of his letter, in the plainest words a man has used at this yard since the third of May, and he has told us he will mark what he has not seen, and he means it, and he is right to.**

"**So there are two things going to happen in a city in July and neither of them is anybody's fault, and the first is that a man with a press is going to print that a bar of gravel comes up to two and three-quarters on a spring flood off this headland on the authority of a sheet nailed to a wall in Morrow Quay, and that is what our wall is for and we knew it when we nailed it up.**

"And the second is that he is not going to be able to find out. **Because the only man alive who can tell him is in a boat in this harbor with a bad wheel of his own, and you are not going to ask him, and I am not going to let you ask him, and if you write a line in that book saying we wanted to, I will take the pencil.**"

"**I was not going to,**" Calder Marr said, "**and it is on the page from the fourth of April in your own hand and I have not improved on it and I am not going to.**"

"Then say the reason out loud, because it is the best reason anybody has given in this shed in five months and it is yours."

---

"**Write it in my own words and in the first column,**" Calder Marr said, "**and read it to me before it goes in.**

"**That a bar of gravel comes up to two and three-quarters of a fathom in the middle of a spring tide at twenty past two on the flood, five miles north of this harbor's headland, inside the water and inshore of the shoals, and that the figure is the figure of a man of forty-eight of this harbor who has worked that water for thirty-one years, given out of a slate of his own before his boat came out, on the twenty-third of May last, on the *Grey Widow's* own account, and that he is not of this party and is not of any association and is not paid and is not a writer and has not been asked to be right, and that this party is not going to ask him now.**

"**And the reason this party is not going to ask him is on the wall and it is this: a figure a yard has asked a man to stand behind is a figure the yard has published. And this yard is not able to publish a figure it cannot check. And on the third of May this yard put a sheet on a wall and on it is a line saying that nobody knows who takes a road off a chart, and if we go and get a man to stand behind our own number then we have made ourselves the man who takes it off the chart, in our own hand, on our own wall, and every man in this harbor will read it in about nine days.**"

He stopped.

"**And the second half of it, and I want that printed as well, because the first half looks like a small refusal and it is not.**

"**A question about a bar of gravel cannot be answered by the people who printed the bar, because the people who printed the bar did not cast the lead. That is not modesty and it is not a rule. It is the shape of the thing. And this yard would rather a man in a city print *we cannot tell you* in a hand that says it has not been told, than print our guess, and this yard is going to tell him so in the first line of the answer and not in the last.**"

---

**The answer went up the coast on the Friday the thirtieth in the ordinary post and it cost nothing, and it went with the ground and the hour and the tide and the lead and the man who swung it and the number of casts, and the state of the moon, and the breeze, and the name of the boat, and every one of those out of the yard's own book in the yard's own left hand, and the last paragraph of it was the sentence above and it went up first.**

They wrote the rest of it at about ten at night and Nessa Pike read it back and Calder Marr read it after her and there was nobody else in that shed.

*First column, checkable by any man who can sail to that coast: that the party can answer the first, second and third of four questions asked by a printer of Veyr on the twenty-ninth of June, and that the fourth it cannot, and that the reason is stated in the answer and is not a rule and is not a refusal of the questioner and is a fact about this party.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that this party has not asked the man whose figure it is, and has not written a line saying it wanted to, and that the clerk of this harbor authority came up this hill in May and has asked about the two times at the head of the third column and was not given them and did not ask again, and that the same thing is happening in this letter and is happening in the whole of this yard and that the party can no longer put a figure anywhere that a man can be held to.*

**And the third column was not filled that week with any of it, and Calder Marr wrote one line at the head of it and nothing under it, in his own left hand, and the line is not about the letter and it is not going to be in any column.**

*That the third column is not where this goes, and that it is not going to go in a second column either, and that the whole of this entry is in the first column where anybody can be asked about it tomorrow, and that the reason is that a thing a man said once in a room cannot be read back by a column and can only be read back by a person, and that this yard has not got the person.*

---

**On Thursday the twenty-ninth they charged the cistern at about ten, on the rota, the sixth time in three weeks, at no figure, and nothing came in or out of that yard in either direction, and nothing came in or out of it on the Friday either.**

**And on Friday the thirtieth, at about half past two, a shipwright of this yard came into a market in this harbor and gave a man four marks for a printed sheet and carried it up the hill and put it on the bench, and the four marks did not come out of the bag and are not in any column and are not the yard's money, and nobody is going to pay her back and nobody is going to thank her and she has not asked to be thanked and does not want to be.**

"That has been on that wall since Saturday," she said, "**and the man in Veyr wrote to us on Thursday.**

"**Five days. He printed it on Saturday and the question came on Thursday and this yard is going to answer it on Friday, and by the time he reads what we have sent him there will be a better sheet in a market in this harbor that has been answering him since Saturday, and he will never know that there was a version of it in our own hand that cost us nothing.**"

"It has his figure on it," Calder Marr said.

"**It has his figure on it and the ground and the hour and the tide and the man's name and the number of times, and it has put the man under it as a man who has worked that water for thirty-one years and has a slate and is not a writer and has not been asked to be right, and it has four marks at the head of it and the price of a copy, and it was put up by a crew of six with a compressor of their own, and that is a man who has never been in this harbor and has never seen a lead go down and got every one of those things right off a slip of paper and a wall.**"

"**So the answer is that he is right. He is right about everything, and he is wrong about one thing, and he is going to be wrong about it in print in about six weeks, and there is not one thing this yard can do about that, and I have put that sheet on that bench on purpose and I want it on the wall of this shed where I can see it.**"

Calder Marr looked at it for a while.

"**Then say the rest of it. You have not said the rest of it and I would rather you said it than that we both sat here.**"

"**No. I said *he is going to be wrong about one thing*, and I have said it out loud, and I am not going to say which thing, and you are not going to write it in the book, and if either of us writes it down then this yard has taken a man with a bad wheel and a slate and asked him about himself by writing his name next to a doubt. And when the man in Veyr prints it in six weeks it will be a stranger's number against a stranger's number, and the man with the slate will be right and will not know that he was right, and that is a trade I am content to be part of.**"

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Friday evening with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street.

**Five marks in a bag**, and nothing came in and nothing went out on the Thursday or the Friday. Thirty-one on the note and not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid. Nothing in a tin. **Ten lines and forty marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, five envelopes on a bench in the chest and not among the five rolled in oilcloth, and the last of them is four marks on Tuesday the fourth of July.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid out of any money that has come into this yard since the eighth of May. **Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, and about four hundred feet of one of their own warps on the bottom of this harbor, and a woman said in a market on the twenty-fifth that she was going to ask a man of another port, and this yard has no standing with her.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away, **and a man of fifty who has said he will say a thing in a room if somebody asks him in a room and not before, and whom this party has not asked in a shed, a boat, a market, a mole or a room this month, and is entered as not having asked, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a number said in a room is a number with no second column to put a reason against, and this yard is two and a half men and has not got a hand that will last a week, and one hand that wrote the book since the fifteenth of May is a hand that goes off the stone.**

A cistern charged on Thursday the twenty-ninth, six times in three weeks, at no figure. Nothing yet entered for the ninth seam's mooring. The north berth out and the third lay full, and both of those now in a list in a city being made up in the first week of July. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead. The light not on and the repair account empty since the middle of February and no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. The hard at Ashtide, unanswered. Nineteen winters in a naval file and in four lines of a letter in a coat. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. **Eleven copies, a list of eleven on a wall, and a printed sheet that has the ground and the hour and the tide and the man's name on it in type, printed in a city, in July, off a letter this yard answered on the Friday.** A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, and this yard has not gone.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope at nothing. Three returns on the road to a city. No occasion in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved, and the delay a hand's width of a second and not taken and not written down.

**And on Saturday the first of July there is a day at the second seam on a hirer's own account, and on Sunday the second there is a room in a boat belonging to a herring skipper with a table in it, and this party has asked a man of fifty to come to it, and he has not said no.**
