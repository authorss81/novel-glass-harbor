# Chapter 105

*The Man Who Was Not on the Writ*

Noll Tarner came up the hill at ten past ten on Friday the twelfth of May with a coil of his own line over one shoulder and no boat, and he came up on his own legs like a man who has been walking down a naval quay for seventy days and has had three things to carry and has left two of them there.

He is thirty-four and he has been at the pile since the fourth of March with his gear and his pump in a lighter, his master's own logbook in a naval file, and a copy of a leaf of this yard's own book in his coat, and his crew may go ashore twice a day and that has not been a punishment and has never been a mercy.

"You are off our slate," he said, in the shed doorway, before he was in. "Since November. I took a job at Ashtide in the middle of a season and I did not tell my own crew first, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to apologize for it in a doorway."

"Nobody asked you to."

"I am telling you anyway because you are going to find out from somebody else in about a week and I would rather you found out from me in about a minute." He put the coil down. "**And I have got no boat, and I have got the best gear on this coast in a lighter I cannot get at, and I have got no instrument of any kind, and I have got eleven weeks of my own money and I have not got a day.** I want a day. I am not asking for the second lay and I am not asking for a mole. I have dived the mole at eight fathoms on a slate and I can do this and I have got four men who would follow me up this hill tonight if I asked them and would not be paid until the summer."

---

Nessa Pike put the cup down on the bench.

"Then you are going to be told no in this shed and you are going to be told why, and the why is the whole of what I have to say to you and I have said it in my own words and Calder is going to say it in his, and then you are going to be offered something that is not work, and you are not to be insulted by it."

"You have not said no yet."

"I said you were going to be, and I am a woman who says a thing before it happens and you will find that out over the next two years or you will not."

**Then Calder Marr said it, and he said it in one sentence and he said it in his own words and he did not explain it twice.**

**"A man who is not on the writ is a man who cannot be reported, and a man who cannot be reported is a man whose accident a clerk has to write about as a stranger's."**

He put the writ on the bench where anybody in the room could put a hand on it.

"That clause asks for a finding and the place and the hour and the keeper and the limits, and it has one other thing in it, which is that where a finding is disputed between persons this office may seat one of them and one other and take what the finding is as it is given. **A finding on this mole is made by four people whose names are on a sheet in a clerk's office, and a fifth man's name is not on it, and if that fifth man puts his hand on a shackle at the second seam and opens it, then a form in a circuit office has a space in it for a man it cannot name, and a space on a form is the worst thing in this coast and everybody in this trade knows it.**"

"You could have me named on something," Tarner said. "There is room."

"There is room on a work permit on a bond and there is room on a claim window and there is room on about nine other instruments that this office and this coast have, and **every one of them is an instrument that the harbor authority has to put its hand to, and this yard has spent the seventh of that reeve's own acknowledgements on Saturday morning and has one left in the book and is not going to spend it on a man who wants a day, and if the office asks me for a name for you I will give them your name and I will not have a hand in the water.**" He turned the writ round on the bench. "So the day is no, and the work is no, and the reason is a piece of paper and not a judgement about you, and I will write it in this yard's book in those words with your name on it, because I have refused four men on this mole since the fourteenth of October and every one of them is on a page and you are going to be the fifth."

"I am not asking to be the fifth," Tarner said. "I am asking for a day."

"You are going to be the fifth," Nessa Pike said, "because he does it anyway. Have a cup of tea and take the wall home with you."

---

**And then they gave him the thing that was not work, and it was a copy of the tenth thing, in his own hand, at no charge, with his name on it.**

"There is no charge," Calder Marr said, "and there is no charge because there is no money in this yard to charge and not because of anything else. The boy will write it out this evening and you can have it in an hour, and the copy will have your name on it at the top, because that is the one thing on that sheet that binds anybody and it is a piece of paper and no more. **The count of the six hand-copies does not change and the yard is entering that, because this is a copy of a copy and it is not one of the six and no two men in this harbor are going to be able to tell in four years which is which unless this yard says so on the page.**"

"Then write it on the page," Tarner said, "and I will take the copy, and I will say now, standing in this shed, that I am not going to sell it and I am not going to give it to a naval officer, and I know you have not asked me for either of those, and a man who has been at a pile for seventy days is not stupid."

"Write it," Nessa Pike said, and the boy wrote it, and it went in the first column because Tarner was in the room.

---

**And then the man who had spent five months refusing this yard in a market sat down on a bench in a shed and said the thing the batch is for, in a working man's mouth and not as a point.**

"You have a road," he said. "Five miles of coast that no chart carries, with a cairn at eleven feet and a stem at nine fathoms and a bar that comes up to two and three-quarters on the flood off a spring, and six boats have your sheet in six hands, and none of them is yours. **A road that no chart carries is a thing a man can sell, and the reason this one cannot be sold is that nobody knows it is a road, and the day a man comes up this hill with a map of it, the first question is not what is under it. The first question is who pays for the map.**"

"That is the question on my wall and you have not answered it, and I did not put it there expecting anybody to."

"I have answered it about a man with a pen and I will say the other half of it and then I will stop. **It is not only that somebody takes a road off a chart. It is that a map is a thing you can sell, and the only reason I cannot sell yours is that I would have to be able to say it is a road, and I cannot, because you have refused me the word in public and you were right.** The day you put a name on it is the day it is worth money, and the day it is worth money is the day somebody takes it, and you have just made certain that neither of those happened by the only means available to you, which was being wrong in public twice in a fortnight and saying so yourself."

"And the other thing," Calder Marr said, "which is the one nobody asked for and which goes in the first column because a man in this room can be asked about it again tomorrow."

"Go on."

"**This yard has been wrong in public twice this month in two columns, with a reason against every line of the second, and a party that has been wrong twice has no business selling anybody a map.** And that is a good thing, and the reason it is a good thing is not that it is humble. It is that the six people who copied that sheet by hand in six boats on the second of May would not have copied it off a man with a map. They copied it off a wall that said *order shown, purpose unknown* in a boy's hand with a boy standing next to him saying he was not one of the six. **A map you can buy is a map somebody made money selling, and a wall you can copy is a wall six men decided was worth the trouble of copying.**"

Tarner looked at him for a moment.

"That is the best sentence anybody in this harbor has said about anything in five months," he said, "and it is a sentence about not being able to sell something, and I am going to be annoyed about it for about a week. I have been at a pile for seventy days and I have had nothing to do except be annoyed, and this is better." He picked up his coil. "**I will say the thing I came to say and then go down the hill. I have got a second boat to buy out of Lowfen since November and there is nobody to sell one to, and there is going to be a low country of reed islands about nine miles off this coast where the channels are kept by hand by men who do not want a diver, and I have not been there and I am not going to be, and I am telling you that because you will hear it from somebody who is not me in about a month and I would rather you heard it from me.**"

Nobody in that shed said anything about Lowfen, and it went in the second column with a reason against it, and the reason was that a man who has been at a pile since the fourth of March and has not been to a place cannot be asked what a place is for.

---

**On Saturday the thirteenth the yard sat down in the shed and wrote the line it is not sending to anybody, and that is a thing this yard has not done in five months: it has written a thing down knowing exactly who is not going to see it, and it has done it because a sentence went up the coast on the second of May and it is time somebody in this yard found out whether the sentence is true.**

*Saturday the thirteenth of May. Since the finding of the sixth of May there has been no finding of worked glass, whole or in part, and nothing has been raised off ground which any other party claims, and no return goes up on this day or on any day after it until there is a finding or a raising to report. The reason is that a return states a fact and a return about nothing is not a smaller return, it is not a return, and this yard entered on the first of May that a finding is not a conclusion and will go on reporting findings and will not go on reporting conclusions, and that sentence is going to be read in a circuit office in June and this line is the test of whether it was true.*

***And this line is not sent to anybody. It is a fact about this yard's book and it is in this yard's book and it is not in the harbor authority's ledger and it is not in a circuit office and no clerk has been asked to see it and no clerk is going to be told of it.***

The boy read it back and Nessa Pike read it after him and Calder Marr read it after her, and it is the third of the lines he has read back since the second of May and it is going to be the last of them, and there is nobody in this yard who is going to read the next one back, and none of the three of them said so out loud on the Saturday.

The bag was thirty-nine and the note was thirty-one and there was nothing in a tin, and the column was five lines and twenty marks with the fifth of March in it, and the wage column was not a Tuesday's business this week because the boy was paid to the end of that week on the Monday and the next fell on the Tuesday after, and that was next week's problem and not this Saturday's.
