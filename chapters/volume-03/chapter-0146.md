# Chapter 146

*An Office With Nothing Left to Put In Its Book, and a Cost Entered Before It Is Paid*

Sunday the sixth of August, at about ten in the morning, on the mole's steps above the third seam's mark, in about eleven people's hearing, in the open air, and the wind in the west-southwest making four hours of it out past the head.

**The officer of this harbor authority was on his own steps at ten in the morning on a Sunday, which he does not do, and he did it because the tide was in his own book and the tide does not know about vessels, and his coat was too clean for the wind and he had a slate under his arm and he had written on it before he came out.**

"**I have entered a thing in my own book this morning at about eight and I am going to read it out and I am not going to soften it and I would rather it was said on a mole than in an office.**

"**That a vessel of this coast, named on a notice nailed to the market wall of this harbor, went on the ground at about half past two in the afternoon on Wednesday the second instant, inside the four hundred yards, with four persons in her, and that the moorings of the third lay were shut on the authority of an examination of this mole's footing entered in that same book on Monday the twenty-sixth of July, at the cost of this office, with the repair account of this office empty since the middle of February and the money not coming; and that this office has had no boat of its own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April and could not have rendered assistance with one, and that the entrance of the examination is in that book and the ground is in that book and the two are eleven words apart and I put them eleven words apart myself and I would like that read out.

"**And the second thing, and it is the one I have entered at my own cost and not at anybody's asking, and it is this. A notice of the Crown was nailed to the market wall of this harbor on the twenty-eighth of July and another on the fourth of August, and both of them are nailed over a sheet that is a return made under a writ of salvage working granted by this office on the first of May, and a sheet that goes up goes up on the outside of whatever is there, and what is there is the only paper in this harbor that any office in any city is obliged to look at.

"**So that the paper of this office is what a notice of the Crown is nailed over in this market, and every man who reads that wall in the next four years is going to read this office's name off the bottom of a Crown paper, and this office cannot take the notice down, and has no power to take it down, and the notice has no seal that this office can put a hand to, and that is a cost and I have entered it as a cost and it is mine.**"

He looked up from the slate.

"**I am going to say the last of it and then I am going to go down to the office and I am not going to be on these steps again this month.

"**I have entered in my own book on Wednesday the twenty-seventh of July that the moorings of the third lay are shut for four days. I have entered today that a boat of this coast went on the ground inside the four hundred yards on the second day of those four. The tide gang says three short and one long was answered from the inner shoals on Saturday and the party says it does not know what it means and has entered that it does not know, and I have read that entry because a sheet was left on the step of this office by a man of the third lay and a sheet left on a step is a thing anybody can read.

"**And I am entered as saying to the holder of the writ of the first of May, in the open air, in about eleven people's hearing, that there is nothing in my book that will put a boat of mine past the outer marks, and I have looked, and there is nothing in yours either, and that is not a failure of either book. It is the design.**"

---

Nobody thanked him. He had not asked to be thanked and there was nothing on that mole to thank him with, and Calder Marr said two things and one of them was about a vessel and the other was not.

"**I am not going to ask you what your entry of the twenty-sixth is about,**" Calder Marr said, "**and I am not going to ask you what the second one is about, and I have not asked you the first one and I am not going to now, and all three of those are going in the book with the date.**

"**And the other thing is this. You have said in front of eleven people that your paper is what a Crown paper is nailed over. I am not going to say thank you for that and I am not going to use it and I am not going to write it on a wall. If a man reads that wall in four years he is going to read it as a reeve's price and it is going to be a reeve's price, and you told a clerk on the fourteenth of July that in four years that was going to be the only reason nobody could take the thing out of that mole, and I have written that down and I have written down who said it.**"

Kest put the slate under his arm.

"**Then we are agreed about what it is and not agreed about anything else, and I have eight acknowledgements in that ledger and both of this year's are spent and there is no ninth and I would be glad if you would keep that as a standing arrangement between us for the rest of the season.**

"**I have nothing left to give anybody. That is not a complaint. It is a figure. I have no second season in my keeping, I have no charter, and I have a ledger with two entries a person in this harbor can use and both of them cost somebody who was not in the room.**"

He went down the steps at about ten past ten on the Sunday, and his coat was still too clean.

---

**And on Monday the seventh of August the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was fourteen Mondays, and the clerk kept his own page and said nothing, which is the seventh year he has said nothing about that wall and the first year the yard has had a reason to want it said.**

**And the cistern was charged for the fifteenth time, on the rota, at no figure, and not by this party, and this party was on the second seam's own face from seven in the morning.** Two men of the third lay and a woman of the second lay gave about an hour and would not take anything and were not thanked, and **fifteen walks now stand on that office's own page and the cistern behind the engine house has held since the nineteenth of June and this party is not going to be thanked for that and is not going to use it for anything.**

A day at the second seam's own face on a hirer's own account, nobody in the water, four casts of the hand lead's line and the string laid out again along the foot of the face with two pins, because the two of them wanted to walk it once more and could not say why and did not write a reason against it in the book.

Gross on the board thirteen. The eighth two. The share two. A hired hand three. The victuals of the day one and rope one. All three of the last paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, that being nine out of thirteen and the yard's being four, and the yard's four is the same four it was paid on the thirty-first and the same four it is paid on Wednesday next, and this party has entered that it is the same four and is not going to make a practice of the sameness. **Eleven marks in a bag on the evening of Monday the seventh of August, and that is the whole of the money this yard has had since the twenty-second of July, and it is the first time the figure has been eleven since the fourteenth of June.**

**And Calder Marr did not go over to the far bench on the Monday, and Alden Marr was at the far bench from about nine until about four with his back to the room and a cough that came every forty minutes and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and the yard has entered that the elder brother did not go, and neither brother has said why, and neither brother is going to.**

---

**And at about nine o'clock on Monday night the shed was shut and the lamp was lit and the holder of the writ of the first of May wrote the whole of it in the first column in his own left hand, and it went in before the day and not after, and the shipwright read it back and he read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed and there has not been anybody else in that shed at nine o'clock since the fifteenth of May.**

*That on Monday the seventh of August instant, at about nine in the evening, before anybody has been wet and before a mark has gone out of the bag, this party entered that on Tuesday the eighth instant it is going past the outer marks of this harbor in its own boat, on its own account, with no instrument of any office, to the vessel named HEARTHWAKE, lying inside the four-hundred-yard line, being about a mile and a half off, in about seven fathoms, with four persons in her; and that this party has entered that the sentence it entered on its own account on Wednesday the fifth of July and repeated in a market on Sunday the twenty-sixth of July, to the effect that the outer marks and the bar of four hundred yards off them stand where the writ of the first of May says they are and are not nearer, and that this party is not going past them this season and has not tried and is not going to, is broken by this entry, and that this party broke it, and that the breaking is entered here in this party's own hand on this date and hour, in advance, and not afterwards and not softly, and that anybody may come up this hill and ask whether the party went and when and what it cost, and the answer is on the next line and the next line is the whole of the payment.*

*And that the cost is these and there is nothing else: a wage of four marks falling on this same Tuesday, which goes into an envelope before the boat is launched; the office's pot of air at this yard's own two-mark rate, two pots, four marks; the master of the Sallow Reach, six marks on the figure he named in the doorway of this shed on Sunday the thirtieth, of which three is not going to be paid because there will not be it; and the sentence itself, which cannot be said again, and about which this party has entered that a yard which has said a thing in a market and has not done it is a yard whose saying is worth nothing in any figure that yard puts on any wall in any year after this one, and that this party has said the sentence, and has broken it, and is going to be poorer on Tuesday the eighth of August than it was on Saturday the fifth, and that the forty marks standing to a boy of twenty-two and the forty marks standing to a woman of thirty and the eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women and the eight marks and a day of a boat standing to a man of fifty in this harbor are not paid either way and are not in the cost of this and are not reduced by it and are not mentioned in it.*

*And that this party has entered that the third lay lost four days of the best of its season to an examination of this mole's footing entered in another book on the twenty-sixth of July at that office's own cost with no money behind it, and that the third lay is not this party and is not going to be asked to pay anything and is not going to be thanked and is not going to be used, and that the party knows the price of those four days and has entered the price and is not going to look at it.*

*And that this party has entered that no person, boat or lead of this party is going anywhere near the imperial line, which lies about three miles beyond that vessel, and that the full Second Bell license is not granted to any person in this harbor and is not approached and is not going to be approached, and that this party would not know what to do with it if it were granted and is not going to be in a position to find out, and that the writ is bounded by the outer marks and the reporting clause is on the writ and there is no writ past the outer marks and no clause past the outer marks and no keeper and no place and no hour and no limits and no copy into anybody's ledger, and that if this party raises anything on that ground this party will not report it to any office and will not tell any office that it has done so, and that this party is aware of what that will look like in a file in five years and has entered the looking like in advance.*

---

"**Read it back,**" said Calder Marr.

Nessa Pike read it back from the top to the bottom, in the shed, at about ten past nine on the night of the seventh, with the lamp lit and the far bench behind her and nobody on it after four o'clock, and he read it back after her.

"**And I am going to say the last thing and it is not in the book, because the book has had enough of me this month.**

"**You did not go over to the far bench today and you have not gone over to the far bench since the thirteenth of November and I am not going to ask you why and you are not going to tell me, and I am not going to say the next sentence.

"**I am going to say a different sentence. In about four years a man is going to come up this hill and ask this yard why it went past the outer marks, and the answer is going to be in the second column of that book, and the second column of that book is a page of things a man cannot check, and a man who cannot check it is going to say that the party went out there for something and got it.

"**We did not get anything. Whatever is in that boat went back into it. I would like that written down by somebody who is not you, and you are not going to write it, so I am going to say it to you now in a shed on a Monday night and you are going to remember it and not write it, and if I am ever the one holding the pencil in this yard you can check whether I kept it.**"

He did not write it.
