# Chapter 114

*Better on a Lead*

The man who hired the pump came up the hill on the Friday morning of the second of June at eight and asked for a day at the second seam, and he asked for it in the doorway the way a man asks for a thing he has asked for before.

**"My own ring and my own shackle and about four feet of my own warp come on the ring, and it is under six feet off the second seam on the mole's own face, and it is my own account and nobody has ever claimed it and I have been in nineteen years and back."**

"What is your name for the figure."

"Fourteen on the board at your rate and the same eight and the same share and the same two marks for the office's air, and I will pay the hired hand and the victuals at the mole, which is what a hirer is for. **I will not argue the eight and I will not argue the share and I have never argued either in nineteen years and I am not going to start on a Friday.**"

"You have hired that pump four times since the twenty-first of March and I am not going to make you wait four days for an answer."

"You did not make me wait. I came up the hill on the twenty-second of April and you were in a coal store and I did not get it, and I have said that in a shed and it is on a page." He put his thumb on the bench. "**And I will say the other half, which is that a man is not a pump and a day is not two days, and I have got a boat of my own lying with a warp on her ring for nine days and I would rather have it up on Friday than on a Tuesday.**"

"Then Friday," Nessa Pike said, "and you will get a column with the figure on it at the head of the dock tonight and the hour written, and if the figure on the board is not the figure you pay, you may read the page."

---

**Nobody went in the water on the second of June and that was decided before the lamp was lit and not on the morning.**

The ground was the second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark, and nobody had any business being in the water over it, and there was no air in this harbor that would have taken a man over it anyway, the compressor's gland having gone onto the yard's own pump on the nineteenth of May and made no difference whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose and there is not one in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms.

*That on Friday the second of June instant this party is working the second seam's own face landward of the third seam's mark on a hirer's own account and not on this office's account and not on any account claimed by any party; that no person of this party goes in the water and nothing under the bedded material is touched and nothing is raised off that ground today; that the work is a repeat of the soundings of that face with a hand lead and a line and a pole, four casts, and that the reason this party is doing it on a man's own account landward of the third seam's mark where no instrument of any office is required is on the page and is not in the harbor authority's book and is not going to be; and that the party is keeping the practice in its own book anyway and is not going to have the two confused.*

**The practice was read out in order, in the shed, at about seven, with the shipwright.**

Named tender who is not the diver — Nessa Pike, who is a shipwright and has never been in the water in her life. An independent line, the lead line, off the boat's own bow. A fixed air limit on the slate, set at the boat by the diver — and there was no diver today and the slate was not used, and the entry says so. A seated named weight, the holder, at the after thwart. A cutoff the tender can pull without asking anybody, and it is hers, and the word is *Enough* and she will say it and nobody will argue it. And a stated reason, entered in the harbor authority's book before anybody goes in the water, in Calder Marr's own left hand, with the clerk present, unedited.

"There is no reason going in the office's book today," Nessa Pike said, "because nobody is going to be wet today, and I have just read the six out loud in a shed with two men in it, and the one that is the office's is the one we did not need, and I want the page to say that we did the other five for nothing on a Friday because I said do it."

**"And a tender's stop is not a request," she said, "and there is nobody in this yard to stop me and that is exactly why it is in the book."**

---

They were off the steps at half past eight on a flat sea with a light breeze and the flood making, and the two hired men were in the boat and the yard was on the after thwart with the pole and the hand lead's line from the net loft, and the man of fifty-one of the *Grey Widow* was on the mole's steps at about eight and a quarter.

He came down and stood on the crown above the third seam's mark and looked at the boat for a while, and Calder Marr said it to him straight, because the yard's whole practice in this harbor is a thing being said straight and in time.

**"You are not on the lead today."**

The hired men did not stop. The pole did not stop. Calder Marr did not stop, because there was nothing to stop, and he went on down to the boat.

He did not ask why. That was the whole of it, and it was the loudest thing that happened on that mole in June, and Nessa Pike wrote it in the book afterward in her own words as *the man did not ask and this yard did not offer and neither of them did it for a reason anybody could hear.*

Then, at about nine, with the boat lying to and the flood still making, he said the thing, and he said it to nobody and out loud, and nobody in that boat asked him one word about it.

**"A lead is not a depth. A lead is a repeat. Four casts on the same mark in the same water is a figure. One cast on a good day with a light breeze is a figure anybody can check."** He put a hand on the mooring post. "You have had three men on this mole since the middle of February and you have not had four, and that is not a sentence about the men."

"Write that down," Nessa Pike said, from the boat, without turning round. "**His own words. First column, because he said it in the open air in front of five of us and we can all be asked about it on this mole tomorrow. And the party is not asking him one word else and is not going to, and the reason the party is not asking is on the page and it is that a man who has asked not to be told is not a man this yard can put on a lead and then write a reason about, and this yard's own book is a place where reasons go.**"

He stayed on the crown until about two and did not go near the boat again, and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him anything, and at about one he rang the handbell off the mole head — not for anything, there was nothing to ring it for, and the tide was making and no boat was in the roads — and it went out flat over the water and came back off the crown, and Calder Marr, on a boat, with a pole in his hands, could not tell in which of the two sounds he had been given the answer which of the two was in the boat.

He did not write it down.

---

**They got four casts, and three of them were on the same mark and one of them was forty feet south of it, and it was the one south of it that was the useful one, and the yard had been told about that at about ten in the morning by a man on a mole who has not been asked to be told anything.**

The line read:

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: the second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark, four casts of the hand lead at the top of the flood on Friday the second of June, being eleven and a half, eleven and a half, eleven and a half, and at forty feet south of the mark eleven and three-eighths; the two men hired for the day, at a mark; the pole; the lead line, being the yard's own flax made in the net loft on the second of May; the tide, top of the flood, the flood making from half past eight; the man of fifty-one of the *Grey Widow*, who said the sentence about a lead and who was not on the lead and was not asked why he was not.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that a man on a mole who has swung a thing in the same water on four different days knows what that water does to a reading, and that no man with a slate can know it in a day, and that the party has the figures of the twenty-eighth of April and the eighth of May and the second of June for that face and no fourth, and that the party is now keeping the first kind of figure without the second, and that this is a worse book than the one this yard had on the fifteenth of May and a different book from the one it had in April.*

*And that the holder on a boat at about one o'clock could not say in which of two sounds the answer to that handbell was in the boat, and has entered that as a fact and not as a name for it, and the reason is that no man in a boat can check where another man's sound went, and the form of that line is the form it has had since the twenty-sixth of December and there is no name in it and there is not going to be one this year.*

---

They were on the mole's steps at about four with the wind in the west and nothing much in it, and the man of fifty-one had gone down the hill an hour before without saying where he was going.

"Write the last one out," Nessa Pike said. "**The one about the practice. And put my name to it and not yours, because it is my trade's sentence and you are going to want to argue with it later and I am not going to let you argue with it in your own hand.**"

Calder Marr wrote it and read it back to her.

*That a method which is only honest while one particular man is holding it up is not a method; that this yard has had two such methods in six weeks and has found out about both of them in about the same week; that the first was a boy of twenty-two with a hand that was going and a third column of his own invention, and this yard is keeping the column and cannot fill it, and the keeping is honest and the filling was; and that the second is a man of fifty-one of another boat who is better on a lead than any man in this yard and who has not been asked what he hears and has asked not to be told; and that this yard went within four inches of asking him on the crown of this mole on Saturday the twenty-seventh and did not, and that a party which has come within four inches of asking a man a thing and stopped cannot then put him on a lead and write down that it wanted to.*

"I did not want to write him off a lead," he said. "I wanted to write down that we wanted him on it."

"**And that is the sentence that would have been in the book and it is the reason I am not letting you write it.** A column is not a place for what a yard would have liked to do. It is a place for what it did, and the day it starts being the other thing it is a column a man in a city can be lied to in without anybody lying."

He looked at the water for a while.

"Eleven and three-eighths at forty feet south," he said. "And three elevens and a halfs on the mark. And I would have had that off him by ten."

"You would have had it off him by ten," she said, "and there is no instrument in this harbor that will tell us whether it was eleven and three-eighths at ten or at one, and he knows that and we know that, and **that is the whole of what a man on a lead is and it is not a thing you can have out of a book with the door shut.**"

The figure was read out at the head of the dock that evening at about seven. Gross on the board fourteen at the yard's own rate. The eighth two, the share two, both gone to the harbor authority and not argued. The office's pot of air at the yard's own two-mark rate, two. A hired hand, three, and the victuals of the day, one, and the rope, one, all three of them paid at the mole by the man who hired the day, which is what a hirer is for. **That is eleven out of fourteen and the yard's is three, and the three is in the bag and the eleven never was.**

Twelve marks in a bag. Thirty-one on the note and not re-written. Nothing in a tin. Six lines and twenty-four marks in the wage column and the fifth of March the oldest. Four hundred feet of a woman's own warp on the bottom of this harbor, undiminished, and eight shillings and tenpence standing. Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, not reachable by the writ, and she is not of it. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright paid in April and a second eight marks and a day of a boat owing him since the twenty-third of May.

**A day lost and two men paid and one man of fifty-one not put on a lead, and the figure that was forty feet south of the mark is in the book and nobody in this harbor can say what it would have read at ten o'clock instead of one, and that is the honest shape of a yard that has decided something and has not got a way of being wrong.**

And it was Saturday the third of June, and there were three days to the first of July, and on Sunday the fourth a boat was to come into the roads out of a storm belt with a man in it who had a copy of this yard's sheet in her and who wanted nothing from a diver, and the wall behind the reeve's bench comes down at eight on Monday morning and has come down bare four Mondays running.
