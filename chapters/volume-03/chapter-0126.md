# Chapter 126

*What the Coast Office Said*

The *Anna Ray* came into the roads at about four on Wednesday the twenty-eighth of June and lay against the north wall with the flood making and about half an hour of it left, and Elias Voke came up the hill at half past six with a paper in his coat he did not take out.

**He had been up the coast eight days. He had not gone to an office with a paper of his own this time. He had gone to sit in a room, and he had been told a thing, and he had asked for it twice, and he had it.**

"**I am going to say it on the boards and then I am going to go home, and I am not going to say it in a shed and I am not going to say it to a clerk, and if anybody here wants to write it down they can write down that I said it and not what I said.**"

"Write that down," Nessa Pike said. "In your words. Both halves of it."

---

**"It is not an answer to my paper. I want that first, because the last three times this coast has said anything to me it has said it in a way that turned a thing into a smaller thing, and I am fifty-one and I have carried other men's paper up here for four months and I am not going to let it do that to this one.**

"**It is the answer to the question your harbor authority asked that office in November, which was what a harbor can moor. And it is two sentences, and I have got them, and here they are in their mouth and not in mine.**

"**First. This office does not moor a fleet and cannot and has no power over a harbor authority and has never claimed to. It has had this harbor's statement of its own capacity since the first of March and it has not answered it and is not going to, because a statement of what a harbor can moor is a thing a harbor says, and this office has nothing to do with harbors and no paper to answer one with.**

"**Second. It has read the statement. It has taken one line out of it and entered it, and the line is the north berth out of service, the ninth seam's mooring out of service since the first of May with no end entered, and the third lay full.**"

Somebody said something at the back of the crowd.

"**That is not me. Those three things are not in my paper. They are in a document this harbor's own reeve sent up this coast in May, about what that notice of October has cost this harbor, and that document went into the same file as my paper, and an office that has a file reads the whole file.**"

---

He put his hand flat on the boards, which he did not do often.

"**And that line is now in a list of what this coast can take next winter, and the list is being made up in the first week of July, and it goes to a man in a city, and my nineteen winters are on the same page as that line and they are not on the same line as that line.**

"That is what I have. **And I am not going to stand here and tell you it is a wrong thing to have done, because it is not a wrong thing. It is a clerk in an office doing what an office does with a number. Somebody up there asked for a figure of what this harbor can moor, and this harbor sent them a figure, and the figure said three berths out and a lay full, and they have written it down, and in November when the fleet comes down this coast there will be a piece of paper in a city that says in advance that Morrow Quay is a place where a boat may not lie.**

"**And that paper will be right.** That is the thing I have come down this coast to say to about forty people, and it is the worst thing anybody has said to me in nineteen years, and it is going to be right, and it is going to be right because we wrote it down ourselves and gave it away for nothing.

"And the hard at Ashtide. I asked in November. I asked again in January and a clerk took it and said the same words twice. **I am not carrying it a third time and I am not a man who stops carrying because the answer is no — I have said that in a room in front of a man who wrote it and I will say it again on a wet board. But I am fifty-one and I have three men and one is a boy and I have a berth I cannot get at and a mooring out of service since the first of May, and there is a place on this coast that could take me in and has not answered since the ninth of November, and I am going to stop asking in words and start asking in a different way, and that is not this office's business and is not your business and is mine.**"

He looked up then, and he was looking at the wall, and there were three things on it.

"**And I will say the last of it because you are standing there and I would rather say it in a market than have it found out.**

"**A vessel in this harbor has bought a chart of this harbor out of a reeve's hand on Thursday last, and the price of it went in a ledger on the Wednesday with a clerk in the room, and the price was a line about a bell at the south end and the line was worth nineteen winters to me, and the reeve wrote to me and told me so in his own hand in four lines and I have them in my coat.**

"**And I am told — told, not told by anybody in this room — that that office has asked four harbors the same question this season, and the answers are in, and the list is being made up in the first week of July, and the chart and the list are for the same office in the same week.**"

He took his hand off the boards.

"**I do not know whether that is a thing or a coincidence and I have decided, in about the nine seconds it took me to walk from the north wall to here, that I am not going to find out, because a man of fifty-one who goes and finds out what an office does with a chart ends up being a man who has gone and found out. And I have a boat lying against a wall with three men and a boy on her and nineteen winters on my back, and I am not going to be the man who goes looking.**"

---

**And then he said the thing that was the reason the yard had said nothing to anybody for two days, which was that he had not been in that room.**

"**I was four days out, and I found out on Friday, and I have had two days with it and I have not slept much, and I want it said before anything else that a man of fifty-one who has been owed for nineteen winters and finds out on a Friday is entitled to be angry, and I am not going to use it, and this yard is not to think I am not angry because I have said so. I was forty miles up this coast with a basket of fish in it when I found out, and I did not shout, and I am not going to shout now, and that is the whole of what I have about it.**"

"So a reeve sat in a room on Wednesday morning and wrote a herring skipper's nineteen winters into a naval file, with a clerk in the room, and read it back, and then wrote the whole of it out again in a letter in his own hand and paid a boy nothing to carry it up the coast, and that is — " he stopped for a second — "**that is the best thing that office has done for me in nine years, and I want it written down that I said that, in a market, on the twenty-eighth of June, and I want it on the wall if somebody ever needs it to be true, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it and he does not want to be.**"

The market went on round them for a while.

---

Nobody went up the hill with him and nobody sent anything with him and the yard did not follow him down.

**And the two columns were filled that night at about eleven, in the yard's own book, in Calder Marr's own left hand, and the first column is the only place the thing is, and it is there because a man said it on a market board in daylight and can be asked about it tomorrow.**

*That on Wednesday the twenty-eighth of June instant, at about half past six in the evening, on the boards of the second lay, in a market, in about forty people's hearing, Elias Voke of the *Anna Ray* said that the coast office of this coast had told him, in a room, and had not put in writing, that it does not moor a fleet and has no power over a harbor authority and has not answered this harbor's statement of its own capacity and is not going to; and that the office has read that statement and has entered one line out of a document of this harbor authority's own, being the north berth out of service, the ninth seam's mooring out of service since the first of May with no end entered, and the third lay full; and that that line is in a list of what this coast can take next winter, the list to be made up in the first week of July, and that nineteen winters of disputed and unpaid moorage dues upon his boat stand on the same page as that line and not on the same line as it; and that he said the office was not doing a wrong thing and that the paper produced in November will be right, and that it will be right because this harbor wrote it down itself and sent it up for nothing; and that the hard at Ashtide has not been answered since the ninth of November and that he is not carrying a paper about it a third time; and that a vessel in this harbor has bought the chart of this harbor out of a reeve's hand on the twenty-second of June and that the reeve told him so in his own hand in a letter of four lines, and that the list and the chart are for the same office in the same week; and that he said he did not know whether that was a thing or a coincidence and had decided not to find out; and that he said all of it in a market on his own account and not to this party and not for this party's benefit, and that he was not thanked and did not ask to be.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that this party was not in that room and was not in the coast office and has never been in either, and that the reason it has entered all of the above in the first column is that a man said it out loud on a market board in daylight in about forty people's hearing and can be asked about it tomorrow; and that this party has entered that it cannot check one word of it and is going to publish it anyway, and that the reason is that the reason is the same reason as everything else on that wall, which is that a thing a man will not say in a market in front of forty people is a thing this harbor will find out about from a clerk in a fortnight, and would rather hear first.*

**And under that, on a line of its own, the sentence that has to be on the page and that nobody in this shed is going to enjoy.**

*And that on Sunday the twenty-fifth of June instant, in a market, Sella Brigg said that she was going to ask a man of another port about about four hundred feet of her own warp, and that the coast office has read this harbor's own account of what it cannot moor and has entered three of the reasons out of it, and that both of those were published by this party on its own wall without a thought, and that this party is the commonest cause in both cases and is not going to be excused from either.*

---

**And the wage went in on the Tuesday the twenty-seventh, at about eleven in the morning, in Calder Marr's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it in the same hand.**

Four marks, a week at the rate from the first of February, for the week of Tuesday the twenty-seventh to Monday the third of July inclusive. The envelope went into the bench chest and did not go among the five rolled in oilcloth, and nothing carried it, and the reason is on the page and the reason is that a wage is not a reason to go to a door.

**Ten lines and forty marks in the column, and the fifth of March the oldest, and five envelopes on a bench.**

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Wednesday evening with about nine people in the street, and about nine people heard the ninth seam and the north berth and the third lay for the sixth time since the first of May and said nothing about any of it, which is about what they said last time.

**Five marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note and not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid. Nothing in a tin. **Ten lines and forty marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and five envelopes on a bench in the chest.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, not reachable by the writ, not an assistant, not asked to be useful. **Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, and about four hundred feet of one of their own warps at the bottom of this harbor, and thirty marks refused in this shed on the fourteenth of June, and she has not been told and has worked it out in a market and has entered that she did.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away, and eight marks and a day of a boat recurs in about four years and the four of them were told on the third of May. A cistern charged on Monday the twenty-sixth, five times, at no figure. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead. The light not on. The repair account empty since the middle of February and no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. **The hard at Ashtide, and a man of fifty-one who is not carrying a paper about it a third time.** Nineteen winters in a naval file and in a letter of four lines in a coat. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found.

Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown, under two tarpaulins, and the yard's own boat back in the water at the north end. Three returns on the road to a city. And no occasion entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved.

**And on Thursday the twenty-ninth a letter came up the coast for this yard with a postmark on it from a city, and it is the first paper that has come out of a city to this yard since the first of May, and nobody in this shed knows what is in it, and the yard is not going to be told before the man who wrote it is in the room.**
