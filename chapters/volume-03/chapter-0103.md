# Chapter 103

*A Day Rate*

The hearing room's slate wall came down at eight on Monday the eighth of May, because it is wiped every Monday at eight whether there is a sitting or not, and the one figure the reeve had chalked on it on the first stood down with the cloth and nothing went up in its place, and there was no sitting, and the clerk said in the yard's doorway at a quarter past nine that his own page had wanted a side and three-quarters that morning and that he was going to keep it, and that the wall was a convenience and the ledger was the record and everybody in Morrow Quay knew the difference between the two of them now and had not needed to be told since March.

Then he put an order on the bench, and it was the harbor authority's own, and it was the first piece of paper to come up that hill in five months that had this yard's name on it as a thing to be paid.

---

**"A day at the second seam," the clerk said, "on this office's own account, at this yard's own rate, under the writ of the first of May. The ground worked is the second seam's own ring and shackle and about four feet of herring warp come on the ring, and nothing under the bedded material and no ground above the third seam's mark, because this office is not paying anybody to stand in a notice.**

"The mole steps are four feet higher than they were in April because the man who clears them was stood off this office's wall on the first of May to mind a cart, and he goes back on the tenth, and the third lay went dark on the nineteenth of March and is not going to be walked in June by a man with a barrow. This office has a repair account that has been empty since the middle of February and this is not a repair and it is not coming out of that account. **It comes out of the harbor's own day work, which is the only line in this office's book that has anything in it, and it comes out at this yard's own rate because this office has no rate of its own and never has.**"

"How much is a day," Nessa Pike said.

"The yard's rate is the yard's rate. The figure is the figure you put on your own board on the fifth of April and I have read it in your own book and I have not altered it and I am not going to." He turned the order round. "**And I am going to say the other half of it now, in the shed, before anybody signs anything, because it is the whole of what a writ is worth in this harbor and this office has never had a paper to say it on. The air on Saturday came out of this office's compressor on this office's own hose and this office paid for it and did not put it on a gross then either. The pump on Saturday was this yard's own and was hired to this office for the day. Today the air is this office's and there is no pump on the mole at all, because this yard has no gland and about ten feet of hose less than it had on the twenty-eighth of April.**"

---

**The reason went in the harbor authority's book in Calder Marr's own left hand at half past nine on Monday the eighth of May, with the clerk present, unedited, and the clerk did not summarize it.**

*Ground, the second seam's own ring and shackle on the face of this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, at the top of the flood, and no ground above that mark and nothing under the bedded material and no ground any other party claims. Purpose, a ring and a shackle of this office's own ground tackle to be freed and about four feet of herring warp come on the ring, and that warp is a herring boat's own and is that boat's on her own account and is not this party's and is not being claimed. The people in the water are proposed to be two men neither of whom is one of the three persons this notice names for this office's work: Tam Slee, of the *Grey Widow* of Morrow Quay, who is the fourth person on the writ of the first of May and was asked in person on the first and is content to be there, and Wil Slee, of this harbor's own tide gang, who went in with Slee on the twenty-eighth of April under a claim window. Neither is of this party. The limit of each is his own and is fixed by him at the boat before anybody is wet and no man in this harbor may put a hand on it. The tender is Neale, foreman of this harbor's tide gang, and he is not a diver, and the cutoff is his to pull without asking anybody. The air is this office's own, out of this office's compressor, carried on this office's own hose, and this yard has no compressor gland and no delivery hose, and the yard is not on this office's work and is standing on this ground as the owner of a bucket and of nothing else. And this office has no power to lift the notice of the thirty-first and is not asked to, and nothing under the bedded material is to be touched, and the warp when it comes up goes back to the boat it came off and this party takes nothing of it.*

The clerk read it and read it back and entered the hour and the two divers and the tender and the cutoff and the air and the limits, and then he said the thing that is his.

"This office cannot enter a bell-metal gun-port in a heap behind a shed, and it is not going to, and I am not going to be asked to. I can enter this day, at this figure, on this ground, and I have entered it, and there is no box in this office for the fact that a ring came up with four feet of somebody's warp on it, and I am keeping the rest of it in the yard's book where it belongs and I have said so five times this year and this is the sixth."

---

They were on the second seam at ten minutes to eleven with the flood running hard up the mole's face and the office's pot hissing on the boards and the office's hose going down to it in a loop that took two men to hold, and the two Slees went over on the account they had used on the twenty-eighth of April, each of them with his own limit at the boat, and Neale went on the lead line with a coil on his arm because Neale is the only man in this harbor who can hold a line and be told to stop without hearing a reason.

Twenty-two minutes is what Wil Slee set himself. Fourteen is what Tam Slee set himself. They were up at half past eleven and six minutes and at a quarter to twelve respectively, with an old ring and a shackle and about four feet of herring warp wound twice round the ring, and the warp was green and hard and had a skin on it and it went back onto the third lay's wharf in Dace's own hands at half past two on the Monday afternoon, and nobody weighed it and nobody priced it and it is the only material this yard has put back on a boat in five months.

**Nessa Pike signed her name against the whole of the day and was right and nobody thanked her.**

"Two things go on this page that were not asked for," she said, "and I am putting them both in. The first is that this yard did not take the eighth and the share off a fisherman's own account to pay this harbor's wages in April and it has taken them off this office's account today, and that is the same argument I made in this shed on the fifth of April with the same man said to be against it, and the man who said it then is the man who ordered it now, and I am writing his name against my name so that nobody has to take my word for it that I argued the other way. **A yard that argues the eighth off a day rate on this office's own ground is a yard that is arguing the mole out from under itself, and I said that in April and I am saying it now and I would like the two statements side by side in four years.**"

"And the second."

"The second is that the air we just spent was the whole of the air in this harbor. Five days bought for a day, off one pot, at this office's compressor, and it is the only pot of air in Morrow Quay and this morning it was the only air in Morrow Quay and tonight it is an empty pot on a cart. **A yard that spends its whole air on a Tuesday has nothing on a Wednesday, and this yard has nothing on a Wednesday and will have nothing on a Thursday, and the only reason we had anything on a Tuesday is that this office cannot pay a man to walk out to a sluice and can pay for a pot of air.**"

---

The money was read out on the Monday night and the boy read it twice.

"Gross on the board, twenty-six, at this yard's own rate for a day's work at a seam on this mole with the office's air going and two men in the water and a tender on the line, and every mark of it is the harbor authority's money on the harbor authority's own account and not a word of it is a fisherman's. **The eighth three. The share three.** The pot of air, this office's own compressor and this office's own hose, at this yard's own two-mark rate, **two**. Rope **one**. The victuals of the day **two**. Tam Slee, of the *Grey Widow*, hired for the day, **three**, and Wil Slee of this harbor's own tide gang is on this office's own hand and is not in this column and is not in this yard's. That is **fourteen** out of twenty-six and the yard's is **twelve**, and the twelve is counted out of the clerk's coat in the shed with the hour written against it and read back twice, and it is the second money this harbor has paid this yard since the first of February and the first of them was six marks for air and a pump on the twenty-eighth of April, and this one is more than double it, and the difference between the two is a writ and a day at a seam and a bellwright's boat and four days, and a writ with four days on it is not worth twelve marks a day and anybody who tells me otherwise has not been on this mole."

**And the boy said the second thing nobody had asked for, in the middle of the list and not at the end of it, which is the way he says things.**

"The day raised a ring and about four feet of a herring boat's own warp on her own account, and there is no worked glass in it, and nothing was raised off ground any other party claims, and so no return goes up on this either, and the party is not sending one, and the reason is that the second limb of the clause is material from ground which any other party claims and the second seam is this office's own ground and the mole's own and nobody in this harbor has put a claim on the face of a mole landward of the third seam since before I was on this stone. **A clause this yard can obey in silence is worth more to it this month than a clause it is afraid of.**"

"Read the owing back," said the shipwright.

**The column at the end of Monday the eighth of May: five lines and twenty marks, the fifth of March the oldest. Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced and not asked to be reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the writ cannot reach her and she is not of it. Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg standing, undiminished, and about four hundred feet of her own warp on the bottom of this harbor. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, PAID on the twenty-ninth of April out of four hands in a market, and the going out it bought was done at ten past three on the second of May in a flat calm and is spent, and it recurs in about four years. Twenty marks of unsold iron with a saw kerf in a crown shackle, and four hundred and forty pounds of old iron in three pieces in the same heap behind that shed, and a bell-metal gun-port among the three, unsold and unweighed and unpriced, in a heap this yard may not sell because it has declared a thing about its own gear in a book. No compressor gland and no delivery hose in this harbor. A hand lead's line made in the net loft on the second of May and made badly once. The ninth seam's mooring out of service with no end of it entered, the north berth out, the third lay full, the tide gang four men short, the cistern on a rota a man does not walk, the fire party's hose dead for the season, the mole-head light not on. A notice of the thirty-first of October that is six months and a week old today and is not lifted and is not argued. And a bucket on a mole's crown under a tarpaulin and two stones and a spare fender, with a lump of green glass in it that is worth nothing to anybody this party has been able to find out about, and a return about that lump due on Wednesday the tenth.**

The fourteenth week of a wage was paid on Tuesday the ninth of May at ten in the morning, four marks, out of the twelve, and it was not announced on the Monday and nobody was told on the Monday that it was coming, because a boy told his wage is coming on a date is a boy given a date, and this yard has spent four months taking dates out of that book.

Nobody in that crew said one word about what the day had cost the yard and the boy and the man who paid him both knew exactly what it had cost and neither of them said it.
