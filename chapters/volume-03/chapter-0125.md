# Chapter 125

*Under Two More Pieces of Paper*

The market on Sunday the twenty-fifth of June was a full one and the wind had gone into the north-east in the night and was making a short sea outside the mole head, and the boards were wet by seven and about forty people went past the wall in the first half hour, which is more than went past it in the whole of the previous month.

There were three things on it.

The naval paper of the eighteenth of March was where it had been for ninety-nine days and no man had ever been asked to move it. The sheet of the seventh of June with a woman's name and a figure and a date on it. And, over the top of both, since Saturday, a printed coasting sheet four feet by three with a scale on it and a price on it and eleven lines at the head of it.

---

Sella Brigg was in front of it at about ten past eight with a basket and her hand going down the yard's own sheet the way it always goes down a page, and she got to the printed one and stopped.

"**There is three on it now.**"

"There is," Calder Marr said. "**A man called Ollett, of a boat called the *Sallow Reach*, out of Ashtide, put that up on Saturday morning for a shilling a copy, and he did it on the same nails because those are the only nails, and he put it over the top because that is what you do with a thing that goes on the outside. Nobody in this harbor has asked him to move it and nobody is going to.**"

"And is it any good."

"He knows more than we do about how to print a thing and he has a compressor of his own and forty fathoms of his own hose and six men, and we have none of the three. **The figures on it are ours and yours and they were on our wall first, and they are on his sheet in a better hand with the day and the ground and the number of times against each one, and that is the whole of the difference and it is a real difference and I am not going to stand here and tell you it is not.**"

She looked at the printed sheet for a while.

"**And the eleven at the head of it.**"

"**That is ours. That is the slip with the eleven names on it that has been on this wall since the third of May for a shilling, and he has printed it, and I am not going to pretend to you that I could have stopped him, because I could not have stopped him, and the only way to have stopped him would have been to have taken the slip off the wall, and a slip that is not on a wall is a list a man in this harbor can read out of a window.**"

"You could have printed your own," she said.

"**I could have, and I have not got eleven marks and I have got nine, and I am not going to pretend to a woman on a wet board in a market at eight in the morning that eleven marks is a thing I do not have.**"

---

Then she asked the thing, and it was not the thing anybody in this yard had expected, and Calder Marr answered it straight because there was no version of it that was any use being dressed up.

"**Mrs Brigg. What I am going to say is the whole of the money of this yard since the eighth of May, and I have said it out loud on this mole's steps and at the head of this dock and in a market on the sixth of June in front of thirty people, and I am going to say it out loud to you here because you are owed the answer more than anybody in this harbor.**

"**That is every mark that has come into this yard since the eighth of May. That is a day at the second seam on a hirer's account and a day at the second seam on the *Anna Beth's* own account and three weeks of a boy's wage that goes into an envelope on a bench and does not go anywhere. Thirty-one on a note. Four hundred feet of your own warp at the bottom of this harbor since the sixth of February, which is a hundred and thirty-nine days, and about twenty-six marks a day to get up, and the yard's own column of one day of it is twelve, and it is not a one-day job and nobody in this harbor can tell me what a second day of it is because nobody has ever done a second day of it.**"

"And a printed sheet with your figures on it went up yesterday," Sella Brigg said, "**with a date on it and a price on it, and that sheet says everything the sheet of the seventh of June says and about four times as much.**

"So I am going to ask a man from Ashtide."

Nobody in that street said anything.

"**You cannot stop me and I would think less of you if you tried," she said. "**I have said no to you in my own voice on a mole's steps in front of four men and I would say it again at four marks as I said I would. But I said no to *you*, and I have not said no to anybody else, and there is a man in this harbor right now with a compressor of his own and forty fathoms of his own hose and six hands, and I have got four hundred feet of my own best tarred stuff lying on the bottom of this harbor with nobody's name on it but mine.**"

"That is your right and this party is not going to stand in the way of it," Calder Marr said, "**and I am going to say the whole of what that costs and then I am going to stop talking about it, because I have said it once in June and I am not going to make a habit of it.**

"It costs this yard the only thing it has left to be right about. That refusal went on a wall on the seventh of June with your name on it and a figure against it and a date, and it is the reason that man printed a better sheet than we can, because we told a market what a thing costs and then we could not do it, and he could print that we could not do it and put it under our own sheet. **And in about three weeks he will come up this hill and ask you for the job and he will be able to do it, and the only reason he will be able to do it is that we could not.**

"**And we would have been able to do it on the tenth of June for four marks, and there was a boy who could hold a line, and he is on a herring boat now.**"

She put her basket down on the boards and stood on it out of the wind.

"**Say the other one,**" she said. "**The one you have been not-saying since the fourteenth.**"

"Not a word of it."

"**I will say it for you, then. You were offered thirty marks on the Wednesday and you turned it down, and you have not told me, and you have been not-telling me for eleven days in a market where I stand every morning, and I have worked out that there was thirty marks in it, because there was a man in a coat up this hill on the Wednesday and I know which coat that was.**"

Calder Marr did not say anything at all, and about four people in that street watched him not say anything, and Sella Brigg went on.

"**Well. I have got a rope at the bottom of the harbor and eight shillings and tenpence of yours, and you have told me the price of my own rope out loud in a market twice, and if there were thirty marks in this yard I would have heard about it inside a week, and there are not, and I am not going to stand on these boards and make a scene about it with a basket at my feet.**

"**But I want it said in front of these people that I have asked you for the price of my rope three times since the seventh of June and you have given it to me three times, and that on the third time you gave it to me in a shorter voice than the first, and that I noticed, and that I am not going to say anything about it in a market, and that I have said it here.**"

She picked her basket up.

"**A man from Ashtide will be up this hill on Wednesday or Thursday with a printed sheet and a price on it, and I will give him a figure and a date, and you are not to be here when it happens.**"

"Then I will not be," Calder Marr said.

---

**And the yard entered both of those on Monday the twenty-sixth, and neither of them went in the second column, because a woman said them on a market board in daylight and can be asked about them tomorrow.**

*That on Sunday the twenty-fifth of June instant, in a market, in about forty people's hearing, Sella Brigg of the third lay said to this party that she was going to ask a man of another port about about four hundred feet of her own warp, and that this party said she had a right to and said it would not stand in the way of it, and that this party has no standing with that woman and has not asked for any and is not going to; and that this party was told by that woman, in the same market and in the same hearing, that the owner of the coat on this hill on Wednesday the fourteenth of June was known to her, and that this party did not deny it, and that this party has said nothing to that woman about the thirty marks and did not deny them either, and that the reason the party has said nothing is that a yard which has been refused by a woman it owes money to does not get to also be the thing that makes her hear about an offer in a street, and that the cost of that silence is that she worked it out herself in eleven days, and that this party is entered that she worked it out herself and is not going to pretend she did not.*

---

**On Monday the twenty-sixth the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was four Mondays, and there was no sitting and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it.**

The cistern was charged at about ten by three people of this yard, the fifth time, on the rota, at no figure, with two men of the third lay who came past and helped for about forty minutes and would not take anything and were not thanked.

**And the boat came off the haul-off.**

She had been on the mole's crown above the third seam's mark since the night of the twenty-seventh of May, hauled up by three men on a capstan at midnight in a westerly, and the wind had gone into the north-east in the night and was making a short sea outside the head, and a boat sitting on a stone crown with a tarpaulin lashed over her takes water from the seaward side in a northeasterly whether anybody is in her or not.

It took four of them from about eleven until about one, on their own capstan, on their own ground, landward of the third seam's mark, with the practice in their own book and the clerk not present and the absence entered rather than filled in. **The bucket with the lump in it and the crate with the shard in it were nine feet away under two tarpaulins and did not move and did not go into the boat, because a boat in this harbor with a thing like that in her is a boat in a shed with a lid on, and the two of them are still on that crown in the open air and that is where they are going to be until somebody in a city writes a different order.**

She went back down to her own mooring at the north end above the twelfth at about one, in the water, on the yard's own ground, at no figure, and the yard entered that a day went into that and not out of it, and that there was no money either way, and that the reason is on the page and the reason is that a boat on a mole's crown in June is a boat that is going to be a boat in a condition by August.

**And the third column was filled on the Monday evening, with the two times and the lateness, and it is the fourth time it has been filled since the second of May and the fourth time since the boy went off the stone.**

*That the entry was read on the steps at about seven in the evening on Monday the twenty-sixth of June and was written in this book at about nine the same evening, the difference being about two hours, which is the same difference the boy of twenty-two used to put at the head of it and which this party has noticed and has entered that it has noticed; and that the party is not going to draw anything out of the four figures it has got — about ten hours, about an hour, about eight hours and about two hours — and that the reason is that a yard which begins to keep a figure on its own lateness is a yard that has stopped keeping a figure on its work.*

Nessa Pike read it back and Calder Marr read it after her and there was nobody else in the shed.

"**Then you are going to have to get somebody to read these back," she said. "**Not a boy. A hand that will last a week. And I have said that twice since May and you have not done it, and I am not going to say it a third time, because a thing said three times in a shed is a complaint and I do not make complaints, I make entries.**"

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about six on the Monday evening with the market coming in behind them and about nine people in the street.

**Nine marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the chandler's note and not re-written and the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid. Nothing in a tin. **Nine lines and thirty-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying where it is, and four envelopes on a bench in the chest and not among the five rolled in oilcloth, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the twenty-seventh and it is not going anywhere that week either.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole and not reduced. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid out of any money that has come into this yard since the eighth of May. **Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, and about four hundred feet of one of their own warps on the bottom of this harbor, and on Sunday a woman said in a market that she was going to ask a man of another port, and this party has no standing with that woman and did not ask for any.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, owing again since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away, **and a bell is going to be out in a boat in about four years at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the four of them were told on the third of May so that in four years there is no argument about it.**

A cistern charged on Monday the twenty-sixth, the fifth time. The ninth seam's mooring still out with no end of it entered. The north berth out. The third lay full. The tide gang four men short. The fire party's hose dead until the autumn. The mole-head light not on and the repair account empty since the middle of February and no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April. The *Kittiwake* on the hard. The hard at Ashtide. Nineteen winters in a naval file since Thursday. The *Sea Wren* at the pile. Eleven copies and a printed sheet on the wall that has all eleven places on it. A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, and this yard has not gone.

**Two things of nobody's in the open air on a mole's crown above the third seam's mark, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope lent at nothing and entered at nothing, and they were not moved today and they are not going into a boat and they are not going into a shed, and the yard's own boat went back into the water this morning from about a foot above them.**

Three returns on the road to a city. No occasion entered in the delayed note since the fifteenth of June. Eleven logged occasions and two empty answers and neither moved, and the delay a hand's width of a second and not taken and not written down.

**And on Wednesday the twenty-seventh the wage goes in again, and on Saturday the first of July there is a day at the second seam on a hirer's own account, and on Wednesday the twenty-eighth a man who went up the coast on Tuesday the twentieth came back into this harbor with two sentences in his mouth about an office that has had a fisher's paper since the first of March and has said nothing until now, and he is going to say them on a market board, and this yard is not going to be asked first.**
