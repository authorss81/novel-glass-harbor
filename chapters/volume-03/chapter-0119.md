# Chapter 119

*The Rota Walked*

The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday the twelfth of June and it came down bare, and that was six Mondays, and there was no sitting called and nothing went up in its place, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, because he stopped mentioning it on the twenty-ninth of May and has not started again and is not going to.

**At ten past nine that morning, at the top of the flood, on the mole's toe and inner face between the third and the ninth seams, the officer of the harbor authority went out on the eighth of the reeve's own acknowledgements with the clerk and two men of the tide gang and wrote down what the notice of the thirty-first of October has cost this harbor authority.**

Nobody of this yard was on that ground. Nobody of this yard was asked to be on it. A person of this yard who had walked down those steps that morning would have been refused, in the plain words the eighth of that reeve's acknowledgements uses, and this yard did not go and did not offer and did not ask when it was being done and was not told.

**And this yard was four hundred yards away from it, at the back of the engine house, with a bucket and a hose, walking out to a sluice, because that is a rota day.**

---

The gate was stiff again and the tank was down to about half, and it took the three of them from about ten until half past one, and about twenty people went past on the way to the fish market and about four of them looked.

**"The office's own book," Ewan Tristram said, at about two o'clock, on the second lay's boards. "Not your book. Mine. I keep the keys and I keep a page, and there has been nothing on that page since the middle of February except a line about a cart, and I am going to put your name on it this afternoon and I am not going to put a figure on it, and I want you to understand that I am doing that against my own trade, because a man who puts a name on a page without a figure against it has written down that his office is being given something, and it is not, and the page will say so if I write it right."**

"Write it right, then."

"I will write it right. **Nothing on a page and a thing in a hand are two different currencies, and this office has been living on the second one since the middle of February, and there is no page in this harbor that says so, and I am putting it on one page and it is going to stay on it.**"

He did it at about half past three with his own hand and read it back to the two of them and neither of them asked him to read it back twice.

*Tuesday the thirteenth of June instant. The cistern behind the engine house was charged on Monday the twelfth from about ten in the morning by three persons of the yard of Calder Marr, of this harbor and not of this office, on their own account and for nothing, and the office has not paid any part of it and no part of it was claimed; and the office records it here against its own interest, at no figure, because a thing an office is given is a thing an office has not paid for, and this office has not paid for it and would have been glad to and could not.*

---

**And on the Tuesday the thirteenth at about ten in the morning, on the boards, in daylight, in a market, Ewan Tristram read a column out of his own hand to about nine people, and four of the nine were people this yard had paid in the last four months, and two of those two were this yard, and the yard stood at the back of the crowd and did not come forward.**

**"February. The repair account went out about the middle of it and it has not had a mark in it since and it is not going to have one in June, and I am the wharfinger and I have asked twice and I have been told twice and I am not going to ask a third time this year.**

"**March. The third lay went dark on the nineteenth of March, and the mole-head light went with it, and it is not going to be relit this year, and I am not going to stand in front of you and call that a decision. A light costs oil.**

"**April. The second lay's fire party laid eleven wet lengths of hose out on our own boards on the second of April and pumped them once and they have been dead ever since and they are dead until the autumn. The *Kittiwake* went on the hard and has been on the hard since the twenty-eighth of November and this office's carpenters have not been to her because they are free, and free is a word that means they are waiting for a repair account.**

"**May. The ninth seam's mooring went out of service on the first of May and there is no end of it entered anywhere, and I have asked for an end to be entered and I was told there is no line on the form for an end. The tide gang went four men short on the first of May and one of them stood off our wall from the first to the tenth to mind a cart in front of the last lay's wharf, and that is why the mole steps are four feet higher than they were in April and are not going to be cleared in June. Forty feet of this office's own marline has been lying in the sea off the north wall since the twenty-seventh of February and there will be seventy before September, and fifty fathoms of the herring fleet's winter warp is on that wall where ninety were to lie, and the north berth is out and the third lay is full.**

"**June. A cistern behind an engine house is charged on a rota on a Monday and a Thursday by a man this office cannot pay, and he did not walk out on Monday the twenty-second, and this office has had no boat of its own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April, and there is about a cart's worth of water in that cistern on any day of the week that a man has not walked out to a sluice.**"

He put the slate down.

"That is the column and it is five months and it is in order and it is in my own hand and I have not read it back to myself because I wrote it and there is nobody else in this harbor to read it to me."

Somebody in the crowd said, "Then what do you want."

**"Nothing. And that is not a pose, because a thing I have wanted for five months and cannot have is not a thing I am going to ask a market for in June. I want the account filled and I cannot fill it and I have said so twice and I am not going to say it a third time.**

"**And I am going to say the other half, because I have thought about it for five months and it is the only thing I know that I know. A town is not a thing that is owed things. A town pays in five kinds of coin — a mooring, a berth, a warp, a boy's summer and a cistern — and that is the whole of the list and there is not going to be a sixth. We have run out of four of them. The berth is out. The mooring is out. The warp is on the bottom of this harbor and about four hundred feet of it belongs to a woman who is owed money and has had a figure put on a market wall about her own rope. And the boy's summer went off this stone on the fifteenth of May and he is not the only one of us who has had a summer go.**

"**That leaves the cistern. That leaves one, and we have got it, and a man walked out to it on Monday at ten in the morning for nothing, and I would rather have that than the money and I am not sure why and I am not going to work it out on a market board.**"

Nobody said anything for about half a minute, and then a man of the third lay asked the question that was the actual question, and Tristram answered it.

"You are going to say that is a small thing."

"It is a small thing. **And the reason I am saying it out loud in a market and not in a room is that a small thing said out loud in a market is the only kind of small thing this town has ever actually got, and I have been in this harbor fifty-one years and I have watched a great many large things be announced in this harbor and not one of them turned up.**"

---

**And the mooring's gear went out on the Monday at about four in the afternoon on the office's own account, at nothing, with a line on the office's page at nothing.**

Ewan Tristram sent a boy up the hill for a spare fender and about forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope, and they went up the mole's steps to the crown above the third seam's mark and were put on the crate with the tarpaulin and on the bucket with the lump in it, and the two of them were weighted properly for the first time since the twenty-seventh of May.

"Nobody is going to sell that fender," he said, at the top of the steps. "**It is on my page at no figure and it is not a gift and it is not a loan and it is not a hire, and if anybody in this harbor asks me in four years what the office did in June I am going to be able to say that it lent a fender to two pieces of nobody's glass that it has no jurisdiction over whatsoever, and that is the whole of the office's June.**"

"And your page is going to have a line on it saying that."

"**My page is going to have a line on it saying that at no figure, and a man in this harbor has been alive long enough to know what a page with a line at no figure on it is, which is a thing somebody decided to do and then had to write down.**"

---

They walked out to the sluice again on the Monday at about ten, and the two books do not agree about it, and that difference is left in.

*The office's page says the twelfth of June. The yard's book says ten in the morning of the twelfth, and the office's page says at the hour, and the hour in the office's page is the hour it was written and not the hour it was done, and the yard has entered that the two of those are different hours and has not corrected the office's page and is not going to ask the office to correct it, because a thing an office has written down in a hand of its own is the office's business and a thing a yard has written down in a hand of its own is the yard's.*

**And the man who walks the rota has not been paid for five pay periods.**

Tristram said it on the Tuesday, on the boards, after the column, in about nine words, and it was the only part of the whole afternoon that anybody in that market wrote down.

"That is a man on this office's wall and he has not been paid for five pay periods and his last pay is the end of January, and the office's pay book has his name in it and the date and nothing since, and I have been his foreman's clerk in all the ways a wharfinger is a foreman's clerk, and I have not asked him for anything, and I am not going to, and I would like somebody to write down that I did not ask him and that it was not kindness, it was arithmetic, because there is nothing to ask for and asking would have been a way of saying it was his fault."

"Was it paid," Nessa Pike said, "on Monday the twelfth."

**"No. It was not paid and there is no money in this office to pay it in and there will not be one before the autumn. He was paid on the twelfth of June for the first time in five pay periods, and he was paid by a party that is not this harbor authority, and he was paid nothing at all, and I have watched him do it and I did not ask him to and he did not look at me, and I am going to stop talking about it now because I have said it and it is on your page and it is the only thing I have said today that I have not said to a room.**"

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock at about seven on the Tuesday.

**Eight marks in a bag.** Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, the three counts of that page unmoved. Nothing in a tin. **Eight lines and thirty-two marks in the wage column and the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and the fourth mark of the week of Tuesday the thirteenth went into an envelope on the bench this afternoon with the week on it in the same hand, and it is not going anywhere this week and nobody in this yard is going to carry it.**

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator with no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not reachable by the writ, and she has not been asked to be useful and is not an assistant. **Eight shillings and tenpence to Sella Brigg, undiminished, with her name and a figure and a date on a market wall since the seventh of June and four hundred feet of her own warp still on the bottom of this harbor.** Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, paid in April, and the same again owing since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and not argued away.

**The cistern charged on the rota on the twenty-ninth of May, the fourth of June and the twelfth of June, and not on the Thursday the nineteenth, and the reason that Thursday is not going to be walked is that there is nothing in that bag to walk it with and the yard has not undertaken it and is not going to.** The ninth seam out, the north berth out, the third lay full, the tide gang four men short, the fire party's hose dead, the light not on, the repair account empty since the middle of February and not going to be filled, no boat of this office's own since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April, the *Kittiwake* on the hard, the hard at Ashtide, nineteen winters, the *Sea Wren* at the pile, eleven copies and four unreached holders not asked, a man of the third lay, nine winters, not found.

A lump of green glass in a bucket and a piece of worked glass of a foot and a half in a crate, about nine feet apart on the crown of this mole above the third seam's mark, under two tarpaulins, one of them now weighted with a fender and forty feet of the harbor authority's own mooring rope, lent at nothing, entered at nothing.

**A mole-head light that is not on, and a repair account that is empty, and a mooring out of service with no end of it entered, and a north berth out, and a third lay full, and forty feet of this office's own marline in the sea, and a cistern behind an engine house that is charged three times this month by people nobody pays. That is the whole of what the town of Morrow Quay has done in June and none of it was fixed and all of it is a smaller thing than it was in February, and a wharfinger read it out in a market on the thirteenth of June to nine people and asked for nothing.**

**And on Wednesday the fourteenth of June a man is going to come up this hill with a paper that is not from an office and not from a court and is not from the Crown, and he has two boats and a following and he has read a fisher's document and a shop's refusal and a court's form, and he has put them together correctly, and he wants the right to use a road.**
