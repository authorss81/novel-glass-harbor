# Chapter 139

*What She Said Before Anybody Asked, and the Calipers She Took With Her*

On Sunday the twenty-third of July, at about eleven in the morning, at the top of the mole's steps, with the tide running and about nine people on the crown and nobody in the shed, Mara Venn said the thing that ends her time on this crew, in her own words, before anybody asked her for it.

"**I am going to say it once and I am not going to be asked and I would like it written down while I am saying it and not after, and I have brought the paper with me.**"

"**Then say it,**" said Calder Marr, "**and we will write it and read it back and there is no office in this and no seal in it and I have got nothing to give you for it except the reading back.**"

"**Then listen to the first half before the second half, because everybody who has ever listened to me has listened to the second half first, and the second half is the one with the money in it and it is the one that goes in a book and it is not the one that matters this morning.**

"**A yard that cannot pay me asks me whether I can be useful.**

"**I have been useful four times since the twenty-seventh of January. Not asked once. Not paid once. The fifth time, I was not asked and I was not paid, and I did the work, and I am not going to do that again.**

"**I know how that sounds coming from a woman who read her own figure at the head of a dock last week and asked for nothing. It is not a complaint and it is not a plea. It is a price. Six marks and two days is my price and it is the same on every coast and it does not come down, and this yard has four marks in a bag and a note at thirty-one, and I have been the reason both of those figures are what they are since the eighth of May and I would like that written down as a fact about me and not as a complaint about anybody.**

"**And I am not going to be useful again. Not at nothing. A trade that says yes to nothing is not a trade. And I would rather be owed at the rate I am owed than be had at the rate I am had, and I have been had since January and I did not know that was the word for it until this morning.**"

She had the paper out. She had written it herself, in her own hand, the night before, in a room above the chandler's, and she gave it to Calder Marr and he read it and gave it to Nessa Pike and she read it back and he read it back after her.

**"Entered," said Nessa Pike, "and it goes in the first column and not the second, because you said it out loud on a mole's steps in nine people's hearing and any of the nine can be asked about it tomorrow."**

---

**And then he told her about the books, and it took about eleven minutes, and he told her all of it, and she had already read the return that was nailed up on the wall on Saturday and she had already read what he had written on the steps in front of it.**

"**A builder's day-book of Ashtide, in the market office press, with six places in it that are the six joints and a builder's name cut into the mole head. A weir-book with a fisher's own storm in it and a repair four years after, and the third joint is a foot and four inches under that weir's own sill. A poor-book with twenty-two children put out of a village and nine of them named, and the village is not on this coast. And a tide-gang's column of figures that stops, and after it a sand lay of this coast that is not on any chart in this harbor.**"

Mara Venn stood on the top of those steps with her book under her arm and looked at the water for a while.

"**That is the best thing anybody has found out in this harbor in five months,**" she said, "**and it took a man with a press four weeks to get less than that out of anybody, and he did it with a bar of gravel, and you are right about him, and I have read his sheet and he is a careful printer.**

"**And it is worth nothing to me.**

"**Not because it is thin. Because I am not being paid for it, and a thing a person is given because she is owed money is not a gift and not a trade and not a finding, it is a thing a yard does to make itself feel better at a cost of nothing, and I have been that twice in nine years and I did not enjoy either.**

"**And you did not do it to make yourself feel better. You did it because you have four marks and a wall and a man in a city who prints what he is given, and I want that said, because I was the one who said on Saturday at the head of the dock that you were a man who has run out of money and a man who has not, and I have had four days to think about that and I am not taking it back and I am not putting it the other way either.**"

"**Then we are not agreed,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No. And I want it in a market, not a shed, and I have already said most of it in a market on Saturday and it is on a wall and I would like it to stay there.**"

---

**And that is the shape of the disagreement, and it has not been repaired and it is not going to be, and this party has entered it whole, and neither half of it was settled by anything anybody handed to anybody.**

"**Say ours, then,**" said Nessa Pike, "**because he has said hers twice and he has not said ours once, and it goes on the wall on the same Saturday with hers on it, and a sheet with one side of it on it is a sheet with a party's opinion on it.**"

Calder Marr stood on the top of those steps for a while with the water going off.

"**Ours is this. Nine places is nine places. A builder who wrote down where he worked because he was paid by the foot is a builder doing his trade and not a man leaving a message, and if the six joints are in his day-book then the joints and the face were made in the same work, and a mole face is the one thing in this harbor that everybody agrees to argue about and nobody agrees to be wrong about.**

"**And ours is the half of it that she has not said and I am going to say now because she is going out of this crew at noon and I am not going to be the only one who says a thing out loud today.**

"**A conservator I cannot pay is a conservator I have to keep asking. And asking her to be useful is asking her to be cheap. And I have been doing the second thing since the twenty-seventh of January and calling it the first, and she has let me do it, and the reason she has let me do it is that she would rather be useful than be forty marks in front of a man who cannot pay her, and that was a kindness and I have spent eight months taking it and calling it a trade.**

"**So the sentence is: we will keep asking. We will not ask her to be useful.**"

---

On Monday the twenty-fourth of July the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was twelve Mondays, and there was no sitting and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it.

**And the cistern was charged for the twelfth time, on the rota, at no figure, and the gate took three of them from ten until about two, and the cistern has held since the nineteenth of June.**

**And she was not on that mole and she was not in that shed and she was not in a boat, and the two of them looked for her once, on the crown, at about eleven, and she was not there.**

The brass calipers were gone off the bench. They were hers, she had bought them herself out of her own apprenticeship money in her second year, and she had said once in a room in this harbor in the spring that she would not be parted from them for a boat, and she was not parted from them on Monday.

*That on Sunday the twenty-third of July instant Mara Venn, thirty, of no office and of no party, stated at the top of this mole's steps in about nine people's hearing that she is not going to be useful to this party at nothing again, and the party has entered the whole of it in the first column in her own hand and read it back, and the party has entered that it does not dispute one word of it and that the reason it does not dispute it is that she is right; and that the party will keep asking her and will not ask her to be useful, and that those are two different things and the party has been doing the second one since the twenty-seventh of January while believing it was doing the first; **and that her finding of the fifteenth and sixteenth of July stands in this book, unsigned at her own request, with the reason against it, and that the party may use it and cannot check it and cannot pay for it and cannot get it done again, and that in about four years somebody is going to come up this hill and ask how a thing four fathoms under a mole is known, and the answer will be that a woman of thirty with no office looked at it for two days and would not be paid, and that there is one such answer and it is gone out of this harbor on the ordinary post and it is not coming back for us.***

---

**And on the Monday afternoon she was at the market office door with her book under her arm, in the open, in about nine people, and she did not come up the hill, and one of the two of them went down and spoke to her at the foot of the steps for about four minutes and came back up.**

"**She is going to be in there on Friday morning,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and on the Tuesday after that, and on every morning that office is open to anybody, because she is a person who uses it, not because we arranged it.**

"**And if anybody in this harbor wants to find a conservator of no office in Morrow Quay, that is where she is, on a Friday, at about ten, and it is not our office and it is not a favor and it is not a counter and there is nobody behind it. That is the arrangement now and we did not make it and I am not going to be thanked for it and I would like it entered that we did not make it.**"

---

**And the money on Monday the twenty-fourth, read out at the head of the dock in about nine people, is short and it is the whole of the money of this yard since the twenty-first.**

**Four marks in a bag and nothing has come in or gone out since Friday and nothing will before the twenty-eighth.** Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, the three counts of that page at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and unmoved, and five marks of compressor's gland on it and not paid and of no use, because the thing after the gland is the hose. **Thirteen lines and fifty-two marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eight envelopes on a bench in the chest and not one of them has been anywhere, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the twenty-fifth of July.** Nothing in a tin.

Forty marks of claim standing whole. Forty marks to a conservator of no office, first after food, **unpaid since the fifteenth of October and going on being unpaid, and the reason is on the page in her own words and is that a trade that says yes to nothing is not a trade.** Eight shillings and tenpence to each of two women, in no sentence together. Eight marks and a day of a boat to a bellwright, unpaid since the twenty-third of May. No air and no hose. The ninth seam out with an end of it for the first of September. The north berth out. The third lay full. The light not on. The hard at Ashtide. Nineteen winters. Eleven copies and a printed sheet and four returns, one of them on a wall. A man of the third lay, nine winters, not found, and this yard has not gone.

**And on Tuesday the twenty-fifth the column goes to fourteen lines and fifty-six marks, and the bag is going to be nothing, and nobody in this harbor knows that yet.**
