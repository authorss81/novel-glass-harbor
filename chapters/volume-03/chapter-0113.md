# Chapter 113

*Two of the Three*

They did it at the top of the mole's steps on the Wednesday the thirty-first of May, in daylight, with the wind in the north and about two hours of it, and the book open on the flat board with two battens under it, and Calder Marr's own hand on the left-hand page and a crowd of nobody at all, because this was not a read-back and there was no man in this harbor who could check a figure on a board.

*That on Wednesday the thirty-first of May instant this party names three recoveries, and costs each of them at this yard's own rate, and states plainly that it can do one of the three and cannot do two of them, and that the one it can do is the smallest of the three.*

**The first is a herring boat's own lost ground tackle on her own account.** It is the *Anna Ray*'s, of the second lay, and it is a ring and a shackle and a swivel and about four feet of her own warp come on the ring, lying at the second seam on this mole's own face landward of the third seam's mark, and nobody in this harbor has put a claim on the face of a mole landward of the third seam's mark since before any of the men standing on these steps were born. It is her own account and it is not being claimed and nothing under the bedded material is to be touched.

*Gross on the board, at this yard's own rate for a day's work at a seam on this mole, one man in the water and a tender on the lead line, sixteen. Off the top: the eighth, two. The share, two. Out of this yard's own book: the office's pot of air at this yard's own two-mark rate, filled on the office's compressor and carried on the office's hose, two; rope, one; a hired hand, three; the victuals of the day, two. That is twelve out of sixteen and the yard's is four, and the four is this yard's whole margin on a day at a seam on this mole, and the yard has never argued the eighth or the share and is not going to start on a Wednesday.*

**The second is four hundred feet of Sella Brigg's own warp.** It came off the second lay's wharf of the third lay on the sixth of February with the fire having been free, and it is lying along that wall under about six feet, and it is her own, and it is hers alone, and it has been at the bottom of this harbor for a hundred and fifteen days, and she has not asked anybody for it.

*Gross, a whole boat's day, and there is no hand lead on that ground that anybody can swear to and there is no pump on those carts and there is no hose in this harbor longer than about nine fathoms, twenty-six at this yard's own rate, the eighth two, the share two, and the yard's own column of it twelve; and that is one day, and it is not a one-day job, and the yard has entered that it is not a one-day job.*

**The third is about nine feet of this mole's own mooring chain and a swivel, off the toe between the third and fourth seams, in the same rubble the lump of green glass came out of at twenty-six minutes past eleven on the twenty-eighth of April.**

*Gross, a day and a chain, nine at this yard's own rate, and the figure is not the difficulty.*

**"The figure is not the difficulty," Calder Marr said. "The difficulty is that we cannot get a person, a boat or a lead on that ground. That is the toe and the inner face from the third seam to the eleventh, and that is the first clause of a notice this yard has not argued and is not going to argue, and this office's own practice is to treat the whole of the mole from the third to the eleventh as inside that clause, because a conservative reading of a notice is not a narrow one. And there are eight of that reeve's own acknowledgements in that office's ledger and both of the two this year are spent, the seventh on the sixth of May and the eighth on the seventeenth. A ninth could not be asked for before Monday the twenty-second and the answer on the Saturday was that there is no instrument of that office in this harbor that will do it without him. And this party is not going to ask a reeve for a ninth for nine feet of chain.**

"**And the second of those two is not for us at all. It is the harbor authority's own claim window for Monday the twelfth of June, on its own account, with its clerk and two men of its tide gang, to go out and write down in its own book what that notice has cost it, and no person of this yard is to be on that ground, and is not to be asked for, and would be refused.** So the chain is not available to this yard and it is not going to be available to this yard in a year either, and it costs nothing today and it costs everything in a year, and both of those go in the book with both of them in it."

Nobody wrote anything down for a while, and then Coll Ferrow said it from four steps down.

**"You have taken the cheapest of the three."**

He was thirty-one and he was of the *Kestrel's Sister* of the second lay, and he had a bad wheel in that boat and had not been out of this harbor in eleven days, and he had come up the steps to look at the water and had stayed, and nobody had asked him to.

"Yes," Calder Marr said.

"**And I am not saying it to be cruel, because I have a wheel of my own and I know what a cheap Tuesday looks like.** I am saying it because it is true and somebody in this harbor is going to be asked about it in a market in about three weeks and it had better be said first by the yard. You have a chain you cannot get at and a warp you cannot pay for, and you have gone and picked a ring and a shackle off the second seam, and the ring and the shackle is the one that pays a man a day rate of four marks and eats twelve of it going out."

"I have said out loud that it is the smallest of the three."

"I heard you. **I am saying the other half, which is that a choice that pays nothing is still a choice, and this yard has not had one since the first of February.** My boat has a wheel that has been bad for eleven days and I have had no day since the seventeenth of May, and there is a yard up this hill that took one of the three and said why and gave the other two back, and I would rather that than a yard that took all three and could not pay for the end of any of them." He shifted his weight. "There are three years of a harbor authority's accounts in the reeve's office on the west basin, and you can go and read them, and every line in them that says a crew did a thing because it was good at it has a number under it."

---

**Sella Brigg came up the steps on the Thursday the first of June at about ten in the morning and Calder Marr read the whole of the second of the three out to her in his own words, both columns, at the top of the steps, in daylight, with the wind in the north.**

She listened to all of it. She had a hand that is round and fair and she had written out the ninth thing on a market wall on the second of May in an hour and a half at her own table and would not be paid for it, and she did not interrupt him once.

"Twenty-six," she said. "And the yard's is twelve."

"Twelve, out of nine in a bag."

"Then no." She said it the way a woman says a thing she has already finished thinking about. "**And I am going to say the rest of it so that nobody in this yard has to be careful with me. It is mine and it is at the bottom of this harbor and it has been at the bottom of this harbor since the sixth of February, and I have not asked anybody to have it up, and I am not asking now that you have told me what it costs, and if you had told me it cost four marks I would still have said no, because I have a warp and a hand and a place to put a warp and I am not going to be a woman who has her rope brought up by a crew that owes her money.**

"And I am not going to say what I have heard said about the eight shillings and tenpence. I know what it is. **You are all thinking about it and I can see you doing it and I want it entered that I can see you doing it, because in four years there is going to be somebody who says this yard could have paid me and did not, and the answer is that I had my money in a bag on a mole's steps and I said no, and the yard did not use it.**"

Nessa Pike said, "It goes in the first column, in your own words, and you can read it back or not read it back, and we are not going to keep a column on what you would have said."

"I will not read it back," Sella Brigg said. "I have been in this harbor forty-one years and I have never once read back a thing another person wrote about me, and I am not starting in June. Write it and put my name to it and let the man who is not in the yard read it when he is in the yard again."

And Calder Marr did not use it, and the not-using was visible, and nobody in that yard pointed at it, and it went in the first column in her own words with her name against it.

---

**A berth was offered on the Thursday and it was not taken up by anybody in that shed.**

A skipper of the third lay came up the hill at about two in the afternoon with a thing in his hand and put it on the bench, and it was a berth for the month of June for one man in a boat of the second lay, and the boat was in and out of this harbor on the herring and there was a bunk and a fish market at the end of a day and no more money in it than a fish market gives anybody.

Nessa Pike asked the three questions she always asked, which were how much, when, and who else is in it, and the man answered all three, and then she said the thing that closed it.

"**We are not going to be the yard that fills a man's berth for him and then holds it.** If we took that, and I am not going to, it would be a wage in another man's boat with another man's discipline and this yard's book underneath it, and the first Tuesday it went wrong this yard would be the one they came and found, and the book would say nothing about where the man was on the Thursday."

"So it is not for one of yours," the man said.

"It is not for one of mine, and it is not for yours either, probably, and I have said so in front of the man who wrote the three, and if a man in this harbor wants it he can come up this hill and ask for it and we will tell him what a berth in June is and what a wage of four marks a week is and they are not the same money and I have never once pretended they were."

Nobody in the shed took it up, and the man took his bit of paper back and put it in his coat and said that he had asked five houses before this one, which was the truth, and went down the hill.

---

The two columns were read out at the head of the dock on the Thursday evening at about six.

**Nine marks in a bag, and nothing has moved since Tuesday.** Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, the three counts of its weeks unmoved. Nothing in a tin. Six lines and twenty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, and an envelope on a bench.

**The notice of the thirty-first of October is seven months old this morning.** It is not lifted and it is not narrowed and it is not argued, and there are eight acknowledgements in that office's ledger and both of the ones this year are spent and the eighth of them is the harbor authority's own claim window for Monday the twelfth of June at the top of the flood, on the mole's toe and inner face between the third and the ninth seams, with its clerk and two men of its tide gang, and no person of this yard upon it and not to be asked for.

**Three recoveries named and one of them taken, and the one that was taken is the smallest of the three, and the yard said that out loud on a mole's steps on the Wednesday morning before anybody could say it for it, and a man of thirty-one of the second lay said it after, and the yard did not deny it.**

**And a woman with four hundred feet of her own warp at the bottom of this harbor was told what it costs and said no in her own voice, and is owed eight shillings and tenpence undiminished, and nobody in this yard used that against her, and she asked to see it written down that she could see them not using it, and it is written down.**

And the first of June was four weeks and a day off the third of May, and the yard is two and a half and has nothing coming and a pot of air to buy and a rope to buy and three days to fill both of them, and the first of them is Friday the second.
