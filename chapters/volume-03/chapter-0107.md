# Chapter 107

*The Answer to the Seventeenth of January*

"There is one thing I want said out loud on the way up and I want it said by you and not by me," Nessa Pike said, at the head of the dock on Tuesday the sixteenth. "Whatever is in that envelope this yard is not working today. There is a pump coming back on Friday that wants stripping before anybody puts a gland in it, and a ring and a shackle and four feet of a herring boat's own warp that want laying out on the mole before the weather turns, and both of those want two men and a daylight. We have two and a half, and the half has said out loud that she is not going in the water."

"Then what are we doing with the day."

"We are going up to the west basin and letting Ors Kest read four pages at us," she said, "and I would rather lose it with a reason on the page than lose it in a shed wondering about it. Put it in this morning's line with the reason against it, and the reason is that a yard with a paper does not get to choose which days of the paper it can afford."

"Then you write it."

"I will write it, and I am right, and you are not going to thank me for it and I am not going to be thanked for it." She put the pencil down on the bench. "**And I want the second thing said in the room, when he has read the grounds, and I want you to say it and not me: that this yard is not going to argue one of them, and that it is going to say out loud, in front of the clerk, that it is not arguing one of them, so that the clerk has got the other half of that sentence from this yard's own mouth and not out of a file in a city. That is worth a day to me. Whether it is worth a day to you is a Tuesday's question and not a Thursday's.**"

The refusal was four pages and it was signed on Friday the twelfth of May and served on Tuesday the sixteenth, and it was better written than the six-clause one that came back on the twelfth of January, exactly as the reeve had said in a barge in January that it would be, and it was better written because the plan it answers has eight clauses where the first one had six, and both of them were right, and only one of them was in the room.

Calder Marr was in the reeve's office on the west basin at ten in the morning with Elias Voke of the *Anna Ray* sitting beside him, because Voke had carried the plan up the coast in January and had carried the statement of the capacity of this harbor to moor the fleet out of the same hand in the winter, and a man who has carried a piece of paper for four months has a right to be in the room when it comes back.

The boy was not sent and was not asked for and was not in the building.

**The four grounds, read out by the clerk in a voice trained to be bored without being slow, and the yard did not argue one of them.**

*First. That the office which would have to countersign the plan of the seventeenth of January is the office which served the notice of the thirty-first of October, and that this office is not the office which will say that its own notice is wrong, and that it is not to be asked to and will not try.*

*Second. That the plan proposes the person in the water, and that the third clause of that notice names Calder Marr, Nessa Pike and Bevin Rook of this yard for this office's work, and that a look under a countersignature of this office is this office's work; and that the fourth person upon the writ of the first of May is a hired man of another boat, and this office will not put a hired man's name upon a look at this mole.*

*Third. That this office has no instrument for a look at ground it has closed by its own served notice, and that it holds one entry upon that ground already, made on the third of November by the officer of this office from the steps inside the mole wall at the top of the tide in the presence of three witnesses whose names are upon it, and that this office has been asked once to strike that entry and has refused, and will not now build an instrument upon the top of an entry it has this month refused to withdraw.*

*Fourth. That the plan asks this office for a place and an hour and a keeper and a limit, and that this office can give a place and an hour and cannot give a keeper, because the three men the notice names are the yard's men, and because a keeper who is not named is a stranger's accident, and this office learned that on its own mole on the twelfth of May, in writing, from a party that was refused a day.*

He read them and then read the last line of the paper.

*That the plate at the fifth seam stands in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and that it is so entered in this office's own book with three named witnesses, and that the notice of the thirty-first is not lifted, is not narrowed, is not re-served and is not altered, and that this office has no power to lift it and is not lifting it and has never lifted it.*

**And Calder Marr said the thing Nessa Pike had told him to say in the room, in front of the clerk, and the clerk wrote it down.**

"I am not going to argue one of those four," he said. "I want that entered in your book this morning, in my own words and not in yours. **This party has been asked for a thing four times in four months and it is not arguing one of the four grounds today, and it is not going to argue them in a letter either, and if this office has a fifth reason it is welcome to serve it and this party will answer it on the day it is served.** A reeve told me in a barge in January that the answer would be no and that the reason would not be a better argument. I have believed him since the eighteenth and I am not going to waste his afternoon."

The clerk looked at Kest, and Kest nodded once, and the clerk wrote it, and read it back, and the reading back took about as long as the writing.

Nobody in that office spoke for about half a minute.

"He wrote that in January," Elias Voke said. "In a barge, on the eighteenth. He said it would be refused, and refused correctly, and that the reason was not a better argument." He turned round in the chair. "You said it, Ors. You said it in a barge and I was in the barge."

"I said it," Kest said. "And it is a better argument than the one the yard has, and I have had it since the sixth of December, and it is in a book in that shed in the yard's own hand with the yard's own reasons against it, and I would like to be asked to say why the better argument does not work, and the answer is short and it is not a matter of this office being difficult."

"Then say the short one," Nessa Pike said.

**"The office that has to countersign it is the office that has to say the notice is wrong. No man in that office is going to say it. A reeve cannot make his own office say a thing and cannot be asked to and will not try, and I have not tried in four months and I am not going to start on a Tuesday."**

He put both hands flat on the desk.

"And I will tell you the other half of it, and Calder Marr will not like it, because I have had it since the sixth of December and it is the best thing anybody in this harbor has written about this mole and it is in a shed and not in an office. A notice of the thirty-first is a paper about contagion, and the office that can judge a paper about contagion is not the office that wrote it, and there is therefore a route to that plate that does not run through this room. **And there is not, and there has not been since the third of November, and the reason there is not is that the only office in this coast that would countersign it is the one that wrote the notice.** I have known that since the sixth of December. I have had it in a book in that shed for five months and I have not put it in an office book, and a reeve who puts a dead argument in an office book in Veyr gets asked by a man with a seal about why this harbor authority thinks a notice is not a notice."

Calder Marr had known that since December too, and had never once been in a room where a man said it to his face, and he found that he could not think of a single useful thing to do with his hands.

"Then what would move it," he said.

"A change of politics," Kest said. "**In an office. On a schedule nobody in this harbor can name, and I am not going to name one, and I will tell you why I am not going to name one: the day this yard has a date is the day this yard has spent something and got nothing, and I have spent it six times.**"

**And then Kest paid for having said it, and the price was named in the room before it was paid, because that is what this office does.**

"There is one thing this office can do," he said, "and it is not on your paper and I am not asking you for it and you are not to ask me for it and I would like it written that I said you are not to ask.

"The account of what the notice of the thirty-first of October has cost this harbor authority is not in anybody's book. It is in mine, and mine is a ledger, and a ledger is not read. Four sets of moorings, bought and not recovered. Forty feet of this office's own marline lying in the sea off the north wall since the twenty-seventh of February, and there will be seventy before the summer. Fifty fathoms of the herring fleet's winter warp on a wall where ninety were to lie. A tide gang four men short from the first of May, with one of them stood off that wall from the first to the tenth to mind a cart in front of the last lay's wharf, so that the mole steps will not be cleared in June. A cistern charged on a rota on a Monday and a Thursday by a man who walks out to a sluice, and the man did not walk out on Monday, because this office cannot pay a man to walk out to a sluice. A mole-head light that is not on, because a light costs oil and this office's repair account has been empty since the middle of February. A fire party's hose laid out in eleven wet lengths on the second lay's boards and dead until the autumn. And nineteen winters of dues.

"**None of that is in any book that anybody reads. All of it is on this mole, on this office's own ground, and the only way to enter it honestly is for a man of this office to stand on that ground and write it down with his own hand while the clerk is there to witness it.**"

"And the cost of that," said Nessa Pike.

"The cost of that is a claim window, of which this office has two a month and has had two a month for nine years, and one date, and the eighth time in this harbor's ledger that this office has put a person of this office upon ground its own notice closes, at the cost of this reeve, which is mine and is never free. **It is Monday the twelfth of June, at the top of the flood, on this mole's toe and inner face between the third and the ninth seams, with the clerk and two men of the tide gang, and it is not for this yard and no person of this yard is to be on that ground and is not to be asked to be and would be refused if he were.** No party has asked for it. The yard has not asked for it. The yard is not to be told that it is a step towards anything."

"And the other half of it," Elias Voke said, from the chair. "The half that is not the ground."

---

**"The half that is not the ground is me, and I am going to say it in a room with a clerk in it, and Mr Voke is going to hear it, because he is owed nineteen winters of this office's money and I have been the man who has not paid it for nine years and I would rather he heard it from me than find it in a file.**

"That account goes up the coast in a document, and it goes up in the same file as Mr Voke's statement of the capacity of this harbor to moor the fleet, which has been on its way since the first of March, and a hard at Ashtide that this office has not answered since the ninth of November, which is the same office or a desk in the same building. **So the first thing a man in a coast office opens in May is this harbor's own account of what it has spent on a notice, and every line of it is money, and the last line of it is a man who has owed me nineteen winters and cannot pay me because the money went on four sets of moorings and a season of warp in the fortnight before the first of March, on my account.**

"The dues are not going to be settled by this. **They are going to be harder to dispute and not easier, and that is the price of this office being honest in public for the first time in nine years, and it falls on Mr Voke and on the herring fleet and not on this yard, and I am telling him so in a room with a clerk in it so that he can say whatever he wants to say about it afterwards.**"

Elias Voke is fifty-one and he has been in this harbor since he was a boy and he did not shout.

"I carried a piece of paper up this coast in October for nothing," he said, "and another one up it in January for nothing, and I have carried a third for the first of March and I do not know yet if it is still on the road. **I am not going to stop carrying them because the answer is no. A man who carries other men's paper up a coast for nothing is not a man who stops carrying it because the answer is no — he is a man who finds out what carrying it costs, and I have found that out, and it is a mooring, and a berth, and nineteen winters, and a hard at Ashtide that nobody will answer, and a clerk in Veyr who is going to read this office's own account before he reads mine.**

"Put my name on it. Put the name of my boat on it and the nineteen winters in my own hand, because I have said the cause out loud twice in this harbor and I am not going to have it read in a file in a city by a man who never met me." He looked at the reeve. "And I will say one thing that is mine and not yours, and I will say it once. **You did not have to do that. Nobody made you do that, and the wall behind your bench came down this morning and you could have put nothing on it, and you put an eighth thing in a ledger instead.**"

"I did not have to," Kest said. "And the clerk will tell you what my office has spent and I cannot enter your nineteen winters, Mr Voke, and I have not entered them and I am not going to, and that is a different thing and it is the only thing I have left."

---

Then the eighth of his own acknowledgements went into the ledger on Wednesday the seventeenth of May, in his own hand, unasked, at the cost of the eighth claim window this office has ever spent and nineteen winters of a herring fleet's dues that are not going to be easier to dispute, and the seventh named the six before it, and the clerk read it back and did not shorten it.

*That on Monday the twelfth of June instant, at the top of the flood, on the mole's toe and inner face between the third and the ninth seams, the officer of this office with the clerk and two men of this office's tide gang will enter in this office's own book, with witnesses named, what the notice of the thirty-first of October has cost this harbor authority; that no person of the yard of Calder Marr is upon that ground and is not to be asked for and would be refused; that the notice of the thirty-first is not lifted, is not narrowed and is not altered; that this office has no power to lift it; that this is the eighth of this office's entries of that kind, the seventh being of the sixth of May, the sixth of the twenty-seventh of April, the fifth of the seventh of April, and the first four being of the eighth, twelfth, seventeenth and nineteenth of January; that the sixth and the seventh were each asked for by a party and this eighth was asked for by nobody; and that the cost of all eight is this reeve's and is never free.*

The clerk entered it and read it back. Kest read it himself, after his own clerk, which he does not do.

The plate at the fifth seam stands in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and nobody in this harbor has said one word about it since the seventeenth of May, and the notice of the thirty-first of October is six months and sixteen days old and is not lifted and is not going to be.

Nessa Pike was right about one thing in that room. It cost this yard the whole of the sixteenth of May, a pump stripped late and a ring and a shackle and four feet of somebody's warp laid out late, and neither the reeve nor the clerk nor Elias Voke thanked her for it and she did not want to be thanked. Calder Marr was right about one thing, and the clerk wrote it down in the office's own book, and it was the other half of a sentence the yard had already said once that afternoon.
