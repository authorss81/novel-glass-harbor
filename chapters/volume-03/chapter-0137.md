# Chapter 137

*What the Fouling Took Apart, and a Word in the Book*

Nothing came in or out of that yard on Wednesday the nineteenth of July, and at about half past two in the afternoon, on the top of the flood, the third joint was scraped and the fourth joint was scraped, and by four o'clock the whole of the statement that had been made in a market at the head of this dock on Tuesday afternoon at half past three was finished.

**The six were read out in order at the boat before anybody was wet, on the eighteenth, on the nineteenth and on the twentieth, in the same words each time, and the limit was the same each time and it was his, and the word was the same and it was hers, and the stated reason went in before each of them and the clerk was not present on any of the three days and the absence is on the page three times.**

**The third joint, which is sixty-eight feet south of the second, read three inches and a quarter. The fourth, sixty-nine feet south of the third, read three inches and a quarter.**

Both men in the boat. The same rule, the same scraper, the same bar laid flat, the same state of tide, the same hand.

A quarter of an inch is new. Three inches and a quarter is not new.

---

"**I have got two readings a quarter of an inch and two readings three inches and a quarter,**" Calder Marr said, "**and the two thin ones are the two north ones, and the two thick ones are sixty-eight and a hundred and thirty-seven feet south of them, and I said on Tuesday at half past three at the head of this dock that the whole of it was made once from the north end down.**"

"**Then it was not,**" said the shipwright.

"**Then it was not, and I would like it in the book in the words I am about to use, and the words are these. Fouling goes on in order. A quarter of an inch is a fortnight. Three inches and a quarter is a hundred and forty days, which is a season, and a season is not a morning. The two north joints are the two newest and the two south ones are older, and I told eleven people in a market on Tuesday that the newest was the first, and it is the last, and I got it backwards.**"

Tam Slee had the other end of the bar and he did not take his eyes off the mole.

"**Say the next part,**" he said.

"**The next part is that it is not one time at all. A quarter, and three inches, and a quarter, is not a morning. It is at least three different days in three different seasons and I cannot tell you how many more and I am not going to guess, and there is a joint in this crevice with nine inches on it, which is the oldest thing in it, and it is in the middle, and I do not know what that means and I am not going to say.**"

Nobody said anything for about a minute, and the boat came off the face, and the tide at that face on the nineteenth of July is not good again until the middle of the next day.

---

**A boatwright of the second lay came down to the shed at about five in the afternoon because somebody he had never met asked him at the top of the mole's steps, and he came for nothing and was not paid and did not want anything and had been in this harbor forty-one years.**

He looked at the two feet of joint that was clear, for about two minutes, and put his own rule on it, and gave the same figure for the nine fastenings in about seven feet at a hand's breadth apart that the shipwright had given from a boat, and said that the spacing was not a spacing anybody in this coast works to and that he had cut fastenings for thirty years and had never seen that one, and that a man who tells you it is a hand's breadth is telling you about two men with two hands and that the figure is a thumb and a bit, and that a thumb and a bit is what you get when a man lays out by hand instead of by batten and pitch, and that men who lay out by hand are men working in a hurry or a place where there is not a batten.

"**I have not said a word about where it came from,**" he said, "**and I am not going to, and I have not said a word about who and I do not know, and I have got a boat to caulk on Tuesday and I would like to go home.**"

He went down the boards and the yard entered all of it in the first column, because a man said it in a shed and any man in this harbor can ask him about it on any day of a week and he has given his name and he will not deny it.

---

**And Edric Pringle came up the hill on the Wednesday evening, and she said the cause out loud before she said the figure, and the cause was this party.**

"**I bought a day on Monday for seventeen and I paid twelve of it at the mole and the five came up this hill, and I got half a day, and I am going to tell you why I got half a day and I am not going to be told it was the tide.**

"**The tide was there at seven in the morning and the tide was there at two in the afternoon. The boat came off that face at eleven because the man in the water could not say whether the basket had gone up four times or three, and he was in the water, and he is of your yard.**"

"**Yes.**

"**Then you will write that down in your own hand, because I am not going to have it in a market that a man's day was cut short by a tide. I would have had the same day done on the Tuesday if I had been told on the Monday that the face is only good at the top of a flood and there is one a day.**"

"**I told you that at seven in the morning on the Monday, before anybody was wet.**

"**You told me a thing about the job. You did not tell me my money.**"

Nobody improved on that.

"**And I am not going to be paid the four marks, because I have not asked you for it and I am not going to, and I am owed eight shillings and tenpence since the sixth of February and that is a different figure and I am not going to let the two of them be in one sentence in any book in this harbor, and I checked your book on Thursday last and it is right and it is going to stay right.**"

---

**And on Thursday the twentieth of July the cistern was charged for the eleventh time, on the rota, at no figure, and the gate took three of them from ten until about two, and the top of the flood at that face was at about half past two that day, and that is why the two of them did not walk the gate and do a day's reading at the same time and the yard entered the hour of the flood against its own account.**

The fifth joint, at half past two, read **nine inches**. The sixth, at the seaward end, at about four, read **three inches and a quarter**.

**And the packing in the fifth was not the color of the packing in the other five. The other five are a pale bone white where they are dry and a wet gray where they are wet. The fifth is a warm brown, and the brown goes about a quarter of the way down into it and then stops, and under the brown there is the same bone white.**

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that six joints lie in a crevice at about four fathoms on the second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark; that the fouling upon the seaward side of each of them, taken with a bar laid flat and a scraper and a rule at the top of the flood on the eighteenth, nineteenth and twentieth of July instant, the same hand and the same rule and the same bar, is a quarter of an inch, a quarter of an inch, three inches and a quarter, three inches and a quarter, nine inches, and three inches and a quarter, in that order from the root end of the crevice to the seaward end; and that the packing in the fifth of them is a different color from the packing in the other five to a depth of about a quarter of an inch and is the same color as the other five below that; and that a boatwright of the second lay, naming himself, put his own rule upon the joint on the nineteenth and gave the same figure for the fastenings as this party and said the spacing is not a spacing anybody in this coast works to.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole: that on Tuesday the eighteenth of July a person of this party said in a market that the six joints were made once, in one time, by one set of hands, from the north end downwards; and that the fouling on the nineteenth and the packing on the twentieth are against that; and that the party has entered that it is against it and does not know what the five thicknesses are and is not going to arrange them into a figure.*

---

**And at about nine o'clock on Thursday night the shed was shut and the lamp was lit and the two of them were reading the second column back, and a man of the *Grey Widow* was asleep on the net loft floor with his coat over him because it had been raining since six, and he was awake, and everybody knew he was awake, and nobody said so.**

Nessa Pike had the second column on the bench and she was reading it from the top, which is what she does, and Calder Marr was listening and not listening, and at about the middle of it Tam Slee said from the loft:

"**Then you made it up, then.**

"**I am not asking a question, I am saying a thing, because you have been talking about a second column since the eighteenth of October and I have been on three boats with you since the April and I have never once heard a man of yours say out loud what he did not know, and you said one on Tuesday in a market to about eleven people and I was one of them.**"

"**Yes.**

"**And I went down on Wednesday on my own account and I scraped two of them for you for nothing, and I am going to scrape the rest tomorrow and I am not doing it because of Tuesday.**"

He lay still on the loft floor.

"**What I am telling you,**" he said, "**is that I did not have to be told, and I have not got anywhere else to be on a Friday, and there is a difference between those two things and I would like it kept as a difference and not put in with the rest of it.**"

---

**And then Calder Marr said a thing in that shed at about a quarter past nine on the night of the twentieth of July that he has not said since the eighth of December, and it went into a column in his own left hand with a name in it, and there is a name in that line and there is no name in any of the four above it.**

"**There is a word for it,**" he said. "**A man of fifty told me it and I have got it and I have not used it since the December, and I am going to use it now, and I am going to write it down, and I am not going to be asked to take it out and I will not fight anybody who wants it out.**

"**It is called a tide-sickness. I have got it. That is the whole of what a tide-sickness is, it is a name a man of fifty uses for a thing he has had since he was thirty-one, and it is not a cause, and I am not writing a cause, and if anybody in this harbor in four years finds the word in this book and wants a cause out of it they are going to have to go and find one themselves and they are not going to find it here.**"

Nessa Pike did not move for about half a minute.

"**There is a line in the second column of the second of June,**" she said, "**in your own hand, that says there is no name in it and there is not going to be one this year.**"

"**There is.**

"**Then you are breaking your own line and you are doing it four days after you wrote a market full of people down as wrong, and I am going to read it back and I am going to read it back the way I read everything else in this book, and I am not going to do it any other way, and I would like you to know before I start that I know what it costs me and I am doing it anyway and I am not going to talk about it.**"

"**Then do it.**

"**Say the other part first, because the other part is the one that is in the room.**"

"**The other part is that you have been asking me for four days where I heard it,**" she said, "**and that on Tuesday, at about four o'clock, in a boat, I asked you a question and you answered me a quarter of a second after you answered yourself, and both answers were to do with the basket, and one of them was right and I do not know which one and I have been not knowing which one for four days and I have not said it out loud because I did not want to put it in a column without your hand on it.**"

"**Then put it in with my hand on it.**"

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, and entered in the holder's own left hand on Thursday the twentieth of July instant, and the word tide-sickness is in this line and there is no word in any of the four above it: that the holder has had it since the thirteenth of October and worse since the eighth of December, that it is a second sound under the first that arrives late, and that on a good day he can tell which sound is in the boat and on a bad day he cannot; and that on the eighteenth, nineteenth and twentieth of July instant, in the water and in a boat, in company, it was a bad day on all three; and that on the evening of the twentieth, in this shed, with the shipwright reading this column, ordinary talk in the room was going on as more than one talk at once, and the holder gave an answer to a question that had been put to him a quarter of a second after the answer that answered it, and that the shipwright was in the room and heard both and could not say which of the two he meant, and that she has said so and that she is not going to put it any other way; **and the reason this line is in this column and carries a name and the four above it do not, is that a man who can lose a quarter of a second out of an ordinary conversation in a shed is a man whose account of a seam under four fathoms of water is a thing that can be asked about, and the party has decided that it can be asked about in this book, on this page, on this date, by anybody who comes up this hill, and that is a different thing from being asked about it by a man with a seal, and it is the only difference there is, and the party would rather have it out in the open where it can be argued with than in a man's head where it can only be guessed at.** And the reason there is no cause in this line is that there is not one.*

**Nessa Pike read the whole of it back from the top to the bottom, in the shed, at about ten at night, in the rain, with a man of the *Grey Widow* lying awake on a net loft floor and not moving, and Calder Marr read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed to read it back after that.**

**And the count of the four above it is still four, and the four above it have no name in them, and this party has not struck them out and is not going to, and a request to strike a line out of a column is refused.**

**And one mark in a bag, and the next of them is four marks on Tuesday the twenty-fifth of July, and there is a day booked at the second seam for the Friday and nobody in the water, and on the Saturday morning something is going on the nails of the market wall over the top of a man's printed sheet, and there is no office in this harbor that has paid this yard a mark since the eighth of May and none that is going to.**
