# Chapter 455

*Sunday the Seventeenth Day of October, the Seventy-Sixth Time That Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Came Down Bare and Went Up at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Nobody Put It Back, Nine Men on That Quay and About Thirty of That Harbor Going Past, a Disagreement Said Out Loud in the Open in Front of All of Them About Whether a Sheet of About Four Feet May Go Down the Coast in a Man's Inside Pocket, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying No Out Loud and Saying Why Out Loud, Four Things Said About It by Four People and Three of Them Beginning and Stopping, a Man of About Thirty-Four Saying He Did Not Know and That Not Knowing Is a Thing a Man Can Put in a Minute, Nothing Resolved, Nobody Thanked, Nobody Asking Anybody to Take a Thing Back, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Sunday the seventeenth day of October, on that quay, in the open, and in that shed.

**The said Sunday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare at about eight in the morning for the seventy-sixth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back, and has not since the twelfth of August and is not going to. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. Mara Venn was on that quay at about eleven in the morning and was not thanked and was not asked one thing about what she said there, and she is of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and did not hear any of it, being about a quarter of a mile off and the wind on that quay. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-three days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-five days. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are the last day of a fourteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them and on the morrow they are a fifteenth week and nobody will say one word about them then either. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-three days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-seven days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-five days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-second day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-seventh day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-fourth day.**

---

There were nine men on that quay at about eleven in the morning on the seventeenth of October and about thirty other people going past, and the holder of that writ said the thing out loud, which is what he does, and then a woman said no out loud, which is also what she does.

"I am going to carry the sheet," he said. "The sheet of about four feet. It is in three pockets in this harbor and two of them are in this room and it is going in my inside pocket down that coast road and it is going to be in the bell with me at the trench."

Nobody on that quay knew what the sheet was. About four of the thirty knew. One of the four was Mara Venn.

She did not lower her voice and she did not take a step towards him and she said it in the plainest words she had, and afterwards three people in that harbor said that they had heard her say it and two of them could not have said afterwards what the argument was about.

"No. And I will tell you why in front of all of these people so that you cannot go and find a quiet room later. **A route is a way boats go. A description is not a way anything goes.** That sheet says on its own face that it is a description of a made thing and not the thing, and it says it at the top in the first line, and if a piece of paper can stand in for the thing it describes then every one of the four heads on that wall upstairs has a hole in it, and the hole is the size of a boat, and it is the size of a boat because that is what you are proposing to put in it."

He said that the account itself authorizes a limited use of the route by four people whose names are on it, and he read the fifth of the nine boundaries out of the copy in a copyist's hand that had been out of that building for nine days, and he read it in a voice, and about four men on that quay could not read and heard it.

"Then read the other half of it," she said. "It says a route and not a scene. You are not going down there to find a route. There are no routes in that trench. There is a hull that somebody stored under a city because they did not want it kept, and you are going to go and look at a hull. That is a scene, and the sheet does not carry a scene, and the sheet says so in the same breath in which it gives you the route, and you have read the half that lets you and not the half that stops you."

"Then I am not reading the sheet down there. I am putting it against a thing and finding out whether a thing answers a description of it, and if a hundred years from now a man with a paper and a bell and no time at all can do that, then this whole coast has spent years building a thing that a man with a pencil can open, and I would rather find that out on a shelf with nine men than read about it in a room upstairs."

"That is the first true thing anybody has said on this quay today and it does not make it true," she said.

---

**Four people said one thing each about it and the four things were in four mouths.**

A man of about forty-four with a boat of about nine tons of his own who crews in nobody else's and holds no office said that a man could not take a paper into a bell to make a thing answer, and that if it answered then the answer would be to the paper, and that a record of an answer to a paper is a record of a man holding a paper, and that is worth nothing and is not worth writing down.

A woman of about thirty-three who had put four marks into the sheet on the Wednesday and who crews a boat of about eleven tons said that if nobody ever found out whether a paper could do it then in fifty years somebody would find out, and that she would rather it was nine men with a writ of their own naming on it than a man in a coat with no name on anything.

A man of about thirty-one, who was one of the four in the bell on the eighth of October past and who came up with an aftertaste and with one afternoon in June he can put a name to and nothing else, said that he had been in a bell on that line and that a thing down there that answered a description would not be a thing anybody had ever put in a bell before, and that he did not know what that meant and that he was not going to be the man who guessed.

**Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, who keeps the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book and has never shown it to anybody, said he did not know, and then said the second thing, which is that not knowing is a thing a man can put in a minute, and that it is a better thing to put in a minute than a thing a man made up at a table with nine men looking at him.**

Nessa Pike wrote the whole of it into a minute on a sheet of good paper that cost a penny and came out of the holder of his own coat, and the minute says that the question was asked on the quay at about a quarter past eleven in the morning of the seventeenth of October, that four people said one thing each and four of the four said it in their own mouths, and that nobody answered it, and that the holder of that writ said he was going to carry the sheet whatever anybody said, and that the woman of thirty of no office said no, and that both of those things are in the minute because the minute is a document and a document says what happened and not what a thing is to be done about.

**Nobody asked anybody to take anything back. Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody apologised for the disagreement on that quay on that morning, and neither of them said anything to each other after about ten to twelve, and what they said to each other after that was about a pump hand and a figure for a compressor.**

---

**A DISAGREEMENT WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN ON THAT QUAY IN FRONT OF NINE MEN AND ABOUT THIRTY PASSING PEOPLE AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND FOUR PEOPLE SAID ONE THING EACH AND NOBODY ANSWERED ANY OF THEM, AND IT IS IN A MINUTE AS ASKED AND NOT ANSWERED, AND THE FOUR HEADS ON THE WALL IN THE ROOM OVER THAT FISH MARKET WERE NOT CHANGED BY ANY OF IT, AND THE SHEET OF ABOUT FOUR FEET IS STILL GOING DOWN THAT COAST IN AN INSIDE POCKET, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO PEOPLE WHO SAID THE LOUDEST THING HAS ASKED THE OTHER FOR ANYTHING, AND NOBODY ON THAT QUAY WAS TOLD WHICH OF THEM WAS RIGHT.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the seventeenth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-sixth time at about eight in the morning and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a disagreement was said out loud on that quay at about a quarter past eleven in the morning in front of nine men and about thirty people going past, being whether a sheet of about four feet may be carried down that coast in an inside pocket, and that four people said one thing each about it in four mouths and that nobody answered any of them; and that the said day was the last day of a fourteenth week of the three nots standing against the eighth of June past and that no word was said about them in that week by anybody in that crew.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the argument was said out loud on a quay with thirty people going past and not in a room is in the holder's own mouth and is that he did not want to be able to go and find a quiet room later, and that the woman of thirty said the same thing about her own side of it, and that both of them wanted the same thing and only one of them knew it; and that the reason the fifth of the nine boundaries was read out on a quay to about four men who cannot read is that a boundary that is only read to people who can read it is not a boundary, and that four men heard it and none of them was asked whether they agreed; and that the reason a man of about thirty-one who has been in a bell on that line said he did not know is in his own mouth and is that he came up on the eighth of October with one afternoon in June he can put a name to and nothing else, and that a man who cannot find his own way back to a Tuesday afternoon is not the man to guess what a thing in a trench is answering; and that the reason Ollum Trevask said he did not know and then said that not knowing is a thing a man can put in a minute is in his own mouth, and that a minute is a document and a document says what happened and not what a thing is to be done about, and that this is the second time in nine days that a thing has been entered in a minute in this harbor because a man did not know; and that the reason nobody was asked to take anything back is that a thing said in the open in front of thirty people cannot be quietly withdrawn afterwards without somebody noticing, and that this is a method and not a rule and is not written on either sheet; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Sunday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
