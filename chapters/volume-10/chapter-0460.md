# Chapter 460

*Friday the Twenty-Second Day of October, a Man of Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud in His Own Mouth at About Nine in the Morning, Before He Was Asked, What He Did With a Sheet of About Four Feet and What Answered It at About Twenty-Two Fathoms, a Boundary Written Down and Printed and Not Private, a Fitting-Out Sheet Read Back in Full on the Deck of a Boat of About Nineteen Tons With Both Totals on the Face of the Reading and No Count of Persons on It, the Sixth Day of a Master's Hire, Being Two Hundred and Fifty Pence, Paid in Coin on the Deck at About Four in the Afternoon and Nobody Thanked, a Leaf Read Back at About Ten Past Five in the Evening With Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading and the Fourth Column Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Five Where It Stood and Taking No Line, Three Statements in Three Voices About the Water and the Paper and This Trip, the Whole of That Harbor's Standing Condition Once and Nowhere Else, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty, and a Man of About Twenty-Nine Whose Left Side Is About Nine Feet Off and a Sheet With Five Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence Left on It*

Friday the twenty-second day of October, on the deck of a boat of about nineteen tons in about nine fathoms, and on that boat two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the whole of the ten days is one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence in and one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence out, being eleven marks, seven shillings and fourpence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight is twenty-eight.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on the deck of that boat at about nine in the morning and was not thanked. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and got up at four and went home, which he has done five times in ninety-five days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-eight days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and the bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is not asked about him. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fifteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column with a name beside it that is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-eight days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-fourth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-two days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-seventh day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-second day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-ninth day. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat could not hear out of the left side of his head on the twentieth, on the twenty-first or on the twenty-second, and has not been asked about it and did not ask.**

---

He told her at about nine in the morning on the said day, on the deck, with eight other men about forty yards off and one of them looking, and he told her before she asked, which is a thing he started doing in July past and has not stopped doing.

"I put the sheet in my pocket on the Friday. I did not ask you and I did not tell you and it is not in either column. I put it against the mouth of that trench at about half past ten yesterday morning and I felt a thing answer it, and Amos went down into the same water an hour after me and felt the same thing, and he came up and told me he had felt it before I said one word to him, and you had not been told that I had taken it down there at all, and that is on me and not on you and I am not going to stand here and let anybody make it a thing about a woman who said no on a quay on Sunday and was right."

Mara Venn took the oilcloth fold out of his coat pocket herself, unrolled it on a hatch cover, and read the head of it, which is the line that says it is a description of a made thing and not the thing it describes, and then she rolled it up and gave it back.

"Write this down," she said, "and put it on a sheet and not in a pocket, and read it out in that room over the fish market with a name and an hour under it, and nobody is to be asked to agree with it. **The echo may inform a public record, and it may not decide anything this party does.** That is all of it. It may be printed, it may be argued with, and a man in a room upstairs may say it is wrong, and if it decides one thing about where a boat goes tomorrow then we have done the thing the Crown does and we have done it with a better hand and a smaller number of boats."

Nobody said that Calder Marr had been right to put the paper in his pocket, and nobody said he had been wrong to put it there, and he did not say it himself, and it is not in either column of that leaf on the said day, and the reason is that a man who is going to say to himself in a minute that he was wrong about a thing is not a man who can be checked with a pencil, and the yard keeps the two things apart.

---

**At about four o'clock in the afternoon the master of that boat was paid the rest of his hire in full, in coin, on the deck, to the penny, and he counted it twice, which is his business and not an insult, and put it in his own bag, and did not say thank you, and said one thing instead.**

"Six days ago you told me you did not know what was on that shelf. I want it in the book that you told me the truth about it on the Saturday, and I want my boy's wages counted twice, because he has not been paid since the summer and I have been ashamed of it since the summer and he is owed it and it is mine to pay and not yours."

**The sixth day of that hire, being two hundred and fifty pence, went onto the fitting-out sheet at about four o'clock in the afternoon on the said day and was paid to him in coin on the deck, and he counted that one twice as well, and then Nessa Pike wrote the payment in the minute and read it back to him and he did not ask to read it.**

---

**And at about half past four in the afternoon, before that minute was shut, the master said the other half of what he had said at that table on the Saturday before, which is that he was going home on that tide. His lease was out at six days and had been paid to the penny, and his boat was his own again, and he was not going to sit on that shelf and wait for a morning.**

He said that the party was going up that coast for two days and that he was going as far as that water goes and no further, and that he would carry them, and that he would not take a figure for it and was not going to be thanked for it.

**Nobody offered him anything, no line was opened on that sheet for it and none is going to be, and the reason is in the second column and is that a man carrying people he is not paid for is a thing a stranger with a pencil cannot check, and that this yard has kept such things out of a column for as long as that leaf has been open and is not opening one at half past nine in the evening. The man who keeps the leaf said one sentence about it, which was that the master had been told the truth twice and had not been thanked for it once, and the master said that was right and went below and did not come up again that night.**

---

**At about ten past five in the evening on the said day the fitting-out sheet was read back in full, on the after deck, in the open, every line with an hour against it, and the head of it was read first and the twelve lines that went out were read with the days they went out on, and the two refusals were read at the same size as the figures, and the sheet was read twice because about four men of that party cannot read.**

Seven thousand seven hundred and sixty pence came in and seven thousand two hundred and two pence went out and five hundred and fifty-eight pence is on that sheet at the close of the day, being three marks, six shillings and sixpence, and there is no count of persons on it and no figure on it for how many people are on it, and the sheet says on its own face that it is a list of what people did with their own money and not an account of a ship.

**And the leaf was read back at about ten past five in the evening of that same day, on the same deck, in the open, in the ship's own left hand, all four columns including the empty one.**

"There are one hundred and ninety-two entries in the first column at this hour and one hundred and ninety-three at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and ten have gone in on the ten days from Wednesday the thirteenth to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has eight hundred and thirty-seven lines in it at this hour and eight hundred and thirty-nine at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and fifty have gone in on the ten days since, five on each of them, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the fifty, forty-five went in on the nine days before this one and three went in this afternoon before this hour, and fifty less two is forty-eight, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and forty-eight is eight hundred and thirty-seven. **That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's one hundred and ninety-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these ten days and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to. The reason it has taken no line was entered in the second column at about ten past nine in the evening of the twenty-eighth day of August, in that yard's own mouth, with the hour against it, and it is entered once and it is not entered again, and the reason it is in the second column and not in the fourth is that a man on a mole cannot check the second column and a man on a mole can check the fourth."

She shut it and put it under her own coat and neither of them said one word about the figure it gave, and the reason she read it on a deck two days' sail from that harbor is in the second column and is that a leaf that is only read back in the building it is kept in is a leaf that has not been read back.

---

Three people said one thing each about that deck at about ten past five in the evening on the said day, and they were said in that order, and none of them was said twice, and none of them was written down.

Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, of the third lay, who cannot hear out of the left side of his head, about the water: "There is more of that trench than we have put a bell into, and what is in it is not the sea and it is not a wreck, and I have stood on a step in the dark on the left of a thing in about twenty-two fathoms of water and I would like somebody to come and tell me I heard it, and I am not going to ask anybody to tell me that."

A man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down, about the paper: "You put a sheet in a pocket on the Friday and told me on the Saturday after that you did not know what was on that shelf, and then you told me on the Wednesday that there was a four-inch mark on the bottom, and both of those were said to me before I had to ask for them. That is three things out of a boat of nine in six days and I do not know what to do with that, and I am going home and I have written the whole of it down in my own book and there is a line in it about my boy."

The holder of that writ, about this trip: "It is two days down, two days back, a bell, a chamber with a stove in it, a trench mouth, a wrench of a man down there and eight accounts in a book that do not agree with each other and a piece of lead in somebody's coat with our own cut in it, and it has cost other people's money and not one penny of ours, and we are going back on this tide with five hundred and fifty-eight pence on a sheet and a man who cannot hear out of one side of his head, and I have not been down to the bottom of that trench and neither has anybody else, and I am not going to write a sentence about this that says we found anything."

---

Here is the whole of what that harbor was carrying on the twenty-second day of October, and it is here once and nowhere else.

There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month and there has been no barley in that market since the middle of September. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August, and a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down and cannot be taken off. Physic is fourteen pence, being one shilling and twopence, and there is none of it. The mole-head light is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide has been unanswered since the ninth of November past. The third lay fills on the first of April and one berth is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and fifty-two weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and was charged on the fourteenth, the eighteenth and the twenty-first days of October and walked on by none of it on any of them, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the other seven days of these ten put no charge on it. The sheet of six printed lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-seventh day, on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence that was not taken down first, and is answered by nobody. The paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-second day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-ninth day. A claim entered in a book at a counter off the west basin against a made thing on the seventh of October is not granted, not refused and not withdrawn. A ropemaker of about sixty-one is not in her room and does not get that room back. A man of about thirty-one with a chart in a bag said on the thirteenth of October that he had one and did not produce it and was not asked for it. **And money went into a sheet in that harbor on three days between the fourteenth and the sixteenth days of October, and one woman who put a figure against her own name said out loud that the money was for flour, and there is no flour in that market, and that money went down a coast road and it did not buy flour and it is not going to be given back, and nobody in that harbor has been told that it was worth it and nobody is going to be.**

**AND THE BAG STANDS AT TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THE FIGURE IT IS SHORT OF THE WAGE OF FOUR MARKS IS SIX HUNDRED AND TWELVE PENCE, BEING THREE MARKS ELEVEN SHILLINGS, AND IT IS NEVER ZERO. MONEY WENT INTO A SHEET THAT CARRIES NO COUNT OF HOW MANY PEOPLE ARE ON IT AND THAT HAD NO HEAD AT THE TOP OF IT FOR FORTY MINUTES AND A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD IN THAT FRONT STREET AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND A BARREL OF OIL WAS BOUGHT FOUR DAYS DOWN THAT COAST AT FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE BECAUSE THERE WAS NONE TO BUY AT HOME, AND COAL FOR A PUMP WENT ON THAT SHEET AT TWICE WHAT COAL IS AT HOME, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE ON A BOAT OF ABOUT NINETEEN TONS CANNOT HEAR OUT OF THE LEFT SIDE OF HIS HEAD AND IS NOT ASKED ABOUT IT AND IS NOT SENT HOME, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WITH A TOOL ROLL WENT UP A LADDER OUT OF ABOUT TWENTY-TWO FATHOMS WITH A PIECE OF WORKED LEAD INSIDE HIS COAT THAT HAS A CUT IN IT THAT A PERSON IN THAT HARBOR RECOGNIZED, AND HE WAS NOT STOPPED AND WAS NOT THANKED AND WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION AND GAVE ONE ANSWER. NOBODY WAS LOST OFF THAT SHELF, AND NOBODY CAME BACK WHO HAD NOT GONE OUT, AND ONE MAN CAME BACK WITH NINE FEET OF SOUND MISSING OFF THE LEFT SIDE OF HIS HEAD AND IS ON THAT BOAT STILL. THE WHOLE OF WHAT THAT COST IS FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE THAT IS STILL ON A SHEET, A THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE WENT IN AND THE SAME WENT OUT OF A BERTH FUND ON TEN DAYS AT A FIGURE IN CHALK ON A SLATE AT AN END STALL, AND AN AFTERNOON IN JUNE THAT THE MAN WHO KEEPS THAT LEAF CAN PUT A NAME TO AND CANNOT PUT A NAME TO WHAT WAS IN THE BOAT WITH HIM, WHICH HE SAID OUT LOUD ON THE EIGHTH OF OCTOBER PAST AND HAS NOT SAID SINCE, AND NONE OF IT IS A WAGE AND NONE OF IT IS A PRICE AND NONE OF IT IS ANYBODY'S.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-second day of October next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and entered by the ship's hand on that boat in about nine fathoms: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, and that the whole of the ten days is one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence in and one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence out, being eleven marks, seven shillings and fourpence each way; and that a man of thirty-two said out loud on the deck of that boat at about nine in the morning, before he was asked, what he had done with a sheet of about four feet and what had answered it, and that a boundary was written down, being that the echo may inform a public record and may not decide anything this party does, and that it is to be read out in a room over a fish market with a name and an hour under it; and that a fitting-out sheet was read back in full at about ten past five in the evening of the said day with every line and an hour against it, and that seven thousand seven hundred and sixty pence came in on it and seven thousand two hundred and two went out and five hundred and fifty-eight pence is on it, and that the sheet carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it; and that the leaf was read back at about ten past five in the evening of that same day in the ship's own left hand, in the open, all four columns including the empty one, with both definitions on the face of that reading.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck in about nine fathoms, and no name is against it: that the reason a leaf is read back on a deck two days' sail from the building it is kept in is in the ship's own mouth and is that a leaf that is only read back in the building it is kept in is a leaf that has not been read back, and that the same two definitions come out of the same mouth in the same words every time, and that is the whole of what makes a figure checkable; and that the reason a man put a sheet in a pocket and said so out loud about eight hours after it answered something is in his own mouth and is that a man who is going to say to himself in a minute that he was wrong about a thing is not a man who can be checked with a pencil, and that the yard keeps those two things apart and does not mix them; and that the reason the boundary is a printed line and not a thing two people agreed about is in a woman of thirty's own mouth and is that a rule that is a sentence can be argued with and a rule that is a line on a sheet with a name and an hour under it can be read out in a room where about eleven people cannot read and can be heard by all of them, and that nobody in that harbor is going to be asked to agree with it and that is why it does not need them to; and that the reason no figure is set against a man of about twenty-nine who cannot hear out of the left side of his head is that a man on a mole cannot check what a man can hear, and that the cost of putting a gasket back so that it does not do it again is a figure of two hundred and forty-four pence and the cost of what it did is not one; and that the reason the sheet carries no count of persons is in the holder's own mouth and in a woman of about fifty-eight's, and that a page which counts how many people are on it is a page a man reads twice, and that the one thing on that sheet which is counted by anybody is money; and that the reason a master whose six days were paid to the penny carried nine men up that coast for two days and would not take a figure for it is in his own mouth and is that he was going as far as that water goes and no further, and that this yard is not opening a line for it at half past nine in the evening because a man carrying people he is not paid for is a thing a stranger with a pencil cannot check and this yard has kept such things out of a column for as long as that leaf has been open; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Friday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day, and the harbor is poorer on the twenty-second day of October than on the thirteenth of it, and eleven people's money has gone down a coast road and has not come back and is not coming back.*
