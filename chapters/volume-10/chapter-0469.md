# Chapter 469

*Sunday the Thirty-First Day of October, a Wipe, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare for the Seventy-Eighth Time at About Eight in the Morning in a Harbor the Party Was Not In, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came, a Party Coming Onto the Crownless Shelf at About Ten in the Morning on the Third Day of a Run, a Bell-Shaped Chamber with a Deck Plate, a Stove, a Stove Pipe and a Gauge Cut Free of Its Four Corners and Taken Down the Outside of a Trench in About Twenty-Two Fathoms and Set on the Floor of It in the Working Day, the Water at That Mouth Coming Up About Two Feet in the Twenty Minutes After the Flood and Not Going Down Again Inside the Working Day, a Party Standing on the Floor of That Trench for the First Time and What Is on It Being a Yard and Not a Wreck, Racks Cut to a Pattern in Rows Holding Nothing With the Marks of What Was Made to Sit in Them, a Worked Blank of Glass About the Size of a Sheet Coming Up Off That Floor in a Hand Cradle in a Bell and Going Into a Crate on That Deck, and Nobody on the Face of That Harbor Able to Read It, a Gasket and a Patch and Stove Pipe Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at Three Hundred and Eight Pence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Sunday the thirty-first day of October, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Sunday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe. The wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning for the seventy-eighth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in that harbor and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on that deck and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-seven days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-nine days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a sixteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and seven days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventy-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in ninety-one days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and nine days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-sixth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the sixty-first day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-eighth day. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat could not hear out of the left side of his head and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

They came onto that shelf at about ten in the morning on the third day of the run and the master put her down on the edge of the reef in about two fathoms again and would not take her over it, and nobody argued with him about it.

The chamber had been standing on the bottom of that shelf since the evening of the nineteenth day of October with sand packed into its four corners and a stove in it that does not draw and a gauge on the side of it reading eighty-four pounds on a face of a hundred and fifty, which is not a depth reading, and the whole of it came free of that sand in about forty minutes on the said Sunday with two men in a bell and a pump and a great deal of swearing about a spade.

**The two men who went down were named on a slate before either of them went, with the reason against it and the hour against it, being that the pads were to be cut and the chamber was to be hung on four slings and the two of them were to work one ebb and come up on it, and the air limit was written on that slate as a figure of forty minutes and the tender on the surface was named and the tender's hand was on the chain weight and the tender could pull without asking anybody's leave.**

Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on the said day and nobody went in twice.

They hung it on four slings off the ring bolts and took it down the outside of that trench on the ebb, twenty-two fathoms of it, and the winch was worked off a spar on the boat and not off the boom, because the boom of a boat of about nineteen tons is not a thing to hang nineteen tons off at a stand, and the master said so and was right and nobody argued with him.

It took the working day and about twenty minutes of the next one, and it was on the floor of that trench and seated on four pads of sawn fir and shimmed level at about ten past four in the afternoon, and the stove pipe had a crack in it about nine inches up from the bend where the slings had it and would not keep a fire, and the gauge's gasket had gone at the same time and the face of that gauge read nothing at all for about a quarter of an hour, and both of those were mended in the bell and both of those went onto that sheet on that day at three hundred and eight pence, being one mark, twelve shillings and fourpence.

---

They went down onto that floor at about half past four in the afternoon on the said day, in that chamber, and the tide was making and the water at that mouth came up about two feet in the twenty minutes after the flood began, and they had about a hundred and five minutes of ebb in twenty-four hours at that harbor and about two hours of it that were usable, and they had about forty of them.

What is on that floor is a yard and it is not a wreck.

**There are racks. They are cut to a pattern, they stand in rows, and they are holding nothing, and in the bottom of every one of them there is the mark of the thing that was made to sit in it, and every one of those marks is the same shape and about the size of a sheet of paper, and they were counted on that floor by three men separately and came to forty, and there is nothing in any of them, and nothing has fallen into them from above, and the floor under them is swept.**

Nobody in that bell named it and nobody in that bell said what it was for, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote nothing on any slate about it on the said day, and on the Monday he wrote two lines in the second column and the two lines say what he saw and do not say what he thinks it is, and that is a different day's entry and is not in the said Sunday.

A man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down said one thing out loud in that bell at about ten to five in the afternoon and it is in the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the hour against it, and it does not contain a name of anything and it does not contain a figure of anything and he has not said it again since.

---

A worked blank of glass about the size of a sheet came up off that floor at about ten past five in the afternoon, in a hand cradle, in that bell, and a woman of about thirty-one who crews nothing and sells nothing and is out of a coast town four days down held it flat with both hands for about two minutes on the after deck of that boat in about nine fathoms of water and put it down on a crate lid and took her hands off it.

Mara Venn looked at it for about a minute and a half and did not say anything about it for the first forty seconds, and then she said the only thing she said about it that day, and it was said to the boat and not to a person.

"I have been reading things out of glass for eleven years and I have read a thing out of a shard the size of my thumb that a man made in a shed in a bad hour and that was more than this. There is nothing on this to read. It is worked, and the working is good, and there is no more in it than there is in a pane of window out of that shed behind us, and I am not going to write a sentence about it tonight that says what it is, and I am not going to write one tomorrow either."

Nobody asked her whether it was any particular thing. Nobody in that boat used a word for it, and no word for it is on any page of that harbor, and the sheet of six lines at the back of an end stall has been answered by nobody for the forty-sixth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street for the sixty-first, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street for the seventy-eighth, and none of them is answered by this either.

**It went into a crate on that deck with a blanket round it and a batten across the lid and the day against it in that yard's own mouth, and the crate is on that deck and is going to Morrow Quay on that boat on the next tide, and there is nobody on the face of that harbor who can read it.**

---

**A CHAMBER WITH A DECK PLATE, A STOVE, A STOVE PIPE AND A GAUGE WENT DOWN THE OUTSIDE OF A TRENCH IN ABOUT TWENTY-FATHOMS AND CAME TO REST ON FOUR PADS OF SAWN FIR AT ABOUT TEN PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND IT IS A THING AND NOT A BUILDING, IT HAS NO DOOR ON THE SHORE SIDE, AND IT IS NOT A ROOM THAT ANYBODY IN MORROW QUAY HAS EVER BEEN INSIDE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS GIVEN IT A NAME. THERE ARE FORTY RACKS ON THAT FLOOR IN ROWS AND THEY ARE HOLDING NOTHING AND THE FLOOR UNDER THEM IS SWEPT. A WORKED BLANK OF GLASS ABOUT THE SIZE OF A SHEET CAME UP IN A HAND CRADLE AND WENT INTO A CRATE, AND IT IS BLANK IN A WAY THAT IS NOT AN EMPTY VESSEL, AND NOBODY ON THE FACE OF THAT HARBOR CAN READ IT, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED ANYBODY TO SAY WHAT IT IS.**

**A GASKET AND A PATCH AND STOVE PIPE WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY AT THREE HUNDRED AND EIGHT PENCE, BEING ONE MARK, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THAT SHEET IS SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING FOUR MARKS, THREE SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirty-first day of October next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, being the seventy-eighth time the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare, and that it went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a party came onto the Crownless Shelf at about ten in the morning on the third day of a run, the shelf being about two days' sail from that harbor and no further; and that a bell-shaped chamber with a deck plate, a stove, a stove pipe and a gauge was cut free of four sanded corners and hung on four slings and taken down the outside of a trench in about twenty-two fathoms on the ebb and seated on four pads of sawn fir at about ten past four in the afternoon, the water at that mouth coming up about two feet in the twenty minutes after the flood and not going down again inside the working day; and that two men were named on a slate before either went down, with the reason and the hour against it, being that the pads were to be cut and the chamber hung and the two of them were to work one ebb and come up on it, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and a cutoff the tender could pull without asking; and that there are forty racks on that floor in rows, cut to a pattern, holding nothing, with the mark of the thing that was made to sit in each of them, and that the three men who counted them came to forty each, and that nothing has fallen into them from above and the floor under them is swept; and that a worked blank of glass about the size of a sheet came up off that floor in a hand cradle in that bell at about ten past five in the afternoon, and that it went into a crate on that deck with a blanket and a batten and the day against it; and that a gasket and a patch and stove pipe went onto the fitting-out sheet on the said day, being three hundred and eight pence, being one mark, twelve shillings and fourpence, and that that sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty in and eleven thousand three hundred and twenty-six out and is short by six hundred and eighty-six pence, being four marks, three shillings and tenpence, and carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck in about nine fathoms, and no name is against it: that the reason the winch was worked off a spar and not off the boom is in the master of that boat's own mouth, and is that the boom of a boat of about nineteen tons is not a thing to hang nineteen tons off at a stand, and that nobody in that party argued with him about it, and that this is the second time that man has decided something about where that boat goes and neither time has anybody minded; and that the reason about forty racks hold nothing and the floor under them is swept is entered here as a thing that was seen and not as a thing that was meant, and that a record of what a floor looks like is checkable by a man on a mole and a record of what it was for is not, and that is the whole of the difference between the two columns; and that the reason a woman of thirty of no office said the only thing she said about that blank out loud and to the boat and not to a person is her own, and is that a thing said to a boat is heard by nine people and a thing said to a person is heard by two, and that she has eleven years of reading glass and there was nothing on it for her and she is not going to invent something for it tonight or tomorrow; and that the reason no word for that blank is on any page of that harbor is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a thing nobody has read is not going to be given a name by a person who has not read it, and that three sheets on three walls in that harbor have been answered by nobody, being on their forty-sixth, sixty-first and seventy-eighth days, and are not answered by this either; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Sunday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
