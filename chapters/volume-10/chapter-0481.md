# Chapter 481

*Saturday the Thirteenth Day of November, a Day That Is Not a Rota Day and on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, Bread and Salt Beef for Nine Men for Ten Days Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Ten in the Morning at One Thousand Six Hundred Pence, Being Ten Marks, and a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, and Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand on Any Line, Three Descents and All Three of Them on Air Out of That Boat's Own Bottles and Not on Any Figure, with a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air on Each Slate and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff That Tender Could Pull Without Asking, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Going Down at About Ten Past Nine in the Morning on That Ebb and Coming Up at About Ten o'Clock Having Found a Second Opening in a Wall at the Far End of That Floor and Not Having Gone Through It, a Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Going Down at About Ten Past Ten and Coming Up at About Eleven o'Clock with a Woven Basket of Brown Folded Paper Out of a Room Behind That Wall That Was Dry, and a Man of About Thirty-Seven Going Down at About Twenty Past Eleven and Coming Up at About Ten Past Twelve with Two More and That Had Been Dry a Very Long Time and That Had Racks in It Like the Forty Racks Do, Nobody on That Deck Saying Out Loud in Any Mouth That It Is the Best Thing This Party Has Ever Had in Its Hands, and the Shortfall Said at Half Past Five in the Yard's Own Mouth at Nine Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Six Pence, Being Fifty-Eight Marks and Sixpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Saturday the thirteenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at one thousand six hundred pence, being ten marks, and a day apiece in advance to the same four men went on at about the same hour at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and they move the way they have moved every day of this trip and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and seventy-nine days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-one; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and nineteen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and eighty-fifth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and three days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its fifty-eighth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-third; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninetieth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-one days. The far bench is occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and the holder of that writ does not go over to it and nothing is put on it and read as a gift. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest, and a week stands fallen due at every one of those Tuesdays and none of those Tuesdays has moved it. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**A slate was written before anybody was off that deck and it was written on the man who was going down and not by him, and the reason on it was in his own words because he had said the words out loud the evening before and the man who keeps the leaf wrote them down as they came out of him.**

The air on that slate was out of bottles that boat had carried off her own coast and filled off her own compressor, and it was not any part of the four days hired off that supply boat on the sixth of November, and the man who keeps the leaf said that out loud on that deck in front of all nine of them and nobody argued with him about it. **The four days that were hired were the sixth, the seventh, the eighth and the ninth, and the ninth was the last of them, and the line on the sheet that carries the money still bears the sixth of November and nothing has been opened under that figure since.** The master of that boat stood on the surface with the tender and said one word about the air, and the word was his own, and nobody asked him for it a second time, and no figure of it is on any sheet in that boat or in that harbor.

**Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, went down at about ten past nine in the morning on that ebb under that slate, and the air limit on it was a figure of forty minutes, and the tender was named and was on the surface of that boat and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.**

---

He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten o'clock.

He came up with a slate in his own hand and put it on the bench in front of the man who keeps the leaf and said one sentence before he took his helmet off, and the sentence was that there is a second opening in a wall at the far end of that floor, about four foot wide and 5 foot high, and that it is not the doorway he went through on the Thursday, and that it is further along the wall past the last of the fifteen frames, and that there is a floor behind it going away into the dark and racks on that floor.

"Then you went through it."

"No, I did not go through it," Trevask said. "My air was at about eleven minutes when I got my hand on the edge of it and I am not a man who goes through a door he has not looked through, and I have been in holds nineteen years and I have seen what comes off the other side of a door that a man walked through on eleven minutes." He put his hand flat on the bench. "There is a draught out of it. Not a movement. A draught, and cold, and it was not cold in that room I came out of, and it is standing in a draught on a floor at twenty-two fathoms, and that is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to say another word about it until somebody has been through it."

Nobody thanked him. No figure is set against him anywhere. He is not a casualty and nobody has named him one. Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice.

---

**And the holder of that writ said out loud in front of all nine of them that he was going to send a second man down on air he did not own, and that the air was the master's and that this was not his to give and that he was going to do it in front of nine people rather than at night on his own.**

"I want it written down that I have not been given that air and that nobody is being thanked for it and that there is no line on any sheet that I can open for it. The man who keeps the leaf will write in the second column at the hour of the reading that the thirteenth was dived on that boat's own air and that nobody asked the master for it twice. If that is not good enough then we do not go through the door today and the door will be there on the sixteenth and the seventeenth and the eighteenth, and it will not be any better at any of those hours than it is now."

The man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down said he would go, and he said his reason in his own words and the man who keeps the leaf wrote it down before he took his helmet off, and the reason was that a man who has been in holds nineteen years can put a hand on a floor and know whether a thing is standing on it, and that he was the only man on that boat who could.

He went down at about ten past ten under a slate of his own with a figure of forty minutes on it and the same tender and the same cutoff, and he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about eleven o'clock.

**He came up with a woven basket about the size of a water butt that had been sitting in that room, and the basket was full of brown folded paper in bundles, and he had the basket because there was a handle to it and the handle was tied to a bracket about the height of a man's chest and the paper was in it and the paper had been dry in a dry room for a very long time and it was not wet and it was not soft and it did not run when he put his thumb into it.**

---

**And at about twenty past eleven in the morning a man of about thirty-seven went down on that same ebb under a slate of his own, with a figure of forty minutes of air on it and the same tender and the same cutoff, being that there was a second basket on the floor of that room with a handle to it like the first one and that a man of fifty-one could not carry two and did not have to try, and he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten past twelve with a second basket and a third one lashed to the back of it, and the third basket was not full and he said so and nobody told him to fill it and nobody thanked him.**

**No man went into that mouth twice that day and no two men were in it at once at any hour, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the three descents down in order with the air they were on, and the three of them are on the face of that day and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take them off it.**

**The paper came into the light of that deck for the first time in a long time and it went onto a tarpaulin on that after deck and it was brown and folded twice and stiff at the folds and it had a string through two holes at the head of the fold and the string had parted long ago and it had a head ruled on it and a column of figures ruled under the head and the figures were small and cramped and the hand was not a hand any man on that boat had seen before and there is no name on it and there is no year on it and there is nothing on the face of it that anybody on that deck could read out as the name of anybody.**

**It is the best thing this party has ever had in its hands. Nobody on that deck said so on that day and nobody has been thanked for any part of it and no figure is set against the finding of it and no figure is set against the man of about fifty-one.**

---

**And there was nowhere to put it, and that was said out loud on that deck at about half past twelve in the afternoon by the master of that boat, and it was the first thing anybody had said about the paper since it came up.**

"You have got about four foot of deck under that bracket and the only dry flat place on this boat is under it, and the man that has been sleeping under it for three weeks has to be somewhere tonight, and the boy has the forepeak and the boy is not being put out of it for a crate of old paper." He did not raise his voice. "I am not saying a word about what is in it. I am saying that a boat is a boat and that paper is not a thing you get to have for nothing, and that the three of you who can carry it are going to have to find a box for it, and that a box is not free and that I am not buying one and that you will have to tell me who is."

**The holder of that writ said out loud that the crate would be bought on the seventeenth and that the paper would go on the tarpaulin under the bracket until then and that the man who slept under the bracket would be put in the forepeak with the boy and the boy had not been asked and was not going to be asked, and that the master had said the whole of what he wanted to say and that nobody was to thank anybody for any part of it.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon on that after deck, in the open, in the yard's own mouth, the position was said out loud and it is said in that harbor's own words because the yard's own words are what a leaf is read in.**

"The sheet that carries the money stands at nineteen thousand nine hundred and twenty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence in, and it is nine thousand two hundred and eighty-six pence short, being fifty-eight marks and sixpence. It carries no count of persons. The three who said no stand on it at the same size as the figures, and none of the three has been asked a second time and nobody in that harbor has been asked anything."

**THE DESCENT OF THE THIRTEENTH WAS ON THE AIR OUT OF THAT BOAT'S OWN BOTTLES AND NOT ON ANY LINE AND NOT ON ANY FIGURE, AND THE MASTER NAMED IT AS HIS OWN OUT LOUD ON THE SURFACE, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM FOR IT A SECOND TIME AND NO FIGURE OF IT IS ON ANY SHEET IN THAT BOAT OR IN THAT HARBOR. A SECOND OPENING WAS FOUND IN A WALL AT THE FAR END OF THAT FLOOR, ABOUT FOUR FOOT WIDE, PAST THE LAST OF THE FIFTEEN FRAMES, AND A ROOM WAS BEHIND IT WITH RACKS IN IT, AND A MAN WENT THROUGH IT AND CAME UP AT ABOUT ELEVEN O'CLOCK WITH A WOVEN BASKET OF BROWN FOLDED PAPER IN IT, AND THE PAPER CAME UP IN THREE BASKETS AND WENT ONTO A TARPAULIN ON THAT AFTER DECK. IT IS THE BEST THING THIS PARTY HAS EVER HAD IN ITS HANDS AND NOBODY SAID SO ON THAT DAY AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST IT. THERE IS NOWHERE ON THAT BOAT TO PUT IT AND A BOX IS NOT FREE AND THE MASTER SAID SO OUT LOUD. THAT SHEET IS NINE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING FIFTY-EIGHT MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirteenth day of November at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all; and that bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days at one thousand six hundred pence, being ten marks, and a day apiece in advance to the same four men at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning, being two thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence together, and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the air on all three slates of that day came out of bottles that boat had carried off her own coast and filled off her own compressor and that the four days hired off that supply boat on the sixth of November were the sixth, the seventh, the eighth and the ninth and the ninth was the last of them and that the line still bears the sixth and nothing has been opened under that figure since; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay went down at about ten past nine in the morning on that ebb under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten o'clock; and that he said out loud on that deck that there is a second opening in a wall at the far end of that floor about four foot wide and five foot high, past the last of the fifteen frames and not the doorway he went through on the eleventh, and that there is a floor behind it and racks on that floor, and that he did not go through it because his air was at about eleven minutes when he got his hand on the edge of it and he is not a man who goes through a door he has not looked through; and that a man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down went down at about ten past ten under a slate of his own with a figure of forty minutes on it and the same tender and the same cutoff and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about eleven o'clock with a woven basket about the size of a water butt that had been standing in that room, the paper in it being brown and folded twice and stiff at the folds with a parted string through two holes at the head of the fold, a head ruled on it, a column of figures ruled under the head, a small cramped hand that is not a hand any man on that boat has seen before, and no name and no year on the face of it; and that a man of about thirty-seven went down at about twenty past eleven in the morning on that same ebb under a slate of his own with a figure of forty minutes of air on it and the same tender and the same cutoff, being that there was a second basket on the floor of that room with a handle to it like the first one, and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten past twelve with a second basket and a third one lashed to the back of it, and that the third basket was not full and he said so and nobody told him to fill it and nobody thanked him; and that no man went into that mouth twice that day and no two men were in it at once at any hour; and that nobody thanked the man of about thirty-four and no figure is set against him; and that the master of that boat said out loud at about half past twelve that there is nowhere on that boat to put it, that the only dry flat place is under the stove-pipe bracket, that a man who has slept under that bracket for three weeks has to be somewhere, that the boy has the forepeak and is not being put out of it for a crate of old paper, that a box is not free and that he is not buying one; and that the holder of that writ said out loud that the box would be bought on the seventeenth and that the paper would sit on the tarpaulin under the bracket until then, and that nobody thanked anybody for any part of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the air of the thirteenth went on the boat's own bottles and not on any line is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a line is a figure a stranger with a pencil can take off a page and an air bottle a man carries off his own coast is not, and that a descent bought out of a kindness is a descent somebody can stop by not being kind, and that this yard would rather have a dive it can argue about than a dive it has been given; and that the reason the holder of that writ said out loud that he was sending a second man down on air he did not own rather than doing it quietly is his own, and is that nine men on a deck are the only place where a thing done for nothing can be seen to be done for nothing before it is done; and that the reason a man of about thirty-four would not go through a door on eleven minutes of air is his own, and is that a man who has been in holds nineteen years has seen what comes off the other side of a door a man walked through on eleven minutes, and that the party has nothing yet it can put a second man in after one; and that the reason no man went into that mouth twice on the thirteenth is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a rule about one person at a time is only worth anything on the days when it is inconvenient, and that a day on which nobody wanted a second turn is not a day on which a rule was kept, it is a day on which a rule was never tested; and that it is in this column and not in the first because a stranger with a pencil can count how many times a man went into that mouth off a slate and cannot count what an hour of that ebb was worth to a man who has spent nineteen years deciding on his own whether a thing can be got out in one tide; and that the reason nobody said out loud on that deck that the paper is the best thing this party has ever had in its hands is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a party which says that about a find on the day it finds it has said it about a find and not about a party, and that nobody on that boat is thanked for a thing nobody asked them to find, and that the day it is said out loud will be a day in a room and not on a deck; and that the reason the master of that boat said the whole of what he wanted to say in four sentences and then would not say it again is his own, and is that he said on the eleventh what he is going to do with his own people and he does not say that twice, and that a number can be said twice to show it has not moved and a thing that is not a number is said once; and that the reason the whole of that harbor's standing debts was left where it stood on the said Saturday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a door four foot wide at the far end of that floor does not pay a wage, and that the wage column stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest at ten o'clock that morning and stood there at four in the afternoon, and that the bag in that shed stands at twenty-eight pence and a stranger with a pencil can take both of those off a wall and find that they agree.*
