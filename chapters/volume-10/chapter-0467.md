# Chapter 467

*Friday the Twenty-Ninth Day of October, a Day Apiece Going to the Same Four Men, Being Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Two Men Taken Out of a Tide Gang That Is Four Men Short in That Harbor and Told They Will Be Back on the Thursday and Are Not to Be Relied on for Anything Else, a Man of About Fifty Asking Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon Whether the Trip Is Going Ahead Because the Money Is In or In Spite of It and Being Answered in the Yard's Own Mouth, a Figure of Four Hundred and Fifty Pence Still on a Board at an End Stall and Not Coming Down, and a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Going Out Again at About Six in the Evening on the Flood With the Sheet Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight Pence Short and Nobody Asked for the Rest, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Friday the twenty-ninth day of October, at the head of that slip, and at the end of that second lay, and in that shed.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was at the head of that slip and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-five days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-seven days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a sixteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and five days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventy-first night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-nine days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seven days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-fourth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-ninth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-sixth day.**

---

A day apiece went to the same four men at about ten in the morning on the said Friday, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, and the same four wrote their names against their own lines and the same four asked the same question they asked on the sixteenth about what happens if a man's air goes, and the same answer was given by the same man, and none of the four said one word about the fact that they had asked it before.

**And two men were taken out of a tide gang that is four men short in that harbor, and the man who counts that gang said out loud at about eleven in the morning that he had not asked for them, that he was not being asked for them, that he had been told and not consulted, and that they would be back on the Thursday, and that he was not going to be able to count them for anything between that Friday and that Thursday and anybody who wanted to plan anything with the tide in that harbor between those days should plan without them.**

Nobody argued with him about the four and nobody offered to make it five and nobody said the word that a tide gang is written down in. Two men went off that slip at about twelve in the afternoon and were not thanked and were not asked whether they wanted to go and were not paid out of the fitting-out sheet, and their days came out of the harbor's own count and not out of that page, and that is the only reason they are not in either column of that leaf.

---

A man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay asked the question at about two in the afternoon on the said Friday, in the open, on that slip, in front of about nine people, and it is in the yard's own mouth because he asked it out loud and not because anybody had written it down for him.

"Is this trip going ahead because the money is in, or in spite of the money."

The man who keeps the leaf answered him in the yard's own mouth and did not soften it and did not make it a speech.

"Because the money is in. It was short before it was in and it is short now and it is short by a figure that is on a page four hundred yards from here in words, and I am not going to stand here and tell you it is any other thing than that. The second reason is a different one and I will give it to you separately, because a man is owed both of them and not one of them dressed up as the other. The first time went because we did not know what was down there and we have now got a number for how deep it is and a line for how wide it is at the top and a bell that has been down a ladder of it. That is a reason and it is not money. If the money had all been in on the thirteenth I would still have gone, and I would have gone with less of it, and I would not have gone a second time on what the first time brought back if what the first time brought back was a number and nothing else. So the money is in, and it is short, and neither of those is the reason, and the reason is standing here and has been since Friday."

Nobody in that slip argued with it and nobody thanked him for saying it. A woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, who is not on that sheet and is not a witness to anything, said that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again and went back inside, and that is the only thing she said and it is the second time in three days that she has said it and she was not thanked for either.

---

They cast off from the dolphin at the end of the second lay at about six in the evening on the flood, and the wind was in the west and the master of that boat said the same thing he says every evening of a run, in a flat voice, in front of his two men and the boy, and nobody in that party was asked to like it.

"Two hours on the flood and then we will be in the roads, and if the flood stands we will be on the shelf by the small hours, and if it does not stand we will lie off the point and come again on the ebb, and I would rather lie off the point than be wrong about it. That is the whole of what I know and it is not a promise."

**The sheet that carries the money was three hundred and seventy-eight pence short at the hour that boat was let go, being two marks, four shillings and tenpence, and it was not concealed and it was not solved, and there is a line still to go on for a gasket and a patch and stove pipe and it has not been costed yet, and it is not going to be less by the time that boat is back, and nobody in that harbor has been asked for the rest of it and nobody is going to be.**

---

**THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE, BEING THIRTY-SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, WAS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL IN THAT FRONT STREET AT SIX IN THE EVENING ON THAT FRIDAY, WITH THE FIGURE OF THE FIRST OF JUNE AND THE FIGURE OF FEBRUARY BOTH STANDING VISIBLE ABOVE IT AND BOTH DATES ON THAT BOARD, AND IT DID NOT COME DOWN AND CANNOT BE TAKEN OFF, AND THERE IS NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR.**

**A BOAT WENT OUT AGAIN WITH A SHEET SHORT AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST, AND TWO MEN WENT OUT OF A TIDE GANG THAT IS FOUR MEN SHORT AND WILL BE BACK ON THE THURSDAY, AND THE HARBOR IS POORER ON THE TWENTY-NINTH DAY OF OCTOBER THAN IT WAS ON THE THIRTEENTH OF IT, AND A WAGE COLUMN AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS HAS FIFTY-THREE WEEKS STANDING FALLEN DUE WITH NO ENVELOPE AGAINST ANY OF THEM.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-ninth day of October at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a day apiece went to the same four men at about ten in the morning, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, each in his own hand, and that the same four asked the same question about a man's air going and were given the same answer by the same man; and that two men were taken out of a tide gang that is four men short in that harbor and the man who counts that gang said out loud that he had not asked for them and had been told and not consulted and that they would be back on the Thursday and that he could not count them for anything until then; and that those two men were not paid out of the fitting-out sheet and their days came out of the harbor's own count and are in neither column of that leaf; and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay asked out loud at about two in the afternoon on that slip, in front of about nine people, whether the trip was going ahead because the money was in or in spite of it, and was answered in the yard's own mouth that it was because the money was in, and that the money was short, and that the other reason was given separately and was that the first trip had brought back a number and a line and a bell; and that that boat was let go from the dolphin at the end of the second lay at about six in the evening on the flood; and that the sheet that carries the money was three hundred and seventy-eight pence short at that hour, being two marks, four shillings and tenpence, and that it carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it, and that there is a line still to go on and it has not been costed yet.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason two men were taken out of a tide gang four men short and not asked is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who counts a gang is not being asked to give two of it away and is entitled to say so out loud before he says yes, and that saying it out loud is the only part of it that anybody can check; and that the reason those two days are not on that sheet and are in neither column is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a day that comes out of the harbor's own count is a day the harbor is paying for already and putting it on a page in another room would be counting it twice, and that this yard does not count a thing twice; and that the reason a man was answered in the yard's own mouth with two reasons and neither of them dressed up as the other is his own, and is that a man is owed both of them, and that a man who gives one reason when he has two is doing the thing this whole page is against; and that the reason a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall said twice in three days that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again is her own, and that she is not on that sheet and is not a witness to anything and is not asked about it, and that what she said is a rule about a slate and a board and it is not a rule about anything on that leaf; and that the reason a boat was let go at six in the evening with a sheet short and the figure said out loud at the head of the slip is that a shortfall a stranger with a pencil can take off the page in a minute is worth more than a shortfall nobody has been told about, and that nobody in that harbor has been asked for the rest of it; and that the reason nothing that was owed in that harbor was settled, advanced or cut down on the said Friday is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a shortfall said out loud at the head of a slip is worth more than a debt taken quietly off in a back room, and that a boat let go with a sheet short is a thing nine men can carry and an old account settled with money that was never theirs is not.*
