# Chapter 472

*Wednesday the Third Day of November, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, a Small Supply Boat Lying Off the Reef Off the Crownless Shelf at About Seven in the Morning Off the Same Boat That Spoke Them on the Twenty-Third Day of October Past, Three Days of Coal for the Pump at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Four Pence, Being Eleven Marks and Fourpence, at the Same Figure and Not One Penny More or Less, a Woman of About Thirty-One Going Down on the Ebb and Bringing Up a Second Part Out of One of the Forty Racks in a Hand Cradle and Laying It on a Crate Lid on That After Deck, Three People Describing That Part in Three Mouths and the Three of Them Not Agreeing With Each Other, All Three Accounts Going into the Second Column in the Order They Were Said and None of Them Settled, the Same Sentence Said Out Loud Twice in That Supply Boat to Two Different Men and Neither of the Two Men Asked Anything About It, the Shortfall at Four Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Two Pence, Being Twenty-Nine Marks, Six Shillings and Tenpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the third day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**Nobody in that crew has said one word about the three nots standing against the eighth of June past, and they are a seventeenth week, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor and he is not on the water. Mara Venn is on a deck two days off that harbor, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf is under her own coat. A ropemaker of sixty-one is on the crown of that mole from ten until four and does not come down for anybody. Nothing else in that harbor moved on the said Wednesday and nothing in it was paid. The nine that do move are these: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and seventy days on its wall in a different street; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and seventy-two, the chest shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat unargued; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and ten days old; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and seventy-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, ninety-four days without moving; the sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall, its forty-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its sixty-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its eighty-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twelve days. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

The supply boat came in off the reef at about seven in the morning and put her alongside the mooring warp at about twenty past it, and she was the same boat that had come out to that shelf on the twenty-third day of October with coal for the pump and three days of it, and her master was about fifty-eight and had a way of saying a thing that made a listener believe he had been told it before.

Three days of coal went onto that sheet at about half past eight in the morning, being one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence, being eleven marks and fourpence, which is the same figure that boat put on that sheet on the twenty-third day of October and is not one penny more and not one penny less, and the figure was said out loud twice by the man who keeps the leaf before any coal came down the chute.

**That man said one thing on that boat's deck at about half past eight and he said it again at about ten past nine, and the second time he said it he said it to the master of the boat of about nineteen tons instead of to the man who keeps the leaf, and the two men who heard it are not the same man and neither of them asked him anything about it.**

"There is coal at that figure while there is coal on this coast," he said. "There will not be coal at that figure after the end of the month and there will not be coal at any figure, and I am not the man who decides that and I am not going to stand here and pretend to be."

Nobody wrote it on any sheet on that deck, and it went into the second column of that leaf that evening in the yard's own mouth with the hour against it, and it is not a figure and it is not a promise and it is not on the fitting-out sheet at all.

---

**Sabra Loach, of about thirty-one, who crews nothing and sells nothing and is out of a coast town four days down and is hired on by the day, went down at about ten in the morning with a written reason and an hour against it and a figure of forty minutes of air and a named tender on the surface of that boat and not in the bell, and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking anybody's leave.**

The reason was that a second rack was to be opened and one object brought up whole and the floor around it was to be swept a yard's width with a board that was not metal, because a metal board on that floor would have put a shine on it that nothing else on it has, and the party had been told by nobody to be careful of that floor and had been careful of it anyway.

She was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-two of them and came up at about ten to eleven on the ebb. She came up with a cold in her teeth and a taste of iron like a nail and a bruise on her right hip where she came off the ladder into that bell in the dark, and the bruise was the size of a hand and it did not come up with her and it stayed on her, and she worked the rest of that day at the winch and she was not thanked and no figure was set against the bruise.

Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on the said day and nobody went in twice.

---

**What came up in that cradle was a second part and it was about the size of a sheet of paper and it was not the same shape as the one that came up on the Tuesday, and three people described it in three mouths, and the three of them do not agree with each other, and all three went into the second column of that leaf that evening in the order they were said, and none of them was settled.**

The man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down said it first.

"That is the lid of a box and I will tell you how I know. There is a lip run round three of its edges and the lip is worn bright on one side of it and not on the other, and a thing that is worn on one side and not on the other was lifted off one way and not the other, every time, for a long time by somebody who never thought about it. That tells you how it was used. It does not tell you what was in the box and it does not tell you what the box was for."

Ollum Trevask said it second, and he did not agree with him, and he said why in his own mouth.

"A lid comes off a box the way a lid comes off a box. This was pulled out of a channel. There is a channel down the middle of that thing about the width of a finger and it goes the whole length of it, and the wear is at the two ends of that channel and not in the middle of it, and nothing that is merely lifted wears the ends and leaves the middle. It was drawn out of that channel from an end. And the two ends are not worn alike and I have not been down there long enough with a light to say which end is older and I am not going to guess at it from a deck."

Ivo Skell, of about forty-four, nineteen years of compressors and in nobody's crew and a member of nothing, said it third and did not agree with either of them and gave them his reason for not agreeing with either of them.

"I will give you the word for the only thing I am sure of, and then I am finished. It is a lap. That is the word in a compressor house for a piece that is made to sit against another piece so that a seal is made where the two of them meet, and I have taken six of them out of machines that were standing before I was born and I did not know what any of the six was for either. It does not matter to me in the least what that thing was for. A piece that is made to be set against another piece is a part, and a part is a part, and that is all you will get out of me on a Wednesday morning."

Nobody took it further and nobody was asked to take it further, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the three of them down in the order they were said with the hour against each and wrote at the foot of them that they do not agree and that the party does not know which of the three is right and is not going to find out by asking one of them twice.

---

**THREE DAYS OF COAL FOR THE PUMP WENT ONTO THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY AT ONE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR PENCE, BEING ELEVEN MARKS AND FOURPENCE, AT THE SAME FIGURE THAT BOAT PUT ON IT ON THE TWENTY-THIRD DAY OF OCTOBER PAST, AND THE MAN IN THAT BOAT SAID ONE SENTENCE OUT LOUD TWICE IN THAT MORNING TO TWO DIFFERENT MEN AND NEITHER OF THEM ASKED HIM ANYTHING ABOUT IT. A SECOND PART CAME UP OUT OF ONE OF THE FORTY RACKS IN A HAND CRADLE AND WENT ONTO A CRATE LID ON THAT AFTER DECK AND WAS DESCRIBED BY THREE PEOPLE IN THREE MOUTHS AS A LID, AS A CARRIER PULLED OUT OF A CHANNEL, AND AS A LAP, AND THE THREE OF THEM DO NOT AGREE, AND ALL THREE ARE IN THE SAME COLUMN IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SAID AND NONE OF THEM IS SETTLED. A WOMAN CAME UP OFF THAT FLOOR WITH A COLD IN HER TEETH AND A TASTE OF IRON LIKE A NAIL AND A BRUISE ON HER HIP THE SIZE OF A HAND AND WENT ON WORKING, AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST THE BRUISE AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THAT SHEET IS FOUR THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING TWENTY-NINE MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the third day of November at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a small supply boat came in off the reef at about seven in the morning on the said day and put her alongside the mooring warp at about twenty past it, being the same boat that came out to that shelf on the twenty-third day of October past; and that three days of coal for the pump went onto the fitting-out sheet at about half past eight in the morning, being one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence, being eleven marks and fourpence, at the same figure as the twenty-third day of October and not one penny more and not one penny less; and that the man in that boat said one sentence out loud twice, once at about half past eight to the man who keeps the leaf and once at about ten past nine to the master of the boat of about nineteen tons, and that nobody wrote it on any sheet on that deck; and that a woman of about thirty-one went down at about ten in the morning in about twenty-two fathoms under a slate with the reason in her own words and the hour against it, being that a second rack was to be opened and one object brought up whole and the floor around it swept a yard's width with a board that was not metal, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking; and that she was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-two of them and came up at about ten to eleven on the ebb, and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice; and that what came up was a second part about the size of a sheet of paper and not the same shape as the one that came up on the said Tuesday, having a lip run round three of its edges worn bright on one side and not on the other, and a channel down the middle of it about the width of a finger with the wear at the two ends of that channel and not in the middle of it; and that three people described it in three mouths, being a lid of a box, a carrier drawn out of a channel, and a lap, and that the three of them do not agree with each other; and that the woman came up with a cold in her teeth and a taste of iron like a nail and a bruise on her right hip the size of a hand and worked the rest of that day at the winch and was not thanked; and that the fitting-out sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and fifteen thousand three hundred and sixty-two pence out and is four thousand seven hundred and twenty-two pence short, being twenty-nine marks, six shillings and tenpence, and that no figure has been asked of anybody to make that otherwise.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason three people were allowed to describe one part in three different ways and none of them was asked to describe it again is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a part on a deck in nine fathoms of water can be looked at by three men who have all been in different rooms for nineteen years and forty-four years and fifty-one years, and that if the yard chose the one it liked best it would be choosing a description and not a part, and that the party is a party of nine and a floor nobody has a chart of deserves three descriptions and not one; and that the reason the two ends of that channel are not both worn alike and no figure was given for which is the older is the man of about thirty-four's own, and is that he has not been down there with a light long enough and he will not give a number off a deck; and that the reason a pump hand of about forty-four was asked nothing about what the machine he compared it to was for is his own, and is that he was hired to work a compressor and has been asked nothing about anything else since he came aboard, and that a man who has said the only thing he knows four times in nineteen years is not going to be asked a fifth time to make it bigger; and that the reason the sentence about coal was said twice and written on no sheet is in the yard's own mouth, and is that the first time a man says a thing he is telling the boat what happened and the second time he is telling the boat that he meant it, and that both of those are worth having and neither of them is a figure and neither of them can be put on a sheet, and that if it had been put on a sheet it would have been something this yard had promised and the yard promises nothing it has not got; and that the reason the sentence about coal was said twice and written on no sheet is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a price a man has said out loud is a price and a price nobody has heard is a rumour, and that this yard does not put a rumour on a page in a coat.
