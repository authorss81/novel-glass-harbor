# Chapter 476

*Sunday the Seventh Day of November, a Wipe, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare for the Seventy-Ninth Time at About Eight in the Morning in a Harbor the Party Was Not In, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came, Four Hands Down on One Ebb One at a Time With a Slate Written Before Each of Them Went Down and a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff That Tender Could Pull Without Asking, and the Fourth of Them Coming Up at About Half Past Twelve With the Order of Two Things He Had Done That Morning Gone Out of His Head in the Middle of Them and Not Going Back Down That Day and Nobody Asking Him Why and Nobody Thanked and Nobody a Casualty, a Second Pair of Hands for a Day off a Supply Boat at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence and Twenty Fathoms of Three-Inch Line at Three Hundred and Forty Pence Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet on the Same Day, Three Days of Air in Front of That Party and No Line Opened for a Fourth, the Shortfall at Five Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Thirty-Seven Marks, Two Shillings and Sixpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Sunday the seventh day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Sunday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe. The wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning for the seventy-ninth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**The wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning and went up again at the angle it has been at, and the party was two days' sail off that deck and saw none of it.** The nine that go on with the days: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and seventy-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and seventy-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and fourteen days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and eightieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, ninety-eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its fifty-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its sixty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its eighty-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one silent about a page of six names for a hundred and sixteen days. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a seventeenth week and it is the last day of that week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. A boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of the third column and his surname is on no page of this harbor. Mara Venn is on that deck, of no office, forty marks first after food standing whole. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**Four men went down on that ebb on the said Sunday, one at a time, and the whole of it was on four slates written before each of them went, and the air on every one of the four slates was a figure of forty minutes, and the same named tender stood on the surface of that boat for all four of them and not in the bell, with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.**

Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day. Nobody went in twice. And the reason for the four was written on the boat before any of them went and it is not a reason about any of them: there are fifteen frames on that floor and eight of the nine holes in the first of them had been looked at with a light, and the party had three days of air in front of it and no line opened for a fourth, and a man of about thirty-four of the second lay had said out loud that a man who does not know is not a man who has been careful, he is a man who has been lucky, and that a yard cannot spend luck.

They were down at about eight in the morning, and about a quarter to ten, and about eleven, and about twelve o'clock.

---

**The fourth of them was a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors, who is in nobody's crew and is a member of nothing and had been hired on that supply boat's word alone, and he came up at about half past twelve and he came up on the air and not on anything else.**

He came up, and he stood on that deck with his hand on the bell rope for about two minutes, and then he said a thing out loud on that deck that he had not been asked for and that nobody has since asked him about.

"I have been down there about nineteen minutes and I can tell you that I put a slate on the sixth channel and that I wrote something on it. I cannot tell you what I wrote on it. I can tell you that it was after the slate and not before it, because I can see the slate is wet and my own hand is not, and the two of those happened in that order this morning and I know that much. Everything between putting the slate down and the slate being wet has gone out of my head in the middle, and it did not go out of it slowly, and I have not read anything out of it yet because I do not have anything to read out."

Nobody asked him why. Nobody asked him what he thought had happened. Nobody asked him to describe it again and nobody asked him to write it down and nobody thanked him, and there is no figure set against it on any sheet in that boat or in that harbor.

He went back to the compressor at about one o'clock and worked it until about six and his hands were steady the whole time and the stove drew and there was no water in that bell on Monday.

**He did not go down again that day and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why he did not.**

---

Twenty fathoms of three-inch line went onto the sheet that carries the money at about two in the afternoon, being three hundred and forty pence, being two marks, one shilling and eightpence, and a second pair of hands for a day went onto it at about four in the afternoon off that supply boat, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and neither of those two lines has anything to do with what happened to the man who came up at half past twelve and no line has been opened for what happened to him.

The man who keeps the leaf said the position out loud on that after deck at about half past five in the afternoon, and it was five thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, and nobody argued with him and nobody was asked for the rest of it.

---

**THE SEVENTY-NINTH TIME THE WALL CAME DOWN BARE HAPPENED IN A HARBOR THAT PARTY WAS NOT IN, AND FOUR MEN WENT DOWN ON ONE EBB ON THE SEVENTH DAY OF NOVEMBER, ONE AT A TIME, UNDER FOUR SLATES WRITTEN BEFORE EACH OF THEM WENT, WITH A FIGURE OF FORTY MINUTES OF AIR ON EACH AND ONE NAMED TENDER ON THE SURFACE FOR ALL FOUR OF THEM AND A CUTOFF HE COULD PULL WITHOUT ASKING, AND NOBODY WENT IN TWICE AND NOBODY WENT IN AT THE TOP OF A FLOOD. THE FOURTH OF THEM CAME UP AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE ON THE AIR AND CAME UP SHORTER THAN HE WENT DOWN, AND HE SAID SO OUT LOUD ON THAT DECK AND HE WAS NOT ASKED WHY, WAS NOT ASKED TO SAY IT AGAIN, WAS NOT THANKED, IS NOT A CASUALTY AND CARRIES NO FIGURE AGAINST HIM, AND HE WENT BACK TO THE COMPRESSOR AT ABOUT ONE O'CLOCK AND WORKED IT UNTIL ABOUT SIX AND DID NOT GO DOWN AGAIN THAT DAY. TWENTY FATHOMS OF LINE AND A SECOND PAIR OF HANDS FOR A DAY WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AND NEITHER LINE HAS ANYTHING TO DO WITH HIM. AND THAT SHEET IS FIVE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT, BEING THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the seventh day of November next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning for the seventy-ninth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that four men went down on that ebb one at a time under four slates written before each of them went, with an air limit of forty minutes written on each slate and the same named tender on the surface of that boat and not in the bell for all four of them with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking, and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice; and that the reason for the four was written on that boat before any of them went and is not a reason about any of them, being that there are fifteen frames on that floor and eight of the nine holes in the first of them had been looked at with a light and that the party had three days of air in front of it and no line opened for a fourth; and that the four of them went down at about eight in the morning and about a quarter to ten and about eleven and about twelve o'clock; and that the fourth of them was a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors who is in nobody's crew and is a member of nothing and had been hired on that supply boat's word alone, and that he was in the water about thirty minutes and on the bottom about nineteen of them and came up at about half past twelve on the air and not on anything else; and that he came up and stood on that deck with his hand on the bell rope for about two minutes and said out loud that he can tell that he put a slate on the sixth channel and that he wrote something on it and that he cannot tell what he wrote on it, and that he can tell that it was after the slate and not before it because the slate is wet and his own hand is not, and that everything between putting the slate down and the slate being wet has gone out of his head in the middle; and that nobody asked him why, nobody asked him what he thought had happened, nobody asked him to describe it again, nobody asked him to write it down, and nobody thanked him, and that no figure is set against it on any sheet in that boat or in that harbor; and that he went back to the compressor at about one o'clock and worked it until about six and his hands were steady and he did not go down again that day and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why he did not; and that twenty fathoms of three-inch line went onto the fitting-out sheet at about two in the afternoon, being three hundred and forty pence, being two marks, one shilling and eightpence, and a second pair of hands for a day went onto it at about four in the afternoon, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and that neither line has anything to do with what happened to the man who came up at half past twelve and no line has been opened for what happened to him; and that the man who keeps the leaf said the position out loud on that after deck at about half past five in the afternoon and that it was five thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence; and that that sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and sixteen thousand five hundred and ninety pence out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody asked him why is in the yard's own mouth, and is that the question a man puts to a man who has come up out of a bell with a hole in the middle of a morning is almost always the question the asker would have asked himself, and that a yard that asks it is a yard that is looking for a shape to put on what happened instead of putting down what happened, and that this party is not going to be that yard on a Sunday; and that the reason what he said went into the second column and not the first is that it is a thing about a man's own head and there is no rule at all for what a man's own head does, and a column a stranger with a pencil can check is for things a stranger with a pencil can check; and that the reason the two lines that went onto that sheet on that day are not connected to him in the first column is in the yard's own mouth, and is that twenty fathoms of line and a day's second pair of hands are costs and a cost is not a payment and nothing was paid to that man and nothing is owed to him and he is not short; and that the reason four men went down on one ebb and one at a time was written on that boat before any of them went is that a working day at that mouth is about a hundred and five minutes of ebb in twenty-four hours and about two hours of it that are usable, and that four men at once in twenty-two fathoms is not four men working and is four men waiting in the dark; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Sunday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
