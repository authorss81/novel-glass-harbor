# Chapter 493

*Thursday the Twenty-Fifth Day of November, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Second Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Fitting-Out Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Nineteen Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Twenty-Two Marks, Twelve Shillings and Fourpence, a Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud on That After Deck at About Eleven in the Morning, in Front of All Nine of Them and Before He Had Said a Word to Anybody, That He Is Going to Leave the Best Evidence This Party Has Ever Had in Its Hands on the Bottom of That Trench in a Room Behind a Machine Three of Them Cannot Agree About, and Saying Also That It Is the Only Thing in That Room That Holds a Route Together, and Not Being Argued With and Not Being Thanked and Not Being Asked Whether He Had Thought About It, a Man of About Fifty-One Going Down at About Ten Past One on Air of the Twenty-Fourth and Adding a Line to One of the Two Slates Left Inside That Doorway on the Eleventh and Coming Up at About Ten to Two, and a Leaf Read Back at Half Past Nine in the Evening in the Ship's Own Left Hand With All Four Columns Including the Empty One and Read Twice Because About Four Men of That Party Cannot Read, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Thursday the twenty-fifth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and second time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty thousand three hundred and eight pence out, being one hundred and eighty-nine marks, five shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is nineteen thousand six hundred and sixty-eight pence short, being one hundred and twenty-two marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, and that figure has not moved since the close of the day before. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninety-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fifteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-three days. The tide gang in that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the middle of the summer. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered and nothing came in on the entry to move it. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head and no figure is set against it anywhere, and the holder of that writ cannot get his hands steady after a can of hot something and put a hand on a gunwale to get himself up off that deck, and no figure is set against that anywhere either and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**At about eleven in the morning on that after deck the holder of that writ said it out loud, in front of all nine of them, before he had said a word to anybody about it, and he said it once, and it took about four minutes, and nobody interrupted him and nobody asked him a question at the end of it.**

"I am going to leave it. That is my decision and it is mine and I have not asked anybody's advice and I am not going to be asked afterwards whether I had thought about it.

And I want two things said about it in my own mouth on this deck, because both of them are true and only one of them is flattering. The first is that it is the best evidence this party has ever had in its hands, and I am leaving it in the dark on the bottom of a trench in a room behind a machine that three of us cannot agree about.

The second is that it is the only thing in that room that holds a route together. Any man alive can come back to that room with that paper in it. The day that paper is in a crate on a boat, that room is a hole in a floor with nothing in it, and the next party down here is nine men with a line and a bell and no reason whatever to come back twice.

So the paper goes to the room and not to the boat, and the crate on that deck comes up empty, and the brown page under the weight on that tarpaulin comes up flat and goes on no sheet of any kind and is not going into the case with the rest. And I am telling you now, in front of all nine of you, that I am not using that room to decide where this boat goes tomorrow or the day after. The boat sails on the morrow at about seven whatever is on that floor and whatever is on that deck, and I have not looked at a line of it this morning and I am not going to look at a line of it this evening."

Nobody argued with him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody was asked to agree with him and nobody was asked whether he had thought about it, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the whole of it into the second column with the hour of eleven o'clock against it and did not ask him a single question about any part of it.

---

**And the crate that had been standing battened on that after deck since the seventeenth of November was carried to the rail at about half past eleven that morning and was not opened and was not turned over, and it went down into the forepeak and the batten across its lid is the batten that was put on there in that yard's own mouth on the seventeenth and has not been off since, and there is no word on any page of that harbor for what was going to be in it.**

The brown page lay flat under a weight on that tarpaulin and it did not move, and it was not folded and it was not cut, and it is on no sheet in that boat and it is on no sheet in that harbor, and it came up off that tarpaulin on the twenty-seventh flat under the same weight with a board over it and it did not go into the case.

---

**A slate was written before anybody was off that deck and it was written on the man who was going down and not by him, and the air on it was off the line of the twenty-fourth and the air limit on it was a figure of forty minutes, and the reason on it was in his own words.** Ivo Sark was the tender on the surface and the pump hand of about forty-four was on the compressor with him.

The man of about fifty-one, out of a coast town four days down, went down at about ten past one in the afternoon on that ebb under that slate, and he was in the water about ten minutes and in that bell and on that floor about thirty of them, and he came up at about ten to two. He wrote a line on the end of one of the two slates that were left inside that doorway on the eleventh of November, being that the room behind the wall and the paper in it stay as they are and that the party is not coming back for them, and he put the hour of the twenty-fifth of November against it and he put nothing else against it, and nobody told him what to write and nobody read it before it was written and nobody read it afterward.

Nobody thanked him. No figure is set against him. He is not a casualty and nobody has named him one. Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice.

---

**And the holder of that writ did not go down at all on that day, and that was said out loud once on that deck and was not argued with, and the reason was his own and it was in the second column and it is not in this one.**

"I would go down if you put the reason in front of me and asked me to write it myself," he said, at about one o'clock, "and I am not going down, because I got up off that deck with two hands at about ten to two yesterday and I am not a man who goes into twenty-two fathoms this week. The man who has been in holds nineteen years is going down because he carried the paper up and I am not going to pretend that I sent him because I could not be bothered."

---

**And at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, in the open, in the yard's own mouth, all four columns including the empty one, the leaf was read back, and it was read twice because about four men of that party cannot read.**

"There are two hundred and twenty-five entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and twenty-six at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and forty-three have gone in on the forty-three days from Wednesday the thirteenth of October to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them, there being one day in between that is the twelfth day of November and that is a day nobody is in. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it.

The second column has one thousand and two lines in it at this hour and one thousand and four at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and five have gone in on each of those same forty-three days since, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the two hundred and fifteen that have gone in, two hundred and ten went in on the forty days before this one and five went in on the Wednesday, and two hundred and fifteen less two is two hundred and thirteen, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and thirteen is one thousand and two. That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and twenty-five and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. **Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments.**

The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since.

And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these forty-three days and it has taken no line in the ten days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

Nobody argued with him. Nobody was asked for the rest of it. The four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and twenty-six, one thousand and four, none and one hundred and twenty-five.

---

**THE BEST EVIDENCE THIS PARTY HAS EVER HAD IN ITS HANDS IS ON THE BOTTOM OF THAT TRENCH IN THE RACKS IN A ROOM BEHIND A MACHINE THAT THREE PEOPLE HAVE SAID OUT LOUD WHAT IT IS AND CANNOT AGREE ABOUT, AND IT IS ALSO THE ONLY THING IN THAT ROOM THAT HOLDS A ROUTE TOGETHER, AND BOTH OF THOSE FACTS WERE SAID BY THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING ON THAT DECK IN FRONT OF ALL NINE OF THEM, IN HIS OWN MOUTH, AND BY NOBODY ELSE, AND HE WAS NOT ARGUED WITH AND WAS NOT THANKED AND WAS NOT ASKED WHETHER HE HAD THOUGHT ABOUT IT. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-ONE WENT DOWN AT ABOUT TEN PAST ONE AND ADDED A LINE TO ONE OF THE TWO SLATES LEFT INSIDE THAT DOORWAY ON THE ELEVENTH AND CAME UP AT ABOUT TEN TO TWO, AND NOBODY TOLD HIM WHAT TO WRITE. THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT DID NOT GO DOWN ON THAT DAY. NOBODY WENT INTO THAT MOUTH AT THE TOP OF A FLOOD AND NOBODY WENT IN TWICE. THAT SHEET IS NINETEEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the best evidence in this party's hands is being left on the bottom is the holder's own and was said out loud in his own mouth in the open on that deck in front of all nine of them, and is that a paper in a room is a door, and that a paper in a crate is a lock, and that this yard has three claims standing against it and is not going to carry a fourth one made out of a room at twenty-two fathoms that only this party knows the way into; and that the reason the two facts of it were both said out loud and neither of them was softened is his own, and is that a man who leaves the best thing he has ever found in the dark and says only that it is a burden gets thanked for it inside a week, and that a man who says it is a burden and says also that it is a way in gets argued with instead, and that he would rather be argued with on a Tuesday than thanked on a Saturday; and that the reason a man of about fifty-one carried that line down and not the man who decided it is his own, and is that a man who cannot get his hands steady after a can of hot something has no business at twenty-two fathoms, and that the man who carried the paper up that room in the first place is the one who can be trusted to write four lines on a slate and come back; and that the reason nobody was asked to agree with the decision is the yard's own mouth, and is that a party of nine that takes a vote on whether to leave the only copy of a thing in the world is a party that has turned a hold into a rule, and that this yard found out what that is on the sixth day of November and has not got over it; and that the reason the crate is going up empty and battened is that woman's of the twenty-second of November, and is that a crate carried ashore in a hand is a thing one man has, and an empty crate carried ashore in a hand is a thing nine men did, and that nobody on that deck is to be told by an inspector in that harbor which of the two they were carrying.*
