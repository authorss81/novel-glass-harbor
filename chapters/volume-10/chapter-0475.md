# Chapter 475

*Saturday the Sixth Day of November, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, Four Days of Air Hired off That Same Supply Boat at Seven Hundred and Twenty Pence, Being Four Marks, Six Shillings and Eightpence, with Three of the Four Days in Front of That Party and No Line Opened for a Fifth, a Man of About Twenty-Nine Going Down on the Ebb at About Ten Past Nine and Coming Up at About Half Past Ten With a Cold in His Teeth and a Taste of Iron Like a Nail and a Grayness in the Middle of the Morning That Did Not Lift Until About Two in the Afternoon and No Figure Set Against It, a Boundary Written on the Twenty-Second Day of October Past About a Piece of Paper Read Out Again on That Deck at About Two in the Afternoon in Front of All Nine of Them and Not in a Room With Two People in It and Reached Exactly as Far as It Reached on the Twenty-Second and Not One Inch Further, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying One Thing About That Out Loud and Nobody Asked to Agree With It and Nobody Thanked, a Pump Hand of About Forty-Four Asking Out Loud What the Rule Is for That Thing Down There and Being Told Out Loud That There Is Not One and That It Is a Hole and Not a Rule, and Not One Word on That Deck About How Many of Anything There Would Have to Be and Not Answered, a Crate With a Blank in It Standing on That Deck With a Batten Across Its Lid and Not Opened and Not Read and Not Named, the Fitting-Out Sheet Five Thousand Four Hundred and Forty-Two Pence Short, Being Thirty-Four Marks and Twopence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Saturday the sixth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in that harbor and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on that deck and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf was under her own coat. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and seventy-three days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and seventy-five days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a seventeenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and thirteen days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventy-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in ninety-seven days. A man of about fifty-one in that harbor has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and fifteen days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the fifty-second day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the sixty-seventh day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the eighty-fourth day. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head and it is not a motive in anything he does and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, went down at about ten past nine in the morning on that ebb under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that nine holes had been counted in one frame and that eight of them had not been looked at with a light since the Sunday, and the air limit was a figure of forty minutes and the tender was named and was on the surface of that boat and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.

He was in the water about forty minutes and on the bottom about twenty-six of them and came up at about half past ten.

He came up with a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron like a nail and a grayness in the middle of the morning that would not lift until about two in the afternoon, and it did not lift because anybody said something to him about it, and he worked the winch from about eleven until about five and nobody thanked him and no figure is set against it anywhere.

Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on the said day and nobody went in twice.

Four days of air were hired off that supply boat at about eleven in the morning, being seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and went onto the sheet that carries the money with the sixth day of November against it, and the party had three of those four days in front of it and no line has been opened for a fifth and nobody has said out loud that there will not be one.

---

**At about two in the afternoon on that deck, in the open, in front of all nine of them, a woman of thirty of no office said one thing about the line she wrote on the twenty-second day of October past, and it was not said in a room and it was not said to one person, and nobody was asked to agree with it and nobody was thanked.**

"The line says that an echo may inform a public record and it may not decide anything this party does. I wrote it on the twenty-second in a shed in that harbor and put my name and the hour under it, and on the twenty-fifth a woman read it out twice in a room over a fish market and a man who sells rope said out loud that he did not agree with it, and he was right to say it, and nobody argued with him, and that all happened before this boat ever left the roads.

It was written about a piece of paper and about something that answered a piece of paper. **It does not say one word about a machine on a floor, and I am not going to stretch it until it does.** The day I stretch a line that was written for one thing until it covers another is the day that line is not worth the name and the hour under it. So the line stands. It reaches exactly as far as it reached on the twenty-second day of October and not one inch further. What has happened since then is that there is a different sort of thing on the bottom of a trench, and that the different sort of thing is not covered by that line.

I have said that out loud in front of all of you and it goes in the second column with the hour against it and nobody is asked to agree with it and nobody is thanked for it."

Calder Marr said one sentence back and it was the only sentence he said on that deck about it.

"Then it stands where it stands and it does not move, and I am not asking you to move it, and I am not going to pretend to you that what I said at half past five yesterday about the reason of this party came out of that line. It came out of a frame on a floor."

---

**And then a pump hand of about forty-four with nineteen years of compressors, who is in nobody's crew and is a member of nothing, asked out loud on that same deck what the rule was for that thing down there, and was answered out loud, and the answer was that there was not one.**

"Then I am the man who decides it with my mouth," he said, "and so is every man on this boat, and I do not mind saying that out loud because you cannot see it from the mole and I would rather it was said in the open. The paper cannot decide it. That line cannot decide it. Nine men in twenty-two fathoms decide it with their mouths and the best of those nine wins and nobody on shore ever finds out which one won."

"Yes," Calder Marr said, "and I am telling you now, on this deck, where it is about two o'clock in the afternoon and there is nothing on that floor but a floor, that that is a hole and not a rule, and that I am not going to paper over it by writing a second line and calling it the first one. When this boat is in a harbor again there will be a room and there will be about eleven people in that room who cannot read and I am going to stand in that room and be told no, and that is not this week and I am not going to pretend to you that it is this month."

Nobody argued with him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody wrote it on the fitting-out sheet, which carries money and not rules, and the sheet with the four heads on it is on a wall in a room over a fish market two days' sail from that deck and it was not read out on that day and it was not changed by anything any of them said.

---

**The crate is on that deck and it has been on that deck since the twenty-ninth day of October past, and nobody has opened it and there is no word on any page of that harbor for what is inside it.**

It was carried into that harbor on the evening tide of the twenty-fourth day of October and it stood in a room off that market for five days, and nobody in that harbor opened it, and it came back out on that same boat on the twenty-ninth with the party, on no line of any sheet, because a crate is a crate and a boat has a deck.

There is a blanket round it and a batten across the lid and the batten has not been off it since the thirty-first day of October past, and the day is on the crate in that yard's own mouth, and it is not read, and it is not named, and nobody has asked anybody to name it, and that is the whole of the sixth day of November on that subject.

---

**FOUR DAYS OF AIR WERE HIRED AT SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE, BEING FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THREE OF THEM ARE IN FRONT OF THAT PARTY AND NO LINE HAS BEEN OPENED FOR A FIFTH. A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE CAME UP OFF THAT FLOOR WITH A COLD IN HIS TEETH AND A TASTE OF IRON LIKE A NAIL AND A GRAYNESS IN THE MIDDLE OF THE MORNING THAT DID NOT LIFT UNTIL ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON, AND HE WORKED THE WINCH FROM ELEVEN UNTIL FIVE AND NOBODY THANKED HIM AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST IT. THE LINE A WOMAN OF THIRTY WROTE ON THE TWENTY-SECOND DAY OF OCTOBER PAST WAS SAID AGAIN IN THE OPEN ON THAT DECK AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON BEFORE ALL NINE OF THEM, AND IT REACHED EXACTLY AS FAR AS IT REACHED ON THE TWENTY-SECOND AND NOT ONE INCH FURTHER, AND A PUMP HAND ASKED OUT LOUD WHAT THE RULE WAS FOR THAT THING DOWN THERE AND WAS TOLD OUT LOUD THAT THERE IS NOT ONE AND THAT IT IS A HOLE AND NOT A RULE. THE CRATE ON THAT DECK HAS NOT BEEN OPENED AND THERE IS NO WORD FOR WHAT IS IN IT ON ANY PAGE OF THAT HARBOR. AND THAT SHEET IS FIVE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING THIRTY-FOUR MARKS AND TWOPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the sixth day of November next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a man of about twenty-nine went down at about ten past nine in the morning on that ebb in about twenty-two fathoms under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and that he was in the water about forty minutes and on the bottom about twenty-six of them and came up at about half past ten; and that he came up with a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron like a nail and a grayness in the middle of the morning that did not lift until about two in the afternoon, and that he worked the winch from about eleven until about five and was not thanked and no figure is set against it; and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice; and that four days of air were hired off that supply boat at about eleven in the morning, being seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and went onto the fitting-out sheet with the sixth day of November against it, and that three of those four days are in front of that party and that no line has been opened for a fifth; and that a woman of thirty of no office said one thing out loud on that deck at about two in the afternoon in front of all nine of them about a line she had written on the twenty-second day of October past, and that the line reaches exactly as far as it reached on the twenty-second and not one inch further, and that it does not say one word about a machine on a floor, and that nobody was asked to agree with it and nobody was thanked; and that the holder of that writ said one sentence back, being that the line stands and does not move and that what he had said on the previous afternoon about the reason of that party came out of a frame on a floor and not out of that line; and that a pump hand of about forty-four asked out loud what the rule was for that thing down there and was answered out loud that there is not one and that it is a hole and not a rule, and that no line was opened for it and the fitting-out sheet carries money and not rules; and that the sheet with the four heads on it is on a wall in a room over a fish market two days' sail from that deck and was not read out on that day and was not changed by anything any of them said; and that the crate is on that deck and has been since the twenty-ninth day of October past, that it was carried into that harbor on the evening tide of the twenty-fourth and stood in a room off that market for five days and was not opened by anybody in that harbor, and that it came back out on that same boat on the twenty-ninth on no line of any sheet, and that the batten across its lid has not been off since the thirty-first day of October past, and that there is no word on any page of that harbor for what is inside it; and that the fitting-out sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and sixteen thousand and eighty-two pence out and is five thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being thirty-four marks and twopence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the line was said again in the open on that deck at about two in the afternoon in front of all nine of them and not in a room with two people in it is the yard's own mouth, and is that a rule that has been stretched once in private is a rule that has been stretched, and that the only place a person can be watched not stretching it is a place with eight other people in it; and that the reason a woman of thirty did not write a second line on the sixth day of November is her own, and is that the name and the hour under the first line mean something only while the first line still says what it said on the day she signed it, and that a second line that quietly covers a machine on a floor would leave her name and the twenty-second of October standing over a sentence she did not write; and that the reason the answer given to the pump hand was that there is no rule and that it is a hole is in the holder's own mouth, and is that nine men on a boat deciding a thing with their mouths is what a line of that kind exists to stop, and that a rule which only holds where somebody can see a man hold it to it is not a rule but a custom, and that this yard has two customs and one rule and it is going to find out which of the two it is when it stands in a room; and that the reason the crate travelled into that harbor and back out again on no line of any sheet is in the yard's own mouth, and is that the sheet carries what people did with their own money and a crate is not that, and that the batten has been on it since the thirty-first day of October and nobody has asked for it to be off and no figure has been asked of anybody about it; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
