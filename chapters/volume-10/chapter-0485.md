# Chapter 485

*Wednesday the Seventeenth Day of November, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, a Crate and Matting and a Lid for What Came Off That Floor Going onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Eleven in the Morning at Two Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Being One Mark and Nine Shillings, and Four Fresh Days of Air Hired off That Same Supply Boat Going onto the Same Sheet at About the Same Hour at Seven Hundred and Twenty Pence, Being Four Marks, Six Shillings and Eightpence, and Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand, Three Descents on That Hired Line and Not on Any Other, One at a Time, Each under a Slate with a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff He Could Pull Without Asking, One Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Going Down at About Ten Past Nine and Giving a Sound, a Man of About Twenty-Nine Going Down at About Ten Past Ten and Giving a Movement, and a Woman of About Thirty-One Going Down at About Twenty Past Eleven and Giving What Was in the Air, the Three of Them Asked on Their Own and Not in Front of Each Other and the Three of Them Not Agreeing and None of Them a Version of the Other Two, the Result of the Three Being More Complete Than Any One of Them and Not a Recording and Not a Verdict and Not Checkable by a Stranger with a Pencil, All Three Going into the Second Column in the Order They Were Said and None of the Three Asked to Give It Better, Nobody Thanked, the Shortfall at Twelve Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Six Pence, Being Seventy-Eight Marks, Twelve Shillings and Twopence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the seventeenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. A crate and matting and a lid for what came off that floor went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and nine shillings, and four fresh days of air were hired off the same supply boat and went onto the same sheet at about the same hour at seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that line bears the seventeenth of November and no other date, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at twenty-three thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence out, being one hundred and forty-five marks, five shillings and sixpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twelve thousand six hundred and twenty-six pence short, being seventy-eight marks, twelve shillings and twopence. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty-three days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-five; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty-three days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and eighty-ninth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and seven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixty-second day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-seventh; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-fourth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-five days. Forty marks first after food stand whole against a woman of thirty of no office and are neither reduced nor paid, and she is not restored and is not forgiven. The hard at Ashtide in that harbor is unanswered. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does, and it went on his own slate on the fifteenth at his own asking.**

---

**Four fresh days of air came off that supply boat at about eleven in the morning in a rack of cylinders with the wear on the necks of them, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the line on that sheet with the seventeenth of November against it and said out loud that the four days were the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth, and that the ninth of November was the last of the earlier four and that nothing had been opened under that figure since and that he was not going to open anything now for the twenty-first or the twenty-second because nobody had asked him for it.**

**And Ivo Sark, of about twenty-four, one of the master's two men, was the tender on the surface for the whole of the three, and he had his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave, and he was not in that bell at any hour of that morning, and the pump hand of about forty-four was on the compressor with him and neither of those two men went into that mouth at all.**

---

**The three went down one at a time, in the order the slates were written in, and each of them was asked on his own in the cabin with the door shut before he went, and each of them was told that he could stop it, and each of them said out loud that he did not intend to, and each of them was asked what he was going to give up and said out loud that he was giving it up when he had said it.**

**Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, went down at about ten past nine in the morning on that ebb under the slate written on the fifteenth with the sentence at the head of it and his own line under it, and the air limit on it was a figure of forty minutes, and the air was out of cylinders hired off that supply boat on that day at the figure on that sheet. He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten o'clock.**

**He gave a sound, and he gave it once, and he would not give it a second time.**

"There is a knocking in there. Nine strokes and then nothing, and the nine are about the length of a heartbeat and they are not even, and the eighth is shorter than the other eight. And I will tell you what it is not. It is not coming out of that wall and it is not coming out of that floor and there is nothing in that room that can make it. I have stood in a hold with a sounding pole in it and I know what a hold sounds like and this is not it. **Whatever it is, it is coming from further off than that room, and that is all I have and I would not be able to say it again this evening.**"

**Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, went down at about ten past ten under the same slate and the same air and the same tender and the same cutoff, and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty of them and came up at about eleven o'clock. He was asked on his own and he was told he could stop it and he said out loud that he did not intend to.**

"I am giving a movement and it is this. Something on the far side of that back wall came down about a foot and stopped. I did not see it move. I heard it. And it is not the water, because I have watched that water come up about two feet in the twenty minutes after a flood on that trench for ten days and I know exactly what it sounds like when it comes and that was not it. And it is late. It is late on something else. There was a gap in it — the knocking had stopped and then there was a space and then that came — and I am the only man on that deck who can tell you the size of that gap, because I was the only one of us with nothing to do but count, and **I do not know what was in the gap and I am not going to stand here and put something in it.**"

**Sabra Loach, of about thirty-one, went down at about twenty past eleven under the same slate and the same air and the same tender and the same cutoff, and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about ten past twelve. She was asked on her own and she was told she could stop it and she said out loud that she did not intend to.**

"I am giving the air. There was water in it in there. Not sea and not a drain. It is the smell of a stone floor that has had a roof over it for a very long time and has never had a fire in it, and there was a sweetness in it the way there is a sweetness in a cellar that has been shut since the last time anybody was in it. And the sweetness came and went about four times while I stood there. I want to be exact, because the man of about fifty-one is going to say that is the paper. **I do not know that it is the paper, and he does not know that, and I have not asked him and he has not offered.** The man who has been in holds nineteen years is standing on this deck and he has not said one word to me about it, and that is the reason I can stand here and say that I do not know."

---

**And the three of them do not agree, and they were not made to.**

**Trevask's account is that the room is shut and that the knocking is coming from outside it, past the wall, from further off, and that there is nothing in that room that could make a noise. Sabra Loach's account is that the room is not shut, because the sweetness came and went four times in about twenty minutes in a way that water does not come and go in a room that has one opening. Amos Trewin's account is that the back wall of that room is thin enough that something behind it came down about a foot, and that whatever it was came after the knocking and not with it, and that the gap in between is the only part of the whole of it that anybody on that deck is certain about.**

**Put the three together and there is more in the three than there is in any one of them, and what there is is a clock: about nine strokes, then a space, then something settling, then a change in the air, and the whole of it inside about two minutes, and not one of the three of them knew at the time that the other two were carrying the other two parts of it. That is not a finding. It is three accounts that agree about the order of four things and disagree entirely about what the four things are, and there is no part of it a stranger with a pencil can take off a page, because every part of it is a thing that one man heard with his own ears or felt in his own body, and none of the three was asked to give it better and none of the three was asked to say it again and none of the three was thanked.**

All three went into the second column of that leaf in the order they were said, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote at the foot of them that the party does not know what is behind that wall and is not going to find out on this trip.

---

**And the crate came on that after deck at about half past two in the afternoon with matting under it and a lid on it, and it stood there and nobody said one word about it except the man who keeps the leaf, who read the line off the sheet with the day against it and the figure against it and put the sheet down and did not say what was going in it, and nobody asked him and nobody said it to him.**

---

**THREE PEOPLE WENT DOWN INTO THAT ROOM ONE AT A TIME ON THAT WEDNESDAY ON FOUR FRESH DAYS OF AIR HIRED OFF THAT SUPPLY BOAT AT SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE ON THAT SHEET WITH THE SEVENTEENTH OF NOVEMBER AGAINST IT, UNDER THE SENTENCE WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF A PAGE ON THE FIFTEENTH, AND EACH WAS TOLD HE MIGHT STOP IT AND EACH SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE DID NOT INTEND TO, AND EACH GAVE UP WHAT HE WAS CARRYING WHEN HE HAD SAID IT. ONE GAVE A SOUND, ONE GAVE A MOVEMENT AND ONE GAVE WHAT WAS IN THE AIR, AND THE THREE OF THEM DO NOT AGREE. THE THREE OF THEM TOGETHER HAVE A CLOCK AND NOBODY ELSE HAS IT, AND IT IS NOT A FINDING AND IT IS NOT A RECORDING AND IT IS NOT A VERDICT AND NO PART OF IT CAN BE CHECKED FROM A MOLE. ALL THREE WENT INTO THE SECOND COLUMN IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SAID AND NONE WAS ASKED TO GIVE IT BETTER AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. A CRATE AND MATTING AND A LID WENT ON THAT SHEET AT TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE. AND THAT SHEET IS TWELVE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING SEVENTY-EIGHT MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the seventeenth day of November at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all; and that a crate and matting and a lid for what came off that floor went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at two hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and nine shillings, and that four fresh days of air hired off that same supply boat went onto the same sheet at about the same hour at seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that the line bears the seventeenth of November and no other date, and that the four days are the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a man of about twenty-four, one of the master's two men, was the tender on the surface for the whole of the three with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave, and that he was not in that bell at any hour of that morning, and that the pump hand of about forty-four was on the compressor with him and neither of those two men went into that mouth at all; and that the three went down one at a time in the order the slates were written in, and that each was asked on his own in the cabin with the door shut before he went and was told he could stop it and said out loud that he did not intend to, and that each was asked what he was going to give up and said out loud that he was giving it up when he had said it; and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay went down at about ten past nine in the morning on that ebb under the slate written on the fifteenth with a figure of forty minutes of air on it, and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten o'clock, and gave a sound, once, and would not give it a second time, being a knocking of about nine strokes of about the length of a heartbeat and not even, with the eighth shorter than the other eight, and not coming out of that wall and not coming out of that floor and coming from further off than that room, and there being nothing in that room that can make it; and that the man of about twenty-nine went down at about ten past ten under the same slate and the same air and the same tender and the same cutoff and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty of them and came up at about eleven o'clock, and gave a movement, being something on the far side of that back wall coming down about a foot and stopping, heard and not seen, and not the water, and late on the knocking with a space in between, and the man of about twenty-nine saying out loud that he does not know what was in the space and is not going to put something in it; and that the woman of about thirty-one went down at about twenty past eleven under the same slate and the same air and the same tender and the same cutoff and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about ten past twelve, and gave what was in the air, being water in the air in that room, not sea and not a drain, the smell of a stone floor that has had a roof over it for a very long time and has never had a fire in it, and a sweetness in it the way there is a sweetness in a cellar shut since the last time anybody was in it, and that the sweetness came and went about four times while she stood there, and that she said out loud that she does not know that the sweetness is the paper and that the man of about fifty-one does not know that either and that she has not asked him and he has not offered; and that the three of them do not agree, and that the man of about thirty-four's account is that the room is shut and the knocking is coming from outside it past the wall, the woman of about thirty-one's account is that the room is not shut because the sweetness came and went four times in about twenty minutes, and the man of about twenty-nine's account is that the back wall of that room is thin enough that something behind it came down about a foot and that whatever it was came after the knocking and not with it; and that put together the three of them have about nine strokes, then a space, then something settling, then a change in the air, inside about two minutes, and not one of the three knew at the time that the other two were carrying the other two parts of it, and that that is not a finding and is not a recording and is not a verdict and no part of it can be taken off a page by a stranger with a pencil, and that none of the three was asked to give it better and none was asked to say it again and none was thanked; and that all three went into the second column in the order they were said, and that the man who keeps the leaf wrote at the foot of them that the party does not know what is behind that wall and is not going to find out on this trip; and that a crate and matting and a lid came on that after deck at about half past two in the afternoon and stood there and nobody said one word about it and nobody asked the man who keeps the leaf what was going in it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason three people were asked for one thing each that they could carry, and not asked what they thought was behind that wall, is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a party of nine which asks three of its own what is behind a wall has a finding by the middle of the afternoon and does not have three people in it, and that the party went down there to look at a floor and what it found behind that wall is not that party's to decide on a Wednesday; and that the reason the three were asked on their own and not in front of each other is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a man who has heard the other two say their two things first will say his third thing to agree with them or to beat them, and that a deck is a bad place to be second in, and that a cabin with a door shut is the only place on that boat where a man can be first in something; and that the reason each of them was told before he went that he could stop it, and asked what he intended to do about stopping it, and gave that answer before he gave any other, is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a man who answers the easy question first and the real one second has already decided, and that the order of two answers on a morning is the whole of what anybody can be told about a man before he goes over a side; and that the reason no line was opened for the twenty-first or the twenty-second of November is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that four days were hired on the seventeenth and the four days are the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth, and that a man who opens a fifth day in front of nine men opens it for a reason he has not got, and that the twenty-first and the twenty-second are a different question and are not this one's; and that the reason the woman of about thirty-one said out loud that the man of about fifty-one had not said one word to her about the sweetness is her own, and is that a man with nineteen years in holds will tell anybody what a smell is if he knows it, and that he did not know it, and that his not saying it is worth more to her than his saying it, and that she wanted the deck to have that in the yard's own mouth in case anybody came up afterwards and said he had assured her; and that the reason not one line of that harbor's standing account was altered on the said Wednesday is the yard's own mouth, and is that forty marks first after food stand whole against a woman of thirty of no office on that Wednesday as they stood on the Tuesday and will stand on the Friday, and that the hard at Ashtide is unanswered on a Wednesday as well as on a Tuesday, and that a crate at one mark and nine shillings is a good night's sleep for two men and that nobody on that deck got one out of it and nobody asked.*
