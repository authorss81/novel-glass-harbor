# Chapter 494

*Friday the Twenty-Sixth Day of November, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Fitting-Out Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Nineteen Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Twenty-Two Marks, Twelve Shillings and Fourpence, a Man of About Fifty-One Going Down at About Ten Past Ten in the Morning on the Third of Five Days of Air Bought off That Same Supply Boat on the Twenty-Fourth and Coming Up at About Ten to Eleven with One Sheet of Paper Out of a Bundle of About Forty That Was in a Case on That Floor About Four Foot Off the Room With the Racks in It and That Was Not Cut and Not Wet and Not Named, a Date on It and a Place on It and a Line Ruled for a Seal About Two Inches Long With an Impression Pressed Into the Paper Beside It and Broken at the Top Edge, and a Custody History in Three Hands in Three Inks That a Stranger with a Leaf and a Pencil Can Check in About a Minute, a Signature at the Foot of the Second Hand Being the Name of an Examiner of an Office That Rebuilt That Machine Three Hundred Years After a Man of About Thirty-Four Said It Was Six Centuries Old, and Four Things Said Out Loud on That Deck at About Two in the Afternoon That It Does Not Show, the First of Them Being Why, Nobody Thanked, a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Getting Under Way at About Four in the Afternoon With the Chamber with a Stove in It Left Standing Where It Has Stood Since the Thirty-First Day of October Past, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Friday the twenty-sixth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty thousand three hundred and eight pence out, being one hundred and eighty-nine marks, five shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is nineteen thousand six hundred and sixty-eight pence short, being one hundred and twenty-two marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, and that figure has not moved since the Wednesday. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-two days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-four; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-two days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninety-eighth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and sixteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventy-first day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-sixth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and third; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-four days. There is one berth short on the third lay in that harbor and has been since the middle of the summer. Nineteen winters are disputed in that harbor and have been disputed for nineteen years and not one of them has been paid. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head and no figure is set against it anywhere, and the holder of that writ still cannot get his hands steady and put a hand on a gunwale to get himself up off that deck, and no figure is set against that anywhere either.**

---

**A slate was written before anybody was off that deck and it was written on the man who was going down and not by him, and the air on it was the third of the five days bought off that same supply boat on the twenty-fourth, and the air limit on it was a figure of forty minutes.** Ivo Sark was the tender on the surface and the pump hand of about forty-four was on the compressor with him, and neither of those two men went down.

The man of about fifty-one went down at about ten past ten in the morning on that ebb and was in the water about ten minutes and in that bell and on that floor about thirty of them, and he came up at about ten to eleven.

**He came up and he said the whole of what he had done before he took his helmet off, and nobody had told him to do it and nobody asked him a question about it, and he said it once.**

"It was not in that room with the racks. It was about four foot off it, on the other side, against the north wall, under a lid, and it was a bundle of about forty sheets tied with a cord that had gone to a hard grey colour, and they were not wet and they were not soft and the cord was under the lid and the lid was on top of them.

I took one out of the forty and I put the other thirty-nine back in and I put the cord back as I found it and I shut the lid, and that is the whole of what I did down there this morning and nobody asked me to do it. It came up in the front of my jersey and it has not been cut and it is not a piece of anything, it is a whole sheet, and there is nothing wet about it."

Nobody thanked him. No figure is set against him anywhere. He is not a casualty and nobody has named him one.

---

**And it lay on that tarpaulin at about half past eleven that morning with four stones on the corners, and it was the first time in six weeks that anything anybody on that boat could read had come up out of that trench, and about nine of them could read.**

It is folded twice and it is about nine inches by six. On the top half, in a small upright hand with a rule ruled under the head and a column of six short strokes ruled down the left of the body, there are two lines and the head and the strokes. Beside the head there is a place, written in the same hand, being the name of a port and not the name of this harbor. And there is a date, being the fourteenth day of a month that the clerk of that office called by a name this coast does not use. **The year is not on the face of it at all, because the year of that office was kept in a register in another building, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can find every part of that on the face of the paper in about a minute.**

To the right of the head there is a line ruled about two inches long and nothing on it in ink. **Beside the middle of that line there is an impression pressed into the paper, about the size of a thumbnail, and the impression is broken at the top edge where the paper has been folded once across it, and the fold has gone through the impression, and the impression on the two sides of the fold does not match up.** Nobody on that deck said the name of the thing that made that impression and nobody has said it since and it is not going to be said in that harbor.

At the foot of the sheet, in a second and larger hand, in an ink that is brown where the first is black, there is an endorsement of one line and a rule under it, and at the right of that endorsement there is a name: **Ilya Serr, being an examiner of that office, and the office is the one whose engineers rebuilt the frame on that floor three hundred years after a man of about thirty-four said out loud on that deck that the frame under the fittings was six centuries old.**

And down the left margin, in a third hand, in a green ink, there are four words and a short stroke under them, being that the sheet was received into that port's ferry file, and there is a third of the hand and a third of the ink and a third of the date, and that is the whole of the custody history and it is in three hands and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of them off the face of the paper in about a minute and find that they are three and not two.

---

**And at about two in the afternoon on that deck the man of about fifty-one said the thing that had to be said out loud, and it was the one description of that sheet that was going to get itself into a record if nobody stopped it, and Calder Marr stopped it, and the stopping was recorded and was paid for in the same quarter of an hour.**

"That is a Crown signature on a transfer out of that ferry's file and that is the end of it," the man of about fifty-one said. "That is the office that put that thing together three hundred years ago putting its name to taking the evidence away from anybody who could have read it. Put that down."

"Put down what he said and then put down the other four things, because only one of them is going to be in a sentence somebody writes in a fortnight," Calder Marr said. "Say the rest of it, all of it, and I will write it and you can stop me at any word.

"It does not show why. There is a name at the foot of an endorsement and there is no line of writing anywhere on that sheet with a reason at the head of it, and a man who signed a transfer in a bad month is the same man who signed it in a good one, and this paper cannot tell the two apart and neither can anybody standing here.

"It does not show that the man whose name is on it knew what was in the case. There is nothing on the face of that sheet about what was in the case. There is a line for a seal and a sheet in a file and a bundle of about forty leaves in a case with a lid on it, and the lid was shut before I was born.

"It does not name anybody as the author of a lie, and it is not a confession, and anybody in a room over a fish market who stands up tomorrow and says that this paper proves a man sat down and wrote a falsehood has been told more by this yard than this yard has been told by the paper.

"And it is a piece of paper. It does not consent. It cannot be asked anything. It does not testify. It is a thing somebody wrote and it is the best thing anybody on that deck has seen in six weeks, and it is going into a case with a lock on it and it is not going into a hand."

**The man of about fifty-one said one word back, which was that he would not argue, and Calder Marr read the whole of the four back to him word for word off his own slate before the man of about fifty-one went below, and Nessa Pike entered all of it in the minute with the hour of two o'clock against it and read it back to the man who keeps the leaf and he did not ask to read it.**

Nobody was thanked. Nobody was asked to be thanked. Nobody was put in a room over a fish market as a name and a seal and an office, and the name on that paper is a name on a paper and it is not in this boat.

---

**At about four in the afternoon that boat got under way, and the chamber with a stove in it is standing where it has stood since the thirty-first day of October past on that floor with its stove drawn out and its hatch shut, and nobody is going back for it, and nobody has been given a key to it that anybody else does not have, and no name has been put on it.**

"That is a good run for a boat of that size off that coast in that month," the man who keeps the leaf said, at about five o'clock, to nobody in particular, "and it is out of a figure of two days, and a good passage is not a skill and is not going into any column of mine as one."

And because that day was the day a sheet of paper came up out of that trench with three hands on it, the four columns were read back that evening as they are on every reading night of that leaf, and there was no other reading on that boat for six weeks.

"There are two hundred and twenty-six entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and twenty-seven at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and forty-four have gone in on the forty-four days from Wednesday the thirteenth of October to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them, there being one day in between that is the twelfth day of November and that is a day nobody is in. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it.

The second column has one thousand and seven lines in it at this hour and one thousand and nine at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and five have gone in on each of those same forty-four days since, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the two hundred and twenty that have gone in, two hundred and fifteen went in on the forty-three days before this one and five went in on the Friday, and two hundred and twenty less two is two hundred and eighteen, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and eighteen is one thousand and seven. That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and twenty-six and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. **Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments.**

The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since.

And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these forty-four days and it has taken no line in the ten days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

The four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and twenty-seven, one thousand and nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five. The four columns at the hour of that evening's entry stood at two hundred and twenty-six, one thousand and seven, none and one hundred and twenty-five.

**A SHEET OF PAPER CAME UP OUT OF A CASE UNDER A LID ABOUT FOUR FOOT OFF THAT ROOM AT ABOUT TWENTY TO ELEVEN ON THAT FRIDAY MORNING AND IT WAS NOT CUT AND IT WAS NOT WET, AND IT CARRIES A DATE AND A PLACE AND A LINE RULED ABOUT TWO INCHES LONG WITH AN IMPRESSION BESIDE IT BROKEN BY A FOLD THAT GOES THROUGH IT, AND IT CARRIES A CUSTODY HISTORY IN THREE HANDS IN THREE INKS THAT ANYBODY CAN CHECK IN ABOUT A MINUTE, AND A NAME AT THE FOOT OF THE SECOND HAND. IT SHOWS THAT THE OFFICE THAT REBUILT THAT MACHINE ALSO SIGNED A TRANSFER OF EVIDENCE OUT OF THAT FERRY'S FILE. IT DOES NOT SHOW WHY. IT DOES NOT SHOW THAT THE MAN NAMED UNDERSTOOD WHAT WAS IN THE CASE. IT DOES NOT NAME ANYBODY AS THE AUTHOR OF A LIE AND IT IS NOT A CONFESSION. IT IS A PIECE OF PAPER AND IT DOES NOT CONSENT AND IT CANNOT BE ASKED ANYTHING AND IT DOES NOT TESTIFY. NOBODY WAS THANKED. THAT BOAT GOT UNDER WAY AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON WITH A CHAMBER AND A STOVE STANDING OVER A MACHINE ON THAT FLOOR AND NOBODY GOING BACK FOR EITHER.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past five in the evening on that deck, being under way, and no name is against it: that the reason one sheet was taken out of about forty and the other thirty-nine were put back is the man of about fifty-one's own, and is that a man who has cut about four hundred pieces of paper in nineteen years knows that a bundle is not a document and that taking a second one out of it turns a thing somebody wrote into a thing this party has, and that nobody on that boat owns a page out of that room including him; and that the reason that sentence was stopped in about a quarter of an hour and four other things were said against it is the holder's own, and is that a sentence with a name at the end of it is the easiest kind in the world to write down and the hardest kind in the world to take back, and that if one line goes into a minute without the four lines under it then in a fortnight this yard has printed a name and a verdict and has not printed a fact;
