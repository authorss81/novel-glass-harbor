# Chapter 453

*Friday the Fifteenth Day of October, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern, a Woman of About Fifty-Eight With an End Stall Saying Out Loud That the Figure on Her Slate Is the Price of a Barrel and There Is No Barrel, One Barrel of Lamp Oil Bought off a Coasting Boat on That Quay at Four Hundred and Eighty-Six Pence Because There Has Been None in That Harbor Since the Twenty-Ninth Day of August, a Sheet of About Four Feet That Says on Its Own Face That It Is a Description of a Made Thing and Not the Thing Taken Out of an Oilcloth Fold in That Shed at About Five in the Afternoon and Put Into the Inside Pocket of a Coat and Said to Nobody, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Friday the fifteenth day of October, at an end stall in that front street, at a counter in a room off that market, and in that shed.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and nobody took her name off either sheet it is on. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he sat on the eleventh piece with his back to the water and worked a seam in the ninth. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-one days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-three days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fourteenth week. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-one days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-seventh night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-five days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-three days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirtieth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-fifth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-second day.**

---

They went to the end stall at about eleven in the morning on the Friday to buy a barrel of lamp oil, because there is no lamp oil in that harbor and there has been none since the twenty-ninth day of August, and because a mole-head light has been out with its lens out since the seventh of May and was not going to be lit on a shelf under the drowned city without oil in it.

The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall did not get up.

"Four hundred and fifty pence," she said. "That is the figure. It has been the figure since the first of June and it is the figure of a barrel of lamp oil and February's three hundred and seventy-eight is standing up above it with both dates on it, and I have been asked three times in a month to take mine off and I am not going to take it off, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it."

"We know what your figure is."

"Then you have come down here to be told about a barrel, and there is no barrel." She put one hand flat on the slate. "I have said that four hundred and fifty pence is the price of a barrel of lamp oil and I have never once said that anybody could go and buy one at that figure, and those are two different sentences and about nine people in this harbor do not know that they are two different sentences."

**The oil was bought on the said day, on that quay at about half past one in the afternoon, at four hundred and eighty-six pence, from a man off a coasting boat who had nine barrels in his hold and was in that harbor for two days with his own painter and his own crew and did not know what they were for. The man who keeps the leaf said the figure out loud twice on that quay and nobody argued with him about it, and the reason it was not bought at the stall at the back of that end stall is a barrel that is not there, and the difference between the figure on that board and the figure on that quay is thirty-six pence, being three shillings, and nobody in that harbor is going to be told that the thirty-six pence is the price of anything.**

---

**And at about four o'clock in the afternoon on the said day, before anybody in that shed had said one word about a boat, the owner of a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay came down the slip and put eight marks against a line on that sheet that carried his own name against it and the hour, being one thousand two hundred and eighty pence, and gave no reason out loud for the figure being larger than anybody else's, and went back up the slip.**

His boat opened about nine foot of garboard and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands on the twenty-eighth day of September past by striking a baulk of oak that this yard's own hands had laid down with nothing on it, and he was not charged a penny for that bill and did not offer and was not thanked for it and was not thanked for this either.

He is the man who said the name of that thing once, at about ten past eleven in the morning on the tenth day of October past, and wrote it at the head of that other sheet himself in a hand that is not a clerk's hand. Nobody in that shed said the name after him then and nobody said it on the said Friday either, and nobody in that shed asked him one question about the figure, and the reason nobody asked him one question about the figure is in the yard's own mouth and is that a man who has put a figure against his own name is not asked what the figure is for, and that he was one of the three people in that room on the Wednesday who were not asked the eleven sentences and wrote nothing, and that nobody has put the two of those together on any page.

---

**And in that shed, at about five in the afternoon on the said day, with the door open on the slip and about four men in it who were all talking, the holder of that writ took a sheet of about four feet out of an oilcloth fold.**

He had brought it down from the room on that hill on his own in a bag on the Thursday, and it has been in three pockets since the eighth of October past and it went out of that building as written words and not as a thing. It is a description of a made thing and not the thing. It carries a line of nine soundings of which the second, fifth and eighth are shallow, and the fifth of them is on the top of a stone in about four and a half fathoms about which three men gave three accounts in one afternoon on the eighth of October past that do not agree with each other and are not settled. **It says at the head of itself that it is a description of a made thing and not the thing it describes, and it carries the fifth of the account's nine boundaries at the same size as the rest of them, and that boundary says the made thing may be used for a route and not for a scene, and it names the four people whose authority that is and there is nothing on that sheet that would put a fifth name there.**

He read the head of it standing up, in about fifteen seconds, in his own mouth, with four men in that shed who were not listening.

Then he folded it back into the oilcloth, and put it in the inside pocket of a coat, and buttoned the pocket, and went back to the bench stone and picked up a caulking iron he had not come in for, and worked at a joint on the head of the third lay for about an hour and ten minutes.

**He did not tell Mara Venn. She was not in that shed on the said day and she is not told in this chapter. He did not tell Nessa Pike, who was across the water on a boat of about eleven tons until about six. He did not put it in the first column of that leaf, which a stranger with a pencil can take and check, and he did not put it in the second column, which a stranger with a pencil cannot check, and there is no line about it in either column of the said day. Nobody saw him do it. He said the reason to himself on the walk down to the slip and the reason was that a hundred years from now a man with a sheet and a bell and no time at all could put a paper against a thing that took nine people nine bells to open, and that if that were possible then the whole of what this coast has spent twenty years being about was a rehearsal for a man with a pencil, and he did not know whether that was the end of the business or the whole of it.**

**He said none of that out loud to anybody on the said day and he has not said it out loud since, and it is in this chapter because this chapter is where it happened and not because anybody heard it.**

Nessa Pike came off the water at about six and asked him what he had done all afternoon and he said he had done a joint, and she said that a joint takes a caulking iron and a boy and about an hour and that he had been in that shed about six hours, and he said that the other five were on the bench stone, and she said that is right, and went and got her day's work, and it went in the second column in the yard's own mouth with the reason against the line, and no name is against it.

---

**THERE IS NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR AND A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE, BEING THIRTY-SEVEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, STANDS ON A BOARD IN THAT FRONT STREET WITH THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING THIRTY-ONE SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, VISIBLE ABOVE IT AND BOTH DATES ON THAT BOARD, AND THE FIRST OF JUNE DID NOT COME DOWN AND CANNOT BE TAKEN OFF. A BARREL WAS BOUGHT OFF A COASTING BOAT ON THAT QUAY AT FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE, BEING FORTY SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND THE FIGURE WAS SAID OUT LOUD TWICE ON THAT QUAY AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH IT, AND THE THIRTY-SIX PENCE BETWEEN THE TWO FIGURES IS THREE SHILLINGS AND IS THE PRICE OF NOTHING. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WHO OWNS A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-TWO TONS OF THE SECOND LAY PUT EIGHT MARKS, BEING ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE, AGAINST A LINE ON THAT SHEET AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND WAS NOT ASKED ONE QUESTION ABOUT THE FIGURE AND WAS NOT THANKED. A SHEET OF ABOUT FOUR FEET THAT SAYS ON ITS OWN FACE THAT IT IS A DESCRIPTION OF A MADE THING AND NOT THE THING WENT INTO THE INSIDE POCKET OF A MAN'S COAT AT ABOUT FIVE IN THE AFTERNOON, AND NOTHING WAS SAID ABOUT IT TO ANYBODY, AND IT IS IN NEITHER COLUMN OF THAT LEAF, AND A CAULKING IRON WAS WORKED FOR AN HOUR AND TEN MINUTES AFTERWARDS SO THAT FOUR MEN IN A SHED WOULD HAVE SOMETHING TO LOOK AT.**

*First column, entered on Friday the fifteenth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a barrel of lamp oil was bought on that quay at four hundred and eighty-six pence, being forty shillings and sixpence, at about half past one in the afternoon on the said day, from a man off a coasting boat with nine barrels in his hold who was in that harbor for two days with his own painter and his own crew and did not know what they were for; and that the figure was said out loud twice on that quay and that nobody argued with it; and that the owner of a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay put eight marks, being one thousand two hundred and eighty pence, against a line on that sheet at about four in the afternoon and was not asked one question about the figure and was not thanked; and that the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall did not get up and said four hundred and fifty pence is the price of a barrel of lamp oil and that she has never once said that anybody could go and buy one at that figure, and that those are two different sentences and about nine people in that harbor do not know it; and that a sheet of about four feet was taken out of an oilcloth fold in that shed at about five in the afternoon and put into the inside pocket of a coat and buttoned there, and that no person in that harbor was told and that nothing about it is in the first column of that leaf or the second; and that a caulking iron was worked on the head of the third lay for about an hour and ten minutes afterwards.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason no penny of that barrel came out of that bag is in the yard's own mouth and is that a barrel of lamp oil is not a caulking iron and a caulked joint and a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, and that the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall is the price of a barrel and there is no barrel; and that the reason the man who keeps the leaf asked the woman of about fifty-eight where the oil was and was told there is none is that the two sentences she says about her own figure are two different sentences and that about nine people in that harbor do not know it, and that he is the tenth and did not say so; and that the reason a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay put eight marks against a line on that sheet at about four in the afternoon and was not asked one question about the figure is in the yard's own mouth and is that a man who has put a figure against his own name is not asked what the figure is for, and that he was one of the three people in the room over the fish market on the Wednesday who were not asked the eleven sentences and wrote nothing against them, and that this yard does not put the two of those together on any page; and that the reason a sheet of about four feet went into an inside pocket instead of into a bag with the rest of the party's papers is not in the yard's own mouth on the said day, and that the yard is not going to put a reason against a thing that is not in either column, and that a thing a man has not written down is a thing a stranger with a pencil cannot check and that is the whole of what this entry is for; and that the reason a caulking iron was worked for about an hour and ten minutes in front of four men is in the ship's mouth and is that a man standing in a shed with his hands in his pockets is a man four men in a shed will look at, and that she asked him what he had done all afternoon and that he said he had done a joint and that she said that a joint takes a caulking iron and a boy and about an hour and that he had been in that shed about six hours, and that he said the other five were on the bench stone and that is right; and that the reason a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line cut a line of nine soundings with three of them deep into a piece of worked lead on the fifth day of October past, and left a place about the size of a thumb out of that line on purpose, and carried no figure and no number of any kind, is that a number cut into a thing is a thing that can be asked after, and that lead is on a hill in a drawer under a drawer of its own and did not come down that hill and is not coming down that hill; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Friday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
