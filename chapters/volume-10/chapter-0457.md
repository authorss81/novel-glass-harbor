# Chapter 457

*Tuesday the Nineteenth Day of October, the Fifty-Second Wage Tuesday, and the Wage Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest Did Not Move on It and Fifty-Two Weeks Stand Fallen Due With No Envelope Against Any of Them at the Close of the Said Day in a Harbor Four Days Behind Them, the Fourth Day of a Run Down That Coast Road and a Tide That Lays a Boat of About Nineteen Tons on the Exposed Shelf Outside the Quietening Cut in About Nine Fathoms at the Top of the Water, a Bell-Shaped Chamber With a Deck Plate and a Stove and a Stove Pipe and a Working Gauge Standing on the Bottom of That Shelf in a Day and a Half, a Gauge Reading Eighty-Four Pounds on a Face of a Hundred and Fifty, a Mark About Four Inches Long Cut Into the Floor of That Shelf About Three Weeks Old That Is a Surveyor's Mark and Not a Scar, Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence Coming Into That Yard and Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Tuesday the nineteenth day of October, on the Crownless Shelf, in about nine fathoms at the top of the water, and in a harbor two days behind them.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and it was the fifty-second wage Tuesday, and the wage column at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room did not move on it, and fifty-one weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of the said day and fifty-two stand at the close of it, and nobody in that harbor is to write one word under any of it saying anybody is going to pay it. Three hundred and thirty-six pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being two days at the figure in chalk, and three hundred and thirty-six pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being two days asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name stands on two public sheets and is on no third. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and got up at four o'clock and went home, which he has done four times in ninety days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-five days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-seven days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fifteenth week. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-five days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-first night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-nine days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-seven days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-fourth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-ninth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-sixth day.**

---

They came onto the shelf at about ten in the morning on the tide, and about nine fathoms under them the bottom of it came up grey and flat with a reef of it about two hundred yards off the boat's port bow, and Simeon Roke put her down on the edge of that reef in about two fathoms and would not take her over it, and nobody argued with him about it, and that was the first time in that boat that the thing the party was doing was decided by the master.

The chamber went down over two days and it was made of a bell and a deck plate and a stove and a stove pipe and a gauge, and the whole of it went down on the first day in about eleven hours of the tide, and it stood on the bottom on the morning of the second day with a man of about thirty-four of the second lay holding the plate level by hand while Ivo Skell packed sand into the four corners of it, which is not a foundation and is not meant to be one and is the kind of thing a thing on a bottom of the sea has to be.

**The gauge on the side of that chamber read eighty-four pounds on a face of a hundred and fifty, and the man who kept the leaf wrote both figures on a slate and the hour against them, and wrote under them, in the yard's own mouth, that the reading is not a depth reading and that the depth of that floor is nine fathoms and comes off the line, and that the two numbers on that slate are about different things and are on the same slate on purpose so that a man who wants to be careless cannot be careless quietly.**

A stove does not draw in a chamber of that size and a man of about thirty-four of the second lay said so and was right, and they ran the pipe out sideways and up about two feet and left the top of it open, and about half of what they put in the stove went out into the water in about an hour, and the cost of that is in the coal and is in the eleventh line of a sheet in three pockets.

**It is a thing and not a building. It has no door on the shore side. It is about the size of a good-sized boat's after cabin and it is a room that nobody in Morrow Quay has ever been inside, and there is a ladder down the outside of it and a handrail on the ladder and the handrail is on the inside face of it so that a man going up with his hands full can hold it without turning round.**

And on the afternoon of the first day, when four of the nine were on the bottom of that shelf in a bell of about eleven tons' own weight with a diver in it, about ninety yards off the port bow, they found a mark.

**It was about four inches long and it was cut, and it was square at both ends, and it was in a flat place on the floor of the shelf where there was no reason for a cut to be, and the man of about thirty-four who was in that bell put his thumb on it and said that it was not a scar and that a scar does not have square ends.**

"How old."

"About three weeks," he said. "The edges of it are still bright. The silt on it is thin. I have been in a lot of holds and I have never once been wrong about how long a cut has been under."

It went into the minute with the hour against it, and into the second column of that leaf with the reason against the line, and the reason is in the yard's own mouth and is that a mark cut by a person is a thing a person did and a person did it about three weeks before that day, and that a person about a quarter of a mile of water from a shelf with a boat on it is not one of the nine.

Nobody said anything else about it that evening. Ivo Skell asked one question, which was whether anybody was going to say it out loud in front of the master, and the man who keeps the leaf said no, because a man of fifty-one out of a coast town four days down had been asked two questions before he took his money and one of the answers had been that nobody knew what was on that shelf, and that answer was now out of date, and that it was going to be given again in the morning.

**THE FIFTY-SECOND WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND WENT IN A HARBOR FOUR DAYS BEHIND THEM AND THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE ON IT, AND FIFTY-TWO WEEKS NOW STAND FALLEN DUE WITH NO ENVELOPE AGAINST ANY OF THEM. A BELL-SHAPED CHAMBER WITH A DECK PLATE AND A STOVE AND A STOVE PIPE AND A GAUGE STANDS ON THE BOTTOM OF AN EXPOSED SHELF IN ABOUT NINE FATHOMS AND IT IS A THING AND NOT A BUILDING AND IT HAS NO DOOR ON THE SHORE SIDE, AND THE GAUGE ON IT READS EIGHTY-FOUR POUNDS ON A FACE OF A HUNDRED AND FIFTY, AND THAT READING IS NOT A DEPTH READING. ON THAT FLOOR ABOUT NINETY YARDS OFF THE PORT BOW THERE IS A MARK ABOUT FOUR INCHES LONG, SQUARE AT BOTH ENDS, CUT, WITH THIN SILT ON IT AND BRIGHT EDGES, PUT IN BY THE MAN WHO WAS IN THAT BELL AS ABOUT THREE WEEKS OLD, AND NINE MEN ON A SHELF HAVE LOOKED AT IT AND SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT, AND ONE OF THEM ASKED WHETHER ANYBODY WAS GOING TO SAY IT OUT LOUD IN FRONT OF THE MASTER, AND THE ANSWER WAS NO.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the nineteenth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in a shed, and entered by the ship's hand in a harbor two days behind the party: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that it was the fifty-second wage Tuesday, and that the wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and did not move on the fifty-second, so that fifty-one weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of the said day and fifty-two stand at the close of them; and that three hundred and thirty-six pence came into that yard and three hundred and thirty-six went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus three hundred and thirty-six less three hundred and thirty-six is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a bell-shaped chamber with a deck plate and a stove and a stove pipe and a working gauge went down onto the floor of an exposed shelf in about nine fathoms at about two in the afternoon on the said day and stood on it on the morrow with sand packed into four corners, and that the gauge on the side of it read eighty-four pounds on a face of a hundred and fifty; and that a mark about four inches long and square at both ends was found cut in a flat place on the floor of that shelf about ninety yards off the port bow of a boat of about nineteen tons lying in about two fathoms over a reef, and that the man who was in that bell put in that it was about three weeks old, and that the mark is a surveyor's mark and not a scar.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed two days behind the party, and no name is against it: that the reason a wage column does not move on a wage Tuesday is in that harbor's own mouth and has been in it for fifty-two Tuesdays, and that this batch is not a batch in which anybody is going to be told that it is about to; and that the reason the reading of that gauge and the depth of that floor are on the same slate is in the yard's own mouth and is that the two numbers are about different things, and that a man who wants to be careless cannot be careless quietly when the careless number is four inches from the honest one; and that the reason a man of about fifty-one put a boat of about nineteen tons down on the edge of a reef in about two fathoms and would not take her over it is his own and is not the party's, and that this was the first decision on that water that was not the party making it, and that nobody in that boat argued with him; and that the reason nine men said nothing about that mark for the rest of the day is in the holder's own mouth and is that a man of fifty-one out of a coast town four days down was asked two questions before he took his money and one of the two answers was that nobody knew what was on that shelf, and that that answer was out of date as of about ten o'clock that morning, and that it was going to be given again in the morning because a boat's master is owed the truth before a boat's master is owed a favor; and that the reason nothing on that fitting-out sheet this week is entered in full is that the sheet is a list and not an account of a trip, and that a list goes on a sheet and an account goes in a book, and this yard keeps both and does not mix them; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Tuesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
