# Chapter 487

*Friday the Nineteenth Day of November, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men Going onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Ten in the Morning at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, Being Four Marks, Two Shillings and Eightpence, and Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand, the Third of Those Four Hired Days of Air and the Last of the Four That Are Opened Under a Line, a Man of About Thirty-Eight of That Party Going Down at About Half Past Ten on That Ebb under a Slate With His Reason in His Own Words and a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff He Could Pull Without Asking and Coming Up at About Twenty Past Eleven on the Air, Not Hurt, Not Thanked, Not a Casualty, and No Figure Set Against Him Anywhere, and Carrying Something in Him That Is Not His and Not Giving It Up and Nobody Asking Him To, He Saying One Thing About It Out Loud on That Deck at About Two in the Afternoon and That Going into the Second Column With the Hour Against It and Not into the First Column and Not onto Any Sheet That Carries Money, and His Going on Working on the Twentieth and on the Twenty-First, the Shortfall at Thirteen Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight Pence, Being Eighty-Six Marks and Four Shillings, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Friday the nineteenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. A day apiece in advance to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that is the third of the four men and the same four men as on the sixteenth of October past and as on the second of November and as on the ninth, and nothing went into that sheet in any hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at twenty-four thousand four hundred and forty-eight pence out, being one hundred and fifty-two marks, ten shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirteen thousand eight hundred and eight pence short, being eighty-six marks and four shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninety-first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and nine days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixty-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-seven days. There is no flour in that market and none coming this month, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column of that ledger and that boy's surname is on no page of that harbor, and he is not on the water. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**A slate was written before anybody went down and the man of about thirty-eight wrote his own reason on it himself, and the reason was that he had heard the crate being filled on that after deck on the Thursday and had looked into it, and that he had never in nineteen years of stands been in a room with a floor in it that was swept, and that he wanted to stand on one.**

**Ruben Skay, of about thirty-eight, went down at about half past ten in the morning on that ebb under that slate, and the air limit on it was a figure of forty minutes, and the air was out of cylinders hired off that supply boat on the seventeenth of November, and the tender was named and was on the surface of that boat and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave. He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-two of them and came up at about twenty past eleven on the air.**

He came up and he took his helmet off and he sat down on the edge of that hatch with his boots off, and he worked that compressor's second shift from about one o'clock in the afternoon until about six and he did not go into that mouth again that day and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why he did not.

**He is not hurt. Nobody has named him a casualty. No figure is set against him anywhere in this trip or in that harbor, and the sheet that carries the money carries no line against him and no line about him of any kind, and the bag is not touched by any part of it.**

---

**And at about two in the afternoon on that after deck, in the open, in front of all nine of them, he said the one thing he was going to say about it, and he said it once, and he said it in about half a minute, and then he got up and went back to the compressor and nobody asked him another question about it on that day or on any day after it.**

"There is a woman in that room who has put a bowl of water down outside a door on the off side, and a man has gone past it and gone in without stopping, and the handle of that bowl is turned to the left as a person goes in, and the rim of it is chipped on the left and the chip is old. That is all of it. That is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to enlarge on it.

I have never seen that bowl. I have not seen that floor and I have not seen that door and I have not seen that woman, and I am thirty-eight years old and I was born about nine miles from a coast where nothing like that is done, and I do not know what it is. **I have worked out, standing on that hatch a minute ago, that a man who says a thing like that out loud and then argues about it spends the rest of his life arguing about it, and I have nineteen years of stands to do and I am not spending them like that.** So I have said it. I am keeping it. Nobody is to ask me for it and nobody is to have it. It cannot be given back because it was never taken, and I am not a casualty and I would like to be back on that compressor at one o'clock, which is what I am doing."

**The man who keeps the leaf asked him one question and it was whether he wanted it in the first column, and he said no, and then the man who keeps the leaf said out loud in front of all nine of them that it would go in the second column with the hour against it and that no figure would be set against the day of it and that the man was not a casualty and had not been thanked and would not be thanked, and Skay said that was the right column and that he had thought so.**

Nobody argued with him. Nobody said the word brave. Nobody put a figure on him. He was not made an example of anything and he was not made a lesson and he was not asked why.

---

**And a man of about thirty-seven went down at about ten o'clock in the morning on that same ebb under a slate of his own with a figure of forty minutes of air on it and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking anybody's leave, and was in the water about forty-five minutes and on the bottom about twenty-nine of them and came up at about ten to eleven, and came up with nothing at all, and said out loud that there was nothing more in that room that he could put a figure on and that he was not going to be the man who put a figure on a wet floor, and nobody thanked him and no figure is set against him.**

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT OF THAT PARTY WENT DOWN ON THAT FRIDAY UNDER A SLATE WITH HIS OWN REASON AND A FIGURE OF FORTY MINUTES OF AIR AND A NAMED TENDER ON THE SURFACE AND NOT IN THE BELL AND A CUTOFF HE COULD PULL WITHOUT ASKING, AND CAME UP AT ABOUT TWENTY PAST ELEVEN ON THE AIR WITH SOMETHING IN HIM THAT IS NOT HIS. HE IS NOT HURT AND NOBODY HAS NAMED HIM A CASUALTY AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST HIM ANYWHERE AND NO SHEET THAT CARRIES MONEY CARRIES ANY LINE AGAINST HIM. HE SAID ONE THING ABOUT IT OUT LOUD AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ON THAT DECK IN FRONT OF ALL NINE OF THEM AND SAID IT ONCE, AND IT WENT INTO THE SECOND COLUMN OF THAT LEAF WITH THE HOUR AGAINST IT AND NOT INTO THE FIRST COLUMN AND NOT ONTO ANY SHEET THAT CARRIES MONEY, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM FOR IT AND HE IS KEEPING IT AND IT CANNOT BE GIVEN BACK BECAUSE IT WAS NEVER TAKEN. HE WENT ON WORKING THE DAY AFTER AND THE DAY AFTER THAT. AND THAT SHEET IS THIRTEEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND EIGHT PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING EIGHTY-SIX MARKS AND FOUR SHILLINGS, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the nineteenth day of November at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all; and that a day apiece in advance to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that nothing went into that sheet in any hand; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the air on the slates of that day was the third of the four days hired off that supply boat on the seventeenth of November and that the line still bears the seventeenth and nothing has been opened under any other figure since; and that a man of about thirty-eight of that party wrote his own reason on his own slate before he went down, being that he had heard the crate being filled on that after deck on the day before and had looked into it, and that he had never in nineteen years of stands been in a room with a floor in it that was swept, and that he wanted to stand on one, and the hour was against it; and that he went down at about half past ten in the morning on that ebb under that slate with an air limit of forty minutes written on it and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-two of them and came up at about twenty past eleven on the air; and that he took his helmet off and sat down on the edge of that hatch with his boots off and worked that compressor's second shift from about one o'clock in the afternoon until about six, and that he did not go into that mouth again that day and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why he did not; and that he is not hurt, that nobody has named him a casualty, that no figure is set against him anywhere in that trip or in that harbor, and that no sheet that carries money carries any line against him or about him; and that at about two in the afternoon on that after deck in the open in front of all nine of them he said one thing about it and said it once and said it in about half a minute, being that there is a woman in that room who has put a bowl of water down outside a door on the off side and a man has gone past it and gone in without stopping, and that the handle of that bowl is turned to the left as a person goes in and the rim of it is chipped on the left and the chip is old, and that he has never seen that bowl or that floor or that door or that woman, and that he does not know what it is; and that he said out loud that he had worked out that a man who says a thing like that and then argues about it spends the rest of his life arguing about it, and that he said it, that he is keeping it, and that nobody is to ask him for it and nobody is to have it, and that it cannot be given back because it was never taken; and that the man who keeps the leaf asked him whether he wanted it in the first column and he said no, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud in front of all nine of them that it would go in the second column with the hour against it and that no figure would be set against the day of it and that the man was not a casualty and had not been thanked and would not be thanked, and that the man said that was the right column and that he had thought so; and that nobody argued with him and nobody said the word brave and nobody put a figure on him and he was not made an example of anything and nobody asked him why; and that a man of about thirty-seven went down at about ten o'clock in the morning on that same ebb under a slate of his own with a figure of forty minutes of air and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and was in the water about forty-five minutes and on the bottom about twenty-nine of them and came up at about ten to eleven with nothing at all, and said out loud that there was nothing more in that room he could put a figure on and that he was not going to be the man who put a figure on a wet floor, and that nobody thanked him and no figure is set against him; and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason a man of about thirty-eight said one thing about what came up with him in about half a minute and then got up and went back to a compressor is his own, and is that nineteen years of stands is a thing a man spends, and that a man who stops working to think about a thing like that has not got nineteen years of stands left, he has got one afternoon of thinking and then a different life; and that the reason he would not have it in the first column is his own, and is that the first column takes one entry against a day whatever else that day printed so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it, and that what came up with him is not an entry against a day, it is a thing that happened to a man, and that putting it in a column a man can count would be putting a figure on him by the back door; and that the reason nobody was thanked for it is the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who brings up a thing that is not his has done a day's work and a day's work is paid at the figure in chalk and not at a word of thanks, and that thanking a man for a thing he cannot hand over is the way a deck gets itself into the habit of asking for them; and that the reason the man of about thirty-seven came up with nothing at all and said so out loud is his own, and is that a man with nineteen years in holds can look at a floor for forty minutes and know there is nothing more on it he can put a figure to, and that saying so on the deck is worth more than a bundle, and that nobody thanked him for it either; and that the reason a day apiece in advance went to the same four men for the third time is the yard's own mouth, and is that a day in advance is a day in advance and is not a promise of a day after it, and that the answer to that was given on the sixteenth of November and was given twice before that, and that a man who is owed a day on a Friday is owed it whether or not anything comes up his ladder, and that this yard does not pay a man more for what he brings off a floor and does not pay him less for what he does not; and that the reason not one of the debts that harbor has been carrying since the spring was settled, reduced, re-dated or written down as done on the said Friday is the yard's own mouth, and is that there is no flour in that market and none coming this month and that the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy is not on the water, and that a man being asked for nothing in a harbor he is not in and a man being sent down in twenty-two fathoms in the same week are two men and neither of them is the other, and that the yard's own book is the only place either of them is entered.*
