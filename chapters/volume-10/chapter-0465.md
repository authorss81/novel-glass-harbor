# Chapter 465

*Wednesday the Twenty-Seventh Day of October, the Second Ask Being Carried Out and Not Reopened, Eleven People Asked a Second Time One at a Time and Nine Lines Opened Against It, Five of Those Nine Putting In More and Four of Those Nine Putting In Nothing, Five of the Nine at Two Marks Each and Two of Those Five at a Mark Each More, Being Seven Lines Against Five People and One Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty and Three Hundred and Twenty Pence on the Said Day, Two of the Four Saying Why in Their Own Mouths in Front of Somebody and One of Those Two Reasons Being That a Person's Wage Is a Figure and a Person's Second Wage Is an Opinion, a Pump Hand of About Forty-Four Asked and Saying His Reason Out Loud and Promising His Two Marks for the Morning, Air Cylinders Rented for Three Days at Five Hundred and Forty Pence, No Figure Set Against Anybody Who Put In Nothing and No List of the People Who Put In Nothing Kept by Anybody, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the twenty-seventh day of October, in that loft over a chandler's and in that shed.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of what went onto that fitting-out sheet on the said Wednesday came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-three days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-five days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a sixteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and three days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-seven days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and five days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-second day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-seventh day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-fourth day.**

---

They were asked one at a time again and not in front of each other, and nobody was asked who had already answered, and the person who was asked said the same thing whether they had been asked on the Tuesday or not, and by the Wednesday afternoon it was known in that loft which nine of the eleven had a line opened against them and which two had not, and nobody said so out loud and nobody wrote it down.

**Five of the nine put in more. Five of the nine who put in four marks put in two marks each, and two of those five put in a mark each more on top of that, and that is one thousand six hundred pence and three hundred and twenty pence, and that is seven lines against five people and not seven people, and the two who added a mark are two of the five and not two more, and the two went on that sheet on the said day with a name and an hour against each, being ten marks and two marks.**

The woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay, who had said out loud on the sixteenth of October that her money was for flour, put in one of the two marks and did not say anything about flour, and nobody asked her about flour, and there is still no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and nobody in that harbor has told her that her first mark and a half is not coming back and nobody is going to.

---

Four of the nine put in nothing on that day, and two of those four said why in their own mouths in front of somebody, and the other two said nothing and were not asked.

**A man of about fifty-six of the second lay said it first and he said it standing up.**

"I put in four marks in October and I have not had a day's work since the fourteenth and there is no flour in that market and there is a figure on a board in that front street that has not come down since the first of June, and I am not saying that to anybody. I am saying I have counted what I have and I have counted it twice, and this is the second time anybody has asked me for it, and a second time is not a different amount of money, it is the same money with a different day on it, and I have given the first day's worth and I am not going to give it again because a week went by."

And then a woman of about thirty-eight who crews nothing and holds a lease on a stall said the other one, and she said it quietly, and this is the reason and it is in her own mouth.

"I will tell you why and then you will not ask me again, and I would rather say it than be one of the quiet ones. My husband's wage is a figure. It comes on a Friday and it is a figure and it is the same every week whether the boat goes out or not. If I put two marks into that page on the Wednesday, then on the Thursday that wage is not a figure any more, it is a thing I have an opinion about, and I do not want to be the reason my husband goes out on a Tuesday in a north-easter because I wanted to help with something I do not understand. A wage is a figure. A second wage is an opinion, and I have never in my life been able to hold one of those and be certain which one I am holding."

Nobody in that loft argued with her about it and nobody told her she was wrong and nobody thanked her for saying it, and what she said went into the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the hour against it and no name against it, and she has not been asked about it since.

**No figure was set against either of the four. Neither of the two names is on a second list, and there is no second list, and a list of the people who put in nothing on the second ask is not a list this yard keeps, and that rule is on the sheet and not only in a head.**

---

The pump hand of about forty-four was asked last, at about half past three in the afternoon on the said Wednesday, and he is in nobody's crew and is a member of nothing, and he put in nothing that day and said why out loud, and then he said he would put in two marks in the morning and not one minute sooner, and both of those were said out loud and neither of them was written down at the time.

"Ivo Skell, nineteen years of compressors and no crew and no sheet and I have never in my life been asked for money by anybody who was not being paid by the person asking," he said. "I am putting in two marks. Here is why, and it is not about the money, and if it was about the money I would not say it. That stove off a supply boat at eleven marks and fourpence has a throat in it that I can hear from the top of that ladder, and I have been hearing it for six days, and it is going to be worse by the time that boat is under me at twenty-two fathoms, and I would rather that somebody had put in two marks than have me be the man who is right and the trip is short six hundred and sixty-six pence when it is not. That is the whole of it. I am not buying a thing and I am not buying a share and I do not want to be thanked, and I will put the two marks in at about nine in the morning when I have heard that throat again and I will put them in whether or not it is any better."

**And nobody asked him a second question about it, and nobody wrote it down that day, and the two marks went onto that sheet on the twenty-eighth day with the day against them and not with the Wednesday, and the reason for that is in the second column and is that a man who says he will do a thing in the morning and then does it in the morning has not been asked twice about a sum of money.**

---

Air cylinders were rented at about eleven in the morning for three days, off a man in the west basin who has let cylinders out for nineteen years, being five hundred and forty pence, being three marks and five shillings, and the man who let them said out loud that the three days were three days and that anybody wanting a fourth would be talking to him again in a different voice.

---

**AND AT THE CLOSE OF THAT WEDNESDAY THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY STANDS AT NINE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE IN AND TEN THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT, AND IT IS SHORT BY SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIX PENCE, BEING FOUR MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TWICE.**

**THE WAGE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE ON ANY DAY OF THIS MOVEMENT AND NO ENVELOPE CAME OUT OF THAT CHEST. ELEVEN PEOPLE WERE ASKED A SECOND TIME AND NINE LINES WERE OPENED AGAINST THAT ASK, FIVE OF THOSE NINE PUT IN MORE ON SEVEN LINES AND FOUR OF THOSE NINE PUT IN NOTHING AND SAID NOTHING AGAINST THEIR OWN NAMES, AND TWO OF THOSE FOUR SAID WHY OUT LOUD, AND THERE IS NO LIST OF THE FOUR, AND A LIST WOULD HAVE BEEN A WEAPON AND THIS YARD IS NOT BUILDING ONE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-seventh day of October at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that eleven people were asked a second time one at a time and not in front of each other, and that nine lines were opened against that second ask, and that five of those nine put in more on the said day, being five at two marks each and two of those five at a mark each more, and that one thousand six hundred pence and three hundred and twenty pence went onto the fitting-out sheet on the said day with a name and an hour against each, being seven lines against five people; and that a man of about fifty-six of the second lay and a woman of about thirty-eight said in their own mouths in front of somebody why they had put in nothing, and that the second of those two reasons was that a person's wage is a figure and a person's second wage is an opinion; and that no figure was set against any of the four who put in nothing, that no second list was made, and that a list of the people who put nothing in is not a list this yard keeps; and that a pump hand of about forty-four said out loud at about half past three in the afternoon that he would put in two marks in the morning and said why out loud and that it was not about the money, and that nobody asked him a second question about it and nobody wrote it down that day; and that air cylinders were rented for three days at about eleven in the morning, being five hundred and forty pence, being three marks and five shillings; and that the sheet that carries the money stands at nine thousand six hundred and eighty in and ten thousand three hundred and forty-six out and is short by six hundred and sixty-six pence, being four marks, two shillings and twopence, and carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man of about fifty-six said that a second time is not a different amount of money but the same money with a different day on it is his own and is entered here once and is not asked about again; and that the reason a woman of about thirty-eight said that a wage is a figure and a second wage is an opinion is her own, and is that she would rather be one of the quiet ones than say a thing out loud that she cannot hold, and that what she said went in with the hour against it and no name against it, and that she has not been asked about it since; and that the reason nobody in that loft argued with either of them and nobody thanked either of them is that a person who has said no for a reason in their own mouth has done the whole of what the page asked of them, and that thanking a person for a refusal is a way of asking them for it again; and that the reason nobody in that loft said out loud which nine of the eleven had a line opened against them and which two had not is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a line opened is a thing that is on a page and a set of names is a thing that can be said across a room, and that this yard has kept the first and has not built the second; and that the reason a pump hand of about forty-four said his reason out loud and it was not about the money is his own, and is that he would rather somebody had put in two marks than be the man who is right and the trip is short when it is not, and that a man who says he will do a thing in the morning and then does it in the morning has not been asked twice about a sum of money, and that is why the two marks go on the day after and not on this one; and that on the said Wednesday nothing standing was touched at all, and that is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who has put two marks in on a day has done the whole of what that day asked of him and is neither to be thanked nor asked about again, and that a day of asking is not a day of settling.*
