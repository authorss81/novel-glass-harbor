# Chapter 480

*Thursday the Eleventh Day of November, a Rota Day, and the Ninety-Eighth Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still off It, a Slate Written Before Anybody Went Down With a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff That Tender Bottles and Not Any Part of the Four Days Hired on the Sixth of November, a Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Sitting on That Deck From About Two in the Afternoon Until Dark With a Slate and a Pencil Writing Out the Diameter of an Older Hole and the Depth of It and the Length of a Collar and the Way Silt Lay in It, Which Is Work and Which Nobody Has Checked and Which Nobody Can, Three Statements About What Is on That Floor in Three Voices and Said Once Each and Not Explained Twice, a Man of About Thirty-Six Named Corwin Tace Still Setting Marks Around It and Working, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Out Loud What She Is Going to Say in a Harbor on Monday and Not Having Said It Yet, a Master of a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Saying Out Loud That He Is Going to Take His Boat and His Two Men and His Boy Home, a Leaf Read Back at About Ten Past Five in the Evening With Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading and All Four Columns Including the Empty One and Read Twice Because About Four Men of That Party Cannot Read, the Fitting-Out Sheet Seven Thousand and Fourteen Pence Short, Being Forty-Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, and the Word That Would Name the Seal in No Mouth, and Not One Word on That Deck About How Many of Anything There Would Have to Be*

Thursday the eleventh day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-eighth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**The last of the nine, and they are the last of them this boat will print before she goes up that coast, and not one of them is a figure of money: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and seventy-eight days on its wall in a different street; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for a hundred and eighty, the chest shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat unargued; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and eighteen days old; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and eighty-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, a hundred and two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its fifty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its eighty-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty days. A woman of thirty of no office is on that deck, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the day's entries are made on that deck at night. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eighteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. A boy of twenty-two's hand is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, went through that doorway at about ten in the morning on that ebb under a slate with the reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that if a room under the water is written on no slate then it is not a room to the next man who comes here, and the air limit was a figure of forty minutes and the tender was named and was on the surface of that boat and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.**

The air on that slate came out of the same bottles that were filled off that compressor on the tenth, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud on that deck that it was the second day of them and that he did not know what was on the twelfth of November and would not open a line for it in front of nine men, and nobody argued with him about it.

He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them. He left one slate on the second plank of that hull planking and one on the floor inside the doorway and came up at about ten to eleven with a cold in his teeth, a taste of iron like a nail, and a cough that came on him about two hours after he came up and was still going on that deck when the leaf was read back at about ten past five.

Nobody thanked him. He is not a casualty and no figure is set against him anywhere. Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice, and the two slates are on that floor for whoever comes after and say nothing at all about who put them there.

**And a man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down sat on that after deck from about two in the afternoon until dark with a slate and a pencil, and wrote out the diameter of the older hole and the depth of it and the length of the collar and the way the silt lay in the bottom of it, and that is what he did instead of bringing the piece up, and it is work, and nobody has checked it and nobody can.**

He did not read it out. It went into the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the hour against it and he is not named in it and no figure is set against the day of it.

---

**Three people said one thing each about what is on that floor on the eleventh day of November, and they said them once each, and nobody explained anything a second time on that deck, and nobody asked any of the three of them to say it again.**

**Corwin Tace, of about thirty-six, said the first, and it was about his own work and it was all he was asked about.**

"I set marks and I have been setting them every working day since the middle of October and I will set more on Monday morning and I will be off that floor by the middle of the day. The warrant has a seal on it and no figure on it and I have never seen a figure on one and I stopped asking for that the second week, because a man who asks for a figure gets a figure and then the figure is on him. Nobody on this boat has offered me anything and nobody on this boat has thanked me and I am not asking either, and I am not going to be on that floor on the Monday after that if you are, and that is not a threat, it is a Thursday."

**Mara Venn, of thirty, of no office, said the second, and it was not what she believed and she said so.**

"I have written down, in my own mouth, what three people saw on that floor and what one man said about it and what five things I said about a mark in a room. I have not written down what any of it was for, and if somebody asks me on Monday what that floor is, I am going to say that I do not know. That is going to be on a page in my own hand with an hour against it, and it is the first sentence of that kind I have written in eleven years and it is not going to be the last one."

**And the master of that boat of about nineteen tons said the third, and he said it to nobody in particular and everybody heard it.**

"We have been sitting on this ground since the last day of October with a machine on the bottom of it and a Crown man's marks set round it, and I have got a boat and two men and a boy and I am going to take them up that coast, and nobody has asked me to and nobody has thanked me and I would have taken them up on the Thursday before we came out if that had been the week. I am not being brave about it and I am not being careful about it. I am going home on the flood that suits me and I am not going to leave two men and a boy under twenty-two fathoms to look at a thing in this harbor while they find out whose it is."

**AND AT ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE IN THE EVENING ON THAT DECK, IN THE OPEN, IN THE SHIP'S OWN LEFT HAND, ALL FOUR COLUMNS INCLUDING THE EMPTY ONE, THE LEAF WAS READ BACK, AND IT WAS READ TWICE BECAUSE ABOUT FOUR MEN OF THAT PARTY CANNOT READ, AND BOTH DEFINITIONS CAME OUT OF THE SAME MOUTH IN THE SAME WORDS THEY CAME OUT OF ON THE TWENTY-SECOND DAY OF OCTOBER AND ON THE FIRST DAY OF NOVEMBER.**

"There are two hundred and twelve entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and thirteen at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and thirty have gone in on the thirty days from Wednesday the thirteenth of October to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has nine hundred and thirty-seven lines in it at this hour and nine hundred and thirty-nine at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and five have gone in on each of the thirty days since, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the hundred and fifty that have gone in, a hundred and forty-five went in on the twenty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and a hundred and fifty less two is a hundred and forty-eight, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and a hundred and forty-eight is nine hundred and thirty-seven. That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and twelve and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments."

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these thirty days and it has taken no line in the twenty days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

**And the sheet that carries the money was read back after that, in full, with the day against every line, and it stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and seventeen thousand six hundred and fifty-four pence out, being one hundred and ten marks, four shillings and sixpence, and it is seven thousand and fourteen pence short, being forty-three marks, eleven shillings and twopence.**

It carries no count of persons and has no figure on it for how many people are on it. **The three refusals stand on it at the same size as the figures, and none of the three people was asked a second time on any day of the ten days of this movement, and nobody in that harbor has asked any of them anything, and nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in the whole of those ten days, and no figure has been asked of anybody to make that shortfall be otherwise.**

**And on every one of those ten days one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and the whole of the ten days is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence out, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that figure is the same one it was at the close of the first of November and no penny of any part of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown, and the seven thousand and fourteen pence is against the yard's own book and is nobody's else.**

**THE CHAMBER WITH A STOVE IN IT IS STANDING ON A TRENCH FLOOR IN ABOUT TWENTY-TWO FATHOMS, SEATED LEVEL ON FOUR PADS OF SAWN FIR AT THE EDGE OF THE SWEPT PART, WITH A SECOND PIPE AND A BRACKET CARRYING IT DOWN A LADDER, AND IT IS A THING AND NOT A BUILDING AND IT HAS NO DOOR ON THE SHORE SIDE AND NOBODY HAS GIVEN IT A NAME. THERE ARE FORTY RACKS ON THAT FLOOR IN ROWS HOLDING NOTHING AND THE FLOOR UNDER THEM IS SWEPT AND THERE ARE FIFTEEN FRAMES BEYOND THEM IN ROWS AND A FITTING WITH ITS NECK CUT LIES ON THAT FLOOR AND WILL NOT COME UP ON THIS TRIP, AND TWO SLATES ARE INSIDE THAT DOORWAY AND ON THE PLANK BEHIND IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX IS STILL SETTING MARKS ROUND ALL OF IT AND WILL BE OFF THAT FLOOR BY THE MIDDLE OF MONDAY. NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS BEEN GIVEN THE MACHINE AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS BEEN STOPPED FROM KNOWING ABOUT IT, AND THAT HARBOR IS TWO DAYS' SAIL AWAY AND NOT ONE PERSON IN IT HAS BEEN TOLD ANYTHING YET. THE WORD THAT WOULD NAME THE SEAL ON THAT WARRANT IS IN NO MOUTH ON THAT BOAT AND IN NO MOUTH IN THAT HARBOR AND NOBODY ON THAT DECK HAS SAID IT AND NOBODY IS GOING TO SAY IT THIS WEEK. AND THE BOAT THAT IS GOING TO CARRY THIS PARTY BACK UP THAT COAST HAS NO FIGURE ON HER ANYWHERE FOR IT, AND HER MASTER SAID OUT LOUD ON THAT DECK WHAT HE IS GOING TO DO WITH HIS OWN PEOPLE AND SAID IT ONCE, AND NINE MEN HEARD IT AND NOBODY ASKED HIM FOR A REASON.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason a man of about fifty-one wrote his measurements out from memory on a deck in about nine fathoms of water instead of reading them back out is his own, and is that a man who has been in holds nineteen years can put a number down that he has taken off a hole with his own thumb in twenty-two fathoms, and that a number taken that way is worth what it is worth and no more, and that it goes in this column because a stranger with a pencil cannot check it and could not check it if he had the slate; and that the reason he did not read it out is that a figure read out on a deck becomes the deck's figure and not his, and that he would rather it stayed his and be doubted; and that the reason two slates were left inside that doorway is the yard's own mouth, and is that a thing under the water that is written on nothing is not there to whoever comes next, and that a slate with a date on it and no name on it costs a man a minute and saves a party a morning; and that the reason the three statements made on that day were made once each and not explained a second time is that a thing explained twice on a deck becomes the deck's opinion, and that three voices that do not agree with each other are worth three times as much on a page as one voice that has been talked into agreement with the other two; and that the reason the master of that boat said he was going home and nobody argued with him is that a man with a boat and two men and a boy under twenty-two fathoms is not a party member and was never asked to be one and has a claim on his own people that outranks any reason on a hull; and that the reason a leaf is read back on a deck in the open with four men of that party listening to it twice is in the ship's own mouth, and is that a count nobody can check has to be heard twice before anybody signs it, and that the first hearing is for the man who keeps it and the second is for the four who cannot read it.
