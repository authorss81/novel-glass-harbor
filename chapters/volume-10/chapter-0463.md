# Chapter 463

*Monday the Twenty-Fifth Day of October, a Rota Day, and the Ninety-Third Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still off It, a Line Written on a Page in a Woman of Thirty's Own Mouth on the Twenty-Second and Read Out Aloud in a Room over a Fish Market at About Half Past Ten in the Morning Before About Twenty-Two People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read, the Line Being That the Echo May Inform a Public Record and It May Not Decide Anything This Party Does, a Name and an Hour Under It and Nobody Asked to Agree With It, a Man of About Thirty-One Who Keeps a Chandler's Store on the Second Lay Saying Out Loud That He Does Not Agree With It and Saying Why and Not Being Argued With and Not Being Thanked, a Day Apiece in Advance to Four Men Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Monday the twenty-fifth day of October, in a room over a fish market, and at the back of that market, and in that shed.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-third time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that room over a fish market and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name is on two public sheets and nobody took it off either and nobody thanked her. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and did not move the eleventh piece. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-one days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-three days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fifteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and one days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-seventh night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-five days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and three days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the fortieth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-fifth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-second day. A man of about twenty-nine in that harbor could not hear out of the left side of his head on the twentieth, on the twenty-first, on the twenty-second, on the twenty-third, on the twenty-fourth or on the twenty-fifth, and has not been asked about it and did not ask and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

There were about twenty-two people in that room over a fish market at about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-fifth of October and about eleven of them could not read, and the reader stood at the long table with her back to the stove that does not draw and read it out twice, once for the people at the front and once for the people at the back, and did not raise her voice for the second one.

The page is a page and not a sheet and not a sheet of rules and not the sheet that carries the money, and it does not go on the wall of that room, and the sheet of four heads that is on that wall is not changed by anything on it.

**"The echo may inform a public record, and it may not decide anything this party does."**

That was the whole of the line. She read it out twice and then she read the two things under it, which were a name and an hour.

"Mara Venn," she said. "Ten past ten in the morning on the twenty-second day of October, in a shed in this harbor, in my own hand, and I wrote it on that page the same morning and I have not changed one word of it since and nobody has asked me to and nobody in this room is going to be asked whether they agree with it, and if you do not agree with it, you have not lost anything by being in this room and you are not on any list because of it."

**And the sheet with the four heads on it stayed on the wall of that room with nothing written above it and nothing written beside it, and the sheet that carries the money was in another room four hundred yards off and was not in that room, and neither of the two sheets was on that wall, and the day that the four heads were written and the day the money sheet was bought are thirteen days and eleven days ago and are two different days in two different rooms and are not going to be put in one room again without both of them being named out loud.**

---

A man of about thirty-one who keeps a chandler's store on the second lay put his hand up at about ten past eleven in the morning and said out loud, in that room, that he did not agree with it, and he said why, and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and his disagreement went into the same minute as the rule and is on the same line of that minute and is not under it.

"I do not agree with it," he said, "and I want it written down that I said so in this room and not afterwards. Here is why. That line was written by the people who were on the water. Five of them and me and nobody else. I sell rope in this harbor and I sell it to the boats that go out on it and I sell it to the boats that come back off it, and I have got no more to do with that thing on the bottom than the man who laid the last course of that wall has. A rule that the people on the water write and the people on the shore have to live under is a rule about a boat, and I have had men tell me a thing was for my own good before and I have never once got a better price off the man who said it. I am not saying it is wrong. I am saying it was not written by me and I would rather it had been, and I would rather still if I had been asked whether I wanted to live under it, and nobody did, and I am not going to pretend to a room that somebody did."

Mara Venn said one sentence back and it was the only sentence she said in that room on that day.

"Then it stands, and you are on the minute, and nobody is going to come to your door about it, and you were not asked and you were not represented and you are not on it." She put the pencil down. "I have written down that you said no and what you said, in your own words and not mine, and it is the same minute and the same line and it is not smaller than the rule."

Nobody in that room took it up. A woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay and who had said out loud on the sixteenth of October that her money was for flour said that it was about time somebody said that, and nobody answered her, and she was not thanked for saying it either.

---

A day apiece in advance went to four men at about two in the afternoon on the said Monday, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, and each of the four wrote his own name against his own line, and one of the four asked what happens if a man's air goes on the second trip and was given the same answer he had been given on the sixteenth, which is that the tender pulls and the man comes up and the day is over and the man is paid the day.

**And on the said Monday the fitting-out sheet stood at seven thousand seven hundred and sixty pence in and nine thousand eight hundred and six pence out, and it is short by two thousand and forty-six pence, being twelve marks, ten shillings and sixpence, and that figure was said out loud in that shed at about half past two in the afternoon to five people and was not written in that room over a fish market and did not go on the wall of that room, and the two sheets are not one sheet and the four heads on that wall decide no contested claim and bind nobody who has not signed them.**

---

**A LINE WAS READ OUT TWICE IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET BEFORE ABOUT TWENTY-TWO PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT ELEVEN COULD NOT READ, AND IT WAS NOT PUT ON THE WALL OF THAT ROOM AND IT DID NOT CHANGE THE FOUR HEADS THAT ARE ON IT, AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM WAS ASKED TO AGREE WITH IT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE DID NOT AGREE WITH IT AND SAID WHY IN HIS OWN MOUTH, AND HE WAS NOT ARGUED WITH AND WAS NOT THANKED, AND WHAT HE SAID IS ON THE SAME LINE OF THE SAME MINUTE AS THE RULE AND IS NOT UNDER IT. THAT IS A BOUNDARY AND NOT A RECONCILIATION, AND IT WAS NOT DECIDED IN A ROOM WITH TWO PEOPLE IN IT, AND THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THE LAST DAY OF THIS MOVEMENT AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-fifth day of October at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-third time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that about twenty-two people were in a room over a fish market at about half past ten in the morning and about eleven of them could not read; and that a page with one line on it was read out twice by a woman of thirty of no office, being that the echo may inform a public record and it may not decide anything this party does, with her own name and the hour of ten past ten in the morning on the twenty-second day of October under it, and that that page is not the sheet that carries the money, is not the four heads on the wall of that room, and was not put on that wall and does not go on it; and that a man of about thirty-one who keeps a chandler's store on the second lay said out loud in that room at about ten past eleven in the morning that he did not agree with it and said why in his own mouth, and was not argued with and was not thanked, and that what he said is on the same line of the same minute as the rule; and that a day apiece in advance went to four men at about two in the afternoon, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, each in his own hand; and that the fitting-out sheet stands at seven thousand seven hundred and sixty in and nine thousand eight hundred and six out and is short by two thousand and forty-six pence, being twelve marks, ten shillings and sixpence, and that figure was said out loud in that shed and was not written in that room over a fish market.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the line was read out twice and not once is in a woman of thirty's own mouth, and is that a room with eleven people in it who cannot read is not served by a page and is served by a voice going round it, and that the second reading was not louder and was not slower; and that the reason nobody in that room was asked to agree with the line is her own, and is that a rule that is a sentence can be argued with and a rule that is a page with a name and an hour under it can be read out in a room where about eleven people cannot read and can be heard by all of them, and that a line nobody was asked to agree with does not need them to; and that the reason a man who keeps a chandler's store was not argued with is in his own mouth and is that he is not a member of anything and has not asked to be represented by anybody, and that what a person says against a rule in a room of working people is not a breach of the rule and is not a reason to leave the room, and that if the yard had argued with him the yard would have been doing the thing the line is against; and that the reason his refusal is on the same line of the same minute as the rule and is not under it is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a figure or a word that is put under another one on a page is smaller whether the person who wrote it meant it to be or not; and that the reason a second ask is coming and is written in different words and on a different page is that a person who is asked a second time in the same words has not been asked a second time; and that the reason nothing standing was bettered, advanced or made smaller on the said Monday is in the yard's own mouth, and is that the day was given to a rule being read out and to a man saying out loud that he did not agree with it, and that a day given to those two things is not given to an account, and that an account left alone on such a day is one somebody chose to leave alone.*
