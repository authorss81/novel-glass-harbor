# Chapter 486

*Thursday the Eighteenth Day of November, a Rota Day, the Hundredth Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Figure of a Hundred Said Out Loud on That Deck Once and Standing on a Slate in That Market and in No Other Place in the World and Not Said Again, Thirty Fathoms of Line for the Lifting Going onto the Sheet That Carries the Money at About Ten in the Morning at Five Hundred and Ten Pence, Being Three Marks, Two Shillings and Sixpence, and Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand, One Descent on the Second of Those Four Hired Days with a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air on the Slate and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff He Could Pull Without Asking, a Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Going Down at About Ten o'Clock and Coming Up at About a Quarter to Eleven with the Last Four Baskets on a Line Instead of in His Own Hands, a Crate and Matting and a Lid Filled on That After Deck, and the Cost of It That Nobody Was Warned About, Being That the Crate Does Not Go Under the Stove-Pipe Bracket and the After Deck Has to Be Cleared and a Man Who Has Slept Under That Bracket for Three Weeks Goes Into the Forepeak With the Boy and the Boy Gives Up His Bunk Without Being Asked, a Page Old and Damp at One Edge That Will Not Sit Flat and Cannot Be Cut and Was Not Cut, Named Out Loud on That Deck and Written on No Sheet, and Nobody Asked to Decide It and Nobody Thanked, the Shortfall at Thirteen Thousand One Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence, Being Eighty-Two Marks, One Shilling and Fourpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Thursday the eighteenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundredth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. Thirty fathoms of line for the lifting went onto the sheet that carries the money at about ten in the morning at five hundred and ten pence, being three marks, two shillings and sixpence, and it is the second of the four days hired off that supply boat on the seventeenth and nothing went into that sheet in any hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at twenty-three thousand seven hundred and seventy-six pence out, being one hundred and forty-eight marks and eight shillings, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirteen thousand one hundred and thirty-six pence short, being eighty-two marks, one shilling and fourpence. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninetieth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eight days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixty-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-six days. Physic in that harbor is at fourteen pence and there is none of it, and one berth is short on the third lay. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does, and he worked that compressor for about two hours that afternoon on his own account.**

**And the man who keeps the leaf said the figure of that hundred out loud on that deck at about half past five in the afternoon, once, and it is on the cistern slate in that market and on no other board and no other paper of any kind, and he is not going to say it again and he was asked by nobody.**

---

**Thirty fathoms of line came off that supply boat on the Wednesday with the cylinders and was not paid for until the Thursday, and the reason the man who keeps the leaf gave for that order is his own, and is that line which is on a boat for a day and not on a sheet is a line that gets cut for a job nobody has agreed to pay for, and that he would rather be an hour out of order than be a man who pays for things after he has had them.**

**A slate was written before anybody went down and the man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down wrote his own reason on it, and the reason was that there are four baskets standing on the floor of that room against the long rack and that a basket on a wet floor with no line to it is a basket a man has to pick up off that floor with his own judgment about how much of it he can hold, and that his own judgment is not a figure anybody can check.**

He went down at about ten o'clock in the morning on that ebb under that slate with a figure of forty minutes of air on it and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave, and the air was out of cylinders hired off that supply boat on the day before.

He was in the water about forty-five minutes and on the bottom about thirty of them and came up at about a quarter to eleven, and the four baskets came up on thirty fathoms of line one at a time and went onto that tarpaulin and nobody carried one of them in his own hands.

---

**And the crate was filled on that after deck from about eleven o'clock in the morning until about three in the afternoon, and it did not go under the stove-pipe bracket, and nobody had been warned about that, and the reason nobody had been warned about it is that nobody had any idea what was in that room until a basket came up out of it on the Saturday.**

**A crate and matting and a lid is about three foot by two foot by two foot and a boat of about nineteen tons has about three foot of clear deck under a stove-pipe bracket and a stove pipe in it, and the crate will not go under the bracket, and the after deck had to be cleared to make a place for it, and the man who had been sleeping under that bracket for three weeks had to go somewhere.**

The master of that boat did the arithmetic in about four seconds and said it out loud once and did not say it again.

"Right. The boy keeps the forepeak and the boy is not being put out of it, and there is a man of about thirty-seven of that crew who has been in my forepeak on and off for three weeks and who can have it tonight. Nobody is to be told that he is being put out of anywhere, because he is not, and because I have had a man stand on my after deck at half past one in the morning this trip telling me what his rights are and I have listened to that speech from a man of about forty-one and I am not having it from anybody at two in the morning."

**Nobody thanked anybody for any part of it. No figure is set against the man who moved and none against the boy and none against that deck, and the boy gave up his bunk without being asked and was not thanked for it and did not say one word about it, and the man who went into the forepeak put his bag down and did not say one word about it either, and the two of them were not thanked and did not ask.**

---

**And a page would not go into that crate, and the party did not cut it, and the reason they did not cut it is that a page is a thing somebody wrote.**

It was brown at the bottom edge for about three inches across the whole width of it and the brown had gone into the fibres and not into the surface, and where it had gone in the fibres the sheet would not fold and would not sit flat, and a folded sheet in a crate of folded sheets is a sheet that will be taken out and looked at once and put back in wet, and a cut is a cut.

**The woman of thirty of no office said out loud what it was and said it once and it went on no sheet of any kind.** "That is the third thing off the second shelf of the rack that is against the back wall on the north side of that room, and it is the one with the brown edge, and it is going back under the weight on that tarpaulin with the other two like it, and I am not writing that down on anything and I am not cutting it. Somebody will have to know where it is and the only way they will know where it is is if somebody on this boat remembers it, and I am the one who is going to remember it, and that is the whole of the arrangement."

**And the man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down said one sentence about it, and nobody argued with him about the page and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him to say it a second time.**

"I have been in holds nineteen years and I have cut about four hundred pieces of paper in my life and I cut every one of them because the man who owned it was standing there telling me to. Nobody on this boat owns a page out of that room and that includes me, and a man who cuts a thing nobody owns is a man who has decided what the thing was, and there is nobody in that harbor to say he was right." He put the sheet down flat under the weight. "It can sit flat or it can sit crooked until somebody comes who owns it. I am not going to decide what it is and neither is anybody else on this boat."

**Nobody was asked to decide it. Nobody voted on it. Nobody put it in a column. It went on no sheet of any kind in that boat and it is on no sheet in that harbor and the reason it is not in the first column is that the first column is a thing a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take off a page, and there is no figure on any paper for a wet page sitting under a weight on a tarpaulin, and the reason it is not in the second column is that the man who keeps the leaf was asked to put it there and said no.**

---

**THE HUNDREDTH TIME THAT CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED WAS ON THAT THURSDAY AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND IT WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND THE FIGURE OF THAT HUNDRED IS ON A SLATE IN THAT MARKET AND NOWHERE ELSE, AND IT WAS SAID OUT LOUD ON THAT DECK ONCE AND IS NOT GOING TO BE SAID AGAIN. THIRTY FATHOMS OF LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE, BEING THREE MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE. A CRATE AND MATTING AND A LID CAME UP FULL AND DID NOT GO UNDER THE STOVE-PIPE BRACKET, AND THE COST OF THAT, WHICH NOBODY WAS WARNED ABOUT, IS THAT ONE MAN OF THAT PARTY SLEPT IN THE FOREPEAK THAT NIGHT WITH THE BOY AND THE BOY GAVE UP HIS BUNK AND WAS NOT ASKED, AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST ANY OF IT. A PAGE THAT WOULD NOT GO FLAT WAS NOT CUT AND WAS NAMED OUT LOUD ONCE AND WAS WRITTEN ON NO SHEET, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TO DECIDE IT. AND THAT SHEET IS THIRTEEN THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING EIGHTY-TWO MARKS, ONE SHILLING AND FOURPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the man who keeps the leaf said the figure of that hundred out loud on that deck at about half past five in the afternoon, once, and that the figure is on the cistern slate in that market and on no other board and no other paper of any kind, and that he is not going to say it again and nobody asked him to; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason a page that would not go flat was not cut is the yard's own mouth, and is that a page is a thing somebody wrote, and that a cut in a page is a statement about what the page is and by whom, and that a party which cuts a page it has found has begun to read it, and that reading it is a thing that belongs to whoever ends up holding it and not to nine men on a deck in twenty-two fathoms; and that the reason it was named out loud once and put on no sheet is that woman's own, and is that the first column and the second column are both columns somebody keeps, and that a page under a weight on a tarpaulin is not a thing a ledger is for, and that the whole of the arrangement is one woman remembering where a wet sheet of paper is, and that she said so out loud on purpose, in front of nine people, on a deck, so that it could not afterwards be said that nobody had been told; and that the reason the man who keeps the leaf said no to putting it in the second column is his own, and is that the second column is for things nine men did and reasons nine men gave, and that a thing that happened to a piece of paper and not to a man is not a thing that belongs in a column about men, and that he would rather have nine people remembering it than have a stranger with a leaf reading it and not knowing who to ask; and that the reason the cost of that crate was put on that deck out loud by the master of that boat in four seconds is his own, and is that there is a man in his forepeak and a boy in it, and that a man of about forty-one has already made a speech on his after deck at half past one in the morning this trip, and that he is not doing the arithmetic twice; and that the reason the crate line went on the sheet a day after the line it was bought with is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that line which is on a boat and not on a sheet gets cut for a job nobody has agreed to pay for, and that he would rather be an hour out of order than be a man who pays after he has had; and that the reason the whole of that harbor's standing condition went into that week where it was on the said Thursday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a hundred is a figure on a slate and a figure on a slate is not a thing anybody in that harbor can do anything with, and that a cistern at the back of that market is charged twice a week and walked on at both charges and neither of those is a thing a deck can do about, and that physic in that harbor is at fourteen pence and there is none of it and one berth is short on the third lay and both of those are on that Thursday and neither of them is on that deck.*
