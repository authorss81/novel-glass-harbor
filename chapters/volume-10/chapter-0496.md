# Chapter 496

*Sunday the Twenty-Eighth Day of November, a Wipe, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench in That Harbor Coming Down Bare for the Eighty-Second Time and Walked on by None of It, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Fitting-Out Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Nineteen Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Twenty-Four Marks, Twelve Shillings and Fourpence, a Room over a Fish Market at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon with About Twenty People in It and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Sheet Written on the Saturday in That Yard's Own Hand Carrying on Its Face the Rule That the Reader's Own Name Goes Under What He Reads and the Hour Under His Name and That Anybody in That Room May Stop Him at Any Word He Is Saying, Read Out Loud Twice by Hobb Lee of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets on the Third Lay, Nobody Stopping Him in the First Reading, a Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Stopping Him in the Second at a Figure of Sheets in a Bundle and That Figure Being Corrected on the Face of That Paper With the Wrong Word Left Where It Was and Readable and the Corrector Refusing to Give a Name and Not Being Asked for One and the Whole of That Sheet Being Read a Third Time From the Top With the Correction on It, Neither the First Column of That Leaf nor the Second of It nor the Fitting-Out Sheet Being in That Room or on Any Wall in It and Both of the Two Sheets Being Named Out Loud There So That Nobody in It Could Say Afterwards They Had Been Mixed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Sunday the twenty-eighth day of November, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is lying off the third lay.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare for the eighty-second time and was walked on by none of it, and it went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty thousand six hundred and twenty-eight pence out, being one hundred and ninety-one marks, five shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is nineteen thousand nine hundred and eighty-eight pence short, being one hundred and twenty-four marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, and that figure has not moved since the Saturday. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-four days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-six; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-four days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundredth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eighteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventy-third day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-eighth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and fifth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-six days. The mole-head light in that harbor is out and its lens has been out since the seventh day of May and there is no figure on any board in that harbor for the putting of it back. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month. The hose is dead until the spring and will be dead at the spring. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does, and the holder of that writ could not get his cup down off a counter at breakfast that morning without putting two fingers on it, and no figure is set against that anywhere either.**

---

**The wall came down at about half past ten in the morning and it is the eighty-second time it has come down bare and gone up unrepaired, and four people of that harbor walked past it between then and the middle of the day and not one of them put a bucket under it, and the man who keeps the leaf did not walk past it at all because he was in that shed with the door shut and a sheet in front of him.**

---

**And the sheet that carries the money was not in that room over that market and was not on any wall in it and was not read out in it, and neither was the first column of that leaf nor the second of it, and the man who keeps the leaf said both of those things out loud in that room in the open at about ten minutes to two in the afternoon, standing where the long table is, so that nobody in that room could go out of it afterwards and say the two sheets had been on one table or in one mouth.**

"Neither of them is in this room," he said. "The sheet that carries the money is not in this room and it is not on that wall and it is not going to be read out to you this afternoon, and the first column of that leaf and the second column of that leaf are two other things and they are not in this room either and neither of them has ever been on that wall. If anybody in here wants either of those two things said out loud in a room like this one, they can ask me for it and I will say it out loud, and it will not be this afternoon."

Nobody asked. A woman of about fifty-eight with an end stall and a slate said, out loud, that a thing being off a wall was a thing that could be got at in a week and a thing being read out was a thing that was heard once, and that the harbor had a great deal of the second kind of thing in it and had never once had the other.

---

**The room over that fish market was let out on a Sunday and a bowl went down on the step and about twenty people went up in the half hour after two o'clock, and about eleven of them could not read. Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay and has done since he was nine, stood at the long table with his back to a stove that does not draw and read out a sheet written the day before in the hand of a woman of about twenty-seven of that yard, being the account of what was done in that harbor on the twenty-seventh of November and what was not done in it.**

He read it out twice. He did not raise his voice for the second one and he did not go slower at it.

At the head of that sheet, under the account and above it, there is a rule, and the rule was written before the account and not after it, and it is on the face of the paper in the same hand as the account and it says that the reader's own name goes under what he reads and the hour goes under his name, and that anybody standing in that room may stop him at any word he is saying and put their own words against his, and that what goes into the book afterward is what is on the paper and not what he makes of it going to it.

Hobb Lee put his own name and the hour under both readings, in his own hand, being his own name and the hour of two o'clock and of twenty-five minutes to two.

Nobody stopped him in the first reading.

---

**And a man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down, who has been in holds nineteen years, stopped him in the second reading, at about twenty minutes to three in the afternoon, at one word in the ninth line.**

"That is fifty."

"That is fifty in a hand that was not in that hatch, and I was in that hatch." He did not raise his voice either. "I counted them on the hatch head on the Tuesday before we came away and I counted them twice, because a man who has been in holds nineteen years does not carry a bundle he has not counted, and there were about forty of them in the four and not fifty."

Hobb Lee stopped. He put his thumb against the ninth line and left it there while the man finished, and then he read the ninth line again exactly as it was written, including the figure of fifty, so that the people at the back of that room heard the wrong figure a second time and knew what was being argued at.

Then he asked for a name to go against the correction.

"Put the trade and the years and the hour against it and not the name," the man said. "I would rather a man who thinks that figure is wrong came down that hatch and counted them himself than had my name on a paper in a room in a harbor where I have never worked."

Hobb Lee wrote it on the face of that sheet, under the ninth line, in his own hand, being the trade and the years and the hour of twenty minutes to three, and he wrote that the corrector would not give a name and was not asked for one, and he wrote that the wrong figure was left where it was and was not rubbed out and not scraped and not taken off with a knife.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody argued with him. A man of about thirty-one who keeps a chandler's store on the second lay said, out loud, that a figure corrected in a room of about eleven people who cannot read is a figure corrected eleven times over, because in a fortnight there would be eleven people saying they heard it changed and none of them could read what it was changed from, and Hobb Lee said that was a fair thing to say and that it was the reason the wrong word was left on the paper and not taken off it.

And then Hobb Lee read the whole of that sheet out a third time from the top, with the rule at the head of it and the ninth line in it as it stands with the correction under it, and about eleven people in that room heard all of it, and it took eleven minutes, and the second time it had taken nine.

---

**And nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day, and that fact is not on the face of that sheet and is in the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth, and the woman who wrote that sheet was asked about it once at about half past three by a man who wanted to put a line under it and was told that a line under it would be a line about the sheet and not a line about the harbor.**

---

**A WIPE CAME DOWN BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH FOR THE EIGHTY-SECOND TIME AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. A SHEET WAS READ OUT TWICE IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET BY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX WHO MENDS NETS AND WAS PAID NOTHING FOR IT AND WAS NOT THANKED, AND A FIGURE OF ABOUT FIFTY SHEETS WAS CORRECTED TO ABOUT FORTY IN THE SECOND READING BY A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-ONE WHO HAS BEEN IN HOLDS NINETEEN YEARS, WHO WOULD NOT GIVE A NAME AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR ONE, AND THE WRONG FIGURE WAS LEFT ON THE FACE OF THAT PAPER UNDER THE CORRECTION AND WAS READ OUT AGAIN AS THE WRONG FIGURE BEFORE THE CORRECTION WAS READ OUT, AND THEN THE WHOLE OF THAT SHEET WAS READ A THIRD TIME. THE FITTING-OUT SHEET IS SHORT BY NINETEEN THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-EIGHT PENCE AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THAT FIGURE IS AGAINST THAT YARD'S OWN BOOK AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-eighth day of November at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare for the eighty-second time and was walked on by none of it and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back, and that nobody in that harbor has put it back on any of the eighty-two; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty thousand six hundred and twenty-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is nineteen thousand nine hundred and eighty-eight pence short, being one hundred and twenty-four marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the day before; and that a room over a fish market was let out on that Sunday and a bowl went down on the step and about twenty people went up in the half hour after two o'clock and about eleven of them could not read; and that Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay and has done since he was nine, read out twice a sheet written the day before in the hand of Nessa Pike, of about twenty-seven of that yard, being the account of what was done in that harbor on the twenty-seventh of November and what was not done in it, and that he put his own name and the hour of two o'clock and of twenty-five minutes to two under both readings in his own hand, and that he was paid nothing for it and was not thanked for it and nobody thanked him for reading a thing out in that room on the tenth day of October past either; and that the rule that the reader's name goes under what he reads and the hour goes under his name and that anybody in that room may stop him at any word and put their own words against him and that what goes into the book is what is on the paper and not what he makes of it going to it is on the face of that sheet and was written before the account and not after it; and that nobody stopped him in the first reading; and that a man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down, who has been in holds nineteen years, stopped him in the second reading at about twenty minutes to three in the afternoon at one word in the ninth line, being that a figure of about fifty sheets in four bundles was about forty and not fifty, and that he said that he had counted them on the hatch head on the Tuesday before that boat came away and had counted them twice; and that the reader read the ninth line again as it was written with the wrong figure in it before the correction was read out, so that the people at the back of that room heard the wrong figure twice; and that he gave no name and was not asked for one, and that the correction went on the face of that sheet under the ninth line in the reader's own hand with the trade and the years and the hour against it and not a name, and that the wrong figure was left where it was and was not rubbed out and not scraped and not taken off with a knife; and that the whole of that sheet was read out a third time from the top with the correction standing under the ninth line and it took about eleven minutes and the second reading had taken about nine; and that nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him and no figure is set against him anywhere and he is not a casualty and nobody has named him one; and that the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on any wall in it and was not read out in it, and that the first column of that leaf and the second column of that leaf were not in that room and neither of them has been on that wall, and that the man who keeps the leaf said both of those things out loud in that room in the open at about ten minutes to two in the afternoon so that nobody in that room could say afterwards that two sheets had been in one room or in one mouth; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with an end stall and a slate said out loud in that room that a thing being off a wall is a thing that can be got at in a week and a thing being read out is a thing that is heard once; and that nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and twenty-nine, one thousand and nineteen, none and one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the account of the Saturday was read out on the Sunday and not kept for a week is the yard's own mouth, and is that a day gets into a harbor by being said out loud while the people in it can still remember the day before, and that a Friday's business read on the following Tuesday is a Tuesday's business and not a Friday's; and that the reason nobody stopped the reader in the first reading is the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who has been in holds nineteen years does not take a stranger's word for a bundle of paper on a first hearing, and that the whole value of a rule that anybody may stop a reader at any word is that it costs nothing until somebody uses it, and that a rule nobody has ever used yet is not known to be real by the people in the room who would want it to be; and that the reason the wrong figure was left on the paper instead of being taken off it is the reader's own, and is that a paper with a wrong word on it and a correction under it can be checked by anybody who was in the room and a paper with the wrong word scraped off it can be checked by nobody at all except the man who scraped it, and that this yard has three claims standing against it and is not going to be the fourth kind of party that edits what it has published; and that the reason a man who would not give his name was not asked for one is the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who will stand up in a room of about eleven people who cannot read and say a number is wrong has done the whole of what that rule was written for, and that asking him his name after that would be asking him for a second thing; and that the reason the two sheets were named out loud in that room as being not in it is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that the damage done to a sheet by being read out in a room it was not written for is done in the first ten minutes and cannot be undone in the next six weeks, and that this yard found out what that is in October and is saying so out loud in a room over a fish market before it is done to it.*
