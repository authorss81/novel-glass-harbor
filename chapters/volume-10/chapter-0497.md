# Chapter 497

*Monday the Twenty-Ninth Day of November, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Third Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Figure of That Hundred Said Out Loud Once on That Deck on the Eighteenth and Not Said Out Loud a Second Time, Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Boat of About Nineteen Tons, Five Days Under Way at Two Hundred and Fifty Pence a Day at One Thousand Two Hundred and Fifty Pence, Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eleven in the Morning With the Twenty-Ninth Against It and Being the Settlement and Not the Hiring, Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand, the Shortfall at Twenty-One Thousand Two Hundred and Thirty-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Thirty-Two Marks, Nine Shillings and Tenpence, a Room over a Fish Market at About Three o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People in It and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, a Man of About Fifty of the Second Lay Proposing Out Loud at About Ten Past Three That There Be a Roll of the People Who Put Nothing In, a Ropemaker of About Sixty-One in That Room Saying No to That in About Four Words, Nobody Writing It Down, Nobody Being Asked a Second Time, No Roll Existing in That Harbor Afterwards, and a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Being Asked in That Room a Question That Was Not About His Book and Answering It in One Word and Not Being Asked Why, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Monday the twenty-ninth day of November, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is lying off the third lay.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and third time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on that deck on the eighteenth of November and is not said out loud a second time. A boat of about nineteen tons, five days under way at two hundred and fifty pence a day, went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at one thousand two hundred and fifty pence, and the twenty-ninth of November is the day that line is settled on and not the day it was hired. Nothing went into that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-one thousand eight hundred and seventy-eight pence out, being one hundred and ninety-nine marks, three shillings and twopence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-one thousand two hundred and thirty-eight pence short, being one hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and tenpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-five days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-seven; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-five days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and first night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and nineteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventy-fourth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-ninth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and sixth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-seven days. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and a further week has not fallen due since the sixteenth of November and does not fall due on this day. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered and nothing has come in on the entry to move it. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does, and it did not lift on the twenty-eighth and it is not going to lift this trip.**

---

**And the boat line went on at about eleven in the morning with the twenty-ninth against it and the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of what that line was before he wrote a figure against it, and nobody argued with him about any part of it.**

"Five days under way at two hundred and fifty pence a day, being one thousand two hundred and fifty pence. That is the settlement of five days she was at sea and it goes on the twenty-ninth because the twenty-ninth is the day the boat's owner came and stood at the end of that bench and asked me for it, and not because the twenty-ninth is the day it was hired. It is the same figure as the last time and I have not moved it and I am not going to move it this week."

"And is she going out again."

"She is going out again when her master says she is, and I have not asked him and I am not going to, and if he sails on Thursday I shall be here on the twenty-eighth paying a day at the figure in chalk and putting that day against his name like anybody else." He put the pen down. "That boat came in on the Saturday with her master in her and her two men and her boy and nobody on this quay has asked him why he came, and I am not going to start on a Monday."

---

**The room over that fish market was let out three days running that week, on the Sunday before it and on the Monday of it, and it had been let out on that Sunday at about half past one in the afternoon with a bowl on the step, and on the Monday the man who lets it out did not ask who was coming and nobody put a bowl on the step, because there is nobody in that harbor who has a bowl on that step for a Monday. About twenty people went up and about eleven of them could not read, and the stove in that room does not draw and did not draw on that day either.**

---

**And at about ten past three in the afternoon a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay, who wrote a thing on the wall of that room on the tenth of October past and has not said the name of it since, proposed out loud that there be a roll.**

"Write down the people who put nothing in," he said. "Not to move them. To stop them being asked again. Any one of us who has said no to that sheet has had to be a person with a memory, and some of us have better memories than others, and there are people in this harbor who could stand in a room in six weeks and be asked a second time because nobody in front of them remembers that they said it once. Put their names down. Write no against them. Then nobody has to remember."

There were about nine or ten people at the back of that room who cannot read and there were about two at the front who can.

**A ropemaker of about sixty-one, who is not in her room on a Sunday and has not been in one for two years, said no to that in about four words, from about nine feet back, and the man who wrote on that wall on the tenth of October did not answer her.**

"There is no roll."

"Then somebody will be asked twice."

"Yes. And a man in this room in six weeks will be able to say to that person, you said no to me once, in a room, and I have a paper, and it will be true, and it will be worse than being asked cold." She had not moved off the wall she was standing against. "And it will be worse for them than it is now because now it is one man with a bad memory and afterwards it is a room with a sheet. **I put nothing in.** Not one penny of mine is on that sheet on any line and nobody asked me for one and nobody asked me twice. I am not going to be on a paper for it, and I came up those stairs this afternoon anyway, because a man who wants a list of us ought to be made to stand up in the room with the rest of you and be told no to his face rather than go home and write it out."

**And then the holder of that writ said the only other thing that was said about it, and it was in his own mouth and it was in the open, and nobody thanked either of them for it.**

"No. And I want it written down that it was no in this room on this day and not in a shed, and that the two of us who said it are the holder of that writ and a ropemaker of about sixty-one, and that is all that goes in the minute about it. It is not going to be the fourth thing on that table with the three others on it, and I am not going to ask this room to hold a list of the people it has not got anything from."

Nobody wrote anything. There is no roll of the people who put nothing in in that room, and there is none in that harbor, and there is not going to be one, and the three who said no stand on that sheet at the same size as every figure on it and none of the three has been asked a second time in any of the forty-seven days of that leaf.

---

**And at about twenty past four in the afternoon a man at the front of that room put a question to the man of about thirty-four of the second lay, and it was not about his book, and he answered it in one word, and nobody asked him why, and nobody asked him a second question.**

"Are you going to say that that thing in a case should be put on this wall?"

"No."

"And why not."

"There was not going to be a why," he said. "I said no to the putting of it on the wall and that is the whole of what I said and you asked me a second question about a thing I had not put on the wall. Ask me about the rope."

Nobody asked him about the rope.

---

**And nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day, and the deficit on that sheet is said out loud in that yard's own mouth about half past five on the Monday afternoon and nobody in that harbor was asked for the rest of it and nobody in that harbor was asked to put a mark on anything.**

---

**THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND THIRD TIME AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. A BOAT OF ABOUT NINETEEN TONS, FIVE DAYS UNDER WAY AT TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE A DAY AT ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE, WENT ON THAT SHEET AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING WITH THE TWENTY-NINTH AGAINST IT AS A SETTLEMENT AND NOT AS A HIRING. A ROLL OF THE PEOPLE WHO PUT NOTHING IN WAS PROPOSED IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET AT ABOUT TEN PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS REFUSED IN ABOUT FOUR WORDS BY A ROPEMAKER OF ABOUT SIXTY-ONE AND AGAIN IN THE OPEN BY THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT, AND NO SUCH ROLL EXISTS IN THAT HARBOR AND NONE IS GOING TO AND THE THREE WHO SAID NO STAND ON THAT SHEET AT THE SAME SIZE AS EVERY FIGURE ON IT. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-ninth day of November at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and third time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it, and that the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on that deck on the eighteenth of November and is not said out loud a second time; and that a boat of about nineteen tons, five days under way at two hundred and fifty pence a day, went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at one thousand two hundred and fifty pence with the twenty-ninth of November against it, and that the twenty-ninth is the day that line was settled on and not the day it was hired, and that it is the same figure as the last time and it has not been moved; and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day and nobody was asked for anything, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-one thousand eight hundred and seventy-eight pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-one thousand two hundred and thirty-eight pence short, being one hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and tenpence, and that the running out on it moved for the first time since the twenty-fourth day of November and went up by one thousand two hundred and fifty pence and nothing came in against it; and that the room over that fish market was let out on that Monday, being the second of three days running that week on which it was let out, and the man who lets it out did not ask who was coming and nobody put a bowl on the step, and that about twenty people went up and about eleven of them could not read; and that at about ten past three in the afternoon a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay proposed out loud that a roll be written of the people who put nothing in, so that nobody in that harbor would be asked a second time because nobody in front of them remembered that they had said it once; and that a ropemaker of about sixty-one, who is not in her room on a Sunday, said no to that in about four words and said that a man in this room in six weeks would be able to say to a person that they had said it once and that he had a paper, and that that would be worse than being asked cold, and that a man with a bad memory is one man and a room with a sheet is a room, and that she said out loud in that room that she put nothing in, that not one penny of hers is on that sheet on any line, that nobody asked her for one and nobody asked her twice, that she is not going to be on a paper for it, and that she came up those stairs that afternoon anyway because a man who wants a list of the people who put nothing in ought to be made to stand up in the room and be told no to his face; and that the holder of that writ said no again in his own mouth and in the open and asked for it to go in the minute that it was no in that room on that day and not in a shed, and asked for the two of them to be named as saying it and for nothing else about it, and that he refused to have it be the fourth thing on that table with the three others on it and refused to ask that room to hold a list of the people it has not got anything from; and that nobody wrote anything and that there is no roll of the people who put nothing in in that room and there is none in that harbor and there is not going to be one, and that the three who said no stand on that sheet at the same size as every figure on it and none of the three has been asked a second time in any of the forty-seven days of that leaf; and that at about twenty past four in the afternoon a man at the front of that room asked the man of about thirty-four of the second lay whether that thing in a case should be put on that wall, and that it was not a question about his book, and that he said no and was asked why and said that there was not going to be a why and that a second question had been asked about a thing he had not put on the wall, and that nobody asked him about the rope; and that nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and thirty, one thousand and twenty-four, none and one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that boat line was settled on the twenty-ninth of November and not on the day that boat came in is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a settlement dated by the day the money was asked for is the only date on that sheet a stranger can go and check, and that a line dated by the day a thing happened is a line that can be argued about for a fortnight by a man who wants the day to have been a different one; and that the reason the figure of that hundred was not said out loud a second time is the yard's own mouth, and is that a figure said twice becomes the sort of figure that people start checking, and that the cistern is charged on a rota and is walked on at every charge and neither of those facts has ever once in this harbor been in want of a number to be said twice; and that the reason a man of about fifty who owns a boat asked for a roll is his own, and is that a man who has been in a room where a person was asked a second time about a thing they had said no to once does not stop thinking about it in a week, and that this yard would rather have its fourth claim be about a bad memory than about a sheet; and that the reason a ropemaker of about sixty-one was in that room on that Monday at all is the yard's own mouth, and is that the room is let out to anybody who comes up the stairs and that a woman who is not in her room on a Sunday and has not been in one for two years came up those stairs on a Monday, and that a party which has spent nine weeks in a harbor hearing from people who cannot come to it has no way of knowing that a person is refusing out of the same habit as themselves until the person is in the room; and that the reason not one standing debt in that harbor moved on that Monday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a Monday in that harbor in that month is a day on which the tide gang is four men short and the hard at Ashtide is unanswered, and that neither of those is a thing that eleven people putting a week's wage each into a different sheet can reach, and that a yard which starts paying at the easy end of a harbor's list of debts is a yard that will be at the hard end by Christmas with a better ledger and nothing bought.*
