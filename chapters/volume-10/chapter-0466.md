# Chapter 466

*Thursday the Twenty-Eighth Day of October, a Rota Day, and the Ninety-Fourth Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still off It, the Man of About Fifty Who Owns a Boat of About Twenty-Two Tons of the Second Lay Putting In Four Marks More, Being Six Hundred and Forty Pence, and the Pump Hand of About Forty-Four Putting In the Two Marks He Said He Would Put In on the Morning, and a Man of About Twenty-Eight Who Crews a Boat of About Nine Tons Saying Out Loud That He Will Not Go Again and Giving a Reason That Names His Own Hand, Written on the Same Sheet at the Same Size as a Contribution and Not Being Asked a Second Question About, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Thursday the twenty-eighth day of October, in that loft over a chandler's, in that shed, and at the back of that market.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-fourth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-four days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-six days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a sixteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and four days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-eight days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and six days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-third day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-eighth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-fifth day.**

---

The two marks went on first, at about nine in the morning, in Ivo Skell's own hand, with the day against them and not the Wednesday, and nobody said anything about it in that loft and nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him whether the throat of that stove was any better.

He said that it was not any better and that he had decided that it did not matter, and that is the whole of what he said about it and it is not in either column of that leaf.

The four marks went on at about eleven in the morning from a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay, whose boat opened about nine foot of garboard and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands on the twenty-eighth of September, and who was not charged a penny for that bill and did not offer and was not thanked for saying he would hold that baulk next time and held it on the fourth of October for about an hour and ten minutes and was not thanked for that either, and who said the name of that thing once on the tenth of October past in a room over a fish market and wrote it at the head of a sheet himself and has not said it since.

**Six hundred and forty pence went on that sheet on the said day with his name and the hour against it, being four marks, and that is the third line that has his name on it and it is the first one that is not a line about a boat of about nineteen tons hired from a master out of a coast town four days down, and nobody in that loft asked him whether he was sure and he was asked nothing about it and he wrote it and put the pencil down.**

---

At about two in the afternoon on the said Thursday a man of about twenty-eight who crews a boat of about nine tons said out loud, in that loft, that he was not going again, and he gave a reason, and the reason was in his own mouth and it named his own hand, and it went onto that sheet on the same day at the same size as a contribution and not under it and not beside it in smaller writing.

"I went on the first one and I am not going on the second one, and it is not the money and it is not the water and it is not anybody here. It is this."

He put his right hand flat on the table and opened it and did not close it all the way, and the second and third fingers stayed about a quarter of an inch short of the palm and they have stayed there since the summer.

"I closed it on a ring on the third of August and it has not closed since, and the man at the physic house in Ashtide says he does not know and will not take my money for it, and there is no physic in that market at fourteen pence and there has not been any since the summer. So I will tell you exactly what I am afraid of, and it is a small thing and it is the only thing I have got. It is that I go down at about twenty-two fathoms on the way back and I want a chain off that thing and my hand does not close, and then it is somebody else's hands on me, and I would rather stand in a room and say this out loud than be the man who is pulled up by three people and cannot get hold of anything on the way."

Nobody in that loft asked him a second question about it. Nobody argued with it. Nobody thanked him. The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall, who was not asked and who is not on that sheet and is not a witness to anything, said one thing from the doorway and that was that he was not the first man in that harbor to have a hand that does not do what he tells it, and that she had a rule that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again, and that a reason put on a sheet could not be taken off either, and then she went out and it was the only thing she said all day and she was not thanked for it.

**The refusal stands on the fitting-out sheet at the same size as the six hundred and forty pence and the three hundred and twenty pence, and it is the third refusal on that sheet and it is the first of the three that is not a refusal of the project, and neither of the two people who said no on the thirteenth and on the fourteenth is asked about this one, and this one is a refusal of a second trip by a man who went on the first and it is a reason about a hand and not about what anybody in that loft believes.**

---

At about half past three in the afternoon on the said day, a man of about twenty-nine who had been in that harbor since the Friday before and who has a hearing that is not right on one side asked one question in that loft, and it was a good question and it was asked out loud and it was answered out loud, and the answer was that the reason was not going to be used for anything.

"Has he been told what you are going to do with that."

"He has been told nothing and there is nothing," said the man who keeps the leaf. "It is on that sheet at the same size as the one hundred and sixty pence that Nessa Pike put in on the sixteenth without saying anything about it and without being thanked for it, and nobody is going to take it off it and nobody is going to do anything with it. If I have ever said one word about that hand to any man in a coat I will put it in the second column myself and you can read it there."

"He is right," said Ivo Skell, from the doorway, and that was all he said.

**And nobody asked the man of about twenty-eight one question more about it on any of the three days after it, and he came down to that slip on the Friday at about four in the afternoon and got his day's work and went home, and the fitting-out sheet carries his reason in his own words and carries it once.**

---

**AND AT THE CLOSE OF THAT THURSDAY THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY STANDS AT TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND TEN THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT, AND NOTHING HAS GONE OUT OF IT SINCE THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-SEVENTH, AND IT IS NOT SHORT ON THAT DAY AT ALL AND IT IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR PENCE IN HAND, BEING ONE MARK, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND THAT IS THE ONLY DAY OF THIS RUN ON WHICH THAT SHEET HAS BEEN IN HAND, AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE IN HAND BY THE TIME THAT BOAT IS BACK, BECAUSE THERE IS A LINE STILL TO GO ON AND IT HAS NOT BEEN COSTED YET, AND THE FIGURE IT STANDS SHORT BY ON THE NEXT DAY IS A FIGURE THAT IS TAKEN OFF IT AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND IS A LINE FOR FOUR MEN AND NOT A FIGURE ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED FOR.** A REFUSAL OF A SECOND TRIP, FROM A MAN WHO WENT ON THE FIRST, STANDS ON THAT SHEET AT THE SAME SIZE AS EVERY FIGURE ON IT AND NAMES HIS OWN HAND, AND THE THIRD COLUMN IS STILL EMPTY AND A BOY OF TWENTY-TWO IS NOT ON THE WATER.**

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-eighth day of October at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-fourth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that two marks went onto the fitting-out sheet at about nine in the morning from a pump hand of about forty-four in his own hand with the day against them, being three hundred and twenty pence, and that nobody thanked him for it and nobody asked him whether the throat of that stove was any better and he said that it was not and that he had decided that it did not matter; and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay put in four marks at about eleven in the morning, being six hundred and forty pence, and was asked nothing about it; and that a man of about twenty-eight who crews a boat of about nine tons said out loud at about two in the afternoon that he was not going again, and gave a reason in his own mouth that named his own right hand and that has not closed all the way since the third of August, and that his refusal went onto that sheet at the same size as the figures and not under them, and that nobody asked him a second question about it on any of the three days after it; and that a man of about twenty-nine asked out loud at about half past three in the afternoon whether that man had been told what the reason was going to be used for, and was told that it was not going to be used for anything; and that the sheet that carries the money stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty in and ten thousand three hundred and forty-six out and nothing has gone out of it since the morning of the twenty-seventh, and that it is not short on that day and there are two hundred and ninety-four pence on it, being one mark, eleven shillings and twopence, and that a line is still to go on and it has not been costed yet, and carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the third refusal on that sheet is the first one that is not a refusal of the project is in the yard's own mouth, and is that the first two were said about whether a thing ought to be done and this one is said by a man who has done it once and is not going to do it again, and that those are different questions, and a sheet that carries both without saying which is which is a sheet that will be read by whoever comes to it next as a threat, and there is nobody in that harbor now who could tell them apart again; and that the reason that refusal is at the same size as the figures and is not under them is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a refusal in smaller writing is a refusal somebody decided to make smaller, and that the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall was not asked and said one thing from a doorway about a figure put on a board and is not a witness to anything and is not on that sheet; and that the reason a man of about twenty-nine asked what a reason was going to be used for is his own, and that he asked it out loud in a room because he could not think of a quieter way of asking it, and that the answer was no, and that the answer is the reason that line is on that sheet and is not anywhere else, and that if it is ever used for anything then the person who used it is to put it in this column himself with the hour against it; and that the reason a boat of about nine tons is not on the water on the second trip and the man who crews her is not on that sheet as a figure is his own and was said once and is not to be asked about again; and that the reason the only day of that run on which that sheet had money in hand was not a day on which a single old account was settled is in the yard's own mouth, and is that two hundred and ninety-four pence is a figure for the water and is not a figure for nineteen winters, and that a yard which spends the hand in a bag on an account is a yard with nothing left on that sheet on the Monday.*
