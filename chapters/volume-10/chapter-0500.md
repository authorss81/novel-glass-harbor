# Chapter 500

*Thursday the Second Day of December, a Rota Day, the Cistern at the Back of That Market Charged for the Hundred and Fourth Time at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Fitting-Out Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything and This Movement Taking Nothing In, the Shortfall Standing at Twenty-One Thousand Nine Hundred and Ten Pence, Being One Hundred and Thirty-Six Marks, Twelve Shillings and Sixpence, Said Out Loud in That Yard's Own Mouth and Not Answered by Anybody, a Leaf Read Back at About Ten Past Five in the Evening on the After Deck of a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Lying off the Third Lay, in the Open, in the Ship's Own Left Hand, All Four Columns Including the Empty One, Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading in the Same Words and in the Same Order as Every Other Reading of It and Its Own Derivation Printed on It, Read Twice Because About Four Men of That Party Cannot Read, Standing at Two Hundred and Thirty-Two and One Thousand and Thirty-Seven at That Hour and at Two Hundred and Thirty-Three and One Thousand and Thirty-Nine at the Close of That Day, a Mole Head in That Harbor at About Twenty to Six in the Evening With the Light on It Out and Its Lens Out and About Eleven People Standing at It and One of Them Asking a Question Out Loud Once and Nobody Answering It and Nessa Pike Entering It in That Minute as Asked and the Hour Against It and It Not Being Asked Again, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Thursday the second day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is lying off the third lay.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and fourth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the figure of that hundred was said out loud once on that deck on the eighteenth of November and is not said out loud a second time. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and this movement has taken nothing in on any line and has asked nobody for anything on any day of its ten. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-two thousand five hundred and fifty pence out, being two hundred and three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for two hundred; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty-two days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventy-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninety-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and forty days. The mole-head light in that harbor is out and its lens has been out since the seventh day of May. The north berth is out and the hose is dead until the spring. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and it is still gone on that Thursday, and he has said nothing about it and nobody in that harbor has asked him. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay has a hole in the middle of a Tuesday and has it still, and no figure is set against it anywhere and nobody has asked him about it a second time and nobody has thanked him.**

**And at about ten past five in the evening, on that after deck, in the open, in the ship's own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, the leaf was read back, and it was read twice because about four men of that party cannot read, and both definitions came out of the same mouth in the same words they came out of on the twenty-second day of October past and on the first day of November and on the eleventh of November and on the twenty-second of November, and the reading prints its own arithmetic on its own face so that anybody with a pencil can take it and find that it agrees.**

"There are two hundred and thirty-two entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and thirty-three at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and **fifty have gone in on the fifty days of the fifty-one from Wednesday the thirteenth of October to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them, there being one day in between that is the twelfth day of November and that is a day nobody is in.** A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has one thousand and thirty-seven lines in it at this hour and one thousand and thirty-nine at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and five have gone in on each of those same fifty days since, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the two hundred and fifty that have gone in, **two hundred and forty-five went in on the forty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and two hundred and fifty less two is two hundred and forty-eight, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and forty-eight is one thousand and thirty-seven.** That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and thirty-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. **Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since, and that boy is not on the water and nobody has asked him why."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these fifty days and it has taken no line in the ten days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

**And then the sheet that carries the money was read back after that, in full, with the day against every line, and it carries no count of persons, and the three who said no stand on it at the same size as the figures, and none of the three has been asked a second time in any of these fifty days and nobody in that harbor has been asked for the rest of it.**

"Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Thirty-two thousand five hundred and fifty pence out, being two hundred and three marks, five shillings and tenpence. **Twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against this yard's own book and is nobody's else.** Eight lines went out in these ten days and nothing went in on any of them. Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days, sixteen hundred, the twenty-third. A day apiece in advance to the same four men, six hundred and seventy-two, the twenty-third. Four days of coal for the pump, two thousand three hundred and fifty-two, the twenty-fourth. Five fresh days of air off that same supply boat, nine hundred, the twenty-fourth. A second pair of hands for a day, one hundred and sixty-eight, the twenty-fourth. A case, a lock and a lining of oilcloth, three hundred and twenty, the twenty-seventh. A boat of about nineteen tons, five days under way, one thousand two hundred and fifty, the twenty-ninth. A day apiece to the same four men, six hundred and seventy-two, the thirtieth. That is seven thousand nine hundred and thirty-four pence, being forty-nine marks, seven shillings and tenpence, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree."

"And on every one of those ten days one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and the whole of the ten days is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence out, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence. **Not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**"

Nobody argued with him. Nobody was asked for the rest of it.

**And at about twenty to six in the evening, when it had gone dark enough for the mole-head light to be missed and it is out anyway and has been since the seventh of May, about eleven people of that harbor were standing at the head of that mole, and the wind was off the land and cold, and a woman of about fifty-eight with an end stall and a slate put her slate against the parapet and asked a thing out loud, once, and nobody answered it.**

"I have got one question and I have had it since Wednesday and I am going to ask it now and I am not going to ask it again."

Somebody at the back said ask it.

"**If they are putting that old thing back together on that floor down there, how many harbors have to be joined up to it before a man can make it work?**"

There was a wind off the land and about eleven people standing at that mole head and the light on it is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May, and nobody answered it.

The holder of that writ was about nine feet off her and he did not answer it. The woman of thirty of no office did not answer it. A man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons did not answer it. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay, who has a hole in the middle of a Tuesday, did not answer it and was not asked it. A man of about twenty-nine, who cannot hear out of the left side of his head and will not until the fourth of December, stood at the front and did not hear it asked and nobody told him he had.

**And Nessa Pike entered it in that minute as asked, with the hour of twenty to six in the evening of the second day of December against it, and read it back to the woman who asked it, and the woman who asked it did not ask to have it read back and did not ask for a word of it to be changed.**

**And it has not been asked a second time in that harbor and it is not going to be asked a second time, and there is no answer to it anywhere on this coast tonight, and the person who asked it did not ask it to be answered tonight.**

**A CASE STANDS ON A TABLE IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET WITH ITS LOCK SHUT AND ITS KEY IN THE KEEPING OF THE WOMAN WHO WROTE THE MINUTE, AND THE CRATE THAT WENT DOWN OFF THAT BOAT ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH WITH THREE BASKETS IN IT CAME UP OUT OF THAT WATER EMPTY WITH A BATTEN BACK ACROSS ITS LID, AND THERE IS NO WORD ON ANY PAGE OF THAT HARBOR FOR WHAT WAS GOING TO BE IN IT. A SLATE LEANS ON THE BENCH STONE IN A SHED AT THE HEAD OF THAT THIRD LAY WITH A FIGURE OF TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE UNDER A FIGURE OF SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY. THE CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND IT IS WALKED ON AT EVERY CHARGE. THERE IS A MOLE HEAD IN THAT HARBOR AND THE LIGHT ON IT IS OUT AND ITS LENS HAS BEEN OUT SINCE THE SEVENTH DAY OF MAY, AND THERE ARE ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE STANDING AT IT IN THE DARK. A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE IS STANDING AT IT AND CANNOT HEAR OUT OF THE LEFT SIDE OF HIS HEAD AND WILL NOT BE ABLE TO UNTIL THE FOURTH DAY OF DECEMBER. A BOAT OF ABOUT NINETEEN TONS IS LYING OFF THAT THIRD LAY WITH HER MASTER IN HER AND HIS BOY IN THE FOREPEAK AND THAT CRATE PUT DOWN BESIDE HIM. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR HAS A HOLE IN THE MIDDLE OF A TUESDAY THAT A WEEK AGO HE COULD PUT A HAND ON EITHER END OF AND NOT ON THE MIDDLE OF, AND A QUESTION HE ASKED AT THE HEAD OF THAT SLIP IN OCTOBER THAT NOBODY HAS ANSWERED AND THAT HE HAS NOT ASKED AGAIN. AND ON THE BOTTOM OF A TRENCH IN ABOUT TWENTY-FATHOMS THERE IS A MACHINE SIX CENTURIES OLD WITH A CROWN MAN'S MARKS SET AROUND IT AND A CHAMBER WITH A STOVE IN IT STANDING OVER THE TOP OF IT WITH ITS STOVE DRAWN OUT AND ITS HATCH SHUT, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS BEEN GIVEN THAT MACHINE AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS BEEN STOPPED FROM KNOWING ABOUT IT, AND THE WORD THAT WOULD NAME THE SEAL ON A WARRANT IS IN NO MOUTH IN THAT BOAT AND IN NO MOUTH IN THAT HARBOR.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening on that after deck, and no name is against it: that the reason a question asked once at a mole head in the dark is entered in that minute as asked and not as answered is the woman of about fifty-eight's own, and is that a question that goes into a book without an answer against it is the only kind of question a harbor can keep, because an answer written under it would be the answer of whoever wrote the book and not the answer of the thing asked about, and that a harbor with nine weeks of unanswered questions on a wall already knows what an unanswered question looks like and has never once written one down;
