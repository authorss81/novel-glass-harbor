# Chapter 468

*Saturday the Thirtieth Day of October, the Second Day of the Run Down, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Who Makes His Own Bed and a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Who Does Not and Who Says One Thing Out Loud About a Silt on a Shelf Floor and Does Not Show the Book He Keeps It In, a Pump Hand of About Forty-Four Putting a Figure in His Own Book Against a Thing Nobody Has Asked Him For and Nobody Opening a Line for It, Half of What Goes Into That Stove Going Out Into the Water in About an Hour, a Sheet That Carries the Money Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight Pence Short With a Line Still to Go On and Not Costed Yet, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Saturday the thirtieth day of October, on the deck of a boat of about nineteen tons, the second day of that run down, and in that harbor the same day behind them.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the whole of the ten days of that first run down the coast was one thousand six hundred and eighty pence in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence out, and the whole of the ten days of this one is another one thousand six hundred and eighty pence each way, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence, and neither of those two sums is the sum of the trip and neither of them was ever meant to be and no figure of the trip is on any page of this harbor.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on the deck of that boat and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-six days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-eight days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a sixteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and six days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventy-second night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in ninety days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eight days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-fifth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the sixtieth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-seventh day.**

---

Teodor Vask made his bed at about half past five in the morning on that Saturday, which he had done on every night of the first run as well, and Ollum Trevask did not make his, which he had not done on any night of the first run either, and the whole of the difference between those two men on that deck was a blanket and a length of line and about four minutes.

"Some men stow a bed," Trevask said, at about half past five, not unkindly, and he was folding a length of three-inch line into a coil about the size of a hat and putting it under the chart he has not got, because he has never had a chart and has never been asked for one and has not said out loud on any of these ten days that he keeps a book. "Some men stow a bed and some men stow a book and a man who stows a bed has to get up in the dark and a man who stowes a book does not. I have never been the first one. It is not a virtue and I would not want anybody to write it down as one."

Nobody wrote it down.

---

He said his one thing at about eleven in the morning on the said day, standing at the rail on the port side with the wind coming over the top of a swell that had nothing wrong with it, and he said it to the man who keeps the leaf and not to the boat.

"There is silt on that floor. Not on the shelf and not in the mouth. On the floor, inside, below the step, banked up on the left and not on the right." He held his left hand out flat and then turned it over. "Banked silt does not tell you which way the water goes through a place. It tells you which way it has been going round, and round is not the same as through, and a man who has been in holds for nineteen years knows the difference and a man who has been in books for nineteen years does not. That is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to say another word about it on the water."

"And you keep the fouling ladder in a book and you have never shown it to anybody."

"I keep the fouling ladder in my own book in my own house and I have never shown it to anybody and nobody in this harbor has asked me for it, and if somebody did I would think about it for a while, and there is no hurry, and there is not going to be a worse time for me to think about it than there is now." He put the coil down. "That is not a refusal of a book. It is a man of about thirty-four who has not been asked."

---

A pump hand of about forty-four put a figure in his own book on the said Saturday at about half past two in the afternoon, and it was against a thing nobody had asked him for, and it was in his own book and not on the fitting-out sheet, and nobody in that party opened a line for it and the man who keeps the leaf read it and read it back and then said no.

"What is that."

"That is a day of a compressor's hours holding a bell full of air at the bottom of that trench, twice, because the water comes up about two feet in the twenty minutes after the flood and it does not go down again inside the working day, and the stove is in there with a gauge on it and the gauge is not a depth reading." He turned the book round. "It is a figure and it is mine and I worked it out of nineteen years and it is a day of somebody's hands, and I have written it down because a thing nobody has asked me for is a thing I am going to be asked for eventually and I would rather have written the figure than have somebody guess it."

"You have not been asked for it."

"No. And you are not going to ask me for it, and if you do I will give you the figure and it will not be this one, because this one is what it costs me and what it costs the man who is not me is a different sum and I have not done that sum and I am not going to pretend I have."

**And no line was opened on that sheet for it, and the figure is in that man's own book and is in neither column of that leaf, and the reason is in the second column and is that this yard does not put a figure on a page for a thing nobody has asked for, and a figure on a page is a promise that somebody is going to be asked.**

---

**ABOUT HALF OF WHAT WENT INTO THAT STOVE ON THAT SATURDAY WENT OUT INTO THE WATER IN ABOUT AN HOUR, AS IT HAD DONE ON EVERY DAY SINCE THE EIGHTEENTH OF OCTOBER, AND THE COST OF THAT IS IN THE COAL AND THE COAL WAS BOUGHT ONCE, ON THE TWENTY-THIRD, FOR THREE DAYS, OFF A SUPPLY BOAT, AT ONE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR PENCE, AND NOBODY BOUGHT A SECOND LOAD AND NOBODY PUT A SECOND LOAD ON THAT SHEET, AND THE REASON IS IN THE YARD'S OWN MOUTH AND IS THAT A MAN WHO BUYS COAL ONCE DOES NOT BUY IT TWICE TO BE ABLE TO SAY SO.**

---

**NO REVELATION CAME UP ON THE SECOND DAY OF THAT RUN. THE COST OF THE SECOND TRIP IS THE SAME COST AS THE FIRST AND IT IS NOT HALVED BY BEING THE SECOND, AND A SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT WITH A LINE STILL TO GO ON THAT HAS NOT BEEN COSTED YET. A BOOK WAS NOT SHOWN. A FIGURE WAS NOT PUT ON A PAGE. AND THE HARBOR IS POORER ON THE THIRTIETH DAY OF OCTOBER THAN IT WAS ON THE THIRTEENTH OF IT.**

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirtieth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, with that harbor the same day behind them: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was not charged on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a boat of about nineteen tons was on the second day of a run down that coast with the wind in the west; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay said one thing out loud at about eleven in the morning about silt on the floor below the step of that trench, banked on the left and not on the right, and said that banked silt tells a man which way the water has been going round and not which way it goes through, and did not show the book he keeps the fouling ladder in and was not asked for it; and that a pump hand of about forty-four wrote a figure in his own book at about half past two in the afternoon against a day of compressor's hours that nobody had asked him for, and that no line was opened on the fitting-out sheet for it and it is in neither column of that leaf; and that about half of what went into that stove went out into the water in about an hour, as it had done every day since the eighteenth, and that the coal for that was bought once on the twenty-third for three days at one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence and no second load was bought and no second line went on that sheet; and that the sheet that carries the money stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty in and eleven thousand and eighteen out and is short by three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being two marks, four shillings and tenpence, and there is a line still to go on and it has not been costed yet.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the whole of the difference between two men of about thirty-four on that deck was a blanket and a length of line and about four minutes is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who stows a bed has to get up in the dark and a man who stows a book does not, and that the second of those is not a virtue and was not written down by anybody; and that the reason a man of about thirty-four said one thing out loud about silt and then stopped is his own, and is that banked silt tells a man which way the water has been going round and not which way it goes through, and that a man who has been in holds for nineteen years knows the difference and a man who has been in books does not, and that he said it once on the water and is not going to say another word about it; and that the reason a book has not been shown is in that man's own mouth, and is that nobody in that harbor has asked him for it, and that a book nobody has been asked for does not have to be answered for; and that the reason a pump hand of about forty-four put a figure in his own book and did not put it on that sheet is in the yard's own mouth, and is that this yard does not put a figure on a page for a thing nobody has asked for, and that a figure on a page is a promise that somebody is going to be asked, and that a figure in a man's own book is his own business until somebody asks him; and that the reason coal bought once on the twenty-third for three days was not bought again is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a man who buys coal once does not buy it twice to be able to say so; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
