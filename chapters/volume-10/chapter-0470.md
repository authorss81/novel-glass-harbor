# Chapter 470

*Monday the First Day of November, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, a Man of About Twenty-Nine Going Down on the Ebb into a Yard of Racks in About Twenty-Two Fathoms at About Ten Past Ten in the Morning Under a Named Tender and a Written Reason and a Figure of Air and Asking at About Half Past Ten in the Middle of the Morning to Come Up and Not Being Argued With and Coming Up at About a Quarter to Eleven, an Hour Against It, a Woman of About Thirty-One Going Down After Him and Not Going Down Twice in a Day and Nobody Going Down Twice in a Day, Two Accounts of the Same Thirty Seconds of That Floor That Do Not Agree With Each Other and Both Entered in the Order They Were Said and Neither Settled, a Crate With a Blank in It on That Deck and Nobody on the Face of That Harbor Able to Read It, a Leaf Read Back at About Ten Past Five in the Evening With Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading and the Fourth Column Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Five Where It Stood and Taking No Line, the Sheet That Carries the Money Six Hundred and Eighty-Six Pence Short, and the Whole of That Harbor's Standing Condition Once and Nowhere Else, and the Thing on That Trench Floor Still Not Identified and Not Named*

Monday the first day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Monday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip has come out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown, and none of it was ever going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in that harbor and was not approached by anybody and a shoe that is not his is on the floor beside it. Mara Venn was on that deck and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf was under her own coat. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and got up at four and went home, which he has done seven times in ninety-seven days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-eight days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and seventy days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a seventeenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor and he is not on the water. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and eight days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventy-fourth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in ninety-two days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and ten days and has been given nothing by anybody.**

---

The slates were written before anybody went down and were read out on that deck in front of every man in that boat, and the tender on the surface was named and the tender was on the deck and not in the bell, and the air was on an independent line and the limit was a figure and the reason was on a slate with an hour against it and the tender's hand was on the chain weight and the tender could pull without asking.

Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, went first, at about ten past ten in the morning, and his reason was written in his own words and it was that he had been on that floor on the Thursday and the Saturday of the first week in nine fathoms and had not been on this floor at all, and that a thing a man has heard once in one place is not a thing he has heard twice, and that the chamber was here now and it was not here then.

He was in the water about thirty-five minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them, and at about half past ten, in the middle of that, he used the line.

Three pulls, which is the signal, and the tender had it in his hand and pulled, and nobody on that boat said a word to him about it, and nobody went down after him to hurry him, and the man who keeps the leaf did not say that there was time.

**His air limit was a figure of forty minutes and he was at thirty-one, and the tender would have pulled at about ten to eleven whatever the diver had wanted, and he came up at about a quarter to eleven and came up because he asked, and there is a line on the fitting-out sheet with the hour against it, and the hour is half past ten in the middle of the morning, and it is the only line on that sheet in this movement that anybody in that boat or in that harbor is glad about.**

He came up with a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron like a nail and a bad night in a place where there was nothing to be frightened of, and he said three things on the deck in that order, and the second of them is the one that went into the second column of that leaf that evening.

"I asked to come up because I did not like it and I am not going to dress that up as a symptom. It is a bell on a floor with a stove in it and a gauge on the side of it and forty racks in rows, and I was thirty-one minutes on the bottom of it, and at about half past ten I did not want to be in there any more, and I have never in my life pretended otherwise to anybody and I am not starting on a day like that. Write that down as the whole of the reason."

**And the man of about thirty-one who crews nothing and sells nothing said one thing about that before anybody said anything else, and it was said out loud on that deck, and it is the reason the two of them were asked separately and it is the reason the accounts below do not agree with each other.**

"He cannot hear out of the left side of his head," she said, "and he has had that since the twentieth and everybody on this boat knows it and nobody has said one word about it to him, and I am not going to be the one who says it, and I am also not going to pretend that a man who cannot hear out of one side of his head has heard the same thirty seconds that I have."

Nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her and nobody told Amos Trewin that a woman of about thirty-one had said it, and he said later, on the same deck, that it was the correct thing to say and that he would have said it about himself.

---

The woman of about thirty-one went down at about ten past one in the afternoon and was in the water about forty minutes and on the bottom about twenty-two of them, and nobody went down twice that day, and the second reason was written down with its hour and the second air limit was the same figure of forty minutes, and the same tender's hand was on the same chain weight.

She came up at about ten to two and came up on the air and not on anything else.

---

**And then, at about half past three in the afternoon on the said Monday, on that after deck, in the open, in front of the whole of that party, the two of them gave their accounts of the same thirty seconds of that floor, and they do not agree, and both are entered in the order they were said and neither of them is settled, and there are now ten accounts in that leaf that do not agree with each other, six of them from a book two days' sail from here and four of them from a boat of about nineteen tons out of this harbor, and not one of the ten has been settled and nobody in that boat was asked to agree with anybody.**

Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, said it first, and his reason was in the yard's own mouth and it is not a finding.

"There was a noise. It came from below and behind the far row of racks and it went on for about thirty seconds and then it stopped, and it stopped all at once, the way a thing stops when it is finished and not the way a thing stops when somebody lets go of it. It was not a bang. It was a bar under load, and it was not one noise, it was a noise that had a thing in it about every four seconds, and I count six of them and I would be willing to be asked how I counted. I could not tell you what it was and I am not going to tell you what I think it was, and if I am asked to guess I will say so and I will say it was a guess."

Sabra Loach, of about thirty-one, said it second, and nobody asked her to say it again, and she was not asked to soften it, and the two of them were not asked to talk it out with each other.

"there is no noise. That is what I am putting in and it stands next to his and it is not going to be moved along. I was on the bottom about twenty-two minutes in the same rows in the same place and the thirty seconds I am talking about are the thirty seconds about eleven minutes after I got there. My left ear was shut. Not deaf, shut, the way it goes when you have been in a bell and the pressure comes up on the way down, and it shut and stayed shut for about thirty seconds and I could not have told you at the time that it had. It cleared while I was holding a rack. And when it cleared there was a noise already going and it was ending. I do not know whether I missed the beginning of it or whether there was never a beginning. I have been in water since I was nineteen and I know what the start of a bar under load sounds like, because I have heard it start, and what I heard was the middle of one and the end of it. I am not saying he is wrong. I am saying I have got thirty seconds and he has got thirty seconds and they are not the same thirty seconds, and neither of us can improve the other's."

Nobody in that party settled it, and nobody in that party asked either of them to be asked again, and both accounts went into the second column of that leaf that evening in the order they were said, with the hour against each, and the column that is not checkable from a mole is the only place in this series where a disagreement like that can sit without either of them being made to lose.

**AND AT ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE IN THE EVENING ON THE SAID MONDAY THE LEAF WAS READ BACK ON THAT DECK, IN THE OPEN, IN THE SHIP'S OWN LEFT HAND, ALL FOUR COLUMNS INCLUDING THE EMPTY ONE, AND IT WAS READ TWICE BECAUSE ABOUT FOUR MEN OF THAT PARTY CANNOT READ, AND BOTH DEFINITIONS CAME OUT OF THE SAME MOUTH IN THE SAME WORDS THEY CAME OUT OF ON THE TWENTY-SECOND DAY OF OCTOBER.**

"There are two hundred and two entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and three at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and twenty have gone in on the twenty days from Wednesday the thirteenth of October to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has eight hundred and eighty-seven lines in it at this hour and eight hundred and eighty-nine at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and five have gone in on each of the twenty days since, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the hundred that have gone in, ninety-five went in on the nineteen days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and a hundred less two is ninety-eight, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and ninety-eight is eight hundred and eighty-seven. That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments."

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these twenty days and it has taken no line in the ten days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

**And the sheet that carries the money was read back after that, in full, with the day against every line, and it stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and eleven thousand three hundred and twenty-six pence out, being seventy marks, ten shillings and sixpence, and it is six hundred and eighty-six pence short, being four marks, three shillings and tenpence, and it carries no count of persons and has no figure on it for how many people are on it, and the two refusals of October stand on it at the same size as the figures and the third refusal of the twenty-eighth stands there too, and the line that says the two people who said no were not asked a second time is on it and is a line and not a figure and is not a name, and no figure has been asked of anybody in that harbor to make that shortfall be otherwise.**

---

Here is the whole of what that harbor was carrying on the first day of November, and it is here once and nowhere else.

There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month and there has been no barley in that market since the middle of September. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August, and a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down and cannot be taken off. Physic is fourteen pence, being one shilling and twopence, and there is none of it. The mole-head light is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short and two men of it went out on the twenty-ninth day of October and come back on the Thursday, and the man who counts that gang said out loud that he could not count them for anything until then and was not going to pretend otherwise. The hard at Ashtide has been unanswered since the ninth of November past. The third lay fills on the first of April and one berth is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and fifty-three weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and a wage Tuesday came and went on the twenty-sixth and the column did not move. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and was charged on the twenty-fifth and the twenty-eighth days of October and walked on by none of it on either of them, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the other six days of that fortnight put no charge on it. The sheet of six printed lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-seventh day, on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence that was not taken down first, and is answered by nobody. The paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the sixty-second day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-ninth day and has been answered by nobody. A claim entered in a book at a counter off the west basin against a made thing on the seventh of October is not granted, not refused and not withdrawn. A ropemaker of about sixty-one is not in her room and does not get that room back. A man of about thirty-one with a chart in a bag said on the thirteenth of October that he had one and did not produce it and was not asked for it. A man of about fifty-one with a page of six names and a stone in his own pocket has said nothing to anybody for a hundred and ten days and has been given nothing by anybody, and a shoe that is not his is on the floor beside a bench that is occupied from ten until four and is not approached. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book has not been asked for it and has not shown it to anybody. A man of about twenty-nine could not hear out of the left side of his head on the twentieth, the twenty-first, the twenty-second, the twenty-third, the twenty-fourth, the twenty-fifth, the twenty-sixth, the twenty-seventh, the twenty-eighth, the twenty-ninth, the thirtieth or the thirty-first days of October, or on this day, and has not been asked about it, has not said anything about it, did not ask, and no figure is set against it in that yard or in that harbor or on any sheet in it. **And money went into a sheet in that harbor on three days between the fourteenth and the sixteenth days of October, and one woman who put a figure against her own name said out loud that the money was for flour, and there is no flour in that market, and that money went down a coast road and came back up it in a crate and it did not buy flour and it is not going to be given back, and nobody in that harbor has been told that it was worth it and nobody is going to be.**

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE ASKED TO COME UP AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MIDDLE OF THE MORNING AND WAS NOT ARGUED WITH, AND CAME UP AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN, AND THE HOUR IS ENTERED, AND IT IS THE ONLY LINE ON THAT SHEET IN THIS MOVEMENT THAT ANYBODY IS GLAD ABOUT. TWO ACCOUNTS OF THE SAME THIRTY SECONDS OF THAT FLOOR DO NOT AGREE WITH EACH OTHER AND BOTH ARE ENTERED IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SAID AND NEITHER IS SETTLED, AND THERE ARE NOW TEN ACCOUNTS IN THAT LEAF THAT DO NOT AGREE WITH EACH OTHER AND NOT ONE OF THE TEN HAS BEEN SETTLED. THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SIX HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING FOUR MARKS, THREE SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE. A CRATE WITH A BLANK IN IT IS ON THAT DECK AND NOBODY ON THE FACE OF THAT HARBOR CAN READ IT, AND NOBODY HAS GIVEN IT A NAME. THE THING ON THAT TRENCH FLOOR IS STILL NOT IDENTIFIED AND IS NOT NAMED, AND THE WORD THAT WOULD NAME IT IS IN NO MOUTH IN THAT HARBOR AND IN NO MOUTH ON THAT BOAT. TWENTY DAYS OF THOSE FIFTY HAVE NOW BEEN PAID AND THE MIDPOINT IS NOT ONE OF THEM.**

*First column, entered on Monday the first day of November next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a man of about twenty-nine went down into a yard of forty racks in rows at about ten past ten in the morning on the ebb, in about twenty-two fathoms, under a named tender on the surface and not in the bell, on an independent line, with an air limit of a figure of forty minutes written on a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it and a cutoff the tender could pull without asking; and that he was in the water about thirty-five minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them, and that at about half past ten in the middle of that he used the line, and that the tender pulled, and that nobody said a word to him about it and nobody went down after him to hurry him; and that the tender would have pulled at about ten to eleven whatever the diver had wanted, and that he came up at about a quarter to eleven because he asked, and that the hour is on the fitting-out sheet against a line and is the only line on that sheet in this movement that anybody in that boat or in that harbor is glad about; and that a woman of about thirty-one who crews nothing and sells nothing said out loud on that deck, before anybody said anything else, that a man who cannot hear out of one side of his head has not heard the same thirty seconds that she has, and that nobody argued with her and nobody told him she had said it; and that the same woman went down at about ten past one in the afternoon and was in the water about forty minutes and on the bottom about twenty-two of them, and that nobody went down twice that day; and that the two of them gave their accounts of the same thirty seconds of that floor at about half past three in the afternoon, in the open, in front of the whole of that party, and that the two accounts do not agree with each other, and that both were entered in the order they were said and neither was settled, and that there are now ten accounts in that leaf that do not agree with each other and not one of the ten has been settled; and that the leaf was read back at about ten past five in the evening in the ship's own left hand, in the open, all four columns including the empty one, and was read twice, and that both definitions came out of the same mouth in the same words they came out of on the twenty-second day of October; and that the sheet that carries the money stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty in and eleven thousand three hundred and twenty-six out and is short by six hundred and eighty-six pence, being four marks, three shillings and tenpence, and that it carries no count of persons and has no figure for how many people are on it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the two accounts of the same thirty seconds of that floor are both in this column and neither was put in the first is that a man on a mole cannot check what a man heard at the bottom of a trench, and that a column a stranger with a pencil can check is for things a stranger with a pencil can check; and that the reason the first of those two accounts was said first is that the man who was in the water first was in the water first, and that the order they were said in is the order they were got in, and that nobody in that boat asked either of them to be asked again; and that the reason a woman of about thirty-one said out loud, before anybody had said anything, that a man who cannot hear out of one side of his head has not heard the same thirty seconds that she has is her own, and is that she is not going to be the one who says it to him and is also not going to pretend it is not so; and that the reason nobody in that party settled the disagreement is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a man on a mole cannot check it and that a man on a mole is not going to be asked to, and that the six accounts in a book two days' sail from that harbor are not settled either and have not been for a long time, and that a yard that settles disagreements about what a man heard is a yard that will be settling them about what a man said next; and that the reason nobody in that boat wrote down what those racks are for is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a record of what a floor looks like is checkable by a man on a mole and a record of what it was for is not, and that the thing on that floor is not identified and is not named and the word that would name it is in no mouth; and that the reason a crate is on that deck with a blanket and a batten across it is that a worked blank of glass is not a cup and does not want a cup, and that it is going into that harbor in that crate whether anybody can read it or not, and that nobody in that harbor has been asked to have an opinion about it before it gets there; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
