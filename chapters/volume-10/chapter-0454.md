# Chapter 454

*Saturday the Sixteenth Day of October, Two Days' Money Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and Two Days' Money Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon Because a Berth Fund Pays a Saturday, a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Hired for Six Days at Two Hundred and Fifty Pence a Day and Five Days of It Paid on the Said Day and the Sixth Day of It to Be Paid on the Twenty-Second and a Master of About Fifty-One Named Simeon Roke Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Who Asked Two Questions and Was Given Two Answers and One of Them Was That Nobody Knew, Bread and Salt Beef for Nine Men for Four Days at Six Hundred and Forty Pence Because There Is No Flour in That Market, Four Men Paid a Day Apiece in Advance at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence, a Mark Put In by One of That Yard, a Mark and a Half Put In by a Woman of About Forty-Three Who Keeps a Store on the Second Lay, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Saturday the sixteenth day of October, at a table in a room over a fish market, and on that quay.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. Three hundred and thirty-six pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being two days at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and three hundred and thirty-six pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being two days asked for by name, being a caulker of about twenty-three and a ship's joiner of about thirty-four, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of what was agreed on that quay on the said day comes out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four on the fourteenth day of a fortnight since the third day of October past and was not approached. Mara Venn was in that room and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said Saturday he stopped the hook in a crack in the ninth piece and looked at it for about two minutes and did not say anything about it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-two days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-four days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fourteenth week. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-two days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-eighth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-six days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-four days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-first day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-sixth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-third day.**

---

**The boat of about nineteen tons was hired at a table in a room over a fish market on the said Saturday at about eleven in the morning, at two hundred and fifty pence a day for six days, and the five days of it that fall before the party sails went onto the fitting-out sheet on the said day at one thousand two hundred and fifty pence with the hour against it, and the sixth day, being two hundred and fifty pence, was to be paid on the twenty-second, which is the day the lease ends, and the man who took the hire money did not count it twice and did not say so out loud and put it in a bag of his own on the table.**

He was called Simeon Roke and he was about fifty-one and he was out of a coast town four days down that road, and he had two men with him and a third who was his boy, and he was not of that harbor and nobody in that harbor had seen him before the said Saturday.

He asked two questions and he asked them before he took the money, which is the thing about him that Nessa Pike said afterwards was the reason the hire went through at that figure.

"Who is paying you."

"Nobody," said the holder of that writ. "Eleven people wrote figures on a sheet in a room upstairs and the sheet has no head on it and binds nobody and the fourth rule on the other sheet in this harbor says a person who has not asked to be represented is not represented, and one of these eleven has not been asked anything and has written a figure against her own name and I would not have got this boat without her."

"And when you get to that shelf," said Simeon Roke, "and there is somebody on it."

"Then you will be a boat hired for six days at two hundred and fifty pence a day with two men and a boy, on a coast you do not know, off a lease that ends on the twenty-second, and I do not know what happens then and neither do you, and that is the honest answer and I would rather have it now than on the Wednesday."

Roke looked at him for a while and then said the second thing, which was that he had wanted to be asked that question out loud rather than have it come out of a hold later, and that he had been hired by a man in a coat in the summer who had not asked him anything at all and had not paid him either, and that this was not that, and that he would take the hire money and go home on the twenty-second and be paid the rest of it or he would not be paid the rest of it and would say so where they could hear it.

"He is not a man in a coat," Nessa Pike said. "He is a man with a sheet with no head on it and a bag with twenty-eight pence in it. If you want a coat to be disappointed in, there is one on that wall in an office off this basin and it is not going to the shelf."

**Provisions went on that sheet at about half past eleven in the morning, being bread and salt beef for nine men for four days at six hundred and forty pence, and there is no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and nobody bought flour, and it was said out loud once at that table by the man who keeps the leaf and was not said again in that harbor.** There is a line on the fitting-out sheet that says the flour was not bought and why, in the yard's own mouth, with the hour against it, and there is no figure against flour anywhere in this volume.

**Four men were paid a day apiece in advance on the said Saturday at about two in the afternoon, six hundred and seventy-two pence, and each of the four wrote his own name against his own line, and one of the four asked to be paid on the second day and not on the sixth and got it, and one of the four asked what happens if a man's air goes and the answer was given by the pump hand of about forty-four and it was that the tender pulls and the man comes up and the day is over and the man is paid the day.**

Nessa Pike put a mark of her own into that sheet at about twenty to three in the afternoon, being one mark, and did not say anything about it and was not thanked.

**And a woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay put in one mark and a half, being two hundred and forty pence, at about half past three, and said out loud, to the room and not to a person, that the money was for flour, and there is no flour in that market, and nobody said a word to her about it and nobody thanked her and she did not ask, and the figure went on the sheet with a name and an hour against it and the sheet carries no count of persons and has no figure on it for how many people are on it.**

---

**And the owner of a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay, whose boat opened about nine foot of garboard and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands on the twenty-eighth day of September past by striking a baulk of oak that this yard's own hands had laid down with nothing on it, and who was not charged a penny for that bill and did not offer and was not thanked, came into that room at about four o'clock on the said Saturday and put eight marks against a line that said *a boat of about nineteen tons*, being one thousand two hundred and eighty pence, and gave no reason for his figure being larger than anybody else's, and went out again.**

He is the man who said the name of that thing once, at about ten past eleven in the morning on the tenth day of October past, and wrote it at the head of that sheet himself in a hand that is not a clerk's hand. Nobody in that room said the name after him then and nobody said it on the said Saturday either.

---

**A BOAT OF ABOUT NINETEEN TONS WENT OUT OF THAT HARBOR ON A LEASE OF SIX DAYS AT TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE A DAY, FIVE DAYS OF IT PAID ON THE SAID DAY AT ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE AND THE SIXTH DAY OF IT TO BE PAID ON THE TWENTY-SECOND, AND THE MASTER OF IT ASKED WHO WAS PAYING BEFORE HE TOOK THE MONEY AND WAS TOLD THAT NOBODY WAS AND WAS TOLD WHY, IN THE WORDS OF THE FOURTH STANDING LINE ON ANOTHER SHEET IN ANOTHER ROOM, AND WAS TOLD A SECOND THING THAT WAS THE TRUTH: THAT NOBODY KNEW WHAT WOULD HAPPEN IF THERE WAS ALREADY SOMEBODY ON THAT SHELF. FOUR MEN WERE PAID A DAY APIECE IN ADVANCE AND ONE OF THEM ASKED FOR THE SECOND DAY AND NOT THE SIXTH AND GOT IT, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-THREE PUT IN A MARK AND A HALF AND SAID IT WAS FOR FLOUR AND THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET. THERE IS NO LINE AGAINST FLOUR ANYWHERE ON THAT SHEET, ONLY A LINE SAYING WHY THERE IS NOT ONE. THE BAG STANDS AT TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT WENT OUT OF THAT HARBOR IN THE MONEY ON THE SAID DAY IS THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING TWO DAYS AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK, AND THAT IS A BERTH FUND AND NOT THIS.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the sixteenth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe; and that three hundred and thirty-six pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being two days at the figure in chalk, and three hundred and thirty-six pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being two days asked for by name, and that twenty-eight plus three hundred and thirty-six less three hundred and thirty-six is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a boat of about nineteen tons was hired at a table in a room over a fish market at about eleven in the morning on the said day for six days at two hundred and fifty pence a day, of which five days went onto that sheet on the said day at one thousand two hundred and fifty pence and the sixth day, being two hundred and fifty pence, is to be paid on the twenty-second, with a master of about fifty-one who is out of a coast town four days down that road; and that he asked two questions before he took the hire money, and that the answers were that nobody was paying and that nobody knew what would happen if there was somebody already on that shelf; and that provisions went on that sheet at about half past eleven, being bread and salt beef for nine men for four days at six hundred and forty pence, and that there is no flour in that market and no flour was bought and the reason is on that sheet with the hour against it; and that four men were paid a day apiece in advance at about two in the afternoon, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, each of them in his own hand; and that one of that yard put in one mark and a woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay put in one mark and a half at about half past three in the afternoon and said the money was for flour; and that the owner of a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay put in eight marks at about four o'clock in the afternoon and gave no reason for his figure being larger than anybody else's.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a berth fund pays a Saturday is in the yard's own mouth and is that a day is a day and the fund pays a day for a day and does not care which day of the week it is, and that two days' money going in and two days' money going out on one day is a figure and not an event; and that the reason a master of about fifty-one asked who was paying before he took the money is in his own mouth, and that the second reason is in his own mouth too, being that he had been hired by a man in a coat in the summer who had not asked him anything and had not paid him; and that the reason the answer to the first question was given as a standing line off another sheet rather than as a name is that a person who has not asked to be represented is not represented, and that one of the eleven wrote a figure against her own name and had not been asked anything, and that this yard is not going to be a man in a coat with better manners; and that the reason no flour is against that sheet is in the yard's own mouth and is that a figure on a sheet is a promise to buy something, and that there is no flour in that market to buy, and that a line saying why there is no figure is a thing a stranger with a pencil can check; and that the reason four men were paid a day apiece in advance and not on the sixth is in the pump hand's own mouth and is that the tender pulls and the man comes up and the day is over and the man is paid the day, and that one of the four asked for the second day and not the sixth and got it, and that a man who asks when he is to be paid is not a man who is going to steal a boat; and that the reason a woman of about forty-three said on two of the days in that week that her money was for flour, and said it on each of the two days on which she wrote a figure against her own name, and both times to a room and not to a person, is that a thing said out loud on the day a person puts a figure down is a thing a man can find out about them afterwards, and that is why it is entered once for each of the two days and is not entered a third time; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
