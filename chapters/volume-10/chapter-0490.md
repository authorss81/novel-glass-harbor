# Chapter 490

*Monday the Twenty-Second Day of November, a Rota Day, the Hundred and First Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Figure of That Hundred Said Out Loud on That Deck Once on the Eighteenth and Not Said Again, the Master of a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Still on That Deck With His Declaration of the Eleventh Standing Where He Put It and No Date Asked For and No Date Given and That Boat Not Put to Sea, a Man of About Thirty-Six Named Corwin Tace Not on That Floor on That Monday and His Tool Roll Not on That Deck at the Close of It and Nobody Asked Him Why and Nobody Thanked Him, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand, a Woman of About Thirty-One Who Crews Nothing and Sells Nothing Saying Out Loud What Is in That Crate and What Is Not in It and That What Is in It Is the Best Evidence This Party Has Ever Had in Its Hands and Is Going into a Crate and Not into Anybody's Hand, and a Leaf Read Back at About Ten Past Five in the Evening in the Open in the Ship's Own Left Hand With Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading and Its Own Derivation Printed on It and All Four Columns Including the Empty One and Read Twice Because About Four Men of That Party Cannot Read, the Shortfall Stated at Thirteen Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Six Pence, Being Eighty-Seven Marks, Four Shillings and Eightpence, Against That Yard's Own Book, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Monday the twenty-second day of November, on a boat of about nineteen tons lying over that trench, two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the hundred and first time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The figure of that hundred was said out loud on that deck on the eighteenth of November and is not said again. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at twenty-four thousand six hundred and sixteen pence out, being one hundred and fifty-three marks, eleven shillings and fourpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirteen thousand nine hundred and seventy-six pence short, being eighty-seven marks, four shillings and eightpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty-eight days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty-eight days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninety-fourth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twelve days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixty-seventh day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-second; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-ninth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty days. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is still on a board at an end stall. There is a sheet of about four feet in the inside pocket of a coat on that deck that describes a made thing and is not the thing, and it has not been used to decide where that boat goes and nobody has been asked to use it, and it is not out of that coat. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

**Nobody went into that mouth on that Monday. The chamber with a stove in it is on the trench floor in about twenty-two fathoms and is a thing and not a building and it has no door on the shore side and nobody has given it a name. A man of about thirty-six named Corwin Tace was not on that floor on that Monday and his tool roll is not on that deck at the close of that day, and he set more marks on the fifteenth and was off that floor by the middle of that day, as he said on the eleventh he would be, and nothing he said on the fifteenth settled anything.**

---

**And the master of that boat of about nineteen tons was still on that deck, and he was asked by nobody for a date and he gave none, and his boat was not put to sea that day and is not put to sea on any day of that trip.**

"I said on the eleventh what I am going to do with my boat and my two men and my boy and that it is on a flood that suits me," he said, to nobody in particular, at about eleven in the morning, and then he went and looked at the gear. "I have nothing to add to that and nobody on this boat is going to get anything out of me by asking me a second time, and I would rather be asked once by nine people than nine times by one."

Nobody asked him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody put a date on it and nobody wrote one down.

---

**And at about half past ten in the morning, on that after deck, in the open, in front of all nine of them, a woman of about thirty-one who crews nothing and sells nothing said out loud what is in that crate and what is not in it, and she said it once, and she did not open the crate to do it, and nobody asked her to.**

"I am not opening it and I am not lifting the batten and I do not have to, because I filled it on the Thursday afternoon and I was one of the four who carried the bundles up out of that hatch and I can tell you what is in it standing where I am standing.

**In it is the best evidence this party has ever had in its hands.** That is not a thing anybody has said out loud on this deck before and I am saying it now, on a Monday, in front of all of you, and I have thought about saying it since about eleven o'clock on Saturday morning. It is the best evidence because it is a record made at the time by somebody who was there, and because it was kept dry for a very long time in a room that was dry, and because it stood in racks in a wall behind a thing on that floor that was built to hold one account up where nine used to be, and because none of it can be read by anybody on this boat and all of it is in that room's own words. **And it is going into a crate and it is not going into anybody's hand.** Not mine, not his, not the man of about fifty-one who has been in holds nineteen years. It is going into a room over a fish market and it is going in there as a thing that a room decides about, and the day it is carried to that harbor by a man in a boat with a coat on his arm is the day it becomes something else, and I am not going to be the one who does that in a straw basket with nine people watching.

And what is not in it. **The third column is not in it.** Nobody has filled it, nobody has been appointed to it, the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it and that boy's surname is on no page of that harbor and that boy is not on the water, and nobody in that boat or in that harbor is going to fill it, and I have said out loud on this deck on this Monday that nobody is going to, and I have said it once.

And a page is not in it either. There is a brown page lying flat under a weight on that tarpaulin about two foot from where I am standing and it is the third thing off the second shelf of the rack against the back wall on the north side of that room, and it is on no sheet of any kind in this boat and it is on no sheet in that harbor, and it is in this crate's way and that is why it is out of it, and I am the one who remembers where it is and I have said that out loud in front of nine people once already and I am not going to say it a second time."

Nobody thanked her. Nobody argued with her. Nobody was asked to decide whether the crate should be carried in a hand and nobody decided it, because she had said the thing out loud in front of all nine of them and the matter was settled by having been said and by nobody else's hands.

**And at about ten past five in the evening on that deck, in the open, in the ship's own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, the leaf was read back, and it was read twice because about four men of that party cannot read, and both definitions came out of the same mouth in the same words they came out of on the twenty-second day of October past and on the first day of November and on the eleventh day of November, and the reading prints its own arithmetic on its own face so that anybody with a pencil can take it and find that it agrees.**

"There are two hundred and twenty-two entries in the first column at this hour and two hundred and twenty-three at the close of the day, and there were one hundred and eighty-three of them at the close of the twelfth day of October past and forty have gone in on the forty days from Wednesday the thirteenth of October to this day, one against each of them, and not one day in two of them. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has nine hundred and eighty-seven lines in it at this hour and nine hundred and eighty-nine at the close, and seven hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the twelfth and five have gone in on each of the forty days since, three of that day's five before the hour of that day's reading and two of them after it, so that of the two hundred that have gone in, a hundred and ninety-five went in on the thirty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and two hundred less two is a hundred and ninety-eight, and seven hundred and eighty-nine and a hundred and ninety-eight is nine hundred and eighty-seven. That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's two hundred and twenty-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. **Those are the two definitions and they are said out loud in the same words and in the same order every time this leaf is read back, so that a man with a pencil taking either figure off a page knows which of the two he is holding and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat before that yard ever put a column on a page and has not been said since."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close, and it has taken no line in these forty days and it has taken no line in the ten days before them either, and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to."

---

**And the sheet that carries the money was read back after that, in full, with the day against every line, and it carries no count of persons, and the three who said no stand on it at the same size as the figures, and none of the three has been asked a second time in any of these ten days and nobody in that harbor has been asked for the rest of it.**

"Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Twenty-four thousand six hundred and sixteen pence out, being one hundred and fifty-three marks, eleven shillings and fourpence. **Thirteen thousand nine hundred and seventy-six pence short, being eighty-seven marks, four shillings and eightpence, and that figure is against this yard's own book and is nobody's else, and it is said out loud in this yard's own mouth and it has not been answered by anybody and no figure has been asked of any person in that harbor to make it otherwise.** Eight lines went out in these ten days and nothing went in on any of them, and the eight are on the face of those ten days with the day against each, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of those figures off that sheet in about a minute and find that they agree.

And on every one of those ten days one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and the whole of the ten days is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence in and one thousand six hundred and eighty pence out, being ten marks, six shillings and eightpence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that figure is the same one it was at the close of the first of November and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown."

Nobody argued with him. Nobody was asked for the rest of it. The four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and twenty-three, nine hundred and eighty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five.

**A CRATE AND MATTING AND A LID WENT UP IN THAT WATER ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF NOVEMBER AT TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AND A THIRTY-FATHOM LINE AT FIVE HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE WENT ON THAT SHEET ON THE EIGHTEENTH, AND THE PAPER IN THAT CRATE IS THE BEST EVIDENCE THIS PARTY HAS EVER HAD IN ITS HANDS AND WAS SAID OUT LOUD ONCE ON THAT DECK ON THAT MONDAY BY A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE AND IS NOT GOING INTO ANYBODY'S HAND. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT CAME UP OUT OF THAT MOUTH ON THE NINETEENTH WITH SOMETHING IN HIM THAT IS NOT HIS AND IS KEEPING IT AND IS NOT A CASUALTY AND CARRIES NO FIGURE AND WENT ON WORKING THE DAY AFTER AND THE DAY AFTER THAT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX NAMED CORWIN TACE IS NOT ON THAT FLOOR. THE MASTER OF THAT BOAT IS STILL ON THAT DECK AND HAS SAID WHAT HE IS GOING TO DO ONCE. THE THIRD COLUMN IS EMPTY AND UNAPPOINTED. A PAGE WITH A BROWN EDGE IS UNDER A WEIGHT ON A TARPAULIN AND IS ON NO SHEET. AND THE WORD THAT WOULD NAME THE SEAL ON A WARRANT IS IN NO MOUTH IN THAT BOAT AND IN NO MOUTH IN THAT HARBOR AND WAS NOT SAID ON THAT DECK AND IS NOT GOING TO BE SAID ON ANY DAY OF THAT TRIP. AND THAT SHEET IS THIRTEEN THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING EIGHTY-SEVEN MARKS, FOUR SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND NOBODY HAS BEEN ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the best evidence this party has ever had in its hands is going into a crate and not into anybody's hand is that woman's own, and is that a thing carried in a man's coat on a boat is a thing one man has, and that a thing a room has is a thing about eleven people can be told a thing about, and that the difference between the two is not a difference of safety and is a difference of who can be asked; and that the reason she said it out loud on a Monday and not on the Saturday when it came up in three baskets is her own, and is that a person who says a thing about a find on the day the find comes up has said it about a find, and that saying it eight days later, in front of the same nine people, with the crate battened and nobody's hands on it, is saying it about a decision; and that the reason the third column was named on that deck as the thing that is not in that crate is the yard's own mouth, and is that a cache of records is a cache of records and not an answer to a question nobody has asked, and that a column with a boy's hand at the head of it and no name against it has been empty longer than that boat has been on that coast, and that a party which fills it because it has found a room full of paper has filled a column with a find, and that is the one thing a column of that kind cannot carry; and that the reason the master of that boat was left alone and was not asked for a date is his own, and is that a man with a boat and two men and a boy under twenty-two fathoms has said what he is going to do once, in front of all nine of them, and that asking him a second time is not curiosity, it is a claim, and that this yard has three claims standing against it already and is not going to make a fourth out of a man who owes it nothing; and that the reason not one of that harbor's standing debts was paid, advanced, reduced, re-dated, struck or closed on the said Monday is the yard's own mouth, and is that there has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and the figure of four hundred and fifty pence is still on a board at an end stall of that market on that Monday, and that a sheet of about four feet is in the inside pocket of a coat on that deck and has not been taken out and has not been used to decide where that boat goes and has not been asked about, and that the sheet with the four heads on it in a room over a fish market and the sheet that carries the money in a shed are two sheets and are named separately here on purpose and are not to be counted together, and that neither of them is on that deck and neither of them has been asked for anything.*
