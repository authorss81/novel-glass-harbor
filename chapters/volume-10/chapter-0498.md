# Chapter 498

*Tuesday the Thirtieth Day of November, the Fifty-Eighth Wage Tuesday, the Wage Column in That Harbor Not Moving at That Tuesday and a Further Week Standing Fallen Due and Fifty-Eight Weeks of It Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wipe, a Day Apiece to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eleven in the Morning With the Thirtieth Against It, Nothing Going into That Sheet in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall at Twenty-One Thousand Nine Hundred and Ten Pence, Being One Hundred and Thirty-Six Marks, Twelve Shillings and Sixpence, Said Out Loud in That Yard's Own Mouth at About Half Past Five in the Afternoon and Not Answered by Anybody, a Room over a Fish Market at About Two o'Clock in the Afternoon With About Twenty People in It and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read, the Holder of That Writ Standing in It to Read the Four Lines Off the Face of a Sheet He Had Written and Being Told No in Front of All of Them by Hobb Lee of About Forty-Six Who Mends Nets on the Third Lay and Whom He Has Never Argued With About Anything, Sitting Down Without Answering, Four Lines Written on a Sheet in That Room and Read Out Loud by That Same Man With the Holder's Own Name and an Hour Against Each of the Four and All Four Heard by About Eleven People in That Room Who Cannot Read Them, the Name on Each of the Four Being His Own and No Restoration Coming Off Any of Them, Nobody Thanked, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Tuesday the thirtieth day of November, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is lying off the third lay.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. The wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move at that Tuesday, and a further week stands fallen due at the close of it, and that is the fifty-eighth week of that column standing fallen due and the fifty-seventh stood at the last one of these ten days. A day apiece to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that line bears the thirtieth of November and no other date. Nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any other line on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-two thousand five hundred and fifty pence out, being two hundred and three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that figure is against that yard's own book and was said out loud in that yard's own mouth at about half past five in the afternoon and was not answered by anybody. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-six days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-eight; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-six days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and second night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventy-fifth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninetieth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and seventh; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-eight days. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month and there has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August. The tide gang in that harbor is four men short and has been four men short since the middle of the summer. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and it did not lift on the twenty-ninth, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does, and the holder of that writ had to put both hands on a table to get his cup off it at breakfast that morning and did it in front of nine people without one word being said about it by anybody.**

---

**And at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf read that sheet back in full in that shed with the day against every line, and said the deficit out loud in his own mouth and in that yard's own mouth, and stopped there.**

"Twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence. That is against this yard's book and it is nobody's else. It has not been answered and I am not going to ask anybody in this harbor to answer it. If the rest of it is wanted, it is wanted in a room over that market and not in a shed, and there is a bowl on the step there on a Sunday and there is not one there on a Tuesday, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it."

Nobody asked for the rest of it. Nobody offered anything. The four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and thirty-one, one thousand and twenty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five.

---

**And at about two o'clock in the afternoon the holder of that writ stood in a room over that fish market in front of about twenty people, about eleven of whom cannot read, with a sheet of four lines in his own hand in his coat, and he was told no in about a quarter of an hour by a man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay and has not argued with him about anything.**

He said it first, out loud, to the room, before anybody had asked him anything.

"I am going to read these four lines myself. I wrote them on Saturday night in a shed and I am the man who went down into that room and got the thing they are about, and if a man in this harbor hears four sentences about a floor that was twenty-two fathoms down read out by the man who stood on it, then those four sentences carry something that they would not carry out of a man who mends nets. I have been the mouth of this thing since the twelfth of October and I am not giving that up over a reading."

**Hobb Lee put his thumb between the pages of the long table's own book and said no, and it was the first no that had ever been given to that man in that room and it was given without any argument at all.**

"Then no, and I will tell you why in one sentence, and then I will read them. A man who reads out a thing he has a stake in cannot be stopped by anybody standing in the room. In this room there are about eleven people who cannot read, and their whole of the power to stop you is one word, and the word is that you are reading it straight, and that word is not a stop. It is you asking them to trust you and asking a person who cannot read to check you is asking them to do the thing they came because they cannot do. **In September a page in an office on a hill was worth a room. That is the whole of what a page is worth and it is not nothing.**"

"And that is the hole in the rule," the holder of that writ said. "I said it out loud on the sixth of November and I said I would stand in a room with about eleven people who cannot read and be told no, and here it is and here I am and I am not going to argue with a man who has never argued with me about anything."

"I know you are not going to argue," said Hobb Lee. "That is the reason it was easy to say it."

**And the holder of that writ sat down on the bench at the back of that room at about ten past three in the afternoon and put the sheet on the long table face down, and he was not thanked for standing in that room and the room was not satisfied and nobody in it said that anything had been settled.**

---

**And Hobb Lee wrote his own name and the hour under each of the four lines as he read them, in his own hand, being his own name and the hours of ten past three, of a quarter past three and of twenty minutes to four in the afternoon of the thirtieth day of November, and he stopped after each one and looked round the room, and nobody put a hand up and nobody said a word against any of them, and about eleven people who cannot read heard all four.**

"**One.** That the office which rebuilt the mechanism standing on the floor of that trench also signed the transfer of the evidence out of that ferry's file. Calder Marr, the holder of that writ. The twenty-seventh day of November, in a shed in this harbor, in my own hand."

He stopped. He looked at the room. Nobody said anything. He read the second.

"**Two.** That the sheet it signed does not show why. Calder Marr, the holder of that writ. The twenty-seventh day of November, in a shed in this harbor, in my own hand."

He stopped. He looked at the room. A woman at the back who cannot read put her hand up about halfway through and he stopped and she said that she had not understood the second of the four and he read it again, exactly the same, and she put her hand down and he went on, and he read the third and the fourth.

"**Three.** That it does not show that the man whose name is on it knew what was in the case. Calder Marr, the holder of that writ. The twenty-seventh day of November, in a shed in this harbor, in my own hand."

"**Four.** That it does not name anybody as the author of a lie and that it is not a confession and that it is a piece of paper which does not consent and cannot be asked anything and does not testify. Calder Marr, the holder of that writ. The twenty-seventh day of November, in a shed in this harbor, in my own hand."

**And the name on each of those four lines is his own, and there are four of them and they are the same name, and the man they belong to was sitting at the back of that room and was not thanked and was not restored by any of them and did not ask to be, and a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons looked at the sheet afterward for a while and then said, out loud, that the four heads on that wall had not changed by any of it and that a fifth head was not going to be added to that wall by anybody on account of a Tuesday.**

---

**And nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day, and the four questions that people in this harbor asked a man in a hall on the west basin in the spring are not asked in that room and were not asked in it on that afternoon and are not answered there, and the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on that wall and neither was the first column of that leaf nor the second of it, and all three of those things were named out loud in that room before a word of the four lines was read.**

---

**THE FIFTY-EIGHTH WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND THE WAGE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE AND FIFTY-EIGHT WEEKS OF IT STAND FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY, AND A DAY APIECE TO THE SAME FOUR MEN WENT ON THAT SHEET AT SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE. THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT STOOD IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON TO READ FOUR LINES HE HAD WRITTEN HIMSELF AND WAS TOLD NO IN ABOUT A QUARTER OF AN HOUR BY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX WHO MENDS NETS ON THE THIRD LAY AND HAS NEVER ARGUED WITH HIM ABOUT ANYTHING, AND SAT DOWN AT ABOUT TEN PAST THREE. FOUR LINES WERE READ OUT LOUD IN THAT ROOM BY THAT SAME MAN WITH THE HOLDER'S OWN NAME AND AN HOUR AGAINST EACH OF THE FOUR AND ALL FOUR WERE HEARD BY ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM WHO CANNOT READ THEM, AND THE NAME ON EACH OF THE FOUR IS HIS OWN AND HE IS NOT RESTORED BY ANY OF THEM. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND IT WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD'S OWN MOUTH AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the thirtieth day of November at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wage column in that harbor stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move at that Tuesday, and that a further week stands fallen due at the close of it and that is the fifty-eighth week of that column standing fallen due; and that a day apiece to the same four men went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that the line bears the thirtieth of November and no other date, and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any other line on that day and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that that sheet stood at thirty-two thousand five hundred and fifty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it moved by six hundred and seventy-two pence and nothing came in against it; and that the holder of that writ said that deficit out loud in that yard's own mouth at about half past five in the afternoon and that it has not been answered and that he is not going to ask anybody in that harbor to answer it, and that if the rest of it is wanted it is wanted in a room over that market and not in a shed; and that nobody asked for the rest of it and nobody offered anything; and that the holder of that writ stood in a room over a fish market at about two o'clock in the afternoon in front of about twenty people, about eleven of whom cannot read, and said out loud before anybody had asked him anything that he was going to read four lines himself, and gave his reason for it, being that a man who stood on that floor carries something in his mouth that a man who mends nets does not; and that Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, said no to that in about a quarter of an hour and gave one sentence of reason, being that a man who reads out a thing he has a stake in cannot be stopped by anybody standing in the room and that the only power to stop him in that room is eleven people who cannot read being asked to check him, and that a page in an office on a hill was worth a room in September; and that the holder of that writ said that this was the hole in the rule he had said out loud on the sixth of November and that he was not going to argue with a man who had never argued with him about anything, and that he sat down on the bench at the back of that room at about ten past three and put the sheet face down on the long table; and that he was not thanked for standing in that room and the room was not satisfied and nobody in it said that anything had been settled; and that Hobb Lee read out the four lines, being that the office which rebuilt the mechanism standing on the floor of that trench also signed the transfer of the evidence out of that ferry's file, and that the sheet it signed does not show why, and that it does not show that the man whose name is on it knew what was in the case, and that it does not name anybody as the author of a lie and is not a confession and is a piece of paper which does not consent and cannot be asked anything and does not testify, and that the holder's own name and the twenty-seventh of November and the hour and the words in a shed in this harbor and in his own hand are against each of the four, and that the reader stopped after each of them and looked round the room, and that a woman at the back who cannot read put her hand up about halfway through the second and had it read again exactly the same and put her hand down and he went on; and that about eleven people in that room who cannot read them heard all four, and that the name on each of the four lines is the holder's own and that he is not restored by any of them and did not ask to be, and that a man of about fifty who owns a boat of about twenty-two tons said out loud afterward that the four heads on that wall had not changed by any of it and that no fifth head was going to be added to that wall by anybody on account of a Tuesday; and that the four questions that were asked in that harbor in the spring are not asked in that room and were not asked in it and are not answered there; and that the sheet that carries the money was not in that room and was not on that wall and neither was the first column of that leaf nor the second of it, and that all three of those things were named out loud in that room before a word of the four lines was read; and that nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day; and that the four columns at the close of that day stand at two hundred and thirty-one, one thousand and twenty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that a wage Tuesday did not move that wage column is in the standing of that column and is not a decision of this yard's, and that a column of twenty-six lines standing at one hundred and four marks does not move because eleven people who have not been paid this month put a week's wage each into a different sheet, and that this yard is not the harbor authority on the west basin and does not pay a day at the figure in chalk to anybody who has not asked for a day by name; and that the reason a deficit of twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence was said out loud and then left alone is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a figure said out loud and then argued about in a shed becomes a claim, and that this yard has three claims standing against it and is not going to make a fourth out of its own arithmetic; and that the reason four lines carry his name and an hour against each of them is that woman's of the twenty-second of October, and is that a line on a wall with no name under it is a rule and a line with a name under it is an account, and that an account is what that harbor is owed and a rule is what it is owed too much of; and that the reason he was told no by a man who mends nets is Hobb Lee's own, and is that he has read out in that room eight times and has never once been asked whether he agrees with what he read, and that a man who has spent eight weeks being a mouth and has never been a mouth that could be stopped has not noticed that he has stopped being a person who can be told what to read, and that somebody in that harbor had to notice it and it was not going to be anybody who had spent the winter on the water; and that the reason the four questions were left unasked in that room is the yard's own mouth, and is that a room of about eleven people who cannot read is the only room in that harbor where a question gets asked badly, and that a badly asked question is worse than no question because it is a question that four people answer and the fifth does not hear.*
