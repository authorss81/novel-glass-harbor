# Chapter 482

*Sunday the Fourteenth Day of November, the Eightieth Time the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench in That Harbor Came Down Bare at About Eight in the Morning and Went Up Whole at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth, a Wipe, and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, the Shortfall Unmoved at Nine Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Six Pence, Being Fifty-Eight Marks and Sixpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty, One Descent on Air Out of That Boat's Own Bottles with a Figure of Forty Minutes on the Slate and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff He Could Pull Without Asking, a Man of About Thirty-Seven Going Down at About Ten Past Ten to Count the Racks in That Room and Coming Up at About Eleven o'Clock, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Reading the Head of One Page of That Paper Out Loud on That Deck at About Two in the Afternoon in Front of All Nine of Them and Saying Out Loud Before Anybody Could Say It About Her That a Record Is a Thing Somebody Wrote and That It Does Not Consent and Cannot Be Asked Anything and Does Not Testify, and a Thing Somebody Made Being Described and Cut and Copied and Carried and Put on a Shelf, Nobody Thanking Her, and That Going into the Second Column and Not into the First Because a Stranger with a Pencil Can Check What a Page Looks Like and Cannot Check What It Was Meant to Do, the Hand on the Paper Being Old and Cramped and No Man's and There Being Nothing on the Face of Any of It That Fixes It to a Reckoning*

Sunday the fourteenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Sunday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning for the eightieth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at nineteen thousand nine hundred and twenty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence in, and it is nine thousand two hundred and eighty-six pence short, being fifty-eight marks and sixpence, and it has not moved since the close of the day before. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and eighty-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and four days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its fifty-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-two days. The mole-head light in that harbor is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eighteenth week and that crew has said not one word about them. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**A slate was written before anybody went down and the man of about thirty-seven wrote his own reason on it and it was in his own words and the man who keeps the leaf copied it, and the reason was that the man of about fifty-one said there were racks in that room and that a rack is a made thing and a made thing has a number of shelves and the number is on the face of it and can be read off a picture.**

He went down at about ten past ten in the morning on that ebb under that slate, and the air limit on it was a figure of forty minutes and the air in it was out of that boat's own bottles, and the tender was named and was on the surface of that boat and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.

He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about eleven o'clock. **He came up and said that there are four racks against the back wall of that room and one long one down the side, and that the four are alike and the long one is not, and that every shelf of every one of the five has a raised lip about two inches deep along the back of it, and that a lip like that is for stopping a thing sliding off backwards, and that he did not touch any of it and he did not go further than the front of the fifth rack and nobody asked him to.**

Nobody thanked him and no figure is set against him anywhere. Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice.

---

**And the paper lay on that tarpaulin under the stove-pipe bracket from about eleven o'clock in the morning until about two in the afternoon, and it lay there with a weight on the four corners and nobody stepped over it, and at about two in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office had it on the after deck with a lamp beside her and a slate, and she read the head of one page of it out loud, in the open, in front of all nine of them.**

**What was on the head of it was a word she could read, and it named what the sheet was for and not who kept it and not where it was kept, and against that word was a figure, and the same figure stood at the head of every sheet in the bundle she opened three of, and under the head of each of them was a column ruled down, and in that column were figures, one to a ruled line, and beside each figure a short stroke of the same length every time.**

"There is no name anywhere on it and there is no year anywhere on it," she said, "and there is a figure at the head of it and the figure is the same on every one of them in this bundle, and I have had a lamp on eleven of these since about two o'clock and I cannot tell you what any of the figures are counting, because there is nothing on the face of this paper that says what a figure on it means. A man could make a table out of this in an afternoon. He could not tell you in a year what any of it was for."

**And then she put the slate down and said the rest of it before anybody could say it about her, and nobody in that boat had said a word.**

"And I am going to say the rest of it now and I am going to say it in front of all nine of you, because in about three days one of you is going to want to write down what that paper says about the people it is about, and one of you is going to want to say out loud that this paper is on our side, and both of those are going to be said in a good cause and both of them are going to be wrong.

A record is a thing somebody wrote. It does not consent. It cannot be asked anything. It does not testify. It is not a witness and it is not a verdict and it is not a voice and nobody ever put a word in it and it cannot be asked whether it meant what it says. If you want to know what it was for, you have to find out who wrote it and why, and that is work, and it is work about people, and it can be got wrong, and it has to be got wrong out loud and by somebody with a name against it. **A thing somebody made can be described, cut, copied, carried and put on a shelf.** That is all a record is. That is what it is good for. It is not good for what we would all like it for this afternoon, and I know what we would all like it for, and I have wanted it since about eleven o'clock this morning as much as anybody on this deck."

**And the man who keeps the leaf asked her one question, which was whether it went in the first column, and she told him no.**

"It goes in the second. A stranger with a pencil can take up a page of paper and look at it and say what it looks like. He can count the ruled lines and he can take the figures off it and he can put a wet thumb on it. He cannot take up what that paper was meant to do. **That is not a figure anybody can check from a mole and that is exactly what the second column is for**, and I am not going to have the only thing we have found in six weeks sitting in a column a man with a pencil can audit as though the pencil had asked it anything."

Nobody thanked her. She was not thanked when she read a line out in a room over a fish market on the twenty-fifth of October past and she was not thanked on the seventh of November and she has not been thanked on any day of this trip and nobody in that harbor has thanked her for anything.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four of the second lay, who has not been asked for his book and has not shown it to anybody and who asked out loud on the eleventh that both of those things stay exactly as they are for as long as that boat is on that coast, sat on the ladder step with a slate on his knee and wrote a figure on it, and he showed it to nobody.**

"About three weeks," he said, when Mara Venn asked him what the sheets in that room had been standing in for, and he said it once and he did not say it a second time and he did not put the other half of it up again, because a number can be said twice to show that it has not moved and a thing that is not a number is said once.

**He was asked for nothing else on that day. He was not asked for a figure of years and he was not asked for his book and he did not offer either and nobody on that deck looked in his coat.**

**THE HEAD OF ONE PAGE OF THAT PAPER WAS READ OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN ON THAT AFTER DECK AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON BEFORE ALL NINE OF THEM BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE, AND SHE SAID OUT LOUD, BEFORE ANYBODY COULD HAVE SAID IT ABOUT HER, THAT A RECORD IS A THING SOMEBODY WROTE AND THAT IT DOES NOT CONSENT AND CANNOT BE ASKED ANYTHING AND DOES NOT TESTIFY, AND THAT A THING SOMEBODY MADE CAN BE DESCRIBED, CUT, COPIED, CARRIED AND PUT ON A SHELF, AND THAT IT GOES IN THE SECOND COLUMN AND NOT IN THE FIRST BECAUSE A STRANGER WITH A PENCIL CAN CHECK WHAT A PAGE LOOKS LIKE AND CANNOT CHECK WHAT IT WAS MEANT TO DO. NOBODY THANKED HER. THE FOUR RACKS AGAINST THE BACK WALL OF THAT ROOM AND ONE LONG ONE DOWN THE SIDE WERE COUNTED OFF THE FRONT OF THE FIFTH RACK BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN AND EVERY SHELF OF EVERY ONE OF THEM HAS A RAISED LIP ABOUT TWO INCHES DEEP ALONG THE BACK OF IT. NOBODY WENT OUT ON ANY LINE OF THAT SHEET IN ANY HAND ON THAT DAY. AND THAT SHEET IS NINE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING FIFTY-EIGHT MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the fourteenth day of November at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning for the eightieth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand, and that the running out on that sheet did not move from the close of the day before; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the air on the slate of that day came out of bottles that boat carried off her own coast and filled off her own compressor and that no figure of it is on any sheet in that boat or in that harbor and the master named it as his own out loud on the surface and nobody asked him for it a second time; and that a man of about thirty-seven went down at about ten past ten in the morning on that ebb under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that a rack is a made thing and a made thing has a number of shelves and the number is on the face of it and can be read off a picture, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about eleven o'clock; and that he said out loud on that deck that there are four racks against the back wall of that room and one long one down the side, that the four are alike and the long one is not, that every shelf of every one of the five has a raised lip about two inches deep along the back of it, and that he did not touch any of it and did not go further than the front of the fifth rack and nobody asked him to; and that the paper lay on that tarpaulin under the stove-pipe bracket from about eleven o'clock in the morning until about two in the afternoon with a weight on the four corners and nobody stepped over it; and that a woman of thirty of no office had the head of one page of it on that after deck at about two in the afternoon with a lamp beside her and read it out loud in the open in front of all nine of them, and that on the head of it was a word naming what the sheet was for and not who kept it and not where it was kept, and a figure, and the same figure stood at the head of every sheet in that bundle, and under the head of each was a column ruled down with a figure to a ruled line and a short stroke of the same length beside each figure; and that she said out loud that there is no name on it and no year on it and nothing on the face of it that says what any of those figures are counting; and that she said out loud, before anybody could have said it about her, that a record is a thing somebody wrote, that it does not consent and cannot be asked anything and does not testify, that it is not a witness and not a verdict and not a voice, and that a thing somebody made can be described, cut, copied, carried and put on a shelf, and that she has wanted that paper since about eleven o'clock that morning as much as anybody on that deck; and that the man who keeps the leaf asked her whether it went in the first column and she said it goes in the second, because a stranger with a pencil can check what a page looks like and cannot check what it was meant to do; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat on a ladder step with a slate on his knee and gave one figure, about three weeks, once, and did not put the other half of it up again and was not asked for a figure of years and was not asked for his book and did not offer either and nobody looked in his coat; and that nobody thanked anybody on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the head of one page of that paper was read out on that deck in the open before all nine of them and not in a room with two people in it is the yard's own mouth, and is that a page read out in a room with two people in it is a page read out to a party, and that there are about eleven people in that harbor who cannot read, and that a thing said once on a deck in front of nine people cannot afterwards be described as something a woman said to a man; and that the reason a record was put outside the reach of the first column on that day is that woman's own, and is that the first column is a count a stranger with a pencil can take off a page and a question of what a page was meant to do is not a count and never will be, and that a person who puts a meaning into a column that can be audited has put their own meaning there and called it a finding; and that the reason she said all of it before anybody could say it about her is her own, and is that there is about three days on that trip between a find and a use for it, and that the uses will all be good, and that she would rather the whole of that be on the record in her mouth with the hour against it before any of them is in anybody's; and that the reason a man of about thirty-four gave one figure once on that deck and did not put the other half up again is his own, and is that he had put the other half in front of nine men three times in four days already and a fourth time would have been an argument and not a figure, and that a man who finds himself arguing is a man who has stopped measuring; and that the reason nobody thanked her and nobody has thanked her on any day of that trip is the yard's own mouth, and is that a woman of thirty of no office who has forty marks first after food standing whole against her and who is thanked for nothing gets thanked for the things she does in a room, and that this was a thing she did on a deck and a room is a different place and the two do not cross over; and that the reason four racks and one long one were counted off the front of the fifth rack and no further is in that man's own mouth and is that a man who has been in holds nineteen years can tell a man what is on a rack from the front of it in about a minute and a half, and that everything past the fifth rack in that room belongs to somebody who is not on that boat, and that the party is not going to be the party that empties a room on the strength of a door found on a Saturday; and that the reason the whole of that harbor's standing condition was left exactly where it stood on the said Sunday is the yard's own mouth, and is that the eightieth wipe of that year is the one morning of a week on which a wall behind a reeve's bench comes down and goes up whole and nobody puts it back, and that a man who counted four racks at the bottom of the sea on that Sunday did not touch a wage column in that market on that Sunday, and that the column stood where it stood and the top name on that rota board is still off it.*
