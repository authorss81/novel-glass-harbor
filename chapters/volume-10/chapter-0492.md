# Chapter 492

*Wednesday the Twenty-Fourth Day of November, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Four Days of Coal for the Pump at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Fifty-Two Pence, Being Fourteen Marks, Nine Shillings and Fourpence, and Five Fresh Days of Air off That Same Supply Boat at Nine Hundred Pence, Being Five Marks, Eight Shillings and Fourpence, and a Second Pair of Hands for a Day at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Being One Mark and Eightpence, All Three on the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eleven in the Morning and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, Every Descent from This Day Onward on That One Line and on Nothing Else, a Man of About Thirty-Two Going Down at About Half Past Twelve in the Afternoon on Air Out of That Line and Not out of That Boat's Own Bottles and Sitting Down in the Eleventh Frame at About Half Past One and Being Out of That Bell Again at About Ten to Two, Nobody Asked Him Before or After, the Word Given at About Forty-One Minutes Past One and a Cutoff Pulled Without Asking, Eleven Minutes on a Clock That Closes, an Aftertaste and a Loss of Physical and Personal Stability That This Trip Does Not Put Right and That Carries No Figure, Nobody Thanked, the Shortfall Stated at Nineteen Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, Being One Hundred and Twenty-Two Marks, Twelve Shillings and Fourpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the twenty-fourth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Four days of coal for the pump went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning at two thousand three hundred and fifty-two pence, being fourteen marks, nine shillings and fourpence, and five fresh days of air came off that same supply boat and went onto the same sheet at about the same hour at nine hundred pence, being five marks, eight shillings and fourpence, and that line bears the twenty-fourth of November and no other date, and a second pair of hands for a day went on at the same hour at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and nothing went onto that sheet in any hand in any line on that day. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty thousand three hundred and eight pence out, being one hundred and eighty-nine marks, five shillings and eightpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is nineteen thousand six hundred and sixty-eight pence short, being one hundred and twenty-two marks, twelve shillings and fourpence. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-two; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninety-sixth night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and fourteen days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixty-ninth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-fourth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and first; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-two days. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and there is no barley in it since the middle of September. The north berth in that harbor is out and the hose is dead until the spring. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does, and it is not going to lift this trip.**

---

**Three lines went onto that sheet at about eleven in the morning and they were read off it out loud as they went on, and the man who keeps the leaf said the whole of what each one was before he wrote it.**

"Coal, four days, off that same boat, at two thousand three hundred and fifty-two. Air, five days, at nine hundred, and the five days are the twenty-fourth, the twenty-fifth, the twenty-sixth, the twenty-seventh and the twenty-eighth, and every descent from this day onward is on that line and on nothing else, and nothing went into that room on any air after the twentieth of November except the air that boat carried off her own coast. A pair of hands for a day, at the figure in chalk, off that same boat, at one hundred and sixty-eight. Three thousand four hundred and twenty pence together."

Nobody asked where the money was coming from, because it was coming from the same place it had been coming from for eight weeks, which was from a yard that is a long way short and does not stop.

---

**And at about half past twelve in the afternoon the holder of that writ said out loud on that deck, in front of all nine of them, what he was going to do that afternoon and how long for, and he said it before anybody had said a word to him about it, and he gave the hour twice.**

"I am going down at half past twelve and I am going to be in the eleventh one from about half past one until about forty-one minutes past one. That is eleven minutes and I am going to count them and I am not going to round them. My air is the line of the twenty-fourth and the limit on my slate is forty minutes and the man who keeps the leaf has written the reason on it and the hour against it. Ivo Sark is the tender and he is on the surface and I am not asking him for anything, and he has a cutoff he can pull without asking anybody's leave and I am telling him in front of the nine of them that I am not going to ask him for it either."

**And then the man who keeps the leaf said the one thing that was said out loud about it, and he said it once, and nobody else on that deck said it after him.**

"Nobody has asked him and nobody is going to ask him, and if anybody in this boat wants to know why, the reason is that a person who is asked whether he will be used as a piece of equipment has already been used as one. That is the whole of it and I am not going to say it again this trip."

Nobody asked him. Nobody put a figure on him. Nobody named the thing he did on that deck and nobody put a word for what it was after the other two had been used, and neither of those was in anybody's mouth that day and neither of them is in this column.

---

**The bell went down at about twenty-five minutes past twelve and the man of about fifty-one and the man of about thirty-seven were already on that floor at the front of those frames, where they had been working since about ten, in the ordinary way, and neither of them was asked anything about the afternoon and neither of them was told a figure.**

**The holder of that writ went down into that bell at about half past twelve and was in the water about nine minutes and was in that bell and on that floor the rest of it, and he came out of it at about ten to two.** Ivo Sark, of about twenty-four, was on the surface with his hand on the chain weight and the cutoff, and he was not in that bell at any hour, and the pump hand of about forty-four was on the compressor with him, and Nessa Pike was on that after deck with the minute open on her knee, and nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood and nobody went in twice.

He sat down in the eleventh frame with his back against the iron of it and his hands on the ring, and the two men at the front of those frames were about nine foot off him, and the thing on that floor took from all three of them, and what it took from the two men at the front came through that frame and into him, and that is what holding them to shore is, and it is not a thing anybody on that boat has to be persuaded is dangerous.

---

**At about thirty-five minutes past one the tender said out loud over the wire that the man in that bell was not answering, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote that hour against the line, and at about forty-one minutes past one the holder of that writ said one word, which was the word, and the tender pulled the cutoff without asking anybody anything, and that bell was up in about six minutes and he was out of it at about ten to two.**

The eleven minutes were eleven minutes. A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take the half hour and the forty-one minutes off the face of that day and find that they agree.

He could not get his knees to work on the ladder. He got up it on his hands and his knees and he sat down on that after deck at about ten to two and stayed sitting there until about half past two, and the pump hand of about forty-four put a can of hot something in his hands at about a quarter past two and did not say a word and was not thanked for it, and the man of about thirty-eight stood about nine feet off him and looked at the water and did not come nearer.

**And from about two o'clock to about four o'clock in the afternoon of that day he could not tell the order in which three things happened, and Nessa Pike has all three of them in that minute with the hour against each, and he read them at about four o'clock and they were right and he could not place them, and he has not been able to place them since and is not going to be asked about it and has not asked.**

"I know that I was on that deck at half past two and I know that the minute has me writing at ten minutes to three and at half past three, and I have read both of those and I could not tell him which of them came first," he said, at about four o'clock, and then he said no more about it on that trip.

His hands were not steady for two days afterward. He put a hand on a gunwale to get himself up off that deck and he put a hand on a gunwale to get himself out of that boat on the twenty-seventh and on the twenty-eighth and on the twenty-ninth and on the thirtieth, and he put a hand on a table in a room over a fish market on the thirtieth, and on the second day of December he could not get his cup down off the counter without putting two fingers on it. **No figure is set against any part of it anywhere and no sheet in that boat or that harbor carries a line about it. He is not a casualty and nobody has named him one. Nobody thanked him and nobody was asked to be thanked and he is not an example of anything and was not made one.**

---

**At about half past nine in the evening on that deck the four columns stood at two hundred and twenty-five, nine hundred and ninety-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five, and the sheet that carries the money was read back in full with the day against every line, and it carries no count of persons, and nobody was asked for the rest of it.**

**THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT SAT DOWN IN THE ELEVENTH FRAME ON THAT FLOOR AT ABOUT HALF PAST ONE AND WAS OUT OF THAT BELL AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO TWO, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM AND NOBODY WAS GOING TO ASK HIM, AND THE REASON FOR THAT WAS SAID OUT LOUD ONCE ON THAT DECK BY THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF AND BY NOBODY ELSE. THE CUTOFF WAS PULLED AT ABOUT FORTY-ONE MINUTES PAST ONE WITHOUT ANYBODY ASKING AND THE ELEVEN MINUTES CLOSE ON THE FACE OF THAT DAY. HE CAME UP WITH A TASTE THAT DID NOT LIFT, A TREMOR IN BOTH HANDS THAT WAS STILL IN THEM ON THE SECOND DAY OF DECEMBER, AND A STRETCH OF HIS OWN AFTERNOON THAT HE CANNOT PUT IN ORDER AND THAT NESSA PIKE HAS WITH THE HOURS AGAINST IT AND THAT HE READ AND COULD NOT PLACE. NOBODY THANKED HIM AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TO BE THANKED AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST ANY PART OF IT AND HE IS NOT A CASUALTY AND NOBODY HAS NAMED HIM ONE. THAT SHEET IS NINETEEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the air for that day came off that same supply boat at nine hundred pence and not out of that boat's own bottles is the yard's own mouth, and is that a boat's own air is a kindness with a day attached to it and a week of standing twenty-two fathoms deep on somebody else's coal is not, and that the second pair of hands was bought at the figure in chalk off that same boat for the same reason and for no other; and that the reason nobody asked the holder of that writ whether he would sit in that frame is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that the question and the asking are the two halves of a use, and that a party which asks its own man for a body has already had the body by the time it has finished asking, and that the only way this yard has of not doing that is to say out loud in front of nine people that the asking is not going to happen; and that the reason he gave the hour twice out loud before anybody had said anything to him about it is his own, and is that a man who is about to stop answering for himself has about eleven minutes in which to make certain that nobody afterwards can say he was asked to do something he did not say he was doing; and that the reason the tender said out loud over the wire that the man in that bell was not answering and did not pull the cutoff on it is Ivo Sark's own, and is that a man on a surface with his hand on a chain weight has two things he may do and one of them is not his to do for another man, and that the hour was written down and the hour is what a tender is for; and that the reason the hot can and the man standing nine feet off are both in this column and neither of them is thanked is the yard's own mouth, and is that the price of a thing done with a body is that everybody near it gets a small job and that the small job is not a share of it and must never be written down as one.*
