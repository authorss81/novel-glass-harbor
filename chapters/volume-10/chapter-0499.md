# Chapter 499

*Wednesday the First Day of December, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Fitting-Out Sheet in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand and Nobody Asked for Anything, the Shortfall Standing Unmoved at Twenty-One Thousand Nine Hundred and Ten Pence, Being One Hundred and Thirty-Six Marks, Twelve Shillings and Sixpence, a Case and a Lock and a Lining of Oilcloth Being Opened at About Ten Past Two in the Afternoon in a Room over a Fish Market With About Twenty People in It and About Eleven of Them Who Cannot Read and Not in a Shed, a Sheet of Paper With a Date on It and a Place on It and a Line Ruled About Two Inches Long With an Impression Pressed Into the Paper Beside It and Broken at a Fold That Goes Through It and an Endorsement in a Second Hand in Brown Ink Where the First Is Black and a Name at the Right of It and Four Words and a Short Stroke Down the Left Margin in a Third Hand in Green Ink, Read Out Loud Twice by Hobb Lee of About Forty-Six With His Own Name and an Hour Under Each Reading, and the Four Limits Read Out With It and Not After It, a Record Written in That Room of What Was Read and by Whom and at What Hour and Entered in the Book of That Room and the Paper Going Back into That Case and That Case Being Locked and the Key Going Back, No Minister in That Room and No Head on Any Sheet in It and Nobody in That Room Being Only Ever a Name or a Seal or an Office, the Man of About Twenty-Nine Not Hearing Out of the Left Side of His Head on That Wednesday and Nobody Saying When It Will Lift, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the first day of December, in that harbor, in about four hundred yards of water and along a stone quay, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is lying off the third lay.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand and nobody was asked for anything, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at thirty-two thousand five hundred and fifty pence out, being two hundred and three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that figure has not moved since the Tuesday and is against that yard's own book and nobody has asked for the rest of it. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and no penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and ninety-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and ninety-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and thirty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the three hundred and third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and twenty-one days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its seventy-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its ninety-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its hundred and eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and thirty-nine days. Nineteen winters are disputed in that harbor and have been disputed for nineteen years and not one of them has been paid. One berth is short on the third lay and has been since the middle of the summer. A man of about twenty-nine who came home on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and it did not lift on the thirtieth and it did not lift on the first, and nobody in that harbor has been told when it will and the man himself has not said. The holder of that writ could not get his hands steady through the whole of that afternoon and put a hand on the long table in that room to get himself up off the bench at about half past three, and no figure is set against that anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**And at about ten past two in the afternoon on that Wednesday the case was opened in a room over that fish market and not in a shed, by the woman who wrote the minute, who had the key and who had not had anybody ask her for it in the four days between.**

Nessa Pike put the case on the long table with the lining of oilcloth folded back and the lock open and did not lift the paper out. She put her hand flat on it for a moment and then took her hand off and said what she had said on the Saturday.

"A case is a thing that can be carried and a case is not a hand. It has been in a room over that market since Saturday and it has not been read by anybody in that room and it has not been shown to anybody in that room. There are about eleven people in this room who cannot read it and I am opening it in front of all of you, and there is no man at the head of this table and no man in this room who is the name on it."

Nobody at the head of the table said anything, because there is nobody at the head of it.

---

**And Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay, read it out in that room at about twenty past two in the afternoon and read it out again at about half past two, and put his own name and the hour under both readings in his own hand, and nobody stopped him at any word, and about eleven people who cannot read heard it twice.**

"On the top half, in a small upright hand, a head and a rule ruled under it, and six short strokes ruled down the left of it, being six lines against six strokes. Beside the head there is a place, and it is the name of a port and it is not the name of this harbor. There is a date on it, being the fourteenth day of a month that the clerk of that office called by a name this coast does not use. **There is no year on the face of it at all, and that is not a thing that has been taken off it; the year of that office was kept in a register in another building and has not been in that register for a long time.**"

"To the right of that head there is a line ruled about two inches long with nothing on it in ink, and beside the middle of that line there is an impression pressed into the paper about the size of a thumbnail, and the impression is broken at the top edge because the paper has been folded once across it, and the impression on the two sides of that fold does not make up into one another. Nobody in this room has said what made that impression and I am not going to say."

"At the foot of it, in a second and larger hand, in an ink that is brown where the first is black, there is an endorsement of one line with a rule under it, and at the right of that endorsement there is a name. That name is a name on a paper. That man is not in this harbor and he is not in this room and nobody in this room is that name and no part of that name is standing at the long table this afternoon."

"Down the left margin, in a third hand, in a green ink, there are four words and a short stroke under them, being that the sheet was received into that port's ferry file. That is three hands and three inks. A stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take all three of them off the face of that paper in about a minute and find that they are three and not two, and that is the whole of what a pencil can do with it."

---

**And the four limits were read out with it and were not read out after it, and about eleven people who cannot read heard the paper, then all four of the limits, and then the paper again, and the order those three things were in went into the record that room keeps with the hour against each of them, because about eleven people who cannot read were never going to remember which of them came first.**

"**And this is what it does not show.** It does not show why. There is no line of writing anywhere on that sheet with a reason at the head of it. It does not show that the man whose name is on it knew what was in the case, and there is nothing whatever on the face of that paper about what was in the case. It does not name anybody as the author of a lie and it is not a confession. And it is a piece of paper, and it does not consent, and it cannot be asked anything, and it does not testify, and nobody in this room can put a question to it and get an answer out of it and anybody who tells you that a paper has told them something is telling you about themselves."

**And then a man of about forty at the back of that room asked, out loud, whether the man whose name was on it was a bad man, and the woman of thirty of no office answered him, and she was not thanked for answering him, and about eleven people who cannot read heard every word of it and none of them said anything.**

"No. And I am not going to tell you he is not, either, and that is not me being careful. A signature on a transfer is a signature on a transfer. I have spent eleven years looking at documents made to sound certain, and the whole of my trade is that a document which settles a question is a document somebody has settled on purpose, and this one settles nothing. It says that a man signed a thing on a day. It does not say what he thought when he signed it, and it does not say what was in the case, and a man who signed a transfer of a case that he had not opened has done something that this paper cannot tell you about, and it may be the worst thing a man can do and it may be the ordinary way that office did its work in that year. **I am not going to hand a room of people who cannot read a worse man than the paper has got.**"

---

**And at about half past three a sheet was written of what had been read in that room and by whom and at what hour, and it was entered in the book that room keeps, and the paper went back into that case and the case was locked and the key went back to the woman who wrote the minute, and no figure is set against the paper, the case, the lock, the oilcloth, the day of the woman of about twenty-seven who wrote it, or the reading of a man of about forty-six who was paid nothing for it, and nobody in that harbor has been asked to hold any of it.**

---

**And the third column of that leaf is empty and nobody has been appointed to it and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it and has not been scratched out and his surname is on no page of that harbor, and that boy is not on the water and has not been asked why and nobody in that harbor is going to ask him. And the fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is not closed and is not struck. And nothing standing in that harbor was paid, advanced, reduced, improved, struck or closed on that day.**

**A SHEET OF PAPER CAME OUT OF A CASE IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET AT ABOUT TWENTY PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ON THE FIRST DAY OF DECEMBER AND WAS READ OUT TWICE BY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX WHO MENDS NETS ON THE THIRD LAY, WITH HIS OWN NAME AND AN HOUR UNDER EACH READING, AND THE FOUR LIMITS WERE READ OUT WITH IT AND NOT AFTER IT AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT ROOM WAS THE NAME ON THAT PAPER AND THERE WAS NO MAN AT THE HEAD OF THAT TABLE. IT SHOWS THAT A SIGNATURE IS ON A TRANSFER AND NOTHING ELSE. A RECORD OF THE READING WENT INTO THE BOOK OF THAT ROOM AND THE PAPER WENT BACK INTO THAT CASE AND THAT CASE WAS LOCKED. THE MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE DID NOT HEAR OUT OF THE LEFT SIDE OF HIS HEAD ON THAT DAY AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHEN IT WILL LIFT. THE THIRD COLUMN IS EMPTY AND THE BOY'S HAND IS NOT SCRATCHED OUT. THAT SHEET IS TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

and that that sheet stood at thirty-two thousand five hundred and fifty pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and is twenty-one thousand nine hundred and ten pence short, being one hundred and thirty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, and that the running out on it did not move from the close of the day before; and that the case that went into that harbor on the twenty-seventh was opened at about ten past two in the afternoon of the said day in a room over a fish market and not in a shed, by the woman who wrote the minute, who had had the key in her keeping since the twenty-seventh and who had not had anybody ask her for it in the four days between; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the case was opened in that room and not in a shed is that woman's of the twenty-seventh, and is that a thing opened where eleven people who cannot read are standing is a thing that about eleven people know the contents of by the end of the afternoon, and a thing opened in a shed is a thing that one person knows the contents of and has to go and find eleven people to tell; and that the reason the paper was not lifted out of the case by the hand that had the key is hers, and is that a woman who unlocks a case in a room and takes the paper out of it has made herself the hand that touched it, and that on the twenty-seventh she had said out loud in that room that a case is a thing that can be carried and a case is not a hand, and that a person who proves a rule by breaking it once in front of witnesses has not got a rule; and that the reason the four limits were read with the paper and not after it is the holder of that writ's own, said on the twenty-sixth, and is that a limit said after a thing has been read is an opinion about the thing and a limit said with it is part of what the thing is, and that about eleven people who cannot read were never going to remember the order of those two things and would have gone home with whichever of them came last; and that the reason no figure was set against the reading is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a man of about forty-six reading a sheet out twice in a room is a man of about forty-six giving up an afternoon, and that putting a figure on that afternoon would be putting a price on a working man's time in a room where eleven people are owed an afternoon and not a wage, and that this yard does not put a figure on a thing because somebody happened to do it for nothing; and that the reason nothing on that harbor's standing list moved on that Wednesday is the yard's own mouth, and is that nineteen winters are disputed in that harbor and one berth is short on the third lay, and that a piece of paper read out in a room over a market does not put a roof on a single one of them and that the party that put a week's wage each into a sheet is not going to be the party that says it did.*
