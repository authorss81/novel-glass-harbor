# Chapter 458

*Wednesday the Twentieth Day of October, the First Descent of That Trip, Everything About It Written Down Before Anybody Went Down With a Tender of About Thirty-Eight Named Teodor Vask on the Surface and Not in the Boat, a Fixed Air Limit of Forty Minutes and a Reason Against the Hour of About One o'Clock, Eleven Minutes on the Bottom of That Shelf by a Man Who Came Up With a Cold in His Teeth and a Taste of Iron Like a Nail, Then a Deck Hand of About Twenty-Nine Named Amos Trewin Down at About Half Past Two in the Afternoon at Nine Fathoms, a Gasket That Let Go, About a Bell's Worth of Water, a Chain Weight Over the Rail at About Twenty to Three With the Reason Written Down at About Five in the Afternoon, a Man Out in About Six Minutes and No Man Lost, a Gasket and a Patch of Sheet Copper at Two Hundred and Forty-Four Pence, Worked Glass Blanks and Tin and a Deck Plate and Stove Pipe at One Thousand Three Hundred and Forty Pence, a Second Hand's Day at the Compressor at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, and What Two Men Said About One Sound and Not Agreeing*

Wednesday the twentieth day of October, on the Crownless Shelf, in about nine fathoms, and in a bell of about two and a half tons.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in a harbor two days behind them and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on that boat and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-six days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-eight days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fifteenth week. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-six days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-second night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-eight days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-fifth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fiftieth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-seventh day.**

---

**Everything about the first descent of that trip was written down before anybody went down, and it was written on a slate and not on the fitting-out sheet, because a fitting-out sheet is a list of what money did and a slate is a list of what a man is about to do.**

It says a tender: **Teodor Vask, of about thirty-eight, of the second lay, on the surface, in a boat, and not in the boat with the bell in it.** It says an air limit of forty minutes from the bell going down, written as a figure and not as an opinion. It says an independent line off the tender's quarter, separate from the bell's own wire, fifteen fathoms of it with a weight on the tail. It says a cutoff, which is a chain weight off that tender's quarter that the tender can pull without asking anybody's permission, and that has been pulled once on this trip, and that the one time it was pulled the reason was written down afterwards with the hour against it. It says a reason, and the reason on the said day was: *to see the floor of the shelf and the four-inch mark and the reef edge, and to bring back the number of fathoms and nothing else.* And the hour against it was about one o'clock in the afternoon.

He was in the water about thirty-five minutes and on the bottom about eleven of them and he came up at about ten past two.

He sat on the after deck of that boat with his coat off and said two things out loud to about four men on that deck, because that is what a body says and a body says it to people and not to a paper. The first was a cold in his teeth. The second was a taste of iron in his mouth like a nail. **And he said, before either of them, that he would not be putting either of those two things in a book, and that the reason was that a man on a mole cannot check a man's teeth, and that this was what the second column was for.**

Then Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, of the third lay, a deck hand who crews in nobody's boat and holds no office and is on no sheet, asked for the next descent and was given it, because a man who asks is a man who is not going to be sent.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon the bell went down again with Trewin in it, to the same nine fathoms, and at about ten to three the gasket in the hatch of that bell let go.

**Teodor Vask was on the surface with his hand on the chain weight and he pulled it, and the weight came over the rail at about twenty to three in the afternoon, and nobody in that boat had to ask him whether to pull it and nobody in that boat spoke to him about it afterwards, and the reason was written down on the slate at about five o'clock in the afternoon with the hour against it and was, in the tender's own words, that water was coming in and the man was in it.**

The bell was on the bottom at about nine fathoms and about a bell's worth of water went into it in about four minutes. Trewin was out through the hatch and up the ladder and aboard in about six minutes. **The bell itself came up on the same wire about forty minutes later, flooded to about the level of the sill, and it stayed on the deck of that boat and was pumped out through the night by two men on the day rate and it is in this harbor's own records as a thing that happened and not as a thing that was nearly.**

Nobody was lost. One man of about twenty-four who is not named on any page of that harbor said afterwards, in his own mouth, on the after deck, that he had got his hands on the cutter that was not there because there was no cutter aboard, and that that was the only thought he had.

**And Amos Trewin came up and sat down on the after deck and was not hurt and was not made a casualty and was not thanked and was not sent home, and about an hour afterwards he said that he could not hear out of the left side of his head. He said it in his own mouth, to the tender, and to about six men, and he said that it was not a pain and that it was not ringing and that the sounds on that side were about nine feet off. He went on working that evening at the chain pump and he went on working the next four days.**

It does not come back inside four days. Nobody in that party says so out loud on the twentieth of October and nobody in that party puts a figure on it and nobody in that party writes the word injury next to it, and it is entered in the second column of that leaf with the hour against it and with the reason against the line, and the reason is in the yard's own mouth and is that a man on a mole cannot check what a man can hear.

---

**He said one thing about what was in that bell and it went into the record with the hour against it, and it does not agree with what another man said about the same forty minutes about four hours earlier, and both of them are in the same book.**

Amos Trewin, at about half past four in the afternoon, on the after deck, with his coat on the rail: "There was a noise down there. It was not the water and it was not the pump and it was not a bell going. It came from about nine fathoms and about forty yards off, and it was there before anything that makes a noise got there, and then it was where it was supposed to be. I have worked a winch in a boat my whole life and I know what a sound does before the thing that makes it, and that is not a thing a sound does."

Calder Marr, at about ten past six in the evening, on the same deck: "I heard nothing. I have been in nine bells in this water and I heard the pump and the wire and my own breathing and about nine fathoms of nothing at all, and if there was a noise in it I did not have it, and I am not going to say he did not hear it, because I do not know that he did not hear it, and a man who has been in nine fathoms of water for eleven minutes is not the man who says a thing did not happen."

**Both accounts are in the second column, in the order they were said, and neither was taken back and neither was asked a second question and no seventh account was asked for to agree with one of the two, and there are now eight accounts in that leaf that do not agree with each other and not one of the eight has been settled and nobody in this harbor is going to settle any of them.**

---

**A GASKET LET GO AT ABOUT TEN TO THREE IN THE AFTERNOON ON THE TWENTIETH OF OCTOBER IN ABOUT NINE FATHOMS AND A TENDER OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT PULLED A CHAIN WEIGHT OVER HIS OWN RAIL WITHOUT ASKING ANYBODY, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE CAME UP IN ABOUT SIX MINUTES WITH HIS LEFT SIDE ABOUT NINE FEET OFF, AND A GASKET AND A PATCH OF SHEET COPPER WENT ONTO THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET AT TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR PENCE, AND ONE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE OF WORKED GLASS BLANKS, TIN, A DECK PLATE AND STOVE PIPE WENT ON TO IT FOR A CHAMBER THAT IS A THING AND NOT A BUILDING, AND A SECOND HAND'S DAY AT THE COMPRESSOR WENT ON TO IT AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAD GONE OUT ON THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET BY THE CLOSE OF THE SAID DAY IS SIX THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE, BEING THIRTY-NINE MARKS, THREE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND NOBODY PUT THE ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE OF THE BERTH FUND ONTO IT, BECAUSE THAT IS A BERTH FUND AND IT IS NOT THIS.**

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twentieth day of October at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and entered by the ship's hand on that boat, with the harbor two days behind them: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the first descent of that trip was written on a slate before anybody went down, being a tender of about thirty-eight on the surface and not in the boat, an air limit of forty minutes written as a figure, an independent line of fifteen fathoms with a weight on the tail, a cutoff the tender could pull without asking, and a reason with the hour against it, being about one o'clock in the afternoon; and that a man was in the water about thirty-five minutes and on the bottom about eleven of them; and that a deck hand of about twenty-nine named Amos Trewin asked for the next descent and was given it; and that at about ten to three in the afternoon a gasket in the hatch of that bell let go in about nine fathoms and a chain weight came over a rail and a man was out in about six minutes and the bell itself came up about forty minutes later flooded to about the level of the sill and was pumped out through the night.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck two days' sail from that harbor, and no name is against it: that the reason a gasket and a patch of sheet copper are the eleventh line on that sheet and not the sixth is that a man came up on the twentieth of October who could not hear out of the left side of his head for four days, and that no figure is set against that in this harbor because a man on a mole cannot check what a man can hear, and that the cost of putting a thing back together so that it does not do it again is a figure and the cost of what it did is not one; and that the reason a tender's cutoff is a chain weight on his own quarter and not a word is in the tender's own mouth and is that a word is a thing a man can be talked out of at four o'clock in the afternoon and a chain weight is not, and that the reason was written down afterwards with the hour against it and that the reason was that water was coming in and the man was in it; and that the reason two accounts of one sound about forty minutes in about nine fathoms stand in that book disagreeing with each other is in the yard's own mouth and is that one man heard it and one man did not and that neither of them was asked a second question, and that a man who was in nine fathoms of water for eleven minutes is not the man who says a thing did not happen; and that the reason two accounts of the same sound are both entered rather than the better one is that there are eight accounts in that book now that do not agree with each other and that a leaf which settles one of them is a leaf that has stopped being checkable; and that the reason nobody in that party wrote the word injury next to a man who could not hear out of one side of his head is that the word is a verdict about a person and a verdict about a person is not on a fitting-out sheet and not in this yard's second column; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Wednesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
