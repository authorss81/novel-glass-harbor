# Chapter 464

*Tuesday the Twenty-Sixth Day of October, the Fifty-Third Wage Tuesday, a Wage Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest in a Front Room and Fifty-Three Weeks Standing Fallen Due With No Envelope Against Any of Them and the Column Not Moving, Eleven People Asked a Second Time in Eight New Sentences Written at the Head of a Page at About Ten in the Morning Before Anybody Was Asked and Read Out of the Page and Not Out of a Memory, Two Thousand and Forty-Six Pence Short Written at the Foot of That Page in Words and Not in Figures, One at a Time and Not in Front of Each Other, the Two Who Said No Not Asked and the Reason for That Printed on the Page in the Yard's Own Mouth, About Eleven People in That Loft Who Cannot Read What Is on the Wall of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of October, in a loft over a chandler's at the head of the third lay, and in that shed.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday in the sense of moving anything, and it was the fifty-third wage Tuesday. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and fifty-three weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and a wage Tuesday does not move that column and a further week stands fallen due at its close, and nobody in that harbor is to write one word under that figure saying anybody is going to pay it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and that column and that bag are two different things and neither of them has been touched since the third day of October past.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that loft and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-two days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-four days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a sixteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and two days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-eighth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-six days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and four days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-first day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-sixth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-third day.**

---

The page was written at about ten in the morning on the said Tuesday, before anybody was asked, and it was in the yard's own mouth, and it was eight sentences and not eleven, and it was read out of the page and not out of a memory, and here it is as it was read.

"This is not the same eleven sentences as the thirteenth of October. It is a second ask, on a day, of the same people, and it is in these words and not in the words of the first one, and both sets of words stand and neither has been taken back."

"The sheet that carries the money is short. It has been short since the twenty-third of October and it is short now by the figure written at the foot of this page in words and not in figures, and anybody in this room who cannot read that figure has just heard it said out loud, and that is the same as reading it."

"Nobody is asked to agree with this page, and nobody is asked to sign anything that is not their own line, and a person who puts nothing into this page loses nothing and is owed nothing by anybody in this harbor and is not on any other list."

"The two people who said no on the thirteenth and on the fourteenth are not asked again, by anybody, on this page or on any other, and the reason is printed here in these words, because the first ask said a person may say no and will not be asked a second time by anybody on any day, and this party keeps its own sentences."

"What is asked for here is not a wage and is not a subscription and is not a share and is not a debt. A person may put in two marks, or one mark, or nothing, and if a person puts in nothing then nothing is written against that person anywhere, and no list of the people who put in nothing is kept by anybody in this harbor, because a list of people who said no a second time is a weapon and this yard is not building one."

"If that sheet is still short when the boat is back, nobody in this harbor has been asked for the rest of it and is not going to be, and that is a line on that sheet, and a line is not a figure and is not a name."

"The four heads on the wall of the room over the fish market are not changed by anything on this page, and this page is not those four heads, and this page does not go on that wall, and this page is not the sheet that carries the money and is not kept with it."

"Your name goes at the foot of your own line in your own hand with the hour against it, and a person who cannot read may have the line read to them, and may say no to that as well."

**And the eleventh sentence of the first ask was put at the head of that page word for word before the other eight, which was done on purpose, and it is said here once and is not said again after this, and the reason it was put at the head is in the yard's own mouth and is that a promise about what a person may refuse is worth more at the top of a page than in the middle of one.**

---

There were about eleven people in that loft over a chandler's and about eleven of them could not read what was on the wall of it, and what was on that wall was the eleven sentences of the thirteenth of October in the hand of a man of about fifty who had said the name of that thing once and had not said it since.

They were asked one at a time and not in front of each other, and it took from about half past ten in the morning until about ten past four in the afternoon, and a person cannot be asked a question in a room with eleven people in it and answer it as though there were nobody else there.

**The master of about thirty-three of the second lay, who said no in a room over a fish market on the thirteenth at about a quarter to five in the afternoon in front of five people, was not in that loft on that day and was not asked, and nobody went to his door about it, and the woman of about thirty-one who crews a lighter, who said no in that shed on the fourteenth at about four in the afternoon in front of nine people, was not in that loft on that day and was not asked, and nobody went to her door about it either. That neither of them was asked a second time is a line on the sheet that carries the money, and it is a line and not a figure and not a name, and neither of those two people is named in that loft on that day and neither of them is represented by anybody.**

Nobody put a figure in on that Tuesday. Nine lines were opened against a second ask and every one of them was left empty that afternoon and the figures went in on the two days after, and the people who put nothing in were not written against anywhere, which is a rule and not an oversight, and which nobody in that loft was told had been arranged for them.

---

**AND AT THE FOOT OF THAT PAGE, IN WORDS AND NOT IN FIGURES, IT SAID THAT THE SHEET THAT CARRIES THE MONEY IS SHORT BY TWO THOUSAND AND FORTY-SIX PENCE, BEING TWELVE MARKS, TEN SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND A STRANGER WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL CAN TAKE IT OFF THE PAGE IN ABOUT A MINUTE AND FIND THAT IT AGREES.**

---

**THE FIFTY-THIRD WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND WENT AND THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE AND FIFTY-THREE WEEKS STAND FALLEN DUE WITH NO ENVELOPE AGAINST ANY OF THEM. ELEVEN PEOPLE WERE ASKED A SECOND TIME IN NEW WORDS WRITTEN AT THE HEAD OF A PAGE BEFORE ANYBODY WAS ASKED, ONE AT A TIME AND NOT IN FRONT OF EACH OTHER, AND NINE LINES WERE OPENED AGAINST THAT SECOND ASK AND NOT ONE FIGURE WENT IN ON THAT TUESDAY. THE TWO PEOPLE WHO SAID NO ON THE THIRTEENTH AND ON THE FOURTEENTH WERE NOT ASKED AGAIN BY ANYBODY, AND THE REASON FOR THAT IS PRINTED AT THE TOP OF A PAGE IN THAT HARBOR'S OWN MOUTH, AND IT IS A LINE ON THAT SHEET AND IT IS NOT A FIGURE AND IT IS NOT A NAME.**

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that it was the fifty-third wage Tuesday, and that the wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and that fifty-three weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and that the column did not move; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a page was written at about ten in the morning on the said day, before anybody was asked, being a second ask in eight sentences and the eleventh sentence of the first ask word for word at the head of it, and that it was read out of the page and not out of a memory; and that about eleven people were in a loft over a chandler's at the head of the third lay and about eleven of them could not read what was on the wall of it, being the eleven sentences of the thirteenth day of October; and that eleven people were asked one at a time and not in front of each other from about half past ten in the morning until about ten past four in the afternoon, and that nine lines were opened against that second ask and no figure went into any of them on that day; and that the master of about thirty-three of the second lay and the woman of about thirty-one who crews a lighter were not asked again, and that nobody went to the door of either of them, and that neither of them is named in that loft and neither of them is represented by anybody; and that the sheet that carries the money is short by two thousand and forty-six pence, being twelve marks, ten shillings and sixpence, and that that figure is written at the foot of that page in words and not in figures.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the second ask is in eight sentences and not eleven and in different words is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a person asked a second time in the same words has not been asked a second time, and that a first ask is a promise about what a person may refuse and a second ask is a different promise about a different day; and that the reason a promise about refusing is at the head of that page and not in the middle of it is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a page a person cannot read is read out by a voice and the voice starts at the top; and that the reason a shortfall is written in words on a page and is not printed in figures is in a woman of thirty's own mouth, and is that about eleven people in that loft cannot read a figure and can hear one, and that a figure printed on a page for the eyes of a person who is not in the room is a figure for somebody else; and that the reason a list of the people who put nothing in is not kept by anybody in that harbor is in the yard's own mouth, and is that such a list is a weapon and this yard is not building one, and that the rule is printed on the sheet and not only held in a head; and that the reason the two who said no were not in that loft on that day is in their own mouths and was said on the thirteenth and on the fourteenth, and neither of them is entered in that second ask in any words and is entered only in the line that says they were not asked again; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Tuesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
