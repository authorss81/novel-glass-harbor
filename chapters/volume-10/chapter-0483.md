# Chapter 483

*Monday the Fifteenth Day of November, a Rota Day, the Ninety-Ninth Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still off It, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, the Shortfall Unmoved at Nine Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Six Pence, Being Fifty-Eight Marks and Sixpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty, Three Slates Written at the Head of a Page Before Anybody Was Asked Anything and the Three of Them Asking the Same Thing in the Same Words, One of Them Asking for a Sound and One of Them for a Movement and One of Them for What Was in the Air, Each of the Three Asked on His Own and Not in Front of the Other Two, Each of the Three Saying Yes Out Loud in a Word of His Own and Not a Word Out of a Book, Each of the Three Told He May Stop It and Each Saying Out Loud Whether He Intends To, and Each of the Three Put on a Tether Before Anybody Asked Him Anything at All, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Writing the Page She Promised on the Eleventh of November on the Fifteenth in Her Own Hand With an Hour Against It in the First Column and Nobody Asking Her What That Floor Is and Nobody Thanking Her, a Man of About Thirty-Six Named Corwin Tace Setting More Marks on That Floor in the Morning and Off It by the Middle of That Day as He Said He Would Be, and the Three Nots Standing Against the Eighth of June Past Being a Nineteenth Week*

Monday the fifteenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-ninth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at nineteen thousand nine hundred and twenty-six pence out and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence in, and it is nine thousand two hundred and eighty-six pence short, being fifty-eight marks and sixpence. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, or out of the Crown.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty-one days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-three; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty-one days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and eighty-seventh night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and five days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixtieth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its seventy-fifth; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-second; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-three days. A ropemaker of about sixty-one in that harbor does not get her room back at any page of this trip. A figure of four hundred and fifty pence is still on a board at an end stall of that market with both dates on it and that figure has not come down and cannot be taken off. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**At about seven in the morning, before that boat had breakfast, the man who keeps the leaf took three slates off the hook and wrote one sentence at the head of a page, and then he wrote that same sentence at the head of all three of the slates in the same words, and he wrote it that way because the house rule is that eleven sentences are written at the head of a page before anybody is asked and the three of these were going to be asked in three.**

Then each slate carried one more line under the sentence, and the three more lines were not alike.

**Is a person told, before anybody is asked anything, that they may go down into that room on the seventeenth, one at a time, under a slate, with a named tender on the surface and a figure of forty minutes of air and a cutoff that tender can pull without asking, and come up with one thing they can carry in their own hands, and say it out loud on that deck, and give it up when it has been said?**

The one who asked for a sound put on the second line: *I would like to carry one sound out of that room and I will not be able to say afterwards whether I heard it in there or have heard it before.*

The one who asked for a movement put on the second line: *I would like to carry one movement of one thing out of that room and I will not be able to say afterwards whether it moved while I was looking at it or moved before.*

The one who asked for what was in the air put on the second line: *I would like to carry the air in that room out of it and I will not be able to say afterwards whether it was in that room when I stood in it or whether it is in this room now.*

---

**And then, at about ten past eight in the morning, before anybody had said a word to any one of the three, the man who keeps the leaf put a short line on each of them, a line off a belt at the waist to a ring on the outside of that bell, and belayed it, and said out loud that it was a rope and not a person.**

"You are all three going to have this on you and I am putting it on you now and before I have asked any of you anything, because if I ask you first and then put it on you then the rope is part of the asking. It is not. It is thirty feet of three-inch line and a ring and a belay and it goes over the after side of that bell and up to a man on the surface with a hand on a chain weight. **Nobody is going to be holding the other end of this. It is a rope off a boat and it is not a person and it is not a man of thirty-four of the second lay with his own arm in it, and if any one of you has been told otherwise by anybody on that deck you are to say so now.**"

Nobody had been told otherwise. The man of about thirty-four of the second lay said four words about it, which were that he had not been asked and had not offered and would not be asked and would not offer, and that is the whole of what he said about it.

---

**And then the three were asked, one at a time, in the cabin, with the door shut, and not in front of the other two, and each of them was told before they were asked anything at all that they could stop it whenever they liked and that nobody would ask them why, and each of them was asked what he intended to do about stopping it, and each of them answered that out loud before he answered anything else.**

The man of about thirty-four, of the second lay, was asked first.

"I intend not to stop it, and I will tell you what I am carrying before I tell you that, because you are going to make me say it in a slate first and that is right and I would have wanted it that way." He put two fingers on the line at his own waist. "**Aye, then. I will take one sound out of that room.** Not the one I expect. There is a thing in that room I have been hearing in my own head for four days and I do not know where I heard it and I would rather bring up a sound than bring up that."

The man of about twenty-nine was asked second, and his left side is the side that cannot hear and he turned his good ear to the man who asked him, and nobody said anything about that.

"I intend not to stop it." He said it the way he said a figure on a slate. "**Yes. I will take one movement.** And I want the man who keeps the leaf to write down that I cannot hear out of the left side of my head, because I have not written it on a slate in six weeks and today I am going down in a room where a thing might be making a noise and the only ear I have got is the one I am going to listen with, and I would rather that was on a page than in a man's memory."

It went on the slate and the man who keeps the leaf read it back to him and he did not ask to read it and did not ask for it to be taken out.

The woman of about thirty-one was asked third, on the after deck with her back to the water and nobody within about nine feet of her.

"I intend not to stop it, and I am not going to say it in a small voice because you have written it on a slate in big letters and I would be insulting the slate." She waited until he had the pencil down. "**Yes, I will take what was in the air in there.** And I want to say one thing into your second column while you are here, and I want it in your words and not mine, and it is that I counted the cups of those fifteen frames from a ladder on the fifth of November and I said out loud that the number was on the face of that floor and a man on a mole could have it off a plan. I am going to be wrong about that on Wednesday, and I am going to be wrong in a way no plan can be checked against, and I want the page to say that I knew it on the fifteenth and went anyway."

Nobody thanked any of the three. Nobody said the word brave. No figure is set against any of them.

---

**At about two in the afternoon, on that after deck, the woman of thirty of no office wrote a page in her own hand and put the hour against it and gave it to the man who keeps the leaf and he put it in the first column where a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take it off, and nobody on that boat asked her what that floor is and nobody thanked her for the page and she is not thanked.**

The page carries one sentence.

*I, Mara Venn, of thirty, of no office, on the fifteenth day of November, at about two in the afternoon, on a boat of about nineteen tons over a trench floor in about twenty-two fathoms: if somebody asks me what that floor is, I am going to say that I do not know.*

**And she said out loud, once, that that is the first sentence of that kind she has written in eleven years, and she did not say it again on that day and nobody asked her to say it again.**

---

**And a man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down went down at about ten past ten in the morning on that ebb under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that the wet comes about a foot into that room on every tide and the front of the fourth rack is eleven inches off the floor and paper that stands in a room like that for a very long time is not paper that can be left standing in a room like that for one more tide, with a figure of forty minutes of air and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty of them and came up at about eleven o'clock with two bundles under his arm and a slate of his own in his hand with a figure of about nine sheets in it, and no figure of that is set against him and nobody thanked him.**

**And a man of about thirty-six named Corwin Tace set more marks on that floor in the morning of that Monday, and was off it by the middle of that day, as he said on the eleventh he would be, and he did not say one word about it to anybody and nobody asked him why and he was given nothing.**

---

**THREE PEOPLE WERE ASKED ON THAT MONDAY, ONE AT A TIME AND NOT IN FRONT OF THE OTHER TWO, FOR ONE THING EACH THAT THEY COULD CARRY, AND ALL THREE SAID YES OUT LOUD IN A WORD OF THEIR OWN, AND ALL THREE WERE TOLD THEY MIGHT STOP IT AND ALL THREE SAID OUT LOUD BEFORE THEY SAID ANYTHING ELSE THAT THEY DID NOT INTEND TO, AND ALL THREE WERE PUT ON A TETHER OF THIRTY FEET OF LINE OFF A RING ON THAT BELL BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED ANY OF THEM ANYTHING AT ALL, AND IT WAS SAID OUT LOUD THAT IT WAS A ROPE AND NOT A PERSON. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE WROTE ONE SENTENCE IN HER OWN HAND AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND PUT THE HOUR AGAINST IT, AND IT IS IN THE FIRST COLUMN, AND NOBODY ASKED HER WHAT THAT FLOOR IS AND NOBODY THANKED HER FOR IT. NOTHING WENT OUT ON ANY LINE OF THAT SHEET IN ANY HAND ON THAT DAY. AND THAT SHEET IS NINE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING FIFTY-EIGHT MARKS AND SIXPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the fifteenth day of November at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-ninth time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that at about seven in the morning three slates were written with one sentence at the head of a page and that same sentence written at the head of all three of them in the same words before anybody was asked anything, being that a person is told, before anybody is asked anything, that they may go down into that room on the seventeenth, one at a time, under a slate, with a named tender on the surface and a figure of forty minutes of air and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and come up with one thing they can carry in their own hands, and say it out loud on that deck, and give it up when it has been said; and that one of the three asked for a sound, one of them for a movement and one of them for what was in the air, and that the second line on each slate is in the words of the man who wrote it; and that at about ten past eight in the morning, before anybody had said a word to any one of the three, the man who keeps the leaf put a short line off a belt at the waist to a ring on the outside of that bell on each of them and belayed it, and said out loud that it was thirty feet of three-inch line and a ring and a belay and that nobody is going to be holding the other end of it, and that it is not a man and it is not a person, and that if any of the three has been told otherwise by anybody on that deck he is to say so now, and that nobody had been told otherwise and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay said four words, being that he had not been asked and had not offered and would not be asked and would not offer; and that the three were asked one at a time with the door shut and not in front of the other two, and that each of them was told before he was asked anything at all that he could stop it whenever he liked and that nobody would ask him why, and that each of them was asked what he intended to do about stopping it and answered that out loud before he answered anything else; and that the man of about thirty-four of the second lay said that he intended not to stop it and said that he would take one sound out of that room, not the one he expected, and that there is a thing in that room he has been hearing in his own head for four days and he does not know where he heard it; and that the man of about twenty-nine said that he intended not to stop it and said that he would take one movement, and asked that it be written on a slate that he cannot hear out of the left side of his head and that the only ear he has is the one he is going to listen with, and that it was written and read back to him and he did not ask to read it and did not ask for it to be taken out; and that the woman of about thirty-one said that she intended not to stop it and said that she would take what was in the air in there, and said out loud that she counted the cups of those fifteen frames from a ladder on the fifth of November and said then that the number was on the face of that floor and could be had off a plan, and that she is going to be wrong about that on the seventeenth in a way no plan can be checked against, and asked that the page say she knew it on the fifteenth and went anyway, and that the page says so; and that nobody thanked any of the three and nobody said the word brave to any of them and no figure is set against any of them; and that a woman of thirty of no office wrote a page in her own hand at about two in the afternoon with the hour against it, carrying one sentence, being that if somebody asks her what that floor is she is going to say that she does not know, and that it went into the first column and nobody on that boat asked her what that floor is and nobody thanked her for the page; and that she said out loud once that that is the first sentence of that kind she has written in eleven years and did not say it again; and that a man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down went down at about ten past ten in the morning on that ebb under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that the wet comes about a foot into that room on every tide and the front of the fourth rack stands eleven inches off the floor, with a figure of forty minutes of air and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty of them and came up at about eleven o'clock with two bundles under his arm and a slate of his own with a figure of about nine sheets on it, and that no figure of that is set against him and nobody thanked him; and that a man of about thirty-six named Corwin Tace set more marks on that floor in the morning and was off it by the middle of that day, as he said on the eleventh he would be, and said no word about it to anybody and was given nothing and nobody asked him why.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason three slates were written with the same sentence at the head of all of them before anybody was asked anything is the yard's own mouth, and is that a question asked in three different words to three different men is not one question asked three times, it is three questions, and that the only thing that can be compared afterwards is the sentence the three of them were asked; and that the reason the tethers went on before the asking is the man who keeps the leaf's own, and is that a rope put on a man after he has been asked is part of the asking whether he knows it or not, and that a man who has a rope on him has to be able to point at it and say what it is, and that thirty feet of line off a ring on a bell is a thing he can point at and say what it is; and that the reason the man of about thirty-four of the second lay said four words about that rope is his own, and is that the whole of what he has asked for on this trip is that nothing on this boat is held together by a person who has not been asked, and that a rope off a ring is the whole of what is left of that when everything else has been taken off it; and that the reason the man of about twenty-nine asked for his hearing to be written on a slate is his own, and is that a man who knows a thing about himself and says it out loud in front of nine people has made it a fact that nine people can act on, and that a thing a man has only ever said once to his own self is a thing nine people have to remember on his behalf and they will get it wrong; and that the reason the woman of about thirty-one asked for the page to record that she was going to be wrong in a way no plan can be checked against is her own, and is that a person who says out loud on a deck that she is going to be wrong, and says it two days before, has bought the only thing she cannot buy back, which is the fact that she knew; and that the reason the sentence of the woman of thirty of no office went into the first column and not the second is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a sentence in a hand with an hour against it is a thing a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can take off a page and see, and that a statement of what a person is going to say if she is asked is the one kind of thing in this trip that is better in a column anybody can check; and that the reason nobody said the word brave to any of the three is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a word like that is what a room says about a person and not what a deck says, and that there is a room four hundred yards from that deck with about eleven people in it who cannot read and that none of them has been told anything; and that the reason not one item of that harbor's standing condition was settled, advanced, rubbed off or written down as done on the said Monday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a rota day is the only day of that week on which anybody in that market writes a figure at all, and that a ropemaker of about sixty-one in that harbor does not get her room back on a Monday either, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it and has been off it longer than the paper in that room has been standing.*
