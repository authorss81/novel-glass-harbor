# Chapter 451

*Wednesday the Thirteenth Day of October, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came, Eleven People Asked One at a Time in the Same Eleven Sentences Written at the Head of a Page Before Anybody Was Asked and Read Out of the Page and Not Out of a Memory, Nine of Them at Four Marks Each and Two of Them at One Mark Each, Two of the Eleven Saying No With a Reason in Their Own Mouths in Front of Five People and Not Being Asked Again on Any of the Nine Days After It, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Out Loud That a Sheet of Money Is Not a Sheet of Rules and the Two Not Being Put in One Room Without Both of Them Being Named, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the thirteenth day of October, in that shed, in a room over a fish market, and at the head of that third lay.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down at all. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon for a day asked for by name, being a ship's joiner of about thirty-four who has mended a frame in that yard's own boat, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of what is said in this chapter comes out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that room over a fish market and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name stood on a public sheet and on a sheet of four heads and nobody took it off either and nobody thanked her and nobody offered to. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he said nothing on the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-nine days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-one days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and the bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line who was paid on the flat of that bench stone on the twelfth is not in that harbor and is not asked one question about him. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fourteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column with a name beside it that is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-nine days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-fifth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-three days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-one days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-eighth day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-third day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixtieth day.**

---

The room over the fish market had a long table in it, a stove that does not draw, and about nineteen people in it at about ten in the morning on the thirteenth of October, and the sheet of four heads was on the wall with nothing written above it and had been on that wall three days.

A man of about fifty, who had said the name of that thing once and written it at the head of it himself and had not said it since, stood at the middle of that table and read out eleven sentences.

They were on a sheet of good paper and they were in his own hand and they were read out of the page and not out of a memory, and they were the same eleven sentences every time they were used that day and the next day and the day after, and here they are as they were read.

"There is a trip going to a shelf under the drowned city, and about nine men are going, and it is not a survey and it is not a recovery and nobody in this harbor has a chart of it."

"It will be two days down that coast road and two days back, and the road itself is four days to the town at the end of it, and there is coal on that shelf, and there is no coal in this harbor."

"It will cost more than any of us has, and it will be paid out of this sheet and not out of the berth fund and not out of the Crown and not out of anybody in this room who has not written a figure against their own name on this page."

"Nobody in this room decides anything, and this sheet does not decide anything, and there is no office anywhere in it and no head of it and no seat of it and nothing in it that anybody can be appealed to."

"If it goes and finds nothing, that is the end of it, and the sheet is shut, and nobody is owed anything by anybody."

"If it goes and finds something, then what is found comes up with the name of the man who found it and the hour he found it on, and the refusals go on the same page at the same size as the rest of it, and a refusal that anybody gave about this trip is on that page whether or not they put money in."

"Nobody is asked to put in more than they have said they will put in, and a person may say no and give a reason, and will not be asked a second time by anybody, on any day, about this."

"Nobody goes who does not want to go, and nobody is sent, and a man who says he will go is paid at a figure written here and not at a figure somebody says out loud afterwards in a shed."

"The four heads on the wall of this room are not changed by anybody putting money into a sheet, and this sheet is not the four heads, and it is not kept with them, and it does not go on that wall."

"If a person in this room is asked a second time by anybody about this trip, on any day, they may stand up in this room and say so, and it will be written down with the hour."

"Your name goes at the foot of your own line in your own hand with the hour against it, and a person who cannot read may have the line read to them, and may say no to that as well."

---

They were asked one at a time and not in front of each other, which took until about half past four, because a person cannot be asked a question in a room with nineteen people in it and answer it as though there were nobody else there.

Nine of them wrote their names against a line and two of them wrote their names against a line, and nobody wrote a figure on the Wednesday, and the figures went on against those nine and two names on the Thursday morning when there was a sheet to put them on. Three of the nineteen did not write a name on the day. **Six of the eleven said out loud what they thought the money was for before they wrote their names, and two of them said a thing that a man of about thirty-four took down and did not read out to anybody.**

**A master of about thirty-three of the second lay said no at about a quarter to five in the afternoon, in front of five people, in his own mouth, and he gave a reason and the reason was that he has a boat of about seventeen tons with a mortgage on her that stands until the spring and four men who have not been paid since the summer, and that he is not putting four men's winter into a thing that nobody in that room can check, and that he would rather say it standing here than have somebody come to his door about it on the Tuesday.** He was not asked again on any of the nine days after it. He went out of that room at about a quarter to five and got his day's work and it is in the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the reason against the line, and no name is against it.

**And the eleventh sentence was in that room at about half past four, and the woman of thirty of no office read it out to a man of about thirty-eight who cannot read and who said he would rather have it read than have it read and did not say which, and she read it to him twice, and he wrote a mark against his own line in the end, and that mark is not a name and is not a figure and is in his own hand.** She did it because the eleventh sentence says she may say no to that as well and not because anybody asked her to, and she is of no office, and her name is on two public sheets, and nobody thanked her for standing in that room doing that either.

---

**At about ten past five in the afternoon, before anybody else had gone, Mara Venn said the one thing she said about it, and she said it to the room and not to him.**

"A sheet of money is not a sheet of rules," she said, "and I want both of those said out loud in the same breath, every time, for as long as there are two of them. Because a sheet of rules is a thing people gave each other and a sheet of money is a thing people gave each other, and a harbor that cannot tell the difference between the two is going to be told what to do by whichever of them is written in the larger hand, and both of them are going to be in this room for a month."

A man of about forty-six who mends nets on the third lay said that this was common sense. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay said that it was a rule and that a rule is a thing that can be argued with, and she said that this one could not, and he said he knew that, and that was the whole of what he said. Ollum Trevask said that the two sheets were not going to be kept in the same drawer either, and that if anybody put them in the same drawer he would say so out loud in front of whoever opened it.

Nessa Pike wrote it into a minute on a sheet of good paper that cost a penny and that came out of the holder's own coat, and the minute says that the two sheets were named separately in that room on the thirteenth of October, and it does not say what the trip is, because the trip had not been named in that room yet and was not going to be named in that room until a man got up and said it.

And a man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book got up at about ten to six in the evening, after nineteen people had gone, and said the name of the place, and nobody in that room had heard it said in that room before.

"A shelf under the drowned city," he said. "Below the causeways. There is a trench in it and there was a hull stored in that trench, and I have got a chart in a bag with a bit of that coast on it that I have never shown anybody, and I am not going to show it to anybody, and that is all I am going to say about it and I would say that at four in the morning as well as at ten to six in the evening."

Nobody asked him for the chart. Nobody said they would come and look at it. It went into a minute that says a person in that room said there was a chart in a bag and did not produce it and was not asked for it.

---

**THE TRIP ON THE FACE OF THAT PAGE COSTS MORE THAN THAT HARBOR HAS GOT IN IT AND THE FIGURE OF WHAT CAME IN ON THE FOURTEENTH IS NINE FIGURES AT FOUR MARKS EACH BEING FIVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTY PENCE AND TWO FIGURES AT ONE MARK EACH BEING THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE, AND NONE OF IT IS IN THE BAG, WHICH STANDS AT TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. A MASTER OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE OF THE SECOND LAY SAID NO AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO FIVE IN THE AFTERNOON IN FRONT OF FIVE PEOPLE AND GAVE A REASON ABOUT A BOAT OF ABOUT SEVENTEEN TONS AND A MORTGAGE AND FOUR MEN WHO HAVE NOT BEEN PAID SINCE THE SUMMER, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED AGAIN ON ANY OF THE NINE DAYS AFTER IT. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT OTHER WALL ARE NOT CHANGED BY ANY OF IT AND THE TWO SHEETS ARE NEVER IN ONE CLAUSE, ONE COLUMN, ONE ROOM OR ONE MOUTH WITHOUT BOTH OF THEM BEING NAMED. A PERSON IN THAT ROOM SAID THERE WAS A CHART IN A BAG AND DID NOT PRODUCE IT AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT, AND HIS NAME IS NOT ON ANY PAGE OF THIS HARBOR AND NEITHER IS THE NAME BESIDE THE BOY'S HAND AT THE HEAD OF THE THIRD COLUMN. AND THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET AND NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR AND A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS ON A BOARD IN THAT FRONT STREET AND DID NOT COME DOWN.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the thirteenth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind the reeve's bench did not come down at all; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a trip to a shelf under the drowned city was named in a room over a fish market at about ten to six in the evening on the said day by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay, out of his own mouth, after nineteen people had gone; and that eleven people were asked one at a time in the same eleven sentences, written at the head of a page before anybody was asked and read out of the page and not out of a memory, with a name and an hour against every line, and that nine of them wrote their names against a line and two of them wrote their names against a line, and that no figure was written against any name on the said day and that the figures went on against nine of those names and two of them on the morrow; and that six of the eleven said out loud what they thought the money was for before they wrote their names and that two of them said a thing that a man of about thirty-four took down and did not read out to anybody; and that a master of about thirty-three of the second lay said no at about a quarter to five in the afternoon in front of five people and gave a reason in his own mouth and was not asked again on any of the nine days after it; and that a woman of thirty of no office read the eleventh of those sentences twice to a man of about thirty-eight who cannot read before he put a mark against his own line, and that mark is not a name and is not a figure and is in his own hand; and that the two sheets were named separately in that room at about ten past five in the afternoon and are not to be put in one clause, one column, one room or one mouth without both of them being named; and that a person in that room said there was a chart in a bag with a bit of that coast on it and did not produce it and was not asked for it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason eleven people were asked one at a time and not in front of each other is in the holder's own mouth and is that a person cannot be asked a question in a room with nineteen people in it and answer it as though there were nobody else there, and that a figure written against a line in a man's own hand with the hour against it is the only figure in this harbor that anybody can take with a pencil; and that the reason the eleventh sentence says a person who cannot read may have the line read to them and may say no to that as well is in a woman of thirty's own mouth, and that she did it because the sentence gave her the right to and not because anybody asked her to, and that she is of no office and is not thanked for it; and that the reason a man of about thirty-three said no in front of five people and not in a doorway afterwards is in his own mouth and is that he would rather say it standing there than have somebody come to his door about it on the Tuesday; and that the reason six of the nine said out loud what the money was for before they wrote their names is in the yard's own mouth and is that a figure written against a name in a man's own hand with the hour against it is a promise about a thing and not a wish about a thing, and that a man who cannot say what four marks is for cannot be asked to find out in nine fathoms of water; and that the reason no figure of the whole of it is against the trip on that page is that the trip has not been costed yet and that a cost is put on a sheet when somebody has said out loud what it is for, and that this yard is not going to put a figure on a thing in front of people who have not been asked about it; and that the reason the fitting-out sheet is not on the wall in that room and does not go on that wall is in a woman of thirty's own mouth and is that a sheet of rules and a sheet of money are two things and a harbor that cannot tell them apart is a harbor that will be told what to do by whichever of them is written in the larger hand; and that the reason a man of about thirty-four said he had a chart in a bag and was not asked for it is that a person who has not asked to be represented is not represented, and that this is the first standing line on the other sheet and it holds on a chart in a bag as well as on a route; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Wednesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
