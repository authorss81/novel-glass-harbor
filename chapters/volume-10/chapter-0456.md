# Chapter 456

*Monday the Eighteenth Day of October, a Rota Day, and the Ninety-First Time That Cistern Was Charged and Walked on by None of It, the Second Day of a Run Down a Coast Road That Is Four Days Each Way in a Boat of About Nineteen Tons with Nine Men and a Master Who Is Going Home on the Twenty-Second Whether He Is Paid the Rest of It or Not, a Harbor Authority on That Coast Road Whose Coal and Water Have Been Paid For in Advance for a Fortnight by a Man Who Gave the Name of an Office and Not the Name of a Person, a Reeve of About Fifty-Nine Who Does Not Know Who Paid and Says So and Whose Book Has an Entry With No Figure on It, a Man Who Works in That Harbor Who Will Not Sell What He Knows and Gives a Reason, One Thousand One Hundred and Eighty Pence of Coal for Two Days Off a Supply Boat Outside That Harbor, Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence Coming Into That Yard and Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the eighteenth day of October, on a coast road, in a boat of about nineteen tons, and at the back of that market in a harbor one day behind them.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-first time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. Three hundred and thirty-six pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being two days at the figure in chalk, and three hundred and thirty-six pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being two days asked for by name, being a shipwright's joiner of about thirty-four and a hand on a compressor at the figure in chalk, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the coal came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the holder of that writ was not in that harbor on the said day and the far bench was occupied from ten until four in a harbor one day behind them and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he took the eleventh piece up and set it down about nine inches away from where it had been. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-four days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty-six days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are the first day of a fifteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-four days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixtieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-eight days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety-six days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-third day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-eighth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the sixty-fifth day.**

---

The run down that coast is two days to where this party is going and four days to the town at the end of the road, and they were on the second of the two, and a boat of about nineteen tons does not sail a coast road at night, and the master of her, Simeon Roke, was in the habit of saying what he was going to do for the morning at the end of the evening before, in front of his two men and the boy, in a flat voice, and he did it that night and every night of that run.

They came into a harbor on that coast road at about two in the afternoon and went alongside a stone with nothing on it, and there was a reeve of about fifty-nine on that stone within about ten minutes, which nobody in that boat had expected, and he came out to them rather than sending anybody, and the first thing he said was that he was not going to touch their gear.

**"I have had a fortnight of my coal and my water paid for by somebody," he said. "Twice. The first was the twelfth of October and the second was the fourteenth, and both went into a book at a counter in an office in a city and both came out here as an entry with a name on it that is a name of an office, and I have sent up a letter asking who it is and I have had no answer and I am not going to pretend to you that I know."**

"Who paid it."

"I do not know, and I want that written down in whatever you are writing in, because in about three weeks somebody is going to come down this quay and ask me whether I gave harbour to a boat, and I will want to be able to say that I asked." He looked at the boat and not at the people in her. "I have had nine years of a harbor where the answer to that question is no, and I am not going to start it on a Tuesday in October because a book in a city told me to."

**He wrote the reeve's own answer into the minute that was being kept on that trip, in his own hand, with the hour against it, and Nessa Pike read it back to him before she closed the minute and he did not ask to read it himself and did not ask to change a word of it.**

---

**A man of about thirty-eight who works in that harbor in a yard and has a boat of about twelve tons of his own was asked, in a doorway, by a woman of thirty of no office, what he knew about the shelf below the drowned city, and he said that he knew a thing and would not sell it, and gave a reason, and the reason was that he had been the second man on a boat out of that harbor in a summer four years ago with three men out of a coat on it, and that he went where he was told for nine days and came back, and that the one thing he has said out loud about it since is that he was the second man and he knows it, and that he is not going to hand that to a stranger for the price of a barrel of oil.**

He was not asked a second time by anybody in that party. The sheet of about four feet was not shown to him. He was not asked whether the party were from Morrow Quay, and he did not say so, and the party did not say so in that doorway.

And the coal was bought at about half past four in the afternoon on the said day off a supply boat lying outside that harbor, at one thousand one hundred and eighty pence for two days, which is twice what coal is in that harbor, and the man who keeps the leaf said the figure out loud to the nine men in that boat and nobody argued with him about it, and the reason is that coal is the only thing on that list that is a price and not a material, and a price is the figure a person says out loud, and this one was said out loud.

---

**A HARBOR AUTHORITY ON THAT COAST ROAD HAD ITS COAL AND ITS WATER PAID FOR TWICE IN A FORTNIGHT BY SOMEBODY WHO GAVE THE NAME OF AN OFFICE AND NOT THE NAME OF A PERSON, AND ITS REEVE OF ABOUT FIFTY-NINE DID NOT KNOW WHO PAID AND SAID SO IN HIS OWN MOUTH AND ASKED FOR IT TO BE WRITTEN DOWN IN A MINUTE THAT WAS READ BACK TO HIM BEFORE IT WAS SHUT, AND HE DID NOT ASK TO READ IT HIMSELF AND DID NOT ASK TO CHANGE A WORD OF IT. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT IN THAT HARBOR WOULD NOT SELL WHAT HE KNOWS AND GAVE A REASON THAT NAMES THREE MEN OUT OF A COAT AND NINE DAYS AND THE FACT THAT HE WAS THE SECOND MAN, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED A SECOND TIME BY ANYBODY IN THAT PARTY AND WAS NOT SHOWN THE SHEET OF ABOUT FOUR FEET. COAL FOR THE PUMP FOR TWO DAYS WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY PENCE, BEING TWICE WHAT COAL IS AT HOME, AND THE FIGURE WAS SAID OUT LOUD TO NINE MEN IN A BOAT AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH IT. THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT WAS NOT IN THAT HARBOR ON THE SAID DAY AND THE BAG WENT ON CARRYING HIS WAGE WITHOUT HIM, BEING THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE IN AND THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE OUT, AND THE REASON FOR THAT IS IN THE YARD'S OWN MOUTH AND IS THAT A BERTH FUND PAYS A DAY FOR A DAY.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the eighteenth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and entered in the same shed by the ship's hand on the said day, in a harbor one day behind the party: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-first time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that three hundred and thirty-six pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and three hundred and thirty-six pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus three hundred and thirty-six less three hundred and thirty-six is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the holder of that writ was not in that harbor on the said day; and that coal for the pump for two days was bought off a supply boat outside a harbor on that coast road at about half past four in the afternoon at one thousand one hundred and eighty pence; and that a reeve of about fifty-nine in that harbor said out loud that he did not know who had paid his coal and his water twice in a fortnight and asked for it to be written down in a minute, and that the minute was read back to him and was shut.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed one day behind the party, and no name is against it: that the reason a berth fund paid three hundred and thirty-six pence into that yard and three hundred and thirty-six pence out of it on a day when the man who keeps the leaf was two days down a coast road is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a berth fund pays a day for a day and does not care which coast the day happened on, and that the two men at that berth are not the man who keeps the leaf and are paid at the same figure, and that this is exactly the kind of thing that cannot be checked from a mole, which is why it is in this column and not the first; and that the reason a reeve of about fifty-nine came out onto a stone himself rather than sending anybody is in his own mouth and is that he is not going to pretend to a stranger that he knows; and that the reason a man of about thirty-eight in that harbor would not sell what he knows is his own and names three men out of a coat and nine days and the fact that he was the second man, and that this yard is not going to ask him a second time and is not going to show him a sheet of about four feet, and that a thing a man has not been asked about twice is a thing that does not get into a minute with his name on it; and that the reason a price is the only line on that fitting-out sheet that is called a price out loud is in the holder's own mouth, and that coal is a price and not a material, and a material is bought at the figure somebody says to somebody else, and that the one thousand one hundred and eighty pence was said out loud to nine men in a boat and nobody argued with it because a price that has been said out loud is a price and a price that has not is a rumour; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
