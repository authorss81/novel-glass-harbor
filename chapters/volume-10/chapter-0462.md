# Chapter 462

*Sunday the Twenty-Fourth Day of October, a Wipe, the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Coming Down Bare for the Seventy-Seventh Time at About Eight in the Morning in a Harbor the Party Was Not In, a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Sailing In on the Evening Tide at About Half Past Seven and Going Alongside the Dolphin at the End of the Second Lay With Nobody Met and Nobody Thanked, a Man of About Thirty-One Who Crews Nothing and Sells Nothing and Hired On by the Day Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Standing on That Dolphin and Not Being Asked Anything, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Who Keeps the Whole Fouling Ladder in That Harbor in His Own Book Asking One Question on a Slip at About Ten Past Eight in the Evening and Not Getting an Answer, a Second Hand's Day at the Compressor Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Sunday the twenty-fourth day of October, at the head of that slip and alongside the dolphin at the end of the second lay, and at the back of that market four hundred yards off.

**The said Sunday was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday. The wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare at about eight in the morning for the seventy-seventh time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was in that harbor from the evening tide of the said day and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name is on two public sheets and nobody took it off either. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and got up at four and went home, which he has done six times in ninety-six days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-two days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and the bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is not in that harbor and is not asked about him. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fifteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-sixth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-four days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and two days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-ninth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-fourth day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventy-first day. A man of about twenty-nine in that harbor could not hear out of the left side of his head on the twentieth, on the twenty-first, on the twenty-second, on the twenty-third, on the twenty-fourth or on the twenty-fifth, and has not been asked about it and did not ask and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

They came in on the evening tide and made the roads at about five in the afternoon and lay off the point for two hours because the wind was in the north-west and the master of that boat would not take her in on it, and nobody in that party argued with him about it and nobody in that harbor was told about the two hours.

She went alongside the dolphin at the end of the second lay at about half past seven in the evening with the last of the light going off the water, and the mooring was made by a boy of about seventeen and nobody met them and nobody came down that dolphin to take a line and nobody said one word to that master about the six days he had been paid.

**Nessa Pike said out loud on that dolphin at about a quarter to eight in the evening that nobody had thanked him for carrying nine of them up that coast on no figure, and that she was not going to be the one to do it now and get it in a minute, and that it was on the sheet as a line and the line said he had not been thanked and that was the whole of what a line is for.**

---

At the head of that slip at about ten past eight in the evening, the man who keeps the leaf went up on the stone to the counter where the figure in chalk is kept, and the woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall and a slate was on that stone and told him, without being asked and without making a fuss out of it, that the berth fund had carried his day at one hundred and sixty-eight pence in and one hundred and sixty-eight out at the same figure at the same hours, and that she had looked at the book twice on the Friday because it looked wrong and it was not wrong, and that it was the same book it has been since the third of October and that nobody in that harbor had asked her about it and she was not going to.

"That is all I have got for you," she said. "It is not a welcome and I am not going to be one. You were two days out and the day came in the same as any other and it will go on coming in the same when you are not here, and if you want a harbor to be glad about, that is a different counter and there is not one."

He wrote it in the yard's own mouth in the second column with the hour against it and no name against that, and he did not say thank you to her either, and she said that she had noticed.

---

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that slip at about ten past eight in the evening and had been there since about half past six, and he had not asked anybody anything in two hours, and he said one thing to that party and it was a question, and it was not answered on that slip and it was not answered that night.

Ollum Trevask kept the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book and had never shown it to anybody and was not on the sheet of four heads and was not asked for it, and he had named the shelf and the trench and the hull in a room over a fish market on the thirteenth of October at about ten to six in the evening, out of his own mouth, after nineteen people had gone.

"You put a paper against that mouth on the Friday," he said, and he did not raise his voice, and nobody else on that slip heard it. "I have been thinking about that for nine days and I have got to the end of what I can think about it and I cannot get past this. How do you know it was the paper."

He did not say it to be caught. He said it because he had carried it up two days and had not been able to put it down anywhere else, and when he had said it he put his hands in his pockets and stood there and waited, and the man who keeps the leaf did not answer him on that slip, and the man of about thirty-four did not ask him a second time, and that is the whole of what happened between them and it is in the second column of that leaf with the hour against it and no name against it.

---

A woman of about thirty-one was standing on that dolphin at about a quarter to eight in the evening with a bag and a coil and nothing else, and she crews nothing and sells nothing and is out of a coast town four days down that road, and she had been on the first run of that trip as a tender's hand and a second pair of hands in a bell and had not been in that harbor when eleven people were asked on the thirteenth of October, because she had not been in that harbor.

Nobody asked her anything on that slip. The man who keeps the leaf looked at her and said that there was a day apiece going to four men on the Monday and that she was one of the four, and she said that she knew that, and that was all the conversation there was.

She is the only other woman on that trip besides a woman of thirty of no office, and she is not the woman of about thirty-one who crews a lighter and said no in that shed on the fourteenth, and the two of them are not to be counted together by anybody in that harbor.

---

**AND A SECOND HAND'S DAY AT THE COMPRESSOR WENT ONTO THE FITTING-OUT SHEET ON THAT SUNDAY AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, BEING ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, PAID IN COIN ON THE STONE AT THE HEAD OF THAT SLIP TO A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN WHO HAD ASKED FOR IT BY NAME ON THE FRIDAY, AND NOBODY THANKED HIM EITHER, AND THAT SHEET IS SHORT BY ONE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR PENCE, BEING EIGHT MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

**A BOAT CAME IN ON THE EVENING TIDE AND NOBODY MET IT. A BERTH FUND PAID A DAY FOR A DAY AND DID NOT MAKE A FUSS ABOUT IT AND DID NOT NEED TO. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR ASKED ONE QUESTION AT THE HEAD OF A SLIP AND WAS NOT ANSWED AND DID NOT ASK AGAIN, AND THAT QUESTION IS STILL OPEN AND IS IN NO OTHER PLACE. THE HARBOR IS POORER ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH DAY OF OCTOBER THAN IT WAS ON THE THIRTEENTH OF IT, AND THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET AND NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR AND A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening on that stone at the head of that slip: that the said day was a wipe, being the seventy-seventh time the wall behind a reeve's bench came down bare, and it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and it went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a boat of about nineteen tons made the roads at about five in the afternoon, lay off the point for two hours because the wind was in the north-west, and went alongside the dolphin at the end of the second lay at about half past seven in the evening, and that nobody met it and nobody thanked that master for six days and for carrying nine of them up that coast on no figure, and that that is on the fitting-out sheet as a line and is not a figure and is not a name; and that a second hand's day at the compressor went onto that sheet at about four in the afternoon, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, paid in coin on that stone to a man of about thirty-seven who had asked for it by name on the Friday; and that that sheet is short by one thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence, being eight marks, seven shillings and tenpence, and carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall told the man who keeps the leaf at about ten past eight in the evening that the berth fund had carried his day in and out at the same figure at the same hours, and that she had looked at the book twice on the Friday because it looked wrong and it was not wrong; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay asked one question at the head of that slip at about ten past eight in the evening, being the question of how it was known to be the paper and not how it was known to be a man, and was not answered on that slip and did not ask it a second time.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening on that stone, and no name is against it: that the reason a master of a boat of about nineteen tons would not take her in on a north-west wind in the last of the light is his own and is nineteen years and a boat that is still afloat, and that nobody in that party argued with him about the two hours and that the two hours are not on any page of that trip; and that the reason nobody in that party thanked that master for carrying nine of them on no figure is in the yard's own mouth and is that a man carrying people he is not paid for is a thing a stranger with a pencil cannot check, and that this yard has kept such things out of a column for as long as that leaf has been open; and that the reason a woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall told the man who keeps the leaf what the berth fund had done and did not make a fuss out of it is her own, and is that a day is a day and the fund pays a day for a day and does not care which coast the day happened on, and that she is not going to be glad about it to a man who has been away, and that she said so to his face and he did not thank her either and she noticed; and that the reason the question a man of about thirty-four asked at the head of that slip was asked at all is in that man's own mouth and is that he had carried it up two days and had not been able to put it down anywhere else, and that he keeps the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book and has never shown it to anybody and was not asked for it and did not offer it, and that the fact that he was not answered and did not ask a second time is the only thing about that question that this yard has entered, and that it is entered here and not in the first column because a question that is still open is not a count; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Sunday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
