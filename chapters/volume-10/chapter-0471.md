# Chapter 471

*Tuesday the Second Day of November, the Fifty-Fourth Wage Tuesday, That Column Not Moving on It and Fifty-Four Weeks Standing Fallen Due With No Envelope Against Any of Them in a Harbor the Party Was Not In, a Slate Written Before Anybody Went Down With a Reason in the Diver's Own Words and an Hour Against It and a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff That Tender Could Pull Without Asking, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Opening One of the Forty Racks With His Own Two Hands and Every Man on That Deck Watching Him Do It, What Was in That Rack Being a Part and Not a Relic, a Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Saying One Thing About It and the Man Who Opened the Rack Saying Another Thing and Neither of Them Agreeing With the Other, a Cut About Two Inches Long Across the Back of a Left Hand Off the Lip of That Rack and No Figure Set Against It and Nobody Thanked, Bread and Salt Beef for Nine Men for Ten Days Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at One Thousand Six Hundred Pence and a Day Apiece in Advance to the Same Four Men at Six Hundred and Seventy-Two Pence and Nothing Going onto That Sheet in Any Hand From Any Person, the Shortfall Said Out Loud on That After Deck at About Half Past Four in the Afternoon at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence, Being Eighteen Marks, Six Shillings and Sixpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Tuesday the second day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was the fifty-fourth wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and a wage Tuesday in this harbor is a day the column is counted on and not a day it is moved on. The column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room, and it did not move, and fifty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in that harbor and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on that deck and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf was under her own coat and the day's entries were made on that deck at night. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixty-nine days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and seventy-one days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a seventeenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and nine days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and seventy-fifth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in ninety-three days. A man of about fifty-one in that harbor has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and eleven days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the forty-eighth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the sixty-third day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the eightieth day.**

---

The slate was written before anybody went down and it was read out on that after deck in front of every man in that boat before the bell was off the chocks.

It carried the reason in the words of the man who was going to use it and the hour against that reason, being that one of the forty racks was to be opened and its contents were to be brought up whole and nothing else was to be touched, and it carried a figure of forty minutes of air and it named the tender, who was on the surface in that boat and not in the bell, with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.

**Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, went down at about nine in the morning on that ebb and every man on that deck stood where he could see the hatch.**

Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on the said day and nobody went in twice.

He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-five of them and came up at about ten to ten with the ebb still making, and there was one object in the cradle with him and it was not broken and it was not wrapped.

---

**What was in that rack is a part and not a relic, and nobody in that boat has used a word for what it belongs to.**

It is about the size of a sheet of paper. It is worked glass with a flange down two of its edges and two squared holes through the flange, and it is not thick in the middle and it is thick at the flange, and along the length of the middle of it the glass is worn bright and in one place it is worn thin enough to see through from one side to the other, and the wear is not on the ends, it is on the middle, and the wear is the wear of a thing that has been slid in and out of another thing many times in a life much longer than the four hundred and odd years the man who looked at it last was willing to put on it.

The man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down held it for about four minutes on that after deck in about nine fathoms of water and said one thing about it, and what he said went into the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the hour against it, and nobody agreed with him and nobody disagreed with him either.

"It came out of a chest in a house four days up this coast and the man it came out of had had it since before he was born and would not say where he got it. There was a figure cut on the flange and it was a figure for the making and not a figure for the worth. A part is a part. Nobody in my life has ever put a part in a rack for a reason that anybody alive would still call a reason."

Trevask said the other thing and it went into the same column in the same evening, in the order it was said, and the two of them are in that column and neither of them is above the other.

"That is what a thing is when a man has carried it up a coast in a chest for four generations and can tell you what it was made for," he said. "I have been in holds nineteen years and I can tell you what it has been *doing*, which is a different thing and the better one, and a thing that has been slid in and out of another thing that many times has been slid in and out of another thing that many times and there is no chest in this world that tells a man how long a wear is. I am not going to give you a figure for it on a Tuesday. I will give you two figures for it on the Thursday, one for the whole of it and one for the newest thing on it, and you can write both down and disagree with me about one of them."

Nobody asked him for either figure that day, and he offered neither.

---

**The lip of that rack is sharp and it has not been ground down in a long time, and it opened the back of a left hand about two inches long at about ten to ten in the morning.**

A man of about thirty-seven dressed it with boiled water and a strip off a shirt and put a man's hand over the top of it and told him to keep it out of the pump water for the rest of the day and the day after, and he did both of those things.

There is no figure set against it. There is no line opened for it on the sheet that carries the money and there is no line in the first column of that leaf for anything except a day's entry, and a man's hand is not a day's entry. Nobody thanked him for going down, nobody thanked the man of about thirty-seven for the dressing, and the man of about thirty-four was on that deck again at about six the next morning with the strip showing at the edge of his cuff and he did the thing he was asked to do.

---

**And then the day was money and nothing else, and the day was the only part of it anybody could check with a pencil.**

Bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto that sheet at about ten in the morning, being one thousand six hundred pence, being ten marks, written out at the head of the line in that yard's own mouth with the day against it, and a day apiece in advance went to the same four men at about two in the afternoon, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and each of the four wrote his own name against his own line.

**Nothing went onto that sheet in any hand on that day, and nobody asked anybody for anything, and there is nobody in that harbor to ask.**

At about half past four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said the position out loud on that after deck to every man standing on it. The sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and thirteen thousand five hundred and ninety-eight pence out, and it is two thousand nine hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being eighteen marks, six shillings and sixpence.

He said it once and put the leaf down and nobody argued with him about it, and nobody was asked for the rest of it, and nobody on that boat has ever known who the rest of it is supposed to come from, and the number went into the first column of that leaf that evening with the hour against it, and into no other book on that deck.

---

**A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OPENED ONE OF THE FORTY RACKS WITH HIS OWN TWO HANDS IN FRONT OF EVERY MAN ON THAT DECK AND WHAT WAS IN IT WAS A PART AND NOT A RELIC. TWO PEOPLE SAID ONE THING EACH ABOUT IT AND THEY DO NOT AGREE AND BOTH ARE IN THE SAME COLUMN IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SAID AND NEITHER IS ABOVE THE OTHER, AND HE SAID HE WOULD GIVE TWO FIGURES FOR IT ON THE THURSDAY AND NEITHER WAS ASKED FOR AND NEITHER WAS OFFERED. THE LIP OF THAT RACK CUT HIM ACROSS THE BACK OF A HAND FOR ABOUT TWO INCHES AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST IT AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. BREAD AND SALT BEEF WENT ONTO THE SHEET AT ONE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED PENCE AND A DAY APIECE IN ADVANCE TO THE SAME FOUR MEN AT SIX HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE AND NOTHING WENT ON IN ANY HAND, AND THAT SHEET IS TWO THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE SHORT, BEING EIGHTEEN MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE, AND IT WAS SAID OUT LOUD ON THAT DECK AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH IT AND NOBODY WAS ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the second day of November next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was the fifty-fourth wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that a wage Tuesday in that harbor is a day the column is counted on and not a day it is moved on; and that the column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest and did not move on that day, and that fifty-four weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a slate was written and read out on that deck before the bell was off the chocks, carrying the reason in the words of the man who was going to use it and the hour against it, being that one of the forty racks was to be opened and its contents brought up whole and nothing else touched, with a figure of forty minutes of air and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking; and that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay went down at about nine in the morning on the ebb, in about twenty-two fathoms, and was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-five of them and came up at about ten to ten, and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice; and that what was in that rack is a part and not a relic, being worked glass about the size of a sheet with a flange down two edges and two squared holes in that flange and the middle of it worn bright and in one place worn thin enough to see through, and that no word for what it belongs to is in any mouth on that deck; and that the man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down said one thing about it and the man who opened the rack said another thing and the two of them do not agree, and that he said he would give two figures for it on the Thursday, one for the whole of it and one for the newest thing on it, and that he was not asked for either and offered neither; and that the lip of that rack cut the back of a left hand about two inches long at about ten to ten in the morning, and that a man of about thirty-seven dressed it, and that no figure is set against it and no line was opened for it and nobody was thanked; and that bread and salt beef for nine men for ten days went onto the fitting-out sheet at about ten in the morning, being one thousand six hundred pence, being ten marks, and a day apiece in advance went to the same four men at about two in the afternoon, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and that nothing went onto that sheet in any hand on that day and nobody was asked for anything; and that the sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and thirteen thousand five hundred and ninety-eight pence out and is two thousand nine hundred and fifty-eight pence short, being eighteen marks, six shillings and sixpence, and that the figure was said out loud on that after deck at about half past four in the afternoon in front of every man on it and nobody argued with it and nobody was asked for the rest of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason one rack was opened and nothing else was touched is in the man who wrote it, and is that a floor that has been swept once and holds forty racks of one size is a floor somebody meant to use again, and that the only way to find out what goes in a rack is to put a hand in it and look, and that looking is not the same as taking and that the difference is on that slate and was read out; and that the reason two people said two things about that part and neither of them agreed with the other is in the yard's own mouth, and is that one of them knows what the thing was for and the other one knows what it has been doing, and that those are two different pieces of knowledge and a party of nine men cannot make them into one by arguing and did not try; and that the reason he would not give a figure on the Tuesday is his own, and is that a man who has been in holds for nineteen years does not give a number for a wear off a shelf before he has had it under his own hands on a day when the water is off it, and that he named the day and the day was the Thursday; and that the reason no figure is set against the cut on that hand is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a figure against it would be a claim that the yard owed a man something, and the yard does not owe that man anything, and a cut dressed with boiled water out of a shirt is not a debt and is not going to become one; and that the reason nothing went onto that sheet in any hand on the said Tuesday, and the reason the man who keeps the leaf said the shortfall out loud to nine men in the open at about half past four in the afternoon and did not lower his voice about it and did not raise it either, is that a yard which borrows against a shortfall quietly and tells its own crew nothing about it is a yard that will be told something by the first man who can read a column, and that there are about eleven people in that harbor who cannot read one and they hear a figure spoken the same as anybody else hears it; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Tuesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
