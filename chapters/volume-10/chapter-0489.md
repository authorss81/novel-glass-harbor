# Chapter 489

*Sunday the Twenty-First Day of November, the Eighty-First Time the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench in That Harbor Came Down Bare at About Eight in the Morning and Went Up Whole, a Wipe, and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday, the Party Off That Floor for a Working Day and Not One Descent, Nothing Going Out on Any Line of the Sheet That Carries the Money in Any Hand and Nothing Going into It in Any Hand, the Twenty-First Not Being Opened Under Any Air Line and Nobody Asked Why, a Crate With Matting and a Lid and a Batten and a Line Round It Standing on That After Deck Not Opened in Front of Nine Men Because Nine Men Is Not a Room, a Woman of About Thirty-One Who Crews Nothing and Sells Nothing Reading the Lid of It and Not the Inside of It and Saying Out Loud What a Crate Is For, the Whole of What She Said Being That a Thing That Is Opened Where the Nine People Who Filled It Are Standing Is a Thing That Has Been Opened in Public and That This One Is Going Into a Room Over a Fish Market Four Hundred Yards From a Shed Where About Eleven People Cannot Read and Where the People Who Are Going to Be Told About It Are Not Going to Be the Nine of Us, the Shortfall Unmoved at Thirteen Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Six Pence, Being Eighty-Seven Marks, Four Shillings and Eightpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Sunday the twenty-first day of November, on a boat of about nineteen tons lying over that trench, two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Sunday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor came down bare at about eight in the morning for the eighty-first time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday laid the same course on the Sunday and did not put it back, and the party was two days' sail off that deck and saw none of it. Nothing went out on any line of the sheet that carries the money in any hand and nothing went into it in any hand, and the running out on that sheet did not move from the close of the day before. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. That sheet stands at twenty-four thousand six hundred and sixteen pence out, being one hundred and fifty-three marks, eleven shillings and fourpence, and ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is thirteen thousand nine hundred and seventy-six pence short, being eighty-seven marks, four shillings and eightpence. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past.**

**The nine that a stranger in that harbor could have read off any wall are the nine below, and not one of them was found by looking: the notice of the seventeenth of May past, one hundred and eighty-seven days; the bellwright of that harbor, not asked for one hundred and eighty-nine; the two objects of nobody's on that crown, five hundred and twenty-seven days; the bench under that pump, bare for the two hundred and ninety-third night; the two lights at about a mile off that point, one hundred and eleven days; the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its sixty-sixth day; the paper of four lines in Fish Street, its eighty-first; the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its ninety-eighth; and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and twenty-nine days. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday and is walked on at every charge and the top name on that rota board is still off it. In an office off the west basin of that harbor there is a box on a table that is not empty, and another on the same table that is not empty, and a man of about fifty who wants that fourth box left empty and is not asked about it. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**It was a working day and not a day on the bottom of the sea, and the reason was said out loud once, at about eight in the morning, by the man who keeps the leaf, and nobody argued with him about it and nobody asked why.**

"Four days of air were hired on the seventeenth for the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth, and the twentieth was the last of them, and there is no line opened for this day or for tomorrow. **We are off that floor today.** The compressor will be run and the coal will be burned and a boat lying still burns a great deal more than a boat working does, and I would rather we burned it here than at the bottom of a trench on an air we have not got. That is the whole of the reason and I am not going to give it a second time, and if a man on this deck wants to go down tomorrow then he can say so at the top of the day and I will open a line or I will not, and today there is nothing under the slate and nothing in the bell."

Nobody went into that mouth on that day. Nobody asked for a reason twice and nobody asked for a reason at all.

---

**And the work of that Sunday was the work of a boat, and it went on from about seven in the morning until about six in the afternoon, and nine people did it and one of them is not a member of anything.**

The boy was up the ladder at the after masthead with a caulking iron and came down twice. The man of about fifty-one sat on the tarpaulin from about ten until about four and went through the bundles that were drying under the bracket, counting them, and wrote the count on a slate and read the count out once to the man who keeps the leaf and did not read it out again. The man of about thirty-eight worked the compressor the whole of that day, from the time the others came up off the water to the time the light went, and asked nobody for anything and nobody put a figure on him for any part of it. The pump hand of about forty-four slept for four hours in the middle of the day because he is a man of about forty-four and there is a stove in that chamber. **And the man of about thirty-eight who came up out of that mouth on the Friday with something in him that is not his was on that compressor all day as well, and no figure is set against him for it and nobody thanked him and he did not say one word about the Friday to anybody.**

---

**And the crate stood on that after deck from about ten o'clock in the morning, with the matting in it and the lid on it and a batten across the lid and a line round the whole of it, and nobody opened it, and nobody opened it because a woman of about thirty-one who crews nothing and sells nothing said out loud at about half past ten in the morning, in front of all nine of them, why it was not going to be opened.**

She put her hand flat on the lid and did not lift it and did not take the batten off.

"I have put a hand on the lid and I have read the lid and I am not going to read the inside, and I want that written down in the words I am using. **A crate is for a thing that has to be somewhere, and a crate is not for a party being proud of itself.** A thing that is opened where the nine people who filled it are standing is a thing that has been opened in public, whatever anybody says about it afterwards and whatever good any of the nine intends by it. And this one is going into a room over a fish market four hundred yards from a shed, where there are about eleven people who cannot read, and the people who are going to be told about it are not going to be the nine of us and are not going to be standing here listening to the lid come off. So the lid comes off in that room, or it does not come off at all. That is the whole of what a crate is for and I have said it once."

Nobody thanked her. Nobody argued with her. Nobody on that deck proposed opening it, and nobody on that deck had proposed it, and she said afterwards that she had put it out in front of nine people on purpose so that it could not afterwards be said that nobody had ever said it out loud.

**And on the lid there is a slate with the day against it in the yard's own mouth and the figure of the crate against it, being two hundred and sixty-eight pence and one mark and nine shillings, and the hour it was filled against it, and the name of nobody at all.**

**And the other crate, the one that has been on that deck since the twenty-ninth day of October past with a batten across its lid and a day on it and no word on any page of that harbor for what is inside it, was on the other side of that after deck the whole of that Sunday, and it was not opened either, and the two of them were not opened on the same day and are not the same crate, and nobody has asked anybody to say which of them matters more.**

---

**THE PARTY WAS OFF THAT FLOOR FOR A WORKING DAY AND NOT ONE DESCENT WAS MADE AND NO LINE WAS OPENED FOR IT AND NOBODY ASKED WHY. A CRATE WITH MATTING AND A LID AND A BATTEN AND A LINE ROUND IT STOOD ON THAT AFTER DECK AND WAS NOT OPENED IN FRONT OF NINE MEN, AND THE REASON WAS SAID OUT LOUD ONCE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING BY A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO CREWS NOTHING AND SELLS NOTHING, BEING THAT A THING OPENED WHERE THE NINE PEOPLE WHO FILLED IT ARE STANDING IS A THING THAT HAS BEEN OPENED IN PUBLIC AND THAT THIS ONE IS GOING INTO A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM A SHED WHERE ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE CANNOT READ. NOTHING WENT OUT ON ANY LINE OF THAT SHEET IN ANY HAND. AND THAT SHEET IS THIRTEEN THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING EIGHTY-SEVEN MARKS, FOUR SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY IN THAT HARBOR TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason a crate was not opened in front of nine men is that woman's own, and is that a deck is a room in front of the people who are standing in it and is not a room in front of the people who are not, and that the nine people who filled that crate would have heard the lid come off and would have had to decide in the moment what that noise meant, and that a party which takes a thing out of its own hands in front of itself has done it in public whether or not anybody afterwards calls it a working operation; and that the reason she put it out loud at half past ten on a Sunday, when nobody had proposed anything, is her own, and is that a rule nobody has ever had to say out loud is a rule that has not been agreed, and that saying a thing in front of nine people who all heard the other side of it is the only way this yard has ever found of making sure a thing is not a custom; and that the reason the party was off that floor for a working day is the man who keeps the leaf's own account of hired air, and is that four days of air were bought on the seventeenth and the twentieth was the last of them, and that a boat lying still over a trench burns more coal than a boat working and that the coal was bought at fourteen marks, nine shillings and fourpence for four days and is a figure a stranger with a pencil can take off that sheet, and that a day of burning it at the top of the water is worth more than a day of burning it twenty-two fathoms down; and that the reason the count of the drying bundles was read out once and not again is the man of about fifty-one's own, and is that a number a man has counted twice is a number and a number a man has read out three times is a performance, and that he has been in holds nineteen years and has never once been asked to make a number sound better than it is; and that the reason nobody put a figure on a man of about thirty-eight for two shifts on a compressor is the yard's own mouth, and is that a man is paid a day at the figure in chalk or he is not paid at all, and that a figure of two shifts is not a figure of work and would put a price on a compressor and not on a man, and that this yard does not price its own; and that the reason that harbor's standing condition was not advanced by a penny on the said Sunday is the yard's own mouth, and is that a Sunday is the one morning of a week on which a harbor can be seen standing still, and that a cistern in that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and walked on at every charge, and that on the Sunday it is not charged at all and is not walked on by anybody, and that there is a box in an office off that west basin that is not empty and another on the same table that is not empty and a man of about fifty who wants the fourth one left empty and is not asked about it, and that all three of those are on that Sunday and none of them is on that deck.*
