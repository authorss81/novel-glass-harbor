# Chapter 477

*Monday the Eighth Day of November, a Rota Day, and the Ninety-Seventh Time That Cistern at the Back of That Market Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It, the Top Name on That Rota Board Still off It, a Slate Written Before Anybody Went Down With a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff That Tender Could Pull Without Asking, a Party Leaving the Floor of That Trench for the Working Day and the Chamber With a Stove in It Staying Exactly Where It Was Seated on Four Pads of Sawn Fir, a Built Thing Under the Water Off the Mouth of That Trench About Two Fathoms Off the Bottom and About Nine Fathoms of Water Over It Off a Line, Two Kerbs and a Gutter a Foot and a Half Wide Down the Middle of It and It Being a Way and Not a Wreck, a Man of About Thirty-Four of the Second Lay Saying One Thing About It and Not Giving a Year and Not Giving the Name of the Thing That Made It, a Man of About Thirty-Seven Going Down on That Way and Coming Up at About Two in the Afternoon With a Headache That Lasted Three Days and a Cold in His Teeth and No Figure Set Against Any of It, the Book That Man of About Thirty-four Keeps Not Shown and Not Asked For and Not Offered and Both of Those Staying Exactly As They Were, the Four Days of Air Hired on the Sixth of November Having One Day Left in Front of That Party and the Line for Them Bearing the Sixth and Not the Eighth, the Shortfall Unmoved at Five Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Thirty-Seven Marks, Two Shillings and Sixpence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Monday the eighth day of November, in about nine fathoms off a line, on a built thing under the water, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe. The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-seventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**The cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-seventh time at about eight in the morning, walked on by none of it, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eighteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. A boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. Mara Venn is on that deck, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf is under her own coat. The nine: the notice of the seventeenth of May past at one hundred and seventy-five days, the bellwright of that harbor not asked at one hundred and seventy-seven, the two objects of nobody's on that crown at five hundred and fifteen days, the bench under that pump bare for the two hundred and eighty-first night, the two lights at about a mile off that point at ninety-nine days, the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall on its fifty-fourth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street on its sixty-ninth, the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street on its eighty-sixth, and a man of about fifty-one who has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and seventeen days. A pump hand of about forty-four came up out of that bell on the seventh at about half past twelve and went back to the compressor at about one o'clock and worked it until about six and nobody has asked him anything since.** **A man of about twenty-nine on that boat cannot hear out of the left side of his head, and no figure is set against it anywhere and it is not a motive in anything he does.**

---

**The chamber with a stove in it was exactly where it was on the Sunday, seated level on four pads of sawn fir at the edge of the swept part of that floor, and nobody moved it and nobody took the stove out of it and nobody has given anybody a key to it that anybody else does not have.**

They went out on the ebb at about nine in the morning and the tender's slate said where they were going and why and by what route back, and the route back was the same route out, because a route back that is a different route is a route that has to be found in the dark.

---

**It is about two fathoms off the bottom there and about nine fathoms of water over it, and the nine fathoms is a figure off a line and not off a memory, and it was on the slate before anybody went down.**

The first thing about it that a man notices is that it is flat, and the second is that it has two kerbs. It is about thirty feet wide where it is clearest and it runs out from the mouth of that trench and away, and it goes far enough on the said Monday that the man at the head of the line could not see the end of it and would not have gone to find out.

It has a gutter down the middle of it about a foot and a half wide, cut into the deck and running with the fall of the thing, and the fall of the thing is to the west, and there is silt banked against the west kerb and not against the east one.

**Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, said one thing about it on that deck at about half past eleven in the morning, and it was the whole of what he said about it that day, and he gave no year for it and he did not give the name of the thing that made it.**

"That is a way and not a wreck. It was made for things with wheels on it and it has a gutter down the middle because a way without a gutter is a way that rots, and I have been in holds nineteen years and I know what a wreck looks like and I have been in enough of them to be sure. I am not going to give you a year for it off a line in nine fathoms and I am not going to give you the name of whoever made it, because the name would be a guess wearing a coat and the year would be worse."

**He was not asked for the book he keeps, and he did not offer it, and he said why in one sentence and it was a reason and not a performance.**

"You have not asked me for it and I have not got it out, and I would like both of those to stay exactly as they are for as long as this boat is on this coast."

Nobody asked him for it that day and nobody asked him for it on any of the three days after it.

---

**A man of about thirty-seven went down onto that way at about ten in the morning on that ebb under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that the gutter had silt in it and that the silt in a gutter says which way it has been running and how long it has been stopped, and the air limit was a figure of forty minutes and the tender was named and was on the surface of that boat and not in the bell with his hand on the chain weight and a cutoff he could pull without asking anybody's leave.**

He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten to eleven with a headache that lasted three days and a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron like a nail, and he worked the afternoon shift at the pump and the day after and the day after that and was not thanked, is not a casualty, and carries no figure against him.

Nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on the said day and nobody went in twice.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four of the second lay went out onto that way again at about twenty to two in the afternoon, under a slate of his own with the reason in his own words and the hour against it, being that the far end of it had not been stood at, with the same figure of forty minutes of air and the same named tender on the surface and not in the bell and the same cutoff he could pull without asking, and he was in the water about forty-five minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about a quarter to three, and at about half past two in the afternoon the man at the head of that line came off the end of that way and there was a wall.**

It is not a cliff and it is not a fall and it is not a wreck of anything. It is a wall going down about fourteen feet off the floor and it is built, and there is a doorway in it about five feet six inches wide and about six feet two high, and it is the only opening in about sixty feet of it, and the party stopped at it and did not go through it on that day, because it was already about half past two on that ebb and the air on that slate was a figure of forty minutes and the day had already spent one of them.

They went back the way they came and they were back on the trench floor at about a quarter to three and the chamber was where it had been and the stove in it was drawing.

**The four days of air that were hired on the sixth of November had one day left in front of that party at the close of that Monday, and the line for them on the sheet that carries the money bears the sixth of November and not the eighth, and nobody moved it and nobody opened a fifth day.**

The shortfall was five thousand nine hundred and fifty pence at the close of that day, being thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, and nothing went on that sheet and nobody argued about it and nobody was asked for the rest of it.

---

**THE PARTY LEFT THE FLOOR OF THAT TRENCH FOR ONE WORKING DAY AND THE CHAMBER WITH A STOVE IN IT DID NOT MOVE. THERE IS A BUILT THING OUT THERE ABOUT TWO FATHOMS OFF THE BOTTOM WITH NINE FATHOMS OF WATER OVER IT OFF A LINE, AND IT HAS TWO KERBS AND A GUTTER A FOOT AND A HALF WIDE DOWN THE MIDDLE OF IT AND SILT BANKED AGAINST THE WEST KERB AND NOT THE EAST ONE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR SAID THAT IT IS A WAY AND NOT A WRECK AND GAVE NO YEAR FOR IT AND DID NOT GIVE THE NAME OF THE THING THAT MADE IT, AND HIS BOOK WAS NOT SHOWN, WAS NOT ASKED FOR, WAS NOT OFFERED, AND HE ASKED FOR IT TO STAY THAT WAY. AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON THAT WAY RAN INTO A BUILT WALL FOURTEEN FEET HIGH WITH ONE DOORWAY IN IT AND SIXTY FEET OF IT, AND THE PARTY STOPPED AT THAT DOORWAY AND DID NOT GO THROUGH IT AND CAME BACK THE WAY THEY CAME AND NOTHING WAS TAKEN AND THE SHORTFALL IS UNMOVED AT FIVE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE, BEING THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND SIXPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the eighth day of November next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged for the ninety-seventh time at about eight in the morning and was walked on by none of it, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the chamber with a stove in it was exactly where it was on the Sunday, seated level on four pads of sawn fir at the edge of the swept part of that floor, and that nobody moved it, nobody took the stove out of it, and no character has been given a key to it that anybody else does not have; and that the party went out on the ebb at about nine in the morning and that the tender's slate said where they were going and why and by what route back, and that the route back was the same route out; and that there is a built thing about two fathoms off the bottom out there with about nine fathoms of water over it off a line, and that the nine fathoms was on the slate before anybody went down; and that it is flat and about thirty feet wide where it is clearest and it runs out from the mouth of that trench and away, and that the man at the head of the line could not see the end of it on that day and would not have gone to find out; and that it has two kerbs and a gutter down the middle of it about a foot and a half wide cut into the deck and running with the fall of the thing, and that the fall of the thing is to the west and that there is silt banked against the west kerb and not against the east one; and that a man of about thirty-four said that it is a way and not a wreck, that it was made for things with wheels on it and that it has a gutter because a way without a gutter is a way that rots, and that he gave no year for it and did not give the name of the thing that made it; and that his book was not shown, was not asked for and was not offered, and that he said he would like both of those to stay exactly as they were for as long as that boat was on that coast, and that nobody asked him for it on that day or on any of the three days after it; and that a man of about thirty-seven went down onto that way at about ten in the morning under a slate with his reason in his own words and the hour against it, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking, and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten to eleven with a headache that lasted three days and a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron like a nail, and that he worked the pump that afternoon and the two days after and was not thanked; and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice; and that the same man of about thirty-four went out onto that way again at about twenty to two in the afternoon under a slate of his own with the reason in his own words and the hour against it and with the same figure of forty minutes of air and the same named tender and the same cutoff, and that he was in the water about forty-five minutes and on the bottom about thirty-one of them and came up at about a quarter to three, and that at about half past two in the afternoon that way ran into a built wall about fourteen feet high with one doorway in it about five feet six inches wide and about six feet two high, being the only opening in about sixty feet of it, and that the party stopped at that doorway and did not go through it on that day and came back the way they came and were back on the trench floor at about a quarter to three and the chamber was where it had been and the stove in it was drawing; and that the four days of air hired on the sixth of November had one day left in front of that party at the close of that Monday and the line for them on the fitting-out sheet bears the sixth of November and not the eighth; and that nothing went onto that sheet on that day and that it stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and sixteen thousand five hundred and ninety pence out and is five thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the route back was written on that tender's slate as the same route out is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a route back that is a different route is a route that has to be found in the dark, and that a man who has been down four times in a week can find a gutter in nine fathoms and cannot find a bend; and that the reason nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for the book he keeps is the yard's own mouth and is the same reason nobody asked him on the eighteenth day of October past, and is that a man who has not been asked has time, and that the whole of what that party wanted from him was a look at a floor and not a look at his own keeping, and that the floor was looked at; and that the reason he would not give a year for that way, having said out loud that a man who has been in holds nineteen years knows what a hundred years looks like and not what six hundred looks like, is his own and is the same reason twice over, and that the two times are not a repetition because the first was about a bar under two inches of silt in the dark and the second is about a thing he has not been down to; and that the reason the party stopped at that doorway at a quarter to three is that the day had already spent four of its forty minutes twice and the air on that slate was a figure and not an opinion, and that a yard which goes through a doorway at a quarter to three because it is nearly there is a yard that does not come back; and that the four days of air hired on the sixth have one day left and there is no line opened for a fifth and nobody has said out loud that there will not be one; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
