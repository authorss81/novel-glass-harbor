# Chapter 461

*Saturday the Twenty-Third Day of October, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, a Party Under Way Home on a Passage with No Figure Set Against It and One Day off That Coast, a Small Supply Boat Speaking Them at About Six in the Morning and Three Days of Coal for the Pump Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at One Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Four Pence, Said Out Loud on a Deck and Not Argued With, a Man of About Twenty-Nine Sitting Where He Can See Everybody Because He Put Himself There and a Second Man Saying So Out Loud, and the Man of About Twenty-Nine Saying That He Did It on Purpose and Not Because Anybody Asked Him, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It With the Harbor a Day Behind the Party, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Saturday the twenty-third day of October, on the deck of a boat of about nineteen tons, a day off that coast, and in that harbor a day behind them.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and the whole of the ten days of that run down the coast was one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence in and one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence out, being eleven marks, seven shillings and fourpence each way, and twenty-eight plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight is twenty-eight, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in that harbor and was not approached by anybody and a shoe that is not his was on the floor beside it. Mara Venn was on the deck of that boat and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf was under her own coat and was read back on decks. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and did not move the eleventh piece. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and fifty-nine days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and sixty-one days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fifteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and ninety-nine days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and sixty-fifth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in eighty-three days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and one days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirty-eighth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the fifty-third day and has been answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the seventieth day. A man of about twenty-nine on that boat could not hear out of the left side of his head on the twentieth, on the twenty-first, on the twenty-second or on the twenty-third, and has not been asked about it and did not ask and no figure is set against it anywhere.**

---

They spoke a small supply boat at about six in the morning a day off that coast, and it was a coal boat out of a town nobody in that party had been to, and it took an hour and forty minutes to come alongside because the man who keeps the leaf would not let a boat come alongside that boat while anybody's pots were in the water, which is a rule and not a fear and which he did not explain on the said day and did not need to.

He said out loud at about ten past seven in the morning, standing at the rail with a slate, that three days of coal for the pump was one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence, being eleven marks and fourpence, and that a price is the figure a person says out loud to somebody else, and that the figure had been said out loud to him by a man in a coal boat about twenty minutes before he said it to nine men, and that if anybody in that boat thought it was too much they had until noon to say so and after noon it was bought or they went without it.

Nobody said so.

Ivo Skell, a pump hand of about forty-four who has nineteen years of compressors in him and is in nobody's crew, said one thing and it was not about the figure.

"I have burned a stove off a supply boat at that figure before and I have burned one off a quay at half of it, and the one off the supply boat is the one that has not had a hole in it, and I am not saying that to make anybody feel better about a figure they have already agreed to." He put his thumb on the rail and looked at it. "I am saying it because there is a line to go on that page and there are two hands to write it and only one of us can read it, and the man who cannot read is going to hear a figure said out loud and I would rather he heard it from me than from a boat."

---

The master of that boat, Simeon Roke, said the same thing he says every morning of a run, in a flat voice, in front of his two men and the boy, and nobody in that party was asked to like it.

"Two hours on the flood before the wind gets up over the point, and we will be in the roads by dark if the flood does not stand, and if the flood stands we will be in the roads by nine, and I would rather be in the roads at nine and be told I was wrong than be on a bottom at four." He put his hat on again. "That is the whole of what I know and it is not a promise."

---

Amos Trewin, of about twenty-nine, of the third lay, who cannot hear out of the left side of his head, had moved his bag and his coat and his oilcloth to a thwart in the middle of that boat on the Friday night and had done it while four other men were watching him do it, and on the said morning at about half past eight he said one thing about it out loud and nobody asked him why.

Teodor Vask said the thing out loud instead, standing up, because he had been thinking about it since the Friday night and had decided that if nobody said it then it would be a thing people were being careful about.

"He has put himself in the middle of the boat so that he can see everybody and so that nobody has to lean across him to get anywhere," he said, and he said it to the boat and not to the man, and then he said the rest of it to the man. "That is all it is and you did it on purpose and you did not have to."

"I did it on purpose," said Amos Trewin, "and I did not do it because anybody asked me, and I want the two of those printed in that order, and I want the second of them printed louder."

Nessa Pike said that she would write it in that order and she would not write it louder, and that was the whole of what she said about it, and she wrote it down at about nine o'clock on the after deck with the hour against it and read it back to him and he did not ask to read it and did not ask for it to be taken out.

**Nobody on that boat asked him what it was like, and nobody in that harbor has been told it, and no figure of any kind was set against the left side of that man's head on any sheet in that harbor or on that boat.**

---

The rest of that day was coal and stores and a stove that does not draw and half of what went into it going out into the water in about an hour, and a slate with three figures on it and the hour against each, and a man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down who coiled a line in a way that two men on that boat stopped to look at and did not say one word about.

At about ten past five in the evening, on the after deck, in the open, the fitting-out sheet was read back in full for the first time since that boat had left a harbor two days off, and about four men of that party cannot read and it was read twice.

**Seven thousand seven hundred and sixty pence came in and seven thousand two hundred and two pence went out and five hundred and fifty-eight pence was on that sheet when that party left the harbor on the evening tide of the twenty-second, and one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence went out of it on the said day for three days of coal, being eleven marks and fourpence, and it is short now by one thousand two hundred and six pence, being seven marks, seven shillings and twopence, and there is no count of persons on it and no figure on it for how many people are on it, and the sheet says on its own face that it is a list of what people did with their own money and not an account of a ship.**

A man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down read that figure back to the man who keeps the leaf and made him do it twice, and neither time did he suggest that a different figure would be easier to carry.

---

**A DAY AT SEA, A LINE OF COAL FOR THREE DAYS AT ONE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR PENCE, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR ASKED FOR THE REST OF IT. A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE PUT HIMSELF IN THE MIDDLE OF A BOAT SO THAT HE COULD SEE EVERYBODY AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE HAD DONE IT ON PURPOSE AND NOT BECAUSE ANYBODY HAD ASKED HIM, AND THE ORDER OF THOSE TWO CLAUSES IS ON A SLATE WITH AN HOUR AGAINST IT. THE PASSAGE HOME CARRIES NO FIGURE ON ANY SHEET AND IS NOT TO CARRY ONE, AND A MAN WHO CARRIES PEOPLE HE IS NOT PAID FOR IS A THING A STRANGER WITH A PENCIL CANNOT CHECK. A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL IN THAT FRONT STREET AND DID NOT COME DOWN ON THAT DAY, AND THE HARBOR IS POORER ON THE TWENTY-THIRD DAY OF OCTOBER THAN IT WAS ON THE THIRTEENTH OF IT, AND ELEVEN PEOPLE'S MONEY WENT DOWN A COAST ROAD AND CAME BACK ON THAT BOAT AND IS NOT COMING BACK UPON THEM.**

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-third day of October at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and entered by the ship's hand on that boat a day off that coast, with the harbor a day behind them: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, and that the whole of the ten days of that run down the coast is one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence in and one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence out, being eleven marks, seven shillings and fourpence each way, and that nine of those ten days carried one hundred and sixty-eight pence each way and the sixteenth of October carried three hundred and thirty-six each way because a berth fund paid that Saturday two days, so that nine times one hundred and sixty-eight is one thousand five hundred and twelve and one thousand five hundred and twelve and three hundred and thirty-six is one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight; and that a small supply boat spoke that party at about six in the morning a day off that coast and three days of coal for the pump went onto the fitting-out sheet at one thousand seven hundred and sixty-four pence, being eleven marks and fourpence, said out loud on the after deck at about ten past seven in the morning by the man who keeps the leaf, and that nobody in that boat said the figure was too much before noon; and that that sheet is short by one thousand two hundred and six pence, being seven marks, seven shillings and twopence, and that it carries no count of persons and no figure for how many people are on it; and that a man of about twenty-nine moved his bag and his coat and his oilcloth to the middle thwart of that boat on the Friday night, and that a second man said out loud on the said morning at about half past eight that he had done it on purpose and had not had to, and that he said in his own mouth that he had done it on purpose and had not done it because anybody asked him, and that the order of those two clauses is what was written down and the second of them was not written louder; and that a man of about fifty-one out of a coast town four days down read that shortfall back twice and suggested nothing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck a day off that coast, and no name is against it: that the reason a man who keeps the leaf would not let a supply boat come alongside while anybody's pots were in the water is his own and is a rule and not a fear and he did not explain it and did not need to; and that the reason a price is the figure a person says out loud to somebody else is in the yard's own mouth, and that a price that has been said out loud to nine men in a boat before noon is bought or the boat goes without it, and that this yard has not opened a line for a thing nobody asked to have a figure against, and the passage home is such a thing; and that the reason a pump hand of about forty-four who is in nobody's crew said the thing he said about a stove off a supply boat is his own, and that he said it because there is a line to go on that page and two hands to write it and only one of those two can read it, and that a man who cannot read a figure is entitled to hear it from the man who bought it; and that the reason nobody in that party asked a man of about twenty-nine what the loss of one side of his hearing is like is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a man on a mole cannot check what a man can hear, and that the cost of a gasket at two hundred and forty-four pence is a figure and the cost of what the gasket did is not one; and that the reason the shortfall on that sheet was read back twice by a man of about fifty-one and that he suggested nothing is his own, and it is entered here and not in the first column, because a man who has been the second man on somebody's boat and knows it is the only thing about him that anybody in that harbor can be told and it does not belong on a page with a figure on it; and that the reason nothing standing in that harbor was touched on the said Saturday, by anybody, in either direction, is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a boat two days from home is not a place where an old account is opened, and that an account opened on a day when nobody can be got to has been opened by weather and not by anybody.*
