# Chapter 479

*Wednesday the Tenth Day of November, a Day on Which No Charge Was Placed on That Cistern and on Which No Wage Tuesday Came and on Which the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Did Not Come Down at All, a Slate Written Before Anybody Went Down With a Figure of Forty Minutes of Air and a Named Tender on the Surface and Not in the Bell and a Cutoff That Tender Could Pull Without Asking, a Man of About Fifty-One Out of a Coast Town Four Days Down Going Down on the Ebb on an Independent Line to Cut One of the Nine Fittings Off at the Neck Because There Are Older Holes Under Those Nine and a Hole Under a Fitting Cannot Be Read While the Fitting Is On It, and That Cut Not Being One That Can Be Undone and the Piece Not Being One That Will Come Up a Hatch, and the Second Stove Pipe and a Bracket to Carry It Down a Ladder Going onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at Three Hundred and Ninety-Two Pence, Being Two Marks, Five Shillings and Eightpence, the Holder of That Writ Saying Out Loud on That Deck at About Two in the Afternoon That He Had Just Taken the Only State That Floor Will Ever Be In Off It, and the Reason for That in the Second Column With the Hour Against It and in the Yard's Own Mouth, Nobody Thanked and No Figure Set Against the Man Who Went Down and Did Not Come Up on That Line, and He Not Named as a Casualty and He Carrying Nothing, the Shortfall at Seven Thousand and Fourteen Pence, Being Forty-Three Marks, Eleven Shillings and Twopence, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Wednesday the tenth day of November, in about twenty-two fathoms, on the floor of a trench, and on a boat of about nineteen tons that is two days' sail from that harbor.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it and the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, being a day asked for by name, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of that trip came out of that bag, out of the owner of any boat in that harbor, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from ten until four in that harbor and was not approached by anybody. Mara Venn was on that deck and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and the leaf was under her own coat. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and seventy-seven days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and seventy-nine days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eighteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column and that boy's surname is on no page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are five hundred and seventeen days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and eighty-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in a hundred and one days. A man of about fifty-one in that harbor has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for a hundred and nineteen days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the fifty-sixth day, the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the seventy-first day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the eighty-eighth day. A pump hand of about forty-four came up out of a bell in that trench on the seventh day of November at about half past twelve with the order of two things he had done that morning gone out of his head in the middle of them and worked the compressor from about one o'clock that afternoon and nobody has asked him anything since and he is not a casualty and no figure is set against him.**

---

**A second stove pipe and a bracket to carry it down a ladder went onto the sheet that carries the money at about eleven in the morning, being three hundred and ninety-two pence, being two marks and six shillings, because the pipe in that chamber cracked about nine inches up from the bend for the second time in a fortnight and would not keep a fire and a bracket was needed because the pipe fouled the winch where it went straight up through the hatch.**

---

**What was under those nine fittings is a hole, and there is one older hole under each of the nine, and a hole under a fitting cannot be read while the fitting is on it.**

The reason that a man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down was the one who went down for it is in the yard's own mouth and is that he had been the only man on that boat with his own hand on the frame around a fitting collar twice, and that nobody else had, and that he had asked for nothing and was owed nothing and this was not a favour and was not going to be paid.

The reason he went on an independent line is that he was not coming up with a piece of three-inch iron in his hand, because a piece of three-inch iron with a collar on it does not go through a round hatch in a bell-shaped chamber with a stove in it, and cutting it smaller would have left a piece that nobody could ever read again, and he said both of those things out loud on that deck before he went.

He was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten to eleven on the bell's line and not on the independent one, and he was not hurt and nobody has named him a casualty and there is no figure set against him anywhere.

**The fitting is on the floor of that trench with its neck cut about a third of the way down and the hole under it is open and the silt in the bottom of it has been disturbed by a man's thumb, and that is what is on that floor now and it is not going to be put back.**

---

**And the holder of that writ said out loud on that deck at about two in the afternoon that he had just taken the only state that floor will ever be in off it, and the reason for it is in the second column of that leaf with the hour against it and in the yard's own mouth, and nobody thanked anybody for any part of it.**

"I want to be plain about it in front of all of you because there are nine men on this boat and about a hundred and forty people in the harbor this came from, and one of those hundred and forty is going to say to another one of them that we did that. He will be right to say it. There was a state of that floor on the Sunday morning and there is not one now and there will not be one again, and I did it because if there is an older hole under those fittings then those fittings were put in over something, and if they were put in over something then somebody had a reason, and a reason is the only thing on that floor that nine of us are actually here for."

"That is the reason you did it for," said the man of about fifty-one, from the deck, "and it is a good one and I did the cutting and I will not have it put on your account and not on mine either, and I am not being proud. I am being exact. A thing done for a reason is a thing somebody has to be answerable for and it is not a thing that gets to be done because a reason sounded well at a quarter to three on the Friday. What is lost is lost and it will be lost whether I was proud of the cutting or not."

---

Nobody thanked the man who went down and nobody thanked the man who decided and nobody said the word brave to either of them and nobody put a figure against either of them, and the piece of three-inch iron is on the floor of a trench in about twenty-two fathoms for the rest of that trip.

The man who keeps the leaf said the position out loud on that after deck at about half past five in the afternoon and it was seven thousand and fourteen pence short, being forty-three marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and nobody argued with him and nobody was asked for the rest of it.

---

**A FITTING WAS CUT OFF AT THE NECK ON THE TENTH DAY OF NOVEMBER AND THE CUT IS NOT ONE THAT CAN BE UNDONE AND THE PIECE IS ON THE FLOOR OF THAT TRENCH AND IS NOT COMING UP ON THIS TRIP. A MAN WENT DOWN ON AN INDEPENDENT LINE TO DO IT AND CAME UP ON THE BELL'S LINE, AND HE IS NOT A CASUALTY AND NOBODY HAS NAMED HIM ONE AND NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST HIM AND NOBODY WAS THANKED. THE HOLDER OF THAT WRIT SAID OUT LOUD IN FRONT OF ALL NINE OF THEM THAT HE HAD JUST TAKEN THE ONLY STATE THAT FLOOR WILL EVER BE IN OFF IT, AND GAVE HIS REASON, AND THE MAN WHO DID THE CUTTING SAID THAT A THING DONE FOR A REASON IS A THING SOMEBODY HAS TO BE ANSWERABLE FOR AND THAT WHAT IS LOST IS LOST WHETHER HE WAS PROUD OF IT OR NOT. A SECOND STOVE PIPE AND A BRACKET WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT THREE HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO PENCE, BEING TWO MARKS, FIVE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. AND THAT SHEET IS SEVEN THOUSAND AND FOURTEEN PENCE SHORT AT THE CLOSE OF THIS DAY, BEING FORTY-THREE MARKS, ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, AND NO FIGURE HAS BEEN ASKED OF ANYBODY TO MAKE IT OTHERWISE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the tenth day of November next at about half past nine in the evening on that deck: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it and that the wall behind a reeve's bench in that harbor did not come down on it at all; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard and one hundred and sixty-eight went out of it, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a second stove pipe and a bracket to carry it down a ladder went onto the fitting-out sheet at about eleven in the morning, being three hundred and ninety-two pence, being two marks and six shillings, because the pipe in that chamber cracked about nine inches up from the bend for the second time in a fortnight and would not keep a fire and a bracket was needed because the pipe fouled the winch where it went straight up through the hatch; and that there is an older hole under each of the nine fittings and a hole under a fitting cannot be read while the fitting is on it; and that a man of about fifty-one out of the coast town four days down went down on that ebb on an independent line at about ten in the morning to cut one of the nine off at the neck, under a slate with the reason in his own words and the hour against it, with an air limit of forty minutes written on that slate and a named tender on the surface and not in the bell and a cutoff that tender could pull without asking; and that he said out loud on that deck before he went that a piece of three-inch iron with a collar on it does not go through a round hatch in a bell-shaped chamber with a stove in it, and that cutting it smaller would have left a piece that nobody could ever read again, and that he would not do that; and that he was in the water about fifty minutes and on the bottom about thirty-four of them and came up at about ten to eleven on the bell's line and not on the independent one, and that he was not hurt and nobody has named him a casualty and no figure is set against him; and that nobody went into that mouth at the top of a flood on that day and nobody went in twice; and that the fitting is on the floor of that trench with its neck cut about a third of the way down and the hole under it is open and the silt in the bottom of it has been disturbed by a man's thumb; and that the holder of that writ said out loud on that deck at about two in the afternoon that he had just taken the only state that floor will ever be in off it and gave his reason for it, being that if there is an older hole under those fittings then those fittings were put in over something and somebody had a reason for it; and that the man of about fifty-one answered that that was the reason the other man did it for, and that it was a good one, and that he did the cutting and would not have it put on the other's account or on his own, and that a thing done for a reason is a thing somebody has to be answerable for and that what is lost is lost whether he was proud of the cutting or not; and that nobody thanked either of them, nobody said the word brave to either of them, and no figure is set against either; and that the fitting-out sheet stands at ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and seventeen thousand six hundred and fifty-four pence out and is seven thousand and fourteen pence short, being forty-three marks, eleven shillings and twopence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening on that deck, and no name is against it: that the reason the cutting was entered here in full with the hour against it and in the yard's own mouth, and not as a line of cost and not as a note in the first column, is that a cut is a thing a stranger with a pencil cannot check and cannot undo, and that a column a stranger with a pencil can check is the wrong place for a thing that has been made permanent in twenty-two fathoms; and that the reason the man who did the cutting would not have the decision put on his account or his own is his own, and is that a decision made out loud in front of nine people is not a decision that belongs to the man who made it, and that a man who puts a decision on his own account to be modest with it has taken it off the other four hands that heard it; and that the reason nobody was thanked is in the yard's own mouth, and is that a thing that cannot be undone is not a favour, and that thanking a man for a thing he cannot take back is the cheapest way there is of feeling better about having asked him for it; and that the reason that fitting is not coming up on this trip is a hatch and a measurement, and that a measurement that cannot be carried is a measurement that stays, and that the yard chose the measurement over the piece and has said so out loud; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August past is not entered in full on the said Wednesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
