# Chapter 697

*Friday the Seventeenth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Not Charged and Its Covers Lying on It From Nine in the Morning Until Four in the Afternoon, a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands Saying at About Eleven in the Morning That There Is a Boat Standing Off the Point That Is Not One of the Nine and That He Is Not Going to Describe It, the Keeper Writing What He Said Down With the Hour Against It and Not Going to Look and Not Sending Anybody, and About Four People Asking That Yard in the Afternoon What It Was Going to Do About It and Being Told Nothing Was Going to Do Anything About It, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty and the Second at Two Thousand and Twenty-Four*

Friday the seventeenth day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. At about eleven in the morning a man of about thirty-three who hires hands said in that shed that there is a boat standing off the point that is not one of the nine, and that he is not going to describe it, and that he wanted it on that page. The keeper wrote what he said down with the hour against it and did not go and look and did not send anybody to look. Between about one and about four about four people asked that yard what it was going to do about it and were told that this yard is not going to do anything about it and that they had been told the same. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters went another day on this morning and not one of the nine has been touched by anything said yesterday. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-fifth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and ninety-seven days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-fifth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and first night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and nineteen days with no figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eighty-ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and six days. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-seven days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column stands empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-five days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

He said it at about eleven in the morning in about nine people and he said it in about twenty words and then stopped.

What he said was that there is a boat standing off the point that is not one of the nine, that she is lying still, and that he is not going to describe her to anybody and he did not describe her.

He said that he wanted it on that page and he did not say why, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote down that a man of about thirty-three who hires hands says there is a boat off the point that is not one of the nine and that he is not going to describe her, and put the hour against it and wrote no name against the boat and no figure against her.

About four people in that nine said out loud that they would go and look and the man who hires hands said that they could all of them, and that he was not going to.

---

The keeper did not go and look.

He wrote on the Friday line of the page of that week that a boat is standing off the point that is not one of the nine and that this yard has not gone and looked at it and has not sent anybody, and that this yard will not go tomorrow either and does not know how it will not go the day after.

About nine people read that line standing up, and about four of them said out loud that it was a strange thing for a yard to write down.

---

Between about one and about four four people asked him what he was going to do about it.

He gave all four of them the same answer, being that this yard is not going to do anything about it, and that a boat off a point is not this yard's business, and that the page of routes in this harbor is not counted on any day but two of them and this is not either of those days.

The first of the four asked him whether he was not interested. He said that he was not uninterested and that he was not going, and that those are two different things and that a man who cannot tell the difference between them is a man who ends up going.

Nobody in that yard went and looked at that boat today and nobody went up that coast road to ask anybody about her, and the Thursday line of that page carries nothing about her and this yard is not going to go and put something there.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender came down that lane with a coil over one shoulder and went into that shed and hung it on its own nail.

She did not say anything to anybody and about six people were in that shed and none of them said anything to her, and she stayed about two minutes and went back up the lane.

Nobody asked her about the boat and she was not asked about the coil and the keeper wrote the hour of her coming down and going up and wrote nothing else.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Friday line on the page of that week and it says four things, being that a boat off the point that is not one of the nine was put on that page at about eleven by a man who said he was not going to describe her, that this yard did not go and did not send anybody, that about four people were told separately that nothing was going to be done about it, and that a coil came back onto its own nail at about half past two and nothing was said.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and nineteen words, to about nine people, and about five of those nine had asked him what he was going to do about it during the afternoon.

"**A boat is standing off the point that is not one of the nine. A man who hires hands told me that at about eleven this morning in about twenty words and then stopped, and he has not described her to me and I have not asked him to, and that is written on that page with the hour against it and no name against the boat. Nothing about her is on that page except that she is there, and that is all any page in this harbor has got about her. Nobody in that yard has gone and looked at her today and nobody went up that coast road to ask anybody about her either. I am going to say the whole of that again this evening so that nobody in this market thinks a slip is where I put things I could not say.**

**And four people asked me between one and four this afternoon what I was going to do about it and I told all four of them that I am not going to do anything about it. One of them asked me whether I was not interested and I said that I am not uninterested and that I am not going, and that those are two different things. I want that said out loud because I think that is the hardest sentence this yard has had to say since the second of May and I do not think anybody has noticed.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and ten days without one.**

**And about yesterday. That paper with the seal on it wanted a person standing on a line from the first day of next month and nobody has been put on a line and nobody has been asked to be, and the name under that seal is still in no mouth, and the page of hands stands at fifteen where it stood on Wednesday and is not going to carry a custodian.**

**And I am not going to end this slip on the boat, because a slip that ends on a boat is a slip that is asking a question, and this yard has spent forty-six days working out what it does not have any business asking about. This yard does not decide where any boat goes. It is not the body that decides that and it is never going to be the body that decides that, and a yard that goes and stands off a point on the strength of one man not describing a hull is a yard that has started to be another thing.**

**And the last of it. There is nothing on that page about that boat except that she is there. That is what today was.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for not looking at a boat, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seven lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS SAID IN THAT SHED, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND IN ABOUT TWENTY WORDS AND THEN STOPPED, THAT THERE IS A BOAT STANDING OFF THE POINT THAT IS NOT ONE OF THE NINE, THAT SHE IS LYING STILL, AND THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO DESCRIBE HER, AND THAT HE WANTED IT ON THAT PAGE, AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF WROTE IT DOWN WITH THE HOUR AGAINST IT AND WROTE NO NAME AGAINST THE BOAT AND NO FIGURE AGAINST HER. THAT YARD DID NOT GO AND LOOK AT HER AND DID NOT SEND ANYBODY, AND IT WROTE ON THE FRIDAY LINE OF THAT WEEK THAT IT WILL NOT GO TOMORROW EITHER AND DOES NOT KNOW HOW IT WILL NOT GO THE DAY AFTER. ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT NINE SAID OUT LOUD THAT THEY WOULD GO AND LOOK AND THE MAN WHO HIRES HANDS SAID THAT THEY COULD ALL OF THEM. THE PAGE OF ROUTES IN THAT HARBOR WAS NOT COUNTED ON THIS DAY AND THE THREE COLUMNS OF IT WERE NOT COUNTED ON THIS DAY. A COIL CAME BACK ONTO ITS OWN NAIL IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND NOTHING WAS SAID AND NOBODY SAID ANYTHING TO HER. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**
