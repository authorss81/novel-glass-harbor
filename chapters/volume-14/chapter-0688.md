# Chapter 688

*Wednesday the Eighth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, About Four in the Morning a Woman of About Thirty-Three Who Stands in the Tender Bending a Wire Into the Ring of Her Own Line With Her Own Hands and Keeping the Loose End of Herself, About Nine Minutes Past Six the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor Going Down Off That Mole in About Four Fathoms, the Twenty-Five Minutes Written Down on Monday and on the Morning, and at About Six and Nine Minutes Past Six in the Morning the Cutoff Pulled by the Woman on the Surface Without Asking Anybody and Nobody Arguing With Her and the Man on the Steps of That Tender About Two Minutes Later, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-One and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Wednesday the eighth day of June, with that market shut for the morning and standing open from about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. At about four in the morning a woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender bent a wire into the ring of her own line with her own hands and kept the loose end of herself. At about six the man of about thirty-two of that harbor went down off that mole in about four fathoms and at about six and nine minutes the cutoff was pulled by the woman on the surface without asking anybody, and nobody argued with her, and he was on the steps of that tender about two minutes later. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on that coast went a day further today and one of them was about a man of about twenty-nine who is not on the water. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and eighty-sixth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-eighth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and twenty-sixth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-second night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and tenth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and sixty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eightieth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-seventh day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-eighth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-sixth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water, and what happened off that mole this morning did not touch that hearing and did not improve it.**

---

About four in the morning it was still dark and the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender went out on the steps of that tender with a piece of wire in her hand and no light but the one off that wheelhouse.

About four people were on that boat. Nobody has said who they were and this yard has not asked.

She bent the wire into the ring of her own line with her own hands and she kept the loose end of herself, and nobody on that boat saw her do it and nobody on that boat was asked to.

---

At about nine the man who keeps the leaf wrote down which five, at the hour he said on Monday he would, and about nine people were standing at that bench stone when he did it.

Two of the five were named by their own communities on Monday in five different hands and three of them had come back on the Tuesday in a second sheet each, and one of those three was the paper that said the person had not yet said and would be asked again on Wednesday morning, and that person had been asked again at about seven this morning and had said that they would.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote the five names down at about nine in the morning and then put the five pieces of paper face down on that stone again and wrote on the face of the page of that week that five names had come from five communities and that no name on that line is a name against a limit and that the page of hands stands at fifteen and was not touched.

Nobody thanked him for writing them down and about four people in that nine read over his shoulder.

---

At about six the man of about thirty-two of that harbor went down off that mole in about four fathoms and there were five other people on that water and each one of them was on a line of their own and about nine feet from the next one.

Nobody was asked to hold anything. That was written at the bottom of all nine papers on Wednesday last and it was said again on Saturday and it was in the keeper's mouth at about nine in the morning in front of nine people, and no figure was set against any of the five.

The twenty-five minutes was on a piece of paper in that shed and in the inside of her coat and on the face of the page of that week, and it was twenty-five minutes at about six in the morning and not twenty-five minutes from whenever somebody decided it was time.

---

He was down about nine minutes.

At about six and nine minutes past six the woman on the surface pulled the cutoff, which was a wire she had bent into the ring of her own line with her own hands at about four that morning and whose loose end she had kept herself, and she pulled it without asking anybody and without waiting for anybody's answer and without saying a word first.

Nobody in about nine people on that boat argued with her and nobody asked her whether she was sure and nobody said she had come up early.

About four of those nine people on that boat had been on that coast for twenty years and about nine people in that market at about ten that morning said the same sentence about it, which was that she had said on Friday that she would do it and that she did it.

He was on the steps of that tender about two minutes later and he came up the ladder with his own hands and nobody helped him and he was not carrying anything.

---

At about ten past six he was sitting on the gunwale of that tender with a blanket round him and he could not hear the woman who was standing over him, and he said so himself, out loud, twice, and she said it again louder and he could not hear that either.

That is the fourth thing that is in this morning and none of the three that came before it is any more important than it is.

Nobody in about nine people on that boat wrote anything down and nobody in that market put anything on paper about it until about eleven, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote it at about eleven in front of about nine people, put the hour against it, and wrote no figure against it.

At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard asked him one question, being whether he could hear the water, and he said that he could not hear the water and that he had heard the wire go before that.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Wednesday line on the page of that week and it is the longest line on that sheet, being eleven sentences, and it says what happened at about four, at about six, at about six and nine, at about ten past six and at about half past six, and it says that the twenty-five minutes was twenty-five minutes and that the time in the water was about nine, and that no figure of money was set against the aftertaste or against the knee or against the hearing or against any part of the morning.

He wrote that about four fathoms is not deep and that no figure of fathoms is set against any other figure of fathoms on any page of that sheet.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody was called anything by anybody in that market that day, and one man in about eleven people at about three in the afternoon said a word that this yard did not write down and did not repeat and is not putting on that sheet.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about eight hundred and thirty-nine words, to about nine people, and about six of those nine had been at that mole head before seven that morning.

"**Nine minutes in the water and twenty-five minutes on the paper and the woman pulled it at about six and nine and nobody argued with her and nobody asked her if she was sure. I am going to give you the numbers first and then the whole of the morning, and I am not going to put a figure of money against one minute of it, and if anybody in this market has come here this evening with a penny in their hand I would be obliged if they put it back in it.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and one days without one.**

**What is proved. Two things and two only, and I am going to say them the way they were said to me and no better than that. One: no single centre held it. Five people on five lines about nine feet apart and not one of them asked to hold anything, and it stood up anyway for about nine minutes. Two: he could be withdrawn rather than owned. He came up because a woman pulled a wire and not because the thing was finished, and if she had pulled it at six minutes he would have come up at six minutes.**

**And what is not proved and what I am not going to be asked about in the next hour. Not proved that this works. Not proved that five is the right number. Not proved that a gap is a thing that can be filled by a person, and I have heard the word in a market in the last two days and I am not going to put it on a wall. Nobody is holding anything. He was there and it did what it did and it will not be told to do it again on Friday.**

**And the cost, and this is the part I want nine people to write down if they want to write anything down at all. There is an aftertaste on him and he has had one of his own for weeks and this one is on top of it and it has not lifted the other one. There is a knee in that left knee of his that a pressure bell should not have touched and that bell was not on that water and this yard is not going to be told which bell. And from about two o'clock this afternoon he has not been sleeping, and this is the first of three nights and I am writing the hours of it down and I am not putting a figure against any of the three.**

**And the hearing. He cannot hear out of the left side since about ten past six this morning and he told me so twice himself before I asked him anything. That is a different hearing from the man of about twenty-nine in this harbor, who is on his one hundred and eighty-sixth day today, and it is a different man on a different morning, and neither one of those two figures has anything to do with the other one and I am not going to let anybody in this market set one against the other. The man of about twenty-nine is not on the water and was not on the water and did not improve by one day because of anything that happened this morning.**

**And the last of it, and then I will stop for the day. Nobody is in a room off this coast because of anything that happened this morning and there is nobody on that coast who will not be at that end stall on Friday. Nobody was hurt in that water by anything except a knee that should not have been touched. Nobody has been thanked and this yard is not going to let anybody put a word on him that he did not put on himself, and if somebody in this market wants to put a word on that man this evening they can come down here and say it to his face and not to a slip.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nine minutes, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had eight lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

AT ABOUT NINE IN THE MORNING THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF WROTE DOWN WHICH FIVE, AT THE HOUR HE SAID ON MONDAY HE WOULD, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND THEN PUT THE FIVE PIECES OF PAPER FACE DOWN ON THAT STONE AGAIN, AND THE PAGE OF HANDS STANDS AT FIFTEEN AND WAS NOT TOUCHED. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**
