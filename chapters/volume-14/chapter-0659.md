# Chapter 659

*Tuesday the Tenth Day of May, the Eighty-First Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Eighty-First Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Eighty-First Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, a Second Page Going Up at About Three in the Afternoon Beside the Sheet Under the Stone Carrying the Eight Lines of That Requirement on One Side and What This Coast Did Instead Beside Each of Them and Saying on Its Own Face That It Does Not Say That Requirement Is False, One Name on One of the Two Pages and Only Against the Line That Says Nobody Knows, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Ninety-Two and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Tuesday the tenth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-first wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. The column on the lid of that step stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-first week stands fallen due at the close of that day. At about three in the afternoon a second page went up beside the sheet under the stone, and it carries the eight lines of that requirement on one side and, against each of them, what this coast did instead, and it says on its own face that it does not say that requirement is false and does not say that requirement was wrong about anything anybody has checked, and no name is on it against any of the seven lines that carry a fact, and one name is on it against the line that says nobody knows. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past, three hundred and fifty-seven days. A bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and fifty-nine. Two objects of nobody's on that crown, six hundred and ninety-seven days. The bench under that pump, bare for the four hundred and sixty-third night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and eighty-one days. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and thirty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fifty-first. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and sixty-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one, two hundred and ninety-nine days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. Those four lines have not come out of an upper room over that market and nothing has ever been written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with the paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fifty-seven days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in four minutes and it did not move, and behind the eighty-first stands behind the eightieth and behind the seventy-ninth, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four and has stood shut on every Tuesday since the frost.

Nobody in that market has ever asked the man of about thirty-one of the second lay what he thinks about reading the same lid out, and he is not on the water, and nobody in that market is going to start.

---

At about three in the afternoon the second page went up on those boards beside the sheet under the stone, on four tacks, and it is the same size as the sheet under the stone.

The left half of it carries the eight lines of the requirement, in a hand that is not the hand of the woman of thirty of no office and not the hand of any person in this harbor, and she copied them out herself and she did not alter one of them and she checked the copy against the original twice.

The right half carries what this coast did instead, in one line against each of the eight lines, and there are eight of those and they are these.

1. *Three lines went up on a wall this week and no custodian was on any of them.*
2. *Eight lines went up on that same wall on a page of their own and no name is on that page.*
3. *Two nights in a back room off a chandler's counter, in an earlier month, and a page of hours.*
4. *Nothing on any of our surfaces records who paid for that room.*
5. *One line on a money sheet since the twentieth of April and no second line.*
6. *Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on a lid, and the figure has not moved on eighty-one Tuesdays.*
7. *Twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, under a slate.*
8. *It is not known. That is put down because that is what is there.*

And at the foot of that page there are six lines of its own, and the fourth of them says this.

**That this page does not say the sheet under the stone is false. That it does not say the sheet under the stone was wrong about anything anybody has checked. That a requirement which cannot be paid is not a rule and is an argument. And an argument has to be had in nine places at once, and there are about nine people in this harbor who can be in nine places at once, and they are tired.**

Nobody agreed with that and nobody argued with it and about four people in that nine heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, and about eleven people in that market cannot read either page and were not asked about either of them.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two asked, in about nine people, that not one name go on that page, and gave about fifty words for it.

"**Put no name on that page. Not one. If there is a name on it then somebody will come along in nine months and read it as a charge, and it will not be a charge, and there will be nothing on the page to say that it was not.**"

About nine of them went on it without a name and one did not.

The one that did not is the eighth, which says it is not known. That one has a name against it and the name is the man who keeps the leaf, because he put that sentence down and a page on which the only named thing is the man who wrote it is a page he cannot pretend he found.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about two hundred and seventy words, to about nine people.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April and the last hand to touch it was on the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and there is no second line and there is not going to be one. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street and it did not move today. That butt behind that market stood covered from eight until four, because a rota day is a Monday and a Thursday and today is neither. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on that lid for the eighty-first time, read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay, and the eighty-first week stands fallen due at the close of today, and behind it stand the eightieth and the seventy-ninth and seventy-seven before those, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them.**"

**And a second page went up on those boards this afternoon and it does not say one thing that page under that stone does not itself say. Every line of it is a fact about this coast and every one of them can be checked by somebody standing there. And the fourth line at the foot of it says that a requirement which cannot be paid is not a rule and is an argument, and that an argument has to be had in nine places at once, and there are about nine people in this harbor who can be in nine places at once, and they are tired. I did not write that line and I am not going to agree with it on a Tuesday.**

**And the eighth line against that requirement says it is not known, and it has my name against it and every other line on that page has none, and I asked this afternoon that not one name go on it, and that is the only name on either of those two pages and it is against the sentence that says nobody knows. That is not modesty. That is the only arrangement I could think of that lets a page carry eight facts and no accusations, because if my name is against the row where we do not know then nothing on either page can be read as a charge against anybody, including the man who drew that sheet up in the first place, whose name is in no mouth in this harbor and is going to stay in no mouth in this harbor.**

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a second page, and that shed stood empty by about six, and those two pages stood side by side on those boards, one under a stone and one with four tacks in it, and neither of them carried a single name except one.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE EIGHTY-FIRST WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. A SECOND PAGE WENT UP ON THOSE BOARDS AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON BESIDE THE SHEET UNDER THE STONE, CARRYING THE EIGHT LINES OF THAT REQUIREMENT ON ONE SIDE AND WHAT THIS COAST DID INSTEAD BESIDE EACH OF THEM IN FROM ELEVEN TO TWENTY-TWO WORDS A LINE, AND SAYING ON ITS OWN FACE THAT IT DOES NOT SAY THAT REQUIREMENT IS FALSE AND DOES NOT SAY IT WAS WRONG ABOUT ANYTHING ANYBODY HAS CHECKED, AND THAT A REQUIREMENT WHICH CANNOT BE PAID IS NOT A RULE AND IS AN ARGUMENT, AND AN ARGUMENT HAS TO BE HAD IN NINE PLACES AT ONCE. NO NAME IS ON THAT PAGE AGAINST ANY OF THE SEVEN LINES THAT CARRY A FACT, AND ONE NAME IS ON IT AGAINST THE LINE THAT SAYS IT IS NOT KNOWN, AND THAT NAME IS THE NAME OF THE MAN WHO WROTE THAT LINE DOWN. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the tenth day of May at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the eighty-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that the butt behind that market was covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it; and that the column on the lid of that step was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four; and that the eighty-first week stands fallen due at the close of that day; and that at about three in the afternoon a second page went up on the boards at the back of that market on four tacks beside the sheet under the stone and it is the same size as that sheet; and that the left half of it carries the eight lines of that requirement copied out by the woman of thirty of no office without one word of them altered and checked against the original twice, and that the right half carries what this coast did instead in one line against each of those eight lines; and that at the foot of that page there are six lines of its own and that the fourth of them says that a requirement which cannot be paid is not a rule and is an argument, and that an argument has to be had in nine places at once, and that there are about nine people in this harbor who can be in nine places at once and they are tired; and that nobody agreed with that and nobody argued with it and about four people in that nine heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told, and that about eleven people in that market cannot read either page and were not asked about either of them; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two asked, in about nine people and in about fifty words, that not one name go on that page, and that about nine of them went on it without a name and one did not, and that the one that did not is the line against that requirement which says it is not known, and that it has the name of this yard's own keeper against it; and that nothing went onto that money sheet and nothing came off it, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that the first column takes one entry a day and three hundred and eighty-three and one is three hundred and ninety-two at the close of it, and that the second takes five a day and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and five is one thousand eight hundred and thirty-four.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason I have printed the number of the Tuesday this yard is on, being the eighty-first, in every one of these pages since the frost, is that it is the only figure about the passage of time in this harbor that a stranger can check against a calendar, and that everything else in this yard runs on whether a person feels the difference between this month and last month, and a person does not; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason I copied those eight lines out twice myself before the woman of thirty of no office copied them is that a page which answers a requirement and gets one of its eight lines wrong in a single word is worthless, and that there is no way on this coast to check a copy against the original except two men with two sticks of chalk sitting down together, which is what this yard did and it took about forty minutes; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason I put the answer on the same surface as the requirement is that a coast that answers a requirement on a different page from the one the requirement is on is a coast that has a private conversation with an office, and this harbor has not got the people for a private conversation with an office; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason my name is against the line that says it is not known and against nothing else is that I asked that no name go on that page and the honest position of a keeper of a page who is asked to hold a fact about his own yard and cannot supply it is to put his name against the gap and not against the facts, and that if I had put it against all eight then the whole page would have been one man's account of himself, and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason I did not agree with the fourth of those six lines when it was said out loud is that it says those nine people are tired and I have not asked them whether they are tired and I am not going to, and that a keeper of a page who writes down that people are tired has started keeping a list of how people are, and this yard has one of those already and it is the wrong one; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason this slip opens on the lid and not on those two pages is that those two pages were put up this afternoon and can be walked up to and read, and the lid cannot, and a man who is going to write down one figure a stranger cannot walk up to, and about forty people have just walked past it this morning, is a man who has picked the worst one on purpose, and I did, because it is the only figure in this harbor that has been the same on every one of sixty-eight days and I want that on the top of this slip where I have to look at it.*
