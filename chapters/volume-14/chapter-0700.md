# Chapter 700

*Monday the Twentieth Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Ninth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Laying on It From About Eleven Until About Four, and at About Two in the Afternoon the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Reading That Leaf Out Loud in the Open at the Flat of That Bench Stone, All Four Columns and the Third One of Them Empty, Twice, With Both Definitions in the Same Words and the Same Mouth as Every Other Read-Back, and His Own Derivation on the Face of the Reading, the Base of It Being Three Hundred and Eighty-Three and One Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Nine at the Close of the First of May and Not Four Hundred and Twenty-Three and One Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Nine, the Count at the Hour of the Reading Being Four Hundred and Thirty-Two and Two Thousand and Thirty-Seven and the Figures at the Close of the Day Being Four Hundred and Thirty-Three and Two Thousand and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the Count of the Three Columns of the Page of Routes Written Down at About Half Past Three for the Second of Twice and Not Said Out Loud, and at About Half Past Four a Question Asked Once in a Working Woman's Mouth at the End Stall of That Market and Answered by Nobody, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand and Thirty-Nine*

Monday the twentieth day of June, with about fifty people on the boards at the back of that market by eleven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-ninth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf read that leaf out loud in the open at the flat of that bench stone, all four columns and the third of them empty, twice, in about nine people of whom about five cannot read a column, and both definitions were given in the same words and the same mouth as every other read-back this yard has done, and his own derivation was printed on the face of the reading and the base of it is three hundred and eighty-three and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine at the close of the first of May and not the figures of last Friday. At the hour of that reading the leaf stood at four hundred and thirty-two and two thousand and thirty-seven and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and at the close of this day it stands at four hundred and thirty-three and two thousand and thirty-nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five. At about half past three in the afternoon the count of the three columns of the page of routes was written down for the second of twice and was not said out loud. At about half past four in the afternoon a question was asked once, at the end stall of that market, in the mouth of a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter, and nobody answered it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters ran the fiftieth day of this yard's fifty and not one of the nine has anything to do with anything that happened today. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-eighth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for four hundred days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-eighth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fourth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-second days with no figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and ninth days. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and fortieth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column of the leaf is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in that harbor and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has put a hand in it. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-eighth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and fifty-ninth time, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and that is the last of them on the rota of this yard.

The four men who carry it have not been asked once since the Thursday before the sixteenth of June, and they have carried it anyway, and about four of them said this morning that nobody had asked them since the week before last and that they were not going to start.

---

At about two in the afternoon that leaf was read out in the open at the flat of that bench stone.

The man who keeps the leaf read it twice and about nine people were there and about five of them cannot read a column. Nobody was asked to read it a third time and nobody asked him to.

He read the four columns off the head of the leaf, and the first column and the second column and the fourth column and, between the second and the fourth, the third one, which is empty, and he read that one as empty, out loud, both times.

The figures at the hour of that reading were four hundred and thirty-two and two thousand and thirty-seven and none and one hundred and twenty-five.

---

Then he said where the figures come from, out loud, in about nine people, and the derivation went on the face of the page in front of about four of them.

He said that three hundred and eighty-three were on that leaf before the first of those fifty days, that one has gone in on each of the fifty days from Monday the second day of May to this day, and that not one of those fifty days has not got an entry against it, and that three hundred and eighty-three and fifty is four hundred and thirty-three at the close of it, and that the entry against this day goes in after that reading and not before it, so the count at the hour of that reading is four hundred and thirty-two.

He said that five have gone in on each of those same fifty days, so that of the two hundred and fifty that have gone in, two hundred and forty-five went in on the forty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and that two hundred and fifty less two is two hundred and forty-eight, and that one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and forty-eight is two thousand and thirty-seven at the hour of that reading, and that one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and fifty is two thousand and thirty-nine at the close of it.

About four of those nine wrote it down and about four of them asked him to say the second one again and he said it again.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the page of routes off its nail and wrote three figures at the head of its three columns, being one and four and four.

One held, four restricted, four dormant, over nine names, and the held one is the Saltmarch Weatherway alone, and the four that are restricted are the Tidewatch Channel, the Ashtide Run, the Ternwatch Passage and the Lowfen Reed, and the four that are dormant are the Morrow Line, the Cinder Bell, the Pale Shoals and the Orison Descent. One and four and four is nine.

He put the day and the hour against those three figures and did not say them out loud and nobody in that shed asked him for them, and they are not in the slip of this evening and they are not in the first column in words.

That is the second time those three figures have been written down and the first and only other time was the twenty-first of May.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter asked a question at the end stall of that market.

She sells rope and she has a counter and she has a right to stand at that stall, and she said it once, out loud, in about eleven people, and about four of them cannot read a notice and heard all of it.

What she said was that when the last connection cannot be cut safely, can a man stay connected on his own terms without putting one person's testimony back in the middle of the world.

Nobody in that eleven answered her. About four of them looked at each other and one of them said nothing and one of them said something that was not an answer and about four did not hear the end of it.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote it into the market's book as asked, at about half past four, with the hour against it and no name against it, and he did not answer it and nobody in that market answered it.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Monday line on the page of that week and it says four things, being that that leaf was read out in the open twice with its own derivation on the face of the reading, that the three columns of the page of routes were written down for the second of twice and were not said out loud, that a question was asked once at the end stall and nobody answered it and it is in the market's book as asked with the hour against it and no name against it, and that the entry against today goes into the first column of that leaf after the reading and not before it.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about nine hundred and sixty-six words, to about nine people, and about five of those nine had been standing at that stone at about two in the afternoon for the reading.

"**I read that leaf out loud in the open this afternoon at about two, twice, all four columns, and I read the third one as empty both times because it is empty and it is going to keep on being empty until somebody who has a right to fill it does, and there is no such person in this harbor and none is going to be named in one. About five of the nine standing there cannot read a column and the second reading was for them and not for the four who can.**

**And here is where the figures come from, and I am saying this out loud because I have been writing into that leaf for fifty days and I have never once said where it started. Three hundred and eighty-three were on that leaf before the first of those fifty days. One has gone in on each of the fifty days from Monday the second of May to this day and not one of those fifty days has not got an entry against it. Three hundred and eighty-three and fifty is four hundred and thirty-three at the close of it, and the entry against today goes in after that reading and not before it, and so the count at the hour of that reading is four hundred and thirty-two.**

**The second column is the one that cannot be checked from a mole. Five go in on each of those same fifty days. Of the two hundred and fifty that have gone in, two hundred and forty-five went in on the forty-nine days before this one and three went in this morning before this hour, and two hundred and fifty less two is two hundred and forty-eight, and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and forty-eight is two thousand and thirty-seven at the hour of that reading, and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and two hundred and fifty is two thousand and thirty-nine at the close of it. So the leaf at this moment reads four hundred and thirty-two and two thousand and thirty-seven and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and it will read four hundred and thirty-three and two thousand and thirty-nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five when this evening's entry goes in.**

**And I am going to say the thing nobody in this market has noticed. I did not take those figures off last Friday's figures to make that arithmetic come out. I took them off three hundred and eighty-three and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine, which is what that leaf said before the second of May, and a man who took them off last Friday would have had to add his own ten days twice and his own figure would not have come out of it, and about four people in that nine would not have caught him and about four would have.**

**Three figures went on the head of the three columns of another page at about half past three this afternoon and they are not said out loud and they are not in this slip and they are on that page with the day and the hour against them, and that is the second time and the only other time was the twenty-first of May.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirteen days without one.**

**And a question was asked at the end stall of this market at about half past four this afternoon, once, in about eleven people, by a woman who sells rope, and nobody in that eleven answered it, and it is in that market's book as asked with the hour against it and no name against it, and I am not going to answer it either and I am not going to put it in this slip. It is written in a book at that stall and a book at that stall has been read by strangers for thirty years and this is the first question that has gone into one.**

**And the last of it. The proof is still in its case in a room over that fish market and has not been read out in fifty days. The restricted remainder is still sealed and a claim to it is neither exercised nor released. That page in that shed still carries two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster between them, and four names are off the page of hands and the figures under them can still be read. The hole in the Tuesday of a man of about thirty-four of the second lay is still a hole and nobody has filled it and nobody has asked him anything. Under that slate is twenty-eight pence and there is a page of hours in that harbor that nobody has converted into a man.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for fifty days, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had one line on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT LEAF WAS READ OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN AT THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON, TWICE, ALL FOUR COLUMNS AND THE THIRD OF THEM EMPTY, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT FIVE CANNOT READ A COLUMN, WITH BOTH DEFINITIONS GIVEN IN THE SAME WORDS AND THE SAME MOUTH AS EVERY OTHER READ-BACK, AND WITH ITS OWN DERIVATION PRINTED ON THE FACE OF THE READING, THE BASE OF THAT DERIVATION BEING THREE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE AND ONE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINE AT THE CLOSE OF THE FIRST OF MAY AND NOT THE FIGURES OF THE TENTH OF JUNE, THE COUNT AT THE HOUR OF THAT READING BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO AND TWO THOUSAND AND THIRTY-SEVEN AND NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE, AND THE FIGURES AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THREE AND TWO THOUSAND AND THIRTY-NINE AND NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE. THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-NINTH, AND THE COVERS LAY ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. THREE FIGURES WENT AT THE HEAD OF THE THREE COLUMNS OF THE PAGE OF ROUTES AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON, BEING ONE AND FOUR AND FOUR, AND WERE NOT SAID OUT LOUD AND ARE NOT IN THAT SLIP. A QUESTION WAS ASKED ONCE AT THE END STALL OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE BY A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WITH A CHANDLER'S COUNTER AND WAS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND IT IS IN THAT MARKET'S BOOK AS ASKED WITH THE HOUR AGAINST IT AND NO NAME AGAINST IT. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**
