# Chapter 672

*Monday the Twenty-Third Day of May, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Second Charge on That Cistern at the Back of That Market, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It, the Covers Lying on It from About Eleven Until About Four in the Afternoon, a Sheet Hung on That Wall Beside the Two Lines at About Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People With the Seven Days of That Week Down the Side of It and Nothing Written Against Any of Them, and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying Out Loud What That Sheet Is and What It Is Not, the Man of About Fifty of the Lowfen Reed's Own Third Lay Not in That Yard at Ten in the Morning and Gone Up That Lane on His Own Account and Not Sent For by Anybody, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Five and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Monday the twenty-third day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-second charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about ten in the morning the man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay was not in that yard, and he had gone up that lane on his own account at about nine, and nobody in that yard sent for him and nobody knew where he had gone and the man who keeps the leaf did not ask. At about two in the afternoon a sheet went up on that wall beside the two lines that are on it, with the seven days of that week down the side of it and nothing written against any of the seven, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud, in about nine people, what that sheet is and what it is not. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things on this coast are carried on other people's backs, and this yard keeps none of the nine and has altered none of them. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and seventieth day. A bellwright of this harbor has gone unasked for three hundred and seventy-second days, and the chest is shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown have now stood seven hundred and tenth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-sixth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have gone two hundred and ninety-fourth days without a figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and forty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-fourth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-first day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twelfth days. On that leaf the third column has taken nothing and nobody has been put in it, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. That board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers, and two of them have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been put on that board this month. In that boathouse there is a basket with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventieth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and fifty-second, and that is the same eleven trips and the same four men and the same stairs on the inside of that wall.

The covers went on at about eleven and came off again at about four, and nothing was done to that water by anybody in this yard and nobody was paid for it out of anything in this yard.

---

At about ten in the morning the man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay was not in that yard.

He had gone up that lane on his own account at about nine. Nobody in that yard sent for him. Nobody in that market saw him go and nobody has said since where he went, and the man who keeps the leaf did not ask him and did not go after him, and that is written down with the hour against it because a page that records where a man of another route's own community has gone is a page about this yard's curiosity and not about this week's work.

---

At about two in the afternoon a sheet went up on that wall beside the two lines, on two tacks out of the man who keeps the leaf's own pocket, and the seven days of that week were written down the side of it, being Monday and Tuesday and Wednesday and Thursday and Friday and Saturday and Sunday, and nothing was written against any of the seven.

Then he read it out, in about nine people, and what he said was that it is the page of that week and it is the page of seven days and not the page of anything else, and that it is not the page of hands and it is not the page of routes and it is not the money sheet, and that he named those three separately and out loud in that nine because this harbor has put three different kinds of paper in one sentence before and got a fortnight out of it.

He said that a thing that happens on a day of that week goes on the line of that day, and that whoever it happens to is written in the doing of it and not in a column, and that no name goes against a day on that sheet and no state of any route goes against a day on that sheet.

He said that anybody in this harbor may read it and anybody in this harbor may write a line on it, and that it is read out in this shed at half past five every day this week whatever is on it.

About four people in that nine cannot read a column and the sheet was read to them and nobody asked whether they had understood it and nobody read it twice.

---

At about half past three a woman of about forty of the Cinder Bell's own community came down that lane for the fourth time this week and stopped at that yard on her way and stood at the end of the shed about a minute and a half and looked at the two lines and at the empty plaster and at the new sheet.

Nobody asked her anything. Nobody in that yard knew what she had come down that lane for and nobody asked, and she went up to the market again and did not come back into that yard.

That is written down with the hour against it and without a name against it, and it is written down because a woman of about forty standing in a doorway for a minute and a half is a thing nine people saw.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and seventy-seven words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been standing at that wall at about two.

"**That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning and the hundred and fifty-second time, and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four, and the four men who carried it have not been asked once in three years what the water is for and this yard is not going to be the one that starts.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is eighty-five days without one.**

**There is a new sheet on that wall and I hung it myself this afternoon out of my own pocket and I want to say exactly what it is, because this harbor has been wrong about sheets twice this month. It is the page of that week. Seven days down the side and nothing against any of them yet. It is not the page of hands, which counts the names of people who have put their names to a route's own limits, and it is not the page of routes, which carries a state against a route and has not moved by one since the first of May, and it is not the money sheet, and I have said those three out loud separately in nine people and I am not going to say them in one breath again for as long as I keep this leaf.**

**And here is the reason I hung it, and it is a small one. On Sunday a man stood at that end stall and said his route was one man short on Wednesday night, and he said he was not going to ask anybody in this yard, and this yard wrote it down and sent for nobody. If that kind of thing happens twice this week it is going to happen in about nine people standing in front of a page, and a page of seven days is cheaper than nine people arguing and it can be read afterwards. It is not a record of anything anybody has agreed to. It is a thing that can be looked at.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a sheet on a wall, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that new sheet had seven days down the side of it and nothing at all against any of the seven, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, IT BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SECOND CHARGE, AND THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON A SHEET WENT UP ON THAT WALL BESIDE THE TWO LINES THAT ARE ON IT, ON TWO TACKS OUT OF THE OWN POCKET OF THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF, AND THE SEVEN DAYS OF THAT WEEK WERE WRITTEN DOWN THE SIDE OF IT AND NOTHING WAS WRITTEN AGAINST ANY OF THE SEVEN, AND IT WAS READ OUT IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AS THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK AND WAS NAMED SEPARATELY AND OUT LOUD AGAINST THE PAGE OF HANDS, THE PAGE OF ROUTES AND THE MONEY SHEET. THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY OF THE LOWFEN REED'S OWN THIRD LAY WAS NOT IN THAT YARD AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AND HAD GONE UP THAT LANE ON HIS OWN ACCOUNT AT ABOUT NINE AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD SENT FOR HIM. THAT MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND AT TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, WHICH LEAVES IT ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP THIS WEEK AND NOTHING ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN MOVED BY ONE EITHER WAY. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason that cistern is written down this week as eleven trips and four men and no charge is that a rota which stops being checkable on the day somebody notices it has stopped being a rota, and that the number on that board has been walked on since the autumn and the only thing that would break it is a day when somebody writes down a different number; and that at about ten in the morning the reason that no hour is put against where the man of the Lowfen Reed went is that the day he went is on the sheet and the hour he went is in this column, and that a page of days is a page of days and not a page of a man's movements, and that if this yard starts writing down where men of other communities go when they leave, then in a month this yard has a list of people and this harbor has been refusing lists of people since before the frost; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason three other sheets were named out loud separately before that sheet went up is that this harbor put two kinds of paper in one sentence in the middle of May and cost about nine people a morning and a wall, and the naming costs four seconds and is the whole of the method; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason a woman standing in a doorway is entered without a name is that this yard has spent three weeks refusing to write down what people want and is now equally unwilling to write down where people have stood, and that a page which holds one of those will end up holding the other, and the day it does that is the day that page becomes something this coast did not agree to; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that sheet is going to be read out every day this week whatever is on it is that a page which is read out only when there is something on it worth saying is a page that gets read out when somebody wants it read out, and this coast has been on the receiving end of that from better-funded people than this yard.*
