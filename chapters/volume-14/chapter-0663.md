# Chapter 663

*Saturday the Fourteenth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and the Market Open for the Morning for the First Time in Six Days, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Standing at the End Stall With a List of Four Names She Read Out One at a Time and Not Asking Any of the Four Anything, and Three of the Four Hearing Their Own Name and One of the Four Not Being There at All, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Ninety-Six and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Saturday the fourteenth day of May, with that market open for the morning for the first time in six days and about sixty people on those boards before eleven, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. At about ten in the morning a woman of thirty of no office stood at the end stall of that market with a sheet in her hand and read out four names, one at a time, and then stood there, and did not ask any of the four anything, and did not say why she was reading them out. Three of the four were in that market and heard their own name. The fourth was not there at all and has not been in that market since before the frost. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the wooden tally slate is still lying flat on that bench stone where it was put down on the first of May, and nothing is on it.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood three hundred and sixty-one days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty-three days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and one days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and sixty-seventh night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and eighty-five days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fortieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and fifty-fifth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventy-second day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and three days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and in that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and sixty-one days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That market had been shut for the morning all week and was open all day on that Saturday, and about sixty people went over those boards before eleven, which is more than this harbor puts on them in a fortnight.

That is a market finding out whether it is a market and not a place where a thing happens.

---

At about ten in the morning a woman of thirty of no office took a sheet of paper to that end stall and stood on the flat of that bench stone and read out four names.

She read them one at a time and she read them at the volume she reads a price list at, and she did not say whose names they were or what they were for, and she did not say that anybody had done anything.

Three people in about sixty turned round. Two of them are of this harbor. The third is a man of about sixty-one and he is not of this harbor and came here in April and has a room over a chandler's shop two doors up that lane that he pays for by the week.

The fourth name was not answered by anybody, and that man of about sixty-one looked at her for a moment when she read it and then looked at the ground, and nobody in that market said a word.

---

She stood there for about two minutes afterwards and read the second half of the same sheet, which was not names. It was that four names stand against the Morrow Line on that page of days and that two of them are not on that route's own page.

She read that out in the same voice and in the same market and nobody answered it either.

---

At about half past ten a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came alongside that market and heard the second half of it from about thirty feet off, and he came up and said one thing, and what he said was that a name written on a page in this harbor does not follow a man around the coast like a debt does.

Nobody agreed with him and nobody argued with him. The man who keeps the leaf wrote down that he said it and wrote down the hour and wrote down no name against it.

---

At about one in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane asked the man who keeps the leaf, in about nine people, whether this yard was going to make a list of the four names in a book.

He said that it was not, and that there was no column in that yard for four names, and that the four names were on a page of days at the head of a line and were going to stay there and were not going anywhere else.

She said that a name that can be read out in a market is a name that has stopped belonging to the person, and he said that it had not stopped belonging to the person and that was exactly the trouble with it.

Neither of them said the next thing.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and sixty-eight words, to about nine people, and nobody in that shed had heard what was read out at that end stall that morning except about four of them.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence on that board in the front street, unmoved. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard has been seventy-nine days without one.**

**And four names were read out at that end stall at about ten this morning and about sixty people were on those boards and three of the four heard their own name and one of the four has not been in this harbor since before the frost. I did not know that yesterday afternoon. I knew two names were not on a page. This morning I found out that one of the four is a man of about sixty-one who came here in April and pays by the week over a chandler's shop, and that is a fact about a person and I did not go and get it.**

**I want to say the rest of it plainly because this yard has spent three days being careful and careful has started to sound like a virtue. Nothing this morning asked anybody anything. Nobody was asked. I did not ask that man of about sixty-one one question, and there is a thing I could have asked him on Tuesday in about four seconds, and I did not ask it because I was busy being careful, and being careful about a difference between two pieces of paper is not the same as being careful with a person who has just found out his name is on both of them.**

**The man of about fifty-three is right that a name does not follow a man. The woman of the chandler's counter is right that a name read out in a market has stopped belonging to the person. Those two men and that woman have not agreed with each other and they have not disagreed with each other, and that is the state of this harbor this afternoon, and there are four names on a page and a man of about sixty-one in a market hearing one of them.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four names read out in an open market, and that shed stood empty by about six, and about four people in that market of sixty had heard the whole of it, and the rest of that market was not told, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it and nobody picking it up.

---

**NOT A ROTA DAY AND NOT A WAGE TUESDAY AND NOT A WIPE, AND THAT MARKET WAS OPEN FOR THE MORNING FOR THE FIRST TIME IN SIX DAYS WITH ABOUT SIXTY PEOPLE ON THOSE BOARDS BEFORE ELEVEN, AND NO CHARGE WAS PLACED ON THAT CISTERN AND ITS COVERS LAY ON IT FROM NINE IN THE MORNING UNTIL FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. FOUR NAMES WERE READ OUT AT THE END STALL OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE, ONE AT A TIME, AND SHE ASKED NONE OF THE FOUR ANYTHING AND SAID NO SUCH THING AS WHY SHE WAS READING THEM OUT, AND THREE OF THE FOUR HEARD THEIR OWN NAME AND THE FOURTH WAS NOT THERE AND HAS NOT BEEN IN THAT HARBOR SINCE BEFORE THE FROST, AND THAT MAN IS ABOUT SIXTY-ONE AND CAME HERE IN APRIL AND PAYS BY THE WEEK OVER A CHANDLER'S SHOP. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS SAID OUT LOUD THAT A NAME WRITTEN ON A PAGE IN THIS HARBOR DOES NOT FOLLOW A MAN AROUND THE COAST LIKE A DEBT DOES. A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WITH A CHANDLER'S COUNTER ASKED WHETHER THIS YARD WAS GOING TO MAKE A LIST OF THE FOUR NAMES IN A BOOK AND WAS TOLD NO. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the four names were read out in the open at that end stall and not inside that shed is that a thing said inside a shed is said to about nine people and a thing said at a stall is said to about sixty, and that three people turning round is the only figure on that page that anybody in this harbor can check, because a man who is not there cannot turn round; and that at about half past ten in the morning the reason the second half of that sheet was read out at the same volume and in the same market and immediately after the first half is that if it had waited an hour then the four names and the difference would have gone into that market as two things, and that this yard wanted them to arrive as one thing, which is a decision and not a fact and I am writing it down as a decision; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason no book was started is that the four names are already on a page at the head of a line and a second copy of four names is not more information, it is more places to look in a month when somebody wants to know who signed what, and this harbor has refused six sheets of that kind and is not starting a seventh over a Saturday; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I wrote in this slip that I did not know on Friday afternoon that one of the four was a man of about sixty-one is that a yard which only writes down what it has established has stopped writing down what it has not, and the second of those two is the one a reader needs on a bad morning; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason the man of about fifty-three was not answered is that his sentence is the best one anybody has said in that yard this month and answering it would have made it smaller, and that the woman of the chandler's counter said the opposite thing at about one and neither of them has heard the other one, which is the actual condition of this harbor and not an oversight in it.*
