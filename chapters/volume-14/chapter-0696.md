# Chapter 696

*Thursday the Sixteenth Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Eighth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Laying on It From About Eleven Until About Four, a Paper With a Seal on It Coming Ashore at About One in the Afternoon Off a Boat From the West Basin and Not Being Read Out Until About Half Past Two on the Boards at the Back of That Market, Read Out Once and Not Twice, by a Man of About Thirty-Three Who Hires Hands, in Front of About Twenty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read It, and the Name Under the Seal Not Read Out and Not Written Down and Not Said in Any Mouth, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand and Nineteen*

Thursday the sixteenth day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-eighth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about one in the afternoon a paper with a seal on it came ashore off a boat out of the west basin and was put on the bench in that shed face down and nobody turned it over for an hour and a half. At about half past two it was read out once on those boards by a man of about thirty-three who hires hands, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it, and it was not read out a second time, and the name under the seal was not read out and was not written down and is in no mouth. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters in that harbor went a day further today and one of them is about a seal. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-fourth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and ninety-six days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-fourth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundredth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and eighteen days with no figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eighty-eighth day. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and five days. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-six days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-four days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and fifty-eighth time, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four.

Nobody has asked the four men who carry it what the water is for since the Thursday of the ninth of June, and they have carried it anyway, and this is the hundred and fifty-eighth time.

---

At about one in the afternoon a paper with a seal on it came ashore off a boat out of the west basin and was put on the bench in that shed face down.

The man of about thirty-three who hires hands was on that boat and brought it up himself and put it down himself and said one sentence, which was that it came out of the west basin in a basket with a lot of other papers in it and that he was not going to be the one to open it and that it wanted reading and not arguing with.

Nobody turned it over for an hour and a half. About four people in that yard looked at the face of it and said nothing about what was on it.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-three who hires hands read it out once on the boards at the back of that market.

There were about twenty people standing there and about eleven of them cannot read a notice. He read it out in a plain voice from the top to the bottom and he stopped at the seal and did not go on past it, and he did not read it a second time afterwards, and nobody asked him to.

What it says is this. It requires that a named living custodian stand on every line that carries a testimony out of this harbor, and that the custodian be named in writing and kept at the port where the testimony was made, and that a boat's account of what it carried and when be kept and produced on demand. It is dated at the top of it and it is sealed at the bottom of it and it says in four places that it is to be complied with from the first day of next month.

And it is correct. Every clause of it is correct, and it asks for nothing this coast has ever heard called wrong, and there is nothing in it to argue with, and nobody in that market argued with it.

---

About four people in that twenty said out loud after it was read that it costs a person.

A man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm said that a named living custodian on a line is a person who is not doing anything else, and that a person who is not doing anything else is not spare. A woman of about thirty-one who holds a particular said nothing at all and was written down as saying nothing at all. A man of about forty-six of the ninth community said that it does not say wrong anywhere and that he has been waiting all week for it to say wrong somewhere so that he could get a pen.

The man who hires hands said that he was not going to argue with it and that he read it because he hires men and that about eleven people standing here cannot read it and if it is not read out to them it is not in this harbor.

Nobody said that the paper was wrong. Nobody in that market has said that it was wrong since one o'clock this afternoon.

---

The name under the seal is on the paper and it is in no mouth.

It was not read out and it was not written down on any page in that yard and it was not said by anybody on those boards or in that shed between one o'clock this afternoon and six o'clock this evening, and the man who hires hands put the paper back into the basket it came in face down and carried it back up to the office on the west basin at about four and put it in at the counter, because that is where it came from.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Thursday line on the page of that week and it says three things, being that a paper with a seal on it came ashore at about one, that it was read out once on those boards at about half past two in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it, and that it was not read out a second time and that the name under the seal was not read.

He wrote against it that the paper is correct and that it costs a person, and he wrote no figure of money against the paper or against the person.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and forty-one words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood on those boards at about half past two this afternoon and heard it read.

"**A paper with a seal on it came ashore at about one this afternoon and was read out once at about half past two on those boards, out loud, by a man who hires hands, in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it, and it was not read out a second time. That is the first document any of us has had to stand in front of in forty-five days and the whole of what I have to say about it can be said in two sentences. It is correct. And it costs a person.**

**I want both of those said again by me because nobody in that market has said the second one with any heat in it and nobody said the first one at all. Not one person said the paper was wrong. Four people said the other thing, that a named living custodian on a line is a person who is not doing anything else, and a man of about forty-six said he has been waiting all week for it to be wrong somewhere so that he could get a pen, and I want it in a slip of this yard's own that the paper was correct on the day it came ashore and that nobody here has found anything wrong with it.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and nine days without one.**

**And the seal. There is a name under the seal at the bottom of that paper and it was not read out this afternoon and it is not written down in this yard and it is not in anybody's mouth, and that is not squeamishness and it is not manners. A name said out loud on those boards at half past two on a Thursday is a name this harbor has by Friday and cannot get back, and the whole of what those nine pages are is words that somebody chose and nobody was required to.**

**And nobody wrote a line about that paper on the fitting-out sheet. Nobody wrote a line about it on the page of hands. The page of hands stands at fifteen where it stood this morning. It is not going to carry a custodian and it is not going to carry a seal, because a page of hands is a count of names against nine written limits and a custodian is neither a name against a limit nor a limit.**

**And the last of it. That paper wants a person standing on a line from the first day of next month and this yard is not going to be the body that decides who that person is on any line, and it is not going to be the body that decides where any boat goes, and it is not going to argue with a document in a market instead of costing it.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a document read once, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had six lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A PAPER WITH A SEAL ON IT CAME ASHORE AT ABOUT ONE IN THE AFTERNOON OFF A BOAT OUT OF THE WEST BASIN AND WAS PUT ON THE BENCH IN THAT SHED FACE DOWN, AND NOBODY TURNED IT OVER FOR AN HOUR AND A HALF. AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IT WAS READ OUT ONCE ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO HIRES HANDS, IN FRONT OF ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT ELEVEN CANNOT READ IT, AND IT WAS NOT READ OUT A SECOND TIME AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO READ IT AGAIN. IT REQUIRES A NAMED LIVING CUSTODIAN ON EVERY LINE CARRYING A TESTIMONY OUT OF THAT HARBOR, NAMED IN WRITING AND KEPT AT THE PORT WHERE THE TESTIMONY WAS MADE, TOGETHER WITH A BOAT'S ACCOUNT OF WHAT IT CARRIED AND WHEN TO BE KEPT AND PRODUCED, AND IT IS DATED AT THE TOP OF IT AND SEALED AT THE BOTTOM OF IT AND SAYS IN FOUR PLACES THAT IT IS TO BE COMPLIED WITH FROM THE FIRST DAY OF NEXT MONTH. IT IS CORRECT AND IT COSTS A PERSON, AND NOBODY IN THAT MARKET SAID THAT IT WAS WRONG. THE NAME UNDER THE SEAL WAS NOT READ OUT AND WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN ON ANY PAGE IN THAT YARD AND IS IN NO MOUTH. THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHTH, AND THE COVERS LAY ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about one in the afternoon the reason that paper lay face down for an hour and a half is that a thing which arrives in a basket with other papers in it is not a thing anybody in this yard has standing to treat as an event, and that turning a sealed paper over is the first of four small decisions and each of the other three is worse; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason it was read once and not twice is that about eleven of the twenty cannot read it and that reading it a second time is a thing a person does with a thing he has not understood, and that a second reading would also have meant a second reading of the name under the seal or an explanation of why not, and this yard does not have an explanation of why not that it is willing to say out loud; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the paper is written as correct on the face of this page is that the whole of this coast's argument for forty-five days has been that a correct requirement costs a person, and that a page which recorded the cost of it would leave a stranger with a document and a grievance and nothing about whether the document was any good, and a stranger who has only the grievance will print the grievance and not the document in twenty years; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on what this yard is not going to be the body to decide is that a requirement which cannot be paid does not become a rule, it becomes an argument that has to be had in nine places at once, and that there are nine places and one shed and a shed is the wrong shape for nine arguments and this yard knows it now.
