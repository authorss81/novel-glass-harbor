# Chapter 681

*Wednesday the First Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and About Nine Route Teams Asked One Thing Each Between About Ten in the Morning and About Two in the Afternoon and the Nine Answers Not Yet Come Back From Six of Them, That Cistern Not Charged and Its Covers Lying on It From Nine Until Four, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fourteen and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Forty-Four*

Wednesday the first day of June, with that market trading all day and about fifty people on the boards at the back of it by eleven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind that market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. Between about ten in the morning and about two in the afternoon nine route teams were asked one thing each, and one thing each is what they were asked and not two things and not a speech, and six of the nine answers had not come back by the close of the day. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on that coast went a day further today and not one of the nine was carried by this yard. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and seventy-ninth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-first days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and nineteenth day. The bench under that pump has been bare for the four hundred and eighty-fifth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and third day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninetieth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-first days. The third column of that leaf has taken nothing and nobody has been put in it, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of them have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-ninth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That was the first day of June and it was a Wednesday, and for the first time in four weeks this yard had nine questions of its own going out at the same time instead of one coming in.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote them out himself, nine of them, one on each of nine pieces of paper off the same pad, and he wrote the same eleven words at the top of each one and then the one thing underneath, so that any stranger reading two of the nine would find the same question on both.

The eleven words at the top of each were the same eleven words that were on the Friday line of the page of that week, being that this coast can be cut in one place at a time, and he wrote them at the top of all nine because he was not going to send nine people into nine markets carrying nine different arguments.

---

The one thing underneath was not the same in all nine and it was about four lines long in each.

He wrote to each of the nine that a boat of about thirty tons would be hired at the figure in chalk for two days off that mole in the second week of this month, and that about four fathoms of the same bottom would be held for about nine minutes by five people from five different communities who would be consenting and tethered and separate from each other, and that the yard wanted to know whether that community would put one of its own people on that water as one of the five.

He wrote that nobody was to be asked to hold anything. He wrote that in those words at the bottom of all nine.

He wrote that a person who had not been asked was not anybody yet, and that this yard would not go and find anybody, and that a community that did not send anybody would not be written down anywhere as having said no.

That last sentence is the one he says he will defend if anybody asks him about it, and nobody asked him about it.

---

They went out in about four hours and he did not go with any of them.

About three of the nine went to men he knew would be at an end stall or a bench or a boat at about two in the afternoon, and the other six went up that coast road on the cart that runs on a Tuesday and a Friday and was standing at the end of that lane at about half past nine with its tailboard down.

He did not ride on it. He wrote on the back of each of the six that went up that road the name of the town and nothing else, and no route's name went on any of the nine.

At about two in the afternoon three of the nine had come back and said that the question had been put to four people in a doorway and that they would ask their own people, and six had not come back at all and there was no figure of what any of them would say and this yard did not go and find out.

---

At about half past two a woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender came down that lane and into that yard and asked one question, and it was whether there would be a line of her own on that water that did not come off the working boat.

The man who keeps the leaf said that there would be one and that it would come off a winch of her own and be clear of the working boat's fall, and that he had not written that anywhere yet because she had not told him she wanted it and a thing that is written before it is asked for is a thing somebody decided.

She said that was the right answer and went back up that lane.

Nobody thanked her for asking and nobody thanked him for the right answer.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Wednesday line on the page of that week, and it is four sentences long, and it says that nine route teams were asked one thing each between about ten and about two, and that one thing each is what was asked, and that three answers had come back saying they would ask their own people, and that six had not come back, and that this yard went with none of the nine and sent for nobody.

He set no figure against it and no name against it and no state against any route, and wrote on the face of the sheet that a community that sends nobody is not a community that has said no.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and twenty-nine words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that shed at about two in the afternoon.

"**Nine questions went out of this yard this morning and they are all the same eleven words at the top of them and one thing underneath, and the one thing is not the same one in any two of the nine, and I want that said before anything else today because the rest of the week is going to be about what comes back.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now ninety-four days without one.**

**And here is the part I put on a slip and not on a wall, and there is one line of it. I have written at the bottom of all nine of those papers that a community which sends nobody will not be written down anywhere as having said no, and I want nine people to hear me say why, because in as long as this harbor has been keeping pages it has got very good at counting what it has been given and very bad at counting what it has not gone and asked for. A community on this coast can be held, restricted or dormant and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can read that off a sheet in four seconds, and there is not one figure on that sheet for the communities that nobody has ever asked.**

**A man of about thirty-four of the second lay in this harbor has stood in this yard and said that he would rather be asked than not asked, and nobody in this yard has asked him, and I have not asked him this month and I am not going to ask him on Saturday. That is on a page in nine words and I am not going to turn it into anything else, and I am not going to use it as a reason for today.**

**So the six of those nine that have not come back this afternoon are six communities this yard has not heard from, and I am not going to tell anybody in this market what they will say, and if anybody asks me at the end of next week I am going to say that they were asked and I do not know, and those two sentences are the whole of what I have learned in four weeks.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nine questions that had gone out of a yard door, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had one line on it and it was the first line of a new week, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**BETWEEN ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON NINE ROUTE TEAMS WERE ASKED ONE THING EACH, BEING WHETHER THAT COMMUNITY WOULD PUT ONE OF ITS OWN PEOPLE ON ABOUT FOUR FATHOMS OF THE SAME BOTTOM OFF THAT MOLE AS ONE OF FIVE CONSENTING TETHERED AND SEPARATE WITNESSES FOR ABOUT NINE MINUTES, AND NOBODY WAS TO BE ASKED TO HOLD ANYTHING, AND THAT LAST SENTENCE WAS WRITTEN AT THE BOTTOM OF ALL NINE IN THE KEEPER'S OWN HAND. A PERSON WHO HAD NOT BEEN ASKED WAS NOT ANYBODY YET AND A COMMUNITY THAT SENT NOBODY WAS NOT TO BE WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE AS HAVING SAID NO. THREE ANSWERS CAME BACK BY ABOUT TWO AND SIX HAD NOT COME BACK AND THIS YARD WENT WITH NONE OF THE NINE AND SENT FOR NOBODY. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the same eleven words went at the top of all nine is that a question with nine different preambles on it is nine arguments and this yard has one, and that a man carrying a paper into a market is not carrying an argument and this harbor has watched a coast get tired of arguments; and that at about ten in the morning the reason the one thing underneath is not the same in any two of the nine is that the nine communities are not nine of a kind and that a question written in the same words to all nine is a question written for whoever wrote it and not for the person who has to answer it; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason nothing was written down about the tender's own line until she had asked for it is that a yard that writes down a woman's requirements before the woman states them is a yard that has decided she wanted them, and that this harbor has one instance of that on a wall in about eleven inches of empty plaster and is not going to make a second; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the six that did not come back and not on the nine that went out is that nine going out is a thing this yard did and six not coming back is a thing that happened to this yard, and a slip that ends on what the yard did is a slip about the yard.*
