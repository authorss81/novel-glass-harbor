# Chapter 687

*Tuesday the Seventh Day of June, the Eighty-Fifth Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes by a Man of About Thirty-One of the Second Lay and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Eighty-Fifth Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, and the Eighty-Fifth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Behind the Eighty-Fourth, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Not Writing Down Which Five Until About Noon on Wednesday and Saying Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon That the Two Days Are Up at Nine and He Is Not Moving Them, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Tuesday the seventh day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-fifth wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. The column on the lid of that step stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-fifth week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the eighty-fourth, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that the two days he gave on Monday are up at nine the next morning and that he is not moving them. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in that harbor are on a wall or in a book this afternoon and a stranger with a leaf could have every one of them off the surface before dark. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and eighty-fifth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-seventh days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and twenty-fifth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-first night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and ninth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and sixty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-sixth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-seventh days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has been put there this month. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-fifth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes and it did not move, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay has read that same lid out on eighty-five Tuesdays now and the figure has not changed on any of them.

Behind the eighty-fifth stands behind the eighty-fourth and behind that one stands behind the eighty-third, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and there is nobody in that market who has ever asked what those envelopes are for because nobody in that market has ever been told.

This yard asked nobody this week and has not asked since the fourth of May.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf stood at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people and said out loud that the two days he gave on Monday were up at nine the next morning and that he was not moving them.

He said that about four people in that nine had tried to get him to move them between Monday and now, and that he had listened to all four and that he was not moving them, and that the four of them were welcome to keep asking and that he would put the same answer on the same slip at half past five today and at half past five tomorrow.

A man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard said that a man who gives a date and then keeps it is worth four of a man who gives a date and then moves it, and the man who keeps the leaf said that was the first time anybody had put it that way round and that he was not going to argue with it.

Nobody thanked either of them for that.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office came down that lane and stood at that stone and did not ask anything and stayed for about six minutes and then went up the lane again.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote down that she was there at about half past three for about six minutes and that she asked nothing and that he did not say anything to her that is going in this yard's book, and that neither of them was in the other's sentence, and that no page of this harbor carries a resolution of it.

That is the fourth time in a month those two have been in one place and said nothing to each other in front of other people and it is the fourth time this yard has written it down in those words.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Tuesday line on the page of that week and left it short, being that a column on a lid was read out in four minutes and did not move, and that he was not moving a date he had given out loud on Monday, and that the two days are up at nine the next morning.

He wrote the eighty-fifth week against the second half of that line and not the twenty envelopes, and that is on the face of the sheet with the hour beside it.

Nobody thanked him for the line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and thirty-two words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been at that stone at about two in the afternoon.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on that lid for the eighty-fifth time, read out at about ten this morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who is not on the water and has not been on the water, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and the eighty-fifth week stands fallen due at the close of today, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. I am not going to say the number of that week out loud and I have not said it once this month.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That butt behind that market was covered from eight until four, because a rota day is a Monday and a Thursday and today is neither.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred days without one.**

**Tomorrow is the day. I am not going to describe tomorrow on this slip because every figure I put against tomorrow this evening becomes a thing nine people have been told about, and that is the mistake this coast made in April with a schedule and it is not going to get made here in June with me.**

**What I am going to say is two things and then I will stop. One: I gave two days on Monday at about eleven in the morning in front of nine people and they are up at nine tomorrow morning and I am not moving them, and about four people have asked me to move them and I have listened to all four.**

**Two, and it is the other half of the week. A woman of thirty of no office stood at that stone at about half past three this afternoon for about six minutes and asked me nothing and I said nothing to her that is going in this yard's book. That is the fourth time this month. I have written it down in the same words four times now and I am not going to write down a fifth thing about it, because a page which records the same silence four times is a page that is waiting for a fifth and a fifth is the first step towards a page about two people instead of a page about this coast.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a Tuesday that did not move, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had seven lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THE SECOND LAY AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT BEHIND THAT DOOR FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE EIGHTY-FIFTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY BEHIND THE EIGHTY-FOURTH. AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID OUT LOUD THAT THE TWO DAYS HE GAVE ON MONDAY ARE UP AT NINE THE NEXT MORNING AND THAT HE IS NOT MOVING THEM, HAVING LISTENED TO ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE WHO HAD ASKED HIM TO MOVE THEM. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE STOOD AT THAT STONE AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE FOR ABOUT SIX MINUTES AND ASKED NOTHING AND WAS NOT IN THE OTHER'S SENTENCE, AND THAT IS THE FOURTH TIME IN A MONTH AND NO PAGE OF THIS HARBOR CARRIES A RESOLUTION OF IT. THE MONEY SHEET REMAINS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND WHAT HAS EVER GONE INTO IT IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP THIS WEEK AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the eighty-fifth is written and the eighty-fourth was written last week is that a figure which is only ever said on a day it is not moving stops being a figure and becomes a sentence about this harbor, and that the twenty-six lines and the one hundred and four marks are the only pair in this shed that two people can stand in front of and check; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason a refusal to move a date was given four times over in one afternoon is that about four people asked him to move it and he owed each of them an answer, and that answering all four with the same twenty words is cheaper for this yard and fairer to the four than four different answers would have been; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason what the man who hires hands said is repeated on a slip is that it is the only sentence this month that put the keeper below the date he set, and that a slip is the only page in this harbor where that is allowed to happen; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason six minutes of two people standing near each other is written down and what they said to each other is not is that this yard was wrong once this month in the other direction and wrote down a silence as though it were a finding, and that a fourth record is a record and a fifth would be a series, and a series is a page about people; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the eighty-fifth week went against the second half of that line and the twenty envelopes did not is that this harbor has been asked for six weeks to keep its papers separate and has got that right three times this month and this was the fourth; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip refuses to describe tomorrow is that the day before is the one day this yard has any business having an opinion about, and that a slip which forecasts a thing is a slip which has already argued with it.*
