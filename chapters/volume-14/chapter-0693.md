# Chapter 693

*Monday the Thirteenth Day of June, a Rota Day and the Fifteenth of the Fifteen in This Yard's Fifty Days and Carrying No Charge, the Butt Behind That Market Down to the Stones in the Bottom of It at About Eight in the Morning and Four Buckets Filled Out of What Was in It and Put Back and the Covers Going On at About Nine and Coming Off at About Four, the Next Charge of Any Kind Being on Thursday and Nothing Standing Between It and That Day, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Taking the Leaf Off Its Nail at About Eleven With the Shed Empty and Looking at the Four Columns and Filling Nothing and Scratching Nothing and Appointing Nobody, and the Man of About Thirty-Two Coming Down at About Half Past Twelve and Being Given the Leaf to Look at for as Long as He Wanted and Saying One Sentence About the Fourth Column and Nothing About Why It Stands Empty, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand and Four*

Monday the thirteenth day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day, and the fifteenth of the fifteen in this yard's fifty days, and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge went onto that cistern today for the first time in a hundred and fifty-nine of them. It was down to the stones in the bottom of it at about eight in the morning, and the four men who carry it filled four buckets out of what was left in it and put them back on the stones and went away, and the covers went on at about nine in the morning and came off at about four in the afternoon. The next charge of any kind is on Thursday and there is nothing standing between it and that day. At about eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf took that leaf off its nail with that shed empty and looked at the four columns on it and filled nothing and scratched nothing and appointed nobody. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters stood another day further this morning and not one of the nine was carried by anybody in this yard. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-first day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and ninety-three days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-first day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-seventh night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and fifteenth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eighty-fifth day. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its three hundred and second day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-three days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine were there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-one days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The four men were at the back of that market at about eight as they are on every Monday and on every Thursday they have done it in, and the butt was down to the stones.

One of the four said the word dry out loud and the other three did not take him up on it and did not go and put a hand in it to see. They filled four buckets out of what was in it and put them back on the stones where the buckets live and one of them said that it will be a Tuesday before anybody has to think about it again.

The man who keeps the leaf was told about it at about half past eight and wrote it down with the hour against it and did not go and look at it himself, and the covers went on at about nine and came off at about four.

There is a figure in the book at the back of that yard and it stands at a hundred and fifty-seven and there is no figure against today and the next charge of any kind goes in on Thursday.

---

At about eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf took the leaf off its nail.

That shed was empty at eleven and he shut the door to the lane and stood at the flat of the bench stone with it in his two hands and read the four columns straight down from the head of the leaf and not from any page he keeps a copy of.

Four hundred and twenty-six. Two thousand and four. None. One hundred and twenty-five.

He put his thumb on the third column where the boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and he did not write anything and he did not rub it out and he did not put a name in it, and he did not close it and he did not strike it, and he did not say a word out loud to anybody because there was nobody in that shed.

Then he hung it back up on the same nail it has been on since the first of May.

---

At about half past twelve the man of about thirty-two came down that lane and the man who keeps the leaf gave him the leaf and said he could have it for as long as he wanted it.

He looked at it for about six minutes and he did not go over the first column, which is the one he writes in every day, and he did not go over the second, which is the one that cannot be checked from a mole. He went to the fourth column and stayed at the top of it.

He said one sentence and it was that that column is still standing open at a hundred and twenty-five and that nobody has shut it and nobody has struck it and that is the whole of what he has to say about it. He did not say why it has taken no line and he was not asked why, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the hour against the sentence and did not write one word of a reason.

The man of about thirty-two gave the leaf back and went up the steps by the rail and did not stop at the end stall.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard came down the lane with a list of four names on the back of a paper and left it in that shed on the bench.

He said the four men have been out since eight and one of them is not coming back this week, and that it was not that yard's business and that he was telling them and not asking them.

The man who keeps the leaf read the four names and wrote them down and put the hour against it, and no name went onto the page of hands and nothing went onto the fitting-out sheet.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Monday line on the page of that week and it says three things, being that the cistern was down to the stones and four buckets were filled out of it and put back and that no charge goes on today and the next charge of any kind is on Thursday, that the leaf was taken off its nail at about eleven with the shed empty and read straight down and hung back on the same nail, and that the man of about thirty-two held it for about six minutes and said one sentence about the fourth column and said nothing about the third.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and fifty-six words, to about nine people, and about three of those nine had been at that bare butt at about half past eight in the morning.

"**No water on that cistern today for the first time in a hundred and fifty-nine times it has been done, and the reason is that it was down to the stones at about eight this morning, and the four men who carry it filled four buckets out of what was in it and put them back and went away. That is the fifteenth day this yard has carried water on a Monday or a Thursday and the fifteenth is the one that carried none. The next charge of any kind is on Thursday and I am not going to pretend I know what Thursday's water is for, because I do not know and I have not known since the second of May.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and not a third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and six days without one.**

**The leaf. I took it off its own nail at about eleven with that shed shut and nobody in it and read the four columns straight down, being four hundred and twenty-six and two thousand and four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and I put my thumb on that third column and I wrote nothing and I rubbed nothing out and I shut nothing and I appointed nobody, and I have got nothing to say about that to nine people except that a man who fills an empty column because it has been empty for forty-three days has stopped being able to leave a thing alone.**

**And the man of about thirty-two had it for about six minutes this afternoon and did not look at the first column or the second, and went to the fourth and said one sentence, and the sentence was that it is still standing open and that nobody has shut it and nobody has struck it, and that is the whole of what he had to say about it. He did not tell me why it stands empty and I did not ask him and I am not going to.**

**And the last of it. Four men were out on that cistern at eight and one of them is not coming back this week and the man who hires hands told us that and not asked us for anything, and the page of hands stands at fifteen and a name does not go on it for telling us a thing we already knew.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a butt that was dry, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had three lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THAT BUTT BEHIND THE MARKET WAS DOWN TO THE STONES IN THE BOTTOM OF IT AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND THE FOUR MEN WHO CARRY IT FILLED FOUR BUCKETS OUT OF WHAT WAS LEFT IN IT AND PUT THEM BACK ON THE STONES AND WENT AWAY, AND THAT IS THE FIFTEENTH ROTA DAY OF THIS YARD'S FIFTY AND IT CARRIED NO CHARGE, THE NEXT CHARGE OF ANY KIND BEING ON THURSDAY. AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF TOOK THAT LEAF OFF ITS NAIL WITH THAT SHED EMPTY AND READ THE FOUR COLUMNS STRAIGHT DOWN FROM THE HEAD OF IT AND PUT HIS THUMB ON THE THIRD COLUMN WHERE THE BOY'S HAND OF TWENTY-TWO IS AND WROTE NOTHING AND RUBBED NOTHING OUT AND CLOSED NOTHING AND APPOINTED NOBODY, AND HUNG IT BACK UP ON THE SAME NAIL IT HAS BEEN ON SINCE THE FIRST OF MAY. AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO HELD IT FOR ABOUT SIX MINUTES AND SAID ONE SENTENCE ABOUT THE FOURTH COLUMN AND SAID NOTHING AT ALL ABOUT THE THIRD. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the thirteenth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was the fifteenth of the fifteen rota days in this yard's fifty days and carried no charge, and that it was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe; and that the butt behind that market was down to the stones in the bottom of it at about eight in the morning, and that one of the four men who carry it said the word dry out loud and the other three did not take him up on it and did not go and put a hand in to see, and that they filled four buckets out of what was in it and put them back on the stones where the buckets live and one of them said that it will be a Tuesday before anybody has to think about it again, and that the man who keeps the leaf was told about it at about half past eight and wrote it down with the hour against it and did not go and look at it himself, and that the covers went on at about nine in the morning and came off at about four in the afternoon, and that the figure in the book at the back of that yard stands at a hundred and fifty-seven and that there is no figure against today and that the next charge of any kind goes in on Thursday; and that at about eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf took the leaf off its nail, and that that shed was empty at eleven and he shut the door to the lane and stood at the flat of the bench stone with it in his two hands and read the four columns straight down from the head of the leaf and not from any page he keeps a copy of, being four hundred and twenty-six and two thousand and four and none and one hundred and twenty-five, and that he put his thumb on the third column where the boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and did not write anything and did not rub it out and did not put a name in it and did not close it and did not strike it, and that he did not say a word out loud because there was nobody in that shed, and that he hung it back up on the same nail it has been on since the first of May; and that at about half past twelve the man of about thirty-two came down that lane and the man who keeps the leaf gave him the leaf and said he could have it for as long as he wanted it, and that he looked at it for about six minutes and did not go over the first column, which is the one he writes in every day, and did not go over the second, which is the one that cannot be checked from a mole, and that he went to the fourth column and stayed at the top of it, and that he said one sentence and it was that that column is still standing open at a hundred and twenty-five and that nobody has shut it and nobody has struck it and that is the whole of what he has to say about it, and that he did not say why it has taken no line and was not asked why; and that at about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard came down the lane with a list of four names on the back of a paper and left it in that shed on the bench and said that the four men have been out since eight and that one of them is not coming back this week and that it was not that yard's business and that he was telling them and not asking them, and that the four names were written down with the hour against them and that no name went onto the page of hands and nothing went onto the fitting-out sheet; and that at about four in the afternoon the Monday line was written on the page of that week; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and fifty-six words to about nine people.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason no charge is written on that cistern today is that it was down to the stones, and that a rota is a day and not an order, and that a man who carries water on a Monday because he carried it last Monday is not a man who is under an instruction and this harbor has spent forty-two days learning that a requirement which cannot be paid does not become a rule; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason that leaf was read with the door shut is that a man who has been writing numbers into the same column for forty-two days needs to read it once the way a stranger would, and that the way a stranger reads a leaf is off the head of it and not off any page in this shed; and that at about half past twelve the reason the thumb on the third column is written down and the filling of it is not is that a column which has stood empty since the first of May is a column somebody is going to want to fill one Tuesday, and that the only way this yard has of not filling it on a quiet Monday is to put a thumb on it and write down that it was empty and that it is still empty; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the page of hands and a name not going on it is that a man who tells a yard a thing about a crew has been inside a thing and a page of hands is about what a community has signed and those are two different kinds of paper and this harbor has been putting them in one mouth since May and paying for it.
