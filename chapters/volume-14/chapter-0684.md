# Chapter 684

*Saturday the Fourth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Seven of the Nine Answers In by About Two in the Afternoon and Two of Those Seven Not Yet Naming a Person, the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor Standing in That Shed at About Half Past Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People and Saying Out Loud That He Is Offering His Body for About Nine Minutes and Not Being Asked for It by Anybody, and the Reason for It Written Down Whole in His Own Words at About Four in the Afternoon and Set to Fifty Words and Read Back to Him Before the Hour Went Against It and Read Out Before Anybody Goes Down, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seventeen and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Saturday the fourth day of June, with that market open all day and about sixty people on those boards before eleven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. Seven of the nine answers were in by about two in the afternoon and two of those seven had not named a person yet. At about half past two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood in that shed in about nine people and said out loud that he was offering his body for about nine minutes, and nobody in that nine asked him for it and nobody had asked him beforehand. The reason for it was written down in his own words with the hour against it at about four in the afternoon and read out before anybody goes down. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in that harbor are nine lines on a wall or a figure on a page and a stranger could take every one of them off before dark. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and eighty-second day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-fourth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and twenty-second day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-eighth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and sixth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and sixty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-third day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-fourth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Nobody in this harbor has opened two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole and nothing has been nailed there this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-second days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

He did not arrive at about half past two. He was already in that shed at about two and he stood at the back of it for about half an hour and said nothing to anybody, and about four people in that nine noticed that he was standing there and did not go over to him.

He said it at about half past two and he said it in about nine people and it took him about a minute and a half.

He said that he was offering his body for about nine minutes on that bottom off that mole and that he was offering it because he was the only person in that shed who had been under that water and had come up able to talk about it, and that he was not asking anybody for anything and that nobody was to take anything away from anybody else because he had said it.

Then he stopped and about four people in that nine did not say anything, and a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish said the word *gap* once and out loud, and the man of about thirty-two said that was the right word and that was all it was.

Nobody thanked him for offering and nobody argued with him about it, and the man who keeps the leaf did not write the word *gap* on that sheet.

---

At about three in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office asked one question in that nine, and it was whether he had asked anybody else first.

He said that he had not, and that he was not going to, and that if she wanted to know why he had said it in a shed in front of nine people instead of in a room with two people in it then the answer was that a room with two people in it is a thing two people own.

That is the whole of what was said about the disagreement this week and neither of the two of them was in the other's sentence.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote down the reason, and he wrote it in the man's own words and he read it back to him before he put the hour against it.

It is fifty words long and this is the whole of it.

"I am offering because I am the only one here who has been under that water and come up able to talk about it. Four fathoms is not deep and I have been under worse on worse days than this. I would rather have offered than have been sent for."

He read it back and the man of about thirty-two said that was his meaning and not a slip of the mouth, and the keeper wrote the hour against it.

Nobody thanked either of them for that.

---

At about half past four a man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard asked the man of about thirty-two one question, being whether he understood that the thing he was offering could be stopped by a woman on the surface without asking him.

He said that he understood it, and that he would have said no to the whole thing if the answer had been that he could not be stopped, and that this is why the twenty-five minutes is on a piece of paper in a shed and not in his head.

Nobody in that nine said anything after that and the man who keeps the leaf wrote all three of those exchanges down with the hour against each.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about seven hundred and two words, to about nine people, and about seven of those nine had heard him offer himself at about half past two.

"**Seven of those nine answers are in and two of the seven have not named a person, and at about half past two this afternoon the man of about thirty-two of this harbor stood at the back of this shed and offered his body for about nine minutes on that bottom off that mole. Nobody asked him for it. I want that said first because everything else this week is smaller than it.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now ninety-seven days without one.**

**What he said, in about a minute and a half, and I wrote it down in his own fifty words and read it back to him before I put the hour on it. He is the only person in this shed who has been under that water and come up able to talk about it. Four fathoms is not deep and he has been under worse on worse days. He would rather be the one who offered than the one who was sent for. A man of about fifty-six said the word gap out loud once and the man of about thirty-two said that was the right word and that was all it was, and I have not written the word on that sheet, and if anybody wants to know what it was they were all standing here.**

**And three things about the way it happened that I am putting on this slip because they are the whole of the method. One: nobody asked him. He was standing at the back of that shed for half an hour before he said anything and not one of those nine people went over to him. Two: he said it in a shed in front of nine people and not in a room with two in it, and when he was asked why he said that a room with two people in it is a thing two people own. Three: when a man who hires hands here asked him whether he understood that a woman on the surface could stop him without asking him, he said he would have said no to the whole thing if the answer had been that he could not be stopped.**

**And what is not settled and what is not going to be. The woman of thirty of no office asked him one question this afternoon and it was whether he had asked anybody else first, and he said he had not and was not going to. That is on that sheet in her question and his answer and it is not a resolution of anything, and no page of this harbor carries a resolution of it, and I am not going to let a slip become the place where it gets settled.**

**And the last of it. The money that has ever gone into that sheet is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence and it has not moved since the autumn, and what is going to go onto it on Thursday is gear, and gear is not a man. I have been asked this month by people in this market whether this harbor is buying its way into something, and I have not got an answer for them yet, and I am not going to give them one tonight.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a man standing at the back of a shed for half an hour before he said anything, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had four lines on it and one of them is fifty words long in another man's mouth, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR STOOD IN THAT SHED IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS OFFERING HIS BODY FOR ABOUT NINE MINUTES ON THAT BOTTOM OFF THAT MOLE, AND HE WAS ALREADY STANDING AT THE BACK OF THAT SHED FOR ABOUT HALF AN HOUR BEFORE HE SAID ANYTHING AND NOT ONE PERSON WENT OVER TO HIM, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM FOR IT AND NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM BEFOREHAND. THE REASON WAS WRITTEN DOWN IN HIS OWN WORDS AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, BEING FIFTY WORDS LONG, AND WAS READ BACK TO HIM BEFORE THE HOUR WENT AGAINST IT, AND HE SAID IT WAS HIS MEANING AND NOT A SLIP OF THE MOUTH. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ASKED HIM ONE QUESTION, BEING WHETHER HE HAD ASKED ANYBODY ELSE FIRST, AND HE SAID THAT HE HAD NOT AND WAS NOT GOING TO. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX OF THE THIRD LAY SAID THE WORD GAP ONCE AND OUT LOUD AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID THAT WAS THE RIGHT WORD AND IT WAS ALL IT WAS. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason nobody went over to him while he stood there is that this yard has spent four weeks refusing to send for anybody, and that a man standing at the back of a room not saying anything is the hardest thing in this harbor to be near, and that the whole of the thirty-one days since the second of May has been practice in leaving people alone; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason the reason was written down in his own words and read back to him before the hour went on it is that this coast has been given two documents in a month by men in rooms and not one of them had the writer's own words in it, and a reason that is not the reason is a reason somebody else can be held to; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason her question about whether he had asked anybody else first was written down with her question and not with an answer from this yard is that the disagreement between those two is about who a document belongs to and it is not about this morning, and that this yard writing an answer into the middle of it would be the first of the three moves that settle a thing that is not being settled; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason that exchange with the man who hires hands is on the page is that an offer which cannot be withdrawn is not an offer, and that the only way anybody in this market can tell the difference between the two is by having the words, and the words are four sentences long and they cost nothing; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the word gap is left off that sheet is that a word a man says out loud once in a shed is a thing that happened and a word a keeper writes down is a word this yard now owns, and this harbor has refused to own words six times and the word in this case is the heaviest one anybody has said in that shed this month; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on gear and not on the man who offered himself is that a slip which ends on a man is a slip about a man, and this sheet has a page of hands for that and it has fifteen on it and is not being added to, and there is exactly one page in this harbor where a name belongs and it is not this one.*
