# Chapter 689

*Thursday the Ninth Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Seventh Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Lying on It from About Eleven Until About Four, One Line Going Onto the Fitting-Out Sheet at About Two in the Afternoon and No Other Line Going Onto It in Fifty Days, Its Five Parts Being Forty Yards of Line at Three Pence the Yard by Forty Times Three Being One Hundred and Twenty, Two Bell Seats Cut and Faced at Eighteen Pence by Two Times Eighteen Being Thirty-Six, a Watch for the Tender and an Independent Line at Sixty Pence, Nine Men at Two Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day by Nine Times Two Times Sixteen Being Two Hundred and Eighty-Eight, and Two Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk by Two Times One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Being Three Hundred and Thirty-Six, the Five Being Eight Hundred and Forty Pence and the Sheet at One Hundred and Twelve Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixteen Pence Out, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Two and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Thursday the ninth day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-seventh charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about two in the afternoon one line went onto the fitting-out sheet and no other line goes onto it in fifty days. Its five parts are forty yards of line at three pence the yard, being forty times three being one hundred and twenty pence; two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence, being two times eighteen being thirty-six pence; a watch for the tender and an independent line at sixty pence; nine men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, being nine times two times sixteen being two hundred and eighty-eight pence; and two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being two times one hundred and sixty-eight being three hundred and thirty-six pence. Those five are eight hundred and forty pence, being five marks, three shillings and fourpence. The day printed against that line is the day the rehearsal was stopped and not the day the work was done, being the eighth, and both days are on the face of it. Nothing else went onto that sheet and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on this coast went a day further today and none of them was carried by this yard. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and eighty-seventh day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-ninth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and twenty-seventh day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-third night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and eleventh day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and sixty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eighty-first. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-ninth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has been put on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-seventh days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, he is not on the water, and the hearing lost off that mole on Wednesday was a different hearing in a different man and did not improve this one.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and fifty-seventh time, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and about four of the men who carried it have asked twice this week what the water is for and have been told twice by this yard that this yard does not know.

They carried it anyway and they were not asked again and this is the hundred and fifty-seventh time.

---

The line went on at about two in the afternoon and it took about twenty minutes and about eleven people were standing in that shed while it went on and the man who keeps the leaf read every part of it out loud as he wrote it.

He wrote it as the yard's own mouth and he wrote the multiplication against each part and not only the product, and he wrote the day the work was done and the day the rehearsal was stopped and both days are on the face of the line.

Forty yards of line at three pence the yard, and forty times three is one hundred and twenty. Two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence, and two times eighteen is thirty-six. A watch for the tender and an independent line at sixty pence, and that is sixty pence and there is nothing to multiply because it is one watch and one line. Nine men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, and nine times two times sixteen is two hundred and eighty-eight. Two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, and two times one hundred and sixty-eight is three hundred and thirty-six.

One hundred and twenty plus thirty-six plus sixty plus two hundred and eighty-eight plus three hundred and thirty-six is eight hundred and forty pence, being five marks, three shillings and fourpence.

---

Then he wrote the new total at the foot of that sheet and read it out twice.

One hundred and eleven thousand eight hundred and seventy-six pence and eight hundred and forty is one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings.

He said out loud that this is the only line that has gone on that sheet in fifty days and that the one that was on it before it is the bill for the storm of the twentieth of April and that the two are not the same kind of thing and are printed the same size.

Nobody thanked anybody for the arithmetic and about four of those eleven people asked him to say the shortfall again and he said it again twice more.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two of that harbor came into that shed and said one thing about that line and it was that it was gear and that gear is not a man and that somebody ought to write that down somewhere.

The man who keeps the leaf said that it was written on the face of the line in those words and read it out to him, and the man of about thirty-two said that was all right and went back up that lane.

Nobody thanked him for asking and this yard wrote the hour against it.

At about four the keeper wrote the Thursday line on the page of that week and it says that one line went onto the fitting-out sheet at about two in the afternoon with its multiplication written at every one of its five parts, and that the day printed on it is the day the rehearsal was stopped and not the day the work was done, and that the second night of not sleeping began at about two this morning.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about seven hundred and fifteen words, to about nine people, and about six of those nine had been in that shed at about two in the afternoon.

"**One line went onto that sheet this afternoon at about two o'clock and it is the only line in fifty days, and I am going to read the five parts of it out again because somebody ought to have heard them from a second man and I am the second man.**

**Forty yards of line at three pence the yard, forty times three, one hundred and twenty. Two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence, two times eighteen, thirty-six. A watch for the tender and an independent line at sixty pence. Nine men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, nine times two times sixteen, two hundred and eighty-eight. Two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, two times one hundred and sixty-eight, three hundred and thirty-six. And one hundred and twenty plus thirty-six plus sixty plus two hundred and eighty-eight plus three hundred and thirty-six is eight hundred and forty pence, being five marks, three shillings and fourpence.**

**And the sheet now stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. A hundred and sixty-eight in this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight out this afternoon and twenty-eight under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, and six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks.**

**Now the two days on that line, and this is the part this yard has never had to do before. The day the work was done is on it and the day the rehearsal was stopped is on it, and they are not the same day, and the day I have printed at the head of it is the eighth, which is the day it was stopped and not the day the line was bought or cut or bent. That is the third time this yard has done that and I am the one who wrote the rule that it could be done and I am telling nine people that I have used it this week on myself.**

**And the other thing. I put the word gear on the face of that line at about half past three this afternoon because the man of about thirty-two came in and said that somebody ought to write it down, and I read it out to him off the paper and he said that was all right and went away. That line is line, seats, a watch, nine men and a boat, and not one of those five parts is a man and the nine men on it are eighteen days of a man at a rate in chalk and they are not nine people who went down yesterday.**

**And what is not on that line and is not going on it. The aftertaste is not on it. The knee is not on it. Three nights of not sleeping, and this is the second of them, are not on it. The hearing he lost on Wednesday morning is not on it and is not going on it and nobody is going to be able to take that sheet to a man who cannot hear out of the left side and read him a figure. That is not a policy. That is me deciding that there are some things a page of this harbor does not carry, and I have not got a rule for it and I have got a line with gear on it.**

**And the last of it. About eleven people stood in that shed while that line went on and about four of them asked me to say the shortfall again and I said it again twice. It is a hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short this afternoon and it was a hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short yesterday, and eight hundred and forty pence of that difference is line and seats and a watch and the nine men and a boat, and the other part of it is not mine to reduce.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for one line in fifty days, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ONE LINE WENT ONTO THE FITTING-OUT SHEET AND NO OTHER LINE GOES ONTO IT IN FIFTY DAYS. ITS FIVE PARTS ARE FORTY YARDS OF LINE AT THREE PENCE THE YARD, BEING FORTY TIMES THREE BEING ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE; TWO BELL SEATS CUT AND FACED AT EIGHTEEN PENCE, BEING TWO TIMES EIGHTEEN BEING THIRTY-SIX PENCE; A WATCH FOR THE TENDER AND AN INDEPENDENT LINE AT SIXTY PENCE; NINE MEN AT TWO DAYS AT SIXTEEN PENCE A MAN-DAY, BEING NINE TIMES TWO TIMES SIXTEEN BEING TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-EIGHT PENCE; AND TWO DAYS OF A BOAT HIRED AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK, BEING TWO TIMES ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT BEING THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE. THOSE FIVE ARE EIGHT HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING FIVE MARKS, THREE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. THE DAY PRINTED AGAINST THAT LINE IS THE DAY THE REHEARSAL WAS STOPPED AND NOT THE DAY THE WORK WAS DONE, BEING THE EIGHTH, AND BOTH DAYS ARE ON THE FACE OF IT. THE SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS. NO FIGURE OF MONEY IS SET AGAINST THE AFTERTASTE OR THE KNEE OR THE THREE NIGHTS OR THE HEARING. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason four men who carry that cistern have now been asked twice what the water is for is that a rota of forty weeks is the longest unquestioned thing on this coast and that this yard would rather be asked badly twice than not at all, and that a man who carries eleven buckets for forty weeks without being asked is a man nobody has looked at; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason every part of that line was read out as it was written is that a sheet which can only be read by a person who can read a column is a sheet that about eleven people in this harbor have never been shown, and that the multiplication is read out loud because a product is a thing a person takes on trust and a multiplication is a thing a person can check in their own head; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the third form was used on this line is that the work was done on three days and the thing was stopped on one and those are two different facts, and that the rule was written by this keeper for a case that had not happened yet and that using it this week on this yard's own rehearsal is the only argument this yard has ever had about it; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason the word gear went on that line is that a man who went into the water yesterday is a figure on somebody's list the moment a page connects the two, and that gear cannot be connected to him because gear does not have a knee; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the eight hundred and forty pence of difference rather than on the sheet total is that the sheet total is the largest figure this yard handles and if a slip ends on it then the slip is about the sheet, and the sheet is not what happened this week.*
