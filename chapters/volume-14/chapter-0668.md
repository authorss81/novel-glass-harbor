# Chapter 668

*Thursday the Nineteenth Day of May, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-First Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, and a Woman of About Twenty-Nine of That Harbor of the Second Lay and One of the About Eleven People Who Wrote the Morrow Line's Own Sentence Saying Out Loud at About Three in the Afternoon in About Nine People in About Two Hundred and One Words That That Line Comes Down off That Wall, and the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor Standing in That Same Nine People and Saying Nothing at All, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and One and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Thursday the nineteenth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-first charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about three in the afternoon a woman of about twenty-nine of that harbor, of the second lay, and one of the about eleven people who wrote the Morrow Line's own sentence, said out loud in about nine people in about two hundred and one words that that line comes down off that wall, and the man of about thirty-two of that harbor was in those same nine people and said nothing at all, and he did not say it and he was not asked to say it and he was not thanked for not saying it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and sixty-six days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty-eight days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and six days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-second night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and ninety days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and forty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixtieth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventy-seventh day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and eight days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been nailed on that board this month, and in that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head; it is one hundred and sixty-six days, no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and fifty-first, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off again at about four.

At about ten in the morning that woman of about twenty-nine came into that yard on her own account and asked whether the people who had written that line on Monday the ninth of May would be in that shed that afternoon.

She is of the second lay and she is one of the about eleven people who wrote that line and four of those eleven cannot read what they wrote.

---

At about three in the afternoon she stood on the flat of that bench stone in about nine people and said out loud, in about two hundred and one words, that the line comes down off that wall.

She said that she wrote it and that she can read about half of what she wrote and that the half she can read is enough.

She said that the finding about the man of about sixty-one is a difference and that a difference is not a proof, and that the thing that happened is that a route came to be standing on a page with two names on it that its own people had not put there.

She said that she is not saying the other two are bad men and she is not saying anybody meant it and she is not saying the page is a lie, because a page with two names on it that were put by somebody else is not a lie, it is a page that has been stood on.

She said that a line on that wall is a thing a route's own people put up and that if the people who put it up cannot stand behind every name on it then it comes down, and that she would rather have two sentences on that wall that are true than nine sentences that are true on their own and borrowed on the underneath.

She did not say that she was sorry and nobody in that yard asked her to be.

---

The man of about thirty-two of that harbor was in those nine people and he did not say anything.

Nobody turned to him. Nobody said his name. He has said out loud in that shed on three occasions this week what this yard is not, and none of those three was this, and he did not step forward and he did not step back.

A man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons said afterwards, to nobody in particular, that it is a long way past the first of May for a yard to be told what it is by the people it has been trying to help, and that the yard took it well.

Nobody thanked him for saying that either, and the woman of about twenty-nine did not answer him.

---

At about four in the afternoon a man of about fifty-three added those figures up with a leaf and a pencil in about a minute, got eighteen, read the nine names down the side of that sheet and got nine, gave the sheet back and went to work.

He was not asked what the eighteen was for. He was not asked twice what he had got. About four people in that shed saw him do it.

---

At about half past five the man who keeps the leaf wrote it in the first column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and wrote that the line is to come down off that wall on the Friday, by the hands that put it up, and that this yard has not been asked to take it down and is not going to offer.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and ninety-one words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been standing on that stone at about three.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April and not one hand on it since. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand this morning between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and fifty-first.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**A woman of about twenty-nine of this harbor, of the second lay, said at about three this afternoon in nine people that a line on that wall is coming down, and she said it in about two hundred and one words and she is one of the people who wrote it, and she said that a page with two names on it that were put there by somebody else is not a lie, it is a page that has been stood on.**

**Four things about that and none of them is mine. One: she is the one who said it and not this yard. Two: this yard has not been asked to take that line down and is not going to offer, and the hands that put it up are the hands that take it off, tomorrow, and that is written down now so that nobody in this harbor can say afterwards that a yard pulled a line down off a wall. Three: the man of about thirty-two was in that nine people and said nothing and was not asked to say anything, and I am not going to write one word about that, in this slip or anywhere else. Four: eighteen is on that page of days now and nineteen is still readable under it and the day and the hour are against both.**

**And the last of it. Three lines went up on that wall this month and one of them is coming down off it tomorrow in front of the people who put it up. That is the correct state of that wall and it is not a defeat and it is not a victory, and I have watched three people this week try to make it into one or the other, and the only reason it is going to be right is that nobody was allowed to do either.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a line coming down, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that wall had three lines on it and one of them would be off it by about ten tomorrow morning, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND IT IS THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIRST CHARGE, THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE OF THAT HARBOR, OF THE SECOND LAY, AND ONE OF THE ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE WHO WROTE THE MORROW LINE'S OWN SENTENCE, SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND IN ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND ONE WORDS THAT THAT LINE COMES DOWN OFF THAT WALL, AND SAID THAT A PAGE WITH TWO NAMES ON IT THAT WERE PUT THERE BY SOMEBODY ELSE IS NOT A LIE BUT A PAGE THAT HAS BEEN STOOD ON, AND THAT SHE IS THE ONE WHO SAID IT AND NOT THIS YARD. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR WAS IN THOSE NINE PEOPLE AND SAID NOTHING AND WAS NOT ASKED TO SAY ANYTHING. THE LINE IS TO COME DOWN ON THE FRIDAY BY THE HANDS THAT PUT IT UP AND THIS YARD HAS NOT BEEN ASKED TO TAKE IT DOWN AND IS NOT GOING TO OFFER. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason that woman asked in the morning for an afternoon is that a person who says a thing like that at about ten in the morning over a cistern has about forty people listening and no control of who they are, and that she wanted nine, and that asking the night before is the only way anybody in this harbor has ever got nine; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason this yard did not offer to take that line down is that a line on that wall is not the yard's line and never was, and that this yard has spent this month saying so in nine words and it would have spent every one of them on the first afternoon it offered to do the work of the hands that put a thing up; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason I have written down that the man of about thirty-two said nothing and not one word about what he said nothing to is that the whole of what this yard has been doing since the second of May is that a silence in nine people is not a finding, and that the yard which writes its own silences down as evidence is the yard that stopped listening a fortnight ago; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the sum of that page was taken again on a Thursday and not on a Wednesday is that this yard takes a count when the day before it has changed something, and that it did not change on a Tuesday and it changed on a Wednesday, and that nobody in that shed decided that and it happened; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason I have written that this is not a defeat and not a victory is that both of those are words about this yard, and that the only fact available about that woman of about twenty-nine is that she wrote a sentence and then took it off a wall herself in front of nine people, and that this yard was not in it and did not have to be.*
