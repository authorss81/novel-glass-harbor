# Chapter 655

*Friday the Sixth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Sheet Under a Stone at the Head of Those Boards With the Top of It Wet and the Bottom of It Dry, Eight Lines at the Head of It and Four Saying What It Is and Four Saying What It Does Not Decide, Read Out Once at About Eleven in the Morning in About Twenty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read It and Stopped at the Seal at the Foot of It With the Name Under That Seal in Nobody's Mouth, and Nobody Asked to Put a Name to Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Eighty-Eight and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Fourteen*

Friday the sixth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. A sheet came into that harbor in the night and lay under a lump of stone at the head of the boards at the back of that market, and the boards were swept at about seven in the morning and the stone came away and left the top of it wet and the bottom of it dry. At about eleven a woman of thirty of no office read it out once, in about twenty people, of whom about eleven cannot read it, and she stopped at the seal at the foot of it and did not read the name under that seal, and nobody asked her to go on past it, and nobody in that market was asked whether they would put a name to anything before the morning was out. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood three hundred and fifty-three days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and fifty-five days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and ninety-three days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and fifty-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and seventy-seven days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and thirty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and forty-seventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and sixty-fourth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and ninety-five days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fifty-three days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

It came in with the rain of the night and it was under a lump of stone at the head of those boards, and when the boards were swept at about seven the stone came away and left the top of the sheet wet and the bottom of it dry, and that is the whole of how anybody knows when it arrived.

It is a sheet of days, and there are eight lines at the head of it, four of which say what it is and four of which say what it does not decide, and at the foot of it there is a seal, and under that seal there is a name, and nobody in that market read the name under that seal out loud and nobody asked to and nobody is going to be asked to.

---

At about eleven a woman of thirty of no office read it out once, in about twenty people, in the open, and stopped at the seal.

About eleven of those twenty cannot read it. Nobody read it out twice, because it is not a schedule and there is no second set of people to read it to, and because it is a sheet that came under a stone and not a line that somebody wrote in this yard.

The four lines that say what it is are these.

1. *That this is a sheet of requirements concerning testimony carried between one port and another on any of the nine coastal routes.*
2. *That no such testimony shall be carried on a line unless there is a living custodian on that line.*
3. *That the writing of that custodian is to be kept at the port where the testimony was made, and not at any other port.*
4. *That a custodian is a person and not an office, and that a name is the whole of what is being asked for.*

The four lines that say what it does not decide are these.

1. *It does not say which office supplies a custodian.*
2. *It does not say what is to happen to a line that has no custodian on it.*
3. *It does not say what a custodian is paid, and it does not say by whom.*
4. *It does not say what happens on the first day it is in force.*

That last of the four is the one about eleven people standing on those boards this morning went straight to, and nobody answered them, and this yard did not either.

---

At about half past twelve in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane asked the yard, in about nine people, whether that yard was going to set up a sheet for the names, and the woman of thirty of no office said no.

Her reasons were in about eighty-four words and they are these.

"**A sheet of names is a sheet that will be filled in, and the only reason a name goes onto a sheet is so that a name can come off it later and be held against whoever put it there. And if this harbor starts a sheet of custodians then this harbor has become the office that supplies them, and the day after that somebody will ask this yard which port it supplies and this yard will not be able to say no twice.**"

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about one hundred and eighty-eight words, to about nine people.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence on that board in the front street, unmoved, and I have stopped saying that figure the way I used to, not because it has moved, but because saying a figure that has not moved sixty times is a way of enjoying it.**

**No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**"

**And I am going to say the thing that is on that wall this morning in the plainest words I have. That sheet under that stone is not wrong. I have looked for the wrong in it since eleven o'clock and I cannot find it and I have been looking for thirty years of other people's papers and I know what wrong looks like on a page. It says no testimony crosses between one port and another without a person on the line. That is true. It says the writing of that person is kept where the thing was made. That is true and it is what our own pages try to do and half of them do not. It says a custodian is a person and not an office, and that a name is the whole of what is being asked. That is true and it is the only sentence on that sheet I would not change if I could.**

**And here is what it costs, and it is not a figure, and I have spent the afternoon turning it over and there is no way of putting it on a page without lying on the page. A person on the line means a person on the line all night as well as all day. Our nine routes run day and night and the fouling on one of them this month was three months old, which means somebody was not on it for three months. A custodian on a line is a wage, and that wage is not on that sheet because that sheet does not say who pays it, and the reason it does not say who pays it is that the sheet does not have to. It only has to be right.**

**And I am not going to say out loud in this shed that that is unfair, because it is fair, and it is the fairest thing that has come on this coast since the account of the fourteenth, and being fair is exactly what makes it hard. What I am going to write down is this. That sheet asks for one thing, and the one thing it asks for is the one thing this coast has never once been able to produce on demand, and the reason we did not produce it this morning is not that we said no. We said nothing. Nobody in this market was asked.**

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eight lines, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that sheet was still under the stone at the head of those boards with the stone put back on it by somebody who did not say they had done it.

---

**A SHEET CAME INTO THAT HARBOR IN THE NIGHT AND LAY UNDER A STONE AT THE HEAD OF THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WITH THE TOP OF IT WET AND THE BOTTOM OF IT DRY, AND IT CARRIES EIGHT LINES AT THE HEAD OF IT, FOUR OF WHICH SAY WHAT IT IS AND FOUR OF WHICH SAY WHAT IT DOES NOT DECIDE, AND IT WAS READ OUT ONCE AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT ELEVEN CANNOT READ IT, AND IT WAS STOPPED AT THE SEAL AT THE FOOT OF IT WITH THE NAME UNDER THAT SEAL IN NO MOUTH. IT ASKS THAT NO TESTIMONY BE CARRIED BETWEEN ONE PORT AND ANOTHER ON ANY OF THE NINE COASTAL ROUTES WITHOUT A LIVING CUSTODIAN ON THAT LINE, AND THAT THE WRITING OF THAT CUSTODIAN BE KEPT AT THE PORT WHERE THE TESTIMONY WAS MADE, AND IT SAYS A CUSTODIAN IS A PERSON AND NOT AN OFFICE AND THAT A NAME IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT IS BEING ASKED, AND IT DOES NOT SAY WHICH OFFICE SUPPLIES A CUSTODIAN, DOES NOT SAY WHAT IS TO HAPPEN TO A LINE THAT HAS NO CUSTODIAN ON IT, DOES NOT SAY WHAT A CUSTODIAN IS PAID OR BY WHOM, AND DOES NOT SAY WHAT HAPPENS ON THE FIRST DAY IT IS IN FORCE. NOBODY IN THAT MARKET WAS ASKED WHETHER THEY WOULD PUT A NAME TO ANYTHING. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that a sheet came into that harbor in the night and lay under a lump of stone at the head of the boards at the back of that market and that the boards were swept at about seven in the morning and that the stone came away and left the top of that sheet wet and the bottom of it dry; and that it has eight lines at the head of it, four of which say what it is and four of which say what it does not decide, and that at the foot of it there is a seal and under that seal there is a name and nobody in that market read that name out loud and nobody asked to and nobody is going to be asked to; and that at about eleven in the morning a woman of thirty of no office read it out once in about twenty people in the open, and that about eleven of those twenty cannot read it, and that she stopped at the seal and did not read the name under it and nobody asked her to go on past it; and that it was not read out twice, because it is not a schedule and because a line that somebody wrote in another port does not get a second reading in this one; and that at about half past twelve in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane asked, in about nine people, whether this yard was going to set up a sheet for the names, and that she was told no, in about eighty-four words, on the grounds that a sheet of names is a sheet that will be filled in, and that the only reason a name goes onto a sheet is so that it can come off it later and be held against whoever put it there, and that if this harbor starts a sheet of custodians then this harbor has become the office that supplies them; and that nobody in that market was asked at any point in that day whether they would put a name to anything; and that nothing went onto that money sheet and nothing came off it, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that the first column takes one entry a day and three hundred and eighty-three and one is three hundred and eighty-eight at the close of it, and that the second takes five a day and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and five is one thousand eight hundred and fourteen.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason I did not read that sheet out twice is that a sheet of days with a day against every day has a second set of people to read it to, because people come in after the reading, and a sheet of requirements has only the one set, because a requirement does not have a time in it that anybody has to arrive before; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason I want it written down in this slip that I have spent the whole day looking for the wrong in that sheet and cannot find it is that this coast has been wrong about Crown paper before, in both directions, and that a keeper of a page who writes down only the sheets he found wrong will end up with a record of his own diligence and not of this coast; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have not written what a person on a line costs in this harbor is that I do not know it, and that I have got three figures and none of them are mine, being the figure in chalk for a day's hand, the twenty-eight pence under that slate, and the eight marks and a day of a boat that a bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for in three hundred and fifty-five days, and that a keeper of a page does not put three figures from three different people next to each other and call the sum a cost; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason I said in that shed that being fair is exactly what makes a thing hard is that a bad instrument can be answered in one place, and this one cannot, and that the whole of what that sheet has done is make it necessary to have the same conversation nine times, and that nine conversations is the price of it and there is no office in the world that will pay nine conversations, and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the stone went back on that sheet is that about eleven people in this harbor cannot read it, and that a stone on a sheet keeps the rain off it and does not keep anything else off it, and that whoever put that stone back did not say so and I did not ask and I am not going to; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason this slip opens on the shortfall is that this yard has had four days in a row of new things on walls and every one of them was somebody else's, and there is exactly one figure on those four days that belonged to this yard and did not move, and I am putting it first while I still can.*
