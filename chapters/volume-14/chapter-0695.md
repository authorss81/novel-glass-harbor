# Chapter 695

*Wednesday the Fifteenth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Not Charged and Its Covers Lying on It From Nine in the Morning Until Four in the Afternoon, the Page of the Twenty-Eighth of May Still Carrying Six Lines and Nothing Underneath Them for the Eighteen Days It Has Been Up, the Man of About Thirty-Two and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Both in That Shed at About Eleven in the Morning in About Nine People With Nobody Sent for Either of Them and Neither Speaking to the Other and No Resolution Written Anywhere, and a Seventh Line Going Under the Woman of About Twenty-Seven's Own Line at About Half Past Three in the Afternoon and Not Under Either of the First Two, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand and Fourteen*

Wednesday the fifteenth day of June, with that market shut for the morning and standing open from about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. The page of the twenty-eighth of May has now carried six lines for the eighteen days it has been up with nothing underneath any of them. At about eleven in the morning the man of about thirty-two of that harbor and the woman of thirty of no office were both in that shed in about nine people and nobody had sent for either of them, and neither of the two of them spoke to the other between eleven and four. At about half past three in the afternoon a seventh line went on that page and it went under the woman of about twenty-seven's own line and under neither of the first two. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-third day. Three hundred and ninety-fifth days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-third day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-ninth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and seventeenth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eighty-seventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fourth days. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-fifth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column stands empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in that harbor and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has put a hand into it. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and not one word has been said in this harbor about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-third days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Both of them were in that shed at about eleven in the morning and neither of them had sent for the other, and the man who keeps the leaf had sent for neither and had not asked either whether they were coming.

That is written down first because it is the only part of this morning that this yard did.

---

The page went up on that wall on the twenty-eighth of May with two lines in the middle of it, one under the other, and four lines under those in four other hands, and neither of the first two carries a name and neither of the two of them is named in the other's line.

It has been on that wall for the eighteen days since the twenty-eighth of May. Nothing has been written under any of the six lines, and nothing has been taken off any of them, and neither of the two people whose lines they are has come at that page to add to it or to take from it.

The plaster below it is bare and it has not been whitewashed and there is nothing on it.

---

At about eleven they were in that nine and they did not speak to each other about any of it.

He was at the end of that bench stone with his hands on it for about forty minutes and she was at the bench against the warm wall with a book of her own that she did not open, and about four people in that nine tried the two of them separately in the same way and got the same answer from both of them, which was nothing that could be written.

At about twelve he went out and stood at the end of the shed where the morning comes in for about twenty minutes and she stayed where she was and read about two pages and did not look at him doing it.

At about four he went up the steps by the rail and she went up the lane behind him by about twenty minutes, and neither of them turned round and neither of them waited.

---

Between about two and about three four people asked the man who keeps the leaf, separately, whether that page was settled.

He said the same thing to all four, which was that it is a page with two lines on it that say what a page does not do, and four lines under them from four people who are not the two of them, and it is not settled and it is not going to be settled by this yard, and that the two of them have not spoken about it in eighteen days and he is not going to be the person who makes them.

All four of them wanted to know what it would take. He said that he does not know and that a man who says he knows what it would take is a man who is about to start doing it to somebody.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon a seventh line went on that page and it went under the woman of about twenty-seven's own line and under neither of the first two.

She wrote it herself in about four minutes and read it out once, and it is her own line of the twenty-eighth of May that it sits under, and what it says is that she has read the two lines above hers again this week, that she does not know which of the two of them is right, that she is not changing hers, and that she is still not going to be the one who decides.

About five people cannot read a column and the four of them who were in that nine heard it read out. Nobody in that nine agreed with her line and nobody agreed with either of the first two lines, and nobody said that a seventh line had settled anything, and it has not settled anything.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Wednesday line on the page of that week and it says three things, being that the two of them were in that shed in about nine people at about eleven and neither spoke to the other and four people were asked separately whether it was settled and were told four times that it is not, and that a seventh line went under one of the four lines and not under the first two, and that nothing came off any of the six lines that were on that page this morning.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and ninety words, to about nine people, and about six of those nine had been in that shed at about eleven in the morning.

"**Two of them were in that shed this morning in about nine people and neither of them spoke to the other and neither of them sent for the other, and I sent for neither, and they were in one room for five hours and the whole of what happened between them is that nothing happened and I have written that down with the hours against it and with no name against it.**

**Four people asked me between two and three this afternoon whether that page was settled and I gave four of them the same answer, being that it is not, and that it is not going to be settled by this yard, and that the two of them have not spoken about it in eighteen days. All four of them asked me what it would take and I told all four of them that I do not know, and I want that said out loud, because in June a man who says he knows what it would take is a man who is about to start doing it to somebody.**

**And at about half past three this afternoon there is a seventh line on that page and it is under the line of a woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar and not under either of the first two. She wrote it herself in about four minutes and she read it out once. It says that she has read the two lines above hers again this week, that she does not know which of them is right, that she is not changing hers, and that she is still not going to be the one who decides. Nobody in that nine agreed with her and nobody agreed with the first two and nobody said it settled anything, and it has not settled anything. It is a seventh line on a page that anyone in this market can walk up to and read, and it binds her and nobody else, and that is the only thing a line under a line has ever bound.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and not a third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and eight days without one.**

**And the last of it. The plaster under that page is bare and it has not been whitewashed and there is nothing on it. The page of hands stands at fifteen. The far bench is occupied from ten until four and nobody has put anything on it and nobody has read anything off it as a gift. The coil that was on that nail on Friday is not on that nail today.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eighteen days of not speaking, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had five lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR AND THE WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE WERE BOTH IN THAT SHED IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND NOBODY HAD SENT FOR EITHER OF THEM AND NEITHER OF THE TWO OF THEM SPOKE TO THE OTHER BETWEEN ELEVEN AND FOUR, AND FOUR PEOPLE ASKED THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF, SEPARATELY AND BETWEEN TWO AND THREE IN THE AFTERNOON, WHETHER THAT PAGE WAS SETTLED, AND WERE TOLD FOUR TIMES THAT IT IS NOT AND THAT IT IS NOT GOING TO BE SETTLED BY THAT YARD. AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON A SEVENTH LINE WENT ON THAT PAGE UNDER THE LINE OF A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN WITH A BURN SCAR ACROSS ONE FOREARM AND UNDER NEITHER OF THE FIRST TWO, AND SHE WROTE IT HERSELF IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES AND READ IT OUT ONCE, AND IT SAYS THAT SHE HAS READ THE TWO LINES ABOVE HERS AGAIN AND DOES NOT KNOW WHICH OF THEM IS RIGHT AND IS NOT CHANGING HERS AND IS STILL NOT GOING TO BE THE ONE WHO DECIDES. NOTHING CAME OFF ANY OF THE SIX LINES THAT WERE ON THAT PAGE THIS MORNING AND THE PLASTER UNDER IT IS BARE AND HAS NOT BEEN WHITEWASHED. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fifteenth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that that butt behind the market was covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either; and that the page of the twenty-eighth of May has now carried six lines for three weeks with nothing underneath any of them, and that nothing has been written under any of the six lines and nothing has been taken off any of them, and that neither of the two people whose lines they are has come at that page to add to it or to take from it, and that the plaster below it is bare and has not been whitewashed; and that at about eleven in the morning both of them were in that shed in about nine people and neither had sent for the other and the man who keeps the leaf had sent for neither and had not asked either whether they were coming, and that he was at the end of that bench stone with his hands on it for about forty minutes and that she was at the bench against the warm wall with a book of her own that she did not open, and that about four people in that nine tried the two of them separately in the same way and got the same answer from both of them, and that at about twelve he went out and stood at the end of the shed where the morning comes in for about twenty minutes and that she stayed where she was and read about two pages and did not look at him doing it, and that at about four he went up the steps by the rail and that she went up the lane behind him by about twenty minutes and neither of them turned round and neither of them waited; and that between about two and about three four people asked the man who keeps the leaf, separately, whether that page was settled, and that he said the same thing to all four, being that it is not and that it is not going to be settled by this yard and that the two of them have not spoken about it in eighteen days, and that all four of them wanted to know what it would take and that he said that he does not know; and that at about half past three in the afternoon a seventh line went on that page under the line of the woman of about twenty-seven and under neither of the first two, and that she wrote it herself in about four minutes and read it out once, and that about five people cannot read a column and the four of them who were in that nine heard it read out, and that nobody in that nine agreed with her line and nobody agreed with either of the first two lines and nobody said that a seventh line had settled anything; and that at about four in the afternoon the Wednesday line was written on the page of that week; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and ninety words to about nine people.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason the seventh line went under her own line and not under the first two is that she wrote it and that a person has a right to say what her own line means and has no right whatever over anybody else's, and that if that line had gone under the two of them it would have looked like a vote and there were about nine people in that shed and about four of them cannot read a column and a vote read out loud to four people who cannot check it is the thing this coast has been refusing since May; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason four identical answers are written down four times is that the four of them asked four different questions and only one of the four was the question they asked, and that a page which recorded one answer four times would look like a count and this yard does not keep counts of what people say to it; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason five hours of two people in one room is written as nothing having happened is that this yard has been wrong once already in the other direction and wrote a silence down as though it were a finding, and that a silence between two people who have not spoken in eighteen days is not a finding and is not a resolution and is two people staying out of each other's sentences, which is the whole of what they are still doing; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the far bench is that a page of a week can be made to look settled by anything at all in a market, and the far bench has been occupied from ten until four every day this month with nothing put on it and nothing read off it, and that is the one thing on this coast that has stayed exactly as it was without anybody deciding that it should.
