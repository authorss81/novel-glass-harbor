# Chapter 678

*Sunday the Twenty-Ninth Day of May, a Wipe and the Hundred and Eighth Time That the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Has Come Down, Twenty-Five Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Twelve Standing Bare and Twenty-Five to Get It Back, Being Sixty-Two the Whole of It From About Ten Past Ten to About Twelve Minutes Past Eleven, a Minute Longer Each Way and a Minute Longer Bare Than the Sunday Before and the Second Minute in a Row That Nobody in This Harbor Has Accounted For, About Four People in That Shed on the Sunday Afternoon Asked One at a Time and by Nobody in Particular Whether Two People in This Harbor Are Still Speaking and Three of the Four Said Yes and One Said She Did Not Know, and This Yard Writing Down the Four Answers and Not One Word About the Two, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eleven and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Sunday the twenty-ninth day of May, with that market shut for the morning and standing open for the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe, and the hundred and eighth time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it, and that butt behind the market covered from nine until four with no charge on it. Two men began to take that wall off its holders at about ten past ten and it was bare by about half past ten and it was back on its holders at about twelve minutes past eleven, at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame is still not whole. Twenty-five minutes off the wall, twelve minutes standing bare, twenty-five to get it back, which is sixty-two minutes the whole of it, and that is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the Sunday before, and that is the second Sunday running that this harbor has spent a minute it cannot account for. At about half past two in the afternoon about four people in that shed were asked, one at a time and by nobody in particular, whether two people in this harbor are still speaking, and three of the four said yes and one said she did not know, and this yard wrote down the four answers and not one word about the two. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on that coast were carried a day today and every one of the nine was carried by somebody other than this yard. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and seventy-sixth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy-eighth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and sixteenth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-second night. Two lights at about a mile off that point are on their three hundredth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventieth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-seventh day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and eighteenth days. The third column of that leaf has taken nothing and is unappointed, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Nobody in this harbor has opened two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole, and nothing has been put on that board this month. In that boathouse there is a basket with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-sixth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came down bare for the hundred and eighth time and it went up again at about twelve minutes past eleven and it did not go up whole.

Twenty-five minutes to get it off the wall, twelve standing with it bare, and twenty-five to get it back, which is sixty-two the whole of it from about ten past ten, and that is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the Sunday before it, and the Sunday before that was a minute longer again, and neither of the two men who does that work has said one word about any of it.

Two Sundays now. This yard has stopped asking, and it is going to go on not asking, and it is not going to guess.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon, on the flat of that bench stone, about four people in that shed were asked one at a time whether two people in this harbor are still speaking.

Nobody asked on behalf of anybody. The man who keeps the leaf asked the four of them and wrote down what each of them said and put the hour against each and wrote no name against any of the four.

Three of the four said yes. One of the four said that she did not know and that she had not seen them together.

That is the whole of what is known about it and it is a fact about a Sunday afternoon and not about the two of them, and this yard has not asked either of them and is not going to.

---

At about four in the afternoon both of them were in that shed at the same time, for about ten minutes, and they were not in the same conversation and they were not avoiding each other, and that is what about nine people saw and about four of them have already put into the four answers this afternoon.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote down that they were both in that shed at about four. He did not write down what they said to each other because they did not say anything to each other, and he did not write down what either of them was thinking, and he is not going to ask.

---

A woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane came down to that yard at about half past four and asked whether the Sunday line on that new sheet is read out like the rest of the week or only when there is something on it worth reading out.

The man who keeps the leaf said that it is read out like the rest of the week whatever is on it, and that there are nine lines on that sheet at present and he was going to read all nine of them at half past five and had not decided to leave any of them off, and that if she wanted to hear it she could stand at the end of that shed at half past five and that is all the invitation there is.

She said that she would be there and she went up to the market.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and eighty words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been asked a question at about half past two that afternoon.

"**Sixty-two minutes the whole of it this morning, twenty-five off the wall, twelve standing bare, twenty-five back, from about ten past ten to about twelve minutes past eleven, and that is the hundred and eighth time, and it is the second Sunday running that this harbor has spent a minute it cannot put anywhere. I have not asked either man and I am not going to. A wall that costs a minute more every week is the only thing on this coast that has been going up by itself since the frost.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now ninety-one days without one.**

**Now the thing I said I would say last week and have not said. Four people were asked this afternoon, one at a time and by nobody in particular, whether two people in this harbor are still speaking. Three said yes and one said she did not know. I wrote all four of those down with the hour against them and no name against any of them and I am not writing one word about the two of them, and I am not going to be asked by anybody in this market to go and find out.**

**And here is the whole of my opinion on it, which nobody asked me for. This harbor has spent three weeks watching two people who are married argue in public about a line on a page, and it has been the most useful thing that has happened in that shed, because everybody in nine people can see exactly what is at stake and exactly what each of them is refusing to give up. If those two make it up on a Tuesday this yard loses the argument and gains nothing, and if they do not, then a page in that shed is going to carry a thing that nine people can read and two people cannot settle, and that is what a page is for.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four answers to a question nobody was asked on anybody's behalf, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TWELVE MINUTES PAST ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK TWENTY-FIVE MINUTES OFF THE WALL, TWELVE STANDING BARE AND TWENTY-FIVE TO GET IT BACK, BEING SIXTY-TWO MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT, AGAINST TWENTY-FOUR AND ELEVEN AND TWENTY-FOUR ON THE SUNDAY BEFORE, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY IT IS GETTING SLOWER. AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT SHED WERE ASKED ONE AT A TIME AND BY NOBODY IN PARTICULAR WHETHER TWO PEOPLE IN THAT HARBOR ARE STILL SPEAKING, AND THREE OF THE FOUR SAID YES AND ONE SAID SHE DID NOT KNOW, AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF WROTE DOWN THE FOUR ANSWERS WITH THE HOUR AGAINST EACH AND NO NAME AGAINST ANY OF THE FOUR AND WROTE DOWN NOT ONE WORD ABOUT THE TWO. BOTH OF THEM WERE IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT FOUR FOR ABOUT TEN MINUTES AND WERE NOT IN THE SAME CONVERSATION AND WERE NOT AVOIDING EACH OTHER. THAT MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND AT TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, SO THAT IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NO GAP WAS READ OUT OF AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN MOVED BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason two Sundays of an extra minute are written as a fact and not as a worry is that this yard has been wrong once on this coast about a thing that was going up by itself, and the cost of being wrong about a wall was four tacks and a piece of plaster the size of a coin, and the cost of being wrong about a person would be that person; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason four people were asked a question nobody was asked is that this yard was asked to do nothing this morning and did nothing, and that a Sunday afternoon with a wall going up and down it is not a day off for this harbor, it is a day on which whatever is in the shed is what the shed is, and that asking four bystanders one question is the smallest possible way of finding out whether the argument that has been filling that shed for three weeks is still filling it or whether it has gone quiet, and that four answers is four answers and not a count of anything; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason ten minutes in a shed is written down and what passed between the two of them in it is not, is that a man who keeps a leaf is entitled to record that two people were in a room and is not entitled to record what passed between them, and that this yard has been wrong about that once this month in the other direction and wrote down a silence as though it were a finding; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason a standing invitation to come and stand at the end of that shed at half past five was given instead of a reading arranged is that a reading arranged for one person is a reading in front of one person, and that a sheet which is read out to whoever turns up is the only kind that can be said to be public, and that this harbor has about eleven people who cannot read a notice and the whole reason for reading things out is them and not the people who can read; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on what this yard would lose if two people made it up is that the house's own argument this month has been that a coast which says what it will not carry out loud is harder to hold than a coast that agrees with itself smoothly, and that if this yard quietly wanted those two to settle it would be wanting a smooth coast, and a smooth coast is what a Crown wants and what this harbor has spent three weeks refusing.*
