# Chapter 666

*Tuesday the Seventeenth Day of May, the Eighty-Second Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Eighty-Second Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, and the Eighty-Second Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day, the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor Saying One Thing Out Loud at About Two in the Afternoon in About Nine People in About Two Hundred and Ninety Words and Not Being Answered, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Ninety-Nine and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Tuesday the seventeenth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-second wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. The column on the lid of that step stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four. The eighty-second week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the eighty-first, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two of that harbor said one thing out loud, in about nine people, in about two hundred and ninety words, and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and sixty-four days. A bellwright of this harbor, not asked for, three hundred and sixty-six. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and four days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventieth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point, two hundred and eighty-eight days. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall, its two hundred and forty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street, its two hundred and fifty-eighth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street, its two hundred and seventy-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one, three hundred and six days without a word to anybody about a page of six names. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has been nailed on that board this month, and those four lines have not come out of an upper room over that market and nothing has ever been written under the fourth of them, and the answer to them is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with the paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and sixty-four days, one more than yesterday, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes and it did not move, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay has read the same lid out on eighty-two Tuesdays and the number has not changed on any of them, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four.

Behind the eighty-second stands behind the eighty-first and behind that one stands behind the eightieth, and there is nobody in that market who has ever asked what those envelopes are for, because nobody in that market has ever been told.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood on the flat of that bench stone in about nine people and said one thing, and it was about a seam, and nobody in this harbor uses that word.

"**A seam is a place where two things that should be the same are not. That is all it is. It does not tell you which of the two is right. A road with a seam in it is not a bad road, and a page with a seam in it is not a false page, and a coast with a seam in it is not a coast that is lying.**

**We have got a seam. We have had one since Thursday. Two names and two hands and one man of about sixty-one who cannot read what he signed. Any fool in this market can put those three things together and come out with a story about somebody, and every fool in this market will, and I have watched two of them start today.**

**So here is the whole of what this yard is going to do about it. It is not going to use that seam to say the other eight lines on that wall are good. And it is not going to use that seam to say the other eight pages of this coast are false either. A difference shows you a difference and that is the entire amount of what it shows, and every time a coast has ever used one to prove the other half it has ended up owning the whole thing and calling it proof.**

**The man of about sixty-one asked to come off that page and he came off it on Monday, and I want it said in this shed that him coming off it is not a finding about the other three. It is one man, and it is his own page, and he is the only authority on it there is.**"

Nobody agreed with him. Nobody argued with him. A woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane said that a seam that proves nothing is a thing that will be here in ten years, and he said that it would, and that he would rather have it there than have it gone and the thing behind it still there.

Nobody thanked him for it.

---

At about half past three a man of about forty of that route's own third lay, who was in that yard on his own account and not on anybody's crew, said that the people who wrote that line on that wall should be asked what they meant by it, and that they were four days down that coast road and the cart takes four days each way.

The man of about thirty-two said that a route's own people say what a route means and not this yard, and that if that yard sent anybody up that coast road to ask what a line means then that yard would be the body that goes and asks, and that it is not.

Nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him for asking.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote it in the first column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and he wrote down that a seam is a place where two things that should be the same are not, in about fifteen words, as a definition, because this yard had never written one down and every fool in that market was going to use the word and none of them had agreed on what it meant.

He wrote that the definition is not a ruling and is not in a slip.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and four words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that market when it was said at about two.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April and not one hand on it since. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That butt behind that market stood covered from eight until four, because a rota day is a Monday and a Thursday and today is neither.**

**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on that lid for the eighty-second time, read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who is not on the water and has not been on the water, and the eighty-second week stands fallen due at the close of today, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and nothing that happened this afternoon came out of it.**

**And at about two this afternoon the man of about thirty-two stood on that stone in nine people and gave a seam a definition, out loud, in about two hundred and ninety words, and nobody answered him. I am not going to repeat his words back in this shed, because he said them in nine people this afternoon and a slip is not the place to make them standing, and I am not going to agree with them on a Tuesday, and I am not going to disagree with them either. What I am going to do is put fifteen words on a page at about four o'clock, being that a seam is a place where two things that should be the same are not, and that is a definition and not a ruling, and it is on that page because four people in this market were already using the word and none of them had the same idea in their heads.**

**One more thing and then I will stop. Somebody is going to try to use that seam. Not today and not out of malice. In about three weeks somebody who has read about those four names is going to come down that lane with a story that fits, and it is going to fit better than what I have got, and the reason it will fit better is that I have got a difference and a height and a man of about sixty-one and no story at all.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a definition, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of days had eighteen against the nine lines of it and nineteen still readable underneath, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE EIGHTY-SECOND WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY AND NOT ONE ENVELOPE IN THAT CHEST HAS BEEN OPENED ON ANY OF THEM. AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR SAID OUT LOUD, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND IN ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY WORDS, THAT A SEAM IS A PLACE WHERE TWO THINGS THAT SHOULD BE THE SAME ARE NOT, THAT IT DOES NOT SAY WHICH OF THE TWO IS RIGHT, AND THAT THIS YARD IS NOT GOING TO USE THAT SEAM TO SAY THE OTHER EIGHT LINES ON THAT WALL ARE GOOD OR TO SAY THE OTHER PAGES OF THIS COAST ARE FALSE, AND THAT A MAN COMING OFF A PAGE OF HIS OWN IS NOT A FINDING ABOUT THE OTHER THREE NAMES ON IT. NOBODY ANSWERED HIM AND NOBODY AGREED WITH HIM AND NOBODY THANKED HIM. AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF WROTE THAT DEFINITION IN NINE WORDS ON THAT PAGE AS A DEFINITION AND NOT A RULING. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the eighty-second is written down this week when it was not written down last week is that this yard has been counting Tuesdays for nine weeks without saying so out loud, and that a yard which notices its own counting in the middle of something else is a yard that has been counting for a reason it has not admitted to anybody; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have not repeated his words back at half past five is that he said them standing on that stone in nine people this afternoon, and that a slip is where a keeper puts the thing he is not allowed to lose, and a speech somebody made in nine people is not mine to keep and is not mine to repeat standing up, and if I repeat it I am the second person who said it and that is how a thing becomes this yard's position; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason the man of about forty-four was not argued with is that she is right that a seam which proves nothing is a thing that will be here in ten years, and that the man of about thirty-two is right that he would rather have it there than have it gone with the thing behind it still there, and that this yard is not going to choose between those two sentences this week; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the definition went on the page and not in that slip is that a definition is checkable by anybody who already uses the word and a ruling is only checkable by the person who made it, and this yard has refused to make rulings since before the frost; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason I wrote in this slip that somebody is going to come down that lane in about three weeks with a story that fits better than mine is that the honest arithmetic of this week is that I have a difference and a height and a man of about sixty-one and no story, and a page that has three facts and no story on it is the most useful thing this coast will own this month, and it is going to be useless within a month and that is the price of it and I am putting it down tonight so that whoever holds that leaf after me knows the price was known.*
