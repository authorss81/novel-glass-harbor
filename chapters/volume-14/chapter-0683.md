# Chapter 683

*Friday the Third Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and About Five of the Nine Answers In by About Two in the Afternoon, a Woman of About Thirty-Three Who Stands in the Tender Standing on the Boards at That End Stall at About Eleven in the Morning in About Eleven People and Saying Out Loud What She Will Do and Not Do on That Water, Being That She Will Be on the Surface and Not in It, That Her Own Line Comes Off a Winch of Her Own and Clear of the Working Boat's Fall, That the Air Is Twenty-Five Minutes and No More, That the Cutoff Is a Wire She Will Bend Into the Ring of Her Own Line With Her Own Hands and Keep the Loose End of Herself, and That She Will Not Ask Anybody Whether She Is Going to Do It, Nobody Thanked and Nobody Argued With, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Sixteen and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Friday the third day of June, with that market trading all day and the boards at the back of it busy from nine in the morning until about two in the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. By about two in the afternoon about five of the nine answers were in, and a woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender stood at that end stall at about eleven in the morning in about eleven people and said out loud what she would do and what she would not do on that water. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on this coast were carried a day today and not one of them by this yard. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and eighty-first day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-third days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and twenty-first day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-seventh night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and fifth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and sixtieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-fifth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-second day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-third days. That third column has taken nothing, nobody has been put in it, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-first days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

She said it at the end stall and not in that shed, and the man who keeps the leaf walked down to hear it and about ten other people were already standing there.

She stood on the boards with her back to the sheet of fourteen gaps and the page of hands beside her and she said it once and she did not say it again, and nobody asked her to say it again.

About eleven people heard the whole of it and about six of those eleven cannot read a column and she did not slow down for any of them and afterwards she said she was not going to, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote that down with the hour against it.

---

What she said was that she will be on the surface and not in the water.

She said that she is not going in and that she has not gone in on any day this year and that anybody who wants that changed can come and ask her and she has got a day coming and it is not this one.

Then she said that her own line comes off a winch of her own and clear of the working boat's fall, and that if it comes off that boat's gear she will take her hands off it and walk ashore, and that this is not a figure of words and she has said it in front of eleven people so that it is.

---

Then she said the air, and she said it as a number and not as a feeling.

She said that the air is twenty-five minutes and no more, and that it is twenty-five minutes whatever else happens, and that the limit was fixed before anybody went and looked at anything and that it is not going to be moved because somebody found something they wanted to look at.

Then she said the cutoff, and she said it slowly, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote all of it down in the first column with the hour against it.

She said that it is a wire, and that she will bend it into the ring of her own line with her own hands on the morning, and that she will keep the loose end of it herself, and that pulling it does not require a word from anybody and does not require anybody's agreement and does not require anybody to have been asked first.

Nobody in that eleven said that was too much and nobody said it was not enough.

At about half past eleven a man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard asked her one question, being whether the cutoff worked if the boat were drifting, and she said that it worked better if the boat were drifting, and that this was the reason for a cutoff and not a bell.

---

At about two in the afternoon five of the nine answers were in and the man who keeps the leaf laid all five out on that stone in the order they came and read them before he answered any.

One of the five was a second sheet in a different hand and it said in about thirty-eight words that the community would put two people on that water and would choose between them on the morning, and that it wanted it written down that it was offering two so that this yard could drop one if the other did not come.

That is the only answer of the nine that has offered this yard something it had not asked for, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote it down and wrote no answer to it and asked no question about it.

Two of the nine had still not come and this yard did not go and find out, and that is on the face of the sheet.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Friday line on that sheet and it says that about eleven people at an end stall at about eleven in the morning heard a woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender say that she will be on the surface and not in the water, and that her own line comes off a winch of her own and clear of the working boat's fall, and that the air is twenty-five minutes and no more, and that the cutoff is a wire she will bend into the ring of her own line with her own hands and that she will keep the loose end of herself, and that pulling it requires no word from anybody.

He wrote the twenty-five minutes as a number against the day and he put no figure of money against her, and he wrote on the face of the sheet that nobody was thanked and nobody argued with.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and ninety-three words, to about nine people, and about five of those nine had been standing at that end stall at about eleven.

"**I am going to do the thing I have done every day this month and then I am going to say the one thing on it, and the one thing on it is a woman standing on those boards this morning in front of about eleven people telling this yard what she is going to do, and I am not going to make it a bigger thing than it was.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now ninety-six days without one.**

**What she said this morning, in the order she said it. She is on the surface and not in the water. Her line comes off a winch of her own, clear of that working boat's fall, and if it comes off that boat's gear she takes her hands off it and walks ashore. The air is twenty-five minutes and no more, and the limit was fixed before anybody went and looked at anything and it is not going to be moved because somebody found something they wanted to look at. And the cutoff is a wire she bends into the ring of her own line with her own hands and the loose end stays with herself, and pulling it does not need a word from anybody and does not need anybody to have been asked first.**

**And here is what I am not going to do with any of it. I am not going to put her name down as somebody who had done this yard a kindness, because a woman who says out loud what she will not have done to her is not doing this yard a kindness and there is no office on this coast she has taken a post in. I am not going to write her name beside this yard's name on that sheet, because neither name is on any page of this harbor and this is not the morning for that. And I am not going to argue with one word of it, and if a man in this market wants to argue with one word of it he can come down to that stall on Monday and she will be the one standing on it and not me.**

**And the last of it. Five of those nine answers are in and one of the five has offered us two people instead of one so that we could drop one if the other did not come. I have written that down and I have written no answer to it. A yard that answers an offer like that is a yard that has started to select, and this yard does not select.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a woman saying four things out loud on a set of boards, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had three lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO STANDS IN THE TENDER STOOD AT THAT END STALL AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE AND SAID ONCE, AND NOT AGAIN, THAT SHE WILL BE ON THE SURFACE AND NOT IN THE WATER, AND THAT SHE HAS NOT GONE IN ON ANY DAY THIS YEAR, AND THAT HER OWN LINE COMES OFF A WINCH OF HER OWN AND CLEAR OF THE WORKING BOAT'S FALL, AND THAT IF IT COMES OFF THAT BOAT'S GEAR SHE WILL TAKE HER HANDS OFF IT AND WALK ASHORE. SHE SAID THE AIR IS TWENTY-FIVE MINUTES AND NO MORE AND THAT THE LIMIT WAS FIXED BEFORE ANYBODY WENT AND LOOKED AT ANYTHING. SHE SAID THE CUTOFF IS A WIRE SHE WILL BEND INTO THE RING OF HER OWN LINE WITH HER OWN HANDS AND KEEP THE LOOSE END OF HERSELF, AND THAT PULLING IT REQUIRES A WORD FROM NOBODY AND THE AGREEMENT OF NOBODY. NOBODY IN THAT ELEVEN SAID IT WAS TOO MUCH AND NOBODY SAID IT WAS NOT ENOUGH, AND NOBODY WAS THANKED AND NOBODY WAS ARGUED WITH. BY ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON FIVE OF THE NINE ANSWERS WERE IN AND ONE OF THOSE FIVE WAS A SECOND SHEET OFFERING TWO PEOPLE INSTEAD OF ONE. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason it was said at an end stall and not in that shed is that a shed has four sheets on its wall and about eleven people who cannot read a column, and that a thing said in a shed gets written on and a thing said on a stall gets repeated, and that a woman who tells this coast what she will not have done to her should say it where the repeating is done by other people and not by a keeper with a pencil; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason she said it once and would not say it again is that a second reading of a limit is an invitation to discuss it and this coast has spent four weeks learning that; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the air was given as a number before anything else was said about that water is that every other thing that morning was going to be an opinion and a number is the only one of the four that anybody in that eleven could have checked afterwards, and that she put it first on purpose; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason no answer was written against the offer of two people is that this yard needs five and has five and an offer of a second is not a spare, it is a community deciding between two of its own people and this yard being told in advance so that it can complain later if it does not like the answer; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the twenty-five minutes went on that sheet as a figure is that it is the only figure on the wall this month that anybody can check and that if it is written down it cannot be quietly made twenty-eight on the morning; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on not thanking her is that this harbor has six refusals of lists of people in it and every one of them turned on somebody being recorded as having been glad, and a kept name beside a kept name is the first line of every one of those six.*
