# Chapter 654

*Thursday the Fifth Day of May, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Forty-Seventh Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Left Lying on It from About Eleven Until Four, a Page Up That Coast Road in a Bundle of Twine With Thirty-Two Words on It, Sixteen on the Front and Sixteen on the Back, in a Hand That Has Been Rained On and One of the Sixteen on the Front Saying That a Fouled Bell Answers Wrong, a Hire at the Figure in Chalk and a Saturday Booked at About Thirty Tons and Not One Penny of It on That Money Sheet and Not One Name Against Any of It, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Eighty-Seven and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Nine*

Thursday the fifth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and forty-seventh charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four. At about one in the afternoon a page came up that coast road in a bundle of twine with thirty-two words on it, sixteen on the front and sixteen on the back, in a hand that has been rained on, and it is the answer of the route that put the line of eleven words on that wall on Monday, and the man who keeps the leaf entered it in the first column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hire at the figure in chalk is not a line on that sheet and is paid by the route and not by this yard. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood three hundred and fifty-two days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and fifty-four days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and ninety-two days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and fifty-eighth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and seventy-six days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and thirty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and forty-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and sixty-third day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and ninety-four days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fifty-two days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and forty-seventh charge, and that is the same eleven trips and the same four men and the same stairs on the inside of that wall.

At about eleven the covers went on and they came off again at about four, and nothing was done to that water by anybody.

---

At about one in the afternoon the page came up that coast road in a bundle of twine, and it had been rained on in the cart and the rain had got into the fold and taken the ink in two places, and the man of about forty-one of Monday had sent it back by the same cart with a man of about nineteen who is of the third lay there and is on nobody's crew in this harbor.

There are sixteen words on the front of it. They are these.

**The Weatherway wants its buoy. A fouled bell answers wrong. We do not carry on wrong.**

And there are sixteen words on the back of it, which are the ones the yard acted on.

**We hire a boat at your figure. You send a hand. You do not give orders.**

Nobody in that yard had asked for any of those thirty-two words and nobody in that yard has been asked anything else about that buoy, and it came up a coast road in a bundle of twine without anybody having written to anybody asking.

The man who keeps the leaf entered it in the first column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and entered separately, in the same column and in the same hand, that the hire of that boat is at the figure in chalk and is a line on that route's own page and not a line on that money sheet, and that the route pays it and this yard does not, and that no penny of it comes out of the twenty-eight pence under that slate or out of any boat's owner or out of the Crown.

---

At about two in the afternoon the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender came into that yard and looked at the water and said that Saturday would do and that she wanted a reason written down before anybody went out in it, in the diver's own words, with an hour at the foot of it, and that she wanted the reason to say what the job was and not what it might turn out to be.

The man of about thirty-two wrote it out and gave it to her and it says that the shackle is fouled and that a fouled shackle answers wrong and that the route cannot carry anything on a line that answers wrong. She read it and put it in the inside of her coat and did not give it back.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two was asked, in about nine people, whether this yard would go out on Saturday, and he said that it would go out if the route's own people said where.

Nobody asked him whether he wanted to. Nobody thanked him for going and nobody argued with him about it, and about four people in that nine heard the whole of it and the rest of that market was not told.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about two hundred and ninety-two words, to about nine people.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April and not one hand on it since. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and there is no second line and there is not going to be one this month. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand this morning and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and forty-seventh, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings.**

**And thirty-two words came up that coast road in a bundle of twine today and not one of them was asked for, and sixteen of them are on the back of the page and all sixteen of those are about what this yard is not allowed to do, and I have put them in that leaf with the hour against them and no name against them. A fouled bell answers wrong. We do not carry on wrong. That is a line and not a reason and it is a better line than the one on the wall, and I am not going to say that to anybody because they wrote it and I did not.**"

**Here is what that page does and it is the reason I have read it out twice in nine people this afternoon and it is not because anybody might have missed a word. A route four days down that coast road has hired a boat at a figure in chalk, and it has told this yard that it may send a hand and may not give an order, and it has not asked this yard for a single thing to be done differently from the way this yard already does it. That is not a favour. I want that written down plainly, because a yard that has been trusted with thirty-two words in a year calls that a favour and puts it on a page, and a page with favours on it is a page that somebody can lean on. That is a hire. The route's people are paying a day's wages out of their own page for a hand off a boat of about thirty tons, and this yard is going to be paid the same figure in chalk as any hand on any berth in this harbor, and the money is not this yard's money and the order is not this yard's order.**

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for thirty-two words out of a cart, and that shed stood empty by about six, and the boat is booked for Saturday at about thirty tons and nobody in that yard has been told what time it sails because that was not on the page.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND IT IS THE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SEVENTH CHARGE, THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. THIRTY-TWO WORDS CAME UP THAT COAST ROAD IN A BUNDLE OF TWINE AT ABOUT ONE IN THE AFTERNOON, BEING SIXTEEN ON THE FRONT OF IT THAT THE WEATHERWAY WANTS ITS BUOY, THAT A FOULED BELL ANSWERS WRONG AND THAT THEY DO NOT CARRY ON WRONG, AND SIXTEEN ON THE BACK OF IT THAT THEY HIRE A BOAT AT THE FIGURE IN CHALK, THAT THIS YARD MAY SEND A HAND AND THAT THIS YARD MAY NOT GIVE ORDERS, AND NOT ONE WORD OF EITHER SET HAD BEEN ASKED FOR BY ANYBODY IN THIS YARD. THAT HIRE IS A LINE ON THAT ROUTE'S OWN PAGE AND NOT A LINE ON THAT MONEY SHEET AND IS PAID BY THAT ROUTE. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the fifth day of May at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, and that it is the hundred and forty-seventh charge and that the covers were left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon; and that at about one in the afternoon a page came up that coast road in a bundle of twine and that the rain had got into the fold of it and taken the ink in two places, and that it had been sent back by the same cart with a man of about nineteen who is of the third lay there and is on nobody's crew in this harbor; and that there are sixteen words on the front of that page and sixteen on the back of it, and that the sixteen on the front are that the Weatherway wants its buoy, that a fouled bell answers wrong, and that they do not carry on wrong, and that the sixteen on the back are that they hire a boat at the figure in chalk, that this yard may send a hand, and that this yard may not give orders; and that nobody in that yard had asked for any of those thirty-two words and nobody in that yard has been asked anything else about that buoy; and that it was entered in this column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and that separately it was entered that the hire of that boat is at the figure in chalk and is a line on that route's own page and not a line on that money sheet, and that the route pays it and this yard does not, and that no penny of it comes out of the twenty-eight pence under that slate or out of any boat's owner or out of the Crown; and that at about two in the afternoon a woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender came into that yard and said that Saturday would do and that she wanted a reason written down in the diver's own words with an hour at the foot of it before anybody went out in it, and that she wanted that reason to say what the job was and not what it might turn out to be, and that it was so written and that it says that the shackle is fouled and that a fouled shackle answers wrong and that the route cannot carry anything on a line that answers wrong, and that she read it and put it in the inside of her coat and did not give it back; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two was asked, in about nine people, whether this yard would go out on Saturday, and that he said it would go out if the route's own people said where; and that nobody asked him whether he wanted to and nobody thanked him for going; and that nothing went onto that money sheet and nothing came off it, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that the first column takes one entry a day and three hundred and eighty-three and one is three hundred and eighty-seven at the close of it, and that the second takes five a day and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and five is one thousand eight hundred and nine.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason I wrote down four men this yard does not know did the carrying of that cistern is that the rota is a rota and not a payroll, and that those four men have carried that cistern eleven times each since the autumn and not one of them has ever been asked what the water is for, and that if this yard ever wants them asked then it will want them asked on a day when somebody is watching; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason I entered that page in the first column and not in the second is that the first column takes one entry a day whatever else that day prints, and thirty-two words off a coast road is the entry for today, and if I put it in the second column then today would have had nothing in it but eleven trips by hand; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the reason was written down before anybody went out in it is that it is the tender's condition and not mine, and that she asked for it in words and not for it in a mood, and that what she asked for was a reason that says what the job is and not what it might turn out to be, and that on Monday there was a man in nine people who would have liked that job to be about a mooring and it is going to be about a shackle; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason I have written that a route four days down that coast road did not ask this yard for one single thing to be done differently is that a yard that has been trusted with nineteen words in a year calls that a favour, and a page with favours on it is a page somebody can lean on, and this harbor has been leaning on pages for thirteen volumes and there are not enough hands left in it, and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I do not know what time that boat sails is that it was not on the page, and that not knowing what time a boat of about thirty tons sails on Saturday is the correct state of this yard's mind and I intend to keep it; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason this slip opens on that cistern is that a cistern is a thing a stranger can watch from a mole, and there are five people in this yard who cannot read a column and who have all been here since eight this morning.*
