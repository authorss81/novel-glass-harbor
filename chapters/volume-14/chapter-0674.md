# Chapter 674

*Wednesday the Twenty-Fifth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and at About Ten in the Morning at That End Stall in About Nine People a Man of About Fifty of the Lowfen Reed's Own Third Lay Asking Out Loud a Woman of About Forty of the Cinder Bell's Own Community for One Night of Work on the Reed's Own Line, and Her Saying She Would Ask Her Own People and Coming Back at About Eleven With the Answer That Four of Them Will Come on Their Own Time and Under Their Own Order and That the Bell Runs Nothing and Has Run Nothing and That Neither of Those Two Routes Owns the Other, and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Writing the Whole of It on the Wednesday Line of the Page of That Week With No Figure Against It and No Name Against Either Route, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seven and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Nine*

Wednesday the twenty-fifth day of May, with that market trading all day and the boards at the back of it busy from nine in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. At about ten in the morning, at that end stall, in about nine people, a man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay asked out loud a woman of about forty of the Cinder Bell's own community for one night of work on the Reed's own line, and she said she would ask her own people, and at about eleven she came back and said that four of them will come on their own time and under their own order, and that the Bell runs nothing and has run nothing, and that neither of those two routes owns the other. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the whole of it on the Wednesday line of the page of that week with no figure against it and no name against either route. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in this harbor run on without anybody watching them, and every one of them can be read off a surface this afternoon. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and seventy-second day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy-fourth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and twelfth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-eighth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point are on their two hundred and ninety-sixth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-third day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and fourteenth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. That board at the head of that mole carries five papers, and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been put on it this month. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-second days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten in the morning the man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay put his hand flat on the boards of that end stall, beside the sheet of fourteen gaps and the page of hands, and asked a woman of about forty of the Cinder Bell's own community for one night of work on the Reed's own line.

About nine people were standing there. He said it out loud and he said it in front of all of them and he did not ask this yard whether he might.

---

She said she would ask her own people and she went up the lane and came back at about eleven, and what she said was that four of them will come on their own time and under their own order, and that they will be on the Reed's line and not on any other, and that they are going because they said they would and not because anybody in this yard asked them.

Then she said the thing that made it an arrangement and not a favour.

She said that the Bell runs nothing and has run nothing for a long time, and that she was not speaking for a route that was doing anything on Wednesday, and that she was speaking for four people who were going to be at home.

She said that the Reed's page does not take the Bell's name as a custodian and that the Bell's page does not take the Reed's, and that four people on the water for one night does not make them the Reed's and does not make the Reed theirs, and that if either of those two routes ever wants to say otherwise it can come to this shed and say it in front of nine people and this yard will write it down.

Nobody in that nine agreed with her and nobody in that nine argued with her.

---

At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-two of that harbor asked one question in that nine people, and it was whether this yard had sent for anybody, and the man of about fifty of the Reed said that it had not, and the woman of about forty of the Bell said the same, and the man of about thirty-two said that was the whole of what he wanted to know and asked nothing else.

He was not thanked for asking. Nobody said one word about him afterwards.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Wednesday line on that sheet and read it out in about nine people, and there is no figure on it and there is no name on it and neither route's name is on it.

It says that at about ten in the morning one route's own third lay asked one community for one night, and that at about eleven that community said four of its own people would come on their own time and under their own order, and that neither of the two owns the other, and that this yard asked nobody and sent for nobody and hired nobody and paid nobody.

He wrote on the face of the sheet, under that line, that the night's work will not put a name against anybody's written limits, and that the page of hands has not been touched this week and will not be touched on the strength of four people being on the water.

Nobody thanked him for the line and nobody asked him whether he had got it right.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and fourteen words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been standing at that end stall at about ten.

"**This morning, at about ten, at that end stall, in about nine people, one of this coast's own routes asked another one for a night, and the other one said yes, and neither of them owns the other, and I want the whole of that said in a shed before I say a single figure, because everything else this week is smaller than it.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now eighty-seven days without one.**

**Four things about this morning and none of them is mine. One: this yard did not send for anybody and did not ask either of them whether they might, and the asking was done at a stall by a man from a route that is short of hands to a woman from a route that runs nothing. Two: they said yes to each other and not to this yard, and if that had gone the other way this shed would have heard about it by Tuesday. Three: neither of them owns the other and both of them said so out loud in nine people before anybody had written a word, and that is the only reason I am willing to write a word. Four: no figure of what that night costs is in this yard's book and none is going in, and I did not go and get one.**

**And the thing I want on this slip and I am going to say it once. For eleven days this coast has been building nine pages of what it will not carry, and every one of the nine was a page about a community saying no to something. This morning a community said yes to a thing that costs it a night of four people's work, out loud, in a market, with nothing behind it. I do not know what that is worth. I know it is not one crew, and I know it is not a company, and I know that a page of limits is a wall and not a rudder, and if this coast ever lets one of these nights turn into the other thing then the nine pages come down and they should.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a night that four people are going to work, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had three lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AT THAT END STALL, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY OF THE LOWFEN REED'S OWN THIRD LAY ASKED OUT LOUD A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY OF THE CINDER BELL'S OWN COMMUNITY FOR ONE NIGHT OF WORK ON THE REED'S OWN LINE, AND SHE SAID SHE WOULD ASK HER OWN PEOPLE, AND AT ABOUT ELEVEN SHE CAME BACK AND SAID THAT FOUR OF THEM WILL COME ON THEIR OWN TIME AND UNDER THEIR OWN ORDER, AND THAT THE BELL RUNS NOTHING AND HAS RUN NOTHING FOR A LONG TIME, AND THAT THE REED'S PAGE DOES NOT TAKE THE BELL'S NAME AS A CUSTODIAN AND THAT THE BELL'S PAGE DOES NOT TAKE THE REED'S, AND THAT FOUR PEOPLE ON THE WATER FOR ONE NIGHT DOES NOT MAKE THEM THE REED'S AND DOES NOT MAKE THE REED THEIRS. NOBODY IN THAT NINE AGREED WITH HER AND NOBODY IN THAT NINE ARGUED WITH HER. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR ASKED ONE QUESTION IN THAT NINE PEOPLE, BEING WHETHER THIS YARD HAD SENT FOR ANYBODY, AND BOTH OF THEM SAID THAT IT HAD NOT, AND HE ASKED NOTHING ELSE AND WAS NOT THANKED FOR ASKING. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the asking is written into the first column as a thing that happened and not as two people who did a thing is that this yard has spent a fortnight refusing to keep names and the only way that holds is if it holds on the morning a thing like this happens and not only on a quiet one; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the whole of what she said is entered and not a summary of it is that a summary of that answer would have been the yard's version of it, and that the four clauses she used are the four clauses that keep this coast out of the thing it is most likely to drift into, which is one coast with nine names on it; and that at about half past eleven in the morning the reason the man of about thirty-two was allowed to ask one question is that a rule this yard works by is worth nothing on the day somebody in that yard breaks it, and that the one question he asked was the one rule anybody in that yard could have broken, and that it is better that nine people heard the answer than that this yard decided in private that it had not sent for anybody; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason nothing was written against the page of hands is that the page of hands counts names against a route's own written limits, and that four people being on the water for another route for one night is the opposite of putting a name to a limit, it is putting a night to somebody else's work, and if this yard wrote it there it would be the first step towards writing on that page the name of a person somebody else needed; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip is given over to one morning and a half page of it is that this is the biggest thing that has happened in that shed since the fourth of May and a slip that spends three quarters of itself on a shortfall nobody can do anything about is a slip that has stopped being a record and become a way of feeling better.*
