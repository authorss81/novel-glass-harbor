# Chapter 656

*Saturday the Seventh Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Boat of About Thirty Tons Out at About Seven in the Morning With a Tender on the Surface and Not in the Water and an Independent Line and a Fixed Air Limit of Twenty-Five Minutes Written Down With the Hour Against It Before Anybody Went Down, a Reason in the Diver's Own Words That Says What the Job Was and Not What It Might Turn Out to Be, About Two Fathoms and About Eleven Minutes and One Handhold and a Join in the Ring Not Used, a Bucket With About an Inch of Green Belt in It and a Ring Left as It Was and Nothing Lifted and No Moorings Retied, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Hire on the Route's Own Page and Not on This One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Eighty-Nine and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Nineteen*

Saturday the seventh day of May, with that market shut for the morning and a boat of about thirty tons lying against that mole at about seven in the morning with her deck washed and about four people on it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. A boat of about thirty tons went out at about seven and was alongside the bell buoy on the Saltmarch Weatherway at about half past nine and came back into that harbor at about one in the afternoon. Nobody went down to more than about two fathoms and nobody went down twice and nobody went down at the top of a flood. The air limit was twenty-five minutes and the time in the water was about eleven, and the reason was written down in the diver's own words with the hour at the foot of it before anybody went down, and the tender was on the surface and not in the water with an independent line and a cutoff she can pull without asking, and nobody argued with her when she used it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and the hire of that boat is a line on that route's own page. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood three hundred and fifty-four days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and fifty-six days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and ninety-four days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and sixtieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and seventy-eight days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and thirty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and forty-eighth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and sixty-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and ninety-six days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fifty-four days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

The reason was written out at about half past six in the morning at the flat of that bench stone in the diver's own words, with the hour at the foot of it, and it says that the shackle is fouled and that a fouled shackle answers wrong and that the route cannot carry anything on a line that answers wrong. It does not say anything about a mooring and it does not say anything about a boat and it does not say that anybody has done anything wrong to anybody.

The tender read it and put it in the inside of her coat and said that if the job turned out to be something else then she would want a second reason and a second hour, and there was not a second reason and there was not a second hour.

---

They were alongside that buoy at about half past nine. It is a bell buoy of about two tons with a shackle and a pin at the top of it and a ring where the moorings go, and the ring is round and about the size of a man's fist, and the green on it is about an inch thick and it has been there three months and not three years.

There is a join in that ring. It is not a weld. Two pieces of iron were made somewhere and put round each other and closed, and where they meet there is a step a thumbnail will go into, and it was made by somebody, and the man of about thirty-two could see it from the surface in about two fathoms with the light going the way it was going.

He did not use it.

He went down on the second line at about ten with one handhold and he came up at about eleven and he was in the water about eleven minutes against a limit of twenty-five, and the tender called him up on the third call and he came up on the third call.

---

What came up was a shackle and a pin and about an inch of green belt in a bucket, and nothing else came up.

The buoy was not lifted. The moorings were not retied. The ring was left as it is, and the join in it was left as it is, and a man of about forty-one of that route's third lay stood on that deck and looked at that ring for a while and then said a word that was not a name and went and coiled a line.

---

At about two in the afternoon, in the cabin of that boat, the man of about thirty-two said four things out loud in about one hundred and eighty-four words to about six people, and nobody argued with any of them.

"**The ring is two pieces of iron and somebody closed them. That is all I know about it. A join tells you two things were made separately. It does not tell you which one was made first and it does not tell you whose hand was on the second one, and I could have used it this morning and I would have got a morning out of it and I would have been wrong about the afternoon.**

**There is an inch of green on that shackle and the shackle is bright under the green where the green is not, and that is what a woman of about twenty-four told us on Tuesday standing up in this yard without reading a word of what she told us out, and she was right, and I went down to find out what she had seen.**"

**My hands were not steady enough to hold a wedge when I came up and I said so at the time and I am saying it again now so that nobody has to ask me. I am not on the water tomorrow.**

Nobody thanked him for the eleven minutes and nobody thanked the tender for the third call.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about three hundred and nineteen words, to about nine people.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April and nothing came off it and nothing went on it today, and that hire is on that route's own page and not on this one and this yard has not been paid a penny of it and is not going to be. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence on that board in the front street, unmoved. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**A boat of about thirty tons was alongside that buoy at about half past nine this morning and a man was in the water about eleven minutes and came up on the third call out of a limit of twenty-five, and the tender was on the surface the whole time and not in the water, and there was a reason on the bench stone at about half past six this morning in his own words with the hour at the foot of it, and I wrote down that it was at about half past six and not that it was written, because a page that records when a thing was written is a page about writing and this is a page about shackles.**"

**And here is what happened and what did not happen, and I want the two halves kept apart in this slip. About an inch of green came up in a bucket and it was three months old and the shackle under it was bright, and that is what a woman of about twenty-four told us on Tuesday. And there is a join in that ring that he could have used and did not use, and the reason is in that cabin's own mouth and it is that a join shows that two things were made separately and not which of them was made first. So we came back with a bucket and a reason and a woman of thirty-three with a watch, and we came back with nothing that anybody can put in front of a Crown boat and say, look what your men did.**

**And I am going to write down the part that this yard gets wrong, because it gets it wrong every time. This yard went out today because a man of about forty-one read one line out loud on Monday and a man of about nineteen brought thirty-two words up a coast road in a cart on Thursday, and between those two things a Crown boat had taken a mooring off a buoy nobody here owns. If those two things had not happened the mooring would still be off and nobody would have gone near it and nobody would have looked at that ring. I do not know what to do with that and I am not going to pretend I have.**

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eleven minutes in about two fathoms, and that shed stood empty by about six, and the bucket with the belt of green in it was on the bench stone at the back of that shed at the close of the day and the green was already going black at the edges.

**A BOAT OF ABOUT THIRTY TONS WAS ALONGSIDE THAT BELL BUOY AT ABOUT HALF PAST NINE THIS MORNING AND A MAN WAS IN THE WATER ABOUT ELEVEN MINUTES AGAINST A FIXED AIR LIMIT OF TWENTY-FIVE MINUTES, WITH A TENDER ON THE SURFACE AND NOT IN THE WATER, AN INDEPENDENT LINE, A REASON WRITTEN DOWN AT ABOUT HALF PAST SIX IN THE DIVER'S OWN WORDS WITH THE HOUR AT THE FOOT OF IT, AND A CUTOFF THE TENDER COULD PULL WITHOUT ASKING, AND SHE USED IT ON THE THIRD CALL AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HER. THERE IS A JOIN IN THAT RING AND IT WAS NOT USED, AND THE REASON WAS GIVEN OUT LOUD ON THE DECK AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR WORDS, AND THAT TWO PIECES OF IRON WERE MADE SEPARATELY AND THAT A JOIN DOES NOT SAY WHICH OF THEM WAS MADE FIRST. ABOUT AN INCH OF GREEN BELT CAME UP IN A BUCKET AND IT WAS THREE MONTHS OLD AND THE SHACKLE UNDER IT WAS BRIGHT, AND THE BUOY WAS NOT LIFTED AND THE MOORINGS WERE NOT RETIED AND THE RING WAS LEFT AS IT IS. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, AND THAT HIRE IS ON THAT ROUTE'S OWN PAGE. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past six in the morning the reason I wrote down that the reason was written at about half past six and did not write down that it was written is that a page that records when a thing was written is a page about writing, and this is a page about shackles, and there are only so many hours in a day that I can afford to spend on either;
