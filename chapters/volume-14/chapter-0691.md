# Chapter 691

*Saturday the Eleventh Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Not Charged and Its Covers Lying on It From Nine in the Morning Until Four in the Afternoon, the Boat of About Thirty Tons Back Against the Third Lay at About Eight With Her Gear Still Aboard and Her Own Line Still on Her, the Five Who Went Down on Wednesday All of Them Back in Their Own Communities by About Ten This Morning With Five Towns Written Down and No Name Against Any of Them, the Woman of About Thirty-Three Taking Her Own Coil Off Its Own Nail at About Half Past Ten Without Being Asked, and the Man of About Thirty-Two Asleep From About Ten on Friday Night Until About Five This Morning for the First Time in Three Nights and No Figure of Money Set Against Any Hour of It, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Four and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Saturday the eleventh day of June, with that market open all day and about seventy people on those boards before eleven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. At about eight this morning the boat of about thirty tons came back up and went alongside the third lay with her gear still aboard her and her own line still coiled where it lay on Wednesday. At about ten this morning all five of the people who went down off that mole on Wednesday were back in their own communities and the five towns were written down with no name against any of them. At about half past ten the coil that had hung on its own nail in that shed since Friday came off that nail and nobody in that yard saw the woman of about thirty-three take it and nobody asked her where she put it. The man of about thirty-two of that harbor was asleep from about ten on Friday night until about five this morning, which is the first sleep he has had in three nights, and no figure of money stands against any hour of it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on this coast were carried a day further today by nobody in this yard, and eight of the nine are about things that are not being done. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has reached its three hundred and eighty-ninth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and ninety-one days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and twenty-ninth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-fifth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and thirteen days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has come to its two hundred and sixty-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and eighty-third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has reached its three hundredth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-one days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two stands at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing went up on that board this month. A basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has reached in a hand. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine were there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said no word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-nine days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The boat came in at about eight and did not sound for anybody, and the man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard went out to her with a line and put her head on and she was alongside the third lay by about ten past.

She had her gear still aboard her. Forty yards of line and two seats and a watch box and the coil of the independent line came ashore off her on Thursday night at about two in the morning, when she was let go, and what is aboard her this morning is her own gear and nobody else's.

Nobody in that yard asked where she had been between Friday and this morning, and the man who hires hands said afterwards that he had not needed to, because a boat is a boat and this yard does not hire a boat's inside.

---

At about ten the man who keeps the leaf wrote five towns down the side of the Saturday line of the page of that week, in the order the nine communities have always been read in, and no name went against any of the five.

The five are the five that stood about nine feet apart off that mole on Wednesday morning, and each one of them walked back to where they sleep at their own hour and on their own two feet, and the last of the five came off that water in a cart going up the coast road and got down at the top of it and walked the last half mile.

Not one of the five was asked what it was like and not one of the five said anything about it, and about four people in that market this morning tried to get one of them to say something and did not manage it.

---

At about half past ten the coil came off that nail in the shed.

It was hanging on its own nail at the end of that bench since Friday morning and nobody in that yard had touched it and nobody had asked why it was there. At about half past ten it was not there and the nail was bare and about four people in that shed saw the bare nail at the same moment and nobody said anything about it.

Nobody in that yard asked her where it had gone and this yard has not written the hour down as a question.

---

The man of about thirty-two slept from about ten on Friday night until about five this morning.

He came into that yard at about ten past nine and sat down on the bench against the warm wall and he said that he had been asleep for about seven hours and that he had not heard the cart go past at about half past four, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote the two hours down and wrote no figure against either of them.

He drank a whole cup of water standing up and set it down and had another one, which is the first he has finished since Wednesday morning, and the aftertaste had not lifted, and he said so himself before anybody asked him, and the keeper wrote the hour of that down and wrote no figure against that either.

The knee is still in the left knee. He came up the three steps of that shed by the rail on the left-hand side this morning and he did not put his weight on it in the middle, and he said that it is the same as it was on Friday and not worse, and nobody in that shed gave him a word about it.

---

At about two in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office came down that lane on her own account and asked the man who keeps the leaf one question, being whether a page of days may be read out on those boards in front of people who cannot read it.

The man who keeps the leaf said that it can, and that it is done at that stone every day of the week, and that on a day with a clock on it a stranger in that market would have to write the hour out of his own head to get it wrong.

She said that was all she wanted and went back up the lane, and the keeper wrote the hour of the question down and wrote no name against it.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Saturday line on the page of that week and left it short, being that the boat came back at about eight with her own gear aboard her, that five towns are written down with no name against any of them, that a coil came off its own nail at about half past ten and nobody asked, that a man slept about seven hours and had two cups of water and the aftertaste has not lifted and the knee is not worse, and that no figure of money stands against the sleep or the water or the aftertaste or the knee.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and seventy-nine words, to about nine people, and about five of those nine had been standing at that bare nail at about half past ten.

"**A boat came in at about eight and went alongside the third lay with her gear still aboard her and nobody asked where she had been, and that is the first day since the fourth of May that this yard has had nothing charged to it that belongs to anybody, and I am not going to put a better word on that than nothing.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines are on it now and there is not going to be a third in this yard's fifty days. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and four days without one.**

**The five. Five towns went down on that page this morning with no name against any of them, and not one of the five has been asked what it was like and not one of the five has said, and about four people in this market tried today and did not manage it. I am not going to write on that page what it was like for them and I would ask anybody who wanted that written to go and ask one of the five themselves, and one of the five is a person and not a column.**

**And the coil came off its own nail at about half past ten and four of us saw the bare nail and none of us said anything, and she was not asked and she was not thanked and she did not stay. That is the whole of it.**

**And the man of about thirty-two slept about seven hours and had two cups of water and drank them both, which is the first cup of water he has finished since Wednesday morning. The aftertaste has not lifted and the knee is not worse and he came up those steps by the rail on the left-hand side this morning and not in the middle. There is no figure of money against the sleep or the water or the taste or the knee, and there is not going to be one, and I would like nine people to go on being the only nine people who have heard me say that this week.**

**And the last of it. The man of about twenty-nine is on his one hundred and eighty-ninth day today and he is not on the water and nothing that happened on Wednesday or since has improved him by a day. The page of hands stands at fifteen and was not touched. The nights carried are not added up again. The three columns of the page of routes are not counted today.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for seven hours of sleep, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had one line on it and a new week, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THE BOAT OF ABOUT THIRTY TONS CAME BACK UP AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND WENT ALONGSTIDE THE THIRD LAY AT ABOUT TEN PAST WITH HER OWN GEAR STILL ABOARD HER AND HER OWN LINE STILL COILED WHERE IT LAY ON WEDNESDAY, AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD ASKED WHERE SHE HAD BEEN. ALL FIVE OF THE PEOPLE WHO WENT DOWN OFF THAT MOLE ON WEDNESDAY WERE BACK IN THEIR OWN COMMUNITIES BY ABOUT TEN THIS MORNING AND FIVE TOWNS WERE WRITTEN DOWN ON THAT PAGE WITH NO NAME AGAINST ANY OF THEM, AND ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT MARKET THIS MORNING TRIED TO GET ONE OF THE FIVE TO SAY SOMETHING AND DID NOT MANAGE IT. THE COIL CAME OFF ITS OWN NAIL IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD SAW THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE TAKE IT AND NOBODY ASKED HER. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WAS ASLEEP FROM ABOUT TEN ON FRIDAY NIGHT UNTIL ABOUT FIVE THIS MORNING, BEING ABOUT SEVEN HOURS, WHICH IS THE FIRST SLEEP IN THREE NIGHTS, AND HE DRANK TWO CUPS OF WATER AND FINISHED BOTH OF THEM, AND THE AFTERTASTE HAS NOT LIFTED AND THE KNEE IN HIS LEFT KNEE IS NOT WORSE AND HE CAME UP THOSE THREE STEPS BY THE RAIL ON THE LEFT-HAND SIDE AND NOT IN THE MIDDLE, AND NO FIGURE OF MONEY STANDS AGAINST THE SLEEP OR THE WATER OR THE AFTERTASTE OR THE KNEE. THE MONEY SHEET CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD AND STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason the gear aboard that boat is written as hers and not as ours is that a boat's gear leaves with the boat and that this yard hired the boat for two days and did not hire the inside of her, and that a yard which wrote down what was in a boat while it was in that yard would be keeping a survey of other people's boats; and that at about ten in the morning the reason five towns went on that page and five names did not is that a town is a place and a person is a person, and that the page of that week takes places and hours and the page of hands takes names against nine limits, and that mixing a name into the first would turn a week of work into a roll of people; and that at about half past ten the reason the bare nail is written down and the hour is not written as a question is that four people saw it at the same moment and that is a fact, and that a question this yard has not been asked has to be asked before it is written down, and that until somebody asks it this yard has got an absence and not a refusal; and that at about half past nine in the morning the reason the two hours of sleep are on that page with no figure against either of them is that a man who has not slept for three nights and then has slept seven is the only arithmetic this yard has this week that is not money and that putting a figure against it would put a price on a man who is owed one; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the three columns of the page of routes not being counted is that a movement which spent nine days asking things has not earned the right to spend its tenth day taking a count of things nobody asked for.
