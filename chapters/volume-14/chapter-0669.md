# Chapter 669

*Friday the Twentieth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and at About Ten in the Morning About Eleven People of That Harbor of Whom Four Cannot Read What They Wrote Taking Down the Morrow Line's Own Sentence off the Wall of That Shed with Their Own Hands on Four Tacks in About Four Minutes in Front of About Nine People and the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor Standing Six Feet Back with a Coil of Line in His Hand and Not Touching the Wall, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Two and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Friday the twentieth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. At about ten in the morning about eleven people of that harbor, of whom about four cannot read what they wrote, took down the Morrow Line's own sentence off the wall of that shed with their own hands, on four tacks, in about four minutes, in front of about nine people, and the man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood six feet back with a coil of line in his hand and did not touch the wall and nobody asked him to. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that butt behind that market was covered from eight until four with no charge on it.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood three hundred and sixty-seven days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty-nine days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and seven days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and ninety-one days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and forty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-first. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventy-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and nine days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and sixty-seven days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

They came at about ten in the morning and there were about eleven of them and about four of them cannot read what they wrote on Monday the ninth of May.

That is a thing this yard knows and this yard has never printed, and it was printed on Monday the ninth of May in the first column of that day because the line was read out twice and the second reading was not for the four who cannot read.

---

It came down in about four minutes on four tacks.

The four tacks came out of a plaster that had been there eleven days and three of them came out whole and one came out with a piece of the wall about the size of a coin still in it. About nine people were in that shed and nobody spoke for the whole of those four minutes.

The woman of about twenty-nine of the second lay put the paper in her own inside pocket and did not fold it and did not leave it on the bench stone.

---

The man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood six feet back with a coil of line in his hand because he had been coiling line all morning and had not put it down.

He did not touch the wall. Nobody asked him to and nobody thanked him for not doing it and nobody said one word about him at all, then or afterwards.

At about half past ten, after the others had gone up to the market, he coiled that line on that bench stone and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular, in about twenty-five words, and what he said was that he has been in that yard nine days waiting for something to do and that he has not got it and that he is glad.

Nobody heard him say it and it is in this slip and it is in the first column and it is not on any page of that harbor.

---

At about half past eleven a woman of about thirty of no office stood in about nine people and said one thing out loud, and what she said was that the four people who could read what they wrote had watched the other seven for four minutes this morning and had not once said stop and had not once said go faster, and that whoever comes after her is going to have to do that for as long as it takes and is not going to get a bell for it.

Nobody answered her and nobody thanked her and she was not asked to say it twice.

---

At about one in the afternoon a woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane asked the man who keeps the leaf, in about nine people, whether that wall was going to be whitewashed.

He said that it was not, and that there were two lines on it and about eleven inches of empty plaster where the third had been, and that the empty part was going to stay empty until somebody who had a right to put something there came and put something there.

She said that an empty space on a wall is a thing people put things in, and he said that it was, and that was the end of it.

---

At about half past three a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came alongside that market and asked one question, which was whether anybody had been hurt.

Nobody had been hurt and nobody answered him and he went away.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote it in the first column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and wrote that four tacks came out of that wall, and that three of them came out whole and one came out with a piece of the wall about the size of a coin still in it, and that the paper went into the inside pocket of the woman of about twenty-nine and was not left on that bench stone.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and twenty-four words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that shed at ten o'clock that morning.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four, and that butt was covered from eight until four with no charge on it either.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**About eleven people took a sentence off that wall this morning with their own hands in about four minutes and about four of them cannot read what they wrote. That is not a thing this yard has done before and it is not a thing this yard was asked to do. I want to be exact that the yard was not asked. That woman said on Thursday that it comes down and she said it in nine people and this yard wrote it down and this morning eleven people came with their own hands.**

**And here is what I am not going to do with it. I am not going to say that this coast is stronger for it, because this morning eleven people found out that four of them cannot read what they wrote, and they found it out in four minutes, and that is not a good morning. I am not going to say it was a failure either, because the other seven watched the four and said nothing and let the four do it, and that is the thing a page of limits is for.**

**What I will say is that the wall is now correct. Two lines on it and about eleven inches of empty plaster. That empty plaster is not going to be filled by this yard and it is not going to be filled in a hurry, and anybody who comes up that lane with a sentence for it can have it if it is theirs to give.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four minutes and four tacks, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that wall had two lines on it and about eleven inches of empty plaster between them and above them, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE OF THAT HARBOR, OF WHOM ABOUT FOUR CANNOT READ WHAT THEY WROTE, TOOK DOWN THE MORROW LINE'S OWN SENTENCE OFF THE WALL OF THAT SHED AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING WITH THEIR OWN HANDS, ON FOUR TACKS, IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR STOOD SIX FEET BACK WITH A COIL OF LINE IN HIS HAND AND DID NOT TOUCH THE WALL AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO. THREE OF THE FOUR TACKS CAME OUT WHOLE AND ONE CAME OUT WITH A PIECE OF THE WALL ABOUT THE SIZE OF A COIN STILL IN IT, AND THE PAPER WENT INTO THE INSIDE POCKET OF THE WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE AND WAS NOT FOLDED AND WAS NOT LEFT ON THAT BENCH STONE. THAT WALL IS NOT TO BE WHITEWASHED. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the four minutes are written down and not the eleven people is that a number of hands is not a measurement and this page has never carried one, and that eleven people turning up is a fact about a morning and four minutes of four tacks is a fact about a wall, and that the wall is the thing that will still be there on Monday; and that at about half past ten in the morning the reason I have put the twenty-five words in the first column and in this slip and on no page of this harbor is that a man who says he is glad he has not got a thing to do is saying something about himself and not about any work, and that this yard has refused six sheets that would have kept a column on what people want, and that those six were all about wanting and none of them was about a person doing sums in his head by a bench stone at half past ten; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason that wall is not to be whitewashed is that whitewashing is the cheapest way in the world of making a page into a clean wall, and that this coast has taken two lines off a wall in four minutes this morning and it was not going to spend the same morning making the plaster look as though nothing had ever been on it; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the size of the piece of plaster is in this column is that a page which records that a thing came down and does not record what came down with it is a page that records an intention and not an event, and that one tack out of four is the whole of this morning in one small number, and that about nine people in that shed know exactly which tack it was and nobody else on that coast does; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason this slip says neither good nor bad about it is that a page of limits in this harbor is not a thing that judges anybody, and that the moment a keeper of that leaf starts writing whether a morning was good he has started keeping the other page, the one with the fourteen gaps on it, on his own account, and that page has never once been read out of by anybody and I intend to keep it that way.*
