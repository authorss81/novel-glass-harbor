# Chapter 653

*Wednesday the Fourth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Four Lines Read Out at About Eleven in the Morning at the Flat of That Bench Stone in About Nine People Saying What a Record of a Line on a Wall Will Carry, and Four More Saying What It Will Not Do, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Reading Both Sets and Stopping at Nothing Because There Is No Seal on Either, the Man of About Thirty-Two Disagreeing Out Loud in the Same Nine People in About One Hundred and Eighty-Four Words and Not Being Answered, and Her Answering Him After in About One Hundred and Ninety-Seven Words and Not Being Answered Either, the Page Going Up Anyway With Eight Lines Under It and Carrying Neither of the Two, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Eighty-Six and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Four*

Wednesday the fourth day of May, with that market shut for the morning and open for the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and nothing done to it that anybody has to write down. At about eleven in the morning a woman of thirty of no office read eight lines out in the open at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people, being four that say what a record of a line on a wall will carry and four that say what it will not do, and the man of about thirty-two disagreed with her out loud in the same nine people, and she answered him out loud after that, and nobody answered either of them and neither of them stood down and the page went up on that wall anyway at about one in the afternoon. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and what it finished the day with under that slate was twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past. And the wooden tally slate is still lying flat on that bench stone where it was put down on the first of May, and nothing is on it.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood three hundred and fifty-one days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and fifty-three days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and ninety-one days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and fifty-seventh night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and seventy-five days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and thirtieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and forty-fifth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and sixty-second day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for two hundred and ninety-three days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and the answer to the four lines in Fish Street is still no on four out of six with the fifth not asked. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fifty-one days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven in the morning, in the open, at the flat of that bench stone, in about nine people, a woman of thirty of no office read eight lines out loud. She did not need the bench stone to read from. She had the page in her hand and she read it standing in the open air, and there is no seal at the foot of that page and there is no name under any seal, and she read the whole of it.

The four that say what it carries are these.

1. *The name of the route, as the route writes it and not as this yard writes it.*
2. *The day it was put up.*
3. *The line itself, in the words of the people who wrote it, with the spelling they gave it.*
4. *Whether a name is on it or that no name is on it.*

The four that say what it does not do are these.

1. *It does not say why a community wrote that line.*
2. *It does not carry a figure of persons and says so on its own face, and will not.*
3. *It does not say whether anybody agreed with it.*
4. *It does not stand in for anybody who wrote nothing at all.*

About four people in that nine heard the whole of it. The rest of that market was not told, and there are people in this harbor who cannot read a column and were not told and were not asked.

---

At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-two disagreed with her, out loud, in the same nine people, in about one hundred and eighty-four words.

"**You have just read out that a record of a wall does not say whether anybody agreed. That is your third line of the four and it is the best one of the eight and I want it kept exactly as it is. I am not against it. I am telling you what happens to a man who puts a name on a line at ten in the morning.**

**He puts his name on it because somebody he works for is standing there. Then at four in the afternoon he decides he does not agree with the whole of what he signed, and he takes it back, and your page prints the morning. So your page keeps a man's yes at ten o'clock and throws away his no at four, and from then on the only thing anybody in this harbor can read about that man is that he was in favour on a Wednesday. That is not a record. That is a man's morning being kept against him for the rest of his life by a woman who is doing him a kindness.**"

Nobody answered him. A woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane said nothing, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay said nothing, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote nothing down and I have asked him about it since and he says he wrote it down.

---

Then she answered him, in about nine people, after he had finished, in about one hundred and ninety-seven words, and she did not raise her voice and she did not answer the part about the man at four in the afternoon.

"**He has told you that a page which prints who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing. Fine. Here is the other half of that and nobody has said it out loud on this coast yet, and it is not about anybody on this wall. A page which prints nobody's disagreement is a page that whoever reads it first can write. That is not a threat. There are six people in this yard who could add one line under that last line tonight saying that everybody here agrees, and put no name to it, and in nine months somebody would come along and read that as a fact and there would be nothing on that page to say it was not.**

**I am not going to take his third line off that page. I am going to put my four lines under it and let the two of them sit there and let everybody who comes to that wall decide which one they believe, and I will write on the bottom of both of them that neither of us can prove the other wrong, and then I am going to go and do Tuesday.**"

Nobody answered her either. Nobody agreed with either of them. The man of about thirty-two stood there for a while and then said one short thing, which was that she was right about the second half and he was not sorry about the first, and she said that was the correct amount for a Wednesday.

---

At about one in the afternoon the page went up on that wall beside the line of eleven words that has been on it since Monday, on two tacks and not four because there were only two hands free.

It carries the eight lines. It carries nothing under them. There is no line on that page that says who agreed with it and there is no line on that page that says who did not, and both of those things were argued about for twenty minutes in about nine people and neither of them got on the paper.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about two hundred and one words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine were still looking at the two pages on that wall instead of at him.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence, and it is the same figure it was on Monday and it will be the same figure on Saturday. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence on that board in the front street, unmoved. No charge on that cistern today and its covers lay on it from nine until four, because a rota day is a Monday and a Thursday and today is a Wednesday.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. And nothing has come out of that bag in sixty-one days and nothing went on that sheet this morning and nothing went on it this afternoon.**"

**And two people said one thing each out loud in this yard today and neither of them was answered by anybody and I have not written either of them down anywhere except on this slip, and I am going to say why. A sheet that carries an argument is a sheet that has to be settled by somebody, and the only person in this yard who could settle either of those two is the two of them, and they are not going to settle it in a shed with nine people in it, because then it would be settled and there would be a line at the foot of that page saying how, and nobody on this coast has ever been able to tell the difference between a thing that was settled and a thing that was worn out.**

**What is on that wall now is two pages and one line of eleven words, and the eight lines do not say whether anybody agreed, and I do not know yet whether that is the finest thing on that wall or the worst. Both of those were said out loud in nine people this morning about half a minute apart and both of them are true and I am not going to choose between them on a Wednesday.**

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eight lines, and nobody was thanked for a disagreement that is still open on that wall and has not been written down anywhere except in this slip, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE READ EIGHT LINES OUT IN THE OPEN AT THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, BEING FOUR THAT SAY WHAT A RECORD OF A LINE ON A WALL WILL CARRY, WHICH ARE THE NAME OF THE ROUTE AS THE ROUTE WRITES IT, THE DAY, THE LINE ITSELF IN THE WORDS OF THE PEOPLE WHO WROTE IT, AND WHETHER A NAME IS ON IT OR THAT NO NAME IS ON IT, AND FOUR THAT SAY WHAT IT WILL NOT DO, WHICH ARE THAT IT DOES NOT SAY WHY A COMMUNITY WROTE THAT LINE, THAT IT DOES NOT CARRY A FIGURE OF PERSONS, THAT IT DOES NOT SAY WHETHER ANYBODY AGREED, AND THAT IT DOES NOT STAND IN FOR ANYBODY WHO WROTE NOTHING AT ALL. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO DISAGREED WITH THE THIRD OF THOSE FOUR IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR WORDS AND WAS NOT ANSWERED. SHE ANSWERED HIM AFTERWARDS IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN WORDS AND WAS NOT ANSWERED EITHER. THE PAGE WENT UP ON THAT WALL AT ABOUT ONE IN THE AFTERNOON ON TWO TACKS AND CARRIES NEITHER OF THEM. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fourth day of May at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that at about eleven in the morning a woman of thirty of no office read out eight lines in the open at the flat of that bench stone in about nine people, being four that say what a record of a line on that wall will carry, which are the name of the route as the route writes it, the day, the line itself in the words of the people who wrote it with the spelling they gave it, and whether a name is on it or that no name is on it, and four that say what it will not do, which are that it does not say why a community wrote that line, that it does not carry a figure of persons, that it does not say whether anybody agreed with it, and that it does not stand in for anybody who wrote nothing at all, and that there is no seal at the foot of that page and no name under any seal and she read the whole of it; and that the man of about thirty-two disagreed with her out loud in the same nine people in about one hundred and eighty-four words, and that what he said was that a page which prints who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing, and that a man will put his name on a line at ten in the morning and take it back at four in the afternoon, and that a page that keeps the morning keeps a man's yes against him for the rest of his life; and that she answered him after that in the same nine people in about one hundred and ninety-seven words, and that what she said was that a page which prints nobody's disagreement is a page that whoever reads it first can write, and that there are six people in that yard who could add one line under that last line tonight saying that everybody agrees and put no name to it, and that in nine months somebody would read that as a fact and there would be nothing on the page to say it was not; and that nobody answered either of them and neither of them stood down, and that the man of about thirty-two said afterwards that she was right about the second half and he was not sorry about the first, and that she said that was the correct amount for a Wednesday; and that at about one in the afternoon that page went up on the wall of that shed on two tacks beside the line of eleven words that has been there since Monday, and that it carries the eight lines and nothing under them and no line saying who agreed with it and no line saying who did not; and that the wooden tally slate still lies flat on that bench stone where it was put down on Sunday the first of May and nothing is written on it; and that nothing went onto that money sheet and nothing came off it, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that the first column takes one entry a day and three hundred and eighty-three and one is three hundred and eighty-six at the close of it, and that the second takes five a day and one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and five is one thousand eight hundred and four.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason those eight lines were read out in the open and not in that shed is that about eleven people in this harbor cannot read a notice, and a page that is read at a table is a page that has been read to the two who can see over the table; and that at about half past eleven in the morning the reason I wrote nothing down while those two were arguing is that I have been in this yard sixty-one days and I have learned that a man who is arguing a thing he believes does not want it taken down in front of him by a stranger with a leaf, because a thing taken down in the middle of an argument becomes a thing that has been agreed; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason I used two tacks and not four is that there were two hands free, and that if this yard had fetched a third hand to hang a page up properly then three people would have touched a page that belongs to somebody else, and there is a difference between hanging a page and helping somebody hang their page; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason I have not written either of those two speeches into that yard's own book is that a book that carries an argument has to be settled by somebody, and the only two people who could settle it are the two of them, and they are not going to settle it in a shed with nine people in it because then it would be settled and there would be a line at the foot of the page saying how, and on this coast nobody can tell the difference between a thing that was settled and a thing that was worn out, and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I have written on this slip and nowhere else that neither of those two things is on that page is that a slip is not a surface anybody reads, and a slip is where a keeper puts the thing he is not allowed to lose, and this yard is going to lose one of those two arguments eventually and I want to be able to say which way it went; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I said in that shed that both of those things are true and I am not going to choose between them on a Wednesday is that a keeper of a page who chooses is a man with an office, and this yard has not got one and is not going to get one.*
