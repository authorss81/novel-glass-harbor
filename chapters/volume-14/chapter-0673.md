# Chapter 673

*Tuesday the Twenty-Fourth Day of May, the Eighty-Third Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes by a Man of About Thirty-One of the Second Lay and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Eighty-Third Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, and the Eighty-Third Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Behind the Eighty-Second, the First Entry Going onto the Page of That Week at About Eleven in the Morning Against Monday and Carrying No Name, a Man of About Fifty of the Lowfen Reed's Own Third Lay Standing in That Shed at About Half Past Two in About Nine People and Saying Out Loud That Whatever Is Paid for Wednesday Night Will Stand on Another Route's Own Page and Not in This Yard's Book, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Six and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Four*

Tuesday the twenty-fourth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-third wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. The column on the lid of that step stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-third week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the eighty-second, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. At about eleven in the morning the first entry went onto the page of that week, against the Monday, and it carries no name. At about half past two in the afternoon a man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay stood in that shed in about nine people and said out loud that whatever is paid for Wednesday night will stand on another route's own page and not in this yard's book, and no figure of it is in this yard's book and no figure of it is in this slip. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine figures stand in nine places on this coast, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil could have every one of them off the surface before dark. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is three hundred and seventy-first days old. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for a figure in three hundred and seventy-third days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and eleventh day. The bench under that pump has been bare for the four hundred and seventy-seventh night. The two lights about a mile off that point are on their two hundred and ninety-fifth day without a figure. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fiftieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-fifth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-second day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and thirteenth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been nailed there this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and no hand has gone into it. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-first days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes and it did not move, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay has read that same lid out on eighty-three Tuesdays and the figure has not changed on any of them, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four.

Behind the eighty-third stands behind the eighty-second, and behind that one stands behind the eighty-first, and there is nobody in that market who has ever asked what those envelopes are for, because nobody in that market has ever been told.

---

At about eleven in the morning the man who keeps the leaf wrote the first line against the Monday on that new sheet, and he read it out in about nine people before he wrote it, and it carries no name.

It says that on Sunday the twenty-second of May, at about two in the afternoon, at that end stall, a man of about fifty of that route's own third lay said in about nine people that his route was one man short on Wednesday night and that he was not going to ask anybody in this yard.

He did not write the man's name and he did not write the route's name against the day, and he said out loud why: because a line on that sheet is a thing that happened and the two of those are the people who were standing there.

Nobody argued with that and nobody added anything to it.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon the man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay came into that shed again and stood in about nine people and said one thing out loud, and what he said was that whatever is paid for Wednesday night will stand on another route's own page and not in this yard's book.

He said that the Reed has a page and that the Reed's page is the Reed's, and that a figure in chalk on a route's own page is a figure between that route and the people it hires, and that this yard did not hire anybody and this yard's book does not hold it and this yard is not going to be handed it.

He said that he was telling this yard that in front of nine people on purpose, so that nobody in nine people can say afterwards that this yard put a night on a page of its own.

Nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody argued with him about it, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote down that he said it, put the hour against it, and wrote no figure at all, because the figure is not his and it is not this yard's and he did not hear it.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Tuesday line on that sheet and left it short, and it says that this yard was asked nothing this morning and that a column on a lid was read out in four minutes and did not move, and that a man told this yard in nine people where a figure for Wednesday night is not going to be.

He did not write the eighty-third week against it and he did not write the twenty envelopes against it, and that is on the face of the sheet with the hour beside it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and seventy-six words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that shed at about half past two.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on that lid for the eighty-third time, read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who is not on the water and has not been on the water, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and the eighty-third week stands fallen due at the close of today, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. I am not going to say the number of the week out loud and I am not going to say it twice this month.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That butt behind that market was covered from eight until four, because a rota day is a Monday and a Thursday and today is neither.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now eighty-six days without one.**

**And at about half past two this afternoon a man stood in that shed in nine people and told me, out loud and on purpose, that whatever is paid for Wednesday night goes on another route's own page and not in this yard's book. I have written down that he said it. I have not written down any figure, because he did not give me a figure and because a figure I went and got would be a figure this yard had gone and got, and a night that this yard went and got a figure for is a night this yard has put a hand on.**

**The last of it, and then I stop. That new sheet has one line on it after two days and it is a line about something that did not happen. Everything that has actually happened this week so far is that wall, that cistern, that lid, and a woman of about forty standing in a doorway for a minute and a half on Monday afternoon. Four things, and all four of them are about work somebody else did.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a Tuesday that did not move, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had one line against Monday and a short one against Tuesday, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THE SECOND LAY AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE EIGHTY-THIRD WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY AND NOT ONE ENVELOPE IN THAT CHEST HAS BEEN OPENED ON ANY OF THEM. AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING THE FIRST ENTRY WENT ONTO THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK AGAINST THE MONDAY, IT CARRYING NO NAME, AND IT WAS READ OUT IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE BEFORE IT WAS WRITTEN. AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY OF THE LOWFEN REED'S OWN THIRD LAY STOOD IN THAT SHED IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT WHATEVER IS PAID FOR WEDNESDAY NIGHT WILL STAND ON ANOTHER ROUTE'S OWN PAGE AND NOT IN THIS YARD'S BOOK, AND NO FIGURE OF IT IS IN THIS YARD'S BOOK. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE LINE AND NO SECOND IS ON IT. NO GAP WAS READ OUT OF AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the eighty-third is written down this week and the eighty-second was written down last week is that a number which is only ever said when it is not moving stops being a number and becomes a sentence about this harbor, and that this yard is not going to be the place where a figure quietly stops being checked; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason that first line carries no name and no route's name is that the sheet hung on that wall yesterday was described out loud as carrying the doing of a thing and not a column, and that a name in the margin of the first line of a new page is the fastest way there is of turning a page of days into a page of hands, and that page of hands already exists and has a keeper and a fall on it and does not need a second one; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason no figure is written anywhere in this yard for Wednesday night is that this yard did not hire anybody and does not know the figure and did not go and get it, and that the whole difficulty this coast has with a requirement is that somebody always goes and gets the number, and a number this yard went and got on Wednesday would be a number this yard had asked for; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the eighty-third week and the twenty envelopes are left off that sheet is that the lid on that step is this town's business and the sheet on that wall is this week's business, and that this harbor has been asked for six weeks to keep its papers separate and it has got that right twice this month and this is the third time; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason this slip ends on the four things that have happened rather than on the man who was short-handed is that a slip is the place where this yard says what it knows, and what it knows on the third day of a week is a wall, a cistern, a lid and a woman in a doorway, and putting a man in front of that would be arranging the week so that the man looks like the point of it.*
