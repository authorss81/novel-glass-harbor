# Chapter 660

*Wednesday the Eleventh Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Nine Figures Written at About Three in the Afternoon at the Head of the Nine Lines of That Page of Days Being Four and Three and Three and Two and Two and Two and One and One and One in the Order the Nine Names Are Always Read In, With the Day and the Hour Against Each of Them, and Nineteen Written Under Them, and Nobody in That Shed Saying Nineteen Out Loud and Nineteen Not in Any Slip, a Man of About Fifty-Three Adding Nine Figures With a Leaf and a Pencil in About a Minute and Getting Nineteen and Then Reading Nine Names Down the Side of That Sheet and Getting Nine Again, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Ninety-Three and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Wednesday the eleventh day of May, with that market shut for the morning and open for the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. At about three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote nine figures at the head of the nine lines of that page of days, being four and three and three and two and two and two and one and one and one, in the order the nine names are always read in, and put the day and the hour against each of them, and wrote nineteen underneath them. A man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons added those nine figures up with a leaf and a pencil in about a minute, got nineteen, read the nine names down the side of that sheet, got nine, gave the sheet back and went to work. Nobody in that shed said nineteen out loud, it is not in any slip, and it is not in this column in words. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the wooden tally slate is still lying flat on that bench stone where it was put down on the first of May and nothing has been written on it and nobody has picked it up.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood three hundred and fifty-eight days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and ninety-eight days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and sixty-fourth night. The two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and eighty-two days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and thirty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and fifty-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and sixty-ninth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred days, and it is three hundred exactly today, and nobody in that market has said one word about it and this yard has not. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and fifty-eight days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it and he is not on the water.**

---

At about three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote nine figures at the head of the nine lines of that page of days with a stub of chalk, and he wrote them in the order the nine names are always read in, and he put the day and the hour against each of them, and then he wrote the sum underneath them.

Four, three, three, two, two, two, one, one, one.

Four plus three plus three plus two plus two plus two plus one plus one plus one is nineteen, and nineteen is written under them in the same hand and in the same chalk.

He then went and got a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons, who was on that mole, and gave him the sheet and a pencil and said nothing else about it.

---

The man of about fifty-three added those nine figures up with a leaf and a pencil in about a minute, got nineteen, and then read the nine names down the side of that sheet, which are the Morrow Line, the Saltmarch Weatherway, the Tidewatch Channel, the Ashtide Run, the Ternwatch Passage, the Lowfen Reed, the Cinder Bell, the Pale Shoals and the Orison Descent, and counted those and got nine, and gave the sheet back and went to work.

He did not ask what the nine figures were for. He was not asked twice what he had got. About four people in that shed saw him do it and nobody in that shed said the figure nineteen out loud.

---

That is the whole of that count and there is no more to it, and I am going to write down what it does not say, because this yard has been wrong before this week about a figure it thought was bigger than it was.

It is not a figure of persons. It is a figure of names on nine pieces of paper.

A man who put nothing on any of those papers is not on that page. He is not written off it. He is not written down at the end of it in brackets. There is no second column on that page with the people who refused in it, and there is not going to be one, and if there ever is one then this page has become the roll that this harbor has spent thirteen volumes refusing to keep, and it will have become it on the day somebody finds it useful.

It is also not a figure of agreements. Four against the Morrow Line is four people who put a name to a sentence about an echo of somebody in this harbor, and one of those four cannot read what he signed, and that is on the page in a different column and not in this one. One against the Orison Descent is not one person who agrees and eight who do not care. It is one person who signed and nobody else from that coast who has been asked, because that coast is four days down this coast road in a winter and the cart is four days each way and there is no boat in December.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and thirty-four words, to about nine people.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and its covers lay on it from nine until four. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past, and this yard has been sixty-nine days without one.**

**And I am not going to say that figure out loud in this shed and it is not on this slip and I have written in this column in words what is on that page in figures and those are two different things and one of them is the page and one of them is me. A figure a stranger can add up goes at the head of the page with a day and an hour against it. A figure a man in a shed says with his mouth in the air does not go anywhere, and this yard has been wrong about that once already in an earlier year and it cost about nine people a morning.**"

**What I am going to say out loud is the shape of the thing and not the number of it. Nine coasts. Nine lines down the side of a sheet of days. Three of those nine have put something on this yard's wall and six of them have not, and that is not the same as six of them having refused, because a man on a coast four days down this road in December has not been asked, and a man who has not been asked is not a man who has said no, and this yard is not entitled to write him down as one.**

**That is the whole of what ten days have come to, and it is a good deal less than I expected on Monday morning and I want to be exact about why. It is not less than nineteen, whatever nineteen is. It is that nineteen is a figure about paper and I have spent ten days watching paper come into a shed from four different directions and every one of the four arrived with its own word for who it belongs to and not one of the four said who was holding it.**

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nine figures, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of days had nine figures at the head of its nine lines and a sum under them and a day and an hour against each, and the wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone where it had been put down on the first of May, with nothing on it, and nobody picked it up.

---

**NINE FIGURES WERE WRITTEN AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON AT THE HEAD OF THE NINE LINES OF THAT PAGE OF DAYS, BEING FOUR AND THREE AND THREE AND TWO AND TWO AND TWO AND ONE AND ONE AND ONE, IN THE ORDER THE NINE NAMES ARE ALWAYS READ IN, AND THE DAY AND THE HOUR WERE PUT AGAINST EACH OF THEM, AND NINETEEN WAS WRITTEN UNDERNEATH THEM IN THE SAME HAND. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE ADDED THOSE NINE UP WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL IN ABOUT A MINUTE AND READ THE NINE NAMES DOWN THE SIDE OF THAT SHEET AND GOT NINE, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED WHAT THEY WERE FOR AND HE WAS NOT ASKED TWICE WHAT HE HAD GOT. NINETEEN IS NOT IN THIS SLIP AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED SAID IT OUT LOUD. THREE OF THOSE NINE HAVE PUT SOMETHING ON THIS YARD'S WALL AND SIX HAVE NOT, AND THAT IS NOT THE SAME AS SIX HAVING REFUSED, BECAUSE A MAN WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED IS NOT A MAN WHO HAS SAID NO. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN AND IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote nine figures at the head of the nine lines of that page of days with a stub of chalk, being four and three and three and two and two and two and one and one and one, in the order the nine names are always read in, and that he put the day and the hour against each of them and wrote nineteen underneath them in the same hand and the same chalk; and that he then gave that sheet and a pencil to a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons who was on that mole and said nothing else about it, and that the man of about fifty-three added those nine figures up in about a minute and got nineteen and then read the nine names down the side of that sheet and counted those and got nine and gave the sheet back and went to work; and that he did not ask what the nine figures were for and he was not asked twice what he had got, and that about four people in that shed saw him do it and nobody in that shed said that figure out loud; and that those nine figures are a figure of names on nine pieces of paper and not a figure of persons, and that a man who put nothing on any of those papers is not on that page and is not written off it and is not written down at the end of it in brackets, and that there is no second column on that page with the people who refused in it and there is not going to be one; and that those nine figures are not a figure of agreements either, in that four against the Morrow Line is four people who put a name to a sentence and one of those four cannot read what he signed and that is on the page in a different column, and that one against the Orison Descent is one person who signed and nobody else from that coast who has been asked, because that coast is four days down this coast road in a winter; and that three of those nine have put something on that wall this week and six have not, and that this is not the same as six having refused; and that the wooden tally slate still lies flat on that bench stone where it was put down on Sunday the first of May and nothing has been written on it and nobody has picked it up; and that nothing went onto that money sheet and nothing came off it, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and that the first column takes one entry a day whatever else that day prints, and that three hundred and eighty-three and ten is three hundred and ninety-three at the close of it, and that the second takes five a day, and that fifty times one is ten and fifty times five is two hundred and fifty across this whole run, and that one thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine and fifty is one thousand eight hundred and thirty-nine at the close of it, and that three hundred and eighty-three and ten is three hundred and ninety-three.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about three in the afternoon the reason I wrote those nine figures at the head of the nine lines and not in a column down the side is that a figure at the head of a line can be read across from the other side of a shed, and this yard has spent sixty-nine days finding out what a page is for and it keeps arriving at the answer that a page is for the people standing at the end of the bench; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason I did not say that sum out loud when I handed that sheet over is that a figure said with a mouth in the air goes into nine people's memories at nine different speeds and a figure written at the head of a page goes into one place and stays there, and that this yard got that wrong once in an earlier year and about nine people lost a morning on it; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I fetched the man of about fifty-three and not anybody else is that he is the one man in this harbor who will add a column up in a minute and then read the names down the side of the sheet and count those, and that a count which only one man can check is a count that has not been checked; and that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason I have written down that a man who put nothing on any of those papers is not on that page is that this harbor has been offered, across thirteen volumes, at least six different pieces of paper that would each have kept a list of everybody who signed nothing, and this yard has refused all six, and a page of names that can be added up is one step nearer the seventh of those and the seventh one is the one somebody eventually uses, and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I said in that shed that I expected more than this on Monday morning is that on Monday morning there was one line on that wall and eleven words on it and I thought I was watching a thing start, and what I have actually watched in ten days is three sentences arriving from three coasts and a woman in a boat on Saturday and nineteen names and a figure about paper that I cannot say out loud; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason this slip ends on that page of days and not on the wall is that a wall is walked up to and a page is carried, and this run is ten days old and it is going to be fifty, and by the end of fifty days the page is the thing that will still be here and the wall will have been taken down nine times and put up again unrepaired, and that is not a criticism of walls.*
