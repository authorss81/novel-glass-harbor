# Chapter 679

*Monday the Thirtieth Day of May, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Fourth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Lying on It from About Eleven Until About Four in the Afternoon, a Man of About Fifty of the Lowfen Reed's Own Third Lay Asking a Woman of About Forty of the Cinder Bell's Own Community at About Ten in the Morning in About Nine People Whether She Will Do It Again Next Week, and Her Saying in About Forty-Three Words That She Will Not Say Until She Is Asked and Until Her Own People Have Talked and That a Route That Has Said Yes Once Has Not Said Yes Twice, and Nobody in That Shed Saying Yes and Nobody in That Shed Saying No, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Naming the Second Sheet of Days at About Two in the Afternoon and Saying He Will Add It Up Tomorrow and Not Today and Not Saying Why in a Room, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twelve and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Monday the thirtieth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-fourth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about ten in the morning, in about nine people, a man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay asked a woman of about forty of the Cinder Bell's own community whether she would do it again next week, and she said, in about forty-three words, that she would not say until she is asked and until her own people have talked, and that a route that has said yes once has not said yes twice. Nobody in that shed said yes and nobody in that shed said no. At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the second sheet of days off its nail, looked at it, put it back on its nail, and said that he would add it up tomorrow and not today and would not say why in a room. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in that harbor are on a wall or in a book this afternoon and every one of them can be had without a key. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and seventy-seventh days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy-ninth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and seventeenth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been three hundred and first days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-first. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and nineteenth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been nailed there this month. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-seventh days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and fifty-fourth, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off again at about four.

That is the same eleven trips and the same four men and the same stairs on the inside of that wall, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it, and has been off it since before the frost.

---

At about ten in the morning, in about nine people, the man of about fifty of the Lowfen Reed's own third lay asked the woman of about forty of the Cinder Bell's own community whether she would do it again next week.

He asked it in front of that nine and he did not ask this yard whether he might, and this yard was not asked anything.

She said, in about forty-three words, that she will not say until she is asked and until her own people have talked, and that a route that has said yes once has not said yes twice.

Nobody in that shed said yes and nobody in that shed said no. Nobody thanked her and nobody argued with her, and the man of the Reed did not ask her a second time and did not say anything else about it, and he went up to the market and the woman went up the lane.

That is the whole of it and it is written down with the hour against it and with no name against either of them.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the second sheet of days off its nail and looked at it for about a minute, and put it back on its nail.

He said in about nine people that he is going to add that sheet up tomorrow, and not today, and that he is not going to say why in a room, and that whoever wants to watch him do it can stand at that bench stone at about three in the afternoon tomorrow.

About four people in that nine said they would and about five said nothing.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Monday line on the page of that week and it is two sentences long, and it says that one route's own third lay asked another community in nine people whether it would do the same thing again next week, and that the answer was that it would not say until it was asked, and that this yard was asked nothing and sent for nobody.

He set no figure against it and no name against it and no opinion either, and wrote on the face of the sheet that the second sheet of days has not been added up and that it will be added up tomorrow off the page.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and forty-six words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that shed at about ten in the morning.

"**One route asked another one this morning, in nine people, whether it would do the same thing next week, and the answer was that it would not say until it was asked, and that is the whole of the answer. Nobody in that shed said yes and nobody in that shed said no, and I have written that down in those words and not in any other.**

**That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning and the hundred and fifty-fourth time, and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four, and the name at the head of that rota board is still off it and has been since before the frost.**

**The shortfall on that sheet is one hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. It has carried one line since the twentieth of April and it has carried no second. What has ever gone into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and nothing has gone in since the autumn. The figure on that board in the front street is four hundred and fifty pence and it has not been touched.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at about ten this morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four this afternoon, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, so that under that slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard has now gone ninety-two days without opening a tin.**

**At about two this afternoon I took the second sheet of days off its nail and looked at it and put it back, and I told nine people I would add it up tomorrow and not today and would not say why in a room, and four of them said they would come and watch and five of them said nothing. I am going to say the reason here and only here. That sheet has been standing at nothing since the first of May, and on Wednesday night it stopped standing at nothing, and if I add it up on the Monday I will be adding it up two days after something nice happened to somebody, and a figure taken two days after a good week is a figure this yard wanted. I would rather take it on a Tuesday, when the week has had a whole wage Tuesday in it and nothing has gone wrong in anybody's hearing.**

**And the last of it. Four people said they would stand at that bench stone tomorrow at about three. I did not ask them to and I did not tell them not to. A count that is going to be taken in a shed with nobody in it is a count nobody can check, and a count that is taken in front of four people who came on their own account is a count four people can check, and the only thing this yard has ever had to offer anybody is that somebody can check it.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for an answer that was neither one thing nor the other, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had ten lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, IT BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOURTH CHARGE, AND THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT AND HAS BEEN OFF IT SINCE BEFORE THE FROST. AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY OF THE LOWFEN REED'S OWN THIRD LAY ASKED THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY OF THE CINDER BELL'S OWN COMMUNITY WHETHER SHE WOULD DO IT AGAIN NEXT WEEK, AND SHE SAID IN ABOUT FORTY-THREE WORDS THAT SHE WILL NOT SAY UNTIL SHE IS ASKED AND UNTIL HER OWN PEOPLE HAVE TALKED, AND THAT A ROUTE THAT HAS SAID YES ONCE HAS NOT SAID YES TWICE, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED SAID YES AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED SAID NO, AND NOBODY THANKED HER AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HER. THAT MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE LINE AND NO SECOND IS ON IT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP THIS WEEK AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason the name at the head of that rota board is entered for the sixth week running is that a rota with nobody's name at the head of it is the strongest argument this harbor has for keeping a rota, and that if this yard wrote a name there it would be appointing somebody to carry water for this harbor, and this yard does not appoint anybody to anything and has not since the fourth of May; and that at about ten in the morning the reason that answer is written down in about forty-three words and in no other words is that a thing which is neither yes nor no is the easiest thing on any coast to rewrite afterwards in whichever direction the writer needs, and that the only defence this harbor has against that is to have the exact words on a page, and that three of the four people who were in that nine could not have told you on Tuesday what was said, and that is exactly why it is written; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason that yesterday's answer about not taking a count today was given in a slip and not in that shed is that a man who tells nine people at about two o'clock why he is not doing a thing yet has told them the answer before he has done it, and that anybody in that nine who wanted the answer could then have gone and told somebody else what the answer was going to be, and a count that has been forecast is a count that has been argued with before it exists; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason no opinion was written against the Monday line is that this yard is not the body that goes and asks either of those two communities anything, and that the moment this sheet carries a line saying that one of them is being difficult it has become a page about this yard's opinion of a route, and that is a page of a kind this harbor has refused six times; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason four people being told they may come and watch is written as an invitation and not as an instruction is that a count taken in an empty shed is a count that only the man taking it can be asked about, and this yard has spent a fortnight refusing to hold a page alone in a room, and a count is the same kind of thing as a page and has to be held the same way.*
