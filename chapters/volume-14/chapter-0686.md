# Chapter 686

*Monday the Sixth Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Sixth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Lying on It from About Eleven Until About Four, All Nine Answers In by About Ten in the Morning and Five Names Written on Five Small Pieces of Paper in Five Different Hands and No Figure Written Against Any of Them and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Saying Out Loud That He Is Not Going to Write Down Which Five Until About Noon on Wednesday, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Nineteen and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Monday the sixth day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-sixth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. All nine answers were in by about ten in the morning and five names were written on five small pieces of paper in five different hands and no figure was written against any of them, and the man who keeps the leaf said out loud that he is not going to write down which five until about noon on Wednesday. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine standing figures on that coast went a day further today and eight of the nine were about people. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and eighty-fourth days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-sixth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and twenty-fourth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninetieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been three hundred and eighth days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and sixty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-eighth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-sixth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has been put on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-fourth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

All nine were in by about ten, and the last two came up that lane together at about a quarter to ten in a cart that was not going anywhere in particular.

That is the first time on this coast in a month that all nine of anything has been in the same place at the same time, and about five people in that yard said so out loud on the morning and then stopped saying it.

---

The five names came in on five small pieces of paper and every one of them was in a different hand and none of them was a signature.

Four of the five said in their own words that the person named had been asked and had said they would, and the fifth said in their own words that the person named had been asked and had not yet said and would be asked again on Wednesday morning and that this yard was not to read that paper as a community turning the thing down.

That fifth paper is the one the man who keeps the leaf read out twice and it is on the page of that week with the hour against it and with no figure against it.

Nobody in that nine was asked which five it was and nobody in that nine was told which five it was, and about four people in that market have since said that they know, and this yard has not asked any of the four and is not going to.

---

At about eleven the man who keeps the leaf put the five pieces of paper on the flat of that bench stone face down and said out loud, in about nine people, that he was going to write down which five at about noon on Wednesday and not before, and that he was saying that on a Monday so that anybody who wanted to say something about the order of it had two days to say it in the open.

A man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish said that two days is not much notice and that he would not have been a man for it.

The man who keeps the leaf said that was right and that it was two days and that a man who did not want two days would have said so this morning and not on Wednesday at about twelve.

Nobody thanked either of them.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender came down that lane and asked two questions and they were both about the boat and not one of them was about the five.

She asked whether the boat of about thirty tons would have a wheelhouse on it and she asked whether anybody would be standing in the wheelhouse.

The man who keeps the leaf said that there would not be a wheelhouse, and that the boat was hired at the figure in chalk for two days and that the hire of it stood on a route's own page and not on this yard's money sheet, and that nobody would be standing in anything because there was nothing to stand in.

She said that was the right answer and that she had asked the second question because of a thing that happened on the Waterway in April that she was not going to describe in that market.

Nobody thanked her for asking and this yard wrote the hour against both questions.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Monday line on the page of that week and it says that all nine answers were in by about ten in the morning, and that five names came on five pieces of paper in five hands, and that four of the five said the person had been asked and had said they would and that the fifth said the person had not yet said, and that he was not going to write down which five until about noon on Wednesday, and that he said that on a Monday on purpose.

He wrote no figure against it and no name against it and he wrote no state against any route, and he wrote on the face of that sheet that a name given by a community is not a name chosen by this yard and that the two things are printed on different pieces of paper and are kept that way.

Nobody thanked him for the line.

---

And at about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and fifty-four words, to about nine people, and about six of those nine had been in that shed at about eleven in the morning.

"**All nine of those answers were on that stone before ten this morning, on five pieces of paper in five different hands, and I am going to say what that is and what it is not, and it is not very much and it is the whole of what this yard has been able to do in five days.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and fifty-sixth time and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, so that under that slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard has now gone ninety-nine days without opening a tin.**

**Four of those five say the person was asked and said they would. The fifth says the person has not yet said and will be asked again on Wednesday morning, and asked me to write that down as a person who has not yet had the question put to them in a room, and I have written it down exactly as it was asked. I am not going to shorten that paper to a turn-down and I am not going to lengthen it to an agreement.**

**And the thing I said at eleven o'clock on that stone and am saying again. I am not going to write down which five until about noon on Wednesday. A man of about fifty-six told me two days is not much notice and that he would not have been a man for it, and he was right, and I said that a man who did not want two days would have said so this morning and not on Wednesday at noon. I am putting that exchange on this slip because in thirteen volumes nobody in this harbor has ever heard me say that a man in a market was right about me.**

**And the two questions the tender asked at about half past two this afternoon, and both of them were about the boat and not one was about the five of them. There is no wheelhouse and nobody will be standing in anything. She asked the second one because of something that happened on the Waterway in April and she was not going to describe it in that market and I did not ask her. Four people have told me this week that they know which five. I have not asked any of the four and I am not going to, and if one of them tells me I am going to write down that he told me and not what he said.**

**And the last of it. That paper of hands stands at fifteen and it has stood at fifteen since the twenty-first of May and this yard has not added to it and is not going to this week. Five people are coming on Wednesday and not one of them is a name against a limit. A name goes on that page when a person puts it there against their own route's own writing, and putting five names on it because they are going to be in the water would be the first step towards a list of people and this harbor has refused six of those.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for nine answers being in one place on one Monday, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had six lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, IT BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIXTH CHARGE, AND THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE NAME AT THE HEAD OF THAT ROTA BOARD IS STILL OFF IT AND HAS BEEN OFF IT SINCE BEFORE THE FROST. ALL NINE ANSWERS WERE IN BY ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AND FIVE NAMES CAME ON FIVE SMALL PIECES OF PAPER IN FIVE DIFFERENT HANDS, FOUR OF THE FIVE SAYING THE PERSON HAD BEEN ASKED AND HAD SAID THEY WOULD AND THE FIFTH SAYING THE PERSON HAD NOT YET SAID AND WOULD BE ASKED AGAIN ON WEDNESDAY MORNING AND THAT THIS YARD WAS NOT TO TREAT THAT AS A REFUSAL. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO WRITE DOWN WHICH FIVE UNTIL ABOUT NOON ON WEDNESDAY, AND SAID THAT ON A MONDAY ON PURPOSE SO THAT ANYBODY WHO WANTED TO SAY SOMETHING ABOUT THE ORDER OF IT HAD TWO DAYS TO SAY IT IN THE OPEN. THE PAGE OF HANDS STANDS AT FIFTEEN AND WAS NOT ADDED TO. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE LINE AND NO SECOND IS ON IT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the two that came up together are written as having come up in a cart going nowhere in particular is that a cart going nowhere in particular is the only kind of errand on this coast that nobody has to justify to anybody, and that if this yard had sent it the two answers would have been answers to this yard's question and not answers to their own; and that at about ten in the morning the reason the five names are written as not being signatures is that a signature is a thing a person gives to a piece of paper and these five pieces of paper gave five names to this yard, and the difference is the whole of the difference between a community choosing a person and a yard receiving one; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason two days notice was given out loud on a Monday is that an order this yard makes about its own pages has to be arguable by nine people before it happens rather than after, and that a man who is going to object has two days to do it in daylight; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the man who lands fish was put in the slip at all is that this yard has written about forty other people this month and has not once written that one of them was right, and a keeper who can only be right in his own slip is a keeper who is not being checked; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason both of the tender's questions are written down and neither of them was answered further is that she asked about the boat and not about the five and this yard answered about the boat, and that a slip which put the two together would be implying that the five and the boat are one conversation when the whole of this week has been that they are not; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the page of hands at fifteen is that five people are going to be on Wednesday's water and this yard has six refusals of lists of people on this coast behind it, and the only safe place for five names that are not names against limits is on five separate pieces of paper in five hands, face down on a bench stone until Wednesday noon.*
