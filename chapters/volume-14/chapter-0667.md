# Chapter 667

*Wednesday the Eighteenth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and the Morrow Line's Own Page Taken Down off the Nail Behind the Reeve's Bench for the First Time Since Before the Frost and Carried Into That Yard and Put Down on the Flat of That Bench Stone, and a Man of About Sixty-One Reading Out From It in About Nine People in About One Hundred and Forty-Six Words the Whole of What He Was Asked and What He Was Not Asked, and Nobody Answering Him, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Wednesday the eighteenth day of May, with that market shut for the morning and open for the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. At about eleven in the morning the Morrow Line's own page was taken down off the nail behind the reeve's bench, where it has hung since before the frost, and carried into that yard and put down on the flat of that bench stone, and at about half past twelve a man of about sixty-one read out from it, in about nine people, in about one hundred and fifty-eight words, the whole of what he had been asked and the whole of what he had not been asked, and nobody answered him and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him. That page went back on that nail at about ten past four in the afternoon, in the same place, at the same height, and nobody in this harbor has said that it was ever off it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the wooden tally slate is still lying flat on that bench stone where it was put down on the first of May, and nothing is on it.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood three hundred and sixty-five days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty-seven days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and five days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-first night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and eighty-nine days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and forty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and fifty-ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventy-sixth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and seven days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been nailed on that board this month. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and sixty-five days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The Morrow Line's own page came off that nail at about eleven in the morning on the Wednesday and it went back on that same nail at about ten past four in the afternoon.

That page has hung in this harbor since before the frost and it is the oldest working page in this yard and in five hours it was the only thing in that harbor that anybody had carried from one place to another on that day without a reason anybody could name.

---

At about half past twelve the man of about sixty-one stood at that bench stone and read out from it, in about nine people, in about one hundred and fifty-eight words, the whole of what he had been asked and the whole of what he had not been asked.

He said he was asked to put a mark beside a line about an echo of somebody in this harbor. He said he was not asked whether he had read it. He said he was not asked whether he agreed with it. He said he was not asked whether he could get away from it afterwards, and he said that last thing twice.

He said that he was given a sheet and not a page and that the difference between a sheet and a page is that a page has a line at the head of it and a sheet does not, and that he had not known there was a line at the head of it.

He said he was not able to read and that he has not been able to read since he was nine years old and that he has never put a mark on a page of anything in his life until April.

He did not say who gave him the sheet. Nobody asked him and this yard did not ask him, and he was not asked again afterwards.

---

Nobody answered him. The man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood about two yards from that bench stone and did not say anything at all and was not asked to.

At about one in the afternoon a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons said one thing out loud, and what he said was that there is a difference between a man who is not asked whether he can get away from a thing and a man who is asked and says he cannot, and that the first one is not a thing anybody can look at afterwards.

Nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him.

---

At about half past two a woman of about thirty of no office asked, in about nine people, and out loud, whether this yard was going to write down what he had just said, and the man who keeps the leaf said that he was going to write it in the first column and not in this slip and not anywhere else, and she said that was the correct answer and that she had wanted to hear it said in nine people and not in a back room.

Nobody thanked her for asking.

---

At about ten past four in the afternoon that page went back on that nail behind the reeve's bench at the same height it has hung at since before the frost, and the man of about sixty-one carried it out himself because he was the one who had asked for it to be taken down there in the first place, and nobody helped him and nobody watched him do it.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and ninety-one words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been standing at that bench stone at half past twelve.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence on that board in the front street, unmoved, and I have stopped saying that figure the way I used to, not because it has moved. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four, and this yard is eighty days without a tin.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now eighty days without one.**

**And the Morrow Line's own page was off that nail and on this stone and back on that nail today, and a man of about sixty-one read out of it in nine people at about half past twelve what he had been asked and what he had not been asked, and one of the things he said he had not been asked was whether he could get away from it afterwards, and he said that twice.**

**Here is the whole of what this yard has got tonight and I am going to put it in order. One: that page of days carries three names against the Morrow Line and it carried four on Monday. Two: the Morrow Line's own page carries four names and one of those four is not on the page of days at all. Three: a man of about sixty-one was given a sheet and not a page and put a mark on it and could not read it. Four: he was not asked whether he could get away from it afterwards.**

**Those four are a case. A case is not a verdict and I am not going to make it one and this yard has got no power to make it one. What I am going to say is that the four of them are about one man, and that the man asked to come off that page on Monday and he came off it, and that the three who are still on it did not ask to come off it and have not been asked whether they want to, and that this yard is not going to ask them, because they were not asked when they were put on.**

**And I am going to say the hardest thing in this slip and then I am going to stop. That man of about sixty-one read his own account out of this harbor's own page at half past twelve today and nobody answered him, and that is correct and I would do it again. But it is also true that this yard has spent nine days finding this out in the way that finds things out slowest and that the slowness is the reason he was still able to stand there and say it. If this yard had been quicker he would have been taken off that page in April by somebody who never spoke to him at all.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a man's own account of himself read out of a page in nine people, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page was back on that nail at the height it has hung at since before the frost, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**NOT A ROTA DAY AND NOT A WAGE TUESDAY AND NOT A WIPE, AND NO CHARGE WAS PLACED ON THAT CISTERN AND ITS COVERS LAY ON IT FROM NINE IN THE MORNING UNTIL FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING THE MORROW LINE'S OWN PAGE WAS TAKEN DOWN OFF THE NAIL BEHIND THE REEVE'S BENCH, WHERE IT HAS HUNG SINCE BEFORE THE FROST, AND CARRIED INTO THAT YARD AND PUT DOWN ON THE FLAT OF THAT BENCH STONE, AND AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-ONE READ OUT FROM IT, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND IN ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT WORDS, THE WHOLE OF WHAT HE HAD BEEN ASKED AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT HE HAD NOT BEEN ASKED, INCLUDING THAT HE WAS NOT ASKED WHETHER HE COULD GET AWAY FROM IT AFTERWARDS, WHICH HE SAID TWICE. HE DID NOT SAY WHO GAVE HIM THE SHEET AND NOBODY ASKED HIM AND HE WAS NOT ASKED AGAIN AFTERWARDS. NOBODY ANSWERED HIM. THAT PAGE WENT BACK ON THAT NAIL AT ABOUT TEN PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AT THE SAME HEIGHT, AND HE CARRIED IT OUT HIMSELF. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason that page was taken off that nail and carried into that yard is that a man asked to be taken off a page cannot be taken off a page he has never been shown, and that showing it to him was the last thing this yard owed him and the only thing anybody could do that did not cost anybody else anything; and that at about half past twelve in the afternoon the reason nobody said anything while he was reading is that every person in that yard of nine had come to that bench stone on one of the last four days for their own reason, and that four of them had a reason that would have made what they said into a defence of themselves, and that a defence of yourself made at a man who cannot read is not a defence; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason that sentence of the man of about fifty-three is written down whole and not paraphrased is that it is the sharpest thing anybody has said about this coast in nine days and this page is the only place it will survive, and it was said once; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason the woman of thirty asked whether it would be written down in nine people rather than in a back room is that she was not asking about the man, she was asking about the yard, and that a yard which agrees in a back room and disagrees in the open is a yard that has two positions and this coast has spent thirteen volumes refusing that; and that at about ten past four in the afternoon the reason it went back at the same height is that a page which goes back in a different place is a page that has been moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever moved a page, and that if this yard took it down it owed putting it back where it was and no lower and no to one side; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason the last thing in this slip is about this yard being slow is that every account this coast has of itself in thirteen volumes is one of being careful, and that careful has turned out this week to have a price and the price has a name and an age and a room he pays for by the week, and I am putting that on this leaf tonight while it is still true and while I still have both halves of it.*
