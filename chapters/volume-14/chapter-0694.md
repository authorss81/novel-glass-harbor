# Chapter 694

*Tuesday the Fourteenth Day of June, the Eighty-Sixth Wage Tuesday, the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes by a Man of About Thirty-One of the Second Lay and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Eighty-Sixth Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, the Eighty-Sixth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Behind the Eighty-Fifth, a Clerk Out of the Office on the West Basin Leaving a Register Copy of Ten Days at About Eleven With a Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence on Each of Them and the Register Copy Going Into the Hands of the Woman of Thirty of No Office and Not Onto Any Page in That Yard, and Nobody in That Yard Reading the Basket in That Boathouse, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand and Nine*

Tuesday the fourteenth day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-sixth wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it. The column on the lid of that step stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten in the morning and it took him four minutes and it did not move. Behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-sixth week stands fallen due at the close of this day behind the eighty-fifth, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any Tuesday this month. At about eleven in the morning a clerk out of the office on the west basin left a register copy of ten days with the hours against them, and it is the first time anybody has handed this yard a paper showing where the hundred and sixty-eight pence a day comes from, and the register copy went into the hands of the woman of thirty of no office and not onto any page in that yard. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on this coast went a day on without any of them being asked anything, and six of the nine have been still since the second of May. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-second day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and ninety-fourth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-second day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and ninety-eighth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their three hundred and sixteenth day with no figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has come to its two hundred and seventy-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street has reached its two hundred and eighty-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street stands on its three hundred and third day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-fourth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column of the leaf is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else in it and nobody in that yard or any other has put a hand in it today. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-second days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes and it did not move, and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay has read that same lid out on eighty-six Tuesdays and the figure has not changed on one of them.

Behind the eighty-sixth stands behind the eighty-fifth and behind that one stands behind the eighty-fourth, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and there is nobody in that market who has ever been told what those envelopes are for.

This yard asked nobody anything this week and has not asked since the fourth of May.

---

At about eleven in the morning a clerk out of the office on the west basin came down that lane with a sheet folded in two and put it on the bench in that shed and did not stay.

He said that it is the register copy and that the office keeps one of everything it is given and that this one is theirs, and that it is ten days of the berth fund and the days are Monday the fourth of June through Wednesday the thirteenth and the hours are against each of them.

The man who keeps the leaf read it standing up and then sat down and read it again, and it says a hundred and sixty-eight pence on each of the ten days, including the two Sundays, and against each one the words a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth.

He has been saying that figure out loud every day since the second of May and had never once said where it came from.

---

At about ten past twelve the man who keeps the leaf asked the clerk one question and it was whether the office had a column of what it owes the same as this yard keeps one, and the clerk said that it does not and that the office pays a day out of a fund and keeps a register of what it was given and does not keep a column.

The keeper wrote that down with the hour against it and no name against it, and the clerk went back up the lane to the west basin without being thanked and without asking for anything.

---

At about half past twelve the register copy went into the hands of the woman of thirty of no office.

She was in that shed at that hour and the keeper put it into her hands and said that it is a municipal paper and that it is hers and that this yard is not going to put a municipal paper on a page of days.

She said that she had a record of her own and did not want it in it, and that she would take it up to the room over the fish market and put it in the press with the other things, and that nobody in this yard needed to know what she did with it.

Nobody asked her what she did with it and the keeper wrote the hour of the handing over down and wrote nothing else.

---

At about two in the afternoon about eleven people were standing on those boards and about four of them cannot read a notice, and a man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish asked the keeper, in front of the other ten, whether the eighty-sixth week was any bigger than the eighty-fifth.

The keeper said that he did not know and that the lid does not carry it and that the lid has not carried it on any of the eighty-six Tuesdays.

The man who lands fish said that was the first answer he had ever got out of anybody about that lid that was worth having, and the keeper wrote the hour of the question down and wrote no figure of money against either of them.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Tuesday line on the page of that week and left it short, being that a column on a lid was read out in four minutes and did not move, and that a register copy of ten days came from the office on the west basin and says a hundred and sixty-eight pence on each of the ten including two Sundays, and that the copy is not on any page of that yard.

He wrote the eighty-sixth week against the second half of that line and not the twenty envelopes, and that is on the face of the sheet with the hour beside it.

Nobody thanked him for the line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and twenty-eight words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been on those boards at about two in the afternoon.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on that lid for the eighty-sixth time, read out at about ten this morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who is not on the water and has not been on the water, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and the eighty-sixth week stands fallen due at the close of today, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any Tuesday this month. I am not going to say that week out loud, and a man of about fifty-six asked me at two o'clock this afternoon whether it was any bigger than the eighty-fifth and I told him I did not know, and he said that was the first answer he had ever had about that lid that was worth having.**

**Now the paper that came this morning, and it is the first time in forty-three days that anybody has shown this yard where a figure it says out loud every single day actually comes from. It is a register copy off the office on the west basin, it is ten days, it is Monday the fourth of June through Wednesday the thirteenth, and it says a hundred and sixty-eight pence on each of the ten of them, including both Sundays, and against each one the words a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth. I have been saying that figure at that stone every evening since the second of May as though it came out of nowhere, and it comes out of a fund, and a fund pays a day for a day and does not care what a sea is under martial custody. That is the whole of what I have learned this morning and it took ten days of somebody else's book.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and seven days without one.**

**And the register copy is not on a page in this yard. It is a municipal paper and it is in her hands and she has taken it up to the room over the fish market and into a press with other things, and nobody in this yard has asked her what she will do with it and nobody is going to.**

**And the last of it. There is a basket in that boathouse at the bottom of that lane with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody in that market has ever put a hand in it and nobody in that market has been asked about it by this yard, and I am not going to stand in that market this evening and pretend that the reason is a good one.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a Tuesday that did not move, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had four lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND WAS READ OUT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING IN FOUR MINUTES BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THE SECOND LAY AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE CHEST WITH THE TWENTY ENVELOPES IN IT STOOD SHUT BEHIND THAT DOOR FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR, AND THE EIGHTY-SIXTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY BEHIND THE EIGHTY-FIFTH. AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING A CLERK OUT OF THE OFFICE ON THE WEST BASIN LEFT A REGISTER COPY OF TEN DAYS, BEING MONDAY THE FOURTH OF JUNE THROUGH WEDNESDAY THE THIRTEENTH, WITH THE HOURS AGAINST EACH OF THEM AND A HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE ON EACH OF THEM INCLUDING BOTH SUNDAYS AND THE WORDS A DAY'S WORK WITH A BOAT AND TWO MEN AT A BERTH AGAINST EACH OF THOSE, AND THAT COPY WENT INTO THE HANDS OF THE WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE AND IS NOT ON ANY PAGE IN THAT YARD AND IS A MUNICIPAL PAPER. THE MONEY SHEET REMAINS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason ten days of a register copy are written down as one sheet and not as ten is that ten lines of a municipal book copied into a page of days would turn a page of days into a copy of somebody else's book, and that what this yard needed from that paper was not the ten days but the fact of the ten days; and that at about ten past twelve the reason the clerk's answer is written down whole and not shortened is that it is the plainest sentence anybody has said in that shed since the second of May, being that the office pays a day out of a fund and keeps a register of what it was given and does not keep a column, and that the difference between that and this yard is the whole difference between an office and a yard and it took one question to find out; and that at about half past twelve the reason that copy went into her hands and not onto a page is that a page of days has a man who keeps it in it and a municipal book has an office behind it, and that the two of them together would be a page that a stranger could not tell the difference of, and that the whole of what this coast has printed in six weeks is that people must be able to tell the difference; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason a man of about fifty-six asking whether a week is bigger than another week was put on a slip is that it is the first question anybody has asked in six weeks which was about that lid itself, and that a question about a lid is a question about the harbor and a question about a week is a question about a person, and that this yard answers the first kind out loud and refuses the second kind on a Tuesday; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on a basket nobody has put a hand in is that the register copy this morning was the one paper that came into that yard in six weeks and was given away again, and a slip that ended on the paper would be a slip about paperwork, and the basket is the thing this harbor has spent six weeks declining to open and that is worth a whole paragraph and no more than a paragraph.
