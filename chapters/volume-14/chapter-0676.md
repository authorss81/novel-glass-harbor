# Chapter 676

*Friday the Twenty-Seventh Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Man of About Fifty-Three With a Boat of About Fourteen Tons Coming into That Shed at About Eleven in the Morning With a Slip of Paper in His Own Hand and Asking in About Nine People for Eleven Words He Wrote a Long Time Ago to Go onto the Friday Line of the Page of That Week Beside the Thursday Line, Being This Coast Can Be Cut in One Place at a Time, and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Writing Them Down and Reading Them Out and Nobody Answering Them and No Figure Set Against Them and the Man of About Fifty-Three Not Being Told He Was Wrong and Not Being Asked to Take Them Off and the Word Thanked at Zero, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Nine and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Nineteen*

Friday the twenty-seventh day of May, with that market open all day and the boards at the back of it quiet by three in the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind that market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. At about eleven in the morning a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came into that shed with a slip of paper in his own hand and asked, in about nine people, for eleven words he wrote a long time ago to go onto the Friday line of the page of that week beside the Thursday line, being that this coast can be cut in one place at a time. The man who keeps the leaf wrote them down and read them out. Nobody answered them. No figure is set against them anywhere on that sheet or in that shed, and that man of about fifty-three was not told he was wrong and was not asked to take them off, and nobody thanked him for coming. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters on that coast have each been carried a day further today, and none of the nine was carried by this yard. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and seventy-fourth day. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy-sixth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and fourteenth day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eightieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point are on their two hundred and ninety-eighth day without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-eighth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and sixteenth days. Nothing has gone into the third column of that leaf and nobody has been put in it, and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been put on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-fourth days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven in the morning the man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons came into that shed with a slip of paper folded in four and put it on the flat of that bench stone and opened it and did not read it out.

He said that he had copied it out, and that the copy is in his own hand, and that the original is on a page four days down that coast road in a box, and that he wrote it a long time ago and has never had it answered and is not asking for it to be answered this morning.

He said that he was not saying anybody was wrong. He said that he had not come about Wednesday night and he was not against Wednesday night and he had heard about it from the woman at the chandler's counter and had not come to argue with anybody.

Then he said the thing he had come for, which was that he would like it on the Friday line of that new sheet, beside the Thursday line, and that he wanted the two of them read out in the same nine people.

---

The man who keeps the leaf read it out, in about nine people, and it is eleven words long.

**This coast can be cut in one place at a time.**

He wrote it on the Friday line of that sheet and put the day and the hour against it, and he put no figure against it and no figure on the other side of it, and he wrote on the face of the sheet under it that no figure is set against it and that is on the face of the sheet on purpose.

He read it out a second time for about four people in that nine who cannot read a column, and nobody asked whether they had understood it and nobody read it a third time.

---

Nobody answered it.

A woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane opened her mouth at about half past eleven and shut it again, and everybody in that nine saw her do it and nobody spoke for her and nobody afterwards said she had been about to agree.

The man of about thirty-two of that harbor looked at the two lines on that sheet for a while and said nothing at all, and nobody turned to him.

At about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard said that the two of those are on the same piece of paper now and that he did not know what that was worth, and the man of about fifty-three said that it was worth that they were on the same piece of paper.

Nobody agreed with either of them and nobody argued with either of them.

---

The man of about fifty-three said two more things before he went, and neither of them was about Wednesday night.

He said that he is not taking it off again, and that if this harbor ever does put a figure against it he would like to be told, and that he would not put a name against it or anybody else's name against it, and that a sentence with nobody's name on it has been on a page of this coast for a long time and is the only sentence on it that has never been answered.

He said that he is sixty in the spring and that he would like something to be on a page before that.

Nobody thanked him for either of those and he did not wait to be thanked, and he went up to his boat.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Friday line in the first column with the hour against it, set the whole of the eleven words against it once and did not write them a second time, and recorded that nobody answered them, that no figure is set against them, and that the man of about fifty-three was not told he was wrong.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and thirty-nine words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that shed at about eleven.

"**A man of about fifty-three came into that shed at about eleven this morning with a bit of paper he had copied out himself and asked for it to go on that sheet beside what happened on Wednesday night, and I read it out twice and wrote it down and there is no figure against it and there is no figure on the other side of it, and nobody in that shed answered it, and I did not tell him he was wrong, and he did not ask me to.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now eighty-nine days without one.**

**Now I am going to say the part of this week I have been not saying, and then I am going to stop for the day. Those eleven words have been on a page of this coast for longer than any wall in that shed has been up, and in all that time nobody has answered them and nobody has been asked to, and this morning a man brought a copy of his own sentence to a new sheet and put it next to a night four people worked for somebody else, and those two things are not the same size and they are on the same piece of paper.**

**I have not answered them and I am not going to this year. A woman of about forty-four opened her mouth at about half past eleven and shut it, and I saw that, and I wrote nothing about it, and if I had written it down it would have been this yard putting an answer on that sheet on her behalf about a sentence she has not read out. A page that carries somebody's half-made answer is worse than a page that carries a question, and I have watched this coast lose a fortnight to one of those.**

**And here is the only thing I want said. Eleven words against one night is not a count and it is not a refutation. It is one man's sentence and one night's work on the same piece of paper, and nobody has put a figure on either side of it, and that man is not wrong, and nobody in this shed has said he is right either, and both of those are the correct state of it.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eleven words that have gone unanswered for as long as that sheet has been up, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had five lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS CAME INTO THAT SHED WITH A SLIP OF PAPER FOLDED IN FOUR AND ASKED, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, FOR ELEVEN WORDS HE WROTE A LONG TIME AGO TO GO ONTO THE FRIDAY LINE OF THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK BESIDE THE THURSDAY LINE, BEING THAT THIS COAST CAN BE CUT IN ONE PLACE AT A TIME, AND HE SAID HE WAS NOT ASKING FOR IT TO BE ANSWERED AND WAS NOT SAYING ANYBODY WAS WRONG. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF READ THEM OUT TWICE AND WROTE THEM DOWN AND PUT NO FIGURE AGAINST THEM AND NO FIGURE ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THEM AND WROTE ON THE FACE OF THAT SHEET THAT NO FIGURE IS SET AGAINST THEM. NOBODY ANSWERED THEM AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WAS NOT TOLD HE WAS WRONG AND WAS NOT ASKED TO TAKE THEM OFF AND NOBODY THANKED HIM FOR COMING. THAT MONEY SHEET IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP THIS WEEK AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason a sentence that old is written on a new sheet in a new week is not that anybody has become convinced of it and not that the sheet is a good home for it, and that a man who has kept a copy of his own sentence in his own hand for that long is a man with a different reason, and that his reason is that he would like something to be on a page before he is sixty in the spring, and that this yard is entitled to write a man's reason down without agreeing with him about it; and that at about half past eleven in the morning the reason nothing at all is written about the woman of about forty-four is that a person opening their mouth and shutting it again is not an answer and is not a position, and that this yard has spent a month refusing to write down what people want and is not going to start writing down what people were nearly going to say, and that the day it does that is the day this shed has become a place where nine people watch each other's faces instead of a place where a leaf is kept; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason the eleven words were read out twice and not once is that the sheet is read out at half past five every day whatever is on it and a sentence that is read once in a market of forty is a sentence four people have, and that two readings is not more than the sentence deserves and three would be an argument; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason no figure is set against those words and no figure beside them is that a figure set against a sentence is a rebuttal and this yard has no standing to rebut a man about his own coast, and that the only figure this yard is entitled to put beside anything is a figure of money out of a tin, and this yard has not opened a tin since the twenty-ninth of April; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason this slip is given over to a sentence nobody answered is that a sheet which only ever carries things this harbor agrees with is a sheet that has stopped being a sheet, and that the test of whether a wall is a wall is what happens on it that somebody would not have chosen.*
