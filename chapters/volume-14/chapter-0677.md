# Chapter 677

*Saturday the Twenty-Eighth Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, and About Sixty People on Those Boards Before Eleven, the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Both in That Shed at About Eleven in the Morning in About Nine People With Nobody Sent for Either of Them, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Writing Two Lines in the Middle of the Saturday Line of the Page of That Week, One Under the Other, Neither Carrying a Name and Neither of the Two of Them Named in the Other's Line, and Then Asking That Nine Whether Anybody Else Wants to Write One Line Under the Two, and Four of Them Writing One Line Each and Nobody Agreeing With Either of the First Two and the Disagreement Not Settled and No Page Carrying Any Resolution of It, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ten and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Saturday the twenty-eighth day of May, with that market open all day and about sixty people on those boards before eleven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind that market covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. At about eleven in the morning the man of about thirty-two of that harbor and the woman of thirty of no office were both in that shed in about nine people, and nobody had sent for either of them and neither of them had come to fetch the other. The man who keeps the leaf wrote two lines in the middle of the Saturday line of the page of that week, one under the other, neither carrying a name and neither of the two of them named in the other's line, and then asked that nine whether anybody else wanted to write one line under the two, and four of them wrote one line each, and nobody agreed with either of the first two and the disagreement was not settled and no page carries any resolution of it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in that harbor are nine lines on a wall or a figure on a page, and a stranger can take all nine off that shed this morning without asking anybody for anything. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and seventy-fifth days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy-seventh days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and fifteenth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-first night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and ninety-ninth days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-sixth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and seventeenth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. That board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers, two of which have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been put on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-fifth days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Both of them were in that shed at about eleven in the morning and neither of them had sent for the other, and the man who keeps the leaf had not sent for either of them and had not asked either of them whether they were coming.

That is written down first because it is the only part of this morning that this yard did.

---

The two of them have been arguing in public since the fourth of May about one line on a page of hers, and the line is the third of the four that say what that page does not do, and it says that it does not say whether anybody agreed.

He says that a page which prints a man's yes at ten in the morning and throws away his no at four in the afternoon is not a record and is a man's morning being kept against him for the rest of his life.

She says that a page which prints nobody's disagreement is a page that whoever reads it first can write, and that six people in that yard could add one line under it tonight saying that everybody here agrees and put no name to it.

Neither of them had answered the other since. That is on the face of the page that hung in that shed from the fourth of May and it is on the face of it still.

---

At about eleven the man who keeps the leaf left a space in the middle of the Saturday line and wrote two lines in it, one under the other, and he read both of them out in that nine, and neither of the two lines carries a name and neither of the two of them is named in the other's line.

The first of the two is his, in his own words, and it says that a line on that wall that records who did not agree keeps a man's yes at ten in the morning and loses his no at four in the afternoon, and that after that the only thing anybody can read about him is that he was in favour on a Wednesday.

The second of the two is hers, in her own words, and it says that a line on that wall that records nobody's disagreement is a line that whoever reads it first can write, and that in nine months somebody would come along and read somebody's one line as a fact and there would be nothing on that page to say it was not.

He did not take his objection off the page and she did not take her third line off the page, and neither of them gave up anything belonging to their own work to get the other one quiet.

---

Then he asked that nine whether anybody else wanted to write one line under those two, and he said that anybody could and that nobody had to and that a line with a name on it would be a name and a line without one would be a line.

Four of them wrote one line each, in their own hands, and about five people in that nine cannot read a column and the four lines were read out afterwards.

A woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter wrote that she sells rope to both of them and would rather have a page she cannot write than a page she can, and that that is not an answer to either of the two of them because she has not read them.

A man of about thirty-one of the second lay wrote that he reads a lid out in four minutes on a Tuesday and does not know what is in a record and is not going to be asked.

A man of about fifty-six of the third lay who lands fish wrote that a line which says who did not agree would have him in it by Thursday and out of it by Friday, and that he would rather write that down himself than have either of the two of them write it down for him.

A woman of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm wrote that she does not know which of those two lines is right and is not going to be the one who decides it, and that she will say that much in writing and stop there.

Nobody in that nine agreed with the first of the two lines and nobody in that nine agreed with the second of them either, and the four lines under them are not on either side.

---

Nobody in that shed said the disagreement was settled and nobody said it was not settled, and the man of about thirty-two and the woman of thirty of no office did not speak to each other about any of it between eleven and four, and this yard wrote down that they did not and wrote down not one word about why.

That disagreement is now on a page that any person in this market can walk up to and read, and it was answered in the same place by four people who are not the two of them, and it is still open, and no page of this harbor carries a resolution of it.

Nobody thanked the four of them for writing and nobody thanked the two of them for standing there.

---

At about four in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office asked the man of about thirty-two of that harbor what time that market shuts on a Sunday, and he told her, and she wrote it on a slip of her own and put it in her own pocket.

Nobody in that yard saw anything in that and the man who keeps the leaf wrote down the hour of the question and did not write down what was on the slip.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and twenty-nine words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had written a line on that sheet that morning.

"**Two people who have been arguing in public in this harbor since the fourth of May stood in that shed at about eleven this morning with nobody sent for either of them, and I wrote down what each of them said on the same piece of paper, one under the other, and neither of them is named in the other's line, and then four other people wrote one line each under the two of those, and that is what happened and I am not going to make it into anything else.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now ninety days without one.**

**Here is what has changed and here is what has not, and I am going to be exact about both. What has changed is where it is. It used to be a thing that two people had said out loud to each other in this shed and that four people in this market remembered. It is now a thing on a page that anybody can walk up to, and four people who are not those two have written their own lines under it in their own hands. What has not changed is the disagreement. Neither of them took anything off. Neither of them gave up her page or his boats. Nobody in nine people said one of them was right. Nobody in nine people said one of them was wrong. And I have written nothing on that sheet that says it is settled, because it is not, and a page that says it is settled is the cheapest thing in this harbor and I am not going to be the one who writes it.**

**And the last of it. In fourteen weeks of keeping that leaf the hardest thing anybody in this yard has had to do was to write down a thing that nobody had asked to be written down. That is all I did this morning. Nobody sent for those two. Nobody asked either of them whether they were coming. They came, they said what they had already said, and four other people wrote under it, and that is a page and not a settlement, and a page is worth a great deal more than a settlement this harbor could have got.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a page with two lines and four more on it, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had six lines on it and two of them were in the middle of the Saturday one, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR AND THE WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE WERE BOTH IN THAT SHED IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND NOBODY HAD SENT FOR EITHER OF THEM AND NEITHER OF THEM HAD COME TO FETCH THE OTHER. THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF LEFT A SPACE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SATURDAY LINE AND WROTE TWO LINES IN IT, ONE UNDER THE OTHER, AND READ BOTH OF THEM OUT, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO LINES CARRIES A NAME AND NEITHER OF THE TWO OF THEM IS NAMED IN THE OTHER'S LINE. HE DID NOT TAKE HIS OBJECTION OFF THE PAGE AND SHE DID NOT TAKE HER THIRD LINE OFF THE PAGE, AND NEITHER OF THEM GAVE UP ANYTHING BELONGING TO THEIR OWN WORK. FOUR OTHER PEOPLE WROTE ONE LINE EACH UNDER THOSE TWO IN THEIR OWN HANDS, AND NOBODY IN THAT NINE AGREED WITH EITHER OF THE FIRST TWO AND THE FOUR LINES UNDER THEM ARE NOT ON EITHER SIDE. NOBODY IN THAT SHED SAID THE DISAGREEMENT WAS SETTLED AND NOBODY SAID IT WAS NOT SETTLED, AND THOSE TWO DID NOT SPEAK TO EACH OTHER ABOUT ANY OF IT BETWEEN ELEVEN AND FOUR. THE FIGURE OUT ON THAT MONEY SHEET IS ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AND THE FIGURE IN IS TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND WHAT IS LEFT OF THE DIFFERENCE IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THAT SHEET CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason two lines were written one under the other in the middle of one day's line and not on a sheet of their own is that a sheet of their own would have been a second page about the same two people, and that this harbor has four sheets already and every one of the four was invented by somebody who wanted their own thing to be the one being kept, and that putting it in the middle of a Saturday is the whole of the method, because it means the two of them are on a page that has a cistern on it and a wall on it and a man who was short of hands on it; and that at about half past eleven in the morning the reason neither of the two lines carries a name is that this yard writes down things that happened and this morning what happened is that two people said two things, and that putting a name against a sentence in a document this yard keeps is how a document becomes a record of people instead of a record of a coast, and that the whole of the fight between the two of them is about that, and that a page that resolved it by printing a name would be resolving it in his favour without either of them asking; and that at about two in the afternoon the reason four people were asked to write and nobody was asked to agree is that a page two people wrote between them is a page two people own, and that the only way a page stops being owned by the people on it is for other people to be able to put something on it, and that what they put does not have to be an answer, and that three of those four lines are about the writer and not about either of the first two, and that this yard is glad of that and is not going to pretend otherwise; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the hour of a question about market hours is written down and what was on the slip is not is that a question asked in a yard between two people who have been at odds in public all week is either about work or it is about the other one, and that this yard is not entitled to decide which, and that a man who keeps a leaf can write down that a question was asked at four and stop there, and that in twenty years somebody can read it and see that they were still speaking; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip gives a whole paragraph to what has not changed as well as to what has is that a page which records an agreement as though it were a settlement is the oldest trick on this coast, and that the second column has one job and it is to say out loud which of the two things happened.*
