# Chapter 675

*Thursday the Twenty-Sixth Day of May, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Third Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Lying on It from About Eleven Until About Four, Four of the Cinder Bell's Own Community Being on the Water on the Lowfen Reed's Own Line From About Seven in the Evening Until About Two in the Morning Under the Reed's Own Order and Nobody from That Harbor Going With Them and Nobody in That Harbor Being Paid for It and the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor Not on the Water and Not Asked to Be, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Asking at About Half Past Two in About Nine People Whether That Night Is on the Page of That Week and Whether It Is on the Page of Hands, and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Answering Both and Naming a Second Sheet of Days in That Shed That He Is Not Going to Add Up This Week, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eight and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Fourteen*

Thursday the twenty-sixth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-third charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Four of the Cinder Bell's own community were on the water on the Lowfen Reed's own line from about seven in the evening until about two in the morning, under the Reed's own order, on the Reed's own boat, and nobody from that harbor went with them and nobody in that harbor was paid for it and this yard did not hire anybody. The man of about thirty-two of that harbor was not on the water and was not asked to be. At about half past two in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office asked, in about nine people, whether that night is on the page of that week and whether it is on the page of hands, and the man who keeps the leaf answered both and named a second sheet of days in that shed which he is not going to add up this week. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine standing things in this harbor are written down where they can be got at, and eight of the nine are about paper. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and seventy-third days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy-fifth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and thirteenth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and ninety-seventh days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-seventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-fourth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and fifteenth days. The third column of that leaf has stood empty and unappointed all week with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five, has taken no line, and is neither shut nor struck. Nobody in this harbor has ever opened two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole, and nothing has been put on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-third days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and fifty-third, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off again at about four.

That is the same eleven trips and the same four men and the same stairs on the inside of that wall, and it went on while four other people were asleep before a night they had agreed to work.

---

At about ten in the morning it was known in that market that four of the Cinder Bell's own community had been on the water on the Lowfen Reed's line from about seven in the evening until about two in the morning.

They went on the Reed's own boat and they took the Reed's own orders and they did what the man of about fifty of the Reed told them to do, and when they came in at about two they went up the lane and not into that market, and nobody met them at the top of that mole and nobody met them at the bottom of it.

Nobody from that harbor went with them. This yard hired nobody and paid nobody and sent nobody, and there is no figure of that night anywhere in that yard's book, and there is not going to be one.

---

The man of about thirty-two of that harbor was not on the water last night and was not asked to be and nobody sent for him and nobody mentioned it to him.

He was in that shed at about eleven in the morning coiling line off that bench stone, and a man of about thirty-three who hires hands in that yard asked him, in passing and about nothing in particular, whether he had heard what had gone on, and he said that he had.

That is the whole of what is known about him and it is written down with the hour against it.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office stood on the flat of that bench stone in about nine people and asked two questions.

She asked whether that night was on the page of that week.

She asked whether that night was on the page of hands.

The man who keeps the leaf said that it was on the page of that week and it was not on the page of hands, and that the page of hands counts people who have put their names to a route's own written limits, and that four people being on the water for another route is not a limit and is not a name and does not go there.

Then she asked him what would carry it, and he said there was a second sheet of days in that shed with the nine route names down the side of it and a figure against each of the nine, and that it had not been added up this month and that he was not going to add it up this week, and that when it is added up it will be added up off the page and not out of anybody's mouth.

Nobody thanked him for answering and nobody argued with him about it.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Thursday line on that sheet and left it to about two sentences, and it says that four people of one community were on the water on another route's line for one night under that route's own order, and that this yard sent nobody and hired nobody and paid nobody.

He wrote no hour of the night against it and no figure against it and no name against it, and he wrote on the face of the sheet that he had left the times out on purpose, because the times belong to the two routes and not to a sheet in this shed.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine were in that shed at about eleven.

"**Four people of this coast were on the water last night for about seven hours on somebody else's line, on somebody else's boat, under somebody else's orders, and they did it because they said they would, and not one of them is anybody this yard sent, and not one of them is on a page of this yard's. That is the whole of it and I am not going to put anything round it.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand this morning between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and it is the hundred and fifty-third, and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now eighty-eight days without one.**

**A woman of thirty of no office asked me at about half past two this afternoon two questions and I answered both of them in this yard and not in a corner. The night is on the page of that week. The night is not on the page of hands and it is never going to be on the page of hands, because the page of hands is names against a route's own limits and that is the opposite of what happened last night. And I told her about the second sheet of days in this shed with the nine names down the side of it, and I told her I am not adding it up this week.**

**Here is why I said that, and then I will stop. If this yard adds that sheet up on a Thursday because something nice happened on the Wednesday night, then this yard has taken a count of a thing because it liked the answer, and every figure on every page in this shed is worth exactly as much as the reason it was taken. That sheet has been standing at nothing since the first of May and it will still be standing at nothing on Friday, and if it is going to be anything it is going to be because somebody looked at it and not because I felt pleased at about eleven o'clock this morning.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for seven hours on somebody else's line, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had four lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**FOUR OF THE CINDER BELL'S OWN COMMUNITY WERE ON THE WATER ON THE LOWFEN REED'S OWN LINE FROM ABOUT SEVEN IN THE EVENING UNTIL ABOUT TWO IN THE MORNING UNDER THE REED'S OWN ORDER AND ON THE REED'S OWN BOAT, AND NOBODY FROM THAT HARBOR WENT WITH THEM AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR WAS PAID FOR IT AND THIS YARD DID NOT HIRE ANYBODY. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR WAS NOT ON THE WATER AND WAS NOT ASKED TO BE AND NOBODY SENT FOR HIM. AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE ASKED IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WHETHER THAT NIGHT WAS ON THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK AND WHETHER IT WAS ON THE PAGE OF HANDS, AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF SAID THAT IT WAS ON THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK AND WAS NOT ON THE PAGE OF HANDS AND NEVER WILL BE, AND NAMED A SECOND SHEET OF DAYS IN THAT SHED WHICH HE IS NOT GOING TO ADD UP THIS WEEK. ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE IS OUT ON THAT MONEY SHEET, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THERE IS ONE LINE ON THAT SHEET AND NO SECOND. NO GAP WAS READ OUT OF AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN MOVED BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the hours of last night are written as about seven and about two and not as a number of hours is that this yard was not on that water and did not see it, and that a figure of seven is a figure this yard would be guessing at, and this sheet does not carry guesses; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the one line about the man of about thirty-two is a line about a question asked in passing is that a person who was not on the water and was not asked to be is not a person anything has happened to, and the only fact available about him this morning is that somebody asked him whether he had heard, and that this yard is obliged to write that down and is not permitted to write down what he thought about it; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason the second sheet of days is named out loud to that woman and not shown to her is that naming a thing and reading a thing off it are different acts, and that if this yard had handed her that sheet at about half past two this afternoon she would have had a figure in her head before the end of the week and that figure would be in this market by Saturday; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason no times are written against the Thursday line is that a page of days in this shed is a page of what this harbor can be shown to have done, and that four people from one community on another route's line is not a thing this harbor did, and writing the times would have dressed it up as one; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the sheet and not on the four people is that if it ended on the four people this shed would have a habit of ending its slips on whoever did something, and the day a slip ends on a name is the day a slip stops being a record and starts being a report, and this harbor has been on the receiving end of a report.*
