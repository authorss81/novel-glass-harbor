# Chapter 699

*Sunday the Nineteenth Day of June, a Wipe and the Hundred and Eleventh Time That the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Has Come Down Bare, Twenty-Eight Minutes to Take It Off the Wall and Fifteen Standing Bare and Twenty-Eight to Get It Back, Being Seventy-One the Whole of It From About Ten Past Ten to About Twenty-One Minutes Past Eleven, a Minute Longer Each Way and a Minute Longer Bare Than the Sunday Before, Seven of Those Sundays Now and Not One Minute of Any of Them Accounted For in This Harbor, a Boat Going Out Off That Mole at About Half Past Eleven With About Nine People on Its Deck and a Hull of a Size Not in That Harbor's Water Being Seen Once From About Half Past One in the Afternoon From Where She Was Standing and Not Understood by One of the Nine and No Figure Set Against Her and Nobody Going to Look for Her and No Word Said in Any Mouth About What She Is For, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand and Thirty-Four*

Sunday the nineteenth day of June, with that market shut for the morning and standing open from about two in the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe, and the hundred and eleventh time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it. Two men began at about ten past ten and that wall was bare by about forty to eleven and it was back on its holders at about twenty-one minutes past eleven, at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame is still not whole. Twenty-eight minutes off the wall, fifteen standing bare, twenty-eight to get it back, which is seventy-one minutes the whole of it, and that is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the Sunday before, and that is seven of those Sundays now. At about half past eleven a boat went out off that mole with about nine people on her deck and at about half past one in the afternoon a hull of a size that is not in that harbor's water was seen once from where she was standing by about nine people on the deck of a boat, and not one of the nine knew what it was for, and no figure was set against her and nobody went to look for her. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters in that harbor ran a day on this morning and not one of the nine has anything to do with the boat that went out. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-seventh day. Three hundred and ninety-ninth days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-seventh day. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and third night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-first days with no figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-first. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighth days. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirty-ninth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed, a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-seventh days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came down bare for the hundred and eleventh time and it went back up at about twenty-one minutes past eleven and it did not go up whole.

Twenty-eight minutes to get it off the wall, fifteen standing with it bare, and twenty-eight to get it back, which is seventy-one the whole of it from about ten past ten.

Seven of those Sundays now, being twenty-two and nine and twenty-two on the eighth of May, twenty-three and ten and twenty-three on the fifteenth, twenty-four and eleven and twenty-four on the twenty-second, twenty-five and twelve and twenty-five on the twenty-ninth, twenty-six and thirteen and twenty-six on the fifth of June, twenty-seven and fourteen and twenty-seven on the twelfth, and twenty-eight and fifteen and twenty-eight this morning.

About four people near enough saw it happen and went on with what they were doing, which is what they did on the six Sundays before it, and neither of the two men who does that work has said one word in seven Sundays and this yard is not going to ask.

---

At about half past eleven a boat went out off that mole with about nine people on her deck.

They went out under a route's own order and not under this yard's, and this yard did not go and did not send anybody, and they were on the Waterway and came back at about four.

The woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender was not on that boat and nobody has said she was and nobody in that yard asked.

---

At about half past one in the afternoon, with the boat lying still and about nine people on her deck, they saw it.

It was a hull, and it was a size that is not in that harbor's water, and it was going slowly, and it was not one of the nine. It was seen from the side and from a little abaft her beam and nobody on that boat went below and nobody on that boat went above her and nobody on that boat got a line out.

It was seen for about two minutes and then it was round the shoulder of that head and it was not seen again that day.

---

At about three in the afternoon four of the nine came down that lane past that yard on their own way.

The man who keeps the leaf asked one question of the first of them, and the question was what it was, and the man said that he did not know.

The keeper asked the second of them the same question and the second of them said that he did not know either, and that it was a big thing and that nobody on that boat had said a word about it for about an hour.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote that down with the hour against it and wrote no figure of money against it and no estimate of what it would cost and no guess at what it was for, and did not ask either of them a second question.

Nobody in that yard went and looked for it after that and nobody went and looked for it on Monday morning and this yard has written on that page that it did not go.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Sunday line on the page of that week and it says three things, being the three numbers of that wall with the hour against them, that about nine people on the deck of a boat saw a hull of a size that is not in this harbor's water at about half past one this afternoon for about two minutes and that not one of the nine knew what it was for, and that four of the nine were asked one question each on their own way up that lane and that both of them said they did not know.

He wrote the words saw it and did not understand it against that line and wrote nothing else against it at all.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and eighty-nine words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been standing on that deck this afternoon.

"**Twenty-eight off that wall this morning, fifteen standing bare, twenty-eight back, from about ten past ten to about twenty-one minutes past eleven, and that is seventy-one the whole of it and that is the hundred and eleventh time, and it is a minute longer each way than it was in May, and there is no figure of money in that sentence and there is not going to be one, and that is seven Sundays of a wall that nobody in this harbor will explain to anybody.**

**And then this afternoon, and I am going to say this plainly and then I am going to stop talking about it. About nine people on the deck of a boat lying off that mole saw a hull at about half past one this afternoon for about two minutes, and she was a size that is not in this harbor's water, and she was going slowly, and she was not one of the nine, and nobody on that boat went below her and nobody went above her and nobody got a line out, and then she was round the shoulder of that head and nobody saw her again today.**

**I asked two of those four men one question each on their way up this lane, and the question was what it was, and the first of them said he did not know and the second of them said he did not know either and that it was a big thing and that nobody on that boat had said a word about it for about an hour. That is the whole of what nine people made of it and I have written it down with the hour against it and no name against any of them.**

**And I want to say three things about what I did not do. I did not put a figure of money on it. I did not work out what it would cost anybody to be in the way of, and I did not ask anybody's advice about that at half past three this afternoon and I did not ask it this evening either. And this yard did not go and look for her, and did not send anybody to look for her, and did not go up that coast road to ask anybody whether they had seen her. That last one is the one I want nine people to hear twice, because there are four people in that market who would have gone by now and one of them who would have gone twice.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and twelve days without one.**

**And the last of it. That page of this week has eleven lines on it and every one of them has an hour against it and not one of them has a figure against it. The page of hands stands at fifteen and was not touched. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. The coil was on that nail at ten this morning and it is not on that nail this evening and this yard is not going to keep saying where it went.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for seventy-one minutes or for two minutes of a hull, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had three lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THE WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT TEN PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT FIFTEEN MINUTES PAST ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS CARRIED SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, IT HAVING TAKEN TWENTY-EIGHT MINUTES OFF THE WALL, FIFTEEN STANDING BARE AND TWENTY-EIGHT TO GET IT BACK, BEING SEVENTY-ONE MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT FROM ABOUT TEN PAST TEN, AGAINST TWENTY-SEVEN AND FOURTEEN AND TWENTY-SEVEN ON THE SUNDAY BEFORE, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY IT IS GETTING SLOWER AND THIS YARD HAS STOPPED ASKING THE TWO MEN WHO DO IT. AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN A BOAT WENT OUT OFF THAT MOLE WITH ABOUT NINE PEOPLE ON HER DECK UNDER A ROUTE'S OWN ORDER AND NOT UNDER THAT YARD'S, AND THIS YARD DID NOT GO AND DID NOT SEND ANYBODY, AND THEY CAME BACK AT ABOUT FOUR. AT ABOUT HALF PAST ONE IN THE AFTERNOON, WITH THAT BOAT LYING STILL, ABOUT NINE PEOPLE ON HER DECK SAW A HULL FOR ABOUT TWO MINUTES, BEING A HULL OF A SIZE THAT IS NOT IN THAT HARBOR'S WATER, GOING SLOWLY, NOT ONE OF THE NINE, SEEN FROM THE SIDE AND FROM A LITTLE AFT HER BEAM, WITH NOBODY ON THAT BOAT GOING BELOW HER AND NOBODY GOING ABOVE HER AND NOBODY GETTING A LINE OUT, AND SHE WAS ROUND THE SHOULDER OF THAT HEAD BY THE TIME THE TWO MINUTES WERE UP. TWO OF THOSE FOUR MEN WERE ASKED ONE QUESTION EACH ON THEIR OWN WAY UP THAT LANE, BEING WHAT IT WAS, AND BOTH OF THEM SAID THEY DID NOT KNOW. NO FIGURE OF MONEY AND NO FIGURE OF TIME WAS SET AGAINST HER AND THAT YARD DID NOT GO AND LOOK FOR HER AND DID NOT SEND ANYBODY AND WROTE ON THAT PAGE THAT IT DID NOT GO. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the nineteenth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the hundred and eleventh time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that that butt behind the market was covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it; and that at about half past eleven a boat went out off that mole with about nine people on her deck, and that they went out under a route's own order and not under this yard's and that this yard did not go and did not send anybody, and that they were on the Waterway and came back at about four; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past one in the afternoon the reason that hull is on that page as two minutes and a size and nothing else is that a thing nine people saw from a deck is a thing nine people saw, and that this yard was not on that deck and has no standing to call a hull in and would only be asking four men to describe a thing they have already been asked to describe once; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason one question was put to two of them and not the same question to all nine is that a question put to a man about his own morning is his to refuse, and this yard has spent fifty days finding out that a man who has said he does not know is a man who has answered; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason no figure of money and no figure of time is set against that hull is that nobody in this harbor knows what it is, and a figure put against a thing nobody can name is not a measurement, it is the beginning of an argument that four people will have by Tuesday and this coast has six refusals of lists of people behind it and is not going to be the seventh; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason this yard wrote on that page that it did not go and did not send anybody is that a yard which writes down only what it did has to be able to be read by the next yard, and the next yard will want to know whether this one went, and that the answer is no and it is on the page and not in a mouth where it could be argued with; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on a coil and a far bench is that a hull nobody understood and a wall nobody will explain are both things this harbor has in common, and that the only honest note to end a Sunday on is one about two ordinary things that carried on all day while a large thing went past outside.
