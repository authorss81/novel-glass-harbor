# Chapter 682

*Thursday the Second Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Fifty-Fifth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight in the Morning and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It and the Covers Lying on It from About Eleven Until About Four, Four of the Nine Answers Come Back by About Eleven in the Morning Saying They Will Put a Person on That Water and Two of Those Four Saying It in Their Own Words on a Second Sheet of Paper in Their Own Hands, and the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Writing Down Every Clause of It and Not One Figure, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifteen and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Thursday the second day of June, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and fifty-fifth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. By about eleven in the morning four of the nine answers had come back and four of those four said that their community would put a person on that water, and two of those four said it in their own words on a second sheet of paper in their own hands, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote down every clause of it and put no figure against any of it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in that harbor can be had off a surface this afternoon without a key to anything. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and eightieth days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eighty-second days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and twentieth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-sixth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been three hundred and fourth days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-fourth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and ninety-first day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twenty-second days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Nobody in this harbor has opened two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole and nothing has been put on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse has a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon, and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and no word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eightieth days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and fifty-fifth time, and it went while four pieces of paper were coming back up that lane in the hands of four different people.

The man who keeps the leaf was at the flat of that bench stone at about eleven and he took the four and laid them out in the order they had arrived, and he did not open any of them until all four were on the stone.

He said out loud, to about six people who were standing there, that he was going to read all four before he answered any of them, and that a man who answers the first one he gets has told the other three what an answer looks like.

---

Two of the four had written on the second sheet.

One of those two said, in about sixty words of her own, that her community would put one person on that water and that the person would be chosen by her own people and not by this yard, and that the person would be told before they agreed that they were going to be there for about nine minutes and not for a day, and that if they said no on the day this yard would use another person or use four instead of five.

The other of those two said, in about forty-four words of his own, that his community would put one person on that water and that his route had a line on the wall that says what it will not carry, and that a man on that bottom for nine minutes is not a thing that line has anything to say about, and that he was sending the answer anyway because not sending it would have been a way of saying no without having been asked a question.

That last clause is the reason it went on the page of that week and the reason it was read out twice.

---

The other two of the four had no paper.

One of them was a man who said that his community would put a person on that water and that he was saying it out loud because he had not got paper, and that he was not going to say anything else and that this yard was not to come back to him for the rest of it.

The other of them was a woman of about fifty who said that her community would talk about it and that she could not say anything today, and that a woman saying that is not a woman saying no and is not a woman saying yes and that this yard should write down that she said neither.

The man who keeps the leaf wrote that down in those words with the hour against it and wrote the four-and-a-half against no figure at all.

---

Nobody in that six argued with any of the four and nobody thanked any of the four.

At about half past twelve the man who keeps the leaf asked the four, one at a time and each one separately, whether their community had been asked or whether their community had asked itself, and three of the four said they had been asked and one said that his community had asked itself before the paper arrived and that he was carrying an answer to a question that had already been answered at home.

That answer is the only one of the four that this yard is going to have trouble with and it is on the page of that week in his own clause and not in the keeper's.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Thursday line on that sheet and it is the longest line on it, being five sentences, and it says that four of the nine answers came back by about eleven, and that two of them came on a second sheet in their own hands, and that every clause of all four was written down with the hour against it, and that three of the four had been asked and one had asked itself, and that this yard put no figure against any part of it and added nothing of its own to any of it.

He wrote on the face of the sheet that a person chosen by a community is not a person hired by this yard, and that this yard does not choose, and that if a community sends nobody by Wednesday then this yard will do it with four and will not go and ask a fifth.

Nobody thanked him for writing the longest line on that sheet.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and fifty-three words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood at that stone at about eleven in the morning.

"**Four of those nine came back this morning and two of them wrote on a second sheet in their own hands, and I have written down every clause of all four and I have not added one word of mine to any of them, and I am going to say the whole of that first and then I will say the money, because this morning is the biggest thing that has come into this shed since the fourth of May.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and fifty-fifth time and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, so that under that slate there is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. This yard has now gone ninety-five days without opening a tin.**

**And the one of the four I want read out twice. One of them said that his community had asked itself before his paper got here, and that he was carrying an answer to a question that had already been answered at his own end. I have written that down exactly as he said it. It is the best answer that has come into this shed in a month and it is also the one that costs this yard the most, because it means that on that coast somebody else is doing the asking and this yard is a fortnight behind them with a pad of paper.**

**Now what I am not going to do about it. I am not going to treat it as a fault and I am not going to treat it as a win. It is a fact about four days and about the speed of a road. And I am not going to go up that road with a second paper, and I am not going to write to them again this week at all.**

**And the other thing on that stone this morning which nobody has put anywhere. One of the two who wrote said that her community would put one person there, and that the person would be chosen by her own people and not by this yard, and that the person would be told before they agreed that they were going for about nine minutes and not for a day. That is in her words and not mine and I have copied it exactly. That is the whole of what consent looks like on this coast and it took her about sixty words and it did not cost this yard a penny and it is the only thing this month I would put on a wall.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four pieces of paper coming up a lane, and that shed stood empty by about six, and that page of that week had two lines on it and one of them was five sentences long, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT CISTERN AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, IT BEING THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH CHARGE, AND THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. FOUR OF THE NINE ANSWERS CAME BACK BY ABOUT ELEVEN AND FOUR OF THOSE FOUR SAID THAT THEIR COMMUNITY WOULD PUT A PERSON ON THAT WATER, AND TWO OF THOSE FOUR SAID IT IN THEIR OWN WORDS ON A SECOND SHEET OF PAPER IN THEIR OWN HANDS, AND EVERY CLAUSE OF ALL FOUR WAS WRITTEN DOWN WITH THE HOUR AGAINST IT AND WITH NO FIGURE AGAINST ANY PART OF IT. THREE OF THE FOUR HAD BEEN ASKED AND ONE HAD ASKED ITSELF, AND THAT LAST ANSWER IS THE ONLY ONE THIS YARD WILL HAVE TROUBLE WITH. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THEM IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE LINE AND NO SECOND IS ON IT. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP THIS WEEK AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason all four were laid out unopened is that this yard has sent nine pieces of paper into nine markets and a market talks, and that if this yard had read the first one where it stood the other three would have been told what shape an answer takes before they had made one, and that is the way a route turns into a schedule without anybody deciding to do it; and that at about eleven in the morning the reason the second sheet is called a second sheet and not a reply is that a reply is an answer to somebody and a second sheet is a thing a person put their name under by hand and it belongs to them; and that at about half past twelve the reason four people were asked one at a time whether they had been asked rather than whether they had agreed is that the answer this yard needs for Wednesday is not how many communities said yes and it is how many of them had the question before they had the answer, and that a community which asked itself has done this harbor's work better than this harbor could have done it and the only honest thing this yard can do about that is write it down; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason a sentence about not choosing was written on the face of that sheet is that in thirteen volumes the word chosen has been the beginning of about four bad afternoons on this coast, and that if this yard chooses one of five people to hold a bottom for nine minutes then this yard has appointed somebody and there is no office in this harbor that would have the standing to be appointed to; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip gives a whole paragraph to the money before the second half of it is that four of nine coming back is the largest number this yard has had from any of the nine this month and that a slip which opens on a number like that is a slip about the number, and the number is the cheapest thing in this shed.*
