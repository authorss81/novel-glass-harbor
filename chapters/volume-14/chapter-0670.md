# Chapter 670

*Saturday the Twenty-First Day of May, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, One Hand Coming Off That Page of Days on the Monday at a Man's Own Asking and Three of Them at About Two in the Afternoon on the Saturday and Three Figures Going Against Three Lines and the Sum Falling From Eighteen to Fifteen With the Day and the Hour Against Both and the Old Figures Left Readable Underneath, and the Three Columns of That Other Page of Days Written Down at About Half Past Three for the First Time Since the First of May and Not Said Out Loud, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Three and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Saturday the twenty-first day of May, with that market shut for the morning and open for the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge placed on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. At about two in the afternoon three hands came off that page of days, one from each of three lines, and the fourth came off that page of days on Monday the sixteenth of May at a man's own asking, and three figures went against three lines, and the sum fell from eighteen to fifteen, and the day and the hour are against both figures and the eighteen is still readable underneath the fifteen. At about half past three the three columns of that other page of days were written down at the head of it, being one and four and four, and it is the first of twice that count has been written down since the first of May and it was not said out loud and it is not in this slip. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past, and the wooden tally slate is still lying flat on that bench stone where it was put down on the first of May, and nothing is on it.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and sixty-eight days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and seventy days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and eight days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and seventy-fourth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and ninety-two days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and forty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and sixty-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventy-ninth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and ten days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been nailed on that board this month, and in that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said that any of them has eased. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and sixty-eight days, one more than yesterday, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

No charge was placed on that cistern and its covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and nothing was done to that water by anybody.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the piece of chalk and went down the nine lines of that page of days one at a time, and four of them had had a figure against them over the week and three of them still had it on that Saturday afternoon.

One came off the Morrow Line, and that one came off on Monday at a man's own asking, and this is the second time it comes off that line.

Three came off at about two this afternoon, one from each of three lines, and the three of them came off on the same afternoon without anybody having asked and without anybody having said why.

He wrote the day and the hour against each of them. He did not scratch any of them out. They are all still legible under the chalk, and he wrote on the face of the page that they are, so that in twenty years somebody can rub a thumb across those four lines and get four names back if they want them.

---

The sum of that page of days is fifteen.

It was nineteen on Wednesday the eleventh of May and it was eighteen on Monday the sixteenth and it is fifteen now, and all three figures are readable on that page and the day and the hour are against each of them, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put all three on one sheet and get the same three numbers out of it in twenty years.

Nobody said fifteen out loud in that shed. It is not in this slip. It is not in the first column in words.

---

At about half past three the man who keeps the leaf took the other page of days off its nail and wrote three figures at the head of its three columns, being one and four and four.

One held, four restricted, four dormant, over nine names, and the held one is the Saltmarch Weatherway alone, and the four that are restricted are the Tidewatch Channel, the Ashtide Run, the Ternwatch Passage and the Lowfen Reed, and the four that are dormant are the Morrow Line, the Cinder Bell, the Pale Shoals and the Orison Descent.

One and four and four is nine.

He put the day and the hour against those three figures. He did not say them out loud and nobody in that shed asked him for them, and they are not in this slip and they are not in the first column in words.

---

At about four in the afternoon a woman of about thirty of no office asked whether the line that came down off that wall on Friday was going back up, and he said that it was not, and that the question was hers and not his page's, and that nobody in this harbor can put a thing on that wall and nobody in this harbor can be asked to put one, and that the eleven inches of empty plaster are still empty this afternoon.

Nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody argued with him about it.

---

And at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and sixty-seven words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine were still looking at the page of days instead of at him.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. No charge on that cistern and the covers lay on it from nine until four.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now eighty-three days without one.**

**Four hands came off that page of days this week, one of them on the Monday at a man's own asking and three of them on this afternoon, and none of them was scratched out. I am not going to say that figure out loud and it is not on this slip and it is not in this column in words, and I wrote the reason for that on the eleventh of May and the reason has not changed and I am not going to write it twice.**

**What I am going to say is the shape. Ten days ago there were nineteen names against nine limits on that page. Tonight there are fewer. That is a fall and it is the first fall on that page since it was started, and this yard has spent nine days building a thing and has now watched about a third of it come apart, and there are exactly two facts about it. The first is that the wall in that shed has two lines on it and about eleven inches of empty plaster and that is the whole of what this coast is offering anybody who wants to know what it will not carry. The second is that every name that came off that page this week came off because somebody with a right to take it off said so.**

**And here is the last of it and then I am going to stop for the day. Three figures went on that other page at about half past three and they are not in this slip and I am not going to read them back to you standing here, because the whole reason that page can be trusted is that nobody in this harbor has ever said those three numbers out loud. Nothing has moved in that page in twenty days and nothing will move in it tomorrow either, and the line that came off that wall on Friday is not in that page and never was and does not go in it, because a page of what a coast will not carry is not the same kind of paper as a page of who has signed what. I have watched four people this week want to put a line coming down a wall on the other page, and it would be true on it, and it would change what the other page is for.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four hands, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of days had a figure at the head of nine lines and three figures underneath the old ones with the day and the hour against every one of them, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it and nobody picking it up.

---

**THREE HANDS CAME OFF THAT PAGE OF DAYS AT ABOUT TWO THIS AFTERNOON AND THE FOURTH CAME OFF IT ON MONDAY THE SIXTEENTH OF MAY, BEING ONE FROM EACH OF FOUR OF THE NINE LINES, ONE OF THEM FROM THE MORROW LINE ON A MAN'S OWN ASKING ON THE MONDAY AND THREE OF THEM AT ABOUT TWO THIS AFTERNOON WITHOUT ANYBODY HAVING ASKED AND WITHOUT ANYBODY HAVING SAID WHY, AND NONE OF THE FOUR WAS SCRATCHED OUT AND ALL FOUR ARE STILL LEGIBLE UNDER THE CHALK, AND THE DAY AND THE HOUR ARE AGAINST EACH OF THEM. ALL THREE FIGURES OF THAT SUM ARE READABLE ON THAT PAGE AND A STRANGER WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL CAN PUT ALL THREE ON ONE SHEET AND GET THE SAME THREE NUMBERS OUT OF IT IN TWENTY YEARS, AND THE THIRD OF THEM WAS WRITTEN AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE TODAY, BEING ONE AND FOUR AND FOUR OVER NINE NAMES, THE HELD ONE BEING THE SALTMARCH WEATHERWAY ALONE, AND IT WAS NOT SAID OUT LOUD AND IT IS NOT IN THIS SLIP AND IT IS NOT IN THIS COLUMN IN WORDS. THE ELEVEN INCHES OF EMPTY PLASTER ON THAT WALL ARE STILL EMPTY THIS AFTERNOON. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about two in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the piece of chalk and went down the nine lines of that page of days one at a time, and that four of them had had a figure against them that had to come off over the week and that three of them still had it on the Saturday afternoon, and that one of the four came off the Morrow Line on Monday at a man's own asking and that this is the second time it came off that line; and that the other three came off at about two that afternoon, one from each of three lines, on the same afternoon, without anybody having asked and without anybody having said why; and that he wrote the day and the hour against each of them, and that he did not scratch any of them out, and that all four are still legible under the chalk, and that he wrote on the face of that page that they are so that in twenty years somebody can rub a thumb across those four lines and get four names back if they want them; and that the sum of that page of days is fifteen, and that it was nineteen on Wednesday the eleventh of May and eighteen on Monday the sixteenth, and that all three figures are readable on that page and the day and the hour are against each of them, and that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put all three on one sheet and get the same three numbers out of it in twenty years; and that nobody said that sum out loud in that shed and that it is not in this slip and not in this column in words; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf took the other page of days off its nail and wrote three figures at the head of its three columns, being one and four and four over nine names, and that the held one is the Saltmarch Weatherway alone, and that the four that are restricted are the Tidewatch Channel, the Ashtide Run, the Ternwatch Passage and the Lowfen Reed, and that the four that are dormant are the Morrow Line, the Cinder Bell, the Pale Shoals and the Orison Descent, and that one and four and four is nine, and that he put the day and the hour against those three figures and did not say them out loud and nobody asked him for them and they are not in this slip and not in this column in words; and that at about four in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office asked whether the line that came down off that wall on the Friday was going back up, and that she was told it was not, and that the question was hers and not that page's, and that nobody in this harbor can put a thing on that wall and nobody in this harbor can be asked to put one, and that the eleven inches of empty plaster are still empty this afternoon; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and sixty-seven words in about nine people.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason the three hands came off this afternoon and not on a day of this yard's own choosing is that a page which loses names when the keeper decides it is time has stopped being a record and become a ledger with a temper, and that the only arrangement on this coast which does not do that is one where the names come off because somebody with a right to take them off said so; and that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason that count was written down today is that the last time it was written down was the first of May and there have been twenty days in between in which a line went up on a wall and a line came down off one and neither of those two things is in that page, and a page of states that has not been written down since a line came off a wall is a page nobody can check against anything that happened; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I said the question about that wall was hers and not that page's is that a line on that wall is not a state of any route and that putting it on that page would make the page say that the Morrow Line has changed state this week, and it has not changed state, it is dormant and it was dormant, and the difference between a line coming down off a wall and a route changing state is the whole of what this coast has learned in ten days and it would take about four seconds to lose it; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I have not written one figure of that sum in this slip is that the figure on that page is a thing a stranger can add up and a figure said out loud in nine people is a thing nine people own, and that this yard has had this argument with itself for nine days and has settled it once and does not reopen it on a Saturday; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason this slip ends on the shape and not on the number is that the shape is the only part of it that is still true on Monday.*
