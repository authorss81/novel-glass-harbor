# Chapter 664

*Sunday the Fifteenth Day of May, a Wipe and the Hundred and Sixth Time That the Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Has Come Down, Twenty-Three Minutes to Get It Off the Wall and Ten Standing Bare and Twenty-Three to Get It Back, Being Fifty-Six the Whole of It From About Ten Past Ten to About Eleven, and Nobody in That Harbor Having Said Why It Is Getting Slower, and Nothing Else at All on the Water and No Charge on That Cistern, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Ninety-Seven and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Sunday the fifteenth day of May, with that market shut for the morning and open for the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe, and the hundred and sixth time that wall behind a reeve's bench has come down, and no rota day and no wage Tuesday in it, and that butt covered from nine until four with no charge on it. Two men began to take that wall down at about ten past ten and it was bare by about half past ten and it was back on its holders at about eleven, at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the thing above the frame is still not whole. Twenty-three minutes off the wall, ten minutes standing bare, twenty-three minutes to get it back, which is fifty-six minutes the whole of it, and that is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the Sunday before, and nobody in this harbor has said why it is getting slower. At about two in the afternoon four men this yard does not know carried eleven buckets of water up the steps at the back of that market on their own account and nobody paid them and nobody asked them what it was for, and that is not a charge on that cistern and it is not a rota day and it goes in that page as four men and eleven buckets and nothing else. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and a berth fund pays a day for a day and does not care what a wall behind a reeve's bench is doing.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and sixty-two days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty-four days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and two days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and sixty-eighth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and eighty-six days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and forty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and fifty-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventy-third day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and four days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head; it is one hundred and sixty-two days, no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Two men began to take that wall down off its holders at about ten past ten and it was bare by about half past ten, for the hundred and sixth time, and about four people near enough to see it happen saw it happen and did not stop what they were doing.

It went back on at about eleven, and it went on at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January, and the gap over the frame is the same gap it has left there every Sunday since the frost came off that wall in the autumn.

Twenty-three minutes to get it off the wall, ten standing with it bare, and twenty-three to get it back, being fifty-six minutes the whole of it from about ten past ten to about eleven, and that is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the Sunday before, and neither of the two men who do that work has said one word about it and this yard has stopped asking them.

---

There was nothing else on the water.

That is worth saying on a Sunday, because on six of the last eight Sundays there has been something on the water, and this week there was a fouled shackle on Saturday and a buoy hung on the Sunday before, and this Sunday there was a man of about sixty-one sitting on the steps at the back of that market with his hands between his knees from about two until about three, and that is all.

---

At about half past two a woman of about thirty of no office came down to that bench stone and put the sheet of four names face down on it and said one thing out loud, in about nine people, in about fifty-one words, and what she said was that she was not going to read them out again in that market, and that a thing read out once in a market of sixty people is a thing sixty people have, and that she is not going to be the reason sixty people have four names.

Nobody argued with her and nobody agreed with her and nobody thanked her. A man of about thirty-two of that harbor said that was the right decision and that it was a decision about a method and not about the four people, and she said that was the distinction and that she was glad he had it.

---

At about half past three a man of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane came down to that yard to ask whether the sheet of four names was still face down on that bench stone, and it was, and she said that it would stay face down until somebody who was owed an answer got one, and that a face-down sheet on a bench stone is a thing anybody standing there can see and that is the whole of what it is for.

Nobody thanked her for that and nobody argued with her about it, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote down that she had asked and that the answer had been no and that both were on the slip.

---

At about four in the afternoon a man of about forty-four who keeps the crossings for that crew of the third lay came past that shed and stopped, and nobody stopped him, and he said that he had been asked nothing this week and had noticed that he had been asked nothing, and that noticing it was the whole of what he had come to say.

Nobody asked him anything then either. He went up the lane.

---

And at about half past five on that Sunday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about three hundred and eighty-five words, to about nine people.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That butt was covered from nine until four with no charge on it.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Fifty-six minutes the whole of it this morning. Twenty-three off the wall, ten standing bare, twenty-three back, from about ten past ten to about eleven, and that is the hundred and sixth time and I am going to say the minutes and not the number, the same as last Sunday and the Sunday before. Nobody has said why it is getting slower. I have not asked either man who does it and I am not going to.**

**Four men carried eleven buckets up the steps at the back of that market this afternoon and none of them was paid and none of them was asked what the water was for, and that is not a charge on that cistern and it is a rota day and I have written it down as four men and eleven buckets and nothing else, and I want it on this page that I have not made a rota day out of it.**

**And a woman of thirty of no office has decided not to read four names out in that market again, and she is right, and I am going to write down the thing I have noticed about that decision, which is that she made it in about a minute and it cost her something to make it and nobody in that shed was asked for anything and I was not asked for anything either.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for fifty-six minutes, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that wall stood at the angle it has carried since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole.

---

**THAT WALL BEHIND A REEVE'S BENCH CAME DOWN BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN ON THAT SUNDAY AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT ELEVEN AT THE ANGLE IT HAS BEEN AT SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, AND IT TOOK TWENTY-THREE MINUTES OFF THE WALL, TEN STANDING BARE AND TWENTY-THREE TO GET IT BACK, BEING FIFTY-SIX MINUTES THE WHOLE OF IT, AGAINST TWENTY-TWO AND NINE AND TWENTY-TWO ON THE SUNDAY BEFORE, AND NOBODY HAS SAID WHY. FOUR MEN THIS YARD DOES NOT KNOW CARRIED ELEVEN BUCKETS OF WATER UP THE STEPS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON ON THEIR OWN ACCOUNT AND NONE OF THEM WAS PAID AND NONE OF THEM WAS ASKED WHAT IT WAS FOR, AND THAT IS NOT A CHARGE ON THAT CISTERN AND IT IS NOT A ROTA DAY. A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO THAT SHE WAS NOT GOING TO READ FOUR NAMES OUT IN THAT MARKET AGAIN, BECAUSE A THING READ OUT ONCE IN A MARKET OF SIXTY PEOPLE IS A THING SIXTY PEOPLE HAVE. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS THE CROSSINGS CAME PAST THAT SHED AND SAID THAT HE HAD BEEN ASKED NOTHING THIS WEEK AND HAD NOTICED THAT HE HAD BEEN ASKED NOTHING, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM ANYTHING THEN EITHER. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten in the morning the reason the eleven buckets of water carried up the steps at the back of that market are written down as four men and eleven buckets and not as a charge is that this harbor has a rota on that cistern on a Monday and a Thursday and that has been walked on since the autumn, and that a page which lets any bucket become a charge on any day is a page that has lost its own rule, and the rule is the only thing anybody can check; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing against her decision except that it cost her something is that a page that records the reason a person gave up a thing is halfway to a page that records which persons gave up which things, and that page has been refused on this coast six times and it is not going to be different because it starts small; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason the man of about sixty-one is written down as sitting on the steps with his hands between his knees and not as anything else is that this is the only description of him that any of us have got, and that the fact that a page cannot do better than that about a person after four days of work is the honest state of this yard and it should stay visible on the page; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the man of about forty-four is entered as having said that he noticed he had been asked nothing is that he said it, and that a man noticing a silence is a thing that happens and it is not a thing anybody should have to point out twice before somebody else does, and that this yard has been silent about that silence for a fortnight and it is written down today because he came and said it himself and not because I noticed it; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason nothing in this slip is about the four names is that the woman who owns those four names on that market's floor has just decided that four names are not a thing to be worked on in public, and that this yard is going to obey a decision of hers about her own page, and that if I write about them again today then the four names have got a keeper and that is the whole trouble.*
