# Chapter 680

*Tuesday the Thirty-First Day of May, the Eighty-Fourth Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Read Out at About Ten in the Morning in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved for the Eighty-Fourth Time, the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four, and the Eighty-Fourth Week Standing Fallen Due at the Close of That Day Behind the Eighty-Third, and at About Three in the Afternoon in Front of Four People Who Came on Their Own Account the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Taking Down the Second Sheet of Days and Writing One Figure Against One of the Nine Names and Nothing Against the Other Eight and One Underneath Them, With the Day and the Hour Against It and the Figure Not Said Out Loud in That Shed and Not in Any Slip, and the Man of About Thirty-Two of That Harbor and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Still Married at the Close of That Day With Neither Name on Any Page and the Form in the Inside of a Coat and on No Sheet, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirteen and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Tuesday the thirty-first day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-fourth wage Tuesday, and not a rota day and not a wipe, and that butt behind the market covered from eight until four with no charge on it. The column on the lid of that step stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out at about ten and it took him four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the eighty-third, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. At about three in the afternoon, in front of four people who came on their own account, the man who keeps the leaf took down the second sheet of days and wrote one figure against one of the nine names and nothing against the other eight, and one underneath them, with the day and the hour against it, and the figure was not said out loud in that shed and is not in this slip. The man of about thirty-two of that harbor and the woman of thirty of no office are still married at the close of this day, neither name is on any page of this harbor, and the form is in the inside of a coat and is on no sheet. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in that harbor are nine lines on a wall or a figure on a page, and this movement closes on one of them and not on the other eight. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and seventy-eighth days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and eightieth days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand seven hundred and eighteenth days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and eighty-fourth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been three hundred and second days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and fifty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and seventy-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and eighty-ninth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and twentieth days. The third column of that leaf is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. The fourth column of that leaf stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. That board at the head of that mole is carrying five papers, and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor, and nothing has been put on that board this month. In that boathouse a basket stands with a paper in it and nothing else in it, and nobody has put a hand in to look. About two sea miles off the head of that mole lie nine hulls this afternoon, and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and nobody in this harbor has said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and seventy-eighth days, one more than the day before, and no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes and it did not move, and behind that door the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the eighty-third, and there is nobody in that market who has ever asked what those envelopes are for.

Nobody in that market has asked this week either, and this yard has not asked, and the eighty-fifth is next Tuesday and this yard does not know what is on it and is not going to find out before it gets there.

---

At about three in the afternoon four people were standing at that bench stone, and they had come on their own account and nobody had sent for them, and the man who keeps the leaf took the second sheet of days off its nail and put it on the flat of that stone with the nine route names down the side of it in the order they are always read in.

He wrote one figure against one of the nine names and nothing at all against the other eight, and then he wrote the sum of the nine underneath them, and then he put the day and the hour against it.

The figure is one and the sum is one, and one and nothing and nothing and nothing and nothing and nothing and nothing and nothing and nothing is one, and a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can put all nine on one sheet and get the same number out of it in twenty years.

He did not say the figure out loud. Nobody in that shed said it. It is not in this slip.

---

He said four things to those four people and none of the four of them was the figure.

He said that it is a count of nights and not a count of persons, and not a count of boats, and not a count of favours, and that the four people who were on the water last Wednesday are four persons and that the number on that sheet is not four and will not ever be four.

He said that a night carried by a route is not a night that route owns, and that no route owns a night because it was kept busy, and that the name that has a figure against it this afternoon is the name of the community whose people were on the water on somebody else's line, and that it has that figure against it because they did the work and not because the Bell has gained a line or a crew or a page.

He said that the same night is entered on the page of that week, against the Wednesday line, where one route's own third lay asked and another community said yes, and that a stranger can get from one of those two sheets to the other in about four seconds and back again, and that it is entered on both sides on purpose and that it is one night and not two.

He said that the page of hands has not been touched this week and has not been added up, and that four people on the water for another route are not a name against a limit and do not go on that page, and that it will not be added up in this shed this month.

---

A woman of about forty-four with a chandler's counter four hundred yards up that lane took that sheet and a pencil and added the nine figures herself in about a minute, got the same figure, wrote it in the margin of her own copy of a price list, gave the sheet back and went to work.

She was not asked what she had got and she was not asked twice, and about four people in that shed saw her do it, and she did not say the number out loud to anybody.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Tuesday line on the page of that week and it is the shortest of the seven, and it says that the second sheet of days was added up at about three this afternoon in front of four people and that this yard said the number out loud to nobody.

He wrote under it, with the hour against it, that the man of about thirty-two of that harbor and the woman of thirty of no office are still married, and that neither of their names is on any page of this harbor, and that the form is in the inside of a coat and is on no sheet, and that this yard has not put it on one and is not going to.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and seventy words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been standing at that stone at about three.

"**Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks on that lid for the eighty-fourth time, read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who is not on the water and has not been on the water, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut behind that door from ten until four, and the eighty-fourth week stands fallen due at the close of today, and not one envelope in that chest has been opened on any of them. I am not going to say the number of that week this year.**

**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. One line on that sheet since the twentieth of April. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. That butt behind that market was covered from eight until four, because a rota day is a Monday and a Thursday and today is neither.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now ninety-three days without one.**

**Ten days. I am going to say what those ten days were, because it is half past five on the last day of them and nobody in this shed is going to read them back.**

**A man of a route that runs inside written limits was one man short on a night and would not take anybody off another boat to make the number come out, and he said so at a stall and not to this yard. He went and asked a route that runs nothing. That route said yes, in a market, in nine people, with nothing behind it, and said at the same time that neither of them owns the other. Four people were on the water for about seven hours and came in at about two and went up the lane. This yard sent nobody, hired nobody and paid nobody, and there is no figure of that night in this yard's book and there never will be. And this morning, when one of them was asked whether it would do it again next week, it said it would not say until it is asked, and nobody in that shed said yes and nobody said no.**

**A man of about fifty-three brought eleven words he wrote a long time ago and put them on a page beside that night, and nobody answered them, and I did not tell him he was wrong, and no figure is set against them. Two people who are married stood in this shed and said out loud one thing each that they have been saying at each other since the fourth of May, and four other people wrote one line under them, and it is still not settled and no page of this harbor says it is. A wall behind a reeve's bench cost a minute more this month than it did last month and nobody has said why. And a count got taken at about three this afternoon off a page, in front of four people who came on their own account, and I am not going to say it out loud, and the reason I am not going to say it out loud is that the second time a figure is said in a shed it stops being a thing anybody can check and becomes a thing nine people own.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for ten days, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that second sheet of days had nine names down the side of it and one figure and a sum under them and a day and an hour against them, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**AT ABOUT THREE IN THE AFTERNOON, IN FRONT OF FOUR PEOPLE WHO CAME ON THEIR OWN ACCOUNT, THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF TOOK DOWN THE SECOND SHEET OF DAYS AND WROTE ONE FIGURE AGAINST ONE OF THE NINE NAMES AND NOTHING AT ALL AGAINST THE OTHER EIGHT, AND WROTE THE SUM OF THE NINE UNDERNEATH THEM, AND PUT THE DAY AND THE HOUR AGAINST IT, BEING ONE, AND ONE PLUS NOTHING AND NOTHING AND NOTHING AND NOTHING AND NOTHING AND NOTHING AND NOTHING AND NOTHING IS ONE, AND A STRANGER WITH A LEAF AND A PENCIL CAN PUT ALL NINE ON ONE SHEET AND GET THE SAME NUMBER OUT OF IT IN TWENTY YEARS. THE FIGURE WAS NOT SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT SHED AND IS NOT IN THIS SLIP. HE SAID IN THAT SHED THAT IT IS A COUNT OF NIGHTS AND NOT A COUNT OF PERSONS AND NOT A COUNT OF BOATS AND NOT A COUNT OF FAVOURS, AND THAT A NIGHT CARRIED BY A ROUTE IS NOT A NIGHT THAT ROUTE OWNS AND NO ROUTE OWNS A NIGHT BECAUSE IT WAS KEPT BUSY, AND THAT THE SAME NIGHT IS ENTERED ON THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK AGAINST THE WEDNESDAY LINE, SO THAT A STRANGER CAN GET FROM ONE SHEET TO THE OTHER AND BACK. THE PAGE OF HANDS HAS NOT BEEN TOUCHED THIS WEEK AND HAS NOT BEEN ADDED UP. THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP STOOD AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE EIGHTY-FOURTH WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF THAT DAY. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the eighty-fifth is named as being next Tuesday and as being a thing this yard does not know about is that a page which says what is coming is a page which has begun arranging itself, and that this yard has spent ten days refusing to write down anything that has not happened yet and is not going to start on the last afternoon of a week; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason four people were let into the taking of that count is that this yard told them on Monday they could come and watch, and that telling a man he may watch a thing is not the same as asking him to be there, and that four people who take themselves to a bench stone at three o'clock on a Tuesday are four people a stranger can ask in twenty years what they saw; and that at about three in the afternoon the reason the four things said in that shed were said and the figure was not is that the four things are the definitions of what the number is and they will still be true when the number is old, and that a definition said out loud on the day a figure is taken is the only thing that stops a figure being re-used for something it was not taken for, and this coast has had a figure re-used once already this year and cost a route; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the fact that those two are still married is written on that sheet and on this column and is not said in this shed is that it is a fact about two people and not about a piece of paper, and that a page of that week is read out in nine people every evening, and that if this yard read out a fact about two people's marriage to nine people on a Tuesday it would be doing the thing that the four lines under those two lines on Saturday were refusing to do, which is to put two people into a document; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on a count not being said out loud is that this yard's whole method this month has been to put things on a page that a stranger can take off it, and that the second time a figure is said with a mouth it belongs to the nine people who heard it and not to the page, and that is how a number stops being checkable without anybody noticing the day it happened.*
