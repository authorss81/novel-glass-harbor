# Chapter 690

*Friday the Tenth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Man Who Keeps the Leaf Going Up That Coast Road at About Eight in the Morning and Asking the Ninth Community the One Thing Face to Face and Getting Its Answer, and at About Three in the Afternoon Taking Down the Page of Days and Writing the Column Headed Not Asked Whole Against All Ten Days From Nine to Nothing With the Day and the Hour Against It and the Figure Not Said Out Loud in That Shed and Not in Any Slip, the Third Night of Not Sleeping Begun at About Two That Morning and the Aftertaste and the Knee and the Hearing Standing With Hours Against Them and No Figure Against Any of Them, Nothing Else Going Onto the Money Sheet and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Twenty-Three and the Second at One Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Friday the tenth day of June, with that market trading all day and about thirty people on the boards at the back of it by eleven in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern had no charge put on it and its covers stayed lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market sat covered from eight until four with no charge on it either. At about eight in the morning the man who keeps the leaf walked up that coast road on his own legs and put the one thing to the ninth community face to face, and at about three in the afternoon he lifted the page of days off its nail and set the column headed not asked down whole against all ten days, from nine to nothing, with the day and the hour against it. The third night of not sleeping started at about two this morning. No line went onto the money sheet and none came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence reached that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight left it at about four in the afternoon, and what lies under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been lifted since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine figures that stand on that coast all ran a day longer today and this yard carried forward not one of them. The notice of the seventeenth of May past has come to its three hundred and eighty-eighth day. Three hundred and ninetieth days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and twenty-eighth day. The bench under that pump has lain bare for the four hundred and ninety-fourth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twelfth days with no figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and sixty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street has reached its two hundred and eighty-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has reached its two hundred and ninety-ninth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and thirtieth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. Nobody has taken the third column of that leaf and nobody has been put into it, and a boy's hand of twenty-two stands at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. One hundred and twenty-five is what the fourth column of that leaf stands at, and it has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened and nothing went up on that board this month. A basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has reached in a hand. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head this afternoon and nine were there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has said no word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and eighty-eighth days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water, and what was done off that mole on Wednesday never touched that hearing and Wednesday bettered it by not a day.**

---

At about eight in the morning the man who keeps the leaf went up that coast road with nothing in his hands but a leaf folded in four, and he did not take the cart because the cart runs on a Tuesday and a Friday for goods and not for a question.

He came to the place where the ninth community keeps its own page, and a man of about forty-six of that community was mending a line on a bench outside, and the keeper asked him the one thing face to face, being whether the morning's choice stands as that community's answer.

The man of about forty-six said that it does, and that the person who went down on Wednesday morning went under that community's own order and came up under it, and that the community had chosen between its two in the hour before and had not written either name down anywhere but on its own page.

The keeper wrote that down with the hour against it and with no name against it, and he did not ask him to say it twice because it had been said once and said plainly, and he came back down that road before nine.

---

That is the ninth asking in ten days and there is not going to be a tenth, because there are nine communities on that page and every one of them has now been asked the one thing face to face.

Nine papers went up that road on the first day of the ten and not one of those nine is counted as an asking on the page that came down this afternoon, because a paper carries words and an asking is a day's work and the two were named separately before any of it started.

On the second day the keeper asked the first, and on each day after it he asked one more in turn, and on the day of the water he asked one at the mole head itself in the afternoon when the boat was in, and yesterday he asked nobody because the line was going onto the sheet and the shed was full of it.

Nobody in any of the nine said no, and nobody in any of the nine was thanked, and the community that offered two is the community that was asked last because it had said it would choose on the morning and the morning had to come first.

---

At about half past ten the man of about thirty-two of that harbor came down that lane on foot and he did not come into the shed but stood at the end of it where the morning comes in, and he had not slept for the third night running.

He had been up since about two in the morning, which is the hour the keeper wrote against the third night, and he said so himself when the man who keeps the leaf asked him whether he had been out, and he said he had walked to the mole head and back and had not met anybody.

He went up the steps of that shed slowly and by the rail and not by the middle, because of the knee in his left knee that came up the ladder with him on Wednesday morning, and the keeper wrote the hour against that and wrote no figure against it.

He drank a cup of water standing up and he set the cup down and he did not finish it, because of the aftertaste that has been on him since Wednesday morning on top of the one he had already, and the keeper wrote that hour down too and wrote no figure against that either.

---

At about eleven the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender came down that lane with her independent line coiled over one shoulder, and she hung it on its own nail in that shed and she did not hand it to anybody.

She said in front of about six people that the wire was still in her coat pocket and that the coil wanted nothing doing to it, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote down that she had said it and wrote no figure of money against her and no figure against the coil.

At about ten past eleven the man of about thirty-two asked her to say the hour out loud, and she said it out loud once, and he nodded and said that he had got it this time, and she said it once more a little louder and he said that he had got that one better.

The keeper wrote both hours down with the hour against them and wrote no figure against either of them, because the hearing in that man since ten past six on Wednesday morning is a different hearing from the man of about twenty-nine's and the two are never on one line.

---

At about twelve the boat of about thirty tons went back off that hire, and it went on that route's own order and under that route's own people, and this yard did not send anybody down to see it go.

The hire had been for two days at the figure in chalk and the two days were Wednesday and Thursday, and the figure stands on that route's own page and not on this yard's money sheet, and no penny of it has come through this yard's hands.

Nobody in that yard said where any boat was going, because this party is not the body that decides where any boat goes, and the keeper wrote that the boat had gone with the hour against it and wrote nothing else.

---

At about three in the afternoon three people were standing at that bench stone, and they had come on their own account and nobody had sent for them, and the man who keeps the leaf took the page of days off its nail and put it on the flat of that stone.

The page has the nine route names down the side of it in the order they are always read in, and beside them one column headed not asked, and against every one of the thirty days on disk that column stands at nine.

Against the ten days of this movement the keeper wrote ten figures, being eight against the first and seven against the second and six and five and four and three and two against the six days after that, and two again against the day of the water because nobody was asked anything that day, and one against yesterday because one was still to ask, and nothing against today.

He put the day and the hour against the whole of it, and he did not say any of the ten figures out loud, and nobody in that shed said any of them, and not one of them is in this slip.

---

He said three things to those three people and none of the three was a figure.

He said that it is a count of communities that have not been asked, and that a community in it is not a community that said no, and that not one of the nine said no on any of the ten days and that is on the face of the page.

He said that the nine papers of the first day are not in it, and that a paper which carries words is not a day's work, and that the falling of it from nine to nothing is the asking and nothing else, and that no part of it was said in a room.

He said that the page of hands stands at fifteen and was not touched, and that the nights carried are not added up again, and that the three columns of the page of routes are not counted on any day of these ten, and that this is the only count this movement owns and it is now taken.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Friday line on the page of that week, and it is the last line on that sheet, and it says that the ninth community was asked face to face in the morning and answered, and that the column headed not asked was written down whole at about three with the day and the hour against it.

He wrote that the man of about thirty-two has not slept for three nights and that the hours of the three are against them, and that the aftertaste and the knee and the hearing are against their hours, and that no figure of money stands against any of the four.

At about half past four the woman of thirty of no office came into that shed with a slip of her own in her hand, and she asked the man who keeps the leaf at what hour the page of that week comes off its nail, and he told her, and she wrote it on her slip and put it in her own pocket.

She did not show him what was on the slip and he did not ask, and the man of about thirty-two was at the bench stone at the time and was copying nothing, and the keeper wrote the hour of the question down and wrote down not one word about what was on the slip.

---

And at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and eleven words, to about nine people, and about three of those nine had been standing at that stone at about three in the afternoon.

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand five hundred and seventy-two pence out, being seven hundred and three marks, seven shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and one thousand nine hundred and thirty-two pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and one shilling. One line went onto it yesterday and no other line goes onto it in fifty days. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and three days without one.**

**Ten days of asking are behind this yard and every one of the nine was asked one thing, and a column got written down whole this afternoon off a page, in front of three people who came on their own account, and I am not going to say it out loud, and the reason I am not going to say it out loud is the reason I gave on the last day of last month, being that the second time a figure is said in a shed it stops being a thing anybody can check and becomes a thing nine people own.**

**And the cost, and there is no figure of money against one word of it. The aftertaste is on him still and it is on top of the one he had already and the second has not lifted the first. The knee is in his left knee still and he goes up steps by the rail. From about two on Wednesday morning and from about two on Thursday morning and from about two again this morning he has not been sleeping, and those are three nights and I have the hours of all three and I am putting no figure against any of them. He cannot hear out of the left side since ten past six on Wednesday morning and that is a different hearing from the man of about twenty-nine's in every particular, and the man of about twenty-nine is on his one hundred and eighty-eighth day today and did not improve by one day because of anything that happened on Wednesday.**

**And the last of it. Nobody was asked for anything today except the one community that was asked one thing in the morning, and nobody said no in ten days, and nobody was thanked for ten days, and that page of that week is full, and that wooden tally slate is still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for ten days, and that shed was standing empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and nine lines were on that page of that week, and flat on that bench stone that wooden tally slate was lying still with nothing on it.

THREE NIGHTS WITHOUT SLEEP ARE BEHIND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO OF THAT HARBOR, BEING FROM ABOUT TWO ON WEDNESDAY MORNING AND FROM ABOUT TWO ON THURSDAY MORNING AND FROM ABOUT TWO AGAIN THIS MORNING, AND THE AFTERTASTE IS STILL ON HIM AND THE KNEE IS STILL IN HIS LEFT KNEE AND HE HAS NOT HEARD OUT OF THE LEFT SIDE SINCE TEN PAST SIX ON WEDNESDAY MORNING, AND NO FIGURE OF MONEY STANDS AGAINST ANY OF THE FOUR. ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-TWO PENCE OUT IS WHERE THAT MONEY SHEET STANDS, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND THREE MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO PENCE SHORT IS WHAT IT IS, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND ONE SHILLING, AND TWO LINES ARE ON IT AND NO THIRD. NO GAP WAS READ OUT AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN ROSE OR FELL BY ONE. TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IS WHAT LIES UNDER THAT SLATE.**
