# Chapter 661

*Thursday the Twelfth Day of May, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Forty-Ninth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, a Woman of Thirty of No Office Going Into the Back of the Second Page With a Copying Pen and the Four Names That Stand Against the Morrow Line and Finding That Two of Those Four Are Not on the Morrow Line's Own Page, and Nobody in That Yard Told Anybody Anything, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Untouched at One Hundred and Eleven Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Six Pence Out and Ten Thousand Six Hundred and Forty Pence In and One Hundred and One Thousand Two Hundred and Thirty-Six Pence Short, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Three Hundred and Ninety-Four and the Second at One Thousand Eight Hundred and Forty-Four*

Thursday the twelfth day of May, with about forty people on the boards at the back of that market from ten in the morning, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern at the back of that market charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven, and walked on by none of it, and the hundred and forty-ninth time, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. At about one in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office began copying the names off the head of the nine lines of that page of days into the back of the second page that went up on Tuesday, because a page which answers a requirement has to be able to say whose hands are behind each of the nine lines, and nobody in this yard had ever done that. She got as far as the first line and the second and the third and the fourth, which is the Morrow Line, and she stopped, and she did not stop for long. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and the same figure went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine things in this harbor can be had off a wall by anybody with the patience to want them, and not one of the nine was found by looking for it. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at three hundred and fifty-nine days. A bellwright of this harbor has not been asked for three hundred and sixty-one days, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown stand six hundred and ninety-nine days. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the four hundred and sixty-fifth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have been two hundred and eighty-three days without a figure against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its two hundred and thirty-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and fifty-third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street is on its two hundred and seventieth day. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for three hundred and one days. Five papers are on that board at the head of that mole and two of the five have never been opened by anybody in this harbor. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off the head of that mole this afternoon and they were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and not one word about any of them easing has been said in this harbor. A man of about twenty-nine can hear out of the left side of his head; it is one hundred and fifty-nine days, no figure stands against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven, and four men this yard does not know did the carrying, and every one of the eleven trips went up the same steps on the inside of that wall, and nobody walked on the water afterwards, and the covers lay on it until about four in the afternoon.

Nobody has ever written down a rule about that. It is a thing that has happened since the autumn, and the man who keeps the leaf writes the same sentence about it every time and has stopped expecting it to change.

---

At about one in the afternoon the woman of thirty of no office took the second page down off the boards at the back of that market, which is the page that went up on Tuesday, and she turned it over, and she copied the nine names off the head of the nine lines of that page of days onto the back of it in her own hand.

She did it because nobody else was going to do it and because the whole point of a page that answers a requirement is that it can be checked. A stranger has to be able to take the back of that page and the front of that page and put one against the other and get the same answer twice, and on Tuesday she had written on the face of it that every line of it could be checked by somebody standing there.

Four names stood against the Morrow Line on that page of days, written in chalk at the head of that line with the day and the hour against them, and she had read them before and had not thought about them.

---

She went and got the Morrow Line's own page.

It has hung in this harbor since before the frost, on a nail behind the reeve's bench, and it is the oldest working page in this yard and nobody has moved it. It carries that route's own hand throughout and it has never once been in this yard's own book.

Four names are at the head of it, and they are the same four.

Two of them are the same two.

---

The other two are not on it.

She stood in about nine people at about half past two that afternoon with a copying pen in one hand and the back of a page in the other and she did not say anything out loud, and then she said one thing out loud, and what she said was that somebody had put four names against a route and two of them were not the route's.

That is a difference. It is not a thing anybody has to answer for and it is not an accusation and it is not a discovery of anything except that two names and two names are not the same four names.

Nobody answered her. The man of about thirty-one of the second lay looked at the two pages and then at the floor. A man of about forty of that route's own third lay, who was in that yard on his own account and not on anybody's crew, said that people come and go and that a name on a page is not a person staying in a place, and nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote it in the first column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and he wrote it in the plainest words he had, which were that the page of days carries four names against the Morrow Line and that the Morrow Line's own page carries four names and that two of them are the same and two of them are not.

He did not write the word mismatch on it, because a mismatch is a word for a mistake and nobody in this yard has established that anybody has made one.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and seventeen words, to about nine people, and about four of the nine were looking at the two pages on that bench stone instead of at him.

"**One hundred and one thousand two hundred and thirty-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-two marks, nine shillings and eightpence. That sheet has carried one line since the twentieth of April and it carries one line now. Ten thousand six hundred and forty pence has ever gone into it, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and there is no second line and there is not going to be one. Four hundred and fifty pence is still the figure on that board in the front street and it did not move this morning and nobody in that market has asked for it to.**

**A cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand this morning between about eight and about eleven and walked on by none of it, and that is the hundred and forty-ninth, and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and the wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, so that what is under that slate is six hundred and twelve pence short of it, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and nothing that happened this afternoon came out of it.**

**And here is the thing that happened this afternoon and I am going to say it once and then leave it alone. Four names stand against the Morrow Line on that page of days. Four names stand at the head of the Morrow Line's own page. Two of them are the same two. That is a difference between two pieces of paper and it is not a thing about anybody, and I have written it down and I have not written a word under it.**

**What I have not done is go and find out. Not this afternoon, and not because anybody told me not to. A man who goes and looks for the two names that do not match is a man looking for somebody to be disappointed in, and if he goes looking on my account then whatever he finds will be a thing he found because I sent him, and I would put it on that page as a fact and it would not be one.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four names against four names, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that wall had three lines on it and a page beside them, and the wooden tally slate was still lying flat on the bench stone where it had been put down on the first of May, with nothing on it and nobody picking it up.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND BETWEEN ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ABOUT ELEVEN AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT AND IT IS THE HUNDRED AND FORTY-NINTH CHARGE, THE COVERS LAYING ON IT FROM ABOUT ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON. FOUR NAMES STAND AGAINST THE MORROW LINE ON THAT PAGE OF DAYS AND FOUR NAMES STAND AT THE HEAD OF THE MORROW LINE'S OWN PAGE AND TWO OF THEM ARE THE SAME TWO AND TWO OF THEM ARE NOT, AND THAT WAS FOUND AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE GOING INTO THE BACK OF THE SECOND PAGE WITH A COPYING PEN, AND IT WAS WRITTEN IN THE FIRST COLUMN WITH THE DAY AND THE HOUR AGAINST IT AND NO NAME AGAINST IT, AND IT IS A DIFFERENCE BETWEEN TWO PIECES OF PAPER AND NOT A THING ABOUT ANYBODY. THE MONEY SHEET IS AT ONE HUNDRED AND ELEVEN THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE OUT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE MARKS AND THREE SHILLINGS, AGAINST TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO MARKS, NINE SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT CARRIES ONE LINE AND NO SECOND. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twelfth day of May at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, and that it is the hundred and forty-ninth charge and that the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon; and that at about one in the afternoon a woman of thirty of no office took the second page down off the boards at the back of that market and turned it over and copied the nine names off the head of the nine lines of that page of days onto the back of it in her own hand, because a page which answers a requirement has to be able to say whose hands are behind each of the nine lines and nobody in this yard had ever done that; and that she got as far as the fourth of those nine lines, which is the Morrow Line, and stopped; and that four names stand against the Morrow Line on that page of days, written in chalk at the head of that line with the day and the hour against them; and that the Morrow Line's own page has hung in this harbor on a nail behind the reeve's bench since before the frost and carries four names at the head of it in that route's own hand and has never once been in this yard's own book; and that two of the four names on that page of days are the same two of the four names on that route's own page and that two of them are not; and that she said one thing out loud at about half past two that afternoon in about nine people, being that somebody had put four names against a route and two of them were not the route's, and that nobody answered her, and that a man of about forty of that route's own third lay said that people come and go and that a name on a page is not a person staying in a place, and that nobody agreed with him and nobody argued with him; and that at about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote it in this column with the day and the hour against it and no name against it, and that he did not write the word mismatch on it because a mismatch is a word for a mistake and nobody in this yard has established that anybody has made one, and that he did not go and look for the two names that do not match; and that at about half past five the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone in about four hundred and seventeen words in about nine people.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight in the morning the reason the eleven trips are written down as eleven every time and not as a figure is that four men carry that cistern and not one of them has ever been asked what the water is for, and a yard that writes down a total of trips and not a total of men is keeping a count of its own stairs and not of the work; and that at about one in the afternoon the reason that page was taken down and turned over is that a page which answers a requirement is the only thing this coast has made this month that anybody outside this harbor could check, and that a page nobody can check is a page that only the people who wrote it believe, and that this yard had been treating Tuesday's page as a fact about this coast when on Tuesday it was only a claim; and that at about half past two in the afternoon the reason nothing was said about that difference out loud except the one sentence that was said is that a difference between two pieces of paper is the safest thing in this harbor to say and the least useful, and that a yard which says a useful thing about it at half past two on a Thursday is a yard that has decided what it found before it finished looking; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason I did not go and find the two names that do not match is that a man who goes looking on my account finds whatever he was sent to find, and that a page is a page and not a place to put a thing a person came back with; and that I want that written down by me about myself and not discovered by somebody else in nine months, because a keeper of a page who sends a man to look is a keeper of a page who has decided the answer and hired the finding; and that at about five in the afternoon the reason I have written nothing at all under that difference is that the two names that are not the route's are two lines of ink on a piece of paper and that an inference about them is not the same kind of thing as a figure, and that this yard has spent six days putting the two on separate lines and is not going to join them up on a Thursday because it would make a better shape.*
