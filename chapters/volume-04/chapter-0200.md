# Chapter 200

*A Name at the Head of a Form That Is an Office, and a Reason Against the Line*

Friday the fourth and Saturday the fifth of December, on the face of the second seam, at the head of this dock, and in the shed.

**On Friday the fourth of December this yard worked about four hours and a half on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times, and the board was written.**

**Gross on the board fourteen.** The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of fourteen paid at the mole and the yard's being three, and the three went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people and no figure was argued.

The day's book went onto the board in the ship's hand and the holder read it back after her and neither of them said anything about it, and the day went at the speed of a day with a man on the pump and a woman on the line, which is not the speed of a day with three on it and has not been since the twenty-third day of October.

**And at about half past two in the afternoon of that Friday, while the tide was still making, one sheet came up this hill in the ordinary post, and it is half a sheet, and there is no signature on it anywhere, and at the head of it, where a name would be if the thing had one, there is the name of a court in a city, and at the foot there is the name of that court again.**

> *To the holder of a provisional writ of salvage working granted at Morrow Quay on the first of May instant, —*
>
> *This matter stands before this court and is not closed by a finding. A person entered in this matter as an interested party is not examined for the full Second Bell license while the matter stands and is not closed by a finding.*
>
> *The holder of the said writ is entered in this matter as an interested party by the making of a copy of a record, the entry being on the face of that copy. The entry is dated the twenty-fourth day of November instant.*
>
> *The said person was put to the examination once before and did not satisfy it. This court has before it no other matter concerning him and knows nothing whatever of his trade.*
>
> *This is not a suspension, not a penalty, and not a finding against anybody. This court finds against nobody. A person who is not examined is not a person who has been refused.*
>
> *The writ of the first of May instant is not before this court, is not a Second Bell license, does not lead to one and renews nothing. This court has not considered that writ and takes no notice of it.*
>
> *Nothing in this paper touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of that writ. That writ runs to midnight on the first of March and the office which granted it is not the office before this court.*
>
> *A copy of the said record is to be produced to this court on the fourth day of January. A party which does not produce it is not fined, because a court does not fine a poor man, and the matter simply goes on.*
>
> *For the Court of Wreck Claims.*

"**Read it again,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and read the sixth paragraph to me twice, because there are two things in it and I want to know which of the two they put in first.**"

He read it again, and he read the sixth paragraph twice.

"**Then sit down,**" she said, "**because I have got about nine things to say and I am not going to say any of them standing up in a tide.**

"**One. It is not the writ. It says so in writing and it says so twice, and I have been waiting since the twelfth of October for somebody to put that on a piece of paper in the plainest words in the English language and it has taken a form of a half a sheet from a city and it is in front of us.

"**Two. It is not a penalty and it is not a refusal and it says so in the sixth paragraph, and that is a thing I did not know courts said and I am not going to be unkind about it. A man who is not examined is not a man who has been refused. Do you understand what that is? It means that in about four years somebody can read that page and it will not say that you were found wanting. It will say that you were not in the room. That is a different thing and this yard is going to be glad of it in four years and is going to hate it now.

"**Three. It is dated the twenty-fourth. The twenty-fourth is the day the copy came off the counter. It came off the counter because we applied for it on the sixteenth, in my hand and your hand and a penny of postage, and we put a room on a Thursday because we asked a reeve for a room on a Thursday, and that is the whole of the chain and there is not one office in it that did anything we did not ask it to do.

"**Four. He wanted it before the writ.**" She looked at him. "**Say the date.**

"**The September of last year,**" said Calder Marr. "**I was put to it in a room in a city and I did not satisfy it, and the writ of the first of May was granted eight months afterwards, and I have not gone near that examination since, and this yard is not going to stand in a street in January and tell anybody the writ did this.**

"**Then that is the first line in the first column,**" said Nessa Pike, "**in your own hand and not in mine, and I want it before the figures. A yard which has been in a room and come out with a clerk saying that a clerk could see what the man could see is a yard whose figures are worth what a clerk's are, and a yard which has been in a room and come out with a form saying it is not eligible is a yard whose figures are worth what a court's are, and the difference between those two sentences is about two hundred and thirty-seven pence and a quarter of a year out of the left side of a shipwright and a morning of a man of the tide gang, and not one of them is going into that form.**"

**And at about six on the Friday evening, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, the two of them read the three weeks out at the head of this dock, and it took about nine minutes, and nobody was asked to be quiet, and the yard's own second column was not read out in it.**

"**A mark, six shillings and five pence,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Two hundred and thirty-seven pence on the first of December and seven hundred and seventeen now, and the bag held two hundred and sixty-two pence on the tenth of November, and the whole of the difference is two days of work on a hirer's own account, a bonesetter's two shillings, one wage and a penny of postage, and there is no figure in this harbor for any part of that which is not a wage.

"**The column is twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and the oldest line in it is the fifth of March. Twenty envelopes on a bench.

"**Six weeks fallen due with no envelope against them. The fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November, the seventeenth of November, the first of December. Two of those were not paid because there was a sheet of paper and a bone, and four of them were not paid because there was nothing.

"**Three marks to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, and it is not being paid out of a bonesetter. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-three of May, and he was not asked anything this month, and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor, with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand, and nobody has been appointed to that column. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and on the twenty-seventh a woman of no office wrote this yard four paragraphs and a price, and the answer was no, and there is not a shilling of it here.

"**Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, said out loud at the head of this dock for the third time in seven months, and the yard is not going to say it a fourth time.

"**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on the sixteenth, on the twenty-third and on the thirtieth, being the twenty-eighth, twenty-ninth and thirtieth Mondays since the first of May, and no sheet has gone up on any wall in this harbor in three weeks and none came off, and that is entered.

"**And the cistern behind the engine house was charged on six rota days in three weeks at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party walked none of them, and the charge of the ninth of November stands entered with no figure against it, and this yard is not going to stand in a street and tell anybody what those six make with that one. The walk is nil and it stays nil.

"**The far bench was occupied on the sixteenth and the nineteenth of November and the twenty-fifth of November and the third of December, and on no other day of the three weeks, and neither brother went over to the other on any of the four.

"**And a man of the tide gang is a morning down for the want of a room over his store, and this yard does not know his name.

"**And twenty-one copies of a form of an office went out of a copying counter off the fish market at a penny a sheet on the first of December and were read out in a market in about forty people's hearing in about nine minutes by a woman of fifty-eight whom this yard did not ask.

"**And there is a thing none of us can check, which is a page and a half on a piece of paper, and a form of a court in a city with a date on it, and a name at the head of it which is an office and not a man, and a certificate of an office which has never had a man asked anything by this yard, and the market of the eighteenth of July read out in a room in a flat voice by a stranger.

"**That is the three weeks. It is in my hand and his and I am not going to read one line of the second column out, and if I am asked in a room in a city in four years I am going to point at a page in our own book that says why.**"

Nobody said anything about the six in a row, which is what about eleven people in a street say about a wage in a hard month.

**And at about half past nine on the Friday night in that shed, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top of the bench by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in the room, the whole of the three weeks went into the first column and four lines of it went into the second, and the four of them had reasons against them, and then Calder Marr stood up and put the half-sheet of the court on the table squared to the edge of the pump, the way she puts a sheet down, and he did not say anything else and he did not sit down again.**

*First column, entered on Friday the fourth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a day of work was worked on the face of the second seam landward of the third seam's mark on a hirer's own account with nobody in the water, and that the gross on the board was fourteen, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it, and that nine of the fourteen was paid at the mole and the yard's was three; and that the day's book went onto the board in the ship's hand and the holder read it back after her.*

*And that one sheet came up this hill in the ordinary post on Friday the fourth instant at about half past two in the afternoon while the tide was making, and that the said sheet is half a sheet and carries no signature anywhere, and that at the head of it is the name of a court in a city and at the foot of it the name of that court again; and that the said sheet states that the matter of the ASTER GANNET stands before that court and is not closed by a finding, and that a person entered in that matter as an interested party is not examined for the full Second Bell license while the matter stands and is not closed by a finding; and that it states that the said person was put to the examination once before and did not satisfy it, and that that court has before it no other matter concerning him and knows nothing whatever of his trade; and that it states that it is not a suspension, not a penalty and not a finding against anybody, and that it finds against nobody, and that a person who is not examined is not a person who has been refused; and that it states that nothing in that paper touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of that writ, and that the said writ runs to midnight on the first of March and that the office which granted it is not the office before that court; *And that the holder entered on the said evening, in his own left hand and before any figure of any kind, that he was put to the said examination in the month of September last year and did not satisfy it, and that the writ of the first of May instant was granted eight months afterwards, and that he has not gone near that examination since, and that this yard is not going to say in a street in the month of January that the writ of the first of May instant did this, and that the reason against the line is that a writ is a municipal instrument of this harbor and a form of a court in a city is not a municipal instrument of this harbor, and that the yard has had a thing taken off it this month that it wanted before it had the writ, and that the two are eight months and two hundred miles apart, and that the yard will not be the party that puts them together because a yard that is looking for somebody else to blame is a yard that has begun to believe in a writ.*

*And that the whole of the release was the party's own doing, and that the party applied in writing on the sixteenth instant in its own left hand at a cost of a penny, and that a room was asked for on a reeve's steps on the nineteenth, and that a clerk came up a hill on the twenty-third and a form came up a hill on the twenty-eighth and a copy came off a counter on the twenty-fourth, and that there is not one office in that chain which did anything this party did not ask it to do; and that the reason this line is in the first column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can get off a mole tomorrow morning and come up this hill and read all of it, and can go to the post and ask on what day a letter came up this road on Friday last, and that both of those are checkable, and that the whole of what it cost is not checkable from a mole, and that a yard which has been shown that in about four years nobody in a city is going to be able to check what a release cost it is a yard that bought the thing on purpose and is not going to be told it was somebody else's fault.*

*And that no sheet has gone up on any wall in this harbor in the three weeks ended on the said day and none came off, and that the two naval notices that were on that wall are not on it and were not taken down by this yard, and that the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and has not mentioned it; and that the far bench was occupied on the sixteenth and the nineteenth days of November past and the twenty-fifth day of November past and the third day of December instant, and on no other day of the three weeks, and neither brother went over to the other on any of the four, and that the said man was not at that bench at any hour on the second day of December instant; and that a man of the tide gang of this harbor is a morning down for the want of a room over his store and this party did not ask for that man and does not know his name and has not asked; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the sixteenth, nineteenth, twenty-third, twenty-sixth and thirtieth days of November past and on the third day of December instant, and that this party walked none of them, and that the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it, and that this party is not going to put a figure on a day it did not see and is not going to say in a street what those six charges and that one make between them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the fourth day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the said day that from the twenty-fourth day of November instant this yard is a party that has seen a document and said nothing about it in a market, and that there is no version of this yard after the said day in which that is not a fact about it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a party is worth in four years to the four people of this coast it did not tell about is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the four of them, who wrote a line in the month of August and who have not been asked.*

*And that a man of the first lay asked in a market in this harbor on the second day of December instant what an office in this country that has kept records for three hundred years thought it was guarding against when it decided what it was allowed to lose, and that this yard did not answer it and has entered why, and that the reason the refusal is in the first column and the question is in this one is that a refusal in a market is a fact about a street and a question about what an office is for is not a fact about anything this harbor holds; and that this yard has not been shown a document in this harbor or in any other that would answer it and does not know whether one exists, and that the question is entered here and is not going to be answered by this yard.*

*And that the holder entered on the said day that the form of the court of the fourth instant has not been shown to the clerk of the harbor authority, to the market, to the copyist of this harbor, or to anybody in this town, and that the only other person in this harbor who has read it is the ship's hand, and that the reason is not a secret and is entered here, which is that this yard is not going to be the thing that puts a date out of a city in front of four hundred people, and that a yard which has produced its own book whole and unshortened in a room in October is not going to produce this one in a market in December, and that the form will be produced to any office that asks for it in a form and has been entered that none has; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether that is a piece of quiet or a piece of hiding is not checkable from a mole, and that this yard does not know, and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike a line out of a column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And that a copy of the said record is to be produced to that court on the fourth day of January, and that this party has not asked any office for anything in aid of it, has not sent to a city, has not written to a man in a coat whose name is on no page of this book, and has not been to the counter of the copying room of that circuit office, and that the first of March is a date in this yard's own instrument and the fourth of January is a date in a form of a court, and that this yard is going to carry both of them and is not going to join them.*
