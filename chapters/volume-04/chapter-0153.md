# Chapter 153

*Three Marks, a Wage, and a Third Lay That Wants a Monday*

Monday the twenty-first and Tuesday the twenty-second of August, at the second seam's own face, in a doorway, and in the shed.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on the Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was sixteen Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and this party has not put anything on that wall in five months and is not going to start on the sixteenth.**

And a woman of the third lay came up the hill on the Monday morning at about half past eight and booked a day, and she said before she said anything else that she would pay twelve of seventeen at the mole the way she always had, and Calder Marr said that was the arrangement and had been since the third of May.

That was the first money this yard had taken since the second of August. It is eleven days.

---

**The day was the second seam's own face, nobody in the water, and the hand lead's line went over the side and came back up four times and was coiled on the crown, and it did not go past the outer marks, and the reason for that is on the page in the yard's own hand and it is that a hand lead's line is not what anybody wanted past the outer marks.**

Gross on the board seventeen. The eighth two. The share two. The victuals of the day two. A hired hand three and a second hired hand three. All three of the last paid at the mole by the woman who hired the day, that being twelve out of seventeen and the yard's being five.

**And nobody was in the water and the pot of air was not charged to the day, and the reason is that there is no pot in this harbor with air in it that this yard has not paid for out of its own bag at two marks apiece, and it did not buy one, and the day was worked on the face and not under it.**

The five went up onto the mole's steps at about one o'clock in the afternoon, in front of about eleven people, in daylight, on a board, in Calder Marr's own left hand.

"**That is five,**" he said. "**And on the twenty-fourth of June the same day of work on that same face brought this yard seven. Both figures are on this board and neither of them is a claim and neither of them is a complaint, and I am not going to stand here and say which one of them I would rather have had, and if anybody in this market wants to work that out for themselves they can and I will not help them with it.**"

Nobody worked it out. A man of the first lay looked at the board and said seven was a good day and went to work.

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon Calder Marr asked the clerk of this harbor authority, in a doorway, as a matter of fact, the way a man asks what the tide is doing, and got the only answer the office has.**

The ninth seam's mooring has been out of service since the first of May, and an end of it was entered against it on Monday the third of July by the officer of this authority in his own hand, and the end of it is the first of September, which is a Friday and is eleven days off.

"**Is there anything under that,**" said Calder Marr. "**Anybody told me. I am not asking you what the entry is for, I am asking you whether there is anything under it.**"

"**I will tell you what my page holds,**" said the clerk. "**My page holds an end of it, entered on the third of July by the officer of this authority, in his own hand, and it is an end of it and it is the first of September. My page does not hold anything under it. There is nothing under it. This office entered an end and this office has nothing under it, and the repair account of this office has been empty since the middle of February and is not going to be filled this year, and that is the whole of what this office has got to say about that mooring or any other.

"**And I have said what a box holds twice this year on this hill and I am going to say it a third time for you and then I am going to stop. A man in a boat can check a mooring. I am not a boat. I have a page and a slate wall and a key to a room, and there is no instrument in this office that will put a boat of mine past the outer marks, and the officer of this authority said that out loud on a mole's steps on the sixth of August in about eleven people's hearing and wrote it in his own book, and I have read his book because it is on my table, and I am not going to add a thing to it that he did not write himself.**"

He went back down the hill.

*That on Monday the twenty-first of August instant, at about half past two in the afternoon, this party asked the clerk of the harbor authority, in a doorway, as a matter of fact, whether anything stood under the end of it entered against the ninth seam's mooring on the third of July, being the first of September; and that the clerk answered that an end of it entered is an end of it entered, that this office entered it, that this office has nothing under it, that the repair account of this office has been empty since the middle of February and is not going to be filled this year, that that is the whole of what the office has, and that a man in a boat can check a mooring and he is not a boat; and that this party asked that question on Monday and did not ask it again on the Tuesday, and has entered that it is not going to ask it on the first of September either.*

---

**And on Tuesday the twenty-second the wage went in at about eleven in the morning, in Calder Marr's own left hand, because there is nobody else in that shed who writes, and the week went on the outside of the envelope in the same hand, and the envelope went into the bench chest and is not among the rolled ones and is not going anywhere this week and nothing is going to carry it, and the reason is on the page and it is the reason it has been since the second of May: a wage is not a reason to go to a door.**

**Seventeen lines and sixty-eight marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. One mark in a bag.**

And on the Tuesday night, at about nine, the shipwright read back the whole of the day and he read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed, and then she took the pencil and did a thing with the envelopes that had not been done since the second of May and had been thought about every Tuesday since the first of August.

She counted them off the bench. Eleven of them. She counted them twice and made him count them once.

"**Eleven,**" she said.

"**Eleven.**

"**And of those eleven, how many have we not made.**"

"**One.**"

"**The first of August, the eighth of August, the fifteenth of August.**"

"**The first and the eighth were made. The fifteenth was not made. There is no envelope with the week of the fifteenth on it anywhere in this shed, because I did not write one.**"

"**Then that is two figures and they are not the same figure,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I want both of them on the page in the same breath, because the day somebody comes up this hill and asks how many weeks this yard has paid, the answer is eleven and the answer is thirteen and both of them are true and I am not going to let the second one die in a man's head because it is the awkward one.

"**And I want the reason it is in the second column and not the first, and the reason is that an envelope that is not made is not a wage. The column is not the wage. The wage is four marks into a hand, and a man who is owed four marks is owed four marks whether or not there is a piece of paper with a week written on it, and the piece of paper is a thing this yard does so that it can tell at the end of a year which weeks it did, and it is not a receipt and nobody has ever received it.**"

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the twenty-second of August instant at about nine in the evening in this shed: that the count of envelopes on the bench in this chest is eleven and that the number of those eleven that were never made is one, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth of August, and that the two figures are different figures and neither of them is the other, and that the reason they are entered together is that a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid ought to be made to say both numbers in the same breath rather than the one that is easier; and that an envelope that is not made is not a wage and this column is not the wage, and the reason for that is that a piece of paper with a week written on it is a thing this yard does so as to know at the end of a year which weeks it did, and it is not a receipt, and nobody in this harbor has ever received one, and the forty marks standing to a boy of twenty-two are not a receipt and are not reduced by anything on this line.*

Nobody read that one back twice. The entry above it says why, and the reason is hers, and she gave it in advance on a Monday night in July and has not changed it.

---

**And on the Tuesday night, after the envelopes, the holder put the whole of the three marks into the first column with the figure and the date on it, and this is the first time in this volume that the name of the man is written in this yard's book in this yard's own hand and it is written once and it is not going to be written again.**

Perry Ollett, master of the *Sallow Reach*, a crew of Ashtide, six men, a compressor of her own and forty fathoms of her own hose, is owed three marks for a day of work on Tuesday the eighth of August, and he named six marks out loud in a doorway on Sunday the thirtieth of July and was paid three at the mole in front of the man who hired the day, and he said at the mole that he wanted it said that he said six and was paid three and both of those are the arrangement.

*That three marks is owing to the said man, with the date of the eighth of August against it; that no part of it is paid, and that this party has entered in the first column with a reason, because a man can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask whether that figure has moved and can go and look; and that the reason it is not paid is that the figure in the bag was one mark after the wage of that Tuesday had gone into the envelope, and that a debt is a debt, and that a man is not paid a shilling of it and told it is a payment; that the said man said six and was paid three and both of those are the arrangement and the arrangement is his and not this party's; and that this party will pay him the rest of it in one figure on a day when it has that figure, and is not going to pay it to him in three instalments of one, and would rather owe a man three marks whole for as long as it takes than have it said in a market that this yard is paying a man of another crew a shilling a week; and that the reason the whole of it is in the first column and not the second is that it is a figure and not a feeling, and anybody in this harbor can check it, and there is no name of any kind against it except the man's own, and his name is there because a man who is owed money is owed it by name.*

**And the yard entered that it is not going to send anybody up this hill to see him, and is not going to ask him a question, and is not going to take a statement from him about anything at all, and the reason is that the first time this yard put a man's figure in a book and then went and asked him about it, it got a page with a limit on it and a man saying *I have thought about it for five days*, and this yard is not going to do that twice in a year to a man it owes three marks to.**

Nobody was thanked. Nobody was written to. The shed was shut at about ten and the lamp was out and there was a mark in the bag and a fifth of March at the head of a column.

**No office in this harbor has paid this yard a mark since the eighth of May and none is going to, and the note is not re-written, its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven as last written on the fourth of January and none of them has moved, five marks of compressor's gland stand on it unpaid and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose, and the tin has been empty since the twenty-eighth of April.**

**No acknowledgement is entered. Nobody in this yard, and no boat of it and no lead of it, has been inside the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first on any day this year. And the mooring at the ninth seam has an end of it entered against it for the first of September, which is a Friday, and there is nothing under that entry, and a man in a boat can check a mooring, and this party is going to be in a boat on that morning and the clerk is not a boat.**
