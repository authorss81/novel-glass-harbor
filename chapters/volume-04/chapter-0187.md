# Chapter 187

*An Envelope Not Made, and a Column Nobody Is Going to Fill*

Tuesday the third and Wednesday the fourth of November, in the shed, and at the head of the dock.

**On Tuesday the third of November the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now FOUR, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth day of August past, the week of Tuesday the fifth day of September past, the week of Tuesday the sixth day of October past and the week of Tuesday the third day of November instant, and all four are entered in the same breath in this book and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all four at once rather than the one that is easier.**

Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and it is the fourth Tuesday in a row that this yard has not known on the Monday what it was going to write on the Tuesday. Eighteen envelopes on a bench in that chest. Eight shillings and sixpence in the bag, which is one hundred and two pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the third day of November instant at about four in the afternoon in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on the said day was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the bag held eight shillings and sixpence in it on the morning of that day and that is one hundred and two pence, and that a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and that the column of this book stands at twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks with the fifth of March the oldest; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now FOUR, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October instant and the third of November instant, and that the four figures are four different figures and none of them is either of the others; and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is eighteen and has not moved since the twenty-seventh of October instant; and that an envelope that is not made is not a wage and this column is not the wage; and that the reason against the line is that a day of work at a seam on the sixth of November is booked and a wage is not a day of work, and that a yard that put the two in one line would be a yard that had started to pay itself, and that this party entered on the sixth of October instant that it would enter the day of a working Tuesday unpaid in the first column on the Tuesday with the figure and the reason against the line rather than wait for a day it does not know is coming, and has entered it again today because it came.*

---

**And at about half past seven on the Wednesday evening in that shed the two of them had the argument that the third column has been waiting for since the seventeenth of October, and it took about forty minutes, and neither of them raised her voice, and it is in the book.**

"**Then somebody has to be appointed,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I have said that first so that you have to say the rest of it and not the first of it.**"

"**No, and I am going to say why, and it is not my reason from the seventeenth of October and you know that it is not.

"**That column is a page where a man wrote down what he said when he was not in the boat. On the second of May a boy of twenty-two read a position back off a slate on the mole's steps and a man wrote down what he had said about a minute after he had said it, and the column is worth what it is worth because nobody ever filled it but him, and it has not been filled since the first of August, and that is the first year of its life.

"**And on Wednesday a clerk of the circuit office read fourteen lines of it out loud in a room in a flat voice and entered that the office cannot make the boy come and cannot make anybody ask him, and that is now in a record in a city. And there is no more of that column in this harbor that anybody can add to. Whatever gets put in it now is put in it after a room has been in it.**"

"**Then put your nine sentences in it.**"

"**And that is the worst thing anybody has said in this shed since the first of May, and you knew it was going to be the next thing anybody said, and I want it written down that you knew.

"**No. And I have already said out loud in this yard in my own hand on the seventeenth of October what the nine sentences are and why they are not going in a page, and I am not going to say it again, and I am not going to be asked for them in a shed and I am not going to be asked for them in a room in four years.

"**And if the column is going to be filled by somebody, then somebody is going to be reading it, and the person who reads it is going to be deciding what goes in and what does not, and a page that one person decides is a page that one person owns. And that is not a column. That is a man with a book.**"

"**Then nobody is appointed.**"

"**Nobody is appointed, and nobody is going to be appointed in a shed on a Wednesday night, and I want the reason written down and it is not the reason of the seventeenth of October, and the reason is that a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills, and that somebody else is a clerk with a pen, and that a clerk with a pen is not a bad thing and is not a villain and is the only way a record gets made at all, and that the day he fills it is the day this yard stops being able to say that the one honest column in it is his.**"

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fourth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the third column of this book was not filled on Tuesday the third or on Wednesday the fourth instant and has not been filled since the first day of August instant, and that this party has not appointed anybody to it and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Wednesday night, and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column; and that the party discussed the filling of that column on the said evening for about forty minutes and that the holder said that somebody has to be appointed and that the said woman said no twice, and that the reason the holder gave for the first of the two was that the column is a page where a man wrote down what he said when he was not in the boat and that the party cannot produce that page and cannot explain it and cannot check it, and that the reason she gave is in the second column below and was given in her own words and was not improved on.

*And that nobody has written to that boy and nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and that the three of those nots stand and stand as they have stood since the eighth of June instant, and that this party entered on the seventeenth day of October past that it wanted to say thank you and did not say it and entered why, and that entry stands and is not improved on, and that this party has not sent for him and is not going to send for him and does not know where he is and has not asked.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the fourth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand said in this shed on the said day that the third column of this book is a page where a man wrote down what he said when he was not in the boat, that it has been worth what it is worth because nobody ever filled it but him, and that on the twenty-eighth day of October instant a clerk of the circuit office read fourteen lines of it out loud in a room in a flat voice and entered that the office cannot make that boy come and cannot make anybody ask him, and that there is no more of that column in this harbor that anybody can add to and that whatever goes in it now goes in it after a room has been in it; and that she said that if the said column is going to be filled by somebody then somebody is going to be reading it and is going to be deciding what goes in and what does not, and that a page one person decides is a page one person owns, and that that is a man with a book and not a column; and that the holder said then nobody is appointed and she said nobody is appointed and that the reason is that a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills, and that the somebody else is a clerk with a pen, and that a clerk with a pen is not a bad thing and is not a villain and is the only way a record gets made at all, and that the day he fills it is the day this yard stops being able to say that the one honest column in it is his.

*And that the holder said in this shed on the said day that the said woman could put her nine sentences in that column, and that she said that that was the worst thing anybody has said in that shed since the first of May instant, and that the holder entered that he knew it was going to be the next thing anybody said, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check what a page is empty of and cannot check what a page is going to be full of in four years.*

**No money came into this yard on the Tuesday and none on the Wednesday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag, and that is the whole of it, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the tenth instant. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, four weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October and the third of November. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-nine days up the crown of that mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the second instant and this party walked none of the twenty-three charges there have been since the third of August. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. Nineteen winters are in a list in a city and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what the woman at that table writes with this yard's four returns. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next with four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask twice to have it lifted and did not ask at all this month.
