# Chapter 189

*The Fourteenth Day, and a Wharf Day Refused*

Saturday the seventh and Sunday the eighth of November, in a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, and in the shed.

**And at about ten in the morning on Saturday the seventh of November a bonesetter came up this hill on foot and looked at an arm in a shed for about nine minutes, on the fourteenth day, and did not charge for it, and had said on the twenty-fourth of October that she would not, and said so again before she had looked at it.**

Hessa Barlow, forty-four, of this harbor, with a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, and a name that has been on a painted board over that door since before either brother in this shed was born.

"**I have not touched it and I am not going to,**" she said. "**You asked me on the twenty-fourth what the fourteen days are for and I told you and I have not changed my mind. There is a thing in a bone that has to be left alone for fourteen days and if anybody puts a thumb on it today, that is the day it does not knit.

"**So. Put your hand there. That is all I am going to do and you can put it there yourself.

"**And the strapping comes off on the twenty-first of November and not on the twentieth and not on the nineteenth, and if it comes off on the twentieth then in about four years you will be a man of forty with a wrist that does not turn all the way round in a cold month, and you will not be able to put the cause of that on anybody but me and I will not be able to help you with it.**"

"**What is the figure of the fourteen days,**" said Calder Marr.

"**There is no figure of the fourteen days. There is a figure of the three shillings and sixpence you paid me on the twenty-fourth and that is a figure and it is paid and it is not going to be charged again by me this winter. And the figure of the rest of it is a quarter of a year out of the left side of the only shipwright on this coast who can hold a pump on a wharf for six hours, and that is not my figure, that is yours, and you have got it in your book and you keep it there and I do not want it in mine.

"**And the day you worked yesterday went slower than a day with three on it and everybody on that wharf knew it and nobody said it, and the day of the twenty-third of October was about four hours and a quarter and I have not forgotten which four hours they were because there were four men standing there and three of them are still standing there.

"**And a day of work that this yard has refused is the most expensive thing anybody can hand you and nobody hands it to you for nothing. Somebody offered you one for Monday and you are going to say no, and I am telling you now, before you have said it, that saying no to it is going to be the correct thing and I am not going to be thanked for knowing that in advance.**"

Nessa Pike had her hand on the table and she had not said anything for about two minutes.

"**Three shillings and sixpence,**" she said, "**and a quarter of a year. And on Wednesday a man read my hand out of a book in a room in about an hour and a half and I sat in that room the whole time and I could not check one figure of it because it was in a book I have never read all of, and I have kept the count of the day and the times on every working day since the fifteenth of August and not one of them was in that book because that book is the whole of this yard's business and the count of the day is not the business.

"**And I want the three shillings and sixpence and the quarter of a year on the same line in the first column and not in two places, and I want it in my own hand, and I want it said that a bone is not a bill and a bill is not a bone and a man who has the two in one line will end up arguing about the wrong one.

"**And I am not going to be thanked for either of them, and I am not going to be at the far bench's business, and that is the end of what I have to say about it today.**"

---

**And at about half past four on the Sunday afternoon a man of the first lay came up this hill and offered a day, and it was about three marks, and this yard refused it, and he was not thanked and was not told it was sorry.**

"**A wharf on the Tuesday,**" he said. "**No, I have said Tuesday and I know what day it is, and I am not asking you to choose between a day of work and something else of yours, because a man of the first lay who comes up a hill in November with a day in his mouth is a man who has not had a winter.

"**I am asking because about a fortnight ago I would have asked a man of your yard, and I have been to three other doors since and all three of them said yes, and one of them has taken the day and is going to be two men on my quay on Tuesday and has not told me yet.

"**And I am not going to make a thing of it. I am going to say that a man with a bone strapped in a sling is not going to hold a pump handle on my quay on Tuesday and that you have told me so yourself at the head of that dock, and that I would rather be told no on a Saturday than turn up on a Tuesday with nobody.

"**That is all of it. It is about three marks and there is no more in it for you than for anybody else.**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to give you the reason in nine words and I am not going to improve on it in a market later.

"**A pump on a wharf takes two and one of them is her and she has fourteen days of a strapping on her and a hand that will not take a handle until the twenty-first of November at the earliest. And this party is not going to book a day it cannot work.

"**And it is not about the day. It is that on the ninth of September a man of the first lay of about ninety tons asked this yard to look at a thing on his stem and this party said no and he said he had not expected it in October and would come again in the spring, and this party is entered that it is not going to be the yard that says yes to everything from November and then cannot do the February ones.**"**"

"**That is a good answer,**" the man said, "**and it is not a cheerful one, and I have a wharf on the Tuesday with nobody on it, and I am going to go and find somebody who has not got a spring in his mouth. Three marks. And I am not going to be thanked for the offer and I would rather you did not try.**"

*First column, entered on Sunday the eighth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on Saturday the seventh instant a bonesetter of this harbor, being Hessa Barlow, forty-four, of a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, came up this hill at about ten in the morning on the fourteenth day and looked at the left forearm of this yard's own shipwright for about nine minutes and did not charge for it, having said on the twenty-fourth of October instant that she would not, and said so again before she had looked at it; and that she said that there is a thing in a bone that has to be left alone for fourteen days and that the strapping comes off on the twenty-first of November instant and not before, and that the figure of the three shillings and sixpence of the twenty-fourth is paid and is not going to be charged again by her this winter, and that the figure of the rest of it is a quarter of a year out of the left side of the only shipwright on this coast who can hold a pump on a wharf for six hours, and that that is not her figure and that she does not want it in her book.

*And that the said shipwright entered in her own hand in the first column on the said day that three shillings and sixpence and a quarter of a year go on the same line and not in two places, and gave the reason, which is that a bone is not a bill and a bill is not a bone and a man who has the two in one line will end up arguing about the wrong one; and that the whole of the cost of the injury to that arm is entered on that one line in the first column in her own hand and in the holder's, and that the said sum is a figure against a named working person and that it was not paid by a dive and was not paid by anybody going into the water, and that no person of this yard was in any water on any day of this month.

*And that on Sunday the eighth instant a man of the first lay came up this hill at about half past four in the afternoon and offered a day of work on a wharf for Tuesday the tenth instant at about three marks, and that this party refused it, and gave the reason in nine words, that a pump on a wharf takes two and one of them is this yard's own shipwright and that her forearm is strapped and a hand that will not take a handle until the twenty-first of November instant at the earliest, and that this party is not going to book a day it cannot work; and that the said man said that that was a good answer and not a cheerful one, and that he was going to find somebody who has not got a spring in his mouth; and that the said man was not thanked and this party did not offer him anything and did not tell him it was sorry, and that the said day of about three marks was there to be taken on the Sunday instant and was not taken, and that this is entered because a day refused is a day not earned and is not entered as earned.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the eighth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man of this party stood in a room in this harbor for two days in the last week of October instant and read out of his own book for three hours and fifty minutes of the first of them, and that on the Wednesday night he did not sleep and on the Thursday night he did, and that this party entered both halves of that on one line with the hour against it and entered that it is not going to name what it is in a shed, and that the reason is that a man on a mole can check whether he slept on a Wednesday night and cannot check what a room does to him two days afterwards, and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike one of them out of this column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.

*And that the holder entered on the eighth of November instant that the whole of what a sitting costs is two days of one man's time and that neither of those two days was on a mole and neither of them earned, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a day on a mole is worth a figure and a day in a room is worth a record in a city and that the two of those are not the same kind of thing and that the second one costs more and that nobody in this harbor can put a figure on the second one.*

**No money came into this yard on the Saturday and none on the Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and five marks and eight shillings and six pence is in the bag, and that is nine hundred and two pence, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the tenth instant. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid and is not going to be paid out of a bonesetter's bill. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and nobody has been appointed to that column. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and three shillings and sixpence out of a bag on the twenty-fourth of October is not a payment of it. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, four weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October and the third of November. A day of about three marks on a wharf was offered on the Sunday instant for the Tuesday instant and refused by this party, and the cost of that refusal is a day not earned and it is entered because a day refused is a day not earned. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the fifth instant and this party walked none of the twenty-four charges there have been since the third of August. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days, and the two objects of nobody's are on the crown of that mole under two tarpaulins about nine feet apart and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. Nineteen winters are in a list in a city and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what the woman at that table writes with this yard's four returns. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next with four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask twice to have it lifted and did not ask at all this month.
