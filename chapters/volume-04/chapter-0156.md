# Chapter 156

*The Ninth Acknowledgement, and a Man With Nothing Left*

Sunday the twenty-seventh and Monday the twenty-eighth of August, on the mole's steps above the third seam's mark, in the open air, and in the shed.

**The officer of this harbor authority was on his own steps at ten in the morning on a Sunday, which he does not do, with a slate under his arm, and he had written on the slate before he came out, and the slate was face up in his hand and nobody was asked to come closer than about nine feet.**

About eleven people were on the crown. It was a clear morning and the wind was light and the mole head's light was not on, and the two objects of nobody's were under their tarpaulins about thirty feet further up the crown, a hundred and three days since either of them had been put where it was, and one of the tarpaulins still had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June.

"**I am going to read this and then I am going to go back down and I am not going to answer a question about it, and I am going to name the price before I give it, which is what I have done every time and I am not going to stop doing it now that there is one left.**

"**I have been asked for a ninth acknowledgement. There are eight in this ledger. Both of this year's are spent. There is no tenth and there is not going to be one, and I want the ten of you standing on those steps to understand that the two in this year's that are spent were not spent by me being generous. One of them was asked for by this party and one of them was not, and the price of the one that was not is four days of the best of a third lay's season in the first week of August, and I entered it in my own book at the office's own cost with the repair account of this office empty since the middle of February, and I have not been paid for it and am not going to be, and I have said before in front of eleven people that an instrument which is entered and not contested is worse than no instrument, because it looks like a decision and there is no money behind it.

"**So here is the price of the ninth, and I am naming it in the open air, and I would like it written down in your book in your own hand and not mine.

"**An instrument of this office which puts a person, or a boat, or a lead, inside the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October carries my acknowledgement on the page and in the record and at my cost, and it is never free. It has been free for nobody in nineteen years. There is no boat of this office's and there has not been one since the evening of the twenty-eighth of April, and I have said on this mole on the sixth of August that there is nothing in my book that will put a boat of mine past the outer marks and that that is not a failure of my book, it is the design.

"**And what this party is asking me for is a day inside the closure. That is the whole of it. The first clause of that notice closes the toe and the inner face of this mole from the third seam to the eleventh to all work and to all approach by all persons, and this office and this yard and any other crew in this harbor are all persons. It is not lifted. It is not narrowed. It is not re-served and it is not altered, and this office has no power to lift it and I would not do it if I had.

"**And a party that asks a reeve for his last entry is buying with somebody else's day, and it has always been that, and nobody has ever been able to name the man on the spot. So I am going to name him now, before I grant it, because I have never in nineteen years granted one without knowing who pays and I am not going to start on the ninth.

"**Corby Vance, of the first lay, had the toe of this mole booked for Monday the twenty-eighth of August for a day of his own, at a figure of his own, and he will be out of a day and a day's work, and he is not this party and he is not this office and he is not going to be asked for anything by anybody, and I have not spoken to him and I am not going to.**"

---

"**Then say it to him,**" said a voice from the boards, and Ors Kest turned the slate about ninety degrees without looking at who had spoken.

"**No,**" he said. "**Because the price of a thing this office does is not a conversation between me and a man I have hurt. If I go up to Corby Vance on a Monday morning and say *I am sorry, a salvage crew on this hill has asked me for my last acknowledgement and it has cost you your Monday,* then in about four years there is a man standing on a mole in another town who says that the harbor authority of Morrow Quay told a man of the first lay he was sorry on a Monday morning, and that is the whole of what that office has left to say for itself in this harbor, and it will be said in a market, and it will be said about this party, and it will be true, and it will be the only true thing anybody in this harbor can say about this party in four years, and it will be about a Monday that a man lost, and not one word of it will be about the ground.

"**A notice does not say what a man loses. That is what a notice is. And a party that has published itself is not a party that gets to be sorry in a street and mean it.**"

He put the slate down on the top step.

"**I am granting it. There is no capacity in me to refuse it and I want that understood, because I do not want a man in four years to read this ledger and think a reeve weighed this. I cannot refuse an instrument of my own office that somebody is legally entitled to ask for. So this is not a favor and it is not a decision and it is the last one.

"**And the second half of the price, said in the open air before anybody goes in the water: this office has now been asked twice by this party and has paid twice, and cannot pay a third time, and I am not going to be asked a third time. If anybody comes up this hill after the twenty-eighth of August and asks this office for an acknowledgement, the answer is in this ledger and there is nothing behind it. Not the plate. Not anything. Eight, and a ninth, and a book with nothing in it that a man in this harbor can use, which is what I told you on the sixth and what I am telling you now with one less thing in it.**"

---

**And Calder Marr asked him one question, in about nine words' worth of an answer, and did not ask him a second one, and the second one is in the book with a date against it and the reason against the line.**

"**Is this about the plate at the fifth seam.**"

"**It is not and I will not tell you what it is about.**"

That is thirteen words and it is the same thirteen words he used on a mole's steps in this harbor in July, and Calder Marr has not asked him anything about the plate on any day since the third of May, and he did not ask him a second question on the Sunday, and he has entered the not-asking with the date and the hour, and the reason against the line is that a reeve who has just given this party the last thing he has is a man nobody asks a question while he is standing in the wind.

"**Then I am not going to thank you,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to say why in nine words and then I am not going to say anything else about it, in front of eleven people, once.

"**In four years a man reading that ledger reads it as a reeve's price.**"

"**I know that,**" said Ors Kest. "**I said the same thing about the fourth of August on a mole on the sixth and I have not taken it back and I am not going to. And it was the only instrument I had left and I would like it written down that I spent it, and that I was not thanked, and that I did not expect to be.**"

He picked the slate up and went down his own steps at about ten past ten, and his coat was too clean for the wind again, and it had been too clean for the wind in June and July and August and it would be too clean for the wind in September, and neither brother in this yard has ever said so to him.

---

**And on Monday the twenty-eighth the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was seventeen Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room since the twelfth of August.**

The woman of the second lay with the gear of her own under the toe came up the hill at about eight in the morning and booked the Monday herself, and she said she would pay twelve of seventeen at the mole the way she always had, and Calder Marr said that was the arrangement.

Her gear is under the toe of this mole off the third seam and there is no other ground in this harbor for it, because the ground she would want it on is dry ground and the ground she has it on is wet stone four fathoms down with a swell on it, and a chain cannot be laid on it by a man wading. **It wants a boat, a pole, a lead line, and three hands, and the only boat in this harbor that can do it is this yard's own, and the mole's toe from the third seam to the eleventh is closed to all work and all approach by all persons, and the three of them go in there under a piece of paper with a reeve's acknowledgement on it and they come out again.**

The entry went in the first column in Calder Marr's own left hand and the shipwright read it back and he read it back after her, with the hour in and the hour out.

*That on Monday the twenty-eighth of August instant this party worked for about four hours and ten minutes on the toe of this mole, inside the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October, between the third seam and the eleventh, under an instrument of the harbor authority carrying the reeve's acknowledgement at his own cost, that being the ninth entry in that ledger and the last entry there is in it; that the party went in at about half past eight in the morning and came out at about twenty to one in the afternoon; that the work was done for a woman of the second lay on her own account and for no office and for no wage, at her figure and at her hiring; that no part of the harbor authority's work was done and no part of the ground was touched on the authority's own account; that no finding of worked glass was made and nothing was raised and no return goes up and there is no fifth return; and that the second clause of that notice keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed and that the two objects of nobody's on the crown of this mole have not been moved and were not moved and the party did not go near them; and that the plate at the fifth seam was not reached, was not measured, was not touched, and was not asked about, and that the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January was not reopened and not referred to and is not going to be.*

*And that a man of the first lay named Corby Vance had this mole's toe booked for this Monday for a day of his own at a figure of his own, and is out of a day and a day's work, and this party has entered his name and the fact and has not paid it and is not going to thank him and is not going to send for him and is not going to say sorry to him in a market, and the reason is on the page and it is that a notice does not say what a man loses and that a party which has published itself is not a party that gets to be sorry in a street and mean it; and that this party did not know on the Saturday that he had the toe booked and would have booked around him if it had known, and that not knowing is not a reason and the reason is the one above and it is the only one.*

---

**And the money went the way it goes. Gross on the board seventeen. The eighth two. The share two. The victuals two. A hired hand three and a second hired hand three. Twelve out of seventeen paid at the mole by the woman who hired the day, and the yard's being five, and nobody was in the water, and the pot of air was not charged to the day.**

**Five marks and twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag at about ten past one on the Monday afternoon, and that is five marks more than it was, and it is the yard's own five out of a board of seventeen, and the eighth and the share come off the top of a day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May and this yard has never argued either.**

**And on Tuesday the twenty-ninth a wage of four goes out of that bag into an envelope in the holder's own left hand, and the column stands at eighteen lines and seventy-two marks with the fifth of March the oldest, and there is no day set on it in this fortnight, and it is a line in the yard's own book and not a scene.**

---

*And that the notice of the thirty-first of October is not lifted and is not narrowed and is not re-served and is not altered, and that the harbor authority of this harbor has no power to lift it and would not lift it if it had; and that a man in about five fathoms in April is not the reason that notice is on the page of this country, and that this party has not said that it was not worth the paper it is on, and has not said that it was, and is not going to, and has entered both nots with the date; and that the two objects of nobody's on the crown of this mole have not been moved in a hundred and three days and were not moved on the twenty-eighth, and that one of the tarpaulins still has a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and it has not been lashed back; and that this party did not go near them and is not going to.*

**And the plate note is open and it did not close in this month. The plate is in eleven feet in the mole's own foundation stone and it has been seen and not measured and not touched, and the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January was refused in a record with a date on it, and this party has not written a better letter and is not going to. Whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed, and this party does not know what that is and is not going to guess, and it has entered that it does not know, with the date, and the not-knowing and the not-asking are both on this page and both dated and neither of them is going to be improved on in a shed.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Sunday from about ten until about five and on the Monday from about eight until about four, and he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing, and neither brother went over to him, and it is a single line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week.

**Nothing came into this yard from any office on any day this month and nothing is going to. One herring berth short. The north berth out. The tide gang four men short. The light not on, because a light costs oil and the repair account of the office that owns it has been empty since the middle of February and is not going to be filled this year. The fire party's hose dead until the spring. A cistern on a rota, holding since the nineteenth of June. The hard at Ashtide unanswered since the ninth of November, which is the better part of a year. A man of the first lay who is out of a Monday. And a harbor that is poorer at the end of this month than it was at the start of it, by one day of a man's work, and that is a standing condition of this place and not a thing that happened this month.**

**And on Friday the first of September, which is a Friday on this book's calendar and is four days off, the end of it on the ninth seam's mooring arrives, and a man is going to go and look at it with a pole and a lead line, and there is no clerk to go with him and no office to ask.**
