# Chapter 155

*The Sheet Under the Two That Came Down*

Friday the twenty-fifth and Saturday the twenty-sixth of August, in the market office and at its press in the morning, at the market wall at about eleven, and at the head of the dock.

**The press is the market office's own, at the back of its counting floor, under a slab of stone that takes two men to shift. It is unlocked on a Tuesday and a Friday morning while the counting is going on and it is not unlocked for anybody who has not business on that floor, and the woman who came out from behind the counter to shift the slab has worked in that room for nineteen years and her mother shifted it before her, and this party has been in that room three times in five months and has not spent a penny in it and is spending a shilling on the twenty-fifth of August for the first time.**

"**A shilling is what the press charges for a sheet a man owns,**" she said. "**A man of Ashtide paid it in the last week of June for his own sheet and he is welcome to come back. You have never paid it because you have never asked us to do anything, and you have been copying your own at the foot of your own dock at no price for five months and forty of them, and that is your business and not ours. Today you want forty off ours, so it is a shilling, and if you want to argue the price you can argue it with the counting and not with me.**"

She shifted the slab with a bar and a second woman came and helped and there was a smell of ink and hot metal and old stone.

---

**And there was a man in a coat standing at the end of the long table with a coat buttoned and his hands at his sides, in a room that is open to anybody on a Tuesday and a Friday morning, and this party did not arrange it and did not ask and is not going to say it arranged it.**

He did not sit down. He spoke for about four minutes and he spoke in the middle of a room with about nine people in it and a counting going on behind him, and he said his name and the name of his office and he did not take his coat off.

"**I am going to say three things and then I am going to let you get on with your morning. I do not know you and I am not going to find out this month, and that is the same standing arrangement you gave me in a room on the twelfth of August and I am holding to it exactly as it was given to me.

"**One. You have asked, by a letter in the ordinary post on Sunday the twentieth, for a copy of a record. That is a request for the record of your own fourth return of the fourteenth of July. It is in this office's possession. You have not been given it, and you are not going to be given it, and nobody in the city where that file is is going to tell you when you will be.

"**Two. I am going to tell you the other one because nobody in this harbor has told you and you have been three weeks finding out in the wrong direction. There is a thing that exists. It is called a provisional public copy record and it is a copy of a record, and the record is about an object, and the object is not in your possession and is not going to be. What the copy carries is the provenance of the record, its limits, and an explicit statement of what it does not show, and the limits are printed on the face of the copy itself and not in a wrapper and not in a second document. You may ask for a copy of that copy. You would pay for it yourself. That is all that would be. You are not being offered the copy, and nobody is making the copy, and nobody is releasing anything, and I am not able to tell you when and neither is anybody in this room.

"**And I am going to be exact about what that is, because you are a yard that has spent five months refusing to put a name on a thing it cannot check, and a thing that is offered in a market is a thing that gets checked.

"**Three. Two things you can check, which is more than you usually get, and which I am giving you because it is cheap for me to give you and expensive for you to find out.

"**The request of the twentieth has been entered. That is a fact and you may print it. The copy is not made and is not released and no date attaches to either, and that is also a fact. But whether anybody in a city has any intention of doing either is not a fact and it is not mine to give you and you are not going to print an answer you do not have.

"**And the second. If that copy came to this room on a Tuesday morning, it would not be a thing anybody in Morrow Quay could check. Not the figures in it, not the dates, not a word of it. Because the paper it would be copied from is not in this harbor, and a man in this harbor cannot check a page he has never seen the original of. And you know that, because you have a thing in this town that is the same shape as that and you have been telling people about it on a wall for five months without knowing you were making my argument for me.**"

He looked at the slab and the ink and the two women standing at the counter.

"**That is all. Go and put your sheet up.**"

He was gone before the slab was back.

---

**And Calder Marr stood in the middle of the counting floor for about a minute with a draft in his hand and then went and paid the woman at the counter a shilling out of a bag that had a mark in it, and it was the third shilling that has moved through this yard's book in five months that was not this yard's income, and all three are in the first column with the dates against them and nobody has ever entered the first two as income and is not going to.**

**Nineteen pence in a bag.**

**And the copy is not obtained and not made and not released and not printed and not put on a wall and not sent to a city, and that is entered in the yard's own book on the Saturday night with a date, and the reason is on the page: a party which prints a thing it has been told exists, in a market, in forty people's hearing, on a Friday, has published the existence of a document and not the document, and in about a year there will be a man in a city asking this party where it got the name from. What the party has is a request with a date on it and a refusal with no date on it, and a request is a thing a party can publish.**

---

**And this is what went up on the market wall of Morrow Quay on Friday the twenty-fifth of August at about eleven in the morning, in daylight, in a market, on the same nails everything goes up on, on the outside of whatever is there.**

What was there was one sheet. It was the yard's own fourth return, made on Friday the fourteenth of July out of a finding of Monday the tenth, and it had been under four papers since the twenty-eighth of July and three of those papers had been taken off that wall in one morning on the sixteenth, and the fourth was on it alone, and it is still on it, and it is still the only paper on that wall that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and a man can read it if he stands at the side of it in the right wind.

> *THE PLACE OF EVERY COPY OF THIS SHEET, as far as this yard is able to state it. It is copied at the foot of this dock, on the day it goes up, at no price, by anybody who asks. Forty went out of the market office press this morning at a shilling for forty. Copies are in about sixty boats of this harbor and of the first, second and third lays and of Ashtide. One copy of the sheet of the fifteenth of August is in a city going to press, and this yard cannot stop that, and the sheet of the fifteenth carried the place of every copy of itself, which is a thing a man can use and this yard is not taking back. Four of the eleven copies of the public sheet of the fourteenth of October are not reached and have never been reached, this yard has not asked and is not going to, and the list of the eleven is on a wall in this harbor since the third of May with a shilling against it.*
>
> *SIX FIGURES FROM A BUILDER'S OWN DAY-BOOK OF THE PORT OF ASHTIDE, in the press of that market office, in a builder's hand and not a clerk's, kept by a man who was paid by the foot and wrote down where he worked and nothing else. Forty feet. Seventy-three feet. One hundred and forty-one feet. Two hundred and ten feet. Two hundred and seventy-nine feet. Three hundred and forty-eight feet. Each of them at the second seam of this mole. The builder's name is cut into a stone at the mole head. The book gives a day against each entry and no year in any hand this yard can read, and this sheet gives the day and says the year is not on it. There were nine places on the sheet of the fifteenth and six of them are these. This yard has entered that it does not know why the other three are where they are and is not going to guess. This sheet does not say what the nine are. It does not call them a chain and it does not call them a compact.*
>
> *THE BAR OF GRAVEL. This yard's figure, made once, on the second of May, with a hand lead's line and a slate: two and three-quarters of a fathom in the middle of a spring tide at twenty past two on the flood, and two and a quarter on the ebb. The figure of a man who keeps a channel nine miles off this coast: two and a half on the flood, and two and none the less on the ebb, made four times in nineteen years. OURS ONCE, TWO AND THREE-QUARTERS. HIS FOUR TIMES IN NINETEEN YEARS, TWO AND A HALF. WE ARE NOT GUESSING. Both figures are in a city going to press this month. Neither is withdrawn and neither is going to be. A ROAD NOBODY ELSE CAN USE IS NOT A ROAD. A ROAD EVERYBODY CAN USE IS NOT THIS YARD'S. This yard has not looked at five miles of that coast and is not going to and has not been asked to by anybody, and the reason is on the page in that book and is that a yard of two and a half people does not have three days for five miles of a coast it published in June. This sheet does not call it anything.*
>
> *THE THING THAT IS UNDER THE WATER AND IS NOT ON THIS SHEET. A case of five bells lies in about five and a half fathoms inside the four hundred yards under the after hatch of a vessel claimed whole by an office in a city. It is the only local copy of a community's account of itself, it is not for sale, and it went back into the water out of a man's hands on the eighth of August and this yard has entered that and has not softened it. The four people it belongs to are living in this town, and they have no boat, and about nine marks between them, and they have not asked this yard for anything.*
>
> *WHAT THIS YARD ASKED FOR ON THE TWENTIETH OF AUGUST AND HAS NOT BEEN GIVEN. This yard wrote on Sunday the twentieth to a circuit office in a city, in the ordinary post, at a shilling of postage out of a bag that held nothing, and asked for a copy of a record. The record is the record of this yard's own fourth return of the fourteenth of July, a copy of which went into the harbor authority's ledger on that Friday morning in a clerk's own hand, and a third copy of which went into a file in a city on the same morning. The request has been entered. The copy is not made and is not released. No date attaches to either and nobody in a city is going to say when. THIS YARD IS NOT PRINTING AN ANSWER IT DOES NOT HAVE. AND A COPY OF A RECORD IS NOT A THING ANYBODY IN THIS HARBOR CAN CHECK, because the paper it would be copied from is not in this harbor, and a man here cannot check a page he has never seen the original of.*
>
> *WHY THIS SHEET IS ON A WALL AND NOT IN A ROOM. A man of fifty-four who keeps a channel by hand stood on the steps of this mole on Wednesday the twenty-third and said that the place of every copy is a page that tells a man where the copies are, and that a wall worth reading is one somebody can be brought up in front of, and that this party ought to say it in a room. THIS PARTY HEARD HIM AND DOES NOT AGREE WITH HIM, AND IS NOT TAKING THE SENTENCE DOWN, AND IS GOING TO PUT IT IN A MARKET. The only room this party can be got into in the next three weeks is a room somebody else has arranged and is holding a form about a book in, and this party did not arrange that room and cannot un-arrange it, and a thing said in such a room is said at a time somebody else chose. This yard has chosen a market, knows what it is doing, and is not going to explain itself out of that.*
>
> *A sheet that goes up goes up on the outside of whatever is there, and that has been the arrangement since the ninth of November. Under this sheet is this yard's own fourth return of the twenty-second of July, and it is still on those nails, and it is the only paper on this wall that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and a man can read it if he stands at the side of it in the right wind.*

---

**And at about eleven o'clock in the morning in front of about forty people Calder Marr read the whole of that out loud, off the sheet, standing on the boards, and then he read it out loud a second time, because a man who has put a sheet on a wall owes the people standing in front of it the whole thing twice, once for the people who can hear and once for the people who could not get near enough the first time.**

He got a figure of it wrong the second time.

**Two hundred and seventy-nine feet** he said as **two hundred and seventy-seven feet**, in the middle of the sixth figure, in the same voice, at the same speed, about two hundred yards from a wall on which the figure was written in large hand.

"**No,**" he said, and he did not stop, and he did not begin again, and he did not go back to the top. He put the sheet up in his left hand, found the line with his thumb, and read the sixth figure off the sheet in his own hand, and then he carried on from the seventh line with the sentence he had been saying. The whole of that took about a second and a half.

Nobody in that market said anything about it. Two men of the third lay looked at each other. A woman of the second lay who had put the four people of the Sill in a room in the first week of August was still looking at the wall when he got to the end.

**And Nessa Pike said the thing at the foot of the boards, in about forty people's hearing, about four minutes later, and it is the argument of the twenty-second of July running in the other direction, and she said it out loud because she has said every hard thing this yard has ever had to say out loud.**

"**Then we are going to have to learn the difference between a book we keep and a record somebody keeps, and we have had it the wrong way round for five months and I did not know that until this morning.

"**I said in a market on the twenty-second of July that the only thing that makes a wall worth reading is somebody else being able to be asked a question about it, and I was right, and this yard has spent every day since the fifteenth of August finding out what happens when a party that has published itself gets asked. That is not a different thing from what I said. It is the same thing coming from the other side, and I have not said the other side and I am saying it now.

"**Every line of that sheet is a fact a man in this market can check this afternoon, and so can I, and so can she. If a man comes up here tomorrow and says that figure is wrong, I can go into that press on a Friday morning and put my thumb on the entry. If he says the return is wrong, I can read it off the sheet underneath this one.

"**And the third block is about a record. We have written to a city for a copy of our own return and we have not been given it and we are not going to be, and we have put the fact that we asked and the fact that we have not got it on a wall, and that is a fact about a piece of paper we cannot see, and there is nothing on this wall this morning that anybody can check about it except the address and the date and my own mouth. That is the whole of the difference and it took us five months and a man with a figure of his own to find out.

"**So: a book a party keeps is a thing people can be asked about. A record an office keeps is a thing nobody can be asked about until the office decides to let them. And this yard has spent five months believing it was publishing the first, and has been holding the second the whole time and did not know it, because a writ is a record and a return is a record and a form is a record. The only instrument this party has ever made is a nail and a sheet in a market, and it is worth exactly what is on it and nothing else.**"

Nobody argued with her. A man of the first lay said *that is right* out loud, to nobody, and went to work.

---

**And on the Saturday the forty copies went out of the market office in a bundle and up the coast with a woman of the second lay who took them for nothing, and about eleven people read the sheet in a market on the Saturday morning and a shilling changed hands for a copy of it at the foot of the dock, and that shilling is not this yard's income and is not in the bag and will not be.**

**And the party entered three things at about nine o'clock on the Saturday night with the lamp lit and the shipwright reading them back and the holder reading them back after her and nobody else in the shed, and the first one is the money and it goes in the same words it always goes in.**

Nothing in a bag but nineteen pence. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks standing at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven as last written on the fourth of January and none of them moved. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. **Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for a day of work on the eighth, with a date on it, in the first column, in no column of this book, and not a shilling of it paid and the reason against the line.** Eight shillings and tenpence standing to each of two women, undiminished, in no sentence together in this book. Eight marks and a day of a boat standing to this harbor's bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, and the chest in this shed not opened and no figure out of it on any wall. **Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two who cannot do the thing he was kept for, and the head of the third column keeping his hand, and a request to scrape it out refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.** Forty marks to a conservator of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, **and this yard has not offered her six marks and has not been asked to and is not going to.**

*And the second one is the shilling, and it is entered with a date and it is a figure and not a feeling: that a shilling was paid out of this yard's bag at the press of the market office at about ten in the morning on the twenty-fifth instant for forty copies of a sheet of this yard's own making; that it is the third shilling that has moved through this book in five months that was not this yard's income, and that the first two were taken by a man of Ashtide for a copy of his own printed sheet at the foot of this dock on the twenty-first of July and the fifteenth of August, and that this yard has entered both of those and is not going to enter either of them again; and that this yard has for five months copied its own sheets at the foot of its own dock at no price because that is the practice and the practice came before the money, and that on the twenty-fifth instant this party had forty sheets of its own finding to make and no time to make them and paid for them, and that paying for a thing is not a fact about a man's character and is not going to be written up as one.*

*And the third one is the second column, and it is entered in the holder's own left hand, and it carries a reason against the line and no name of any kind in it, neither a man's nor a word.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the twenty-sixth of August instant at about nine in the evening in this shed: that at about eleven this morning in a market, in about forty people's hearing, the holder of the writ of the first of May read a sheet of his own out twice, and that on the second reading he said the sixth of the six figures in a builder's own day-book at two hundred and seventy-seven feet, and that the figure is two hundred and seventy-nine, and that he corrected himself off the sheet in his own hand in the same voice without going back to the top, and that the whole of the correction took about a second and a half, and that nobody in that market said anything about it; and that the reason it is entered in this column and not the first is that the sixth figure is a figure a man in this harbor cannot check this afternoon, the builder's book being in a press in a counting office that is open on a Tuesday and a Friday morning and not on a Saturday, and because there was nobody standing in front of that wall who could have put a hand on the entry and stopped him; and that this yard has built its whole practice on the proposition that a figure anybody can check is worth something and a figure only its maker can check is worth less; and that the first time this yard has had to say one of its own figures out loud in a market it got one of the six wrong, and on the sixth, and on the one about a man in another place; and that this party is entering it against itself, with the date and the hour, because a party that enters other things against itself and not this one is a party whose second column is worth nothing at all, and this is the only entry in that column in five months that is about a mistake of this yard's own and not about a limit of a man in a boat, and it is entered without a name of any kind in it because the column is a column and a name would turn it into a complaint.*

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Friday from about eight until about five and on the Saturday from about nine until about four, and he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him on either day, and the yard entered that he did not and that neither brother has said why and neither brother is going to.

**And on Sunday morning the twenty-seventh a reeve with a spent ledger came out onto his own steps at ten o'clock, which he does not do, with a slate under his arm, and he had written on it before he came out, and what he had to say was that he had been asked for a ninth acknowledgement and had one to give and it was the last one he had.**
