# Chapter 168

*Asked to Certify, and a Figure Read Out at the Head of the Dock*

Monday the twenty-fifth and Tuesday the twenty-sixth of September, on the mole's steps, at the head of the dock, in a doorway, and in the shed.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was twenty-one Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room since the sixteenth of September and the next one is to be appointed for the week following the twelfth of October.**

**And at about half past ten on the Monday morning Wil Tranter came up the hill again with a paper in a coat pocket, and he took it out and put it on the bench face up, and it was not the penny sheet.**

"**That is the paper I told you about on Friday. I have had it in my coat since Friday. I have not sent it to anybody and I have not said no and I have read it about nine times and I understand every line of it, and I would like you to read it and then I am going to ask you for something and I would rather you had the paper in front of you when I ask than not.**"

Calder Marr read it out, in the yard, standing up, in about four minutes, and then gave it back.

> *Ailsa Rorke, surveyor. Fourteen Wharf. —*
>
> *I am instructed by the underwriters to report upon the loss of the coaster* Ardent *on the second day of August last, and upon the grounds of her total loss.*
>
> *I find that the said vessel drew eleven feet six inches aft at the last cargo and that the said vessel did not enter this harbor on the said morning.*
>
> *I am further informed that the ground of this harbor is barred to two and three-quarter fathoms at the middle of a spring tide, and I am further informed that a vessel of this draught could not have crossed that ground at twenty past two in the afternoon of the second day of August without taking the ground.*
>
> *I am unable to verify the said figure. I have applied in writing to the yard of Morrow Quay, whose sheet is set in type in this city, and I have had no answer, and I record the yard as declining to certify the figure, and I record that I have not had that from the yard and have it from a man of the first lay who crossed the water to tell me so, and I would rather set it down with that on it than leave it off. I record that the vessel is not mine, and that her owner is a man I have not met and cannot meet before Thursday, and that no person in this city can check any figure on the sheet of that yard, and I set out my finding in that uncertainty rather than in another one.*

"**And there is the whole of it,**" said Wil Tranter. "**And he has written down that you decline, and he has been careful about it, and he did not write *refused to stand behind his own figure*, and he wrote *declines to certify the figure*, and he has written under that line that he has not had it from you. And I told him in a letter in the first week of this month, before I came up your hill on the Friday, that a yard which has printed a figure of its own cannot be got to swear to it afterwards, and he had it in his hand when he wrote that line, and I have not sent you one word of that letter and am not going to. And I have not asked you yet, and I am going to ask you now.**

"**And I want to know what to write back to him on Monday.**"

---

"**I am going to say no,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to give you the reason before you ask me for a second thing, and then I am going to go and do the one thing I am going to do, which you are not going to like either, and then you can go home and be as unpleasant as you like about both of them and I will not have said a word against you.

"**The reason is a sentence of ours. It is the best sentence in that book and it has been in the first column in my own hand since the second of May and it goes: *a finding is not a conclusion.* A return states a fact and not a conclusion. I could not use it a fortnight ago in a room and I have thought about that a great deal since and I have got to the bottom of it, and here is the bottom: it is no use in a room because a room wants conclusions, and it is the only sentence I have got for a doorway, and a doorway is what you are standing in.

"**You are asking me to certify. A certification is a conclusion with somebody's name under it, and you do not want a figure, you have got a figure, it is on a sheet in a city and you have it in your coat. You want a man. And I am not going to be that man on a figure of my own for four reasons, and I am going to say all four and then stop.

"**One. It was made once, on the second of May, by one man with a hand lead's line and a slate, in daylight, in about a quarter of an hour. You may tell your man in the city that a hand lead's line going over a side and coming back four times is a thing a man in a boat can check and that nobody in this harbor checked it that day except the two of us.

"**Two. There is a man of fifty-four who has made two and a half on that same bar four times in nineteen years, and both figures are in a press in a city, and I am not going to tell a city which of them is right, and I would be telling him a different thing if I put my name under one of them, and he would be right to act on it and I would be wrong to have said it.

"**Three. A figure is a thing about water. Your man's loss is a thing about a boat and a day and a cargo of eleven hundred tons, and a figure about water is not an answer to a question about a boat, and if I put my name under it I will be a man in a city saying that a boat was or was not on that bar, and I have not been in that water and neither has he.

"**And four. It is in print already, and I did that on purpose, and I have not taken it back and I am not going to, and a printed thing cannot be certified by a man afterwards without turning it into a different kind of thing, which is a thing with a man's name on it, and I have found out what my name is worth this month and it is a sheet of paper at a shilling for forty copies.**"

---

**And then he did the one thing, and it was at the head of the dock at about half past twelve on the Monday afternoon, in daylight, in about forty people's hearing, and it is the thing this yard has been telling this harbor since the third of May that a wall is for.**

He took the book out of the bag on the boards and he read it out off the page in his own hand, and he read it out twice, because a man who has put a figure in the air owes the people standing in front of it the whole thing twice.

"**Here is the whole of the bar of gravel out of this book's own page, and there is a reason against every line of it and I am reading the line and the reason.

"**The bar was sounded on the second day of May instant, on the flood, at twenty minutes past two in the afternoon, at the top of a spring tide, by two men, one of them in this yard's boat with a hand lead's line made in this yard's own net loft and the other of them on the boards with a slate and a pencil, in about a quarter of an hour. The lead went over and came back four times and the reading was two and three-quarters of a fathom in the middle. On the ebb of the same day, at about forty minutes past six, the same two men, the same lead, the same slate, the reading was two and a quarter.

"**That is the figure. It is the yard's own, made once, in this yard's own hand, on a day with a date on it, and the reason it is entered in this column with a reason against the line is that a man in a boat can check the water and cannot check the number, and it is here so that anybody who was not in that boat that afternoon knows exactly what did and did not happen.

"**And I am not going to add a word to it, and I am not going to say the word *soundings*, and I am not going to call it a survey, and there is no instrument of this party that could be produced in a city in October and laid on a table.**"

Nobody in that market said anything. A man of the third lay said *that is the whole of it* to the man beside him, and the man beside him said *that is the whole of it.* That is what a read-out is for and about forty people heard it and two of them said the sentence afterwards and the rest of them went to work.

**And it did not do Tranter one thing.** He stood at the edge of the boards and heard all of it and it was not a certificate and it was not going to be one, and when Calder Marr came down off the boards he said the thing that is in the second column of this yard's own book and was read back to him at nine o'clock that night.

"**That was a figure,**" said Wil Tranter. "**That was a good figure and about forty men have just heard it and I have got nothing.**"

"**You have got nothing and I have not lied to you,**" said Calder Marr. "**Those are the same fact and I would rather have it that way round than the other.**"

---

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Monday the twenty-fifth day of September instant a man of the first lay named Wil Tranter came up this hill at about half past ten in the morning with a paper of his own, which is a report of a marine surveyor of a city upon the total loss of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons, and that this party read that paper out in the yard in about four minutes and gave it back; and that the said paper records that the surveyor has applied in writing to this party for the figure of the bar of gravel and has had no answer, and records this party as declining to certify it, and records on the face of that entry that she has not had it from this party; and that a letter from that city came into this yard in the ordinary post and is in that letter box and was not answered on the day it came and has not been answered since, and that this party gave that answer in a doorway at about half past ten on the instant and is not going to put it in a letter either, and has entered why: that a man who asks a yard to stand behind a number in a city is asking for a name under it, and the answer to a question like that is given to a man in his own mouth and not on paper; and that the said paper was not sent to this party by anybody and came into this harbor in the ordinary way in a coat pocket, and that this party has entered that it does not know and has not asked and is not going to guess who sent it.*

*And that the said man asked this party to certify the figure of the bar of gravel for use in a paper of his own, and that this party refused, and that the refusal is entered here with the reason against the line, and the reason is a working one: that a certification is a conclusion with a man's name under it and the figure is already in print in a city and this party cannot un-print it; that the figure was made once, on the second of May instant, by two men, one in a boat with a hand lead's line and one on the boards with a slate, in about a quarter of an hour, and that a man in a boat can check the water and cannot check the number; that there is a figure of two and a half for the same bar made four times in nineteen years by a man of fifty-four and that both figures are in a press in a city and that this party is not going to tell a city which of them is right; that a figure about water is not an answer to a question about a boat and a cargo of eleven hundred tons; and that this party put the figure in print on purpose on the twenty-fifth of August and is not taking it back.*

*And that on the Monday instant, at about half past twelve in the afternoon, at the head of this dock, in daylight, in about forty people's hearing, the holder of the writ of the first of May read out of this yard's own book the entry for the bar of gravel, the place, the hour, the lead, the slate, the four times over, the flood and the ebb, together with the reason against the line, and read the whole of it twice; and that nobody in this party asked him to and that he had entered in advance that he was going to do it, and that two men of that market said afterwards to each other that it was the whole of it, and that this party entered that it did nothing for the man who asked for it and did not lie to him, and that those are one fact.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the twenty-fifth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this yard has spent five months telling this harbor that the only thing which makes a wall worth reading is that somebody can be brought up in front of it and made to answer, and that on the twenty-fifth instant about forty people heard this yard's own figure read out of this yard's own book in daylight at the head of this dock, and that it answered for the water and it answered for the number and it answered for the day and the hour and the four times over, and that it did not do one single thing for the man who came up the hill to be helped by it, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a market can be shown a figure and cannot be given a name; and that the thing this yard has been selling since the third of May is figures, and that a figure is the cheapest thing in this harbor and is not the thing anybody is short of.*

---

**And on Tuesday the twenty-sixth the wage of four marks went in at about eleven in the morning, into an envelope, in the holder's own left hand, with the week on the outside of it.**

Twenty lines and eighty marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. **A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence in the bag.** Fifteen envelopes on a bench in the chest, and two weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August and the fifth of September, and the two figures are different figures and neither of them is the other.

*A wage of four on the twelfth of September was not paid. A wage of four on the nineteenth was paid. A wage of four on the twenty-sixth was paid. Two days of work in three weeks, the fourteenth at three marks and the twenty-second at five, and eight marks still in nobody's hand for the fifteenth of August and the fifth of September, entered with the figure and the date against it and entered once.*

**No money came into this yard on the Monday and none went out, and a wage of four came out of the bag on the Tuesday into an envelope on a bench, and that is the whole of what moved in two days. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written. Five marks of compressor's gland on it, unpaid, useless. Eight marks and a day of a boat to the bellwright, unargued. Seven pence to the shipwright. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved since the eighth of August, and he is not named in that book again. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The cistern behind the engine house is charged on a rota and this party has not walked it since the third of August. And the plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and was not reached, and the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January is refused, and this party has not written a better letter and is not going to.**
