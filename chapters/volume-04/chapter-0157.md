# Chapter 157

*The First of September Is a Friday*

Friday the first and Saturday the second of September, at the ninth seam's mooring, at the mole's toe, in daylight, and in the shed.

**The end of it on the ninth seam's mooring was the first of September and the first of September is a Friday, and the end of it arrived, and what arrived was a working answer and not a decision.**

The two of them read a figure out of the yard's own book in the shed at about half past seven that morning before anybody went down to the water, because the bag is what it is and a man who is going out on a mole at four feet on a spring tide is entitled to know what a week of this yard costs.

A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag. **Eighteen lines and seventy-two marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, the eighteenth line entered on Tuesday the twenty-ninth of August out of the money of Monday the twenty-eighth, in an envelope, in the holder's own left hand, with the week on the outside of it, and there is no day set on it in this fortnight, and it is a line in this book and not a scene.** Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for a day of work on the eighth, with a date on it, in the first column, not a shilling of it paid, and the reason against the line. **Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter on the twentieth of August, out of her own pocket and not out of that bag, and not paid.** **Thirteen envelopes on a bench in the chest, and one week fallen due this year with no envelope standing against it, which is the week of Tuesday the fifteenth of August, and the two figures are different figures and neither of them is the other.** Five marks of compressor's gland on a page that has not been re-written, unpaid, and of no use whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, three counts of weeks unmoved since the fourth of January. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. **And no wage falls on this Friday, because a wage falls on a Tuesday and no Tuesday has come.**

---

**They were off the crown at about ten past eight with a pole, a lead line, a rule and a lantern that was not lit, and the tide was four feet and making, and the two men who were already out there had been out there since about half past seven.**

Tam Rundle was thirty and of the tide gang, which is four men short of what it was, and Danno Fisk was of the third lay and is the man who stood in a doorway on the sixteenth of August and gave his name and the hour about three sheets coming off a market wall. Neither of them is of this party. Neither of them was asked to be there. Neither of them was paid.

**And Calder Marr knew the tide-gang man's name off the head of a page before he knew the face, because it has been at the head of that page in the ship's own hand since the twenty-ninth of July, with an hour against it, on the footing that any man in this harbor can come up this hill and ask you whether you said it — nine days of a pump heard at half past eight from a mole's crown, and said to nobody in his own office, and then said out loud on a crown in front of three people because the shipwright asked him his name. The yard has not asked him one word about any of that since and is not going to, and neither of them said anything about it on the Friday morning, and that is the whole of what passed between them about it.**

They were standing in about four feet on the mooring stones of the ninth seam with a pole in one of them and a lead line in the other, and they had been out to that mooring on four days in the month of August on their own time, and the reason they had was written in a book in a municipal office in a hand that was not theirs.

"**The third of July,**" said Tam Rundle. "**It is in the book and it is in the officer's own hand and it says the end of it is the first of September. That is the whole of it. That is the whole reason there are two of us standing in this water at half past seven in the morning on a Friday.

"**We walked out to it on the sixth, on the fourteenth, on the twenty-first and on the twenty-eighth, and on the twenty-eighth we came out here and put the shackle back on the ring and took a turn on the eye and put the seizing back on with a maul and did about forty minutes, and we did it on our own time, and we did not ask that office for one penny and we are not going to, and that office did not tell us to and would not have known we came.**"

"**The ground of it is sand and shell,**" said Danno Fisk. "**And the mooring is a ring and four stones and a length of chain and it was never in a good state, and it has been out of service since the first of May with a date on it, and what we have done is not a repair. It is a man taking a ring off a stone, scraping it, and putting it back. If you want a word for it, the word is *looked at*. It is looked at. That is the whole of the difference between the first of May and this morning.**"

The lead went down at about twenty past nine. Calder Marr took the rule and put it on the ring and read it and wrote it on the slate and read it back.

"**Four foot one on the stone and the ring is home and the chain is home and the seizing is sound and it is bedded in sand and shell that has not moved since the twenty-eighth of August,**" he said. "**It is in service. I am not signing for it and I have not been asked to sign for it, and the man whose book it is has not been asked to do anything, and if anybody later on wants to know when this mooring was last looked at, the answer is the first of September and the answer came from two men who were not paid and were not asked.**"

---

**And the clerk of the harbor authority was on the steps at about eleven and said what a box holds, and this was the fourth time this year and he did not pretend it was a new thing.**

"**This office entered an end of it against that mooring on Monday the third of July in the officer's own hand and the end of it was the first of September, and that is what my box holds and it is all it holds. My box does not hold a repair because there has not been a repair and I am not going to write one down. It does not hold two men. It holds a date and an end of it.

"**And the repair account of this office has been empty since the middle of February and has not been filled this year and is not going to be filled, and there is nobody in this harbor who is going to be surprised by that and I am tired of saying it. The light on the mole head is not on for the same reason and will not be until somebody buys oil.

"**A man in a boat can check a mooring. I have said it four times now and I am not going to say it a fifth time in a market, and the reason I am not is that four is enough and the fifth one would only be me being interesting.**"

---

**And on the Friday afternoon Danno Fisk came up the hill and put the other half of it in the shed himself, with his name against it, and asked for nothing, and did not want a thing off anybody.**

"**I read that end of it off a wall on the third of July,**" he said. "**Not the book. The wall. Your own list of things, the one that has been up since the third of May, had a line on it that said the ninth seam was out of service with an end entered against it and I read the line and I thought: that is a date a man can be behind. Because a date is not a length of time, it is a day, and a day arrives.

"**And I told two other men about it in a market on the Wednesday and neither of them said anything, and about a week after that one of them mentioned a mooring that had been out since May and I said the end of it is the first of September, and he said, *right,* and that was the end of the conversation and we had a pint.

"**I am not telling you that because I want something out of it and I am not telling you because I did you a favor, because I did not do it for you. I read it off your wall and I used it for me and for two men I drink with. That is all that happened.

"**And I am not going to be thanked for it, and if anybody on this hill tries to thank me I am going to be unpleasant about it, and I have said that in advance so that nobody has to be awkward. A man who does a thing with a number on it that you printed with your own name on it is not doing you a favor. He is using your sheet. It happens to have been right.**"

---

**And the party entered the whole of it in the first column on the Saturday night at about nine, with the hour, and the two names against it, and then it entered a second thing underneath in a different mood, and the second thing is the one that is against this yard's own interest.**

*That on Friday the first of September instant, being the first day of that month and a Friday, the mooring at the ninth seam of this mole was found in service at about twenty past nine in the morning by a tide-gang man and a man of the third lay, with a pole and a lead line and a rule, in about four feet of water on the flood, and that it was found in service because two men had walked out to it on their own time on the sixth, fourteenth, twenty-first and twenty-eighth days of August and had scraped a ring off a stone and put it back and re-seized it on the twenty-eighth; and that the reason those two men were out there at all is an end of it entered in the book of this harbor authority on Monday the third of July in the officer's own hand, being the first of September; and that the names of the two men are against this line and neither of them is of this party and neither of them has asked this party for anything; and that this office of the harbor authority did not order it, did not know of it, and did not pay for it, and its repair account has been empty since the middle of February and was not filled; and that this party did not pay either man and did not ask either man and was not there.*

*And that this party is entered against its own interest, in the first column and with a date, and the reason is on the page: that this party has been telling this harbor since the third of May that the only thing that makes a wall worth reading is that somebody can be asked a question about it, and that on the first of September a man in this harbor was asked a question about this party's wall by nobody, answered it out loud, in a market, to two men drinking, and two strangers then walked out to a mole four times on their own time and did a thing that was not a repair; and that this party is not going to claim it, and is not going to be thanked for it, and does not want to be, and has not been told that it was thanked for it by anybody.*

*And that the reason this party was not there on the first of September is a working reason and is on the page: that a yard of two and a half people which has spent a fortnight being asked for a book by an office cannot be on a mole at four feet at the top of a spring flood on a Friday morning, and has said that about itself before and is saying it again, and that a reason of that kind is true and is also the reason a thing gets done by somebody else, and both of those are on this line and neither of them cancels the other.*

**The mooring is not on the list of costs any more. That list is read out at the head of this dock in nineteen days' time in the same words it is always read out, and one line of it is going to be different, and this party has entered that it will be different and has not asked anybody to notice it.**

**And the two of them did not discuss it. That is in the book's own account of the day and not in the yard's, and the reason is hers and she gave it in one sentence at about ten past four on the Friday afternoon on the mole's crown with the light off behind them, and the not-discussing of it is the closest these two have come in four volumes and neither of them is going to make anything of it.**

**No money moved on the Friday or on the Saturday. A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence is in the bag and it is the whole of it, and the two men who went out onto that mooring four times in August were paid nothing by anybody, and the north berth is still out, and the tide gang is still four men short, and the mole-head light is not on, and the cistern is charged on a rota on a Monday and a Thursday and this party has walked it thirteen times since the end of May at no figure and has not walked it since the third of August, and the cistern behind the engine house has held since the nineteenth of June.**

**And on Sunday the third a conservator of no office will be in the market office at about ten in the morning, because that room is open to anybody on a Tuesday and a Friday morning and this party did not arrange it and has not asked her for anything since the twenty-third of July. The next such morning is Friday the eighth. And on Monday the fourth the wall behind the reeve's bench comes down at eight and comes down bare, and that is the eighteenth Monday since the first of May.**
