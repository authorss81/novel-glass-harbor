# Chapter 192

*Three Instruments on a Bench, and a Room That Is Not Free*

Wednesday the eighteenth and Thursday the nineteenth of November, in the shed, and on the mole's steps.

**There was no day of work booked in either of these two days and the board was not written and nobody came up the hill with a day in his mouth, and this party entered that on the Wednesday, with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and the only way it gets into a book that can be read in a room in four months is by somebody writing it down on the day.**

---

**And at about ten in the morning on the Wednesday, with the pump on the bench and the slate beside it, the ship's hand put three things on the bench where the holder could see them, and none of them was glass and none of them was a tool.**

A sheet of this yard's own paper, folded once. A sheet of the same yard's own paper, folded once. And a sheet of nothing at all, which was the absence of a third thing and was put down so that the other two would be looked at.

"**A page in this shed,**" she said. "**A page in a market. A page in a room in a city. And I have been up since five and I have not come down off it and I am going to say the whole of it now and you are not going to improve on any of it.**

"**The shed. A page in this shed is read by the man who wrote it and by one other person, and one of us has a bone in her forearm and the other one is the holder, and a page in this shed is worth what two men in one room are worth. It is the instrument this yard has been building since the third of May and it is the only one of the three that a man in a market can be shown and cannot be given.**

"**The market. About four hundred people can read a page in a market. It is the only one of the three that anybody in this town can argue with, and it is the only one of the three that can be taken down by any man with a knife, and this yard put a sheet on that wall on the twenty-fifth of August on the same nails everything goes up on, on the outside of whatever was there, and nothing has been nailed over it since and it has not been asked about it since.

"**And the room. A page in a room in a city goes on a record and cannot be got off. And we have had one of those and I have read it, and I know exactly what is in it and I know exactly what is not in it, and what is not in it is every reason this yard has ever written.**"

"**Say the last part again,**" said Calder Marr, "**because I want to hear where you are going with it and I would rather hear it than guess at it.**"

"**I am going to say the thing neither of us has been saying since the sixth of June.**" She did not move her hand off the pump. "**This yard has spent four volumes saying a reason does not travel. And a reason does not travel. And a page in a market is the only one of these three that arrives with its reasons on it, because a reason can be read off the same sheet by four hundred people at the same time, and four hundred people can say that a yard wrote *I do not know* and gave a reason for it, and that is a different thing from a clerk's line.**

"**And the reason I am not going to let us have it is that the same wall is the same wall, and there are four people of this coast alive in this town with no boat and about nine marks between them who wrote a line and signed it, and that line is a reason, and a reason on a market wall is a reason in a market.**"

Nobody said anything for a while.

"**Then we do not put it on a wall,**" said Calder Marr.

"**No. And I want the reason for that in the shed tonight and not in a market in a fortnight, and the reason is that we are not a party that decides who is in the room. That limit is a limit they wrote, and we have not asked to have it lifted once in three months and we are not going to ask twice, and if this yard cannot put a sheet on a wall without deciding who is standing in front of it, then this yard is not going to put a sheet on a wall.**"

"**And the third thing,**" said Calder Marr. "**Say it out, because you have had your hand on it since you put it down.**"

"**The room.**" She picked the third thing up, which was nothing, and put it back down. "**And here is the whole of the price, and I have been carrying it since Saturday. A page in a room goes on a record and cannot be got off. Which means that on Thursday, in that room, whatever is read out goes onto a page in a city whether this yard is in the room or not, and it goes without the reasons, and we already know what happens to a line without its reason because it happened to five of them in October and a man in a city is going to read them in four months.**

"**So the room is the one that costs us. And I am for the room. And I want it written down tonight that I am for it and that I know what it costs, because in about four years one of us is going to have to say why this yard put a true thing in front of a clerk, and I do not want it to be that nobody could say.**"

---

**And on Thursday the nineteenth of November, on the rota day, at about ten in the morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and this party has walked none of them and the walk is nil and stays nil.**

**Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Thursday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Wednesday at any hour.**

---

**And at about eleven on that Thursday morning, in the open air, at the foot of the mole's steps, with the market coming in behind them and about eleven people in the street, the holder of the writ of the first of May asked the officer of the harbor authority of this place for a room and a day, and asked him for it in the open, and did not go into his office to ask for it.**

"**I am going to say what I am not asking for first, because in about four years somebody is going to read that a yard went up a hill in November and I would rather the record had the order right.

"**I am not asking your office for an instrument. There are eight acknowledgements in that ledger and the ninth went in on the twenty-eighth of August at this party's asking and it is spent, and a tenth may not be entered, and this party is not going to ask for the ninth again and is not going to ask for the tenth. A room is not an instrument. A day is not an instrument. I am asking for a room and a day and I want that in a book and I want it in your own hand and not in mine.

"**And I am not asking you to seat anybody. The thing that can seat one of two disputants is the inquiry's and not the writ's, and they are two instruments and they have never been the same instrument and I am not going to be the man in a market who calls them the same. And I know that your office cannot certify a glass witness and cannot overrule a Registry seizure and cannot renew the writ, and I am not asking it to do any of the three.**"

"**A room and a day,**" said Ors Kest. "**That is all of it.**"

"**That is all of it.**"

"**Then I am going to say the price before I say yes, because I have been made to do this in front of people about four times this year and every time I have said yes first it has cost somebody who was not standing here.**"

He turned round and looked at the steps above the two of them, where nine or ten people had stopped.

"**The room is over the tide-gang store. It has been over the tide-gang store since before I came to this office. If there is a sitting in that room on a Thursday, the store is shut, and the men who count the catch in it are in another building, and one of them loses his Thursday morning, and the tide gang of this harbor is four men short and has been four men short since before I came, and it will be four men short at the first of March.**

"**That is the price. It is one morning of one man who is not in your yard and not in my office and has not been asked about this and does not know it is happening on Thursday. I am not going to name him to you because the moment I name him he is a man in this business.**"

"**Say yes or do not,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Yes.**" The reeve took the book out from under his arm and did not open it. "**And I am going to say three more things and none of them is a favor.

"**One. That ledger is spent. I have nothing left to put in it. There is no line I can enter this week that would mean anything to anybody in March, and I have known that since a Tuesday in June and I have said it out loud on a mole and I am not going to keep saying it on a mole.

"**Two. My office cannot pay for a clerk's day. There has been nothing in the repair account of this office since the middle of February and there is not going to be anything in it this winter. So the clerk of that room will enter what goes in that room in his own hours at his own table, and this office is going to owe him a day, and the debt is entered against this office and not against your party, and I would rather tell you that on a Thursday than have it found out in a market in March.

"**And three. I am not a friend of yours. I have never said I was and I am not going to start on a Thursday in November, and if you need a man in this harbor to be on your side then you have counted me twice and you should take one of them out.**"

"**Then I will take none of them out,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I will enter on the page tonight that a man of forty-four said in the open that he is not a friend of this party and that this party agrees with him, and that a reeve who has nothing left to put in his book is not a man who is going to be asked twice.**"

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the nineteenth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that no day of work was booked on Wednesday the eighteenth or on Thursday the nineteenth instant and that the board was not written and that nobody came up this hill with a day in his mouth on either day, and that the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened; and that on the said Thursday at about eleven in the morning, in the open air, at the foot of the mole's steps, in about eleven people's hearing, the holder of the writ of the first of May instant asked the officer of the harbor authority of this place for a room and a day; and that he asked in the open and did not go into that office to ask, and that he said first that he was not asking for an instrument, that there are eight acknowledgements in that office's ledger and the ninth was entered on the twenty-eighth day of August past at this party's asking and is spent and a tenth may not be entered, and that this party did not ask for the ninth again and did not ask for the tenth; and that he said that a room is not an instrument and a day is not an instrument; and that he said that he was not asking that office to seat anybody, that the power to seat one of two disputants belongs to the inquiry and not to the writ of the first of May instant and that the two instruments are not the same instrument; and that he said that he knows that office cannot certify a glass witness, cannot overrule a Registry seizure and cannot renew the writ of the first of May, and that he did not ask it to do any of the three.*

*And that the said officer said that the room is over the tide-gang store and that a sitting in it on a day shuts the store for that day; and that before he answered, and in about eleven people's hearing, he named the price, which is that one man of the tide gang of this harbor loses his Thursday morning, and that the tide gang is four men short and has been since before that officer came to the office and will be four men short at the first of March next; and that he did not name the man and gave the reason, which is that the moment he names him he is a man in this business; and that the said officer then said yes.*

*And that the said officer said three further things in the open, and that they are entered here in his own words and were read back to him in this shed by the ship's hand on the said evening and that he said they were his own words and did not improve on them; and that the first is that the ledger of that office is spent and that there is no line the officer can enter this week that would mean anything to anybody in the first of March and that he has known that since a Tuesday in June and has said it out loud on a mole; and that the second is that that office cannot pay for a clerk's day, that there has been nothing in the repair account of that office since the middle of February and there is not going to be anything in it this winter, that the clerk of that room will enter what goes in that room in his own hours at his own table, and that the office is going to owe him a day and the debt is entered against that office and not against this party; and that the third is that the said officer is not a friend of this party, that he has never said he was and is not going to begin on a Thursday in November, and that if this party needs a man in this harbor to be on its side then it has counted him twice and should take one of them out.*

*And that the holder answered in the open that this party will take none of them out and will enter on this page that a man of forty-four said in the open that he is not a friend of this party, and that this party agrees with him; and that neither brother went over to the other at the far bench on the said Thursday and that the said man was not at that bench at any hour on the Wednesday.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the nineteenth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand put three things on a bench on the morning of Wednesday the eighteenth instant, being a page in a shed, a page in a market and a page in a room in a city, and said that the first is worth what two men in one room are worth, that the second can be read by about four hundred people and can be taken down by any man with a knife, and that the third goes on a record and cannot be got off; and that she said that this yard has said for four volumes that a reason does not travel, and that a reason does not travel, and that a page in a market is the only one of the three that arrives with its reasons on it because a reason read off one sheet by four hundred people at once is a different thing from a clerk's line; and that she said that she is not going to let this yard have it, and that the reason is that four people of this coast alive in this town wrote a line and signed it and that a line is a reason, and that a reason on a market wall is a reason in a market; and that the holder said then we do not put it on a wall, and that she said yes and gave the reason in the yard's own words; and that she said that she is for the room and that she wants it written down that she knows what the room costs.*

*And that the holder entered on the nineteenth instant that this party has decided to put a copy of a record into a room and not into a market, and that the decision is this party's own and was made in this shed and not by any office, and that the reason against the line is that a page in a market is the only one of the three instruments that arrives with its reasons on it, and that a page in a room arrives without them, and that this yard has had five of its own lines go into a record in a city without the reason against any of them and knows what that is worth, and that the yard has chosen the instrument that costs it because the other two cost four people of this coast a thing they wrote themselves; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a decision is worth in four years to the four people it does not touch is not checkable from a mole, and that is the whole of what this yard could not put in a market if it put it in a market.*

*And that the holder entered on the nineteenth instant that a matter is not going into that room, and that the list of it is entered in the first column of the twenty-second instant, and that the reason against this line is that the list itself is not a thing that can go into a room and is not going to be tried.*

**No money came into this yard on the Wednesday and none on the Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and one mark and six shillings and a penny is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the twenty-fourth instant. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and nobody has been appointed to that column. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid. Twenty-five lines and one hundred marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, nineteen envelopes on a bench, five weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November and the seventeenth of November instant. A man of the tide gang of this harbor is a morning down for the want of a room over his store, and this party did not ask for that man and does not know his name and has not asked. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and fifty-nine days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the nineteenth instant and this party walked it, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it, and this party is not going to stand in a street and tell anybody what the two of those make between them, because it did not see the days those men come on. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short, and is four men short on the day this yard took a morning out of it. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and no occasion was entered in these two days and no measurement was taken, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the live question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and did not ask in these two days.**
