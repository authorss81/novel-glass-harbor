# Chapter 152

*A Form That Does Not Mention the Second Season*

Saturday the nineteenth and Sunday the twentieth of August, in the shed, on the mole's crown above the third seam's mark, and at the head of the dock.

**A clerk of the circuit office came up the hill at about ten in the morning on the Saturday with a form in his own hand and his own page under his arm, and he did not go up to the shed, and he stood in the yard and read it out at the top of his voice so that a man on the boards could hear it.**

He gave his name before he read it, which nobody had asked him to do. Ambrose Sell, a clerk of the circuit office, and he said the office out loud, and he said that he was the mouth of a clause and not a man with anything of his own in this, and that he had said that in a room in this harbor in the month of June and had it written down where it was written down.

"**I am going to read a form and then I am going to go down the hill, and I am not going to sit down, and I am not going to take anything, and if you want to write any of it down you write it while I am reading it and not after, because after I am gone you will get it right and I will have said it wrong.**"

---

> *That a formal inquiry is opened into certain matters touching the working of a salvage concern at Morrow Quay between the first day of May instant and the fifteenth day of August instant.*
>
> *That the parties named in the said inquiry are four, and are those four whose names are on a certain provisional writ of salvage working granted at this harbor on the first day of May instant.*
>
> *That the party is required to produce its crew's book, and the returns made under a reporting clause, at a sitting to be appointed.*
>
> *That nothing in this form touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the said writ.*

"**That is the whole of it,**" said Ambrose Sell. "**Four lines and no more, and I have not got authority to add a line to it and I would not add one if I had.**

"**And I am going to say the two things about those four lines that I am the clerk of and not the man who wrote them.

"**The third line. The day of the finding is not counted and a return is due on the fourth day after it, and that is the clause's own rule and it has been the clause's own rule since the first of May, and there are four returns and I have a copy of the index and not one of them was late except the first, which was two days late, and which was about a lump in a bucket and not about a bucket anybody can check today. And I am going to say, because I say what a box holds and this is what my box holds, that nobody in this harbor has asked me for a fifth and that a return is a fact and not a conclusion, and that this office has not had to ask this party for one of them and is not asking for one now.**"

He turned the sheet over and looked at the back of it, which was blank, and turned it back.

"**The fourth line. I have read it out word for word and I have read it out slowly, and it says that nothing in this form touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the said writ. I want that taken down exactly like that and I want both halves of it taken down. It does not grant it. It does not refuse it. And I am going to tell you, because I am a clerk and it is my job, that I have read this form four times today on a walk and there is nothing behind those four words. It is not a soft refusal dressed as a silence. There is no paper behind this one. Whoever drafted it has left that out on purpose, because the last line of your own writ says you will be told when it has been decided and not before, and a form that said it had been decided either way would be telling you in a way your writ forbids anybody to tell you in.

"**So the second season is a named absence and this form does not decide it, and the only thing in this harbor that could decide it is not in this form, and I am not going to guess what is.**"

---

**And then he said the thing that was not the mouth of a clause, in the doorway, unasked, with the form already folded.**

He was on the step and Calder Marr was inside and he turned round to go and said it over his shoulder, the way a man says a thing he has decided to say and is not going to make a ceremony of.

"**I will tell you why it came. I am not telling you it came because of you, because I do not know that and I am not going to write it down that I know it. I am telling you what a clerk in my position can see from a piece of paper and a file, and it is this.

"**A yard that has put a sheet of its own figures on a public wall in a working harbor has told the office that keeps the page that those figures are checkable. That is what publishing is. You cannot put nine places on a wall in about sixty boats and one of them in a city at a press and have it be a thing that only means something to you. You have handed somebody a question and you cannot take the question back, and neither can I, and I am a clerk and I keep a page and my page has had your nine places on it since the twenty-second of July and it is going to have them on it after every one of us here is dead.

"**An office cannot un-ask a question that a party has answered in public. I have no power to do that and I have never wanted one. I have a form and four lines and a room in this harbor that is not mine and a day that is not appointed, and I have been up that hill nine times this year and I have never once come up it to be pleasant to anybody.**"

Then he read it back to himself in his own hand on his own page, once, and went down the hill, and the hill was steep and he went slowly.

---

**And the yard did not refuse the form and did not sign anything on the step, and it asked for one thing, and it got it.**

"**The room,**" said Calder Marr. "**And the day.**"

"**The room is the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin. It is that room because it is the room this office has used since April and I have a key to it and the key has not changed hands since the spring. There is no other room in this harbor with a table in it that anybody can be got into.**"

"**And the day.**"

"**There is no day,**" said Ambrose Sell. "**I have said what a box holds and my box holds a sitting to be appointed. I do not know what day it will be and I am not going to guess at you about it, because a man who is given a guess writes the guess down. When a day is appointed you will get a paper naming it and it will be served on you and not left with anybody, because I was told to serve it and not to leave it, and I will read you the paragraph that gives this office the power to seat one of two disputants and take such finding as it is given, without prejudice to any claim, and that is not mine and it is in your form and I have not read it to you today because it is not in the four lines I brought up the hill.

"**That is the whole of it and I have given you more of it in a doorway than the form had in it, and you did not ask me for it, and I am going to say why I gave it. Because in about a year there is going to be a page in a file in a city with your name at the top of it and somebody in that office is going to have to write a sentence about how the form came to be in front of you, and I would rather the sentence was that a clerk told them the room and the truth about the day than that nobody wrote one.**"

---

**And the whole of that went into the first column on the Sunday night with the hour against it, and the shipwright read it back and he read it back after her.**

*That on Saturday the nineteenth of August instant, at about ten in the morning, a clerk of the circuit office named Ambrose Sell came up this hill with a form in his own hand, gave his name and the name of his office before reading it, stated that he was the mouth of a clause and not a man with anything of his own in it, read the four lines of it out at the top of his voice in the yard and not in the shed, gave them the hour, and went down the hill without sitting down and without taking anything.*

*That the said form opens a formal inquiry into certain matters touching the working of a salvage concern at this harbor between the first of May and the fifteenth of August instant; that the parties named are four and are the four whose names are upon the writ of the first of May; that this party is required to produce its crew's book and the returns made under a reporting clause at a sitting to be appointed; and that nothing in the form touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the said writ.*

*And that this party has entered both halves of the last of those, that the form does not grant the second season and does not refuse it, and that a paper that does not mention a thing is not a decision about the thing, and that the second season of the writ of the first of May is a named absence and stands as one, and that the writ runs to midnight on the first of March and is not the Second Bell license and is not a charter and renews nothing and its ground is not widened and its four names are four and no fifth has been added.*

*And that the said clerk entered, of his own motion and in his own words and not being asked, that a yard which has put a sheet of its own figures on a public wall in a working harbor has told the office that keeps the page that those figures are checkable, and that an office cannot un-ask a question that a party has answered in public; and that this party has entered that it does not know whether the form came because of the sheet of the fifteenth or for any other reason, and has entered that the clerk did not know either, and has entered that neither of those is going to be improved on in this book.*

*And that the said clerk gave this party the room, which is the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and stated that no day is appointed, that he does not know what day it will be, that he is not going to guess at this party about it because a man who is given a guess writes the guess down, and that when a day is appointed a paper will be served on this party and not left with anybody, and that there is in that paper a power to seat one of two disputants and take such finding as it is given without prejudice to any claim, which is not the clerk's and which was not in the four lines he brought up the hill and which this party has not seen.*

*And that this party did not refuse the form and did not sign anything on the step and did not go to the office from which the form came, and has entered why it did not go: that the channel this party knows of is the writ of the first of May, and that the writ is about the second season, and that this party is not going to go up a hill in this harbor to a second door because the first one was open on the twelfth of August.*

---

**And in the shed on the Sunday morning the two of them argued about one thing for about forty minutes, and it is the only argument of that week, and the shipwright won it in nine words.**

"**Then say out loud what you are going to say when they ask you for the book,**" said Nessa Pike. "**You have been standing in this shed since Saturday with a look on you like a man about to read a list. I have watched you do it. You are going to go in there and you are going to take the book and you are going to open it at the second column and you are going to read the second column out to a man with a pen.**

"**Are you going to do it.**"

"**I do not know.**"

"**Then you have told me the answer and you have taken about two minutes to get to it and you took the forty minutes on the way.**"

She put her hand flat on the bench, the way she does when she is not going to improve on a thing.

"**Here is the nine words and then I am going to make the tea. A column is not a defense of anything and was never one. That is what you wrote in the first column in a hearing room in this harbor in April and it is the only thing in that book that has ever been true of the second column. It is not a wall you stand behind. It is a page where a man writes down what he cannot check and puts a reason against it so that a person reading it in four years can see at once that a man knew he was writing something he could not check. If you read it out in a room, a man with a pen will write down the lines and not the reasons, because reasons are long. You will hand that man about five lines of a page of things neither of you can check and he will use them as five facts, and in four years somebody will read them in a file and think this yard knew things and would not say them.

"**And if you do not read it out, then the man in that room has a book with a page he cannot get at, and he will find that out, and he will not know what is on the page, and that is a different thing from a man in a room knowing what is on the page and not being able to do anything about it. I do not know which of those two is worse for us. I have been turning that over since Saturday and I have got to the end of it and the end of it is a column and not a defense, and you can write that in the shed tonight and you can put it in the shed and not on a wall, because a shed is two people and a wall is forty.**"

"**Then put it in the shed,**" said Calder Marr, "**and not on a wall.**"

"**That is what I said.**"

---

**And on the Sunday afternoon they went up onto the mole's crown above the third seam's mark, in daylight, with the wind in the west about three hours of it, and they stood where they have stood four times this year, and neither of them touched anything.**

The two objects of nobody's were about nine feet apart under two tarpaulins, and one of the tarpaulins had had a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and it had not been lashed back, and neither object had moved in ninety-five days and neither moved that afternoon, and the marking on one of them is not identified and is not going to be. Clause two of the notice of the thirty-first of October keeps recovered transparent mineral out of every shed and that is the live reason those two things are living in the open air on a stone mole in a working harbor, and the notice is not lifted and the harbor authority has no power to lift it.

"**Ninety-four,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**Ninety-five,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then write ninety-five, and write that we did not move them, and write that we came up here to look at them, and write that we have come up here four times this year and have not once had anything to do with them and I would like that written down as a fact about this party and not as a thing we are proud of.**"

---

**And on the Sunday night, after the entry above, the holder wrote a second thing in the first column and read it back, and it is the only thing this party did on the twentieth of August that was not about a form.**

*That on Sunday the twentieth of August instant this party wrote to a circuit office in a city and posted the letter in the ordinary post at about seven in the evening, at seven pence of postage, and that the seven pence was not out of this party's bag, which held nothing on that day and had held nothing since the evening of Tuesday the eighth of August, and that the shipwright put the seven pence on the envelope out of her own pocket and said out loud that it was hers and not the yard's and that the yard would owe it to her and pay it when it had it; that seven pence is owing to the said woman with the twentieth of August against it, and is not paid and is not going to be paid this month, and the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill and ask her whether she has been given it, and can go and look, and there is no name of any kind in it but her own; that the record asked for is the record of the fourth return made by this party on Friday the fourteenth of July, a copy of which went into the harbor authority's ledger on that Friday morning in the clerk's own hand and a third copy of which went into a file in a circuit office in a city on the same morning in a jacket with the place and the hour and the keeper and the limits against the head of it; that this party has never seen that file and does not know what is in it and does not know what has been copied out of it; that this party is asking for a copy of a record of its own making and not for anything belonging to anybody else; that the reason it is asking is that a return is the only paper in this harbor that any office in any city is obliged to look at and that this party put it on a wall in a market on the twenty-second of July and cannot now see a copy of it anywhere except on that wall; and that the date is written on the envelope as well as in the letter, in this party's own hand, because a thing that has not been answered in a month is going to be a thing that somebody says was never asked.*

**Nothing came in and nothing went out of that yard's own bag on the Saturday or on the Sunday. The seven pence was the shipwright's and this yard owes it to her, and the bag is still nothing, and there is no figure in this book on either day.**

**And the wall behind the reeve's bench comes down at eight on Monday the twenty-first of August, which is the sixteenth Monday since the first of May, and it will come down bare.**
