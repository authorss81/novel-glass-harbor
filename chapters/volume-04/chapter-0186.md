# Chapter 186

*A Man at the Head of a Dock Who Did Not Say the Yard Was Wrong*

Sunday the first and Monday the second of November, at the head of the dock, at the mole's steps, and in the shed.

**On Sunday the first of November, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, a man of the first lay said out loud at the head of this dock that a yard which has been in a room and come out with a clerk saying a clerk could see what the man could see is a yard whose figures are worth what the clerk's are, and he said it to nobody in particular, and about eleven people heard it.**

Gwil Lasker, thirty-six, of the first lay, with a boat of about a hundred and twenty tons of his own and a man of forty on the second hand, who has crossed this bar on twenty-one springs and has never once said out loud that the yard's figure was wrong and is not going to.

"**I am not saying you are wrong,**" he said. "**I want that said first and I want it said by me and not by the man of the third lay who is going to say it in a week because he has been waiting since the ninth of July for something to be wrong with.

"**I am saying a man in a city is going to read two things on the same page. One of them is two and three-quarters of a fathom on the flood and two and a quarter on the ebb, made once, on the second of May, by a man in a shed. And the other one is that the same man stood in a room on the twenty-ninth of October and told a clerk that he could see a join in a sheet of paper and could not say what it meant, three times, and would not say it a fourth time in a different way.

"**And I do not know what I would do if I were that man with a book of my own. I would take the first figure off my page and leave the second one on, and I would do it in about four seconds, and I would not be lying when I did it.

"**And I have crossed this bar twenty-one times and I have a boat of a hundred and twenty tons, and I was twenty-two the first time and I took a sounding off it off a master's own figure at that master's own asking, and I was a foot and a half out in a slack water, and I did not tell him, and he had it himself inside a month off a man out of a city, and he has never said one word about it to me and I have never said one word about it to him, and that is the only figure of mine anybody has ever caught me out in, and I am telling you it in a street because I am not a man who is going to sit on a wall about anybody else's arithmetic."

"**So that is what a man at the head of a dock says. And I am going to say my name, because you are going to want it, and it is Gwil Lasker and I am of the first lay and you may put it in your book with the hour against it and check it on any day of a week.**"

"**Gwil Lasker,**" said Calder Marr, "**of the first lay, and you have said it at the head of this dock and it goes in with the hour on it and you are not thanked, and I am not going to stand here and tell eleven people that a man who has crossed this bar twenty-one times has come about on the figures, because you have not and everybody in this street has heard you not.**

"**And I am going to say one thing back and I am going to say it to you and not to the street, and then I am going to write it down and you may read it.

"**In about four years a man in a city is going to read that page, and if this yard's figure is on it, then this yard is the yard that said *I do not know* about a thing it could see. And that is worth what it is worth, and I am not going to argue with you about it, and I am not going to withdraw a figure off this mole because a man at a dock has told me what a city will do with two lines on one page.**

"**And I am going to say the other half, because this yard has been wrong in a market before and it did not get up and say sorry about it, and it is not going to do that now. Two and three-quarters was made once and it is not withdrawn and it is not going to be, and the pilot's two and a half is not withdrawn either, and neither of us can check the other and neither of us has ever pretended to.

"**And both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other. The figure is the figure and it was made by a man who has been in a room and could not answer a question, and a man who cannot answer a question about a mark on a paper is not a man who cannot make a figure on a bar of gravel with a pole at half-tide, and he is going to have to go on standing behind the figure anyway.**"

Nobody in that street said anything, which is what about eleven people say about two men who have both told the truth at the same time about the same thing.

---

**And the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was twenty-six Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and the last thing on that wall was a sitting two days in front of a paper that is now in a bundle in a city.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party did not walk it, and that is the twenty-third charge since the third of August, and this party has walked none of the twenty-three.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Monday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him.

---

**And at about half past nine on the Monday night in that shed the whole of the two days went into the first column, against itself, with the hour against it, and it is on the page and it is not going to be taken off it.**

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Sunday the first day of November instant, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, a man of the first lay, who gave his name as Gwil Lasker and who is thirty-six years old and who has a boat of about a hundred and twenty tons of his own and a man of forty on the second hand, said out loud at the head of this dock that a yard which has been in a room and come out with a clerk saying that a clerk could see what the man could see is a yard whose figures are worth what the clerk's are, and said first and said out loud that he was not saying this yard was wrong, and gave the whole of the reason, which is that a man in a city is going to read two lines on the same page and is going to take the first off and leave the second on; and that he said that he had crossed this bar twenty-one springs and that at twenty-two he had taken a sounding off it off a master's own figure at that master's own asking and had been a foot and a half out in a slack water and had not told him, and that the said master had it himself inside a month off a man out of a city and that neither of them had said one word about it to the other since; and that this party wrote his name down with the hour against it and did not thank him, and that he offered his name before he was asked for it.

*And that the holder answered him in about two hundred words in the open air and did not withdraw the figure of two and three-quarters of a fathom on the flood and two and a quarter on the ebb, and did not ask that the said figure be re-taken, and did not say that the pilot's figure of two and a half was wrong, and entered that neither of the two figures can be checked by the other and that neither has ever been pretended to, and that neither is withdrawn and that this volume does not decide which of them is right and that the Kettle was not looked at.

*And that the reason this line is in the first column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can get off a mole tomorrow morning and ask any man on that quay whether the said man said it, and can ask a man at the head of this dock whether his name is Gwil Lasker and whether he has a boat of about a hundred and twenty tons; and that the cost of the sitting of the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth instant is entered here in the yard's own hand and against itself, and the cost is this: that a yard which has spent four volumes saying that its own record is the only kind of record anybody can check has come out of a room less able to be checked, and that it happened by the party's own method, in public, in about nine and about eleven people's hearing, and that nobody was convicted of anything and nothing was decided and no office was a villain in it, and that a man who is the best reader of joins in this harbor was made less credible by a thing he is good at and said so himself in a room and cannot unsay it, and that the yard entered on the second day of November instant that it is not going to be told it is sorry about it in a market and is not going to thank anybody for it and is not asking.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the second instant, being twenty-six Mondays since the first of May, and that the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it, and that the last thing on that wall was the sitting of the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth instant and that what went on that wall is now in a bundle in a city; and that Alden Marr was at the far bench on the said Monday from about ten until about four and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that Calder Marr did not go over to him.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the second day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the thirtieth day of October past that the second column of this book is now the most dangerous column in this harbor, and that on the second day of November instant the holder entered against himself that the same trade that has made this yard's first column the only page in this harbor that anybody could check is the trade that a clerk of the circuit office said in a room he could have done without him, and that the two of those are the same trade and are the same page and that a man cannot have one of them in a market and the other of them in a record; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a figure is worth on a page in a city in four years is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the people who are going to have to cross the bar.*

*And that the holder entered on the second of November instant that the party worked out on the twenty-fifth of October past, in this shed and before the sitting and not after it, that the reason against a line does not go into a record, and that the party has entered that it does not know whether the five bare lines of that record are the work of a man who was careful or the work of a man who had somebody to be careful about, and that there is no version of this book in which anybody in a city finds that out, and that the reason is not that anybody lied.*

**No money came into this yard on the Sunday and none on the Monday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the third instant and the bag is five hundred and thirty-eight pence short of it. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and the column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and nothing in this month's book has asked her for anything. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-eight days up the crown of that mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the second instant and this party walked none of the twenty-three charges there have been since the third of August. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him and has not asked him for a thing. Nineteen winters are in a list in a city and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what the woman at that table writes with this yard's four returns. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next with four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the live question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in this month. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands and the holder did not ask twice to have it lifted and did not ask at all this month.
