# Chapter 185

*The Most Dangerous Column in This Harbor*

Friday the thirtieth and Saturday the thirty-first of October, in the shed, and on the mole's steps.

**Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Friday from about ten until about four and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and it is one line in the first column and either brother can check it.**

**And on the Saturday nothing happened and this party entered that, with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and the only way it gets into a book that can be read in a room in a fortnight is by somebody writing it down on the day.**

---

**And at about eight in the evening on the Friday, in the shed, with the lamp not lit because it was not dark yet at the end of October in this latitude, the ship's hand put her left forearm on the table in a sling that a bonesetter of this harbor had put there on the twenty-fourth, and said the thing she had been carrying since eleven o'clock on the Wednesday morning.**

"**I have been trying since Wednesday afternoon to work out a way of saying this that does not sound like a woman who has been frightened, and I cannot do it, and I am not going to try any more, and I would rather say it badly than say it well and be listened to in the wrong tone.**

"**Our second column is the most dangerous column in this harbor.**

"**And it was not, in September, and it was not on the fourth of October, and this yard wrote on the sixth of June that it is the only place a man in this harbor is allowed to say that he does not know and mean it, and that has been the truest thing this yard has ever put in a book, and on Wednesday morning a man said it in a room and a clerk wrote it down and it is in a record in a city and it cannot be got off.

"**Here is the half of it I did not see coming and I have been carrying for two days. I have got a crew of two and a half. One of the two and a half has a bone cracked about two inches above her wrist and a figure of three shillings and sixpence on a Saturday and a quarter of a year out of the left side of her, and she is the tender, and the pump, and the fall, and the board, and the slate, and the standing shutdown, and there is nobody in this harbor we can hire to do any of it and we have not tried.

"**And if this yard says *I do not know* in a room about the ground of the eighth of August, there are four people of this coast who are alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and they wrote a limit themselves and they signed it, and it says the account is not to be read out, is not to go into a book that leaves this harbor, is not to be said to a man with a seal, and not one name to any office in any city.

"**And that limit is not in the record. It is not in the record because it is a reason, and reasons do not go in. And so a man in a city in four years is going to read five bare lines out of this yard's book and four lines of them with no name against them, and he is going to read them as a man who was being careful, and there is going to be nothing in the paper anywhere that says four people on a weir wrote a line and signed it.

"**And we cannot put it there. That is the whole of it. We cannot say *I will not tell you because four people of this coast wrote a limit and I have not asked to have it lifted*, because this yard has a writ on it and a writ is a channel, and a man with a writ is exactly the thing they wrote the line about.**"

"**Say the other half of it,**" said Calder Marr, "**because you have said one half and it is the half that is going to keep me awake and I have got a fortnight to sleep in before the next Monday and I would like the other half tonight.**

"**Both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other. The second column is the only honest column in this harbor and it is the only column in this harbor that can now be used against four people who have nine marks between them, and the fact that it is the only honest one is not a reason to shut it and the fact that it is the only dangerous one is not a reason to keep saying it out loud in rooms, and there is no third thing to say and I have looked.**"

"**There is a third thing and it is the whole of what I actually think,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I am going to say it and you are not going to like it, and then I am going to go and bed and I am not going to answer it tonight.

"**You are going to keep saying it. And you are going to be right to. And in about four years a man in a city is going to read five lines of this yard's own book in a record and he is not going to know whether they are the work of a man who was careful or the work of a man who had somebody to be careful about, and there is no version of this book in which he finds out, and the reason is not that anybody lied to him. It is that the reason is the part that does not travel, and I have known that since Sunday and I did not want to know it on Wednesday morning at about eleven.**

"**And I am not going to fill the third column and I have said why in the first column in my own hand on the seventeenth of October and I am not going to say it again, and if somebody asks me in a room in four years I am going to point at that page.**"

She got up then, and went to the bench, and came back with a slate and a piece of chalk, and put them on the table in front of him.

"**And that is the rest of it,**" she said, "**and it is small, and I am telling you that it is small so that you do not get a night of it.

"**Three nights. You are going to sit on the mole's steps from the turn of the tide to the turn of it and you are going to write down in this book every pump that goes off and every boat that goes out and every boat that comes in, with the hour, and you are not going to write down anything about why any of it happened.

"**That is the first thing anybody learns about a listening buoy and it is the whole of the first thing. A buoy does not care what a man is sure of. A buoy is a book of hours and a man who will not write the hours down, and if he writes them down wrong, then in about four years there is no way anybody finds out that he wrote them down wrong, because there is nothing to check them against.

"**And I am not going to tell you what the rest of it is, because the rest of it needs a crew that keeps them, and there is no crew, and there is not going to be one in November, and you have not got the one you would need and neither have I. So you are going to have the book of hours and nothing else and I am not going to let you have the other thing, and if you ask me for it in a market in a fortnight I will say no in front of everybody.**"

He did the three nights. The pump went off nine times in the three nights and he wrote nine lines, and four boats went out and three came in, and he wrote seven lines, and he did not write down a single reason against any of them, and the ship's hand read them back and the holder read them back after her and nobody else was in the shed.

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirty-first day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the third column of this book was not filled on Friday the thirtieth or on Saturday the thirty-first instant and has not been filled since the first day of August, and that this party has not appointed anybody to it and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Saturday night, and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column; and that on Saturday the twenty-fourth and Sunday the twenty-fifth instant the party's own shipwright entered in the first column in her own hand that she has nine sentences and did not write them and gave the reason, and that this party entered on the said day that it will not ask for them and will not have them written down, and that neither of those entries is improved on this month and neither is being entered twice.

*And that on the Friday instant the holder sat on the mole's steps from the turn of the tide to the turn of it on three nights of the thirtieth and thirty-first of October and the first of November, being Friday, Saturday and Sunday, and wrote in this book every pump that went off and every boat that went out and every boat that came in with the hour against each, being nine pumps and four boats out and three boats in, and that he wrote no reason against any of those sixteen lines, and that the said lines are a book of hours and are not a hearing and are not a reading and settle nothing and say no word.

*And that nothing happened on Saturday the thirty-first instant and this party entered that with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened; and that Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Friday instant from about ten until about four and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that Calder Marr did not go over to him, and that this party has entered that it did not and that neither brother has said why and neither brother is going to.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the thirty-first day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the thirtieth instant that the second column of this book is now the most dangerous column in this harbor and entered why, and that the reason is that this yard has a crew of two and a half of whom one has a bone cracked about two inches above the wrist and a figure of three shillings and sixpence against it and a quarter of a year out of the left side, and that four people of this coast are alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them who wrote a limit themselves and signed it, that the account is not to be read out, is not to go into a book that leaves this harbor, is not to be said to a man with a seal and not one name to any office in any city, and that the said limit is not in the record of the sitting of the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth instant because a limit is a reason and reasons do not go into a record, and that this party cannot put it there because this yard holds a writ and a writ is a channel and a man with a writ is the thing that limit was written against.

*And that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check what a limit says and cannot check what a record in a city is going to do with a line that has got nothing behind it, and that the holder entered on the same day that both halves of that are true and that neither of the two cancels the other, and that the ship's hand said on the same day that there is no third thing to say and that she has looked, and that neither of them improved on either half and that the entry of the sixth of June instant, that the second column is the only place a man in this harbor is allowed to say that he does not know and mean it, stands and is not struck out and is not to be improved on by what happened in a room on Wednesday.

*And that the holder entered on the thirtieth instant that the said woman's arm is in a sling and that she is the tender and the pump and the fall and the board and the slate and the standing shutdown of this crew, and that there is nobody in this harbor this party can hire to do any of it and that this party has not tried, and that a man in a room on the Wednesday instant answered a question about a join on a sheet of paper with the words *I do not know* and that a clerk wrote it down and that it is in a record in a city, and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out and a request to strike one out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**No money came into this yard on the Friday and none on the Saturday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the third of November instant and is five hundred and thirty-eight pence short of being paid. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and nobody has written to him, nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and the head of the third column keeps his hand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and this party has not asked her for anything since the twenty-ninth of September past and is not going to. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-seven days up the crown of that mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth instant and this party walked none of the twenty-two charges there have been since the third of August. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on the thirtieth or the thirty-first. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open and the said woman did not mention it in this shed on the thirtieth and was not asked about it. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. Nineteen winters are in a list in a city and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what the woman at that table writes with this yard's four returns. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and nobody has said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years and the chest was not opened. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next with four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book and the live question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them and the limit they wrote stands and the holder did not ask twice to have it lifted and did not ask at all this month.
