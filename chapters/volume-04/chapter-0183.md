# Chapter 183

*The Twenty-Fifth Wall, and a Wage That Goes In*

Monday the twenty-sixth and Tuesday the twenty-seventh of October, at the mole's steps, at the head of the dock, and in the shed.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was twenty-five Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of that office kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room since the sixteenth of October and the next one is on Wednesday morning at ten and is not in this party's keeping.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party did not walk it, and that is the twenty-first charge since the third of August, and this party has walked none of the twenty-one.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about four on the Monday with his back to the room and the same net in his hands, and he was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him.

---

**And there was a man standing on the mole's steps at about ten on the Monday morning who is not usually on the mole's steps, and this yard went up and asked him why, and it is entered in this chapter that he was asked a question and not given a request.**

Fenn Ardle, forty-three, of the second lay, with a boat of about seventy tons and a boy of seventeen on the second hand, who keeps the standing watch on the mole's steps for the harbor authority on a Monday and a Thursday when the tide gang is short, which it is, and has been four men short since August.

"**Two days,**" he said. "**Wednesday and Thursday of this week and no other, and the officer of this place came up the steps on Friday and told me himself in the open air where I would have heard him, and he told me before he told anybody else, which I will say for him and which I will also say does not make it any different.

"**And I am out of a Tuesday on the water on the twenty-seventh, which is tomorrow, and I have told him that, and he has written it nowhere that I can see, and I am not asking him to, because I have been on these steps nineteen years and I know what that ledger is.

"**It is spent. He told me so himself in September and he told the yard so in the open air, and there is no more of it, and whatever I lose this week is lost between the two of us and not in a book. And I have told you that so that you can put it in yours, and I would rather you put it in yours than put nothing, because there is going to be a man in a room on Wednesday who is going to say that this harbor is working, and it is, and I am the part of it that is not working on the twenty-seventh, and a thing nobody has written down is a thing that did not happen in about four years.**"

"**I am not going to ask that office for a tenth thing in that ledger,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have not asked it for anything since the fourth of October and I am not asking it now. I am asking you what your Tuesday is worth, because you are standing on these steps in the wind for a Wednesday and Thursday and I would like the yard's book to have one line in it about a man who is out of a Tuesday and did not agree to be.

"**And I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to say I am sorry, because I did not do it and neither did that office without a reason, and a man who says sorry in a market for a Tuesday he did not take gets a Tuesday back for nothing.**"

There were about eleven people on the steps and the quay behind them at ten on a Monday morning, and none of them said anything, and the counting of the fish was going on somewhere inside the market building.

**And Ors Kest was at the foot of his own steps at about a quarter to eleven on the same Monday morning with his coat on and about four people near him, and he had been there before this yard came up and he did not go inside.**

"**I have not asked for a thing and I am not going to,**" he said. "**I want to say one thing in the open air and I am going to say it once and then I am going to go inside and shut my ledger.

"**That man is out of a Tuesday and I put him on these steps for two days and I told him on Friday and not on Monday morning, and I did it on the Friday because a man who is told on a Friday has a Saturday to be angry about and a man who is told at ten on a Monday morning has got about four hours.

"**And it is not in my ledger because my ledger is spent, and it is spent, and I told the yard that in September in the open air and I am not going to pretend to a man on a Tuesday that it is anything else. It is not entered because there is no room to enter it in, and a thing that cannot be entered cannot be produced, and a thing that cannot be produced cannot be argued about, and a man who has been in this harbor nineteen years knows exactly what he is being handed and what it is worth, and that is not a favor to him.

"**And my repair account has been empty since the middle of February and is empty this morning, and I am not going to open it for two days of a man's time on a pair of steps in October, and I would rather say that out loud to about eleven people than have it found out in a market in March.**"

Nessa Pike said nothing at all on that, and the holder wrote it down at about half past four that afternoon, in the first column, with the reason against the line.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Monday the twenty-sixth day of October instant a man of the second lay, who gave his name as Fenn Ardle and who is forty-three years old and who has a boat of about seventy tons of his own and a boy of seventeen on the second hand, was standing on the mole's steps at about ten in the morning, and that the harbor authority had put him there for the Wednesday and the Thursday instant on account of the sitting in the room over the tide-gang store, and that the officer of that office told him so on Friday the twenty-third instant in the open air and told him before he told anybody else, and that the said man is out of a Tuesday on the water on the twenty-seventh instant, and that the harbor authority has not entered that day's loss in its ledger and cannot, and that the said man said in about eleven people's hearing that a thing nobody has written down is a thing that did not happen in about four years; and that this party asked him a question and did not make a request of any office, and has entered why, which is that this party asked that office for nothing on the fourth of October instant and has not asked it for anything since, and that the ninth acknowledgement in that ledger is the last and a tenth may not be entered, and that a yard which has been told in the open air by the officer of that office that his ledger is spent does not go back up those steps with the same request; and that this party did not say it was sorry and is not going to and did not thank anybody, and that the said man said he would rather it was in this party's book than in nothing.*

*And that the officer of the harbor authority, being Ors Kest, said in the open air at the foot of his own steps at about a quarter to eleven on the said morning, in about four people's hearing and about eleven people's near enough to hear it, that he had put the said man on those steps for two days and had told him on the Friday and not on the Monday morning because a man told on a Friday has a Saturday to be angry about, and that the loss of the said man's Tuesday is not in that office's ledger because that ledger is spent, and that a thing which cannot be entered cannot be produced and a thing which cannot be produced cannot be argued about; and that the repair account of that office has been empty since the middle of February and is empty on the said morning, and that the said officer said he would rather say that out loud to about eleven people than have it found out in a market in March; and that this party took nothing and was given nothing and asked for nothing and has entered that a man of forty-four is not a friend of this party and that this volume does not pretend he was right about everything and that the two of those are not the same thing.*

---

**And on Tuesday the twenty-seventh of October at about eleven in the morning the wage of four marks went in, into an envelope, in the holder's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it, and it was the fourth wage out of two days of work in five weeks, and it went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about eleven people in about two minutes.**

Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. Eighteen envelopes on a bench in that chest. Three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September and the sixth of October, entered in the same breath and not one after the other, and the three figures are three different figures and none of them is either of the others.

Eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag, and that is one hundred and two pence, and it is the whole of it, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and this party entered on the twelfth of September that it does not know what any given Tuesday is going to bring and has not improved on that entry since.

---

**And at about half past eight on the Tuesday night in that shed the two of them went through what was going to happen on Wednesday and Thursday, out loud, once, and it is in the book.**

"**The book goes down on that table at ten,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and it goes down whole, and I have counted the pages twice since Sunday and it is what it was, and nobody has torn anything out of it since the second of May and nobody is going to.

"**And the clerk is going to read it. That is new. On the ninth of September it was produced and not one line was read. On the sixteenth of October it was produced and not one line was read. On Wednesday morning somebody in a room is going to read this book out loud to about nine people, and the whole of it, and the first column and the second column both, and the third column at the top of a page in a boy's hand which nobody has filled since the first of August.

"**And here is the part I have been carrying since Sunday and have not said out loud, and I am saying it out loud now so that it is in the room instead of in my head.

"**There are five lines in that second column that nobody in this harbor can check. Four of them have no name against them at all. And a clerk is going to enter a line, a date and an hour, because a line, a date and an hour is what a record has room for, and the reason against every one of those five lines is a paragraph and is not going in, and it stays in this shed.

"**So on Wednesday there is going to be a record in a city with five bare lines out of this yard's own book in it and no reason for any of them, and every one of the five is going to look worse without its reason than it looks here, and two of them are going to look like a man saying he did not know, and one of them is a line of the fourteenth of August of which the man who wrote it can bring up four words.

"**And I want it written down tonight that we worked that out on Sunday, in this shed, before we went in, and not after.**"

"**It goes in,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to put my thumb on it, and you are not going to either.

"**And I want two things said about it, because they are true and they are not the same thing and I am tired of finding out afterwards that they are not.

"**One. A line with a reason against it is worth more than a line without one, and that is the whole of the second column, and the reason does not travel and the line does, and that is a fact about records and not about anybody's honesty.

"**And two. It is going to be read out in a room by a man who wrote it, and the room is going to be full of people who are not going to know which of those two things they are looking at.**"

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that on the Sunday instant the holder and the ship's hand entered that five lines of this column are going into a record in a city on the Wednesday and the Thursday instant without the reason against any of them, and that the holder entered on the said day that a line with a reason against it is worth more than a line without one and that the reason does not travel and the line does, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a shed can be checked for what he wrote on a Sunday and cannot be checked for what four hundred people will make of five bare lines in a fortnight; and that the ship's hand entered on the said day that the party worked that out before the sitting and not after it, and that neither of them put a thumb on it, and that the third column of this book was not filled on the said day and has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it.*

*And that the holder entered on the twenty-seventh instant that the party is not going to leave a line of that book out of that room and is not going to soften one, and that this is entered as a decision and is not a virtue, and that no person in this yard is going to thank this party for it and this party is not asking, and that the entry of the sixth of June instant on the same thing stands and is not being entered twice.*

**Nothing came into this yard on the Monday and on the Tuesday a wage of four marks went out of it and three shillings and sixpence had gone out of it on the Saturday, and eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag and is the whole of it. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven as they were written on the fourth of January. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid since the nineteenth of May, and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for a day of work on the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and no letter is going to him. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, out of her own pocket, and it is not paid and is not going to be paid out of anything that comes in. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it, and the chest in that shed was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure in the first column at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and nobody has written to him, nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and the head of the third column keeps his hand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are on the crown of that mole about a hundred and forty-five days, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on the Monday or the Tuesday. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it is open, and the only mechanism anybody has ever named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office and not a better letter from a shed. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the twenty-sixth instant and this party walked none of the twenty-one charges there have been since the third of August. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short and this morning a man of the second lay was standing on the mole's steps for that office instead of on the water. The hose of the fire party of this harbor is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him and has not asked him for anything. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table who has this yard's four returns in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and nobody in this harbor has ever said why. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ of the first of May runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence and a paper served in a doorway on the twenty-third instant does not touch it and does not deal with it. The custody room was not asked for and nothing came out of it and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure, neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted.
