# Chapter 181

*A Day to Be Named, and a Paper Served in the Doorway*

Thursday the twenty-second and Friday the twenty-third of October, at the mole's steps, in the doorway of the shed, and on a wharf on the boards.

**At about ten on the Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and that is the seventh rota day of this month and the twentieth since the third of August, and this party has walked none of the twenty, and it has held since the nineteenth of June.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about four on the Thursday and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and it is one line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week.

---

**And at about eleven on that Thursday morning a clerk of the circuit office came up this hill on foot, as he has done three times since September, and he gave his name and the name of his office before he said anything else, which is a practice and has been one since April.**

Ambrose Sell, clerk of the circuit office.

"**I am going to say four things and then I am going back down the hill, and two of them are on a paper that is coming and two of them are not on anything, and I have thought about which is which and I have not got it right twice yet this year, so you may take the two that are not on the paper as the softer ones and be disappointed.

"**One. A day is to be named.

"**And I am not going to name it in a doorway, and I am going to tell you why, and the reason is my own convenience and not the office's, and I would rather you had the truth of that. A day named in a doorway is a day a man can turn round and refuse before he has heard it, and I am not going to give either of us that, and I am not going to put it in a paper either until I have walked back up this hill with the paper in my hand.

"**Two. A paper will be served on this party and it will name the day and the room, and I will serve it myself, and you will not be asked to argue with me about it at the door.

"**Three, and this one is not on the paper, and it is the only thing I am going to say in this doorway that costs me anything to say. Your book is going to be read. Not the parts of it you choose. From the first of May to the last page, in the order it is in, and this office has decided that and has not asked whether the party minds, because if the office asks a party whether its own book is to be read, then the party has chosen, and a party that has chosen can be asked afterwards why it chose what it chose and did not choose the rest.

"**And I am telling you a day early on purpose, so that you have tonight to think about the parts you would have left out, and I would rather you spent tonight on that than spent the morning of it in a room with a clerk and about nine people in it.**"

Calder Marr did not answer for a moment. There was a pump on the bench behind him and a slate on the bench beside it and the light in the shed was the light it is in this harbor in the last week of October.

"**Then I will tell the party has thought about it, and I will tell you the answer, and you may put it in whatever it is you keep and read it back to me if you have a practice of that.**"

"**I have a practice of that,**" said Ambrose Sell, "**and I am not going to tell you what I have on my own page.**

"**Say it.**"

"**This yard entered on the sixth of June, in its own first column, in the holder's own left hand, that the second column is the only place a man in this harbor is allowed to say that he does not know and mean it, and that both columns stand and neither is scratched out and a request to strike a line out of either is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in the other.

"**And I will tell you what this party has entered about the reading, and it was entered in advance on the sixteenth of September and again on the twentieth of October, and both of those entries are on the page with a date on them, and neither of them is going to be improved on because of anything that is said in the next three days.

"**It is this. If the office reads that book, it reads the second column. The second column is not a set of facts about this harbor. It is the only place this party has ever written down anything it could not check, and it has written it down with a reason against every line, and a man on a mole can check what a day brought and cannot check what a man was sure of at nine in the evening.

"**And I am not going to leave a line of it out and I am not going to soften one, and if a line of it makes this yard look worse in a room than it is, then the line is what we wrote, and we wrote it because we could not check it, and that is the whole of our reason and it has never been a better one than that.**"

The clerk wrote it down. It took him about two minutes and he read it back to the man in about as long, and the man said that it was his own words and that he was not going to better them, and the clerk entered that too.

"**Then that is the party's position on the paper and it is in the room before anybody has said a word about the eighth of August,**" said Ambrose Sell, "**which is more than most people manage.**

"**Four, and last. A party may be represented, and you have told this office in September that you are not going to be, and I am not going to make you say it twice and I am not going to write it into the paper as though you had not said it. And whatever goes into the record on the day is what a clerk can enter and not what a clerk can be asked about afterwards, and I am telling you that now because it is the only warning I am going to give this party and I do not intend to give it twice and I have not given it to anybody else on this coast.

"**A wall you can take down. A record you cannot. You have a book that says a thing can be checked, and I have a bundle that says a thing can be kept, and neither of us has a way of taking back the one we use.**"

He went down the hill. Nobody followed him.

---

**And on Friday the twenty-third of October at about a quarter to eight in the morning, in the doorway of that shed, with the pump already on the shoulder and two men of the third lay on the boards below, Ambrose Sell served a paper of this party in about two people's hearing and did not read it out of it.**

He said his name and the name of his office. He put it into the holder's hand and said that a copy was not given to any party and that a party may apply in writing at its own cost for a copy of any record, which grants no copy and makes none and releases nothing and no date attaches to any of it. And then he went down the hill again in the dark.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that a paper of the circuit office of this place was served on this party in the doorway of this shed at about a quarter to eight in the morning on Friday the twenty-third day of October instant, in about two people's hearing, by the clerk of that office, who gave his name and the name of his office before he did it and did not read it out of it; and that the said paper names a day and a room, and that the said day is the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth days of October instant at ten of the morning at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and that the holder read the said paper himself and did not read it aloud in this yard, and entered why, which is that a party's own book is going to be in that room and a day has to be in this book and not only on a paper four hundred yards away; and that no copy of any record was given to this party and none was asked for.*

*And that on Thursday the twenty-second instant, in this doorway, at about eleven in the morning, the clerk of that office said that a day was to be named and did not name it, and said that a paper would be served, and said that this yard's own book was to be read whole and unshortened from the first of May to the last page and that this office had decided that and had not asked whether the party minded, and gave his reason, which was that if the office asks a party whether its own book is to be read then the party has chosen and a party that has chosen can be asked afterwards why it chose what it chose and did not choose the rest; and that the holder answered in this yard in about two hundred words and the clerk read the answer back and entered it, and that the answer is that the second column of this book is not a set of facts about this harbor but the only place this party has written down what it could not check, and that this party is not going to leave a line of it out and is not going to soften one, and that the entry of the sixth day of June last on the same thing stands and is not being entered twice, and that the entry of the sixteenth day of September past and the entry of the twentieth day of October instant, that the reading of this book in a room is coming and that this party is not going to soften a line and is not going to leave one out, both stand and neither is being improved on because of anything said in the last three days.*

*And that the said clerk said that a party may be represented and that this party has said in September that it is not going to be, and did not ask again, and entered it; and that he said that whatever goes into the record on the said day is what a clerk can enter and not what a clerk can be asked about afterwards, and that he said that he was giving that warning once and had not given it to anybody else on this coast; and that this party has entered that and has not improved on it and is not going to.*

*And that the wage of four marks for the week ending on Tuesday the twenty-seventh instant will fall due on that Tuesday and this party does not know at the time of writing what it is going to write on that Tuesday and entered that on the twelfth day of September past and has not improved on that entry since.*

---

**And then the two of them went down to the boards at eight, because a pump had been hired for the day by a man of the third lay who had a vessel with a started plank and had asked for a pump and not for a party.**

**Gross on the board ten.** The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, two. A second hired hand, two. Seven out of ten paid at the mole and the yard's being three, and the eighth and the share came off the top of the day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May.

A mark and twelve shillings was in the bag on Thursday evening. Four marks and twelve shillings was in it on Friday afternoon, and that is three hundred and four pence plus four hundred and eighty.

They worked from eight until about half past two. Nobody was in the water. A hand lead's line went over the side and back four times and the count and the times were in the ship's hand on a slate and the slate went into the book on the board in her own writing and the holder read it back after her and neither of them said anything about it.

At about half past two in the afternoon Nessa Pike was on the delivery with a wrench, and the delivery union was a copper union the yard had put in itself in August and had not been told anything about by anybody, and the nut on it was not the union's, and the pressure on the discharge side was up because the vessel's man had shut a valve he should not have shut, and the union let go in a quarter of a second with a noise like a dropped plate.

The pump stopped dead. The handle has a quarter of a turn of its own in it and it came round through that quarter of a turn, and her left forearm was in the arc between the handle and the frame because she had been holding the handle down with that arm while she worked the nut with the other, and it went across the bone about two inches above the wrist with a sound that three men on a wharf heard.

---

"**That is the bone and not the joint,**" she said, before anybody else had said anything. "**I want it said now, out loud, on the boards, and I want it said in that order, because a bonesetter in four hours is going to tell me which of the two it is and I am not going to have it be a question anybody asked me in the next four hours.**

"**And I want it in the book tonight in my own hand and not in his.**"

The man of the third lay finished the pumping himself with the pump on a bar, which takes one man and takes all day and is not work anybody does twice, and he paid the day's figure at the figure at about ten past three without anybody arguing with him about a quarter of an hour that was left on the boards, and the three marks went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people and no figure was argued.

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-third day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a day of work was worked on a wharf on the Friday instant, hired by a man of the third lay for a vessel with a started plank, that the gross on the board was ten and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it and that seven of the ten was paid at the mole and the yard's was three; and that the day's work stopped at about half past two in the afternoon because the copper union on the delivery let go under pressure and the handle of the pump came round through the quarter of a turn that is in it and the said handle struck the left forearm of this yard's own shipwright across the bone about two inches above the wrist, and that she said before anybody had said anything that it was the bone and not the joint and asked that it be said on the boards in that order, and that it is entered in her own hand and not in his, and that three men on that wharf heard it; and that the pumping was finished by the said man's own hand with the pump on a bar and the day's figure was paid at the figure at about ten past three without anybody arguing with anybody about the quarter of an hour that was left on the boards; and that this party entered against itself that three marks was paid for about four hours and a quarter of a day and that this column does not enter that as a day and that a figure written on a note is not a figure paid until somebody puts the money on the counter and the money went up the mole's steps in front of about nine people and neither of those two facts makes the other one smaller.*

*And that a bonesetter of this harbor was sent for on the Saturday instant and came at about ten in the afternoon of that day, and that her name is Hessa Barlow and that she is forty-four and that she has a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, and that she said that there is a crack in the bone about two inches above the wrist and that there is nothing in the joint out of place and that the thing wants to be in a thing that is tight for six weeks and that the forearm is not going to be a hand that takes a pump handle in a month, and that her figure for the strapping and the visit was three shillings and sixpence and that it was paid out of the bag on the Saturday instant at about ten past four in the afternoon in her own hand at her own counter and that she said a return on the fourteenth day would not be charged and that she would not be thanked for it and that she has not been thanked.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the twenty-third day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man stood in a doorway on the Thursday instant and agreed out loud, in about two hundred words, with a clerk of a circuit office, that this yard's own book is to be read in a room whole and unshortened from the first of May to the last page, and that the holder entered on the sixth day of June past, in the first column and in advance, that the second column is the only place a man in this harbor is allowed to say that he does not know and mean it, and that the entry stands and is not being improved on, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a doorway can be checked for what he said and cannot be checked for what a room will be worth on the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth instant, and that the said clerk gave this party one warning and said he would not give it twice, and that the warning was that what goes into a record is what a clerk can enter and not what a clerk can be asked about afterwards.*

*And that a woman of this harbor, being this yard's own shipwright, went on a wharf on the Friday instant and had the bone of her left forearm struck by a pump handle at about half past two in the afternoon, and that she said out loud and before anybody that it was the bone and not the joint, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check a day and can check a bill and cannot check what a room is going to do with the second of this book, and that the said woman is the person holding the pencil and that this party has not thanked her and is not going to ask.*

**Four marks and twelve shillings is in the bag, and that is seven hundred and eighty-four pence, and the whole of what went out of it on the day was three shillings and sixpence and a quarter of a day of one woman's arm. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved since the fourth of January at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and is not going to be sent for. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August out of her own pocket, and it is not paid and is not being paid out of the bag out of anything that has come into this yard, and the said woman has said out loud that she does not want it paid out of a bonesetter's bill. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and that man was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it, and the chest in that shed was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking and not on any wall. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and the six marks and two days were not offered and have not been reduced. Twenty-three lines and ninety-two marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, seventeen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September and the sixth of October, entered in the same breath and not one after the other. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-five days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has four clauses in it and has not been lifted and not narrowed and not re-served, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on the Thursday or the Friday. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hose of the fire party of this harbor is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the twenty-second instant and this party walked none of the twenty charges it has been charged since the third of August. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nobody has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and is not going to ask at all. The custody room was not asked for and nothing came out of it and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book.
