# Chapter 160

*The Seat*

Sunday the tenth of September, at the top of this mole's steps above the third seam's mark, in the open air, in about nine people's hearing, and in the shed at night.

**A clerk of the circuit office came up this hill at about eleven in the morning on a Sunday with a paper in his hand, and about nine people were on the crown, and the mole head's light was not on behind them, and the two objects of nobody's were under their tarpaulins about thirty feet further up the crown with a corner off one of them since the seventeenth of June, and he served it there.**

---

> *First. That a sitting of the inquiry opened into certain matters touching the working of a salvage concern at Morrow Quay between the first day of May instant and the fifteenth day of August instant is appointed for the day of the week and the week following this day, at ten of the clock in the morning, at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin; and that the parties named in the said inquiry, being four, are required to attend.*
>
> *Second. That where a finding is disputed between persons, the harbor authority of this harbor may seat one of the two disputants and one other, and take such finding as is given to it, without prejudice to any claim; and that the construction of this paragraph is that of the second limb of a reporting clause and is applied by this notice to this party, and that the party did not write it and cannot un-write it.*
>
> *Third. That nothing in this notice touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the writ of salvage working granted at Morrow Quay on the first day of May instant, and that the said writ runs to midnight on the first day of March next and is not the Second Bell license and does not lead to one and renews nothing, and that its ground is not widened by this notice and is not to be widened.*
>
> *Fourth. That the party is required to produce at the said sitting: the crew's book kept by it, whole and unshortened, with a copy of that book; the said writ; the four returns made under the reporting clause upon it; any record of any finding made on or about the eighth day of August instant; any sheet or paper bearing the party's own figures, made by the party and put up on a public wall in this harbor on or about the twenty-fifth day of August instant, with a copy of it; and any record the party keeps of a place, an hour, a keeper or a limit.*

Then, at the foot of it, in the writing of the paper and not in a seal:

> *A. Sell, clerk.*

**And the clerk read the first paragraph out of the paper in his hand, in the open air, standing still, with the wind behind him. And he read the second and third and fourth paragraphs out of his own memory, at the top of his voice, in about nine people's hearing, in a sun that was in the eyes of everybody on that crown. And then he folded the paper and put it under his arm and said that he had said it from the paper, and the yard has written down that he did not and that he said he did, and the yard is not going to improve on either of those.**

---

Nobody thanked him. He had not asked to be thanked and there was nothing on that crown to thank him with.

"**I served it here because I was told to serve it and not to leave it,**" said Ambrose Sell. "**I am a clerk and I do what I am told and I have a page and I am going to go down that hill and put the hour on my page and the hour I wrote it and not the hour it was done, because that is a practice and it has been one since April.

"**And I am going to say the one thing I am going to say, and then I am going to go.

"**You built that second paragraph. I know you built it, because I have had your writ in my hands and I have read the clause on it, and that is the clause's own second limb with the words put in it that a form wants instead of the words that a writ has got. It is the same box. Somebody in a city has taken a thing this yard agreed to in April and turned it round so that it points at this yard instead of at the water, and this yard agreed to it in good faith and wrote it out in public on a market wall in July, and forty copies of it are in about sixty boats, and one of them is in a city at a press that cannot be stopped.

"**I do not know who drafted that and I have not been told and I am not going to find out, and I would not put it in my page if I did, because my page holds what I can enter and not what I can guess.**"

He went down the hill.

---

**And the yard did not refuse the notice, and did not sign anything on the step, and let nobody on that crown say in a market what this yard thought it was, and at about nine o'clock that night, in the shed, with the lamp lit and the shipwright reading it back and the holder reading it back after her and nobody else in the room, the whole of it went into the first column in the holder's own left hand, in advance, with the hour against it and the reason against the line.**

*That on Sunday the tenth of September instant, at about eleven in the morning, at the top of the steps of this mole above the third seam's mark, in daylight, in about nine people's hearing, a notice of four paragraphs was served on this party by a clerk of the circuit office, being the clerk whose name is at the foot of it, and that the said notice appoints a day in the week following this day for a sitting at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and that this party has entered the whole of the four paragraphs, unshortened, and has entered the day and the hour and the room.*

*And that the second paragraph of the said notice gives the harbor authority of this harbor power to seat one of two disputants and take such finding as is given, without prejudice to any claim, and that that construction is the second limb of a reporting clause, and that this party wrote a clause with that construction in it in April and agreed to it and put it on a market wall in July, and that the said notice has applied it to this party, and that this party did not write the said notice and cannot un-write it and is not going to try.*

*And that the third paragraph of the said notice says that nothing in it touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the writ of the first of May, and that this party has entered both halves of that, that the second season is a named absence, and that a paper which does not mention a thing is not a decision about the thing, and that the writ runs to midnight on the first of March next and is not the Second Bell license and does not lead to one and is not a charter and renews nothing and its ground is not widened and its four names are four and no fifth has been added.*

*And that the said notice contains no position of any office, and no document, and no phrase belonging to any office, and that a man reading it in five years will not find in it one word about who changed a rule or what a rule was called from, and that the question that was put in a market in this harbor on Sunday the thirteenth of August in about forty people's hearing was not answered then and is not answered by this paper and is not going to be answered by this party.*

*And that the fourth paragraph of the said notice requires a sheet or paper bearing this party's own figures, made by this party and put up on a public wall in this harbor on or about the twenty-fifth day of August instant, with a copy of it; and that this party has entered that whoever drafted that paragraph has read a market wall of this harbor or has had somebody read one to him; and that this party does not know who; and that this party has entered that it does not know and is not going to guess, because this yard has a decision of the fifth of July on a page in its own book that it does not put a name on a thing it cannot check, and that decision was made about a question asked in a market and it binds the same way in the other direction.*

*And that this party will be at the said sitting; and that this party will not be represented by anybody; and that this party will answer what is asked and not what it would have chosen; and that this is a decision and not a virtue, and this party is not going to be thanked for it and is not asking.*

*And that this party is going to keep producing its book whole whatever that costs, and the reason is on the page and is that a yard which has said in a market that it produces its book whole had better be the first to produce it, and the worst available place to produce a book is a room somebody else has arranged.*

---

**And at about half past nine that night the shipwright asked him the only question anybody asked him about it, and she asked it in her own trade's voice, and it was one question.**

"**Are you going to read the second column out.**"

"**I do not know.**"

"**Then put that in the second column with a reason and not in the first, and if you put it in the first you will have written a decision and you have not made one, and in three weeks somebody in a room is going to ask you what you decided in September and you are going to have to say *I do not know* and I want that on a page with your hand on it so that it is a thing you said in September and not a thing you worked out in a chair with a pen in front of you and a clerk writing it down.**"

He did it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the tenth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of the writ of the first of May does not know whether he is going to read the second column out in a room; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a boat can check which of his two own hands was on a line at a given second and a man in a room can be checked, and that this one prefers to be checked and does not yet know what he will say; and that this party has three weeks and has entered that it is not going to spend them reading anything, and that a man who tries to find out what he will say in a room by reading is going to find out what he wishes he would have said.*

---

**And at about ten the shipwright said the thing about the box, and it is the argument of this month in one sentence and neither of them is going to improve on it.**

"**That is the same box we built in April,**" she said, "**and it has been turned round so that we are the thing on the table and not the person reading the table. That is all that has happened. I have been trying since Friday to find a part of this week that is worse and there is not one, and I am not going to invent one, because the whole of it is that box and it is going to be a box on a Wednesday morning three weeks from now and I am going to be standing in it.

"**And I have found the thing that is actually going to cost us, and it is not the notice, and I have been not-looking at it since Friday and I looked at it in the shed at about half past eight and it is this. We have an objection. We have had it since the second of May and it is in the first column in your own hand and it is the best sentence in that book: a finding is not a conclusion. A return states a fact and not a conclusion. That is ours and it is true and it has kept us out of a great deal for four months.

"**And we cannot use it. It will not go in there. It was written for a return and this is not a return, and the whole of the trouble is that we have been standing in front of a clause for five months without noticing that the clause was about glass.**"

---

**And the third column was not filled on the Sunday night, and that is the second day since the second of May that it has not been filled on a day, and there is no entry in it and there is a line in the first column about that.**

*That the third column of this book was not filled on Sunday the tenth of September instant; that it was not filled on the eighth of August and this is the second day since the second of May that it has not been filled on a day; that this party does not know who will fill that column next and has not appointed anybody and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Sunday night, and that a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills, and that this party entered that on the ninth of August and has entered it again tonight because it is still true; and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that the forty marks standing to him stand whole and are not reduced and are not going to be, and that this party is not going to hire a boy to read a position back at four marks a week and call that the same thing.*

**No money moved on the Sunday. A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence is in the bag, and it is the whole of it, and a wage of four falls on Tuesday the nineteenth, which is nine days off, and three marks is owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August and has not moved since the eighth of August, and seven pence is owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of the twentieth of August and has not been paid. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. No air in this harbor except what this yard buys at its own two-mark rate and has not bought. A day at the second seam's own face has brought this yard five and five this month and has not brought it more than seven since the twenty-fourth of June, and a wage is four. The bell off the south end is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years, and in about four years a bell is going to be out in a boat at eight marks and a day of a boat then as it is now, and the whole of the craft is the going out, and that standing sentence is unreversed and nothing in this month has touched it.**

**Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about five and he was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and it is one line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week. No name of anything was said in that shed tonight and none went on any page of that book, and the four bare lines in it are still four and are still bare and not one of them has been struck out. No fifth return is made and none is going to be made out of a day that produced nothing. No acknowledgement is entered and the ninth stands as the last and there is not going to be a tenth.**

**And this party cannot answer one question, and knows it cannot answer it tonight, and is going to be asked it in a room.**

**It is not *who changed the rule*. That was asked in a market in August and was not answered and is still not answered and is not this yard's to answer. It is a narrower one and it is three weeks off and it was put to the holder in a shed tonight and he wrote it down in the second column with a reason against it and his own hand: whether he is going to read the second column out in a room.**

**And on Monday the eleventh the wall behind the reeve's bench comes down at eight and comes down bare, and that is the nineteenth Monday since the first of May and the second one of this month, and it is the fourth Monday this party has seen come down, and the sitting is on a day in the week after that one, and this yard is going to be in a room in one week and on a wall in another, and the two are never the same day.**
