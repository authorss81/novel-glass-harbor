# Chapter 163

*A Gland, a Note, and a Sitting at Ten in the Morning*

Friday the fifteenth and Saturday the sixteenth of September, at the mole's toe off the second seam, in the shed, and in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin.

**A compressor's gland has been fitted to this yard's own pump since the nineteenth of May at five marks in a creditor's own hand, and it is on that creditor's page, and it has not been paid, and it is of no use whatever to this yard, and the reason it is of no use whatever is that the thing after the gland is the hose.**

This harbor has no delivery hose. The longest thing of hose in it is about nine fathoms, and that is a piece of hemp with a coupling on the end of it that belongs to a man of the second lay and is lent and is not this yard's. The compressor the yard charges its pots off is the harbor authority's own, and the yard buys a pot on that office's own hose at this yard's own two-mark rate when it buys one at all, and it has not bought one since the eighth of August, and the reason it did not buy one on the fourteenth of September is that nobody was in the water on the fourteenth of September.

---

**A man of the second lay came up the hill on Friday the fifteenth at about eight in the morning with a day booked for the following Friday at his own figure, and the work was ballast shingle spilled against a plate on the wet stone at the toe of the second seam, and it wanted a hose.**

"**Then it cannot be done,**" said Calder Marr. "**Say that now and go and hire somebody with a hose, because there is nobody on this coast within nine miles of here with a hose long enough to reach that stone and I am not going to have you find that out on Friday afternoon with a boat's pay going.**"

"**I have known that since Tuesday,**" the man said. "**I am here because I want it on your book that I asked.**

"**Then it is on our book and I am not taking the booking and I am not taking your money either, and there is nothing in that for either of us and I would rather you had not come, and I am not going to say that last part twice.**"

He wrote it down in front of the man and read it back and the man did not ask to hear it read back and went down the hill.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Friday the fifteenth day of September instant a man of the second lay booked a day with this party for the Friday following at his own figure, that the work was ballast shingle spilled against a plate on the wet stone at the toe of the second seam and required a suction hose, and that this party told him at about eight in the morning that the work could not be done; that the booking was refused and the day was not taken and no money changed hands either way; that the longest thing of hose in this harbor is about nine fathoms and belongs to a man of the second lay and is lent and is not this yard's, and that this harbor has no delivery hose, and that the compressor's gland fitted to this yard's own pump on the nineteenth of May instant is fitted and is sound and is paid for by nobody and is of no use whatever to this yard because the thing after the gland is the hose; and that this party entered the whole of it with the hour against it because a man who booked a day and was told no is a man who is owed an answer and the answer is that there is no hose.*

---

**And then the note came out, and it came out because a shilling did not go in on it, and Calder Marr said the thing about it out loud in the shed at about ten past four in the afternoon and it is nine words and then a reason.**

"**Then we do not re-write it.**

"**That is nine words and there is a reason and I am going to give it once. Re-writing a note you cannot pay is a way of saying you are going to pay it. A man who is owed five marks and is not being paid five marks is owed five marks, and if I go down there on Friday and put a fresh figure on that page then in about four years that page is the only thing in this harbor anybody can hold up and say *on the fourth of January that yard said it would clear this*.

"**Thirty-one stands. Twenty-two and thirteen and eleven, as last written on the fourth of January, and none of them has moved, and none of them is going to move because a man in a shed had a bad morning. The five marks of gland stand on it and are not paid and are not paid for, and the thing they bought cannot do anything, and that is on the page where I put it, and I would rather a creditor's page in this town said a true thing about this yard than said a new one.**"

"**And if he asks you for the five.**"

"**Then he asks me for the five and I say the five is not payable this month and he writes that down, and he has been writing it down since the nineteenth of May and he will go on writing it, and that is the arrangement and it is a bad one and it is not a new one.**"

---

**And on Saturday the sixteenth, at ten in the morning, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, this party was in a room for the second time in a fortnight, and it was not represented by anybody, and it went in the morning with the book in its bag.**

About nine people were in the room. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the eighteenth of August being the seventeenth and on Monday the first of September being the eighteenth and on Monday the eleventh being the nineteenth, and it was bare again on the morning of this sitting with a slate on it and nothing on the slate, which is how it has been for nineteen Mondays while this harbor has had a form coming up its hill.

**Ambrose Sell was at the table with a paper and he said his name and the name of his office before he read it, and he said that he was the clerk of the sitting and not the man who directed it, and that he had not been told anything he could not put on a sheet of paper and was not going to say anything he could not.**

> *First. That the said inquiry stands directed to a sitting to be appointed, to be held at this room in the week following the twelfth day of October next, and that a paper naming the day will be served upon the party and not left with anybody.*
>
> *Second. That the party may apply in writing, at its own cost, for a copy of any record produced or referred to at any sitting, and that this direction grants no copy and makes none and releases nothing, and that no date attaches to any of it, and that whether any office in any city has any intention of doing any of it is not a fact and is not given to the party by this paper.*
>
> *Third. That the construction of the second paragraph of the notice served upon the party on Sunday the tenth of September instant stands, and that nothing in this direction touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the said writ of salvage working granted at this harbor on the first day of May instant.*

"**And I am going to say three things that are not on that paper, and I am going to say them once each, and I am a clerk and this is my trade and not a favor.

"**One. There is a thing that gets written down in a file in a city as one thing which is two things, and I would rather the sentence in that file said it was two. The writ of the first of May is a municipal instrument of the harbor authority of this harbor. It can be suspended by one man with a key and a book, after an inspection for cause, and that man is the officer of this authority and I am not he. This inquiry is an act of a circuit office and of a court in a city. The power to seat one of two disputants and take such finding as is given, without prejudice to any claim, is in the notice of the tenth and it is not the writ's power and it did not come out of the writ, and if it is ever read in a file as the writ's power then that file is wrong and I have put it in my own book today so that it is not wrong on my side.

"**Two. The party is not represented. Nobody has instructed anybody for this yard and this party has retained nobody, and I have written the date and the hour I wrote this and not the hour it was done, which is a practice and has been one since April, and I am going to have to do it in October as well.

"**Three. There is a record in a city that I have in my hand in a file and I am not going to say what is in it, and I am not going to say why it is not in this room, and if you ask me that in October I am going to give you the same answer, and the answer is a clerk's answer and it is the only one I have got.**"

---

**And the fourth paragraph of the notice of the tenth asked for six things, and this party produced all six of them, and said out loud that two of the six are the same line of the first column and one whole item of the list is a line saying that there is no finding at all, and that it is not going to make a seventh thing out of that on the morning it is asked for.

The writ, face down, the way a thing is put down that is going to be picked up again. The four returns, in order, with the days against them, the fourth of May two days late and the tenth of May on the day and the seventeenth of June on the day and the fourteenth of July on the day out of a finding of Monday the tenth. The sheet of the twenty-fifth of August, with a copy of it, which this yard had made itself at the press of the market office on the twenty-fifth of August at a shilling for forty copies and has had in a box in that shed ever since. And the record of any finding made on or about the eighth of August, of which there is one, and it is a line in the first column of the yard's own book in the holder's own left hand with an hour against it, and it was handed over whole and unshortened, and it was not read out.

**And the crew's book itself was produced a second time, whole and unshortened, in about nine minutes, and not one line of it was read aloud in that room, and nobody asked it to, and no copy of it was made in that room.**

"**There is no finding on or about the eighth of August and there was none, and the line that exists says so, and you can have it, and I have given it to you, and I am not going to say a word about it, because a word about it in this room would be a word about it in a file.**

"**And the sixth thing is the one about a place, an hour, a keeper and a limit. There is one record of that and it is the same line and the same hand and I have given it to you. I have not made a second one and I am not going to invent one between now and October.**"

Then he put one sheet of paper on the table face up, in Calder Marr's own left hand, and did not read it out either, and said four sentences about it.

"**I wrote that at nine o'clock this morning in a shed and it is a form and it is not a return. It says four things and they are the four things on it.

"**That no finding of worked glass, whole or in part, was made on or about Tuesday the eighth of August instant, by this party or by anybody in its boat. That nothing was raised on that day by this party. That no return has gone up on that day and none is going to. And that this party will produce its crew's book whole and unshortened at any sitting appointed to it, and that a copy of that book is already in a file in a city and this party has entered that and cannot stop it.

"**A return is a fact and not a conclusion. That is the clause's own rule and it is the yard's own sentence and it is in both. This is not a return and it is not a conclusion and it is not a confession, and I am not going to argue about limbs in this room, because if I start arguing about limbs I will be in this room in March.**"

---

**And they were in that room for fifty-one minutes and nothing else happened in it, and nobody asked to read the second column out, and nobody asked about the third, and nobody asked the one question the holder has had written in his own book in the second column since Sunday the tenth with a reason against it and no name in it.**

They came down the hill at about eleven and went to the boards and did not talk about it, and at about nine that night in the shed with the lamp lit and the ship's hand reading it all back from the top and the holder reading it back after her and nobody else in the room, two lines went into the second column and one went into the first.

*First column, entered on Saturday the sixteenth of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed: that this party attended at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin at ten in the morning of that day, un-represented, that about nine people were in the room, that the crew's book was produced whole and unshortened a second time in about nine minutes and not one line of it was read aloud in that room and no copy of it was made in that room, that the writ and the four returns and the sheet of the twenty-fifth of August with a copy of it and the party's own first-column line for the eighth of August were produced, that a form of four sentences in the holder's own left hand was given to the table and was not read out, and that no copy of any record was made, released or promised to the party at that sitting and no date attached to any of it; and that this party was not represented by anybody and has not instructed anybody and is not going to be represented at the next one either, and that this is a decision and not a virtue.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the sixteenth of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man in this party stood in a room for fifty-one minutes on Saturday the sixteenth instant with a book in a bag at his feet containing five lines that neither he nor anybody in this harbor can check, and that nobody in that room asked him for them, and that he did not read them out, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man can be checked for what he said in a room and cannot be checked for what he did not say, and that the whole of the answer this party has to give about the second column of its own book is that nobody asked; and that on the tenth of September he entered in this column that he did not know whether he was going to read it out in a room, and that on the sixteenth he did not read it out, and that he has entered that he does not know whether the not-reading on the sixteenth was a rule or an answer, and that these are not the same thing and that he is going to be asked which of them it was in the week following the twelfth of October and that he is not going to know by then either.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the sixteenth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a clerk of the circuit office said out loud at a table in this harbor that there is a record in a city in a file which was not in that room and would not say what is in it and would not say why it was not in that room, and that this party did not ask him twice, and has entered why: that a clerk who says what a box holds is the only voice any of the offices in this matter has ever given this harbor, and that a party which spends that voice asking a second question is a party that has been told what a box holds and does not like it.*

**No money moved on the Friday and none on the Saturday. Four marks and twelve shillings and fourpence is in the bag and is the whole of it, and a wage of four goes out of it on Tuesday the nineteenth. The note is not re-written. The gland is fitted and unpaid and useless. The cistern behind the engine house is charged on a rota and this party did not walk it. The light is not on. And the hearing room was used once in September, and the market wall was not touched on any day of this fortnight, and the two of them are never the same day.**
