# Chapter 196

*A Page and a Half, and a Thing in a Room in This Town*

Thursday the twenty-sixth and Friday the twenty-seventh of November, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin.

**On Thursday the twenty-sixth of November the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday, and this party has walked none of them, and the walk is nil and stays nil. And the store over that room was shut from ten in the morning of the said Thursday and the men who count the catch in it were in another building, and one of them is a morning down, and that morning was named on the mole's steps on the nineteenth instant and it is not named here because he is not in this business.**

---

**At ten of the morning on the Thursday, in that room, there were about eleven people in it and a stove against the far wall that had not been lit since the middle of September, and the windows were high and green with salt, and the light came in from behind the people rather than over them, and a clerk of the circuit office of this place took the chair at the end of the table and wrote the date and the hour and the place on a sheet before he said anything, which is a practice and has been one since April.**

Ambrose Sell, clerk of the circuit office and clerk of that sitting.

"**Three things and then the sheet.

"**One. This office is not sitting to decide anything. Nothing said in this room is a finding against anybody, and this office has not convicted this yard of anything and is not going to today, and I am going to write that on the first line of the record so that nobody in a city can pick up a page out of it and leave it on the table.

"**Two. The sheet is read out whole, by the person who is going to be responsible for the reading of it, and not by the man who brought it up the hill. There is a reason for that and the reason is mine and not yours, and it is that in October a man read a book in this room and the book had a column in it that he gave up, and this office is not going to do the same thing twice with the same yard in the same room.

"**And three. If anybody in this room cannot hear, say so and I will stop and start again. I am not going to have a sheet read out in this room in a voice that half of it did not get.**"

"**The ship's hand of the party will read it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then I will enter that, and I will enter the hour.**"

---

**And the ship's hand of the party did read it, and she read it from the top of the bench and she did not read it fast, and she read each head as a head and she read out loud what was under each head as well as what was in it, and the holder of the writ of the first of May sat two feet off with his hands on his knees and did not read it and did not interrupt her once.**

She read the first head, being that this sheet is a copy of a record and not the thing, and that the thing is in the custody room of that office and is not on the hill and is not going to be. She read the second head, being one item of recovered transparent mineral, and that the number against it is not on the sheet, and she read that out twice because about four people at the back of that room had not heard it the first time and she said so.

She read the third head, being found in water in a bucket in a boat and entered in the keeper's line of a party's own return without being asked, not dried, not wiped, no cloth put into it, sealed by a courier and entered, and she said at the end of it, *and that head is a check on this yard and not on that office, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be given the credit for it either.*

She read the fourth head, being the limits the record carries, printed in the record's own words, and she read it slowly. A record of a material is a record of one person's frame. It is not a record of a sea. Nothing on this sheet is a finding of consent. This sheet is a record of provenance and not of the water.

She read the fifth head, and her voice did not change on it and the room noticed that it did not change on it. A manifest referred to in this record, as that manifest stood when that office first entered the item, and as that manifest stands now, do not agree. A line of it has been altered. The alteration bears a date. This record does not show by whose hand, at whose instance, or with what intent the alteration was made, and this record names no person.

And then she read the sixth head last, and she read it slower than the other five, and it is the head that is about nothing at all.

Not who altered it. Not what the item carried. Not who held it before it was in a bucket. Not consent. Not motive. And not that anything on this sheet is true.

She put it down squared to the edge of the table, and about eleven people in a cold room did not say anything, and the clerk wrote for about two minutes and read it back and the ship's hand said it was her own words and would not be improved on, and the clerk entered that the answer was given by the ship's hand and not by the holder of the writ, which is the second time that has been entered in that room and the clerk said so and gave the reason, which is that a party which has been made to do a thing once in front of about nine people will be made to do it again, and that the second time ought to be on the record as the second time.

---

**And at about half past eleven on that Thursday morning the clerk put the second thing on the schedule, and said that he had written the words of it in his own page first, and that it is a question and not a trick, and that he wanted it answered in the room and not in a doorway afterwards.**

"**There is one item of recovered transparent mineral in the custody room of this office. It has not moved. Nobody has applied for it since the fourteenth day of October two years past, including your party, and I am not going to make a mystery out of that and neither are you.

"**Does the party apply for it.**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I want the no and the reason in the record, and the reason is not that this yard is frightened of it.

"**The sheet on that table is a copy of a record, and the record is a record about a thing, and the thing is in a room in this town behind a door this yard has not been asked for and is not asking for. A copy cannot be turned over in a man's hand. There is no hand in this harbor that has held that thing since the fourteenth of October two years past, and mine is not one of them, and this yard has entered that four times and I am going to enter it a fifth time today because in about four years somebody is going to read a page and decide this yard got the thing.

"**And the reason we are not applying is not a reason about the thing at all. It is that the only road to that thing on this coast runs through a court that has not sat, and this yard holds a writ with four names on it and a reporting clause on it, and every office that could put a person in front of that thing would have to ask us something we entered on the sixteenth day of November, which is that this party does not put a name on a thing it cannot check.

"**And now the half of it that costs something to say, and I am saying it in the room and not in a shed, because a thing said in a shed on a Sunday is a thing said in a market on a Tuesday by the Thursday.

"**If that thing came up this hill this afternoon it would be worth more to this yard than every mark that has gone through this yard's hands since the first of May, and I would not know what to do with it. I would be a man in a working harbor holding a thing that cannot be checked, in a town where about four hundred people can read about him on a wall, and there are four people of this coast who wrote a line in October about what gets said and what does not, and this yard has not asked to have that line lifted and is not going to, and it is not going to use a piece of glass to be the reason it does.

"**Both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other. The thing is not ours. This yard wants it. This yard is not going to ask, and I am not going to stand in a market in four years and say I did not want it, and I am not going to stand in a market in four years and say that wanting it was a reason.

"**Enter it. And enter that the item stays in the custody of that office, and that this party accepts that in this room, in front of a clerk, on this date, and is not going to be told it was sorry about it in a street.**"

Ambrose Sell wrote it down. It took him about four minutes, which was the longest thing he had written in either of the two days, and he read it back and the man said it was his own words and that he was not going to improve on them, and the clerk entered that too.

---

**On the Friday the twenty-seventh there were about nine people in that room and the light was going by a quarter past three and the clerk had a bundle on the table that was forty days old and belonged to no part of this.**

"**I am going to ask the party one question that is not about the sheet,**" said Ambrose Sell, "**and I have written it in my own page first, and I am going to give you my reason for asking it and the reason is my own trade and not yours, and if you think I have come to catch you in something then you have not been in the room in October and I would rather you had that in front of you now than in a bundle in a year.

"**The record in front of me says that on the twenty-ninth day of October last the holder of the writ of the first of May told this office in this room that a sheet of paper had been in two hands at two times, and gave four grounds for it, and said in the same breath that it does not say which of the two hands is right, or why, or whose the two hands were. It says that I said in that room that I had had that paper since the tenth of September and could see the two inks myself, and that I entered the fact in that order. And it says that the holder said *I do not know* three times and would not say it a fourth time in a different way.

"**And four lines below that, on the same page, it carries an entry of the eighteenth day of July last out of a second column, in the holder's own words, about six joints in a crevice and the spacing the same at every one and the fastenings the same at every one, and it carries the line of the twenty-third day of July that says the party did not stop him and that it was wrong in three parts out of four and has not taken it back.

"**And on Thursday a sheet was read out in this room that says a line of a manifest was altered and does not say by whose hand. And the party that is holding that sheet is the party that cannot say what a join on a piece of paper is for.

"**So here is the question and it is not a trap and it is not about the sheet. Can the holder of the writ say to this room, in his own words, what was in the water off the south end of this mole on any day this year.**"

The stove that was not a fire put out more cold than usual and about nine people waited.

"**Four things, and you can have all four,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to give them in about a minute and I am not going to be long about it because there is not a great deal in it.

"**I can give you a figure and an hour and a state of the tide and the name of the man who hauled the line, and every one of those four you can check against a table in a building you have been in.

"**I cannot tell you what was under that water. I have never been able to. I went down on a line and I came up on a line and what I know is what came up and what a slate says and what four men saw with their own eyes, and that is the whole of what I have ever known about any water and it always was.

"**And I said a thing in a market in this harbor on the eighteenth of July in about eleven people's hearing that was wrong in three parts out of four of the same sentence. I have not taken it back. I am not going to take it back in this room and I am not going to be asked to, and I am not refusing to be asked to. It is on that page four lines above the question you have just asked me and it is going to be on the page with it, and this yard is not going to come to this office and ask for two rooms.

"**I cannot vouch for the water with my own ears. I have not been able to since the seventh of July and I have got no better at it in seven weeks, and the best reader of joins on this coast is a man who can tell you that two things were not made at one time and cannot tell you which of the two is the true one, and that is a thing about a man and not about a trade.

"**And I am not going to get out of it by being right about something today. A yard that has been in a room like this is a yard whose figures are worth what a clerk's are, and a man of thirty-six of this harbor said that out loud at the head of this dock on the first of November and he was not wrong, and I have entered his sentence against myself in my own book in my own hand with a reason against the line, and I am not going to improve on it in a room.**"

Ambrose Sell wrote for a long time and read the whole of it back, and the man said it was his own words, and the clerk entered it, and about nine people in a cold room watched the best reader of joins in that harbor say out loud in front of a clerk that he could not vouch for the water, and nobody in the room said a single thing, which is what about nine people say about a man who has told the truth about himself in a room where a page is going to catch it.

"**Then that is the sitting,**" said the clerk, "**and I am going to close it in my own words and read it back and neither of you is to stop me.

"**There was a sheet of about a page and a half read out in this room, whole, with its limits on it, by the ship's hand of the party and not by the holder of the writ. It carries on its face a statement that a line of a manifest was altered and it carries on its face a statement that it does not show by whose hand, and this office has not been asked to say and is not going to be asked again. The item itself is in the custody of this office and the party does not apply for it and accepts that in this room. The holder of the writ of the first of May said in this room that he cannot vouch for the water with his own ears, and said that he said a thing in a market in July that was wrong in three parts out of four and has not taken it back, and said that a yard which has been in a room like this is a yard whose figures are worth what a clerk's are.

"**Nothing was decided. Nobody was convicted. Nobody was charged with anything. No office in this harbor is a villain this week and I have put that on the first line of the record as well.**"

Nobody followed him down the hill and the two of them walked up it at about twenty past three in the light going and neither of them said anything about the room for about a hundred yards.

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-seventh day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a sitting was held in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin on Thursday the twenty-sixth and Friday the twenty-seventh days of November instant at ten of the morning on both days, and that about eleven people were in the room on the first day and about nine on the second, and that the store was shut behind them on the first day from ten in the morning and the men who count the catch in it were in another building, and that one man of the tide gang of this harbor is a morning down for the want of the room, which was named in the open on the mole's steps on the nineteenth instant and is not named in this book and this party does not know his name and has not asked; and that a clerk of the circuit office of this place, being Ambrose Sell, took the chair at the end of the table, and entered the date and the hour and the place before he said anything, and entered on the first line of the record that this office is not sitting to decide anything, that nothing said in that room is a finding against anybody, and that this office has not convicted this yard of anything and is not going to; and that the said clerk stated that the sheet is read out whole by the person responsible for the reading of it and not by the man who brought it up the hill, and gave the reason, which is that in October a man read a book in that room and gave up a column of it, and that this office is not going to do the same thing twice with the same yard in the same room; and that he stated that if anybody in that room could not hear he would stop and start again.*

*And that the sheet of the twenty-fourth instant was read out in that room on the said Thursday, whole, by the ship's hand of this party, and that she read each head as a head and read out what was under each head as well as what was in it, and that the holder of the writ of the first of May did not read it and did not interrupt her once, and that she read the second head twice because about four people at the back of the room had not had it the first time and said so; and that she said at the end of the third head that that head is a check on this yard and not on that office and that she would not be thanked for it and would not be given the credit for it either; and that the clerk read the whole of the reading back and the said woman said it was her own words and would not be improved on, and that the clerk entered that the answer came from the ship's hand and not from the holder of the writ, which is the second time that has been entered in that room, and gave the reason, which is that a party which has been made to do a thing once in front of about nine people will be made to do it again, and that the second time ought to be on the record as the second time.*

*And that the clerk then put a question in that room in his own words, having written the words of it in his own page first, which was whether the party applies for the item of recovered transparent mineral in the custody room of that office; and that the holder answered no in about four minutes, and gave the whole of the reason, and asked that the answer and the reason be entered; and that the reason is that the sheet is a copy of a record and the record is a record about a thing and the thing is in a room in this town, and that a copy cannot be turned over in a man's hand, and that no hand in this harbor has held that thing since the fourteenth day of October two years past and the holder's is not one of them, which this yard has entered four times and entered a fifth time on the said day; and that the reason the party is not applying is not a reason about the thing, and that the only road to that thing on this coast runs through a court that has not sat, and that every office that could put a person in front of it would have to ask this party something it entered on the sixteenth instant, which is that this party does not put a name on a thing it cannot check; *And that on Friday the twenty-seventh instant the said clerk put a second question in that room, having written the words of it in his own page first, which was whether the holder of the writ of the first of May could say in that room, in his own words, what was in the water off the south end of this mole on any day this year; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the twenty-seventh day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a true sheet was read out in a room in this harbor on the twenty-sixth instant by the person responsible for reading it, and that the two costs of that are not on any page and cannot be, and that the first is that a yard which has been in a room like this is a yard whose figures are worth what a clerk's are, and that the second is that about nine people heard a man of thirty-six say that at the head of a dock on the first of November and about nine people heard a man of thirty-six of this harbor hear the same thing said about himself in a room on the Friday, and that the first cost was on a market wall and the second one is going into a file in a city, and that neither of them is a thing a man on a mole can get at the bottom of; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what this yard is worth in four years to the four people of this coast it did not tell about is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the four of them.*

*And that the said man said in that room that he wanted the item, and said it in front of a clerk and about nine people, and that this yard has entered that against itself in the first column above and is not going to be thanked for it and is not asking and has not asked to have the limit of four people of this coast lifted once in three months and is not going to ask twice; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether a man is honest about wanting a thing is not a thing anybody can check from a mole, and that a man in a market in four years will take the wanting and leave the not-asking, and that both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, and that the reason does not go into a record and there is nowhere for it to go.*

**No money came into this yard on the Thursday and none on the Friday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and one mark and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the first day of December instant.
