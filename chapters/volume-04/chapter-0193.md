# Chapter 193

*Four Marks on a Board, and Two Shillings on a Counter*

Friday the twentieth and Saturday the twenty-first of November, on the face of the second seam, at a bonesetter's room off the market, and in the shed.

**On Friday the twentieth of November this yard worked about four hours and forty minutes on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line went over the side and back four times.**

**Gross on the board thirteen.** The eighth, one. The share, one. The victuals of the day, one. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Nine of thirteen paid at the mole and the yard's being four, and the four went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people and no figure was argued, and the eighth and the share came off the top of the day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May.

The strapping came off on the Saturday and not on the Friday, and everybody in that yard knew it on the Friday, and the hirer knew it before he came and did not ask about it.

"**The book is mine today,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and the count and the times, and I haul the line over with this hand,**" and she held up the right one, "**and I do not take a handle. The handle is his and it is a bad handle and he has been complaining about it since the fourteenth of October, and I am not going to be the reason it comes off.**"

The man of the third lay took the pump himself and said one thing at about eleven o'clock, which was that he had asked for a pump and not for a party, and that a party of one and a half is a party of one, and that he had asked for a party of one and a half and had got it and was not going to say anything about it in a market. Neither of them said anything about that afterwards.

They worked from eight until about half past one. A hand lead's line went over the side and back four times and the count and the times went onto a slate in the ship's hand and the slate went into the book on the board in her own writing and the holder read it back after her and neither of them said anything about it. Five marks and eight shillings and five pence is in the bag by about half past one, which is nine hundred and one pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty of those.

---

**And at about ten in the morning on Saturday the twenty-first, in a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, a bonesetter of this harbor took a strapping off a forearm and charged two shillings for it and put the money in her own hand on her own counter.**

Hessa Barlow, forty-four, whose name has been on a painted board over that door since before either brother in the shed up the hill was born.

"**Two shillings,**" she said, "**and I am going to tell you why it is not the three shillings and sixpence of the twenty-fourth of October, so that you do not think I have gone up on the figure.

"**That was a set. A setting is a thing I do once and I am paid for once. This is a strapping coming off, and a strapping coming off is not a bone coming off, and if it were a bone coming off it would be four shillings and there would be a conversation.

"**And on the seventh of November I did not charge you, and I want the reason on your page because I am not going to be asked about it in four years. A visit on the fourteenth day is not a visit. It is a look. You asked me what the fourteen days were for and I told you and I have not changed my mind, and a man who is thanked for a look is a man who will be thanked for it again and the next time it will be a thing that matters.**"

She worked it off in about four minutes and did not hurry the last corner, and the arm came out of the sling and the skin across the inside of the wrist was the color of the rest of her and was stiff.

"**There. Now I am going to say what it is, in my own words, and it is not in your book and I do not want it in mine.

"**The bone has knitted and it knitted straight. That is the good of it and I am not going to be coy about the good of it. The wrist does not turn all the way round in a cold month and it is not going to turn all the way round in a cold month for two years, and that is not a thing that gets better by working at it, and the only thing that makes it worse is being in a boat on a cold tide with a fall in your hand.

"**And I am not going to be thanked, and you are not going to be thanked, and if you thank me I will tell you the figure for being thanked.**"

"**I am not going to thank you,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to pay for the two visits, because there were not two visits, and that is the same sentence twice and it is not an improvement.**

"**What I am going to do is book days.**"

"**Then book them.**" Nessa Pike had her right hand flat on the counter. "**And I am not going to be the tender and the pump on a day that matters, and I am not going to be a person on a handle and a person on a fall in the same four hours until the handle is not a bad handle. I have got a quarter of a year out of the left side of me and I told you the figure of it on the seventh and it has not moved.

"**So: the yard is a party of one and a half until the first of March and not two, and the board is the board, and every day you book from now on this month is a day you book with a man in it. I do not care how it comes out in the figure. I want it on the page in your hand and not in mine.**"

"**In my hand,**" said Calder Marr, "**and tonight, and with the day of the twentieth on the same line, because the day of the twentieth is the same argument in a different place.**"

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-first day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on Friday the twentieth instant this yard worked about four hours and forty minutes on the face of the second seam landward of the third seam's mark on a hirer's own account with nobody in the water, and that the gross on the board was thirteen, and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it, and that nine of the thirteen was paid at the mole and the yard's was four; and that the ship's hand kept the book and the count and the times and hauled the line over with her right hand and did not take a pump handle, the reason being that a strapping comes off on the twenty-first and not before; and that the hirer said he had asked for a pump and not for a party and that a party of one and a half is a party of one; and that the said holder entered that the day was booked by this party with a person in it and not a person short, and that the cost of that is a day that went slower than a day with three on it, and that a day that goes slower is a day not earned whole and is entered as a day not earned whole and not as a day at a figure; and that the said woman entered in her own hand on the said evening that until the first of March next this yard is a party of one and a half and not two, and that every day this yard books in this month is a day it books with a man in it, and that she does not want it in her book and it is in the holder's.*

*And that on Saturday the twenty-first instant at about ten in the morning, at a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, a bonesetter of this harbor, being Hessa Barlow, forty-four, took a strapping off the left forearm of the said woman and charged two shillings, and that the two shillings were paid out of this yard's bag into her own hand at her own counter at about ten past ten in the morning, and that the figure of the three shillings and sixpence of the twenty-fourth of October instant is a figure for a setting and this figure is a figure for a strapping coming off and that the two are different figures and neither of them is the other; and that she said that she did not charge on the seventh instant and gave the reason, which is that a visit on the fourteenth day is a look and not a visit, and that she does not want to be thanked about it in four years; and that she said the bone has knitted straight, and that the wrist does not turn all the way round in a cold month and will not for two years, and that the figure of the rest of it is a quarter of a year out of the left side and that figure was given on the seventh instant and has not moved.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the twenty-first day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a bonesetter of this harbor said in her own room on the said morning that the good of it is that the bone knitted straight and that the rest of it is a wrist that does not turn all the way round in a cold month for two years, and that the only thing that makes it worse is a boat on a cold tide with a fall in the hand, and that she said the whole of that in her own words and would not have it in this yard's book and it is in here because the holder wrote it down, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check a strapping and a figure and cannot check what a bone is going to do in a cold month in two years, and that the only person in this harbor who can check it is a woman of forty-four who has said she does not want it in her own book.*

*And that the said woman said on the said morning that she is not going to be the tender and the pump on a day that matters and is not going to be a person on a handle and a person on a fall in the same four hours until the handle is not a bad handle, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that the difference between a yard of two and a yard of one and a half is a figure on a board and is not a figure anybody can check by getting off a mole, and that the whole of what it costs is a day that went slower and a quarter of a year out of the left side of the only shipwright on this coast who can hold a pump for six hours, and that the quarter of a year is on the first column of the seventh instant and has not been moved and is not going to be.*

**Five marks and eight shillings and five pence came into this yard on the twentieth of November and nothing came in on the twenty-first, and two shillings went out of it on the twenty-first, and five marks and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, and that is eight hundred and seventy-seven pence. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and none of them has moved. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid, useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and nobody has been sent for him. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, and it is not being paid out of a bonesetter's figure and the two figures have not been on one line. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and no one has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and two shillings to a bonesetter is not a payment of it and is not going to be entered as one. Twenty-five lines and one hundred marks stand in the wage column with the fifth of March the oldest, and there are nineteen envelopes on a bench, and five weeks have fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November and the seventeenth of November instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and sixty-one days up the crown of this mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October stands with its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and no better letter about it was written in these two days. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in the hand of the woman making it, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them; nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ of the first of May runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted.**
