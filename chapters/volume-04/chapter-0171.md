# Chapter 171

*A Rota Day, and a Woman With a Counter Off the Fish Market*

Thursday the first and Friday the second of October, on the mole's crown, at the head of the dock, in a doorway, and in the shed.

**The cistern behind the engine house was charged at about ten on Thursday morning at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party did not walk it, and that is the first of six rota days in this month and this party has walked none of the six and has walked none of the fourteen since the third of August.**

The pump came off the bench at seven because a pump is a thing that comes off a bench, and it stayed off the bench all day because there was no day of work booked for either Thursday or Friday, and the first of October is the ninth day of a month with a wage in it and the yard has earned eight marks in it and has paid four.

A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag. Three hundred and eight pence, and the slate said so when Calder Marr wrote it on Thursday morning, and it was the same figure on the thirtieth of September and it is going to be the same figure on the last page of this month or it is not, and nobody in that shed said so out loud.

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the first of October instant at about four in the afternoon in this shed: that the note against the plate at the fifth seam stands open, and that this party has not reached the plate, has not measured it and has not touched it, and has not reopened the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January; and that this party entered on Sunday the tenth of September instant that whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed, and that this party does not know what that is, and that the entry still stands and is not going to be improved on in a month.*

---

**And at about eleven on the Thursday morning a woman of fifty-eight came up the hill with a flat board under her arm and about nine sheets of paper on it, and she gave her name before she said anything else, which is what a person does who has come up a hill to be left alone.**

Pernel Saye, of this harbor, a copyist, with a counter in a room off the fish market that is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, where a sheet is a penny and forty sheets a shilling.

"**I want four minutes of a man who reads a page,**" she said, "**and I am not going to stand here and explain the whole of it, because the whole of it is a number.

"**I copied for that circuit office on the eighth of September. Eleven sheets, out of a file they keep in a room on the second floor that I have been up to twice in my life and am not going up again. It came in on the Friday and went out on the Monday, and the office paid me at the going rate and did not argue, and I have not been paid yet and I am not going up there about it, because I will be paid on the twentieth and it is eleven days and I am not a fool.**

"**And here is the thing. There are two leaf numbers on my sheet. Their slip says one of them and my sheet says another, and they are in the same hand on the same piece of paper about two inches apart, and it is not my hand, and I did not copy either of them, I only wrote the one at the head of my own sheet the way I write it on everything I have ever copied in this town.

"**And the head of my own sheet is the only part of it that is mine. Everything else on that board came out of their file, and it is not mine, and I did not look at it and I would not look at it if it was left out on the step.**"

She put the board down on the bench and turned the top sheet face up, and it was a pale ruled thing with a head at the top in a small steady hand, and at the top it said:

> *Job 4 — Fri. 8 Sept — Morrow circuit office, copying room.*
> *From the file of the ASTER GANNET. Leaf 62. One leaf, copied entire, eleven sheets, returned with the file.*

And the rest of it was somebody else's writing and was not looked at.

Calder Marr read the four lines at the top of it twice and then a third time, which was a thing he did and was aware he was doing.

"**I cannot help you with that,**" he said.

"**I did not think you could. What I want to know is whether the number at the head of my sheet is the number on their slip, and I cannot read their second clerk's figures, and I am not going to take a sheet up two flights of stairs on my own time to ask her whether she can count to sixty-two.**"

"**Then read me the number off their slip.**"

She had it on a second sheet, and it was the same, and she put the two side by side on the bench and let him look at both of them and said nothing at all while he did it.

"**It is the same number,**" he said. "**Sixty-two. And that is the whole of what I can do for you, and it is not a thing you needed a man for.

"**And I am going to say the other thing, because you carried it up this hill and I would rather say it now than have you find out that I did not notice it. That file is the *Aster Gannet*'s. There are three people in this harbor who would say that name and be frightened by it, and you are not one of them and you have just told me you have been up those stairs twice in your life, and I believe you, and I believe it because of how you said it, and believing a person is not the same as being able to use what they have told you.

"**And I have got a book of my own in a file in a city that went into a file on the ninth of September and cannot be got out of it, and I have got a form served on me on the tenth of September that I do not know who wrote, and I have not got that file and I am not going to ask for it, and there is nobody in this town who can get it for me and if there were I could not pay her.**"

"**Then it is not for you,**" said Pernel Saye, and picked her board up. "**And I have made a mistake and I am sorry, and I will not keep you up this hill again about a number.**"

"**You keep your sheet,**" said Calder Marr. "**And I want that number in a book of mine in my own hand with your name on the line and the date against it, because I do not know what it is and a thing a man cannot check belongs in the first column with the reason that he cannot check it.**"

"**You are not going to get a reason out of me. I have been up those stairs twice and I am not going up again, and that is a trade fact about my legs and my trade and it is not about a file.**"

"**Then the reason is that you have been up the stairs twice in your life and are not going a third time. That is checkable. Put that.**"

She put her thumb on the corner of the board and said, *that is checkable*, and went down the hill, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.

*First column, entered on Thursday the first of October instant at about half past eleven in the morning in this yard, in about six people's hearing: that a woman of this harbor, who gave her name as Pernel Saye, who is fifty-eight and keeps a copying counter in a room off the fish market that is open to anybody, came up this hill with a board of papers and asked this party to read a page for her; that she said that on Friday the eighth of September instant she copied eleven sheets for the copying room of the circuit office of this harbor out of a file that office keeps in a room on a second floor, and that the file is the file of the ASTER GANNET, and that the leaf number at the head of her own sheet is sixty-two, and that she copied the leaf entire and returned it with the file; and that this party read the four lines at the head of that sheet twice and did not read the rest of it and did not ask to; and that the number sixty-two is entered in this column in the holder's own left hand with the date against it and the said woman's name on the line and a reason against the line, and that the reason is that this party cannot check the number, cannot see the file, has not asked for the file and is not going to, and that the said woman has been up those stairs twice in her life and is not going up a third time, which anybody in this harbor may come up this hill and ask her.*

---

**And on Friday the second a letter went into the letterbox on this hill, and it cost four pence, and it was in the holder's own left hand, and it is the only money this yard has spent on itself in nine weeks.**

The letterbox on the hill is a box with a slot in it and it is emptied on Tuesdays and Fridays, and the man of the first lay who has a boat of about two hundred tons wrote to this yard on Wednesday and asked for one thing, which was the figure of the bar of gravel, so that he could put his word under a line in a paper of his own about a boat of his own that went on the ground on the second of August. The yard had refused him that figure in a doorway in about eleven sentences on Monday the twenty-fifth of September, and had refused it four times over, and the four reasons were good and checkable and one of them was that a printed thing cannot be certified afterwards without becoming a different kind of thing.

"**Then it goes in the same four lines and in the same hand,**" said Calder Marr, "**and the reason it goes at all is that a man has asked me a question in writing, and a man who asks in writing and gets nothing in writing has been told no by a man with a door and not by an office, and I am not going to let that stand in about sixty boats without a decision of mine behind it.**"

"**You are going to put a refusal in a letterbox,**" said Nessa Pike, "**on the fourth day after a refusal in a doorway that came back as four words in a city in two days, and I want it said out loud in this shed that I have said the shape of that and you have heard it.**"

"**I have heard it.

"**And I am going to do it, and here is the whole of my reason and it is not a good one. If that man stands in a market in a fortnight and says the yard would not give him a figure, he is going to be right, and there is no way to stop a man saying a true thing. The only thing I have got is that if he says it, I said it too, in the same hand, in a line, with the four reasons on it, and a man in a city who has got four words off a doorway off a man of this yard can look the whole of that up.

"**And it does not get better. I have found that out in three weeks and it is not going to get better in another three, and the first time I published something it cost a boy four days of a month and the second time it cost a wage, and the third has cost four pence, and I do not know what the fourth is going to cost and I am not going to find out by not posting a letter.**"

"**Post it,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and pay the four pence out of the bag where anybody can see you pay it, and do not put the four pence on the same line as mine.**"

"**It is not the same four pence, and there is going to be a line in the book on Tuesday morning that says so, and your seven pence is the seventh of August and it is standing and it is not being paid and it is not going to be paid out of the bag this month, and if one of those two ever gets put beside the other I will take the book off the bench and go down to the boards.**"

*First column, entered on Friday the second of October instant at about four in the afternoon in this shed: that a letter was put into the letterbox on this hill on the Friday morning in the holder's own left hand, and that the postage of it, four pence, was paid out of the bag at the counter in this town in the holder's own hand and is entered here with the hour; that the said letter states in four lines that the figure of the bar of gravel was given in print on the twenty-fifth of August past and stands, that a certification is a conclusion with a person's name at the foot of it, that there is a figure of two and a half for the same bar made four times in nineteen years which this party is not going to decide between in a shed, and that the party has not taken the printed figure back; and that it is the holder's own hand and not a clerk's; and that this party has entered that it has published a refusal in the ordinary post and has entered why, and that the reason is that a man who has been refused in a doorway is going to say so in a market whether this party writes to him or not, and that the whole of the difference this party has to offer him is his own copy of the four reasons; and that the postage of the said letter is not the seven pence owing to this party's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, and that those are two different figures and neither of them is the other, and that the seven pence stands unpaid.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the second of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the thirteenth day of September instant that a document is a thing an office says about itself, and that on the second day of October instant a woman of this harbor carried up this hill, unasked and at her own cost, four lines at the head of a sheet carrying the number of a leaf of a file that the Registry of this harbor keeps in a room on a second floor, and that the holder can check the number and the date and the office and can check nothing else whatever; and that this is the first time in four volumes that this yard has found out a thing about a document, and that he cannot check it and cannot use it and cannot ask for it, and that the reason it is in the first column and not the second is that any man in this harbor may come up this hill and ask Pernel Saye whether she brought that number to this yard on the first of October instant, and can go and look at her book, and the answer is yes.*

**Alden Marr was at the far bench on Friday the second from about ten until about half past three, and he was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and the far bench behind the shipwright was empty when she came in at nine on the Friday morning and empty again when she left it at half past ten at night.**

**No money came into this yard on the Thursday and none on the Friday, and four pence went out of it on the Friday, which is three hundred and four pence, or a mark and twelve shillings, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the sixth of October, four days off. Three marks is owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August and unmoved since that day, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence is owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved since the fourth of January at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Twenty lines and eighty marks in the wage column with the fifth of March the oldest, fifteen envelopes on a bench in the chest, and two weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August and the fifth of September, entered together and never one after the other. Five marks of compressor's gland on a creditor's page, unpaid since the nineteenth of May and of no use whatever to this yard because the thing after the gland is the hose. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and that man was not asked anything this month. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and the column has not been filled since the first of August. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the six marks and two days have not been paid and have not been offered. The two objects of nobody's are on the crown of this mole about a hundred and thirty-seven days and neither of them has moved. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in her hands. The cistern behind the engine house was charged by strangers at no figure on the first instant, and this party did not walk it.**

**The two objects of nobody's are on the crown of this mole about a hundred and thirty-seven days, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide has been unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by an officer this yard emptied, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and no occasion was entered this month and no measurement was taken, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and nobody in this harbor has ever said why.**

**And a hearing is to sit in the week following the twelfth of this month at ten of the clock in the morning at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and a paper naming the day will be served on this party and not left with anybody, and this party has entered that it will be in the room, that it will not be represented by anybody, and that it will produce what it is asked for and not what it would have chosen, and that this is a decision and is not a virtue and this party is not going to be thanked for it and is not asking.**
