# Chapter 178

*A Paper Served in a Doorway, and a Sitting at Ten in the Morning*

Thursday the fifteenth and Friday the sixteenth of October, in a doorway, at the mole's steps, and in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin.

**At about eleven in the morning on Thursday the fifteenth of October a clerk of the circuit office came up this hill with a paper in his hand and three people in the doorway of this shed, and he served it there, and he said his name and the name of his office before he read it, which is what he did on the tenth of September and the sixteenth of September and is a practice and has been one since April.**

Ambrose Sell, clerk of the circuit office, and clerk of that sitting.

> *First. That the sitting appointed by the direction of the sixteenth day of September last is appointed for Friday the sixteenth day of October instant, at ten of the clock in the morning, at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and that the said sitting is to be held before the magistrate of this circuit.*
>
> *Second. That the parties named in the said inquiry, being four, are required to attend; and that a party may be represented if it chooses to be.*
>
> *Third. That the said party is required to produce the six things required of it by the fourth paragraph of the notice served upon it on Sunday the tenth day of September instant, and that the said six things are required again and are not a new requirement, and that the production of them is not a concession and is not an admission, and that the said party has produced them once already and is not asked to argue them again.*
>
> *Fourth. That a schedule of the records before the said inquiry is read into the record of the said sitting by the clerk of it, and that a copy of the said schedule is not given to any party, and that a party may apply in writing at its own cost for a copy of any record produced or referred to at the said sitting, which grants no copy and makes none and releases nothing and no date attaches to any of it.*
>
> *Fifth. That the registry file of the vessel named ASTER GANNET, one volume, is before the said inquiry; that one leaf of the said volume is called for and is not produced; that the registry has entered the reason of that non-production on a form of its own, dated the second day of September last, a copy of which is on the record of the said inquiry; and that the said form is not before the said sitting and is not to be produced to the said party.*
>
> *Sixth. That nothing in this paper touches, grants, refuses or deals with the second season of the writ of salvage working granted at this harbor on the first day of May instant.*
>
> *A. Sell, clerk.*

"**And the magistrate's name is not on this copy,**" said the clerk, "**because the copy carries the appointing line and not the foot of the original, and the foot of the original carries a name, and the name is *Halven Rook*, magistrate of this circuit. He has not been in this room and he is not going to be, and nothing that has happened in this harbor in nine years has put him in a room, and I am going to be asked in a fortnight by a man with a pen where that name comes from and I am going to tell him the truth, which is that it is on a paper and not in a room.

"**And I am going to say two things that are not on that paper and I am going to say them once each.

"**One. I have read what that form says, because the copy that goes on the record is in the bundle and I have had the bundle since Tuesday. I am not going to tell you what is on the leaf. I am not going to tell you that I have read what the form says either, and the reason for the second of those two is that in about four years a man in a city is going to be able to say that a clerk of this circuit knew what a page of that file said and could not deny it. I have a page. I am going to put on it what I can enter and not what I can be asked about afterwards. That is the whole of my trade and it is not a courtesy.

"**Two. You are not represented and I am going to have to do that again in about forty minutes, and I have written the date and the hour I wrote this and not the hour it was done, and that is a practice.

"**And that is all of it. The party may say anything it has come to say and I would rather it said it now than in a fortnight on a doorstep.**"

Nessa Pike had the book under her arm and she said one sentence, which was: **"Then say it now, and we have come to say one thing, and we have said it twice on the way up the hill.**"

---

**And on Friday the sixteenth of October at ten of the morning, in that room, the wall behind the reeve's bench was bare, and it had been bare since the Monday of the twelfth, which was twenty-three Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and about nine people were in the room and the tide gang store was shut behind them and the counting of the fish was going on somewhere else in the building.**

The party was not represented. It had instructed nobody and had retained nobody. It produced the six things of the fourth paragraph, whole, in about eleven minutes.

The writ of the first of May, face down, the way a thing is put down that is going to be picked up again. The four returns, in order, with the days against them, the fourth of May two days late and the tenth of May on the day and the seventeenth of June on the day and the fourteenth of July on the day out of a finding of Monday the tenth. The sheet of the twenty-fifth of August with a copy of it, which this yard made itself at the press of the market office on that day at a shilling for forty copies. The record of any finding made on or about the eighth of August, of which there is one, and it is a line in the first column in the holder's own left hand with an hour against it, and it says there is no finding, and it was handed over whole and unshortened. And the record the party keeps of a place, an hour, a keeper and a limit, which is the same line and the same hand.

**Not one line of the crew's book was read aloud in that room. Nobody asked it to be, and it was not read, and no copy of any record was made in that room, and the party has entered that and does not intend to be thanked for it.**

Then the clerk read the schedule into the record in about four minutes, and a schedule is a list and a list is read out, and he read the names of the files and the numbers of the leaves called for and the numbers produced and nothing else. The file of the *Aster Gannet*, one volume, was fourth on the list. Leaf sixty-two was called for. Leaf sixty-two was not produced. The reason of the non-production was entered by the Registry on a form of its own dated the second day of September last, a copy of which was on the record. The form was not before the sitting and was not produced to the party.

Nobody in that room said anything at all, and the clerk waited about four seconds, which is longer than a clerk has to wait, and then he said, **"That is the end of the schedule. Nobody is required to say anything about it and nobody has asked to and I am not going to invite it.**"

---

**And at about twenty past to eleven the clerk put his pen down and said the thing he had said he was going to let them say first, and the reason he said it himself was, as he gave it, that a question asked in a room is asked in a room and a question put in a letter is put in a letter.**

"**I am going to ask one question and I am a clerk and this is my trade and I have written the hour against it and the words of it on my page and you may read them afterward from the outside if you want to.

"**Is there any person in this harbor whose hand is on a page of that file and who is not an officer of any office in it.**"

Calder Marr stood up from the bench at the end of the room, and about nine people looked at the back of his head.

"**Yes,**" he said. "**And she is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is not a holder and is not on the four names of the writ of the first of May, and I am not going to tell you her name, and I am not going to tell you that she is in this harbor, and I have entered both of those in my own book with a reason against them, and the reason is that this party was refused by her in a room open to anybody in this town in eleven people's hearing on the twenty-ninth of September and has not asked her one thing since, and has entered why.

"**And I want two things said, and the first of them is that there is a person, because there is, and I am not going to have a record in a city that says this harbor produced nothing on a question a clerk of this circuit asked it in a room. And the second of them is that I have got nothing else. I have got a number off a copying sheet. I have got a form that says the Registry agrees with a withdrawal and will not produce the page and will not produce the form. I have got a woman in this town who is not a door and that is not a thing I can put in front of you, because I have got no instrument for it and I could not pay for it if I had.

"**And that is a party that published itself in July. It is in about sixty boats and one copy of it is in a press in a city and it says where every copy of it is, and it is now going to have a line in a record in that city that says there is a woman in this harbor who knows something and the yard of Morrow Quay will not say who she is. I entered that on the eighth of August in the first column in my own hand in advance and I am not going to say that a man is sorry for it in a room, and I am not going to be told I should not have said it, and I would like it noticed that I said it out loud in a room before I was asked twice.**"

The clerk wrote it down. It took him about a minute and a half and he read it back to the man in about as long, and the man said that it was his own words and that he was not going to improve on it, and the clerk entered that in the record too.

Nobody asked about the second column. Nobody asked about the third. Nobody asked the question the holder has had written in his own book in the second column since Sunday the tenth of September with a reason against it and no name in it. Nobody asked him what a seam is or whether he looks at one, and nobody mentioned the word.

They were in that room for about forty minutes and nothing else happened in it, and they came down the hill at about eleven and went to the boards and did not talk about it, and at about nine that night in the shed with the lamp lit and the ship's hand reading the day's entries back from the top and the holder reading them back after her and nobody else in the room, four lines went into the first column and two went into the second.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that this party was served in the doorway of this shed at about eleven in the morning on Thursday the fifteenth day of October instant with a paper of six paragraphs by the clerk of the circuit office, who gave his name and the name of his office before he read it; and that the said paper appoints the sitting for Friday the sixteenth instant at ten of the morning at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and that it is to be held before the magistrate of this circuit, whose name is Halven Rook and stands on the original at the foot of a line in a file in a city and is not on the copy, and that the said magistrate has not been in this harbor and is not going to be; and that the said paper states that the registry file of the ASTER GANNET is before the said inquiry, that one leaf of it is called for and is not produced, and that the reason of that non-production is entered by the Registry on a form of its own dated the second of September past, and that the said form is not before the sitting and is not to be produced to this party; and that this party has not applied for a copy of any record and has entered why, and the reason is that a copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check, and that this party said that out loud in a market in July and has not said it better since.*

*And that this party attended at the said sitting on the Friday instant at ten of the morning, un-represented, that about nine people were in the room, that the six things required by the fourth paragraph of the notice of the tenth of September past were produced whole and unshortened in about eleven minutes, and that not one line of this yard's own crew's book was read aloud in that room, and that no copy of any record was made in that room, and that a schedule of the records before the said inquiry was read into the record by the clerk of it and that leaf sixty-two of the said file was called for and was not produced; and that this party has entered that it now knows, on a paper served on it, that a page has gone out of a file in this town and that the office which kept the file has written down a reason for it on a form of its own and will not produce the form, and that this party does not know what was on the page, has asked nobody, and is not going to guess.*

*And that the clerk of the said sitting asked in that room at about twenty minutes to eleven whether there is any person in this harbor whose hand is on a page of the said file and who is not an officer of any office in it; and that the holder of the writ of the first of May instant answered in that room that there is; and that he said that the said person is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is not a holder and is not on the four names of the said writ, and that he is not going to give her name and is not going to say that she is in this harbor, and that the clerk read his answer back and entered it, and that the said answer is in the record of that sitting in a city; and that this party entered on the eighth day of August past, in the first column, in the holder's own left hand and in advance, that this party is not going to put a name on a thing it cannot check, and that the said entry stands and is not going to be improved on, and that the cost of it is on the page with the date and the hour against it and is not going to be said sorry about in a room or in a market.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the sixteenth day of October instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man stood in a room in this harbor for about forty minutes on the Friday instant with a book in a bag at his feet containing five lines of the second column that neither he nor anybody in this harbor can check, and that nobody in that room asked him for them, and that he did not read them out, and that the clerk of the circuit office asked one question in that room and it was not about the second column; and that the holder entered on the sixteenth day of September past that he did not know whether the not-reading of the second column in that room on that day was a rule or an answer, and that on the sixteenth day of October instant he did not read it out again, and that he has entered that he still does not know, and that these are not the same thing and that he entered on the fourth of October instant that he is not going to improve on that entry either.*

*And that the holder entered on the fourth day of October instant that he was going to tell a person of no office a thing and has not told her, and that on the twelfth, the fourteenth and the sixteenth days of October instant he has had three occasions not to, and that the third of them was in a room, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a room can be checked for what he said and cannot be checked for what he did not say, and that a party which has said out loud in a market that it produces its book whole is now going to a room with a document it cannot produce and a person it cannot pay and a number it cannot use, and that the shipwright said in a doorway on the twelfth of October instant that a yard has four things and not one of them can be put in front of anybody, and that this party agrees with her and is not going to improve on it in a market in a fortnight.*

**No money came into this yard on the Thursday and none on the Friday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and five marks and twelve shillings is in the bag, and a wage of four marks goes out of it on Tuesday the twentieth. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, three counts unmoved since the fourth of January at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid, and useless, because the thing after the gland is the hose. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and is not going to be sent for. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, and that is not the four pence of the second of October. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand, and the column has not been filled since the first of August. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she is not coming to the market office of this town on a Tuesday or a Friday morning any more, and the reporting clause on the writ of the first of May does not reach her and never did. Twenty-two lines and eighty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, sixteen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them. Up on the crown, about a hundred and forty-six days, under two tarpaulins about nine feet apart, the two objects of nobody's have not moved, and one tarpaulin still has a corner off it since the seventeenth of June. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the first, the fifth, the eighth, the twelfth and the fifteenth of October, and this party walked none of the five, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in her hands. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and the note against it is open, and whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. That book's delayed note stands where it stood at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, nothing was entered against it this month, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. And the market wall was not touched on any day of this month and the hearing room was used once, on the sixteenth, and the two of them are never the same day.**
