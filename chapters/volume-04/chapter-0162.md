# Chapter 162

*Three Marks, and a Cistern Charged by Strangers*

Wednesday the thirteenth and Thursday the fourteenth of September, on the second seam's own face, at the head of the dock, and in the shed.

**Wednesday was a day of getting a boat out and a day of arguing about a room, and about nine people on the crown heard the argument and about four of them understood it.**

The pump came off the bench at about seven in the morning, which is a sentence about a pump and not about anything else, and Nessa Pike had it on the boards and the fall rigged and the board in her own hand before the tide was half made, and the pot of air did not come off the bench at all, because there is no pot in this harbor with air in it that this yard has not paid for out of its own bag at two marks apiece, and it did not buy one, and the day was going to be worked on the face and not under it.

**And on the Wednesday morning, at about half past eight, the two of them had the argument that the whole of this fortnight is made of, and it was not about a figure.**

"**You have had six days,**" she said. "**Six days since Sunday. And I want to know what you have done with them, because the answer is that you have not read a page of anything, and I told you on the tenth that you were not going to, and you are not, and neither am I, and that is agreed between us.

"**And so I am going to say the thing out loud once, in a shed, on a Wednesday, and you are not going to answer it, and then I am going to go and rig a fall.**

"**On Saturday morning you are going to open that book in front of a man with a pen. And you are not going to read the second column out. And I know that, and you know that, and we have both known it since the Sunday, and it is the fourth time this month that you have told me something you do not know and let me have the answer, and I am not going to keep collecting them.**"

"**Then stop collecting them.**"

"**No, because there is a thing I have not said and I am going to say it, and then I am going to go and rig a fall.**"

She put her hand flat on the bench, the way she does when she is not going to improve on a thing.

"**Here is the thing and then I am going to the pump. A man reads the second column out in a room and the man with the pen writes down the line and not the reason against it, because reasons are long, and he goes away with five facts about a thing neither of you can check. I said that to you on the twentieth of August in this shed and it is still true and I have not improved on it in twenty-four days.

"**And here is the other half and I have been holding it since Sunday. In four years, when that book is in a file in a city and somebody reads the second column of it without a reason column beside it, and there is no reason column, because reasons do not go into a copy, and the copy went into a file on the ninth of September in about two minutes with a pen—**

"**Then the five lines in it are going to be the only thing in forty-seven weeks that this yard knows and will not say.**"

"**That is the same thing you said on the twentieth.**

"**It is the same thing I said on the twentieth, and I have now had those twenty-four days to find out whether I was right, and I was right in a way I did not expect, and I am not pleased about it and I am not going to be nicer about it than that.**"

She picked up the fall and went out to the boards, and the tide was making, and nobody on that crown said anything, and four of the nine of them had understood the whole of it and had said afterward on the boards that this yard argued about its own book in front of a market and did not care who heard, which is the sentence this yard has been living inside since the third of May.

---

**The day was Thursday the fourteenth and the ground was the second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark, at about four, on a hirer's own account, and nobody was in the water and the pot of air was not charged to the day.**

The hand lead's line went over the side at about half past eight and came back up four times and was coiled on the crown, and it did not go past the outer marks, and the reason for that is on the page in the yard's own hand and it is the reason it has not gone past the outer marks on any day in nine months: a hand lead's line is not what anybody wanted past the outer marks, and this yard has not worked past the outer marks on a day of work in nine months and is not going to start on a day a form has arrived.

The two of them were off and on the face about five hours and came up about half past one.

**Gross on the board fifteen.** The eighth, two. The share, two. The victuals of the day, two. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. That is twelve out of fifteen paid at the mole by the woman who hired the day, and the yard's being three, and nobody was in the water and nothing was raised.

**Three went up onto the mole's steps at about ten past two in the afternoon, in daylight, in front of about eleven people, on a board, in Calder Marr's own left hand, and it went up next to two other figures and he read all three out.**

"**Three, and that is the day of Thursday the fourteenth. The twenty-first of August at that same face on a hirer's own account brought this yard five, and the twenty-eighth of August on the mole's toe brought five, and the twenty-fourth of June brought seven.

"**Those four figures are on this board. None of them is a claim. None of them is a complaint. I am not going to stand here and say which of them I would rather have had, and if anybody in this market wants to work that out for themselves they can and I am not going to help them with it.

"**And the eighth and the share come off the top of a day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May, and nobody in this yard has ever argued either, and I am not going to start on a day a form has arrived.**"

A man of the first lay looked at the board and said three was a poor day and went to work. A man of the second lay said nothing at all and went to work, which is what a man does with a figure he has not got an argument about.

---

**And at about the same hour, at the cistern behind the engine house, four men this yard does not know charged it, at no figure, because it is on a rota and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and it is not this yard's rota and has never been this yard's rota.**

This party walked that cistern thirteen times between the end of May and the third of August at no figure, in fours, on the days it was on a rota, because four men with a hose and a gate that takes four hours and three of the gang is four hours, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. This party has not walked it since the third of August and is not walking it in September, and the reason is a working reason and is on the page in the yard's own hand: a yard of two and a half people that is on a face on a Thursday cannot also be behind the engine house on a Thursday, and the men who charged it on the Thursday of the fourteenth of September were strangers and were not asked and were not paid and will not be thanked, and the cistern did not want asking.

---

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Thursday the fourteenth day of September instant this party worked about five hours on the second seam's own face of this mole, landward of the third seam's mark, at about four, on a hirer's own account, that the hand lead's line went over the side four times and came back up four times and did not go past the outer marks, that nobody was in the water, that no pot of air was bought and none was charged to the day, and that no finding of worked glass was made, nothing was raised, and no return goes up and there is no fifth return.*

*And that the gross on the board that day was fifteen, the eighth two, the share two, the victuals of the day two, a hired hand three and a second hired hand three, that being twelve of fifteen paid at the mole by the woman who hired the day, and that this yard's figure out of it is three, which is the first day of work this yard has done since Monday the twenty-eighth of August and the lowest figure of a day at a seam since the twenty-seventh of July.*

*And that this party did not charge the cistern behind the engine house on the Thursday of that date although it is on a rota on a Monday and a Thursday, and that it was charged at no figure by four men this party does not know, and that the reason is on the page: that a yard which is on a face on a Thursday of a rota week cannot also be behind the engine house on that Thursday, the gate taking about four hours with three men, and that this party walked that cistern thirteen times between the end of May and the third of August at no figure and has not walked it since, and that the cistern has held since the nineteenth of June and this party is not going to be thanked for a thing it did not do and is not going to ask anybody else to do it for it either.*

*And that no person, no boat and no lead of this yard was inside the first clause of the notice of the thirty-first of October on that day or on any other day of this month, and that the two objects of nobody's on the crown of this mole have not moved and were not moved, and that the plate at the fifth seam was not reached, not measured, not touched and not asked about, and that the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January was not reopened and is not going to be.*

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the fourteenth of September instant at about half past eight in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the shipwright of this party said out loud on the Wednesday morning in this shed, in front of about nine people on the crown, that if the second column of this yard's book is read out in a room by anybody then in about four years the five or six lines in it that neither she nor the holder can check will be the only thing in forty-seven weeks that this yard knew and would not say, because reasons are long and reasons do not go into a copy; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check what a book contains and cannot check what a man in a room will do with a page, and that what was said on the Wednesday morning was said out loud on a mole's crown in nine people's hearing and is a fact about this party and not a fact about the water; and that the holder of the writ of the first of May did not answer it, and has entered that he did not, and has entered the not-answering with the date, and that the not-answering is the same not-answering that was entered in this column on Sunday the tenth of September and the two are one thing and not two.*

**A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence was in that bag on Wednesday the thirteenth and four marks and twelve shillings and fourpence was in it on the evening of Thursday the fourteenth, and a wage of four goes out of it on Tuesday the nineteenth, and the day of Thursday the fourteenth brought three of the four and this yard is going to say out loud on the Tuesday that a day of work paid a wage and that it was short by twelve shillings and fourpence, and is not going to say it as a complaint.**

**Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written. Nothing in a tin. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The mooring at the ninth seam is in service and is harbor ground and is not this yard's. And tomorrow is a Friday and this yard has nothing booked for it and the hearing room over the tide-gang store is open at ten in the morning on Saturday for a sitting that was appointed on a mole's steps in nine people's hearing on Sunday the tenth, and the two of those dates are a day apart and the market wall and that room are never the same day, and this party is not going to a wall on Saturday.**
