# Chapter 158

*Six Marks and Two Days, and a Man on the Steps*

Friday the eighth of September, at the market office of Morrow Quay, from the steps outside it, and in the shed in the evening.

**He did not go in. He was on the steps from about ten past ten until about half past eleven with his back to a notice board, and the door of that office stands open on a Friday morning because the office is open to anybody on a Tuesday and a Friday morning, and about half of what was said inside came out of the door and onto the boards, and he heard enough of it to know whose voice it was inside about a minute in, and about half of nothing else.**

At the long table there were three people and one of them was Mara Venn, thirty, in a plain coat, with a book of her own, a pencil, and her own brass calipers laid on the table beside her, and there was a man in a coat standing at the end of the table with a paper, and there were about nine people in the room and a counting going on behind them.

**She did not ask anybody to be there. This party did not ask her for anything and did not arrange any of it and has not asked her for anything since Sunday the twenty-third of July, and it is entered in this yard's own book with the date, twice, that it did not ask and that it was not going to.**

He stood on the steps for about eighty minutes and then he went down the hill and did not go to the market office at the end of the day and did not go to the end of the boards either, and the reason he did not go to the end of the boards is in the first column that night and it is a reason about a limit and not about manners.

---

**What came out of the door, in Calder Marr's hearing and no more than that, was this.**

The man in the coat was asking about five bells in a case under an after hatch, and about the year, and about a fire that four people of a community of this coast will not name in a market, and about what a bell-archive is. He was asking her because she is the only person in this harbor who can be asked anything about glass without it being a fee for somebody's opinion.

"**I am going to answer this room and not the four people it is about,**" she said. "**They did not ask me and they are not going to and if I say it to the four of them it will look as though I had been sent.

"**Six things, and no seventh, and you are welcome to all six.

"**One. A case of that size in that position was in the boat when she was built. A hatch under a grating is a space a thing is carried in, and the pins on that grating were seized, and the seizing is the only thing in this harbor's knowledge that was ever holding that case where it was, and it is not the reason the case is at the bottom of the sea. The reason is the bottom of the sea.

"**Two. Five bells of that size is not a cargo. A cargo is counted and sold and goes in a manifest. About five bells of that size is a fifth of a ton, and the ballast in that boat was stone, and the stones are stone and the bells are bells, and a boat that carried stones and bells was carrying two kinds of weight and the two were not in one trade and were not in one hand and were not in one crew.

"**Three. Bell-metal is a trade in about four hands on this coast and every one of them would know a casting of that size in a fortnight by the sound of it, and would know one in nine months as a casting nobody in the county has heard. That is a fact about how long a thing has to exist before anybody in this trade has an opinion about it.

"**Four. A line cut in the lip of a bell is cut after the casting. All four of the bells the Sill have are cut. So somebody who was not a founder sat down with each of them in turn, one at a time, and did it, and a man who does that four times is a man with a method and not a man with a message.

"**Five. Bell-metal cracks in a fire. A crack is a hole. A book burned is a book gone. A bell burned is a bell still in the world with a piece of it missing, and a bell with a piece of it missing is a bell that can no longer do the whole of what it was for. That is a difference in the material and it is the only difference in the material and I am not going to stand in this room and tell anybody what it means.

"**Six. There are four of them left of a number I do not know, and the four that are left are the four that did not go, and I have not asked why those four and not those four and I am not going to.**"

A man at the back of the room said something about the water. She said no before he had finished.

Then she said the seventh, which is the one they had all been in the room for.

"**And I cannot give you the seventh and neither can anybody else in this room, and I am going to say the price before I say the rest of it because I have said it before in a market office in this harbor in front of nine people and I said I would not do a thing at nothing again and I am not doing it at nothing today, and I did it in August and I said so in the room when I did it.

"**Six marks and two days. That is my price. It is the same on every coast and it does not come down and it is not a thing I have invented to keep a boat out of the water. It is what the trade is.

"**And there were six things on that list and the seventh is six marks and two days, and I have put those two sixes next to each other on purpose this morning and I would like somebody in this room to notice it and be annoyed about it, because it is the shape of the whole of this and nobody in this harbor has said so out loud. Six things I can give away and the seventh I cannot. A finding of no office carries no seal and cannot be produced on demand by anybody, which is why my finding of the fifteenth and the sixteenth of July is on a wall in this harbor unsigned at my own asking with the reason on the page, and I would like it noticed that a man in a city can order the one of those and cannot order the other, and does not know which of them he has got.**

"**I have not got six marks. I have not got two days. I have forty marks standing in this harbor since the fifteenth of October, first after food, and nobody has paid them and I have written that figure on the mole's steps in my own hand. And this yard has not offered me six marks and has not been asked to and I am not going to be, and if it offers them I am going to say no, and I would like that written down too, and I have said it in a market office four times now and I am saying it a fifth.**"

---

**And then a woman of about sixty with a basket at her feet asked her a question about the sheet of the twenty-fifth of August, and it is the only thing Calder Marr heard in full from the steps, because the counting had stopped by then and the room was quiet.**

"**Then tell us about the sheet your yard put up on the wall on Friday the twenty-fifth,**" the woman said, "**because I have read it four times and I cannot make head nor tail of the third block, and that is the block about a record.**

"**I have read the sheet and I have not read any record behind it. I have not got it. Nobody in this harbor has got it, and I have asked for it in a letter and been told no, which is a thing a person can be told, and I want to know what a wall means when what is on the wall is a piece of paper I have never seen and never will.**

"**A sheet carries the limits its own lines carry. It does not carry a thing they do not say. If a sheet says that a yard asked for a copy of a record and has not been given one, then what is on that sheet is a fact about a request, and the fact is checkable by asking the yard, and the record is not on the sheet and is not checkable by anybody in this harbor including me. If the sheet had said the record shows a thing, then it would be a different sheet, and I would want to see the record before I said a word about the thing, and I would have to charge six marks and two days to look at a page of it properly and this harbor has not got six marks and two days in it.

"**And I cannot tell anybody what is in that boat. Nobody in this harbor can. The trade that can is my trade and it is six marks and two days, and that has not changed since the fourth of April, and you can go and put that on your own wall and check it, and it will be the same sentence tomorrow.**"

Nobody in the room said anything about the money. The counting started again and went on behind them.

---

**And Calder Marr went down the hill at about half past eleven, and he did not go to the end of the boards, and he did not go to the market office at the end of the day, and he did not write to her, and he did not ask her what she had said, and none of that is a rule he made on the steps. It is a limit four people of the Sill wrote in their own words on a mole's steps in front of forty witnesses on Thursday the tenth of August and signed, and one line of it says the account is not to be said to a man with a seal, and the man in the coat at the end of the long table is a man with a seal and this party is not going to be the reason one word of it is asked for.**

*That on Friday the eighth of September instant this party was outside the market office from about ten past ten until about half past eleven, which is about eighty minutes, and did not go in, and did not send anybody in, and did not ask any person afterwards what had been said in that room, and has entered why: that a party which has said out loud in a market that it does not ask a man one single question about a boat has got to be able to say the same about a woman, and that it said it about a woman on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of July, and that the man inside that room on the Friday morning had a paper and the four people whose bells they are have a written limit in their own words and a signature on it, and that this party is not going to be the reason any part of it is asked; and that the not-asking is entered here in the first column with the date and the hour so that in four years a man can find out whether it stood, and a request to strike this line out of this column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*And that this party has entered, in the first column, that a person of no office is being asked questions in a room about a thing this party published on the twenty-fifth of August, and that this party is not going to be the reason any of it is asked, and that a person of no office is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is not a holder and is not on the four names of the writ of the first of May, and that the reporting clause on that writ does not reach her and never did, and that she said so out loud in a room in this harbor in the month of June where it was entered, and that this party has not asked her for anything since the twenty-third of July and is not going to and will not pay her six marks and two days and has not been asked to, and that if it is ever asked to, it is going to say no, and that it has entered that it is going to say no.*

---

**And in the shed at about nine that night the shipwright read the whole of the day back and he read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed, and then the holder took the pencil and did the thing he has not done for six weeks, which is put something into the second column that is a sentence rather than a fact about a hand.**

"**I am going to write down the thing you told me not to write down,**" he said.

"**I know you are. I have known since about eleven o'clock this morning.**

"**Then here it is and it goes in the second column and not the first, and I am going to say why before I say it, and the reason is that a man in a boat cannot check it and a man in a market can check most things, and I am not going to put a sentence in the first column that is only true because I was tired.

"**And I am not going to tell you I have written it. You will find it in about four years if this book is still being kept, and if it is not, then it does not matter, and I am not going to come and tell a woman that she has been right about a sentence for six weeks while she is in the middle of a Friday morning with nine people in a room and a man with a paper at the end of the table.**"

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the eighth of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, and it is a sentence and not a fact about a hand, and no name is against it: that whatever was in that boat went back into it and this party got nothing out of the water on the eighth of August and there is nothing in this harbor that has come of it but four people alive; and that the reason it is in this column and not the first is that a man in a boat cannot check what he came back with, and that the party has had a version of this sentence in its head since the eighth of August and has had nothing on a page, and that a thing a party has only said to itself for six weeks is a thing that can be improved on by being said again and cannot be improved on by being written, and that it is written now because the party stood on the steps of a market office for the best part of eighty minutes this morning and heard the half of it it could hear and understood the whole of it, and that a man who is owed forty marks first after food by this harbor's own work is not to be told that a sentence of hers was right, and has not been told, and is not going to be.*

**No money moved in or out of this yard on the Friday, and the party spent nothing and gained nothing, and the bag is a mark and twelve shillings and fourpence, and the day was a day this yard was not on a mole, and the north berth is still out, and the light is not on, and the hard at Ashtide is unanswered, and there is a woman of thirty in this harbor who is owed forty marks and has been asked six questions by a man in a room she went into herself, and this party was on the steps.**

**And tomorrow the paper that man has is read in the hearing room over the tide-gang store, with a date against it, and this party is going to be asked for its crew's book, and the hearing room and the market wall are never the same day, and this yard is going to have to be in a room in one week and on a wall in another.**
