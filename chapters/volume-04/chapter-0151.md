# Chapter 151

*The Two That Came Down*

Wednesday the sixteenth and Thursday the seventeenth of August, at the market wall, on the boards, and in the shed.

**The wall was bare at about eight in the morning on the Wednesday and it had not been bare at five, and a man standing at the foot of it with his back to the fish market could see four nails in a board about the height of his own chest with nothing on them and a fifth nail below them with nothing on it either, and he could see what was underneath, because a sheet that goes up goes up on the outside of whatever is there and has been that way since the ninth of November.**

What was underneath was the printed sheet of a crew of Ashtide, which had gone up on Monday the twenty-seventh of July in front of about thirty people without knowing what it was going to be covered by. Under that was a sheet of the seventh of June. Under that was this yard's tenth thing. And under the whole of it, under four papers since the twenty-eighth of July, was this yard's own fourth return, made on Friday the fourteenth of July out of a finding of Monday the tenth, going up this coast in a sealed jacket with a copy into the harbor authority's ledger in a clerk's own hand.

**That return is still on those nails. It is the only paper on that wall that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and a man can read it if he stands at the side of it in the right wind, and Calder Marr stood at the side of it at about ten past eight on the Wednesday morning and read all of it without taking a page out of his own coat.**

What was gone was three sheets. The notice of the Crown of Friday the twenty-eighth of July, with *Hearthwake* named in it and four days in it and an officer's hand at the foot of it. The longer notice of Friday the fourth of August, which superseded the first and which said *the said vessel and every part of her contents being thereby Crown material* in the fourth and fifth lines, and which had a Crown stamp on it struck in ink and off by about a sixteenth of an inch to the right of where it should have been. And this yard's own sheet of Tuesday the fifteenth of August, carrying the place of every copy of itself, the nine places, the two columns with a reason against every line of the second, a limit signed by four people, the place of a case of five bells in about five and a half fathoms, and the plain statement that this party went past the outer marks on the eighth and broke a sentence it had published on the fifth of July and repeated on the twenty-sixth.

---

**Nobody in that yard asked anybody anything about it on the Wednesday morning, and that is entered, and the reason is on the page in the yard's own hand and it is a rule the yard set itself.**

*That this party has not asked the harbor authority of this harbor to do anything about a sheet being taken off the market wall, and has entered why: that an instrument of that office which would put a boat of that office to sea carries the reeve's acknowledgement on the page and in the record and at the reeve's cost, and that there are eight of those in that ledger and both of this year's are spent and there is no ninth, and that this party has entered twice in the month of July that it did not ask for a ninth and is not going to, and is not going to break that standing arrangement over a sheet of paper.*

*And that this party did not go to the man in a coat, and has entered why: that a man in a coat is a channel and the channel is the writ of the first of May, and that writ is about the second season and this is not about the second season, and that this party has noticed that it is not about the second season and is not going to assume that a thing which is not about the second season is a favor.*

---

**And there was a man in the shed on the Wednesday afternoon who had seen a boat at the wall at about seven in the morning, and he said so with his name against it and the hour, and nobody asked him anything he did not volunteer.**

Danno Fisk was of the third lay and about thirty-eight and had a boat of his own and had been in this harbor thirty-one years. He came up the hill on the Wednesday at about two in the afternoon because he had something and because the party was the only set of people on the hill he could give it to, and he gave it standing in the doorway with his hat in his hand.

"**I was at the wall at about seven,**" he said. "**I was not early. I had the cart and I was putting the cart away at the north end and I looked across because the lamp behind the fish market is off and the wall is the brightest thing in that end of it at that hour, and there was a boat lying in against the wall with her bow up under it and no light burning in her and a man in a coat standing on her boards doing nothing at all, which I noticed because I have been in this harbor thirty-one years and I have never seen a man in a coat stand on a board doing nothing.

"**And I did not get a word out of either of them. He had his back to me and he was waiting on something. There was a second man in the boat, in the bottom of her, and I could not see his face and I am not going to say what he was doing.

"**And I stood there and I waited, and that is a thing I am not proud of and I am telling you anyway, because you can check the rest of it and you cannot check me standing there. Then the barge came out from the naval pile about two hours later, and she came alongside, and they put the three sheets into her in a bundle and the third one was under the other two so it went in wet and it did not matter, and the two of them got into the barge and the barge went back in, and the boat went out after her with her bow still up and under the wall.

"**And that is what I have got. I did not see a face and I am not going to give you one and I am not going to say what I think it was. I have got a boat, a man in a coat, a bundle of three sheets, and the hour, and the hour is between about seven and a quarter past and I have given you a quarter of an hour on purpose instead of a minute because I did not have a watch on me.**"

Calder Marr wrote it down in the first column and read it back and Danno Fisk stood in the doorway and listened to his own name and the hour being read back, and then he said one more thing and went down the hill.

"**I have not been asked to say that and I am not going to say it. I would like it noticed that the three sheets that came off that wall were the two naval ones and the one of yours, and that the one underneath with the office's eye on it is still on the wall, and that whoever took three off a wall stopped at three and that is either a rule or a coincidence and I have been in this harbor thirty-one years and I do not know which.**"

---

**And the clerk of this harbor authority came up the hill at about half past four on the Wednesday with his own page under his arm, and he had not been asked to come, and he had come because a wall in his own market is his business whether or not anything is on it.**

He is not a man of any consequence. He has a bench, a ledger, a slate wall behind it, and a practice of saying what a box holds.

"**I can enter that on the morning of Wednesday the sixteenth instant a person told me that three sheets were taken off the market wall in one morning,**" he said. "**I have entered that, with the date, in my own hand, in my own book. I cannot enter whose they were, because he told me and did not give me a name to enter, and I am not going to ask a man of the third lay to go and stand at a wall for an hour so that I can write a name in a book, and I have written the date and the words a person told me and that is the whole of what my box holds.

"**And I want to say the other half, because I have said things this year in rooms and nobody has printed them. My page does not have a line in it for a sheet going off a wall. It has a line in it for a permit and a line in it for a claim window and a line in it for an acknowledgement, and it has had a line in it since the third of May for the wall behind the reeve's bench, which is a different wall and comes down every Monday at eight whether there is a sitting or not and has come down bare fifteen times, the last of them on Monday, and the next one is on Monday morning and will make sixteen. That wall is my business. That other wall is a set of nails and it is nobody's business, and a set of nails is not in any book in this harbor and it never has been.

"**So I have nothing to give you and I have not come to give you anything. I have come because a man of this harbor told me something at about eleven this morning in the yard and I do not ask him what he is doing on that hill any more than he asks me what I am doing on this one.**"

He put the page under his arm and looked at the boards for a moment.

"**I will tell you what I do not know, since you have been writing that down all year. I do not know whether the four papers under those nails came down or not and I am not going to walk down there and look, because if they have, then somebody in this market took a sheet off a wall and it is a thing that will be in a book in four years, and if they have not, then it is a man of Ashtide who came up this coast to put a sheet up and took it away again when the paper on top of it went, and both of those are things this office has no line for.**"

---

**And at about five in the evening on the Wednesday, when the clerk had gone back down the hill, the two of them went down and looked at the wall properly, with a rule and not a stick, and three more sheets had come off it since the morning, and the bare nails were the same bare nails, and nobody in that yard saw it happen and nobody was asked about it.**

*That on the evening of Wednesday the sixteenth of August instant it was found, on going down to the market wall, that four further sheets had come off it since the morning, being the printed sheet of a crew of Ashtide put up on Monday the twenty-seventh of July, a sheet of the seventh of June instant, and this yard's own sheet of the tenth thing; that this party does not know on what day or at what hour those three came off, and has not asked, and is not going to ask, and has entered that it does not know; and that what remains on that wall is this yard's own fourth return, made on Friday the fourteenth of July, and that it is the only paper on that wall that any office in any city is obliged to look at, and that it is still on those nails and a man can read it if he stands at the side of it in the right wind.*

*And that this party has entered that it is not going to put anything back on that wall until it has something of its own to put there, and that a wall with nothing on it is a different problem from a wall with something on it, and that the party did not know that until the Wednesday morning and has entered the not-knowing with the date.*

---

**And in the shed that evening the shipwright asked him one question, in her own trade's voice, and it is the whole of what he said about it that night.**

"**Then tell me what you think it is,**" Nessa Pike said. "**A sheet coming off a wall. Is that a thing a man did or a thing an office did. I am asking it in the way I would ask whether a plank on a hull let go in a blow or was cut, because in my trade those are two different repairs and one of them is somebody's fault and the other one is the wood.**

"**I have been standing on those boards since Wednesday morning and I have been turning it over, and I keep arriving at the same place and the place is that a set of nails in a wall is not anybody's instrument. Nobody put those nails in. They have been there longer than the market office. If a man in a coat stood on a boat at seven in the morning and took three sheets off, then a man in a coat did it. And if a man in a coat is a channel, and a channel is a writ, and a writ is about the second season, and this was not about the second season, then either there is a second channel in this harbor and this party has not been told about it, or a man did a thing on his own account with an office's paper in his hand.

"**And I am not writing either of those down and I am not saying them out loud twice, and the reason is that a thing said twice in a shed in a fortnight is a thing somebody in a market will have heard by the middle of October and will have joined the two halves of.**"

"**I do not know,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Right,**" she said, and she took the pencil and put it in the second column in her own hand, and she read it back to him, and he read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed and there has not been anybody else in that shed at nine o'clock since the fifteenth of May.

*That this party does not know whether the sheets that came off the market wall of this harbor in one morning on the sixteenth instant were taken by a man or by an office; that the man in a boat saw a man in a coat and a bundle and gave his name and the hour and did not give a face and was not asked for one; that the clerk of this harbor authority can enter that a person told him three sheets went off that wall and can enter nothing else and said so; that a sheet going off a wall is a thing a man did or a thing an office did and that this party has gone through the two of them and has arrived at the same place in both and that the place is that a set of nails in a wall is not anybody's instrument, since nobody put those nails in and they have been there longer than the market office; and that this party is not going to improve on that in a shed in a fortnight and has entered that it does not know.*

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about eight until about four on the Wednesday and from about nine until about three on the Thursday, with his back to the room, and he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him on either day, and the yard entered that he did not and that neither brother has said why and neither brother is going to.

**Nothing came in and nothing went out of that bag on the Wednesday or on the Thursday. A morning of a man's time and a night's reading, and a sheet of this yard's own is in a boat going somewhere, and one of the forty copies is in a city at a press that cannot be stopped, and neither of those is coming back, and the party did not ask and is not going to.**

**And behind the reeve's bench the slate wall came down at eight on Monday morning, the fourteenth of August, which was fifteen Mondays since the first of May, and it came down bare, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it. It comes down again at eight on Monday the twenty-first, which is the sixteenth, and it will come down bare, and this party has entered that it has not put anything on it in five months and is not going to start on the sixteenth.**
