# Chapter 166

*A Sheet Off a Stall in a City, and Two Figures That Cannot Check Each Other*

Thursday the twenty-first and Friday the twenty-second of September, on the second seam's own face, at the head of the dock, and in the shed.

**A man of the first lay named Wil Tranter, who is fifty-one years old and is master of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons of his own, came up this hill on Friday the twenty-second at about half past two in the afternoon with a printed sheet in a coat pocket and took it out and put it on the bench face up and said he had bought it for a penny.**

He said it the way a man says a thing he is not yet sure he is glad about.

"**Off a stall in a counting office in a city, on Friday morning, for a penny, and I have had it in my coat since Friday morning and I have not been to sea in it. And I am not going to pretend I did not read it on the boat coming up. I read it twice and I have been sick about the second time and I have come up here anyway, because a man who does not come up here with a thing like that is a man who is going to be told about it in a market and would rather have heard it in his own boat first.

"**And I am not here about the paper. I am here about one line of it.**"

He put his finger on the sixth line from the top of the second block.

"**It says the bar is two and three-quarters of a fathom on the flood and two and a quarter on the ebb, and it says the man who keeps the channel nine miles off made two and a half four times in nineteen years, and it says *we are not guessing*. And it says under that, in the same size of type, *a road nobody else can use is not a road*, and *a road everybody can use is not this yard's*.

"**And I have had a hundred and ninety tons drawing eleven feet six aft, and I have crossed that bar on twenty-two springs in nineteen years at half past two on the flood, and I have put her on it once, in the second year, and I came off. So I know what the answer is. Everybody who is anybody who crosses that bar knows what the answer is, and it is not two and a half, and I am not going to say so out loud in a market, and I am not going to say so out loud to you either, and that is the whole reason I have come up this hill and I would like you to notice that I have not said it.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for a moment.

"**Then what do you want,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I want to know which one of them is right. Not for the paper. For me.**"

---

**And what happened next took about eleven minutes and it went into the book the same night, and when it was done the two of them were still standing at the bench and the penny sheet was still face up on it.**

"**I am not going to tell you which one of them is right,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to tell you that I do not know, either, because I do know that I do not know and that is a different thing and I have not got time to explain it to a man at half past two on a Friday.

"**Here is what I can put on this bench in about nine words. That figure was made once, on the second of May, by one man with a hand lead's line and a slate, in this yard's own hand, and he is the man standing in front of you. The other one was made four times in nineteen years by a man with a boat and two other men and no harbor on her anywhere.

"**A figure made once is a figure. It is not a certificate. I have had a thing like this printed in a city and I cannot take it back, and I have not got a way of standing behind it for you and I have not got a way of taking it away from you either, and both of those are on the same sheet and one of them was written by me and the other one was written by a man of fifty-four on my own steps, and the two of them have been in a press in a city since the beginning of this month and neither of them can check the other and neither of them ever will.

"**And I am not going to be the man who tells a city which of the two is right. If I do that, then in four years a man reads two figures on a printed sheet and one of them has this yard's name on it and the other one has not, and he will think the one without a name is a guess, and it is not, and there is no way on that sheet to say so, and the sheet is in a city and I cannot get at it.**"

Wil Tranter took his hand off the paper.

"**Then what is a man supposed to do with a printed sheet.**"

"**That is a better question than the one you came up here with, and I am not going to improve on it in a shed.**"

---

**And a man who had been standing at the far end of the boards with a pot of paint and a brush in his hand said the thing that everybody in that yard was thinking and that Calder Marr had already entered in his own book on the twenty-third of August and had not gone and looked at.**

"**Then somebody ought to go and have a look at it.**"

"**Five miles of that coast has not been looked at by this yard and is not going to be this season,**" said Calder Marr, "**and that is written on the face of that sheet in my own words, and a man in a city can read it, and the man who set the type could not leave it out because I printed it myself and it is in his stick and not mine.

"**And I am not going to be talked out of it in a shed, and I am not going to be talked into it on the mole either. If somebody in this harbor wants five miles of that coast looked at, the way to do it is not to ask a yard of two and a half people that has a book in a file in a city. It is to hire a boat. It is about four days with a man on a lead and it is not a thing any of us can pay for, and that is the honest end of it.**"

"**You put it on a wall,**" the man with the brush said. "**You put it on a wall and told sixty boats where the copies were.**"

"**I did. And I have not taken it down and I am not going to, and a road everybody can use is not this yard's, and that half of it is on that same sheet, and if you want to be angry with me about the first half of that sentence then you have to be angry with me about the second half as well, and I have printed both and I have not pretended the second one is not mine.**"

Nobody said anything after that for a while. Wil Tranter folded his sheet and put it back in his coat, and then took it out again and put it on the bench and said the thing he had really come up the hill for, and he said it as a man putting a card on a table.

"**Then I will tell you the other half, because I have been carrying it since Friday morning and it has been sitting on the other side of the penny all morning.

"**I have a paper of my own. It came on the Saturday boat and it is a man's own paper about a boat of my own, and it is not a salvage paper and it is not about glass and it is nothing anybody in this harbor would want. And in it there is a line that says a vessel of a hundred and ninety tons could not have crossed that bar on the second of August past with eleven feet six aft, and it is going into a thing in a city in October, and the man who wrote it has never been on this coast and has never seen the water and is asking me whether I will put my word under his line.

"**And I have not said yes and I have not said no, and I came up here first, and I would like to know what a man in this harbor is supposed to put his word under when there are two figures on a printed sheet and neither of them belongs to him.**"

"**Nothing,**" said Calder Marr. "**Come back on that.**"

---

**And on the Thursday of the twenty-first, which is a rota day, the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers, and this party did not walk it, which is the sixth rota day of this month, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. And on the Friday the yard worked about four hours and forty minutes on the second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line over the side and back four times.**

**Gross on the board seventeen.** The eighth, two. The share, two. The victuals of the day, two. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Twelve out of seventeen paid at the mole and the yard's being five, and the five went up on the mole's steps at about half past twelve in daylight in front of about nine people, beside the three of the fourteenth and the two fives of August and the seven of the twenty-fourth of June, and no figure was argued and the eighth and the share came off the top of the day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May.

**Five marks and twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag**, and a wage of four goes out of it on Tuesday the twenty-sixth, and that is the second wage out of two days of work in three weeks.

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-second of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed: that on the Friday of that date a man of the first lay, who gave his name as Wil Tranter and who is fifty-one years old and is master of a coaster of about two hundred tons of his own, came up this hill at about half past two in the afternoon with a printed sheet in a coat pocket which he said he had bought off a stall in a counting office in a city for a penny on the Friday morning, and that the said sheet is a setting in type of this party's own sheet of the twenty-fifth of August past or of a copy of it, that it carries at its head the name of a printer in a city and a date and nothing else, and that a copy of this party's own sheet of the twenty-fifth of August went up this coast in a boat in the ordinary way and this party neither gave it nor withheld it and has entered that it has no standing to say a word about a copy of its own sheet once the copy is out of this harbor.*

*And that the said sheet carries this yard's figure for the bar of gravel and the figure of a man who keeps a channel nine miles off this coast, side by side, with the words *we are not guessing* between them, and with the sentence that a road nobody else can use is not a road and the sentence that a road everybody can use is not this yard's, both of which are this yard's own and are set in type in a city without leave; and that neither figure is withdrawn and neither is going to be; and that neither of them is decided by anybody in this harbor and that this party is not going to decide it in a shed, and has entered that it is not going to, and that the reason is a working one and is on the page: that a figure made once by one man with a hand lead's line and a slate is a figure and is not a certificate, and that a man who is going to be a name at the bottom of a printed line in a city has to be answerable for four decades of water and this yard is not going to put a name of its own under a figure in order to be rid of a question in a shed.*

*And that the said man said, and this party entered, that he was asked by a man in a city for his word under a line in a paper of his own concerning a vessel of a hundred and ninety tons and the bar on the second of August past, and that he has not said yes and has not said no, and that this party told him to come back on it, and that this party did not ask him for the paper and did not ask to see it and did not ask him a second question about it.*

*And that on the Thursday of the twenty-first instant this party did not charge the cistern behind the engine house, it being on a rota, and that four men this party does not know charged it at no figure, and that the cistern has held since the nineteenth of June.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the twenty-second of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a sheet of this yard's own making is now set in type in a city and cannot be un-set, and that a man in a city who has never been on this coast can hold both figures in his hand in a fortnight, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check a figure and cannot check what a compositor does with a sentence after the sentence has left this harbor, and that this party entered on the twenty-third of August that it had published a wall and cannot un-publish it, and that a printed sheet is the same instrument as a wall with one more step between it and the man who is not going to be able to be brought up in front of it; and that the four figures on this party's page about that coast, and the two figures of the other man's, and the Kettle, which was not looked at, all stand where they stood on the twenty-fifth of August, and none of them is settled in a shed on the twenty-second of September and none of them is going to be.*

**Five marks and twelve shillings and fourpence is in the bag and the wage of the twenty-sixth comes out of it. Nothing in a tin. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered, and a man in a city is making up a list of every vessel reported, claimed, entered and closed upon this coast for the year, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The hearing room was used on the sixteenth and the market wall has not been touched in September, and the two of them are never the same day.**
