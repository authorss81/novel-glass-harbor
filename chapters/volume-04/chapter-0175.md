# Chapter 175

*Five Marks, and a Counterfoil in a Box Under a Counter*

Friday the ninth and Saturday the tenth of October, on the second seam's own face, at the mole's steps, in a room off the fish market, and in the shed.

**On Friday the ninth of October the yard worked about four hours and forty minutes on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line over the side and back four times, and it brought five marks.**

**Gross on the board seventeen.** The eighth, two. The share, two. The victuals of the day, two. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Twelve out of seventeen paid at the mole and the yard's being five, and the five went up the mole's steps at about half past twelve in daylight in front of about eleven people, beside the three of the fourteenth of September and the five of the twenty-second of September and the three of the third of May, and no figure was argued, and the eighth and the share came off the top of the day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May.

A mark and twelve shillings was in the bag on Thursday. Six marks and twelve shillings was in it on Friday, and that is three hundred and four pence plus eight hundred, and a wage of four marks goes out of it on Tuesday the thirteenth and is four pence short of being two wages.

---

**And at about half past ten on that same Friday morning, a woman of thirty sat down at a copying counter in a room off the fish market, and the counter is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, and the woman behind it is a woman of fifty-eight who has not been thanked and did not ask to be.**

Mara Venn, thirty, of no office and of no party, who was at the market office from nine until about ten that morning because that office is open to anybody on a Tuesday and a Friday morning and she is a person who uses it, came in with four sheets of her own writing folded in a fold of oilcloth and put them on the counter.

"**I want a fair copy of that at a penny a sheet and I want it done while I wait, and the fold has gone soft on two of them and I do not want to lose a word to a crease.**

"**And I want you to do the head of it the way you do the head of everything, with the leaf number on it, because the last time I came in here I taught you to do that and I have not walked in here since.**

"**And I am going to say the rest of it now and not in four minutes, because there is a thing in this room and it is going to come out of me either way and I would rather it came out while I was asking for a copy.**"

"**Go on,**" said Pernel Saye, and did not put her pen down.

"**You carried a number up a yard on Thursday morning of last week and a man looked at it and could not help you with it, and I have not been in this town when you did it and I do not know how you came to be in that yard, and I am not going to ask, and I am going to assume that you came because there is one man on this coast who reads a page for what it is and not for what it says, and that is true, and that it was sensible of you, and that he was right not to be able to help you.**

"**And I am sitting here on a Friday morning because on the Friday after next there is a thing going into a room in a city and I have not been asked anything about it and I am not on anybody's paper, and I would like to know one fact before the thirteenth of October and I have got no instrument and no office and no money and there is nobody in this harbor I can pay.

"**So I am asking you, and you are not going to be thanked for it, because I am not going to be able to do anything with the answer and I have not got a thing to give you.**"

Pernel Saye put the pen down, which she did not do in the middle of a morning either.

"**Then here it is,**" she said, "**and I have been sitting on it since the Friday, which is four days, and I did not bring it to you because I did not know whether you were in this town and I did not know whether you would want it, and I have been not-knowing about it for four days and I am a woman of fifty-eight and I do not have a great many four days in a year.

"**When a leaf comes out of a file to be copied, the copying room fills a slip. It goes in duplicate. The top copy goes into the removal register at the end of the day's work and the counterfoil comes to the woman who did the copying, and the counterfoil is hers for two years and after that it is waste paper, and I have nine years of waste paper in a box under this counter and I have never once in my life thrown one of them away.

"**And I have not read it. I will not tell you I have not read it, because that would be a lie and I have been a copyist for thirty-one years and I read everything I copy, because I cannot copy a hand I have not looked at. What I did not do is take it home and think about it, and I have not, and I have had four days in which to.

"**Here.**"

She went down and came up with a grey card about the size of a man's hand and put it on the counter flat and turned it round so that it faced the woman standing there.

> *Morrow circuit office — copying room. Second day of September.*
> *Withdrawn: file of the ASTER GANNET, leaf 62. One leaf, to the copying room, copied entire, returned with the file.*
> *On the application of a commissioned officer of the Crown Navy, by letter of the first instant, a copy of which letter is on the file. This office agrees to the withdrawal.*
> *Reason entered by this office: risk management.*
> *The leaf is not to be produced. A copy is held.*

Mara Venn read it twice. She put one thumb on the fourth line and kept it there and did not say anything for about a minute and a half, which was three times as long as she had taken over the six things about a Crown notice in August.

"**That is a form,**" she said at last, "**and the form is the thing, and I have been in four offices in nine years and I have never once had a piece of paper in my hand that told me what an office had done to itself.**"

"**Then what is it.**"

"**It is an office saying what it is. In its own hand. About a page of its own file. **That is all it is and that is the whole of what has happened in this town in the last month, and it took two words, and the two words are not about the page.**

"**A page is a thing a person wrote. A form is a thing an office wrote about the page. And when the office writes the form, the form goes in front of the page, because that is what forms are for, and a man in a room in October who is handed the form will read the form. He will not turn it over. He will not ask what was on leaf sixty-two before the second of September. Nobody ever has.**"

She took her own four folded sheets out of the oilcloth and opened them on the counter beside the grey card, and they were her own writing, and at the head of the first of them in her own hand was a leaf number.

"**And now the rest of it, and I am going to say it fast because I have been not-saying it since Friday.

"**That number is sixty-two. That is my number, in my hand, written by me in June, because I write a leaf number on everything I sign, and I have never in nine years had a reason to write a leaf number on anything except my own fair copies.

"**And leaf sixty-two of that file is a form of the eleventh of November last, and the form is mine, and it is my refusal, and it is on four grounds, and I signed it and I dated it and my trade is on it and the office counter-signed it because I would not let it leave without a counter-signature and they knew my trade and they put their stamp on it anyway.

"**And the first of my four grounds is that the office will not countersign an instrument that puts a person inside a closure that the same office's own notice created. And I have not thought about that ground once in eleven months and I thought about it for eleven minutes before I wrote it down.

"**And they have taken my page out of their file, and they have had it copied, and the copy is to be read with a leaf I have never seen.

"**That is all of it. There is no more. I do not know what leaf eighty-eight says. I have never been in that room. I have no office, I have had none since the fourth of January, and the office itself told a yard on the thirtieth of December that I am not a person through whom it can be answered, and it was right then and it is right now, and that is the sentence that did this.**"

"**That is a hard thing to have happened to you,**" said Pernel Saye.

"**It is an ordinary thing that has happened to me,**" said Mara Venn, "**and that is worse and I would like it noticed. There is no villain in it and nobody has lied. A man asked a question and an office agreed to it and wrote down two words, and the two words are about the office, and the page underneath mine is somebody else's account of a thing, and my page is going to be read next to it because somebody in that room thought my four grounds said something about their account, and I have not got the office that would let me find out.

"**And I am owed forty marks first after food since the fifteenth of October by the work of this harbor, and six marks and two days is my price on every coast and it does not come down, and there is nobody in this town who has got either.

"**So here is what I have got. I have got a number that two people in this town can check and neither of us has seen the page. I have got a counterfoil in a box under your counter that is going to be waste paper in four years. I have got my own copy of a page that is in a city. And I have got a slip that says the copy of my page is to be read with a page I have never seen.

"**Three things that all agree with each other and not one of them is the page.**"

She stood at the counter for a while after that and then she took her four sheets and put them back in the fold of oilcloth.

"**Make me the copy,**" she said. "**And put the leaf number at the head of it, and charge me a penny a sheet, and do not do it for nothing, because the last time I did a thing for nothing in a room in this town I said out loud in front of nine people that I would not do one again and I have not and I am not going to start on a Friday morning.**"

---

**And on Saturday the tenth the yard did no work and heard nothing, and the whole of the day went into mending gear, and a man of the third lay came up the hill at about noon to say that the pump was wanted on a wharf on Monday if this yard wanted the day, and that it is the first day anybody has offered this yard without being asked for.**

"**I cannot take the Monday,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to say the reason out loud and you are going to tell me it is a bad one, because it is.

"**This yard is in a room on the Thursday and the Friday of that week, at ten in the morning, for about two hours, and then it is on the boards and the quay for the rest of it like any other week. And I have a hearing in eight days that I have entered I will not be represented at, and I have not asked anybody to represent me, and I have not got a second hand and I have got a shipwright and a boy of twenty-two who cannot do what he was kept for and does not want to be asked about it. A pump on a wharf is a day. It is a day that a crew of two and a half cannot lose on a Monday in the week before a sitting, and if I take it I will do it badly and be paid for it and I will be a man who took a day before a sitting.

"**And that is the whole of my reason and it is a working one and it is not a virtue, and I am not going to thank you for offering it and I am not going to say I am sorry about it, and both of those are the same words I have said in that shed since May.**

"**Come back on it after the seventeenth and I will take whatever you have got.**"

"**I will come back on it,**" the man said, "**and I am not going to argue with a man about a Monday, and I have had a Monday out of a month this year and so have you.**"

*First column, entered on Saturday the tenth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a day of work on a wharf was offered to this party on the Saturday instant by a man of the third lay for the Monday instant, being the twelfth, being the first day anybody has offered this yard without being asked for; and that this party refused it, and entered why, and that the reason is that this party is in a hearing room on the Thursday and the Friday of that week and has nobody to stand in for it and is not going to hire anybody to stand in for it; and that the said man was not thanked and was not told this party was sorry, and said in this yard that he had had a Monday out of a month this year as well and was not going to argue about it, and that the said man said he would come back on it after the seventeenth instant and this party said he would take whatever he had got; and that the cost of that refusal is a day of about three marks that was there to be taken and was not, and that it is entered here because a day that is refused is a day that is not earned and not entered as earned; and that on Friday the ninth instant a day was worked on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water and a hand lead's line over the side and back four times, and that the gross was seventeen and the yard's was five, and that twelve of the seventeen was paid at the mole and the eighth and the share came off the top.*

*And that on Friday the ninth instant, at about half past ten in the morning, a person of no office, being Mara Venn, thirty, of this harbor and of no party, was in a room off the fish market at a copying counter, and that this party was not in that room, was not asked anything by anybody in it, did not know that she was in it, and has not been told since by anybody; and that this party has entered that and has entered that it is not going to ask, and has entered why, and the entry is made on the nineteenth day of October instant and not before, and the reason is that this party has been refused once inside three weeks by that woman in a room open to anybody in eleven people's hearing, and that a party which has been refused once and then turns up with a second errand is a party which has decided that a person is a door, and that she told this party in that room that she is not a door.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the tenth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the second day of October instant that a document is a thing an office says about itself, and that on the tenth instant he has entered that he does not know that a document has been said about an office in this town, and that he has not been told and has not asked and is not going to ask, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a boat can check a number in a book and cannot check what is done with a number in a building about four hundred yards from that boat, and that a party which has published itself is not a party which gets to be sorry in a street and mean it, and that a party which has been refused in a room is not a party which gets to go round the back of the room to a woman with a counter.*

**No money came into this yard on the Saturday and none went out, and six marks and twelve shillings is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the thirteenth, and a day of about three marks on a wharf was refused for Monday the twelfth and the man who offered it is coming back on it after the seventeenth. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, three counts unmoved. Five marks of gland standing unpaid and useless. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. A man of Ashtide is owed three marks for a day of work on the eighth of August, unmoved since that day, and he is not named in that book again and is not going to be sent for. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's bellwright, unargued, and that man was not asked anything this month. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the line at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the six marks and two days have not been paid and have not been offered, and a fair copy of four sheets of her own writing was charged to her at a penny a sheet in a room off the fish market and was paid, and those are three different facts and none of them is either of the others. Twenty-one lines and eighty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, fifteen envelopes on a bench, and three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September and the sixth of October. The third column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-two days up the crown and neither of them has moved. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The cistern behind the engine house was charged by strangers at no figure on the first, the fifth and the eighth of October and this party walked none of the three. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by an officer this yard emptied, and this yard's four returns are in her hands. And on Saturday the tenth of October a woman of thirty was in a room off the fish market with a piece of card on a counter, and this yard was four hundred yards up a hill mending gear and did not know it, and neither of those two facts is going to be a decision this month.**
