# Chapter 165

*A Wage That Goes In, and a Mooring That Is Not This Yard's*

Tuesday the nineteenth and Wednesday the twentieth of September, at the head of the dock, in a doorway, and in the shed.

**The wage of four marks went in at about eleven in the morning on Tuesday the nineteenth, in Calder Marr's own left hand, because there is nobody else in that shed who writes, and the week went on the outside of the envelope in the same hand, and the envelope went into the bench chest.**

Nineteen lines and seventy-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and it has moved twice in three weeks and it has not moved since the twenty-ninth of August before those two.

**Twelve shillings and fourpence in the bag.** Fourteen envelopes on the bench, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is two, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth of August and the week of Tuesday the fifth of September, and both figures are entered in the book in the same breath and neither of them is the other.

---

**And then the whole of it went up on the mole's steps at about half past eleven in the morning, in daylight, in front of about eleven people, on a board, in Calder Marr's own left hand, and it took about two minutes and nobody argued any of it.**

"**Twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag. Nineteen lines and seventy-six marks in the column and the fifth of March the oldest. Fourteen envelopes on a bench in that chest and two weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, which are the fifteenth of August and the fifth of September, and that is eight marks this yard has not put into a man's hand and has entered with the figure and the date against it and is not going to enter a third time.

"**Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, with a date on it, in no column of that book, and not a shilling of it paid, and he is not named in that book again.

"**Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, out of her own pocket, and not paid, and her name is on the line.

"**Five marks of compressor's gland on a creditor's page that has not been re-written since the fourth of January, unpaid since the nineteenth of May, and of no use to this yard whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no delivery hose and the longest thing of hose in it is about nine fathoms and it is not ours.

"**Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, three counts of weeks standing at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven and none of them moved. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April.

"**Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two who cannot do the thing he was kept for. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October. Eight shillings and tenpence standing to one woman of this harbor. Eight marks and a day of a boat standing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unpaid, unargued, and the chest in that shed has not been opened and no figure out of it is on any wall in this town.

"**And eleven logged occasions in the delayed note since the twenty-sixth of October, two of them logged with no call in them, and neither count moved, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and was not taken on the eighth of August and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first.

"**And this is a yard of two and a half people whose book went into a file in a city on the ninth of September and cannot be got back out of it, and I am not going to say one word about that, and if anybody in this market wants to know what a poor crew does with a form they can come up the hill and watch us not do it.**"

Nobody argued any of it. A man of the second lay said *that is a hard thing to have on a wall* and Calder Marr said it is not on a wall, it is on a board in the rain, and the man said *it is on a wall, you have had a wall since May*, and Calder Marr said that was fair and went in.

---

**And on Wednesday the twentieth, at about half past two in the afternoon, a man of the first lay came into the yard with a boat of about ninety tons of his own and asked whether the mooring at the ninth seam would take her.**

Calder Marr did not go down to the water and did not offer to and did not tell anybody else to.

"**No,**" he said. "**And I am going to give you the whole of that no in about eleven sentences and then I am going to go back to a bench, and the reason I am doing it in one go is that I would rather you had it all at once from a man who cannot help you than in four pieces from four men who each can.

"**That mooring is harbor ground. It is not this yard's. I am not the harbor authority, I have no power over it, I have never been asked for a permit in respect of it and I am not going to be, and the office that does have power over it has a repair account that has been empty since the middle of February and is not going to be filled this year and has nothing to give you and cannot give you a place.

"**What I can do is tell you what three things in my own book say and who said them, and take nothing for it, and be told not to be thanked, and here they are.

"**On Monday the third of July an end of it was entered against that mooring in the book of this harbor authority in the officer's own hand, and the end of it was the first of September, which was a Friday. On the sixth, the fourteenth, the twenty-first and the twenty-eighth days of August a man of the tide gang named Tam Rundle and a man of the third lay named Danno Fisk walked out to that mooring on their own time, and on the twenty-eighth they put the shackle back on the ring and took a turn on the eye and put the seizing back on with a maul and did about forty minutes of it. On the first of September the two of them found it in service with a pole and a lead line and a rule, at about four feet on the flood at about twenty past nine in the morning.

"**They were not paid by the harbor authority. They were not paid by this yard. Nobody in this harbor ordered them and the office did not know they came and would not have known. And that mooring is in service this afternoon because two men scraped a ring off a stone four times on their own time, and the only reason those two men knew there was a date coming is that the date was printed on a list this yard put up on a market wall on the third of May.

"**And I am not going to be thanked for that and I have not been. I have entered the whole of it in my own book against this yard's own interest, on the first of September, and the reason I gave is that a party that has been telling this harbor for five months that the only thing that makes a wall worth reading is that somebody can be asked a question about it, got asked a question about it by nobody, and two strangers acted on the answer.

"**So the favor on that mooring is not mine. It is not this yard's. It belongs to the man of the third lay who did the forty minutes, and if you want to use that mooring you want to go and ask the tide gang and not me, because the tide gang is four men short of what it was and they will tell you what it is worth to them and I am not going to translate it for you.**"

---

**And the man asked one question at the door, which was not about the mooring, and got an answer.**

"**Then is it yours, the wall. The list.**"

"**The wall is a set of nails and nobody put them in and they were here before the market office. The list is ours and every figure on it is ours and I put my own name to all of it. And I am not going to be asked about the list in a doorway twice in one week, and if you have a question about a figure on it, come up on a Tuesday when there is a market and ask me in front of people, because that is the whole of what a wall is for and I am not doing it standing in the rain.**"

"**I did not mean it like that.**"

"**I know you did not. I said it that way so that you would remember that I said it.**"

---

**And at about nine in the morning on the Wednesday the man came back up the hill with Tam Rundle of the tide gang, and neither of the two of them had asked the man a question about the nine days he gave on the twenty-ninth of July and neither has asked him one now and neither is going to.**

Tam Rundle said the figure and said the terms and said that the tide gang would carry a boat of ninety tons to that mooring on a slack water for a day and a half and that a day and a half of the gang was the whole of the price and it was not a figure and it was not this yard's business what figure the gang put on it. And he said that the ring had been looked at four times and would want looking at again in about a month and that whoever looked at it next would do it for nothing as well, because that is what the last two men did, and that he was not going to say anything about that either.

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twentieth of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed: that the mooring at the ninth seam of this mole is harbor ground and is not the property of this party, and that on the twentieth instant a man of the first lay asked this party whether that mooring would take a boat of about ninety tons, and that this party said that it would not answer for the mooring, that it is not the harbor authority, that it has no power over that mooring and has not been asked for a permit in respect of it and is not going to be, and that it gave the man what its own book holds, namely the entry of the third of July in the officer's own hand, the four walks of the sixth, fourteenth, twenty-first and twenty-eighth days of August by a man of the tide gang and a man of the third lay on their own time, the forty minutes of seizing on the twenty-eighth, and the finding of it in service on the first of September at about twenty past nine in the morning at about four feet on the flood.*

*And that this party took no money for that and was not thanked for it and did not ask to be, and has entered why: that the favor of that mooring belongs to the man of the third lay who did the work and to the tide gang, and not to this yard; and that the reason that mooring is in service at all is that a list on a market wall said when the end of it was, and that this party has entered the whole of that against its own interest on the first of September and has not taken it back; and that the tide gang is four men short of what it was and that a party which is two and a half people does not get to speak for a gang of eight that is four short, and did not try.*

*And that the wage of four marks for the week ending on Tuesday the nineteenth of September instant was paid at about eleven in the morning of that day into an envelope in the holder's own left hand with the week on the outside of it, that the column stands at nineteen lines and seventy-six marks with the fifth of March the oldest, that the bag stands at twelve shillings and fourpence, that the count of envelopes on the bench is fourteen, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is two, being the fifteenth of August and the fifth of September.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the twentieth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a day of work on the Thursday of the fourteenth instant brought three marks and paid a wage of four on the Tuesday, and that a man in this harbor can check both halves of that and cannot check the next one, which is what the week of the twenty-first instant is going to bring, and that this party does not know and is not going to be told by anybody, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a figure for a week that has not happened is not a figure at all and writing one down would be writing down a hope, and a hope in a first column is a lie that a man in four years cannot disprove.*

**The cistern behind the engine house is on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday. The last time it was charged was the Thursday of the fourteenth of September and the next will be the Thursday of the twenty-first, and this party has not walked it since the third of August and did not walk it on the fourteenth, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and was not reached, and the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January is refused in a record with a date on it. The two objects of nobody's are on the crown about a hundred and twenty-six days and have not moved, and one of the tarpaulins still has a corner off it since the seventeenth of June.**
