# Chapter 164

*A Man Who Came Up the Hill and Was Not Asked Anything*

Sunday the seventeenth and Monday the eighteenth of September, on the mole's crown above the third seam's mark, in the shed, and at the head of the dock.

**A man of the third lay came up the hill on Sunday the seventeenth at about eleven in the morning and stood in the yard in the rain and said the thing he had come to say, and nobody asked him anything.**

He gave his name before he said it, which is what a man does when he has come up a hill in the rain to say a thing to two people and wants it in a book. Denzil Hare, of the third lay, about fifty, a boat of his own and no crew of his own since the spring.

"**I had him in a fore berth in June,**" he said. "**From about the sixth to about the twenty-first, and I paid him out of my own pocket and not out of a wage, because he was not on a wage, and I am not going to be asked about that and I am not going to be asked why.

"**And he is not in that berth now and he has not been in it since the twenty-first of June and I am not going to be told why, because I am not asking.

"**That is what I came to say. Where he was, and where he is not. In June. And that is the whole of it and I have been standing in your yard for four minutes waiting for one of you to ask me a question and neither of you has, and I want it written down that I came up here and said it and was not asked anything, because I have read your wall and I know what a party that publishes itself is supposed to be, and I have decided I am going to come and say a thing and be left alone, and that is a thing I have decided and not a thing that has been arranged.**"

"**Then here it is,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to ask you a question and you are not going to get one out of me either, and I would like both halves of that on the page.**"

He wrote it down standing up, on the bench, in the doorway, because it was raining on the shed floor and neither of them was going to move the book.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Sunday the seventeenth of September instant a man of the third lay, who gave his name as Denzil Hare and who is about fifty years old and has a boat of his own, came up this hill at about eleven in the morning and said of his own account and unasked, that a boy was in a fore berth of his from about the sixth to about the twenty-first day of June past, that he was paid out of the man's own pocket and not out of a wage, and that he is not in that berth and has not been since the twenty-first of June past; and that this party asked him no question, and entered that, and entered the reason, which is that the three nots of the eighth of June stand and that nobody in this crew writes to that boy, goes to see him, or asks anybody who has seen him one word about what he said, and that the whole of what a man of the third lay came up a hill in the rain to say on Sunday the seventeenth instant was where the boy was and where he is not, and that this party has entered that and has not asked for a word more and is not going to; and that this party did not write to the boy on that day, has not written to him since the eighth of June, and is not going to; and that this party has entered that it wanted to say thank you and did not say it, and has entered why, which is that a man who has come up a hill to say where a boy was does not come up a hill to be thanked, and that the yard said so out loud to him at the time and he did not answer it.*

---

**And then the third column, and it is the reason this chapter is the shape it is.**

The head of it is in a boy's hand and has been since the second of May, two times in the boy's own writing, and a request to scrape them out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column. Beneath them, the last entry in the third column is the first of August and it was filled at about four hours' lateness.

**It has not been filled since. It was not filled on the eighth of August, which was the first day since the second of May, and it was not filled on Sunday the tenth of September, which was the second, and it has not been filled on any day this party has done work on since the third of August, and nobody has been appointed, and a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills.**

"**Then I am not going to fill it,**" said Nessa Pike, on the Sunday night, in the shed, with the lamp lit. "**And I want it on the page that I did not fill it and that I thought about it for about a minute and a half, because I am the person holding the pencil in this yard and I am the only one here who could have filled it tonight with two strokes and nobody would ever have known.

"**And the reason I did not is that it is not mine and that it is not a column of facts. It is a page where a man wrote down what he said when he was not in the boat, and the whole of its value is that nobody filled it but him. A column kept by a shipwright in a shed on a Sunday is a column about a shipwright.

"**And a request to scrape the two times out of the head of it is refused, in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and the count of four stands in the first column in a ship's hand with no name against it, and the count of four is the count of four.**"

---

**And there was a quarter of an hour in the middle of that Sunday that the two of them were not alone in, and it was with the man of the third lay, and it is the whole of what is in the second column on that date, and there is nothing else in that column that night.**

He was still in the doorway with his hat in his hand and the rain coming in past him, and he said something about his own boat and about a pump that had been trouble, and Calder Marr answered him, and then answered him again about two things he had already said, and the second answer was not the answer to the second thing.

Nobody in that doorway said anything about it, because there was only one other person in it and she was on a bench with a lamp and a pencil and she was not going to say to a man of fifty in the rain that he had been spoken to twice.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the seventeenth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, in the holder's own left hand, and it is a sentence and not a fact about a hand: that the holder of the writ of the first of May cannot always tell which of two things he said in the last minute was the one he had decided to say, and that it happened on the Sunday afternoon of this date in a doorway to a man of the third lay who came up a hill in the rain to say where a boy was, and that the holder was not in the water and has not been in the water since the eighth of August, and that this is a tide-sickness and has been got since the fourteenth of October of last year and is not going off; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check what he said and cannot check what he will say in a minute, and that the reason it is entered now and was not entered on the eighth of August is that on the eighth of August it cost him a boat and four people and a case and he wrote the fact and not the name, and that a man who writes the fact and not the name six weeks running has stopped writing a column and started keeping a habit, and there is a shipwright in this shed who has been telling him for five months that a column is not a defense of anything.*

*And that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past in this same column are still four, and are still nameless, and are not struck out, and a request to strike one out of this column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

**That is the second time the word has been written in this yard's own book, and the first time since the twentieth of July, and it is in the second column, in his own left hand, with a reason against the line, and nobody in this shed read it back to him and he did not ask.**

---

**And on Monday the eighteenth the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was twenty Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room since the sixteenth of September and the next one is to be appointed for the week following the twelfth of October.**

The two objects of nobody's were up on the crown about a hundred and twenty-four days, one of the tarpaulins with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them moved, and nobody in this party went near them on the Sunday or the Monday and did not count them twice.

**And on the Monday night the shipwright read the whole of the two days back from the top of the bench to the bottom and the holder read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed, and the far bench was behind her with nobody on it after four o'clock on both days.**

*First column, entered on Monday the eighteenth of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed: that the third column of this book has not been filled since the first day of August, that it was not filled on the eighth of August and not on the tenth of September and not on any day this party has done work on since the third of August, and that the party's own account does not carry a line for that column on any day since, and that this party does not know who will fill it next and has not appointed anybody and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Monday night, and that a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills, and that this party entered that on the ninth of August and has entered it again tonight because it is still true; and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that the forty marks standing to him stand whole and are not reduced and are not going to be, and that this party is not going to hire a boy to read a position back at four marks a week and call that the same thing.*

*And that Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Sunday from about ten until about four and on the Monday from about nine until about three, that he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing, and that Calder Marr did not go over to him on either day, and that this party has entered that he did not and that neither brother has said why and neither brother is going to, and that it is a single line in this column and either brother can check it on any day of a week.*

**No money moved on the Sunday and none on the Monday. Four marks and twelve shillings and fourpence in the bag, and a wage of four goes out of it tomorrow on the Tuesday, and the day of Thursday the fourteenth brought three of that four and the other mark and twelve shillings and fourpence has been in the bag since the twenty-ninth of August. Nothing in a tin. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written. The cistern is charged on a rota and this party did not walk it on the Thursday. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. And the plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in the mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January is refused in a record with a date on it, and this party has not written a better letter and is not going to, and whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed.**
