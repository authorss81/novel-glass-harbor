# Chapter 195

*The Face of a Copy, and Two Different Lengths*

Tuesday the twenty-fourth and Wednesday the twenty-fifth of November, at the mole's steps, on the counter of the circuit office and back up the hill, and in the shed.

**On Tuesday the twenty-fourth of November the wage of four marks went in, into an envelope, in the holder's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it, and it went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people in about two minutes, and no figure was argued.**

Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. Twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest. Five weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November and the seventeenth of November, entered in the same breath and not one after the other.

**And at about four in the afternoon of that Tuesday the holder of the writ of the first of May came back up this hill with a sheet of paper in the inside pocket of a coat, and he had been down at the counter of the circuit office of this place since about two in the afternoon, and a clerk of that office had given his name and the name of his office and had said three things about a thing leaving a counter, and the three things are in the first column tonight.**

---

**And at about half past eight on Wednesday the twenty-fifth, in that shed, with the lamp lit, the ship's hand read the whole of it out from the top of the bench, and the holder read it back after her, and nobody else was in the room.**

It is one sheet. It is six heads. It is about a page and a half of writing, and the writing is a clerk's, and the words of the fourth head and the fifth head and the sixth head are the words of the record and not the words of a clerk, because a clerk does not get to add a limit to a record.

> **One. What this sheet is.**
>
> *This is a provisional public copy record. It is a copy of a record and it is not the thing. The thing is in the custody room of this office. The thing is not on the hill this sheet was carried up and it is not going to be.*
>
> **Two. The item.**
>
> *One item of recovered transparent mineral, entered in the custody room of this office. The number against it is not on this sheet. The party that made the application knows what the item is and has known since the fourteenth day of October two years past, and this office is not going to write it out again on a sheet that goes up a hill.*
>
> **Three. How it came here.**
>
> *Found in water, in a bucket, in a boat. Entered in the keeper's line of a party's own return without being asked. Not dried. Not wiped. No cloth put into it. Sealed by a courier and entered.*
>
> **Four. The limits this record carries, which are printed here in the record's own words.**
>
> *A record of a material is a record of one person's frame. It is not a record of a sea. Nothing on this sheet is a finding of consent. This sheet is a record of provenance and not of the water.*
>
> **Five. The discrepancy this record carries.**
>
> *A manifest referred to in this record, as that manifest stood when this office first entered the item, and as that manifest stands now, do not agree. A line of it has been altered. The alteration bears a date. This record does not show by whose hand, at whose instance, or with what intent the alteration was made, and this record names no person.*
>
> **Six. What this sheet does not show.**
>
> *Not who altered it. Not what the item carried. Not who held it before it was in a bucket. Not consent. Not motive. And not that anything on this sheet is true.*

Nessa Pike put it down on the bench with the edge of it squared to the edge of the pump, and neither of them said anything for about a minute.

"**Now the part I have been waiting to say since Saturday,**" she said, "**and I am going to say it in the order it comes.

"**On the face of it: about a page and a half. Six heads, and a clerk's hand, and a line and a date and an hour at the foot, and an office's name where a man's name would be. That is the whole of what is on it.

"**And the yard can check none of it. Not the third head, because the bucket and the boat and the return are this yard's own and that is a check on the yard and not on the office. Not the fourth, because a limit is a thing an office says about itself. Not the fifth, because the manifest is in a file in a city and the first copy of it is in the same file and this yard has seen neither and has not asked for either and is not going to. And not the sixth, because the sixth is the only head on the sheet that is about nothing at all and a sheet about nothing at all is the last thing in the world anybody can check.

"**So it is a page and a half on the face of it and it is the rest of this book's life underneath it, and those are two different lengths, and they were two different lengths before anybody in this room had read a word of it.**"

"**Say the fifth head again,**" said Calder Marr, "**and then say the thing underneath it, because I want to hear whether you have heard it the way I have heard it.**"

"**A line of it has been altered. The alteration bears a date. And it does not say by whose hand, at whose instance, or with what intent, and it names no person.

"**And I want to say the part that is going to be hard to write down, so I am going to say it now while there are only two of us in the room. A copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check. That was this party's own sentence in July and it has not been improved on by asking for one, and I am not going to stand in this shed and pretend that holding a piece of paper has made it a better sentence than it was in July.

"**What has changed is not the sentence. What has changed is that this yard has decided which not-knowing it will put up with. In July it did not know what a record said and could not ask. Now it knows one thing, on a page, that a line of a manifest was altered, and it does not know who, and it is going into a room on Thursday with that, and the price of it is that from this day forward this yard is a party that has seen a document and said nothing about it in a market, and there is no version of this yard after the twenty-fifth of November in which that is not a fact about it.**"

"**Then put that in the first column,**" said Calder Marr, "**all of it, and put in the same paragraph that neither half of this is a discovery in this yard's book, because both have been on this page since June and the tenth of November said in as many words what a copy of a record would show and what it would not, and I am not going to be a man in four months who says he found something out.

"**And we are not going back up that hill to ask which hand. And I want the reason on the page, and the reason is not that the clerk would not tell us, because he would have told us if he had it. The reason is that an office which has put a line in a form that says it does not name a person has said it in a form, and a form that names a person is a different form, and a party that has been refused once in this town is not going to go up a hill four days later and ask a different one to be braver.**"

"**And the last thing,**" said Nessa Pike. "**It is dated the twenty-fourth. That is the date of the counter and not the date of the record, and the record is thirteen years old and a part of it is older, and there is a date on the top of that sheet and it is about a room and not about thirteen years.

"**And in about four years somebody is going to read that date and stop there.**"

"**Then they will stop there,**" said Calder Marr, "**and we will have put it there ourselves, which is a thing we have not done yet.**"

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-fifth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on Tuesday the twenty-fourth instant was paid into an envelope in the holder's own left hand at about eleven in the morning in daylight in front of about nine people with the week written on the outside of it, and that the column of this book stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest, and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is twenty, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is five, being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November and the seventeenth of November past; and that one mark and six shillings and five pence was in the bag on the morning of the said day, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence, and that four marks was taken out of it at a counter of this yard, which is six hundred and forty pence.*

*And that at about two in the afternoon of Tuesday the twenty-fourth instant the holder went down the hill to the counter of the copying room of the circuit office of this place and at about four in the afternoon came back up this hill with one sheet of paper in the inside pocket of a coat, and that a clerk of that office gave his name as Ambrose Sell and gave the name of his office before he said anything else, and said three things about a thing leaving a counter; and that the first was that a copy is made at a counter and not sent, and that this office does not send copies and does not deliver them and has not sent one in four months; and that the second was that the sheet carries at its foot a line and a date and an hour and the name of an office and no man's name; and that the third was that a copy which has left a counter cannot be un-read, and that this yard had better be certain it wanted it read in a room before the first of December, and that after the first of December that question is not going to be asked again by anybody in this harbor.*

*And that the said sheet is a provisional public copy record in six heads and about a page and a half of writing, and that the heads are: what the sheet is, being a copy of a record and not the thing; the item, being one item of recovered transparent mineral entered in the custody room of that office, with the number against it not on the sheet; how it came here, being found in water in a bucket in a boat and entered in the keeper's line of a party's own return without being asked, not dried, not wiped, no cloth put into it, sealed by a courier and entered; the limits the record carries, printed in the record's own words, being that a record of a material is a record of one person's frame and is not a record of a sea, that nothing on the sheet is a finding of consent, and that the sheet is a record of provenance and not of the water; the discrepancy the record carries, being that a manifest referred to in the record as it stood when that office first entered the item and as it stands now do not agree, that a line of it has been altered, that the alteration bears a date, and that the record does not show by whose hand, at whose instance or with what intent, and names no person; and what the sheet does not show, being not who altered it, not what the item carried, not who held it before it was in a bucket, not consent, not motive, and not that anything on the sheet is true.*

*And that this party entered on the said evening that the sheet shows that a manifest was altered and does not show who altered it, and that the sheet says so on its own face, and that neither half of that is a discovery in this yard's book, because this yard entered in June, and again on the eighteenth day of September instant, and again on the tenth day of November instant, what a copy of a record would show and what it would not show, and because a copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check was this party's own sentence in the month of July last and applying for one has not improved it and is not going to be improved on by anything said in a room on Thursday; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a man in this harbor can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask to see the sheet and can ask the clerk of the circuit office whether it came off that counter on the twenty-fourth, and that both of those are checkable, and that what a page and a half is worth underneath it in four years is not checkable from a mole.*

*And that Alden Marr was at the far bench on Wednesday the twenty-fifth instant from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Tuesday at any hour.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the twenty-fifth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand read the whole of the said sheet out in this shed on the evening of the said day and the holder read it back after her and nobody else was in the room; and that she said that on the face of it there is a page and a half, six heads, a clerk's hand, and a line and a date and an hour at the foot, and that the yard can check none of it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that the distance between a page and a half and the rest of a party's life is not a distance a man on a mole can walk, and that a man on a mole can look at a sheet and cannot look at a file in a city, and that the only thing in this harbor that could tell the difference is a person of no office who is owed forty marks and who has said in writing that she will not be cited, quoted, produced or asked again.*

*And that the holder entered on the said evening that from the twenty-fifth day of November instant this yard is a party that has seen a document and said nothing about it in a market, and that there is no version of this yard after the said day in which that is not a fact about it, and that he entered it against himself in the first column above and is not going to be thanked for it and is not asking; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a party is in four years is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except the people who have to cross a bar and buy a fish and go in a boat.*

*And that the said sheet is dated the twenty-fourth instant, which is the date of the counter and not the date of the record, and that the record is thirteen years old and a part of it is older, and that a date at the head of a sheet is a fact about a room and not about thirteen years, and that this yard put that date there itself and knows what it is for, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a reader in a city is going to stop at is not checkable from a mole.*

**No money came into this yard on the Tuesday and none on the Wednesday, and a wage of four marks went out of it on the Tuesday, and one mark and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and nobody has written to him and nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, five weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and sixty-five days up the crown of this mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in the hand of the woman making it, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted.**
