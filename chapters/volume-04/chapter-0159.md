# Chapter 159

*The Book Is Produced Whole*

Saturday the ninth of September, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, in daylight, in about nine people's hearing, and in the shed at night.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday the fourth and it came down bare, and that was the eighteenth Monday since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and it was bare again this morning with nine people standing in front of it and a slate on it with nothing on the slate, which is how it has been for eighteen Mondays while this harbor has had a form coming up its hill.**

The book is fifty-one weeks of this yard's own weather and this yard's own shame. It begins on the fourteenth of October and it is a working book in a working hand with three columns in it, and the first of the three was made by a boy of twenty-two and has two times at the head of it in his hand, and a request to scrape those two times out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.

---

**A man in a coat came in with a paper and he did not sit down, and he said his name and the name of his office, and it was the same name and the same office as on the twelfth of August and as on the nineteenth of this month in a doorway, and this party still does not know him and has not asked and is not going to find out this month.**

"**I am going to read a paper and then I am going to ask for two things and I am going to write down what you say, the way I wrote down what you said in this room on the twelfth of August.

"**One. This paper is a production and not an appointment. The party is required to produce, at this room, on this day, at about ten of the clock in the morning, its crew's book kept under the reporting clause upon the writ of salvage working granted at this harbor on the first of May instant, whole and unshortened, and a copy of that book.

"**Two. The party is required to produce the said writ and the four returns made under the clause.

"**Three. The party is required to answer, at this sitting, one question, and I am going to read the question at the end and it is the question the clause itself asks and it is not my question.

"**Four. Nothing in this paper touches, grants or refuses the second season of the said writ, and a sitting to be appointed will follow this one and will be served on the party and not left with anybody, and that is not my paper either.**"

He put the paper on the table face up.

"**The question is this. On or about Tuesday the eighth of August instant, did this party make a finding of worked glass, whole or in part; and if it did, state the place, the hour, the keeper and the limits.**"

---

**And Calder Marr said out loud why he was opening the book before he opened it, and the reason is the clause's own construction and has been the reason since the second of May, and he said it in the same words he said it in this room in June and in a room in April and he did not soften it or improve it.**

"**This book has three columns and the first two are ours, and the second one is a page of things a man cannot check, and I want that said in this room before anybody sees the inside of it, and I want it said by me and not by one of you.

"**The second column is not a defense of anything. It never was. It is a page where a man writes down what he cannot check and puts a reason against the line so that a person reading it in four years can see at once that the man knew he was writing something he could not check. That is all it has ever been. There is a line in the first column in a ship's hand, with no name against it, that says the count of four. That is the count of four and it is not a fifth thing and it is not a tally of anything and it has not moved since the twelfth of July and it is not going to move this month.

"**The third column was made on the second of May by a boy of twenty-two for what he said when he was not in the boat. It is filled late. The lateness is entered against every entry. It has not been filled on the eighth of August and there is a line in the first column saying why, and the line says this party does not know who will fill it next and has not appointed anybody.

"**And at the head of the third column there is a line saying that a number in a lead plug in a chest in that shed is not where that goes. I am not going to say one word about that number to you or to anybody and I never have, and I have not been asked about it and I am not going to be.**"

He opened it.

---

**And he did not read one line of it aloud in that room, and neither did the shipwright, and the man in the coat did not ask them to, and there were about nine people in the room and nine people watched a man turn the pages of a book from the fourteenth of October to the ninth of this month without hearing a figure out of any of them.**

It took about ten minutes to go through it and about two minutes to make the copy, and the copy was made with a pen and not a press, and the making of a copy is a thing a clerk does and the clerk of the circuit office did it himself, and the man in the coat looked at the copy and did not open it.

"**I am not going to open it here,**" he said. "**I am going to take it to a city and read it there, and it will be read in a room with a table and a pen in it by somebody who is not me, and I am telling you that now so that you can have it in your own book tonight and not be told about it in a fortnight. I would rather you had it from me at a table on a Saturday morning than find out in October.**"

**And the copy went into a file. That is the whole of what happened to it in this room. A party's whole record, fifty-one weeks of it, went into a file in a city in another man's hand, and that is the first time in four volumes that a copy of this yard's own record has left this harbor.**

Ambrose Sell wrote on his own page the date, the hour at which he wrote it, and not the hour at which it was done, because that is a clerk's practice and has been one since April.

"**And that is the ninth time this year I have said a thing I could not enter,**" he said, "**and I am going to say what the first eight were, because this room is a room and the man who reads a book in a city will not have my page.

"**The first of the eight was in a room in this harbor in the month of April. The seventh of them was on the steps of this mole on Tuesday the twenty-fifth of July and it is in the read-out of that day, out of a man of the third lay's mouth, and the yard has it on a wall. The eighth was on Saturday the nineteenth of August in a doorway on this hill and I gave this party a room and told it the truth about a day. And this is the ninth.

"**And the thing I cannot hold this time is a line in the second column of that book. It is a line about a man in a boat on the eighth of August not being able to tell for about a second and a half whether a shout came off the water or off the deck of a vessel lying in about seven fathoms with four persons in her, and that line has a reason against it and the reason is that no man on a mole can check which of his own ears a sound came in through.

"**What a box holds is this: that a line exists, that it is in that book, that it is in the second column and not the first, that it has a reason against it, and that it was entered on the Wednesday night by the man whose line it is. What a box does not hold is what was in it. I have said a thing like that nine times this year and I have kept all nine in a book of my own and I have stopped mentioning the wall two months ago and I am mentioning it now because a man is going to read that book in a city and he is going to find the line, and he is going to ask the clerk, and I would rather the answer came from me than from a stranger's guess.**"

---

**And the man in the coat asked his one question, and it was the clause's own question, and the yard answered it in a form and it is in the first column tonight in the holder's own left hand with the hour against it, and it is a form and it is not a conclusion.**

"**Here it is, in the order the clause wants it and in no other order.

"**The place. The party was on the water on Tuesday the eighth of August instant at about a mile and a half off this harbor, inside the four-hundred-yard line off the outer marks, in about seven fathoms, on a vessel lying in about seven fathoms with four persons in her.

"**The hour. The party was on the water from four minutes past two in the afternoon until about ten past four.

"**The keeper. Calder Marr was in the water. The tender was Nessa Pike, who was in the boat, and the cutoff was hers, and the word was *Enough*, and she did not use it and has entered why and the why is on the page.

"**The limits. Seventeen minutes a call, three calls, set by the diver at the boat and entered as the diver's and not raisable.

"**And the answer to the question itself. No finding of worked glass, whole or in part, was made on that day. Nothing was raised. Four persons were taken off a vessel. No return goes up on that day and no return has gone up for it and none is going to. The party told no office and will tell no office. That is entered in the party's own book with the date, and it is a fact about that book and it is in no office's book and it was not sent to anybody.

"**And I am not going to tell you which part of a clause caught that day, and I would like it minuted that I was not asked to and that I declined to be asked, because if I start arguing about limbs in a room I will be in that room in March.**"

"**Then produce the writ,**" said the man in the coat.

Calder Marr put it on the table face down, which is the way a thing is put down that is going to be picked up again, and then the book went back into its bag and the bag went under the arm of a woman of twenty-seven and the whole of it took about eleven minutes including the copy.

---

**And at about nine o'clock on the Saturday night the shed was shut and the lamp was lit and the far bench was behind her with nobody on it after four o'clock, and the shipwright read the whole of the day back from the top to the bottom and the holder read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed, and there has not been anybody else in that shed at nine o'clock since the fifteenth of May.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the ninth of September instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, in the holder's own left hand: that this party produced its crew's book and a copy of it, whole and unshortened, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin at about ten in the morning of this day, having said out loud, before the book was opened, why it was being opened, and that it produced it closed again in about ten minutes and read nothing out of it in that room; that a copy of the whole of it is in a file in a city in another man's hand, and that this is the first time in four months that any copy of this yard's own record has left this harbor, and that a man in a coat said out loud at the table that he is not going to open the copy in that room and is going to take it to a city and read it there.*

*And that this party has entered that it knows the reading of that book in a room is coming, and that it is not able to say what it will say when it does, and that it is not going to soften a line of that book and is not going to leave one out, and that the reason is on the page and is that a yard which has said in a market that it produces its book whole had better be the first to produce it, and the worst available place to produce a book is a room somebody else has arranged.*

*And that both columns stand and neither is scratched out, and a request to strike a line out of either one is refused in the same words; and that the head of the third column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words; and that the count of four stands in the first column in a ship's hand with no name against it and is the count of four and not a fifth thing; and that nothing was asked for in that room that is not on the face of the paper; and that no acknowledgement was entered in that room and the ninth stands as the last and there is not going to be a tenth.*

**And the second column was filled about half an hour later and the shipwright read it back and he read it back after her, and the line is about the not-reading, and it carries a reason against it and no name in it.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the ninth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that for about ten minutes this morning this party had its whole record on a table in a room in daylight in front of nine people and a man with a pen, and did not read one line of it; and that there was a line in that book which the holder would have read, and did not, and that this entry does not say which line and does not say that he wanted to; and that the reason it is entered against himself is that a line read out in a room is a line somebody else writes down, and that a man writes down the line and not the reason against it, because reasons are long; and that this party has entered the not-reading against itself and cannot tell yet, standing here, whether the not-reading this morning was a rule or a fear, and that it is going to have to be able to answer that in about three weeks, and that the answer to that question is not in any column of this book.*

**Nothing moved in or out of that bag on the Saturday. The bag is a mark and nineteenpence. The wage column stands at eighteen lines and seventy-two marks with the fifth of March the oldest, and three marks is owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August and has not moved since that day.**

**And on Sunday the tenth a form is going to be served on this party at the top of this mole's steps in daylight, and it is going to name a day, and this chapter does not have the day in it.**
