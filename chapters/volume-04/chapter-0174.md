# Chapter 174

*A Day That Was There to Be Booked and Was Not*

Wednesday the seventh and Thursday the eighth of October, in the shed, in the market office, and at the head of the dock.

**There was a day of work on Wednesday the seventh of October that was there to be booked and was not booked, and the reason it was not booked is a fact about this harbor and not about this yard, and it is on the page, and it is the third reason of that kind in five weeks and none of the three is a virtue.**

The second seam's own face, landward of the third seam's mark, about five hours, nobody in the water, the hand lead's line over the side and back four times, on a hirer's own account, at her own figure, twelve of seventeen at the mole. It is the standing arrangement and it has been since the third of May and it has not been argued once in eleven weeks.

She did not come at eleven. She came at about ten past one and she said it before she sat down, which is the way that lay says a thing.

"**I cannot this week. The third is short and it has been short since August and the tide gang is four short on top of us, and one herring berth out of two is what we have got, and I have a girl in a berth of mine and the other one is lying up with a started plank. I have not got the week. I have not got the day either. I have not got the morning.**

"**And I am telling you instead of not coming, because I have booked off that yard and I know what it is when a man has put a day in a book and then says in November that he did not think anybody wanted it, and I am not doing that to you.**"

"**Then it is not booked,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it is not going in the column, and I am not going to write *no work found* on the Wednesday of the seventh, because that is a lie and a flattering one and this column is not the place for either.**

"**Give me the Friday instead and I will take it at your figure and you will not be the one who is out.**

"**I can do the Friday. I will do the Friday and I would rather do it than not, and I am telling you that you are not rescuing me by booking it. The other three days are gone and the Friday is not gone and I would have been a fool to say no to it.**"

*First column, entered on Wednesday the seventh day of October instant at about half past four in the afternoon in this shed: that a day of work on the face of the second seam on Wednesday the seventh instant was not taken, and was not entered in this book as no work found, and that the reason it was not taken was given by the woman of the third lay who had offered to book it, in this yard, in about nine minutes, and that the reason is that the third lay of this harbor is short and has been short since August, that the tide gang is four men short, that one herring berth of two is out, and that her vessel had a started plank; and that this party did not enter the day as found and has entered why, and that a yard which has said out loud in a market that it produces its book whole is not going to begin producing it whole by entering a day that was not there; and that the said woman booked the following Friday instant at her own figure, on the same terms, twelve of seventeen at the mole, and said in this yard that she would have taken the Wednesday if she had had it and that the Friday is not a favor.*

---

**And on the Thursday the eighth, which is a rota day, the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers and this party did not walk it, and that is the third rota day of this month and the sixteenth since the third of August, and this party has walked none of the sixteen, and it has held since the nineteenth of June.**

And at about half past ten on the Thursday morning the holder went down the hill to the market office, which is open to anybody from nine and where the counting is going on behind the long table and the press is at the back of the floor under a stone slab, and where the press-keeper is a woman who has worked in that room for nineteen years and whose mother shifted that slab before her.

**Nobody asked her for anything.** That is the whole of it and it is entered here because this yard has a habit of asking.

"**Then you have come down this hill for nothing,**" said the press-keeper, without looking up from her own book.

"**I have come down this hill to buy a penny's worth of nothing and to be in a room where eleven people are not talking to me, and I would like to be able to say in a book that I came and did not ask, and that is the whole of my business and it took twenty minutes.**

"**And I am going to say three things in that office's trade and then I am going to go up the hill.

"**One. A sheet is a shilling for forty and a penny a sheet and I have not had the yard's money since the twenty-fifth of August and I am not going to have it again this month and I know that without being told, because the last time a man in a yard bought forty sheets off me he paid for them in coin out of his own hand at the counter and I wrote the number on the back of the copy.

"**Two. I have set about nine hundred of those sheets since the fifteenth of August and I have been paid for every one of them at a shilling, and every one of them has gone out of that door in a man's hand to a boat, and I have never once been asked by anybody what is on one, and I have not read one and I am not going to read one. That is not modesty. That is that I am paid by the sheet and the sheet is not mine and the two facts have been the arrangement since my mother shifted that slab.

"**And three. I have been asked three times this month by men with money to bring that slab forward and put the yard's sheet out on the boards under the naval paper that came down in August, and I have not, and I am not going to, and I have not told the yard that I have been asked and I am telling it now because the yard is in my room and it costs me nothing to say it out loud to a man who has not asked me for it.**"

"**Then here it is entered,**" said Calder Marr, "**in your own words and in mine, and with the date on it, and nobody has to be thanked and nobody is going to be.

"**And I am not going to improve on what you have just said, because you have said the whole of my position in nineteen years in about a minute and a half and I have been trying to say it in a market for five months.**"

"**I have not said your position,**" said the press-keeper. "**I have said my counter.**"

---

**And on the Thursday afternoon the two of them went at the plate note in the shed, and it is the first time since the twenty-ninth of March that anybody in that yard has gone at it, and they went at it for about forty minutes and it did not move.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the eighth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that this party went at the note against the plate at the fifth seam for about forty minutes on the Thursday instant, and that the note stands open, and that the plate is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone and has not been reached, and has not been measured, and has not been touched; and that the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January past stands refused in a record with a date on it and this party has not reopened it and has not written a letter to any office about the plate since the twenty-ninth of March past; and that this party entered on the first day of October instant that whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed, and that the entry stands.*

*And that this party has entered on the eighth day of October instant, against itself and with the hour against it, that it does not know whether the removal of a leaf from a file in a room on a second floor of this town, of which it knows the number and the date and the office and nothing whatever else, is the same thing as the change of politics in an office that it has been waiting for since the seventeenth of January past; and that this party has entered that it does not know, and has entered that it is not going to guess, and has entered that the reason it is not going to guess is that a party which has said in a market on the fifth of July that it does not put a name on a thing it cannot check is a party that has bound itself twice and cannot un-bind itself by finding out one thing; and that a number in a book in a shed is not a change of politics in an office and is not going to be made into one by anybody in that shed, and that this party entered the first of October that it had not written a better letter and it has not written one.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the eighth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the second day of October instant that this yard cannot check a number and cannot use a number and cannot ask for a file, and that on the eighth instant he has entered that the whole of what he would want an office to give him is a document about its own conduct, and that he has got the first half of that in a woman's book off the fish market and has not got the second half, and does not know how a man in a shed goes about the second half, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a boat can check whether he has been in a shed for forty minutes and cannot check whether forty minutes has moved a thing, and that a yard which finds out that a thing can be moved by a document is a yard which has stopped doing the work and started waiting for paper, and that this party has not done that yet and is not going to, and that if it does, the first column of this book is the place where anybody will be able to see the date it happened on.*

**No money came into this yard on the Wednesday and none on the Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and a mark and twelve shillings is in the bag, and the yard is two marks and twelve shillings short of a wage on Tuesday next and there is a day of work booked for Friday the ninth instant at a figure of about seventeen gross with twelve of it at the mole, and the eighth and the share come off the top the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May, and this party has not argued the eighth or the share on a mole's step in eleven weeks and is not going to start.**

**Alden Marr was at the far bench on Thursday the eighth from about ten until about half past three, and was not asked for anything on the Wednesday or the Thursday and asked nothing on either day, and Calder Marr did not go over to him on either day. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, three counts unmoved. Five marks of gland standing unpaid and useless. Nothing in a tin. Three marks to a man of Ashtide, unmoved since the eighth of August, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, unargued, and that man was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the line at her own asking and not on any wall. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she is not on this crew and has not been asked for anything by this party since the twenty-ninth of September and is not going to be. Twenty-one lines and eighty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, fifteen envelopes on a bench, and three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September and the sixth of October. The third column has not been filled since the first of August and the head of it keeps the boy's hand. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-one days up the crown, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in her hands. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The cistern is on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday and this party has walked none of its sixteen charges since the third of August, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. And a working harbor is poorer on the eighth day of October than it was on the first, and a yard of two and a half people is poorer with a mark and twelve shillings in a bag than it was with a mark and twelve shillings and fourpence, and both of those are this yard's own figures and neither of them has been argued with.**
