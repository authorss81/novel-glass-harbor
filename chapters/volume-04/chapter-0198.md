# Chapter 198

*A Penny a Sheet, and a Wage Not Paid*

Monday the thirtieth of November and Tuesday the first of December, at the fish market, at the mole's steps, and in the shed.

**On Monday the thirtieth of November the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was the thirtieth Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and there has not been one sitting in that room since the twenty-seventh.**

**And on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday, and this party has walked none of them, and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it, and this party is not going to stand in a street and tell anybody what that and this morning make between them, because it did not see the days those men come on and is not going to put a figure on a day it did not see.**

---

**And on the Monday morning, in the market, with the boards down and about forty people in it, a man of the third lay was telling two other men about a sheet of paper that had come up this hill in the ordinary post on the Sunday and had been carried down to the market and read out in a doorway on Monday morning at about eleven by a man who said he had read it in his own shed and was not going to say who had it.**

Pernel Saye heard the word *manifest* from about nine feet away and came over, and she is fifty-eight and keeps a copying counter in a room off the fish market that is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, where a sheet is a penny and forty sheets a shilling.

"**Say that again,**" she said, "**and say it slower, and if you say it wrong I will put you right and you will not like it.**"

The man said it again, and it was not wrong, and she looked at the sheet for about half a minute without touching it.

"**A sheet of that office is a public sheet,**" she said. "**That is the only kind of sheet worth the candle and I have been copying sheets for thirty-one years and I have copied one of that office's for nothing in all that time and I am not going to start. A public sheet is a sheet anybody may pay a penny for and anybody may read standing up, and that is the whole of what a copying counter is for and the rest of it is a trade.

"**I will copy it at a penny a sheet and I will put the copies on a board and I will read it out myself, because I have read a great many forms and I know which ones have a reason in the middle of them, and that one has, and it is in the fourth paragraph.

"**And I am not doing it for that yard and that yard is not to come up to my counter and say it is for them. If they come up I will tell them the same thing I am telling you, which is that a sheet is a penny.**"

She copied it on the Monday afternoon and the Tuesday morning, at about nine minutes a sheet, and had twenty-one copies on a board by about eleven on the Tuesday, and the yard did not go to the counter and did not send anybody and did not stand near her while she read it out.

---

**On Tuesday the first of December the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now SIX, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth day of August past, the week of Tuesday the fifth day of September past, the week of Tuesday the sixth day of October past, the week of Tuesday the third day of November past, the week of Tuesday the seventeenth day of November past, and the week of Tuesday the first day of December instant, and all six are entered in the same breath in this book and not one after the other.**

Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. Twenty envelopes on a bench in that chest. One mark and six shillings and five pence in the bag, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and the figure the bag was short of the wage is four hundred and three pence.

---

**And at about a quarter past eleven on the Tuesday morning, at the head of the fish dock, in the market, in about forty people's hearing, a woman of fifty-eight read out a form of an office, whole, in about nine minutes, with twenty-one copies on a board in front of her at a penny a sheet, and the two of them from this yard were about eleven feet off and did not read anything out and did not stand near her.**

She read the heading, and then she stopped for a moment before the first paragraph and said, "**This is not that yard's sheet and I want the forty of you to know whose it is, because there is a man in this market who is waiting to tell you that everything on a wall is theirs, and this one came out of a post on Sunday and I copied it because it is a public sheet and a public sheet is the only kind worth a penny.**"

Then she read it, and she read it well, and at the fourth paragraph she slowed down and said, "**That is the part they put in on purpose. Listen to it. That is an office saying out loud why it is doing a thing it did not want to do, and there is not one office on this coast that has ever done that in writing, and I have been copying forms for thirty-one years and I know the difference between a paragraph that is there to be read and a paragraph that is there to be got past.**"

At the end of it she squared the sheet on the board and said, "**That is a penny. Forty of them is a shilling. I am going to be here until the market is off the boards and I am not going to read it a second time, because a thing read twice in a market is a thing that has been made to sound better than it is, and this one does not need it.

"**And I am not going to be thanked for this. I am not going to be thanked by that yard and I would rather they did not come and stand by me while I do it, and I have not been to them and they have not been to me and neither of us has sent anybody.**"

A man of the first lay bought four copies at four pence and put them inside his coat. A woman of the second lay bought one. Two men of the third lay read the whole thing standing up and gave it back to her without buying a sheet, and she said that was the correct decision and that she would not argue with it.

"**There,**" said Nessa Pike, at about eleven forty-five, in the middle of the market, at the head of that dock, at the volume of about forty people. "**Eleven marks, eight shillings and five pence in a bag, six wages in a row with no envelope against them, twenty-one copies of a form about us on a board at a penny a sheet, and not one of those twenty-one is ours and not one of those pennies came out of this yard. That is what it costs. Not a mark of it goes on our page tonight except the four pence a man of the first lay just put in his coat, and that is not ours either.

"**And the words on that board are the office's words. Every one of them. Not one of them is ours and we could not have improved them by a word, and this yard is going to be asked in about four years whether it put that up and the answer is going to be no, and the sheet is going to be read anyway, because a form of an office is a thing that goes on a page and cannot be got off and there is nothing anybody can do about that at all.**"

---

*First column, entered on Tuesday the first day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on the said day was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the bag held one mark and six shillings and five pence in it on the morning of that day, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence, and that a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and that the figure the bag was short of the wage is four hundred and three pence; and that the column of this book stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the fifth of March the oldest; and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is twenty; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is six, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past, the seventeenth of November past and the first of December instant, and that the six figures are six different figures and none of them is either of the others; and that the reason against the line is that this yard holds two hundred and thirty-seven pence and that it entered on the twenty-ninth instant that it was not going to pay a penny a sheet on about four hundred people and was not going to ask a copyist to make one for nothing, and that a copy was made and read out in a market in this harbor on the said Tuesday at about a penny a sheet and that not one of those pence came out of this yard and that the four pence a man of the first lay paid into a copyist's hand is not this yard's money and has been entered here because a man can stand in that market on any day of a week and ask a woman of fifty-eight whether she was paid by this yard and the answer is no.*

*And that a copyist of this harbor, being Pernel Saye, fifty-eight, who keeps a counter in a room off the fish market that is open to anybody from eight until the market is off the boards, where a sheet is a penny and forty sheets a shilling, said in the market on Monday the thirtieth instant at about eleven in the morning, of her own account and unasked, that a sheet of the circuit office of this place is a public sheet and is the only kind of sheet worth the candle; and that she said that in thirty-one years of copying she has never copied one of that office's for nothing and is not going to start; and that she said in the market that she would copy it at a penny a sheet and put the copies on a board and read it out herself, and gave the reason, which is that she has read a great many forms and knows which of them have a reason in the middle of them, and that the said form has one and that it is in the fourth paragraph; and that she said that she is not doing it for this party and that this party is not to come to her counter and say that it is for them, and that if they come she will tell them the same thing she told the market, which is that a sheet is a penny; and that this party did not go to that counter, did not send anybody to it, and did not stand near the said woman while she read the said form out, and has entered that it did not and that neither of them sent anybody.*

*And that the said woman copied the said form on the Monday afternoon and the Tuesday morning at about nine minutes a sheet and had twenty-one copies on a board by about eleven o'clock on the Tuesday, and that at about a quarter past eleven on the said Tuesday, at the head of the fish dock, in the market, in about forty people's hearing, the said woman read out the whole of the said form in about nine minutes with twenty-one copies on a board in front of her at a penny a sheet; and that she said first that the said form is not this party's sheet and that there is a man in that market waiting to tell anybody who will listen that everything on a wall is theirs, and that the said form came out of a post on the Sunday and that she copied it because a public sheet is the only kind worth a penny; and that she said that the fourth paragraph of the said form is the part that was put in on purpose and is an office saying out loud why it is doing a thing it did not want to do, and that in thirty-one years of copying she knows the difference between a paragraph that is there to be read and a paragraph that is there to be got past; and that she said at the end that the sheet is a penny and forty of them is a shilling and that she was going to be at that counter until the market is off the boards and was not going to read it a second time, and gave the reason, which is that a thing read twice in a market is a thing that has been made to sound better than it is; and that she said that she is not going to be thanked by this party and would rather this party did not come and stand by her while she does it; and that a man of the first lay bought four copies at four pence, and that a woman of the second lay bought one, and that two men of the third lay read the whole of it standing up and gave it back without buying a sheet, and that the said woman said that was the correct decision and that she would not argue with it; and that neither of the two of this party read anything out on the said day and that this was not a reading of this yard's own columns and is entered here so that nobody in four years can call it one.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the thirtieth instant, being the thirtieth Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it, and that there has not been a sitting in that room since the twenty-seventh instant; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Monday and this party walked it, and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it, and this party is not going to stand in a street and say what the two of them make between them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the first day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the whole of what a form of an office costs in a market in this harbor is a penny a sheet and about nine minutes of a copyist's morning and four pence out of the pocket of a man of the first lay, and that the cost of it is not in this yard's book at all and cannot be, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what an office is going to say about itself in four years is not checkable from a mole and that this yard has entered in its first column that the words on that sheet are the office's words and not this yard's and that this yard could not have improved them by a word and did not try; and that this yard is going to be asked in about four years whether it put that sheet up and the answer is going to be no, and that the sheet is going to be read anyway, because a form of an office goes on a page and cannot be got off and there is nothing anybody can do about that at all.*

*And that the said woman said in the market on the said Tuesday that eleven marks, eight shillings and five pence in a bag and six wages in a row with no envelope against them and twenty-one copies of a form about this yard on a board at a penny a sheet, none of which is this yard's and none of whose pennies came out of this yard, is what it costs; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether this yard is better or worse off in four years than a man in this harbor can see from a mole is not a thing a man on a mole can check, and that a working harbor of this coast is poorer on the first day of December than it was on the twentieth of October and nothing this yard has published in two volumes has made it better.*

**No money came into this yard on the Monday and none on the Tuesday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and one mark and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month, and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid and not one shilling of it offered. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, six weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November, the seventeenth of November and the first of December instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and seventy days up the crown of this mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved in a hundred and seventy days. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in the hand of the woman making it, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted.**
