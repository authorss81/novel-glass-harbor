# Chapter 188

*A Name for a Reason, and Five Marks at the Second Seam*

Thursday the fifth and Friday the sixth of November, at the wall outside the market office, on the mole's steps, and on the face of the second seam.

**And at about ten on the Thursday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party did not walk it, and that is the twenty-fourth charge since the third of August, and this party has walked none of the twenty-four, and it has held since the nineteenth of June.**

---

**And at about eight in the morning on that Thursday, before the market was off the boards, a man was standing in front of a sheet of paper on the wall outside the market office of this town, reading the reason on the page out loud to himself, and this party came up the hill at eight and went to the wall and stood about four feet off, and the man knew whose sheet it was before anybody said anything.**

It is a sheet of about the size of a good tablecloth, and it carries a finding of a tide conservator about a thing she examined, and there is no name at the foot of it and there is no seal on it, and there is a reason on the page in her own hand about four lines long, and the reason is that a finding of no office carries no seal and cannot be produced on demand by anybody, and that a signature would make it a document, and that she would rather it could not be produced than be produced.

The man was of the first lay and about fifty-five and he had a coasting boat of his own and a letter from a city in his coat.

"**I have been at four doors in three days,**" he said. "**And this one is the fifth and it is a wall, and I am not saying that anybody has lied to me, because nobody has. I am saying that a thing on a wall with no name on it is a thing I cannot be made to answer for, and a thing on a page in an office with a name on it is a thing I can be made to answer for, and I have got a letter in my coat that says my paper is going to be looked at on the strength of a finding, and the finding is that, and there is no name on it.

"**And I am not asking you what is on the Registry's page and I am not asking you to write to a city. I am asking one question and it is the only one I have got, and I am asking it at eight in the morning outside a market because it is the only place I am going to be able to ask it in front of people.**"

"**Then ask it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Who is she.**"

"**No,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to give you the whole of it and it is going to be about four minutes, and you are not going to like it and I am not going to improve on it while you are standing there.

"**She is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is not a holder and is not on the four names of the writ of the first of May, and the reporting clause on that writ does not reach her and never did. She left this crew in her own words on the twenty-third of July and that is a different thing from anything that has happened to her since and I am not going to put the two together in a street.

"**She is owed forty marks first after food by the work of this harbor and she has been owed it since the fifteenth of October and not one mark of it has been paid and the six marks and two days that is her price on every coast have not been offered and have not been reduced and are not going to be. I have not got it and I am not going to offer it in a street in November because it is the same price and it has not moved and I would be a man trying the same door twice.

"**And the sheet on that wall is unsigned because she asked for it to be unsigned, and the reason is on the page in her own hand and you have just read it out loud, and I am not going to have it signed and I am not going to have it taken down.

"**And this party was refused by her in a room open to anybody in this town on the twenty-ninth of September and has asked her nothing since, and has entered why, and the why is that a party which has been refused once and then tells a man in a street where the woman is, is a party which has decided that a person is a door, and she told that room she was not a door.**"

The man stood there for a while.

"**That is the same answer you gave me in July,**" he said, "**and I want to say something about it and I am not being clever.

"**In July it sounded like a yard being careful. This morning it sounds like a wall. And I am not saying you are lying and I am not saying she is not a woman of her trade, because she plainly is, or that the finding is not worth the four lines of reason under it, because it plainly is.

"**I am saying that I have got a letter in my coat that a man in a city is going to act on, and the thing he is going to act on is that sheet, and there is nothing on that sheet anybody can be made to answer for, and I know that, and you know that, and about nine people standing at a wall know that.

"**And I know the other half of it as well, because about four hundred people can read a page in a city and I cannot, and neither can you, and that is the half of it that is on the record and the half of it that is on the wall is the half I can stand in front of.

"**So I have got a page I cannot reach and a wall I cannot argue with, and I would rather have the page. And I would rather have it from you than from anybody else, and I am not going to pretend to you that I would rather have a yard than a wall, because I have been in four rooms in this harbor this month and I have found out what that is worth.**"

"**Then you have got it the right way round,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I have not got a better version of it than you have, and I am not going to stand at this wall and improve on what you have just said, because you said it better than I would have and I have been wrong in a market in this harbor before.

"**And here is the one thing I am going to add and it is against this yard and not for it. A sheet with no name on it is a sheet a man cannot be made to answer for. A page in a city is a sheet a man can be made to answer for. And this yard has spent four volumes saying that the second one is the only kind of record anybody can check, and it went into a room on the twenty-ninth of October and came out with that, and I am the man who put it there.

"**And I would rather have the wall than the page, and I have not got a reason for that which is worth anything, and you have asked me one question this morning and I have answered it and it is no.**"

Nobody at that wall said anything, and the market came in behind them, and the two of them went back up the hill.

**And on Friday the sixth of November this yard worked about four hours and thirty minutes on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line over the side and back four times.**

**Gross on the board seventeen.** The eighth, two. The share, two. The victuals of the day, two. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Twelve out of seventeen paid at the mole and the yard's being five, and the five went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people and no figure was argued, and the eighth and the share came off the top of the day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May.

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Friday from about ten until about three, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at it on the Thursday at any hour.

They swapped jobs for the day, and the swap is entered. The pump was on the boards and the holder of the writ ran it, which is a thing he has not done since the second of May and which he did badly for the first hour and then did well enough that a man of the third lay said nothing about it. The ship's hand kept the book and the count and the times and hauled the line over with her right hand and wrote the four turns on the slate in her own writing, and the slate went into the book on the board at the end of the day and the holder read it back after her and neither of them said anything about it.

The hirer knew about the arm before they came and did not ask about it, and paid the day's figure at the figure, and said one thing at about half past twelve which was that the day went slower than a day with three on it, and Calder Marr said that it did, and the man said *I know it does, I have had a yard of two before*, and neither of them said anything else about it.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that on Thursday the fifth day of November instant, at about eight in the morning and before the market was off the boards, a man of the first lay, about fifty-five years old and with a coasting boat of his own and a letter from a city in his coat, asked this party at the wall outside the market office of this town for the name of the person whose finding of the eleventh day of November past is on that wall unsigned, and gave his reason out loud, which is that a thing on a wall with no name on it is a thing he cannot be made to answer for and a thing on a page in an office is, and that he had a letter in his coat that a man in a city was going to act on the strength of that sheet; and that this party told him in about four minutes that the said person is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is not a holder and is not on the four names of the writ of the first of May instant and that the reporting clause on that writ does not reach her, and that she left this crew in her own words on the twenty-third day of July past and that the said party did not put that together with anything that has happened to her since; and that she is owed forty marks first after food and has been since the fifteenth of October instant and that not one mark of it has been paid and that the six marks and two days have not been offered and have not been reduced; and that the finding on that wall is unsigned at her own asking and the reason is on the page in her own hand and this party is not going to have it signed and is not going to have it taken down.

*And that the said man said out loud at that wall that in July the same answer had sounded like a yard being careful and that on the fifth of November instant it sounds like a wall, and that he knows that a page in a city is a sheet he can be made to answer for and that a wall is not, and that he would rather have the page, and that he would rather have it from this party than from anybody else, and that he is not going to pretend he would rather have a yard than a wall because he has been in four rooms in this harbor this month; and that this party said that he had got it the right way round and that this party has not got a better version of it, and that a sheet with no name on it is a sheet a man cannot be made to answer for and a page in a city is a sheet a man can be made to answer for, and that this party has spent four volumes saying that the second one is the only kind of record anybody can check; and that nobody at that wall said anything and no name was given and no sheet was taken down and no sheet was put up.

*And that on Friday the sixth instant this yard worked about four hours and thirty minutes on the face of the second seam landward of the third seam's mark on a hirer's own account with nobody in the water and a hand lead's line over the side and back four times, and that the gross on the board was seventeen and that the eighth, the share and the victuals of the day and two hired hands came off the top of it and that twelve of the seventeen was paid at the mole and the yard's was five; and that the said party swapped jobs for the day, the holder of the writ running the pump and the ship's hand keeping the book and the count and the times and hauling the line over with her right hand, and that the swap is entered and that the said holder had not run that pump since the second of May instant and did it badly for the first hour; and that the hirer knew about the arm and did not ask about it and paid the day's figure at the figure and said that the day went slower than a day with three on it, and that the said holder said that it did, and that the said man said he knew it did and had had a yard of two before; and that Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Friday instant from about ten until about three and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and that Calder Marr did not go over to him, and that he was not at that bench on the Thursday instant at any hour.

*And that this party entered on the said Friday that it now holds two documents and not one, being a paper served on it in a doorway on the twenty-third of October instant and a number written on a copying job sheet on the first of October instant, and that a form is a form whichever office writes it, and that the whole of what this party knows about the second day of September past is the shape of a form, and that no person in this harbor has ever seen that form and that this party is not going to ask for it and is not going to ask for the said paper's second copy either, and that this is the same entry as the one of the twelfth of October instant and is not being entered twice and is not being improved on.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Friday the sixth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that on the fifth of November instant a man said out loud at a wall in this town that he would rather have a page in a city than a wall, and that the holder agreed with him in about nine people's hearing and entered against this party that he has not got a reason for that preference which is worth anything, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a man in a city does with a page in four years is not checkable at a wall this morning, and that the said man is not a party to anything and is owed nothing by this party and is not owed anything by that woman and has been given nothing and has paid nothing.*

*And that the said woman has not been in this harbor since the twelfth day of October instant and is not coming to the market office of this town on a Tuesday or a Friday morning any more at her own asking with her own reason, and that she did not come up this hill on the fifth or the sixth and was not asked to and that this party has not sent for her and is not going to, and that the forty marks stand first after food and are not reduced and are not being paid out of a day of work at a seam that brought five marks on the sixth of November instant, and that those are two different figures and neither of them is the other.*

**Five marks came into this yard on Friday the sixth of November and nothing went out of it on either day, and five marks and eight shillings and six pence is in the bag, and that is nine hundred and two pence, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the tenth instant. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and a day of work at a seam on the sixth of November instant is not a payment of it and is not going to be entered as one. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, four weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October and the third of November. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the fifth instant and this party walked none of the twenty-four charges there have been since the third of August. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days, and the two objects of nobody's are on the crown of that mole under two tarpaulins about nine feet apart and neither of them has moved. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. Nineteen winters are in a list in a city and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what the woman at that table writes with this yard's four returns. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next with four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask twice to have it lifted and did not ask at all this month.
