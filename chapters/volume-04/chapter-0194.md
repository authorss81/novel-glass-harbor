# Chapter 194

*A List of What Is Not Going Into the Room, and a Form With an Office's Name on It*

Sunday the twenty-second and Monday the twenty-third of November, in the shed, and on the mole's steps.

**There was no day of work booked on the Sunday and the board was not written, and this party entered that, with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened.**

---

**And at about ten on the Sunday morning, in that shed, with the lamp not lit because it was not dark yet, the ship's hand wrote a list on a sheet of this yard's own paper and read it out, and the holder wrote it into the first column in his own left hand with the date on it, and neither of them added to it and neither of them took anything off it.**

"**Things that are not going into that room,**" said Nessa Pike. "**That is the whole sheet and it is eleven lines and it is the only piece of work this yard has done this month that I wanted to do.

"**One. The place of the case of five bells. Two. The four people it belongs to. Three. The limit they wrote themselves. Four. The plate at the fifth seam. Five. The two objects on the crown of this mole. Six. The boy. Seven. The forty marks standing to him. Eight. The forty marks to a person of no office. Nine. The delay. Ten. The letter that is coming back up this hill on Saturday. Eleven. The two figures for the bar of gravel and the Kettle, which was not looked at.**

"**And I am going to say why, once, and then I am not going to say it again in this yard, and you may not improve on it either.

"**It is that a reason does not travel. That is the whole of the reason and it is a thing this yard worked out on the thirtieth of October and it is not going to work out again in a fortnight in a better version. Every one of those eleven lines has a reason against it and the reason is the only part of it that would keep four people safe, and the reason does not go into a record, and so if we put the line in there without the reason then in about four years a man in a city is going to read it as a man who had somebody to be careful about.

"**So they stay here. And the reason that this yard is not sorry about the whole of it is that we are not going to be able to do anything about nine of the eleven in a room on Thursday, and we would rather have said so on the Sunday than be asked on the Friday.**"

"**Stop at nine,**" said Calder Marr.

She stopped.

"**Read the tenth again,**" he said, "**and then tell me what happens to it on Wednesday.**"

"**It comes in the ordinary post on Saturday and it is one sheet in her own hand and it is dated and it is the answer to a question this yard asked her on Monday, and I am not going to say what the question is in a market in four months, and I am not going to have it said for me either.**

"**And I will tell you the other half of why it is on the sheet, and then you can decide whether to argue. That letter is the only piece of paper this yard has ever held that is about a person of no office, and if it goes into a room it is a document about her, and she has been in a record in a city since the sixteenth of October without anybody asking her, and this yard is not going to be the second.**"

"**It is not on the sheet,**" said Calder Marr, "**and you are not going to put it on the sheet, and that is the second time this month one of us has been wrong about a list before it was written and that is a thing I want noted in a room we are not in.**"

---

**On Monday the twenty-third of November the wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight and it came down bare, and that was twenty-nine Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and this party has walked none of them, and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it and this party is not going to say in a street what the two of those make between them, because it did not see the days those men come on.**

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon of that Monday, a clerk of the circuit office of this place came up this hill on foot, as he has done four times since September, and he gave his name and the name of his office before he said anything else, and he had a form folded in the inside of his coat and not in his hand, which is a thing a clerk does when a thing is not to be seen on a hill.**

Ambrose Sell, clerk of the circuit office.

"**I have come with one sheet and it is going to take about six minutes and then I am going back down, and I am going to tell you the three things on it that are not in my trade's habit of saying things out loud, and the first of them is that I have not come to say a day.

"**One. Your sheet of the sixteenth was received on the seventeenth at about ten in the morning and is entered, and the entry is a line and a date and an hour, because that is what an entry is, and it grants nothing and makes nothing and releases nothing.

"**Two. The magistrate of this circuit has directed that a provisional public copy record of the record you name be made. And I am going to say what that means in my own trade's words and not in a lawyer's, and it means that a copy is made at the counter of my office and is taken away by the applicant, and that when it is made it will carry on its face the limits that the record itself carries, printed there in the words of the record and not in the words of a clerk, because a clerk does not get to add a limit to a record and a clerk who does is a clerk who has made a document.

"**And three, and this is the one I came to say, and I would rather you had it on a Monday than in a market in March.**" He put the sheet on the bench. "**No date attaches. Not to the making of it and not to my office's answering of it. I am not able to tell you when it will be ready. I am not withholding a day, there is no day in my hand, and if there were a day in my hand I would not be allowed to give it to you, and if I were a different sort of clerk I might tell you one anyway and you would book a Thursday round it, and that is the single most expensive thing that can be done to an office that has said yes to something.

"**So there is no day. And I am telling you that in a doorway because a party that has asked for a thing it cannot hurry has handed my office an open-ended job, and in about four months one of you is going to decide I forgot it, and I have not, and I would rather be told now than then.**"

"**We have not forgotten it,**" said Calder Marr, "**and we have not booked anything round it. We have a room on Thursday and we put the day on the nineteenth and we did not ask for a room round a copy. The room is a room and the copy is a copy and they are two things this yard has not joined and is not going to join.**"

"**Then say the rest of it,**" said Ambrose Sell, "**and then I will read back what you have written, because that is what I do and I am not going to do it any differently today.**

"**And the last thing on my sheet is at the foot, and it is where a signature would be if this were the sort of document that had one, and it is not a man's name. It is the name of an office and the office wrote it, and the reason it is not my name is that I am not the man who directs it. If I put my name at the foot of it then in about four years a man in a city will be able to say that a clerk of this circuit decided that a copy of a record should be made, and that is not what happened and I am not going to be the man it happens to.**"

He read back the eleven lines of the list, slowly, twice, and said the second time that he had not asked about any of the eleven and was not going to, and that a clerk reads a page and a clerk does not read the eleven lines of a yard's book and go and look for the eleventh of them.

He went down the hill. Nobody followed him.

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-third day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on Sunday the twenty-second instant at about ten in the morning, in this shed, the ship's hand wrote on a sheet of this yard's own paper a list of eleven things that are not going into the room of the hearing room over the tide-gang store on Thursday the twenty-sixth instant, the said eleven being the place of a case of five bells; the four people it belongs to; the limit they wrote themselves; the plate at the fifth seam; the two objects on the crown of this mole; the boy; the forty marks standing whole to him; the forty marks first after food to a person of no office; the delay; a letter that this yard is expecting in the ordinary post on Saturday the twenty-seventh instant; and the two figures for the bar of gravel together with the Kettle, which was not looked at; and that the said list was read out in this shed and the holder wrote it into this column in his own left hand with the date on it, and that neither of them added to it and neither of them took anything off it; and that a clerk of the circuit office of this place read the said list back twice in this shed on the afternoon of the said Monday and said that he had not asked about any of the eleven and was not going to, and that he gave the reason, which is that a clerk reads a page and does not go and look for the eleventh line of a yard's book.*

*And that on the said Monday at about half past two in the afternoon, in the open air, a clerk of the circuit office of this place, who gave his name as Ambrose Sell and gave the name of his office before he said anything else, came up this hill with a form folded inside his coat and not in his hand; and that the said form states that the application of this party of the sixteenth instant was received on the seventeenth instant at about ten in the morning and is entered, and that an entry is a line and a date and an hour and grants nothing and makes nothing and releases nothing; and that the said form states that the magistrate of that circuit has directed that a provisional public copy record of the record named by this party be made, and that a copy is made at the counter of that office and taken away by the applicant, and that when it is made it will carry on its face the limits which the record itself carries, printed in the words of the record and not in the words of a clerk; and that the said form states that no date attaches to the making of the said copy and to the answering of the said application, and that the clerk of that office is not able to give a date and is not withholding one; and that at the foot of the said form, where a signature would be if the form were the sort of document that had one, there is the name of an office and no man's name, and that the clerk gave the reason, which is that a clerk's name at the foot of it would in about four years allow a man in a city to say that a clerk of that circuit decided that a copy of a record should be made, and that that is not what happened; and that this party entered that it had booked a room and a day on the nineteenth instant and had not booked either of them round a copy, and that a room is a room and a copy is a copy and the two have not been joined.*

*And that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the twenty-third instant, being twenty-nine Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Monday and this party walked it, and the walk is nil and stays nil, and the charge of the ninth day of November past stands entered with no figure against it, and this party is not going to stand in a street and say what the two of them make between them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the twenty-third day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the ship's hand said in this shed on the Sunday instant that every one of the said eleven lines has a reason against it and that the reason is the only part of it that would keep four people safe, and that the reason does not go into a record, and that if a line goes into a record without its reason then in about four years a man in a city reads it as a man who had somebody to be careful about; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that what a man in a city is going to make of a bare line in four years is not checkable from a mole, and that the eleven lines are on the page in the first column and their reasons are in here and the pairing is the only thing this yard has that nobody else can read, and that a pairing is not a thing an office can be given.*

*And that the said woman said in this shed that the letter which is expected in the ordinary post on the said Saturday is the only piece of paper this yard has ever held that is about a person of no office, and that if it goes into a room then it is a document about her, and that she has been in a record in a city since the sixteenth day of October past without anybody asking her, and that this party is not going to be the second; and that the holder said that the letter is not on the list and that she is not going to put it on the list, and that that is the second time in three days that one of them has been wrong about a list before it was written, and that the said woman said it was the third and that she was counting differently because she counted the thirtieth of October, and that neither of them improved on that and the count is in the first column above and not here.*

**No money came into this yard on the Sunday and none on the Monday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and five marks and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the twenty-fourth instant. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and has not been sent for. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright, and it is not paid and is not being paid out of anything that has come in this month. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor, with her name on the figure in the first column at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid. Twenty-five lines and one hundred marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, nineteen envelopes on a bench, five weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November and the seventeenth of November instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and sixty-three days up the crown of this mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence and a form came up this hill on the said Monday that does not touch it, grants it, refuses it or deals with it. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted.**
