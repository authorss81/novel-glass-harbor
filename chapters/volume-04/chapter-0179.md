# Chapter 179

*A Tin Cup on the End of a Bench, and a Column Nobody Fills*

Saturday the seventeenth and Sunday the eighteenth of October, in the shed, on the mole's crown above the third seam's mark, and at the head of the dock.

**Nothing happened on Saturday the seventeenth of October, and this party entered that, and the entry is in the first column with the date and the hour on it, and the reason against the line is that a day on which nothing happens still happened and the only way it gets into a book that can be read in a room in a fortnight is by somebody writing it down on the day.**

There was no day of work booked. The pump was on the bench and came off it and went back on it. A man of the first lay of about ninety tons came up the hill at about eleven and asked whether the yard would look at a thing on his stem and was told that a man of this yard does not turn a thing over for nothing and that the yard is not taking it on in October, and the man said he had not expected it in October and would come again in the spring. The tide made about a foot and a half and went out again and the two of them did not go out on it.

And a man of the third lay who had been in the yard on Thursday said one thing at the head of the dock, on his way past, and it was that the cistern behind the engine house had been charged on the Thursday morning and not on the Monday, and this party has entered that it did not know that and has entered that it does not know it now, and that the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and has been since June and that this party has walked none of sixteen charges since the third of August and has not been on the ground on two of the days of any of them.

---

**And at about half past four in the afternoon on the Saturday, in the shed, with the lamp not lit, Nessa Pike put the pencil down on the book and said the thing she had been carrying since Monday the twelfth.**

"**I could fill the third column today. I have got nine sentences. I have had them since Monday and I have thought about them every evening and I have not written one of them down anywhere, and I am going to tell you what they are so that you know what I am not writing, and then I am not going to write them.

"**They are what a woman of thirty said on this bench on Monday the twelfth at about four in the afternoon, in about nine minutes, in about six people's hearing, in a doorway. Nine sentences. I have got them in my head the way I have got a plate in my head, and I could put them in that column in about two minutes, and nobody would ever have known, and there is nobody in this harbor who could check them and nobody in a city who could check them and you could not check them.

"**And I am not going to do it, and the reason is not that I have not asked you. The reason is that the third column is not mine and it is not a column of facts.

"**It is a page where a man wrote down what he said when he was not in the boat. That is the whole of its value. On the second of May a boy of twenty-two read a position back off a slate and a man wrote down what he had said on the steps about a minute after he had said it, and that is what the column is for, and the only reason it has ever been worth anything is that nobody filled it but him.

"**And on Monday a woman came up this hill and told us four things about a card and a file and a plate, and she told us out loud in a doorway, in daylight, in front of six people, and she is not on this crew and she is not of this yard and she is owed forty marks first after food, and she was not asked and she was not sent.

"**If those nine sentences go in that column, then this yard is holding a page about what a woman said to it in a doorway, and that is not a column of what a man said when he was not in the boat. That is a thing this yard wrote down because a person said something to it and the yard thought it was worth keeping, and that is a different instrument, and it is the instrument a party uses when nobody can check it.

"**And in about four years there is going to be a page in a city that says what a yard of Morrow Quay knew in October, and it is going to say that the yard kept a record of its own conversations with a woman who would not sign anything. And anybody can read it. And I have seen that page in about four different shapes in my head this month and I do not want it to be this one.**"

"**Then do not fill it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I am not going to fill it,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I have said that out loud and I want it on the page in my own hand and not in yours, because in a year one of us is going to be asked which of us it was.**

"**And there is a second thing and I have been carrying it since the fourth of October and so have you.

"**You are going to tell me or you are not. I am not going to ask you. I am not going to ask you tonight and I am not going to ask you in a market and I am not going to ask you in a room in a city in four years, and I want it on your page tonight in your own hand that you had a week to ask me and that you did not, and I want it there with a reason against it, and the reason is that if I had to ask, then a page in a city is a thing that got made because I asked for it, and I am not going to be the woman in a record who asked a man of a yard for a sentence.

"**Write it down the other way. Write down that you did not tell me and that I did not ask, and put both of those on the same line, and put a reason against the line that is about you and not about me. And in about four years somebody can read that line and work out that a man of a yard had a thing in his own book for six weeks and a woman knew there was a thing in it and neither of them ever said so out loud, and that is a true thing about two people and it is not a thing anybody can be produced for.**"

He wrote it down. It took about nine seconds and the read-back took longer because she read it twice.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that the third column of this book was not filled on Saturday the seventeenth or on Sunday the eighteenth day of October instant, and has not been filled since the first day of August, and that this party has not appointed anybody and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Saturday afternoon, and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column; and that the ship's hand of this yard entered on the seventeenth instant that she has nine sentences in her head and did not write them, and that the said nine sentences are not written in this book or in any book of this party, and that this party cannot check them and has not asked to see them and has entered that it will not.*

*And that on the seventeenth instant a man of the first lay of about ninety tons asked whether this party would look at a thing on his stem, and was told that a man of this yard does not turn a thing over for nothing and that the yard is not taking it on in October, and that the said man said he had not expected it in October and would come again in the spring, and that this party took nothing and asked him nothing further and did not thank him; and that a man of the third lay said at the head of the dock on the said day that the cistern behind the engine house had been charged on the Thursday morning and not on the Monday, and that this party entered that it did not know that and does not know it now, and that the said rota is of a Monday and a Thursday and has been since June, and that this party walked none of the sixteen charges since the third of August and was not on the ground on two of the days of any of them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the seventeenth day of October instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the fourth day of October instant that he had entered a decision to tell that woman a thing and had not told her, and that on the seventeenth instant he entered that the said woman said in this shed that she was not going to ask him, and that he has entered both halves of that on one line, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a shed can be checked for what he wrote and cannot be checked for what he was going to say in October, and that the two of those facts are now on a page together and neither of them is going to be moved.*

*And that the holder entered on the seventeenth instant that there are nine sentences in that shed tonight that are not in this book, and that he has not asked for them and has entered that he is not going to, and that the reason is the reason the ship's hand gave out loud in this shed and it is in the first column, and that the holder agrees with it in every particular and has added nothing to it, and that in about four years there is going to be a page in a city that says what this yard knew in October and this party has entered that it is not going to be that page, and that this is a decision and is not a virtue, and that the holder entered on the seventeenth instant that it is not going to be a virtue and is not going to be thanked for it and is not asking.*

---

**And on Sunday the eighteenth of October, at about eleven in the morning, Calder Marr put a tin cup of water on the end of the far bench and went back to his own bench and sat down and did not say anything.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about three with his back to the room and the same net in his hands. He did not turn round. He did not drink it and he did not refuse it and he did not put his hand out and he did not say one word, and the cup was still on the end of that bench on Monday morning at about nine, and the two of them did not speak about it on either day and neither brother has said why and neither brother is going to.

"**That is a thing I have done once,**" said Calder Marr, at about four in the afternoon, "**and I am going to say out loud in this yard that it is a thing I have done once, and that I am not going to do it again, and that the reason is that a man of sixty-one who has not said a word since the thirteenth of November last year is not a man who has been asked a question, and a cup of water on the end of a bench is a question in a language I made up, and I do not get to make up a language for him.

"**And I did not go over to him and I am not going to, and neither of us has said why and neither of us is going to, and that entry is in this book from the eighteenth of September and it stands.**"

*First column, entered on Sunday the eighteenth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Sunday from about ten until about three, and on the Saturday he was not at it at any hour, and that he was not asked for anything on either day and asked nothing on either day, and that Calder Marr did not go over to him on either day; and that on the Sunday instant at about eleven in the morning the holder put a tin cup of water on the end of the said bench and went back to his own bench and said nothing, and that the said man did not turn round and did not drink it and did not refuse it and did not say one word, and that the cup was still on the end of that bench on the Monday morning at about nine; and that this party entered that it has done that once and is not going to do it again, and has entered why, which is that a cup of water on the end of a bench is a question in a language this party made up, and that this party does not get to make up a language for a man who has not been asked anything; and that neither brother has said why neither brother does not go over to the other and neither brother is going to.*

*And that the two objects of nobody's were on the crown of that mole about a hundred and forty-eight days on the Sunday, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and that neither of them moved on the Saturday or the Sunday, and that this party went past both of them on the Sunday morning at about ten and touched neither and counted them once.*

*And that the plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in this mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and that the note against it stands open, and that the said woman said in this yard on Monday the twelfth instant that the first of her four grounds of the eleventh of November past is about that plate and about a closure, and that this party did not ask her one word about it and has entered that it will not.*

**No money came into this yard on the Saturday and none on the Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and five marks and twelve shillings is in the bag, and a wage of four marks goes out of it on Tuesday the twentieth and there is nothing in the week between this one and that. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, three counts unmoved since the fourth of January at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid, and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, and that is not the four pence of the second of October. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking and not on any wall. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and nobody has written to him and nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she is not coming to the market office of this town on a Tuesday or a Friday morning any more, and this party did not ask her for anything in October and is not going to. Twenty-two lines and eighty-four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, sixteen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hose of the fire party of this harbor is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the first, the fifth, the eighth, the twelfth and the fifteenth of October and this party walked none of the five, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by an officer this yard emptied, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years, and the standing sentence said in a street on the third of May is unreversed and nothing in this month has touched it. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and no occasion was entered this month and no measurement was taken, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. And a working harbor is poorer on the eighteenth of October than it was on the first of it, and neither of those is a thing this yard has done or can mend.**
