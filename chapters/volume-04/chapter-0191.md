# Chapter 191

*One Sheet in the Holder's Own Hand, and an Envelope Not Made*

Monday the sixteenth and Tuesday the seventeenth of November, at the mole's steps, and in the shed.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was twenty-eight Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and there is a day entered on that clerk's own page for Thursday of this week with a room against it, and nothing has gone on the wall about it, which is a clerk's practice and has been one since April.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and the rota is a Monday and a Thursday and has been since June, and this party has walked none of them, and the walk is nil and stays nil.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench on the Monday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and it is one line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week.

---

**And at about half past eight on that Monday night, in that shed, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, they wrote out a thing that had been standing on the table for five weeks, and it took about forty minutes, and it is one sheet, and it is in the holder's own left hand, and the date is on it, and neither of them improved on a word of it after it was written.**

"**Say the head of it again,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and say it in the shape of a question and not in the shape of a favor.**"

"**To the circuit office of this place. From the party holding the writ of salvage working granted at Morrow Quay on the first of May instant. The party applies in writing for a copy of one record of that office, that record being the record of the entry of one item of recovered transparent mineral in the custody room of that office, and the party asks for a copy of the record and not for the object, and it does not ask for the object, and it has not asked for the object at any time in four volumes.**

"**And the party says on the face of this sheet, because it wants it in its own words and not in anybody else's, that a copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check. That sentence was this party's own in July and it is still this party's own and it is not improved on by applying for one.

"**And the party knows that a party may apply in writing at its own cost for a copy of any record, which grants no copy and makes none and releases nothing, and that no date attaches to any of it, and that the party is applying anyway.**"

"**And the last line,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it is the one I wrote three times.**

> *The release of such a copy, if it is ever made, is not in this party's keeping and has never been in it, and this party is not saying no to it in four years and is not saying yes either, and both halves of that are on this sheet with the date on them.*
>
> *Held at Morrow Quay, the sixteenth of November instant, in the holder's own left hand, and no copy of this sheet has gone to any office and none will.*

**That is the whole of it,**" he said. "**And it is one sheet and it is dated and it cost a penny to put in the post and the penny is out of the bag and the bag was a mark and eight shillings and sixpence when I started this.**

"**And you are not signing it,**" said Nessa Pike.

"**I am not signing it. There are two of us and a sheet has one hand on it and the hand is mine, and the reason is that on the twenty-ninth of September a woman in a market office priced a line in a room and the price was that this yard would ask her for nothing again, and the price has not moved, and a second signature on that sheet would be a way of putting this yard's name on a thing that has to carry one name and not two.**"

---

**And then she took the pencil, because it is her pencil, and read the day's entries back from the top of the bench, and he read them back after her, and nobody else was in that shed.**

---

*First column, entered on Monday the sixteenth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that a written application in the holder's own left hand, in one sheet, dated the sixteenth instant, was made by this party to the circuit office of this place for a copy of one record of that office, being the record of the entry of one item of recovered transparent mineral in the custody room of that office; that the said sheet asks for a copy of a record and not for the object and says so on its face; that a penny of the ordinary post was paid on the said sheet out of this yard's bag at about half past two in the afternoon in the hand of the holder and the bag held a mark and eight shillings and sixpence before that penny went on it and one mark and six shillings and a penny after it, which is two hundred and sixty-two pence and two hundred and sixty-one pence; that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare on Monday the sixteenth instant, being twenty-eight Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it; and that the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know on the said Monday, and this party walked it, and the walk is nil and has been nil since the third of August.*

*And that the reason against the second column line of the sixteenth instant is that the reason against a line of this column does not go into a record and this yard knows that now in a way it did not know it in June, and that it is applying anyway, and that a reason which does not travel is a reason a man has to stand behind in a room and not a reason that stops him doing the thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Monday the sixteenth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this party entered in June, and again on the eighteenth day of September instant, that it would not apply for a copy of a record, and gave the reason both times, and that the reason is that this party does not put a name on a thing it cannot check and a copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check; and that the reason it is applying on the sixteenth instant is not that the reason has gone soft and is not that a month has made it smaller, and that the reason is that a copy of a record carries on its own face the limits the record itself carries, and that a limit printed on the face of a sheet is the one kind of limit that does not arrive in a city as a bare line with nothing behind it, and that this party has spent four volumes saying that a reason does not travel and has not worked out what else travels; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a sheet went in the post on a day and cannot check what an office is going to do with a request in four months, and that what an office is going to do with it is the whole of the price and there is no figure for it in this harbor.*

*And that the holder entered on the said day that this party has not told the woman of no office what it is applying for and has not told her about a room on Thursday and is not going to, and that it has had a sixth occasion in the last six weeks not to tell her a thing it wrote down in the second column on the eighth of September instant, and that it did not, and that the reason is the reason of the fourth of October instant and there is nothing new to add to it and he is not going to invent a second one, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a shed can be checked for what he wrote and cannot be checked for what he was going to say, and that this is a thing that is not paid in this month and is not going to be paid by anything this yard does.*

---

**On Tuesday the seventeenth of November the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now FIVE, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth day of August past, the week of Tuesday the fifth day of September past, the week of Tuesday the sixth day of October past, the week of Tuesday the third day of November past, and the week of Tuesday the seventeenth day of November instant, and all five are entered in the same breath in this book and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all five at once rather than the one that is easier.**

Twenty-five lines and one hundred marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and it is the fifth Tuesday in a row that this yard has not known on the Monday what it was going to write on the Tuesday. Nineteen envelopes on a bench in that chest. One mark and six shillings and a penny in the bag, which is two hundred and sixty-one pence, and a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and the figure short is three hundred and seventy-nine pence.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the seventeenth day of November instant at about ten past ten in the morning in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on the said day was not paid and no envelope was made, and that the bag held one mark and six shillings and a penny in it on the morning of that day, which is two hundred and sixty-one pence, and that a wage of four marks is six hundred and forty pence, and that the figure the bag was short of the wage is three hundred and seventy-nine pence; and that the column of this book stands at twenty-five lines and one hundred marks with the fifth of March the oldest; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is five, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past, the third of November past and the seventeenth of November instant, and that the five figures are five different figures and none of them is either of the others; and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is nineteen; and that an envelope that is not made is not a wage and this column is not the wage; and that the reason against the line is that the money which would have paid it went on a sheet of paper at a penny in the ordinary post on the Monday and that a sheet of paper is either worth a penny or it is worth nothing and this yard has not got a figure for which, and that it is entered in the first column and not the second because a man can come up this hill on any day of a week and ask whether the wage of the seventeenth of November was paid, and the answer is no.*

**Nothing came into this yard on the Monday but a penny of somebody's else and a penny of this yard's own going out, and nothing came in on the Tuesday and nothing went out, and one mark and six shillings and a penny is in the bag. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August and is not paid and is not being paid out of anything that has come into this yard. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and a penny of the ordinary post is not the forty marks and is not going to be entered as a payment of it. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and fifty-six days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and no occasion was entered in these two days and no measurement was taken, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence and nothing this yard has done in two days touches it. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the live question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and did not ask in these two days.**
