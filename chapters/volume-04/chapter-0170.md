# Chapter 170

*The Price That Is Not Six Marks and Two Days*

Friday the twenty-ninth and Saturday the thirtieth of September, in the market office of Morrow Quay, and in the shed.

**He went in. That is the whole of the change and it took twenty-one days to happen and nobody arranged it.**

On Friday the eighth of September he stood on the steps outside that office from about ten past ten until about half past eleven with his back to a notice board and heard about half of what was said inside and did not go in, and he entered both halves of that in this yard's own book with the date and the hour, and the reason against the line was that a party which has said out loud in a market that it does not ask a man one single question about a boat has got to be able to say the same about a woman.

On Friday the twenty-ninth of September that office was open to anybody from nine in the morning and the counting was going on behind the long table and the press at the back of the floor was unlocked under its slab, and he came in at about ten past ten and stood at the end of the table with his hat in his hand, and she was there with a book of her own, a pencil, and her own brass calipers, and about eleven people in the room.

"**You stood on the steps,**" she said, without looking up.

"**I stood on the steps.**

"**For eighty minutes.**"

"**For eighty minutes. And I did not go in, and I did not send anybody in, and I did not ask you afterwards what you had said, and all three of those are in my own book with the date on them and I have not taken one of them out.

"**And I have come in. And I am not going to pretend that is a different kind of courage from the other thing, because it is the same man in the same coat and it is only that this time I have something to ask you for.**"

"**Then say it, and say it before the counting stops, because I do not stop a counting for anybody and I would rather you did not be the reason a count came out wrong.**"

---

**And he said it in about nine sentences, standing at the end of a table in a room open to anybody, in front of about eleven people, and he did not lower his voice and he did not raise it, and the whole of it is in the book in the shed and read back at nine o'clock that night.**

"**There is a piece of paper in a city with four words of mine at the bottom of a sentence I never said, and I cannot get at it and I am not going to try.

"**And a man of the first lay came up my hill on Wednesday and told me why, in about nine sentences, and what he told me is that a man with a page cannot act on a refusal. He can act on a name. And that there are three people on this coast who can put a trade at the foot of a line and that I have got none of them, and that one of them is in this room, and that she is not on my crew and is not of my yard and is owed forty marks first after food by this harbor's own work since the fifteenth of October, and that I am not going to pay her, and that I have said so out loud in a market office in this town and would say it a second time.

"**So here is the ask, and it is small, and I have made it small on purpose. In the week following the twelfth of October a man in a room is going to ask what this yard found, and this yard's answer is one sentence of its own, and a sentence of my own is not a trade. I am not asking you about a bar of gravel and I would not take your word about a bar of gravel from anybody.

"**I am asking you for a line. One line, in your trade's words, with your name on it and your price on it, saying what a lump of green glass off a mole's face is and what a chip of worked glass is, and whether the two of them are the same thing. And I want to be able to put that in front of a man in a city in front of a page of mine, and the page of mine is a refusal and a refusal is worth nothing to a man with a pen.

"**And I have not got your price and I am not going to pay it, and I want you to tell me the price anyway, because I have come into this room and I am not going to stand in it and pretend I came for the air.**"

---

**And she put the calipers down, which she does not do in the middle of a morning, and priced it before she answered it, and the price is in the book and was not six marks and two days.**

"**You did not come in here with an offer,**" she said, "**so I am not going to give you a figure, and I would like that understood before anything else, because a figure is the easiest thing in the world for me to say and it is the one thing that would be a lie this morning.

"**So here is the price, and it is not money, and it is the only thing I have got to price it in, and you are going to hear it and you are not going to be able to do it, and I am telling you that now so that you do not stand there finding out.

"**It is an undertaking. In your own book, in your own left hand, in the first column, with a date on it, and it is that this party does not ask me for anything again. Not for six marks and two days. Not at nothing. Not for a line, not for a name, not in a room, not in a shed, not in a market, and not in about two years when you will have had a bad month and need me to say that a thing is what it is.

"**And the reason I am pricing it that way is a sentence of your own, and it is a true sentence, and you worked it out in the first week of August and you have not had to say it out loud since and I have.

"**A conservator you cannot pay is a conservator you have to keep asking. And asking me to be useful is asking me to be cheap. Those are your words and they are in your book and they are right, and they are right about me tonight in a way that has nothing to do with forty marks.

"**Here is what happens to me if I give you a line. Not this year. In about two years, or three. There is a page in a city with a trade on it and a name on the trade. And then there is a second page. And then somebody says *the conservator* in a room, and by then it does not mean a trade, it means a woman, and a woman can be applied to, and asked, and produced, and made to answer for what a yard found, and made to answer for what a yard did not find, and neither of those is my trade and both of them would be done in my name.

"**And that is the whole price and you cannot pay it, and I am not going to let you try, and I want you to stand there and hear yourself not be able to pay it rather than have me be the one who stops you.**"

"**I worked out that I cannot pay it about six days ago and I have not written it down and I am not going to write it down tonight, and I am not going to pretend to you that I came in here tonight not knowing, because I came in here tonight not knowing, and that is the honest of the two versions of that sentence.**"

"**Then it is more or less what I thought.**"

---

"**Then the answer is no,**" she said, "**and I am going to say the no properly, because a thing said badly in a room is a thing that gets said badly later by somebody else.

"**No. I will not put a name on a line for this yard, and I will not put a name on a line for any yard, and I will not do it at a figure and I will not do it at nothing. And I have not done it at nothing since the fourth of August, in this room, in front of nine people, where I said in front of nine people that I would not do a thing at nothing again, and I have not, and I am not going to start on a Friday morning because the man who stood on the steps has finally come in.

"**And you are going to go out of this room and go up a hill and be poorer than when you came in, and I am not going to feel bad about it, and if you want to put that last part in your book you may, in your own hand, on your own column, on your own date.**"

"**I am going to put all of it in the book,**" said Calder Marr. "**Every word you have said to me this morning, and the price, and the no, and the fact that I came in. And I am not going to pay you and I am not going to be able to and I am not going to pretend to you that I am going to. I am going to go on needing you and I am going to go on not paying you and that is the arrangement and it is a bad one and it is not a new one, and it was in this yard's book in May and it was in this room in July and it is in the book again tonight.

"**And I am not going to say thank you, because you have not given me anything, and I am not going to say I am sorry, because I have not been asked and a man in a market who is sorry is a man who has already been told what a thing cost.**"

Then she picked the calipers up and went back to her book, and about four minutes later, at about ten past eleven, he went out into the counting and down the hill, and the slab was still unlocked at the back of the floor and he did not shift it and did not pay a shilling and did not ask for forty sheets.

---

**And on Saturday the thirtieth the whole of it went into the first column in the holder's own left hand, and about half an hour later two lines went into the second column, and the ship's hand read all of it back from the top of the bench to the bottom and the holder read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-ninth of September instant at about ten in the morning, in the market office of this harbor, in a room open to anybody, in eleven people's hearing, and entered again in this shed on the Saturday morning: that this party went into that room, having stood on the steps of it for eighty minutes on the eighth instant and not gone in; that this party asked a person of no office for one line in her own trade's words, with her name on it and her price on it, saying what a lump of green glass off a mole's face is and what a chip of worked glass is and whether the two of them are the same thing; and that she priced it before she answered it, and that the price is not money and is not six marks and two days, and is an undertaking in the first column of this yard's own book in the holder's own left hand with a date on it that this party does not ask her for anything again, and that this party did not give that undertaking and did not offer it and did not pretend to be about to, and has entered that it cannot.*

*And that the answer was no; and that it was no at a figure and no at nothing; and that she said out loud in that room, in eleven people's hearing, that she has not done a thing at nothing since the fourth of August and said in front of nine people in that room on that day that she would not do one again, and that she is not going to start on a Friday morning because the man who stood on the steps has come in at last.*

*And that the reason she gave for the price is entered in her own words: that a conservator this party cannot pay is a conservator this party has to keep asking, and that asking her to be useful is asking her to be cheap; and that in about two years, or three, a page in a city with a trade on it has a name on the trade, and then somebody in a room says *the conservator* and means a woman and not a trade, and a woman can be applied to, and asked, and produced, and made to answer for what a yard found and for what a yard did not find; and that this party entered the whole of it because a man in a city has written four words at the foot of a sentence this party did not say, and a party which has published itself is not a party that gets to be sorry in a street and mean it, and cannot get a man in a city to take four words out of a page.*

*And that no money changed hands in that room on that morning; that this party did not ask the office for forty sheets at the press and did not pay a shilling and did not shift the slab; that a person of no office is not on this crew, is not of this yard, is not a holder, and is not on the four names of the writ of the first of May; that the reporting clause on that writ does not reach her and never did; that her forty marks stand unpaid, first after food, since the fifteenth of October, and are not reduced; and that a request to strike this line out of this column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Saturday the thirtieth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder of the writ of the first of May entered on the twelfth of September that a poor crew does not get an inquiry's costs waived, and that on the twenty-ninth he learned in a room in about nine minutes that a poor crew does not get anybody to speak for it either, and that those two are one thing and not two; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man can check what he asked for and what he was refused and cannot check what it is going to cost him in two years, and that in two years this yard is going to want her very badly and is going to have no instrument and no money and no name in any office; and that the holder has NOT entered in the first column that he intends never to ask her again, and has entered why he has not: that a man who undertakes not to ask is a man who has decided that a person is a door, and she has told him in a room on Tuesday and Friday mornings in this harbor for nine months that she is not a door, and the thing underneath the price on Friday was not to turn her into a reason.*

**And the third column of that book was not filled on the thirtieth, and it has not been filled since the first of August, and there is a line in the first column about it, and nobody has been appointed, and a column nobody fills is a column somebody else fills.**

---

**No money came into this yard on the Friday and none went out, and none on the Saturday. A mark and twelve shillings and fourpence is in the bag, and it is the whole of it, and it was a mark and twelve shillings and fourpence on the eleventh of September, and in between the two of those figures this yard worked two days at a seam, paid two wages of four marks, did not pay two wages of four marks, refused a certification, sent a figure into a market in forty people's hearing, went into a room it had stood outside of for eighty minutes, and was refused a person.**

**And the two wages that were not paid on the twelfth of September and the fifth of September are still not paid, and are entered with the figure and the date against them once and not twice, and there is a mark and twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag and a wage of four on Tuesday the third of October, which is the next Tuesday and is three days off.**

Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, three counts of weeks unmoved since the fourth of January. Five marks of compressor's gland on it, unpaid since the nineteenth of May, and of no use whatever to this yard because the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August and not paid. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for a day of work on the eighth of August, unmoved since that day, with the date on it, and he is not named in that book again and is not going to be asked a question. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and unargued, and the chest in that shed was not opened this month, and the man it belongs to was not asked anything in it and is in no part of it. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor, undiminished, entered with her name on it at her own asking, and not on any wall. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two who cannot do the thing he was kept for, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and nobody has written to him, and nobody has gone to see him, and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the six marks and two days have not been paid and have not been offered and are not going to be. A sheet of a refused license with nine sentences on the back of it in the holder's own left hand, folded into the back of the writ of the first of May, and about four hundred feet of a woman's warp on the bottom of this harbor, and twenty-six marks and a day, and eight shillings and tenpence, and the refusal is a cost and is not a decision.

The cistern behind the engine house is on a rota of a Monday and a Thursday and was charged at no figure on the fourth, the seventh, the eleventh, the fourteenth, the eighteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-fifth and the twenty-eighth of September, which is eight days, and this party walked it on none of the eight and has not walked it since the third of August, and it has held since the nineteenth of June. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered since the ninth of November. Nineteen winters are in a naval file, in four lines of a letter in a herring skipper's coat, and in a list being made up in a city by an officer this yard emptied, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this party has not thanked him and has not been asked to.

**The two objects of nobody's are on the crown of this mole about a hundred and thirty-six days, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved in that time and neither of them moved in September. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet in the mole's own foundation stone, seen and not measured and not touched, and the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January is refused in a record with a date on it, and this party has not written a better letter and is not going to, and whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years, and the delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first.**

**And a working harbor is poorer on the last page of this month than it was on the first of it, and a yard of two and a half people is poorer with a mark and twelve shillings and fourpence in a bag than it was with the same, and both of those are this yard's own sentences and neither of them has been improved on in a month.**
