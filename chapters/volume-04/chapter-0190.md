# Chapter 190

*The Twenty-Seventh Wall, and a Reason Against the Line*

Monday the ninth and Tuesday the tenth of November, at the mole's steps, at the head of the dock, and in the shed.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was twenty-seven Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and the last thing that was ever on that wall was a sitting four days ago that is now a bundle in a city.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and this party has walked none of the charges there have been since the third of August, and it has held since the nineteenth of June.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about four on the Monday, with his back to the room and the same net in his hands, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and that is the sixth and last day of the three weeks on which either brother can check it.

---

**And on Tuesday the tenth of November at about eleven in the morning the wage of four marks went in, into an envelope, in the holder's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it, and it went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about eleven people in about two minutes.**

Twenty-five lines and one hundred marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. Nineteen envelopes on a bench in that chest. Four weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October and the third of November, entered in the same breath and not one after the other.

**A mark and eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag.** That is one hundred and sixty pence and ninety-six pence and six pence, and it is two hundred and sixty-two pence, and the same bag held three hundred and four pence on the evening of the twentieth of October, and the whole of the difference is three shillings and sixpence and it is a bonesetter's bill on the twenty-fourth and it is the only money this yard has spent on itself in twenty-one days.

---

**And at about six on the Tuesday evening, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, the two of them read the three weeks out at the head of this dock, and it took about nine minutes, and that is what a reading at the head of a dock is, and nobody was asked to be quiet.**

It is not a reading of the book. It is the shipwright, standing on the boards, saying the figures out: what the bag holds, what the column holds, how many weeks have fallen due without an envelope and which weeks those are, how many envelopes are on the bench, what a day of work brought and on what day and off what, what was refused and by whom and why, what a bonesetter charged, and what the whole of a sitting in a room came to.

"**A mark and eight shillings and sixpence,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Nine hundred and two pence on Friday the sixth and one hundred and two pence this morning. Four marks out of it. The column is twenty-five lines and one hundred marks and the oldest line in it is the fifth of March.

"**Four weeks fallen due with no envelope against them. The fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November. Nineteen envelopes on the bench.

"**Three shillings and sixpence to a bonesetter on the twenty-fourth of October and a quarter of a year out of the left side of the shipwright, and the strapping comes off on the twenty-first of November and not before.

"**Five marks off the face of the second seam on the sixth. Three marks refused on the wharf on Monday the twelfth of October because a crew of two and a half cannot lose a Monday in the week the sitting is in and has nobody to stand in for it, and three marks refused again this week on the Sunday, because a pump on a wharf takes two and one of them has a forearm strapped.

"**Nothing on the twenty-eighth and nothing on the twenty-ninth of October. Two days in a room and not one mark came in and none went out and both days are in the book with the hour on them.

"**The cistern behind the engine house has been charged twenty-four times since the third of August and this party has walked none of the twenty-four, and it was charged again this morning by men this yard does not know, and this party is not going to stand in a street and tell you whether that makes it twenty-five, because it did not see the days those men come on and it is not going to put a figure on a day it did not see.

"**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down this morning for the twenty-seventh time since the first of May and nothing went on it.

"**And the far bench was occupied on the twenty-second and the twenty-sixth and the thirtieth of October and the second, the sixth and the ninth of November, and on no other day of the three weeks, and neither brother went over to the other on any of the six, and that is a line either brother can check on any day of a week.

"**And a man of the second lay stood on those steps on the twenty-sixth because that office put him there for the two days of the sitting, and he is out of a Tuesday and it is not in that office's ledger and it is not in ours and it is in this yard's book.

"**And there is a thing none of us can check, which is a record in a city with five bare lines of our second column in it and no reason against any of them, and a clerk's entry that he could see a join in a sheet of paper before the man who keeps our book pointed at it, and a market of the eighteenth of July read out in a room in a flat voice by a stranger.

"**And a man of the first lay of about fifty-five stood at the wall outside the market office on the Thursday morning and said he would rather have the page in the city than the wall, and he was right, and we said so, and neither of the two of us is going to unsay it.

"**That is the three weeks. It is on the page in my own hand and his and I am not going to read one line of the second column out, and I am not going to be asked to in a market, and if I am asked in a room in a city in four years I am going to point at a page in our own book that says why.**"

Nobody said anything about the fourth in a row, which is what about eleven people in a street say about a wage in a hard month.

---

**And at about half past nine on the Tuesday night in that shed, with the lamp lit, and the two of them, and the day's entries read back from the top by the ship's hand and read back after her by the holder and nobody else in the room, the whole of the three weeks went into the first column and four lines of it went into the second, and the five of them had reasons against them.**

"**Stop there,**" said Nessa Pike, at about a quarter to ten, when the read-back had got to the Thursday of the twenty-ninth of October and had not got past it.

"**I have not got to the end.**"

"**You have got to the end of the part I want to stop. Read that one back again and then write this down, in the first column, tonight, and I want it in your own hand and not in mine.

"**A man of this yard was right about a thing in a room in this harbor on the twenty-ninth of October. That sheet had been in two hands at two times and there is a clerk's own entry in a record in a city that says the clerk could see it before you did and told the room so.

"**And the same man has been less believed in a market in this harbor ever since, and says so, and is not asking anybody to put that back.

"**Both of those go on one line in the first column with a reason against it, and the reason is that a man in this harbor can get off a mole tomorrow morning and walk to that room and ask a clerk of the circuit office whether it is in his page, and can ask a man at that wall on a Thursday morning whether a man of about fifty-five said what he said he was going to say on the fifth of November, and both of those can be checked and neither of the two of us is the only person in this town who can check them.

"**And the reason it is in the first column and not the second is that the cost of it is public. It is not something you did in your own head at nine at night and it is not something I can hold for you in a shed, and the whole of what happened to you in that room happened in front of a clerk and about nine people and it is on a page, and a thing that is on a page is a thing that goes in the first column whether anybody in this yard likes it or not.**"

*First column, entered on Tuesday the tenth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on the said day went in into an envelope in the holder's own left hand at about eleven in the morning, and that the column stands at twenty-five lines and one hundred marks with the fifth of March the oldest, and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is nineteen, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is four, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past, the sixth of October past and the third of November instant; and that the bag held a mark and eight shillings and sixpence on the morning of that day, and that is two hundred and sixty-two pence, and that the same bag held three hundred and four pence on the evening of the twentieth of October past, and that the whole of the difference between those two figures is three shillings and sixpence and that figure is a bonesetter's bill paid out of that bag on the twenty-fourth of October instant, and that a figure written on a note is not a figure paid until somebody puts the money on the counter, and that the money went onto a counter at the back of a chandler's shop off the market at about ten past four in the afternoon on the twenty-fourth in the hand of the bonesetter herself.

*And that the whole of the three weeks was read out at the head of this dock on the evening of the said day in about eleven people's hearing in about nine minutes, and that the reading is a reading of figures and not a reading of the book, and that not one line of the second column was read out in it, and that the entry of the sixteenth day of September instant, that a copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check, and the reason the party's first column is checkable and its second column is not, both stand and are not improved on; and that this party entered on the said day that a reading at the head of a dock in about nine minutes can be checked by anybody who was in the street and a record in a city cannot be checked by anybody in this harbor, and that both of those are true and that the reason this line is in this column and not the second is that a party which has been shown, in a room, that a wall has nothing in it you can be made to answer for is not going to start arguing that a dock is the same instrument as a wall.

*And that a man of this yard was right about a thing in a room in this harbor on the twenty-ninth day of October instant, being that the notice served on this party on the tenth day of September instant has been in two hands at two times, and that a clerk of the circuit office entered the same fact in the record of that sitting and said in that room that he had seen it first, and that the holder said write it; and that the same man has been less believed in a market in this harbor since the second day of November instant and has entered that and is not asking anybody to put it back; and that both halves of that are on one line in this column, and the reason against the line is that a man in this harbor can get off a mole tomorrow morning and walk to that room and ask a clerk of the circuit office whether it is in his page, and can ask any man on that quay on the fifth of November instant whether a man of about fifty-five said at that wall what he said in that street he was going to say, and that both of those are checkable and that the cost of what happened in that room is public and is not a thing either of them can hold in a shed.

*And that the question standing on the table on the said day is a document this party has never seen and has never asked for, being a copy of a record about an object, with the limits the record itself carries printed on the face of the copy, and this party entered that if it ever got hold of one it would show that a manifest was altered and would not show who altered it, and that the copy would say so on its own face, and that a man of the first lay of this harbor cannot check it and the only men who could are in a city, and that this party is not applying for one this month and has entered why, and the reason being the entry of the eighteenth day of September instant that this party does not put a name on a thing it cannot check, and that this party is not saying no to it in four years and is not saying yes either, and that the release of that copy is not in this party's keeping and has never been in it, and that the party entered in June that it published a road and cannot un-publish it and entered the same about this.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the tenth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man stood in a room in this harbor for two days in the last week of October instant and read his own book out loud in it and came down the hill with a bundle in a city that says two things about him that he has not chosen, and that this party entered that it does not know yet which of the two of those will cost more in four years, and that the answer is not going to be found out by not knowing about it, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a bundle exists and cannot check what is in it or what a man is going to do with it.

*And that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a join was put on a notice and a clerk's own entry against him about it, and cannot check what a man in a city is going to make of a record in which a yard of Morrow Quay is the only thing anybody in this harbor can be got to say.

*And that the holder entered on the said day that this yard has four things and not one of them can be put in front of anybody, being a number off a copying job sheet, a counterfoil and a fair copy in two other people's keeping, a file on a second floor it has not asked for, and a woman it cannot pay, and that the ship's hand said the same of them in a doorway on the twelfth day of October instant and this party agrees with her and is not going to improve on it in a market in a fortnight, and that the reason it is in this column and not the first is that the whole of what those four things are for is to be used by somebody and none of them can be.

*And that the holder entered on the third day of October instant that he was going to tell that woman a thing, and on the fourth that he had not told her and the reason he did not, and that on the seventeenth of October instant she said in this yard's shed that she was not going to ask him, and that both halves of that are on one line in this book and were read back; and that he has had a fifth occasion in the last three weeks not to tell her, being a room on the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth of October instant, and that he did not, and that the reason is the reason of the fourth of October instant and there is nothing new to add to it and he is not going to invent a second one; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a shed can be checked for what he wrote and cannot be checked for what he was going to say, and that this is a thing that is not paid in this month and is not going to be paid by anything this yard does.*

*And that the third column of this book was not filled on any day of the last three weeks and nobody has been appointed to it and the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two, and that a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column, and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that nobody has written to that boy and nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and that this party has not sent for him and does not know where he is and has not asked.*

**A working harbor is poorer at the end of these three weeks than it was at the start of them, and nothing this yard has done in three weeks has made it better, and nothing this yard published on a market wall in October has made it better. In the bag there is a mark and eight shillings and sixpence. Three marks to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved. Seven pence to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and she is not coming to the market office of this town on a Tuesday or a Friday morning any more, and this party did not arrange that and is not going to un-arrange it. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and fifty days up the crown of that mole, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved in a hundred and fifty days and will not move for anything this yard does. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on any day of these three weeks. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on, because a light costs oil. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city by a woman at a table, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the live question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody this month. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at and five miles of that coast was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote themselves stands, and the holder has not asked twice to have it lifted and did not ask at all this month.
