# Chapter 184

*The Whole of It, Whole and Unshortened, in a Room Over a Tide-Gang Store*

Wednesday the twenty-eighth and Thursday the twenty-ninth of October, in the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin, and on the boards.

**On Wednesday the twenty-eighth of October at ten of the morning the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin had a slate wall behind the reeve's bench that had been bare since Monday the twenty-sixth, which was twenty-five Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk of the harbor authority had kept his own page and had not mentioned it, and about nine people were in the room, and the tide-gang store was shut behind them, and the counting of the fish was going on somewhere else in the building.**

The room was cold. A stove against the far wall had not been lit since the middle of September, and a man put his hand on it and said *that is a stove and not a fire* and took his hand off it. The windows were high and green with salt and the light came in from behind the people rather than over them.

The party was not represented. It had instructed nobody and had retained nobody and had entered that on the twentieth of September and had not improved on that entry.

Ambrose Sell, clerk of the circuit office and clerk of that sitting, took the chair at the end of the table, wrote the date and the hour and the place on a sheet before he said anything, which is a practice and has been one since April.

"**This yard's book is to be read, and the clerk of the first of May told me on Thursday last in that yard's doorway that it would be read whole and unshortened from the first of May to the last page in the order it is in, and I read it back and entered it, and I am not going to take a vote on it.

"**And it is to be read by the man who keeps it, because a book read out by a stranger is a document and a book read out by the man who wrote it is a man's account of his own book, and this office is entitled to the second of those. If the man who keeps it cannot read it, this office will say so on the record and read it itself. He is going to tell me now whether he can read it.**"

"**I can read it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then that is entered, and if your voice goes I will not stop you and I will enter that it went.

"**One other thing, and it is about my own hand. I am going to enter a line, a date and an hour. I am not going to enter a reason. A record has a column and the column is the width of my page, and a reason against a line in a book is a paragraph. And if I take four words out of a man's paragraph and write those four words down, then in about four years a man in a city reads four words and thinks the man wrote four words, and he did not, and I will have made him a man who wrote four words, with a pen and no bad intent, and that is worse than a lie because it will hold.

"**Now. Begin at the first of May.**"

---

**And the holder of the writ of the first of May read his own book out loud in a room for three hours and fifty minutes and did not skip anything, and was asked twice by a man in the room to put a thing in a second time, and both times he did.**

He read the writ's operative lines and then read them again in his own words, because the clerk asked for the ground and the four names and the four-day clause in a form that could go on a page, and he read the four returns in order with the days against them. The fourth of May, two days late. The tenth of May, on the day. The seventeenth of June, on the day. The fourteenth of July, on the day, out of a finding of Monday the tenth. And he read the line of the tenth of September that says the day of a finding is not counted and a return is due on the fourth day after it, and the clerk put his pen down and wrote it twice, once as a fact of the reading and once as a fact of the record, and said: *that is the first thing anybody has said in this room that is worth a page.*

He read the sheet of the twenty-fifth of August with the place of every copy of it on the face of it, and the eleven copies and the six hand-copies and the four unreached holders who have not been asked. He read the day of the eighth of August whole, and the days of May and June and July, and the four lines of the second column of the twentieth of July, and the bare fact of the fourteenth of August, of which he can bring up four words and the rest of it.

At about two o'clock he came to the twenty-ninth of September and read the line about a price that was not money, and then the eleven words about a woman in this harbor who is not on four names, and the clerk wrote those eleven words down and read them back and the man said they were his own words. And then he came to the fifth of May and read the line that says the first column is checkable and the second column is not, and the clerk asked him to stop there and go back four pages and read it again.

"**Say that again, and slower, because I want to write down what kind of instrument this book is and not what kind of instrument I have been told it is. What is checkable in it?**

"**The first column. Every figure in it can be got at by a man who can get off a mole in this harbor. What is not?**

"**The second column. Five lines of it, by your own count, four with no name against them, and a reason against every one of them that is a paragraph. Not checkable by anybody, including you, and you have said so in that yard twice.

"**And the third column.**"

"**I have not come to it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**I know that you have not come to it. I have been looking at the top of that page for an hour and a half. It is in a boy's hand, it is dated from the second of May, and there are fourteen lines in it.

"**You are going to read the two columns and leave that one to me, and I want you to say so out loud before I read it, because in about four years somebody is going to find a page at the top of this yard's book that the man who keeps it did not read out, and I am not going to have that be a thing that happened without anybody deciding it.**"

"**Read it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then I am, and I will say one thing first, and it is my own trade and it is not on anybody's side. That column is a record of what a man said when he was not in the boat. It is the only one of the three that nobody can check and the only one that is not about a mole, and I am going to read it in a flat voice, because it is a page and a clerk reads pages.

"**And nobody is going to be asked about it afterwards by this office, and that is not a favour to you. It is that the office cannot. The only person alive who can explain those fourteen lines is the boy whose hand wrote them, and he is not in this room, and this office cannot make him come and cannot make anybody ask him, and the three of them are standing in your own book and I have read them and I am not going to act on them.**"

He read the fourteen lines. It took about eleven minutes. They are short, they are in a boy's right hand, and they carry the time a thing was read on the steps and the time it was written in the book, and about four hundred yards from that room about four hundred people will one day be able to read what a man said on a step in May, and none of them will be able to check it.

---

**And at about four o'clock on the Wednesday, with about eleven people in the room and the light going, Ambrose Sell put his pen down and said that he had a right to ask the party a question, and that he was going to say what he thought the answer was and ask it anyway.**

"**You say in this book, in two places, that a bare fact with a reason against it is the only place a man in this harbor is allowed to say that he does not know and mean it. Explain that to me in your own words, and I am going to enter the answer.**"

Calder Marr began.

"**A first-column line is a thing a man can go and look at. A second-column line is a thing a man cannot go and look at, and the reason against it is the whole of why he wrote it down, and if the reason goes then the line is a man saying something with nothing behind it, which is**"

He stopped.

"**Which is what it is going to be in about four years. Stop there. I am not going to have a man finish a sentence in a room that he cannot finish in a shed, and I am not going to have a clerk write down the end of it either.

"**You have been in this room about six hours and a great deal of the last hour has been the first column, which is the part of your book a man can check, and the man who has been keeping it cannot explain the other half of it in a sentence.

"**So I am going to ask the other half of this party, and the reason is that I have got a party of two and a half people and one of them can say it and the other one cannot, and a record has no column for *which of the two*.**"

He turned round on the bench.

"**Ma'am. What is a second column.**"

Nessa Pike did not move and did not take a second.

"**A column a man writes in when he cannot be checked,**" she said. "**And the reason goes against every line of it, and the reason does not go into a record, and so the line goes by itself, and in about four years the line is a bare sentence with no reason behind it and it will look like a man saying he did not know.

"**That is the whole of it. It took me about a year to learn it and it took him a year and a half.**"

About eleven people looked at the two of them, and Ambrose Sell wrote it down and read it back, and she said it was her own words and that she was not going to better them, and then he entered in the record that the answer had been given by the ship's hand of the party and not by the holder of the writ, and he read that part back too, and Calder Marr listened to it and said nothing at all.

---

**And on the Thursday the twenty-ninth of October at ten of the morning the room was fuller, and at about ten the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party was in a room and did not walk it, and that is the twenty-second charge since the third of August and this party has walked none of the twenty-two.**

"**There is one document left that I put on the schedule in June and that has not been read,**" said Ambrose Sell. "**It is the notice of the tenth of September, served on this party by me, in a doorway, in about nine people's hearing, and it is the paper the whole of this sitting is built out of. Everybody in this room has read it. I am asking whether the man who keeps it has anything to say about it that he has not already put in his own book.**"

"**I have got something,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to say it, and then I am going to tell you that it is not a thing that can be used, and you are going to write down both halves or neither.

"**There is a join in it. The first three paragraphs and the sixth are in brown ink and the fourth is in black, and the brown has gone the colour the brown goes. And the fourth paragraph is folded the other way from the others, which is a thing about how a sheet was folded before it went into a bundle, and there is a place on that fold where the paper has been opened and shut again, and the head of that paragraph starts a third of an inch lower than the head of the third one, and a clerk writing at a different sitting with a different nib starts a third of an inch lower than the clerk before him, and I have watched about four hundred men do it.

"**And that is the whole of what I can see. That paper has been in two hands at two times. It does not say which of the two hands is right. It does not say why. It does not say whose the two hands were, and I am not going to say, and I would not be able to say if I tried, and I have been asked by nobody and I am not going to ask.**"

"**I have had that paper since the tenth of September,**" said Ambrose Sell.

"**I know you have.**"

"**Then hear the next part from me, because I would rather you had it now than worked it out in a market in March. I can see the two inks. I could see them on the day it was served, and I did not need the holder of the writ of the first of May of this harbor to come up a hill on a Friday and point at them.

"**And I am going to enter it in that order, and the order is: a clerk of this office saw it, and the man who keeps the book saw it afterwards.

"**You have been telling this harbor since the seventh of July that a thing a man can check is worth more than a thing he cannot. And you have just told this room that on a sheet of paper you can see what a clerk could see. That is not a discovery and I am not going to write it down as one, and neither is anybody else going to be allowed to write it down as one.

"**And I am entering the second half and not leaving it out, and the reason is that if I leave it out then in about four years the only thing in this bundle anybody is proud of is a thing a man of this harbor put there, and I am not going to be that man.**"

"**Write it,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then the only question I am going to ask today, and I have written the words of it in my own page first.

"**What does it mean.**"

The room was quiet for about four seconds.

"**I do not know,**" said Calder Marr.

The clerk wrote it down. It took about four seconds, which is the shortest thing he had written all day.

"**Say it again, and slower, because this is the only sentence in this bundle that is going to be read in four years by a man who was not in this room.**"

"**I do not know,**" said Calder Marr, "**which hand is right, or why it was done, or whose the two hands were. And I have said the same thing three times in this room and I am not going to say it a fourth time in a different way.

"**And put the reason in your record, and the reason is that I have got a trade and it is a good one, and it finds out that two things were not made at one time, and it does not find out which of the two is the true one, and I have never once in my life pretended to anybody that it did, and I am not going to start in a room.**"

Ambrose Sell wrote that down and read it back, and the man said it was his own words and that he was not going to improve on them, and the clerk entered that as well, and about eleven people in a cold room over a tide-gang store watched a man's own method make him smaller in front of them and not larger.

"**There is one more thing in your own book and I am going to read it,**" said the clerk, "**because it is in the book and the book is being read, and I am not going to ask you whether you would rather I did not, because if I do not, then it is the only line in this bundle that has been read in two rooms and not in one.**"

He read the entry of the eighteenth of July out of the second column, in the holder's own words, in a flat voice, in about forty seconds: six joints in a crevice on the second seam's own face, the spacing the same at every one, the fastenings the same at every one, nine in about seven feet, and he had never in his life seen a thing like it and neither had anybody in this harbor; made once, in one time, by one set of hands, from the north end down, and he would put the two ends of it against a wall in this harbor and it would run true; whatever it was for it was not to keep two boats off the bottom in a blow, because there was nothing in that hole that would hold anything in a sea; it was to hold two things together that somebody wanted to go on being one thing, and that is not a repair, and that is a man tying a knot.

And then he read the line of the twenty-third of July out of the first column: that on the eighteenth the party did not stop him, that it entered the whole of what was said and has not shortened it, and that on the twenty-third it was entered in the second column that the said statement was wrong in three parts out of four, and that the party is not using the word *chain* and is not going to.

"**That goes in with everything else and it goes in in the same hand as the rest of it,**" said Ambrose Sell, "**and I am going to write down one thing that is not in your book, and I am going to write it because it is in your mouth and not in your book.

"**You are going to say now that a page in a book is not the same instrument as a sentence in a street. Say it if you are going to say it, and then I am going to tell you that they are both going to be in the same bundle in about four years and I am not the man who decides that they go in separate rooms.**"

"**They are not the same instrument,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am not going to pretend to you in a room that they are, and I am not going to pretend the other way either.

"**And there is a fifth thing and it is not in this book and I cannot produce it, and I am telling you that in this room instead of not telling you. I said five things in a market on the eighteenth of July and four of them are in this book and you have read them. I found the fifth one missing on the Saturday third of October when I came to a line of my own and could not bring up the rest of it. I am not going to put it in a room and I am not going to pretend it is not there.**"

"**That is entered,**" said Ambrose Sell, "**and I am not going to say what I think of it, because I do not think anything about it, and if I do in about four years I will be able to read this page and find out that I was wrong.**"

Then he closed the bundle and said that the office would write and the parties would go, and that nothing had been decided and nothing had been proved and nobody had been charged with anything.

Nobody was convicted. Nobody was proved wrong. A man who is the best reader of joins in this harbor had told a room that he could see a join in a sheet of paper and could not say what it meant, and a market on the eighteenth of July had been read out in a room above a tide-gang store, in a flat voice, in his own words, by a clerk who was not doing a part.

**And the party went down the hill at about one in the afternoon and did not talk about it, and on the boards at half past two the shipwright said one thing, and it was about a woman.**

"**A form with her hand on it was in that room,**" she said, "**and she was not in that room.

"**And I am not going to let it be a small thing and I am not going to let it be a big thing either, because a big thing is what somebody in a market is going to make it into in about a fortnight. A sheet with her hand on it was in there and it was read out and it went into a record in a city, and she is four days up the coast and she does not know it was read, and nobody in that room asked whether she had read it.

"**And it is the same as the eleven words of the sixteenth of October and it is not the same as the eleven words, and I want the difference written down while the two of us can both still remember the room.**"

"**Write it,**" said Calder Marr.

*First column, checkable by any man who can get off a mole in this harbor: that the party attended at the hearing room over the tide-gang store on the west basin on Wednesday the twenty-eighth and Thursday the twenty-ninth day of October instant at ten of the morning on both days, un-represented, having instructed nobody and retained nobody; and that about nine people were in the room on the first day and about eleven on the second, and that no copy of any record was made in that room and none was given to any party and none was asked for.

*And that the crew's book of this yard was produced whole and unshortened on the first day and was read out by the holder of the writ of the first of May instant in his own voice from the first of May to the last page in the order it is in, and that it took three hours and fifty minutes; and that the two columns were read out and the third column at the head of a page, being fourteen lines in the hand of a boy of twenty-two dated from the second of May instant, was not read by the holder and was read by the clerk in a flat voice, and that the holder gave that up in that room in a sentence and did not take it back, and that the clerk entered that this office cannot make that boy come and cannot make anybody ask him, and that the three nots of the eighth of June instant stand and that this office is not going to act on them.

*And that on the first day at about four o'clock the clerk asked the holder what a second column is and the holder began an answer and stopped and the clerk entered the answer as not given, and then asked the ship's hand of this party the same question, and she answered it in one sentence, and it was entered that the answer was given by the ship's hand and not by the holder of the writ.

*And that on the Thursday instant the holder stated in that room that the notice served on this party on the tenth day of September instant has been in two hands at two times, and gave four grounds for it, being the colour of the ink in the first three paragraphs and the sixth and in the fourth, the fold of the sheet at that paragraph, the opening and shutting of the paper at the fold, and the depth at which the fourth paragraph begins; and that he said in the same breath that it does not say which hand is right, or why, or whose the two hands were; and that the clerk stated that he had himself had the said notice since the tenth day of September instant and could himself see the two inks, and entered the fact in that order, the clerk first and the holder of the writ second, and gave his reason, which is that if the fact is left out of the record then in about four years the only thing in the bundle anybody is proud of is a thing a man of this harbor put there; and that the holder said write it.

*And that the clerk asked what it means, having written the words of the question in his own page first, and the holder answered that he did not know, three times, and gave the reason, that his trade finds out that two things were not made at one time and does not find out which of the two is the true one; and that the clerk read the entry of the second column of the eighteenth day of July instant in the holder's own words and in a flat voice, and also read the line of the twenty-third instant out of the first column, that the party did not stop him and has not shortened it and has entered that it was wrong in three parts out of four and has not taken it back; and that it was entered that a page in a book and a sentence in a street are not the same instrument, and that the holder said in that room that he said five things in a market on the eighteenth of July and that four of them are in that book and that the fifth he cannot produce.

*And that nothing was decided on either day and nobody was convicted and nobody was charged with anything; and that this party's entries of the sixteenth day of September and the twentieth day of October, that the reading of that book in a room is coming and that this party is not going to soften a line and is not going to leave one out, both stand and neither was improved on; and that this party entered before the first day that it would not soften one and was right about that, and entered on the Thursday instant that it was not right about what the two days cost.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a man stood in a room in this harbor for two days and read his own book out loud in it and gave up a column at the top of a page to a clerk to read in a flat voice, and that a man who is the best reader of joins in this harbor said in a room that he could see a join in a sheet of paper and could not say what it meant and said it three times and would not say it a fourth time in a different way, and that a clerk entered the fact that a clerk could see the same join and had seen it first.

*And that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a book was read and how long it took and cannot check what a record in a city is worth in four years, and that on the twenty-fifth day of October instant this party worked out in this shed, before the sitting and not after it, that five lines of the second column were going into that record without the reason against any of them, and that the reason does not travel and the line does; and that the holder entered on the twenty-ninth instant that the finding of that clerk is going to be read as a finding against the party, and that the party has entered it against itself in the first column and is not going to improve on it and is not going to be thanked for it.

*And that the ship's hand said in that room that a form with that woman's hand on it was in that room and that the woman was not in that room, and asked for the difference between those two facts to be written down while the two of them could both still remember the room, and that the reason it is in this column and not the first is that whether a person has read a thing written about her is not checkable from a mole and is not anybody's business except hers, and that this party has not asked her and is not going to ask her, and that the forty marks stand and are not reduced.*

**No money came into this yard on the Wednesday and none on the Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and eight shillings and sixpence is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the third of November instant. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and its three counts of weeks stand unmoved at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright and is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and the chest was not opened. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and the reporting clause on the writ of the first of May does not reach her and never did. Twenty-four lines and ninety-six marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, eighteen envelopes on a bench, three weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-seven days up the crown of that mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the twenty-ninth instant and this party walked none of the twenty-two charges there have been since the third of August. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either day of the sitting. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open and was not mentioned in that room. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. Nineteen winters are in a list in a city and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what the woman at that table writes with this yard's four returns. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next with four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence and nothing in that room touched it. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn and this volume does not decide which of them is right. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands and the holder did not ask to have it lifted this month.
