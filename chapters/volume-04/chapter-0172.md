# Chapter 172

*A Man Reading His Own Book*

Saturday the third and Sunday the fourth of October, in the shed, and on the mole's crown above the third seam's mark.

**A man of this yard sat down on Saturday the third of October at about eight in the morning with his yard's own crew's book in front of him and read it, and it took him until the light went, and nobody had ever asked him to and he had never done it.**

The book is a ledger of about forty-seven weeks with two ruled columns and a third one at the head of a page that a boy of twenty-two started on the second of May and that has not been filled since the first of August. It is written in three hands: a boy's right up to the fifteenth of May, a shipwright's in the middle stretches, and the holder's own left hand in most of it. The paper is the kind a chandler keeps his own page on, because a chandler in this harbor is a man who does not throw a page away.

"**Then read it out,**" said Nessa Pike, "**from the top, and I will follow you down the page and if you have got a line wrong I will put my thumb on it.**

"**I have been writing it since the second of May and I have read it back about forty times and you have read it back about forty times and I have never once sat down and gone through it the way you would go through a lease.**

"**So read it.**"

He read it. He started at the first of May, which is the writ, and he read the four returns in order with the days against them, and he read the sheet of the twenty-fifth of August, and he read the day of the eighth of August, and he read the day of the twenty-fourth of July, and he read the day of the fifteenth of July in about four hours, and the whole of it was in his own left hand and he knew what every line of the first column meant and where it was made and by whom and at what hour.

He got to the fourteenth day of August at about half past four in the afternoon.

*Second column, entered on Monday the fourteenth day of August instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the said day a line in this column of which he can bring up the first four words and cannot bring up the rest, and that the four words are *a man in a boat*, and that the remainder of the line is in his own left hand in this book and was written in this shed at about nine in the evening of that day by the man who is now looking at it, and that he cannot remember writing it and has entered that he cannot remember it and has not entered anything else about it.*

He sat with his own finger on the line for about two minutes.

"**Well,**" he said.

"**Well,**" said the shipwright.

"**I know what I did on the fourteenth of August.**"

"**Then say it.**"

"**I was on the face of the second seam from about eight in the morning until about half past two with a hand lead's line over the side and back four times, and the pump was on the boards and you had the slate, and gross on the board was fifteen and the yard's was three, and three went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about eleven people and I read every one of the three figures out and said which of them I would rather have had. And that is the first column and I could say every word of it standing in a market.**

"**And at about nine that night I came in and I sat down at this bench and I wrote a line in the second column, and I know that I wrote it because it is here in my own hand, and the four words at the start of it are *a man in a boat*, and after that it is gone.**"

Nessa Pike put her hand flat on the page and looked at the line for a while and then took her hand off it.

"**Then you are not going to be able to read that out,**" she said.

"**No.**

"**Then say the rest of it to me, because I am going to be asked about it and I would rather have it from you.**"

"**That is exactly why I am not going to read it out, and you know it is, and I have not got time to pretend I do not.**

"**On the tenth of September I wrote in this column that I did not know whether I was going to read the second column out in a room. On the sixteenth of September I did not read it, and I wrote in this column that I did not know whether the not-reading was a rule or an answer and that those are not the same thing. And I wrote in this column on the twenty-ninth that a man can be checked for what he said in a room and cannot be checked for what he did not say, and that the whole of the answer this party has about that column is that nobody asked.

"**And now it is the second of October and there is going to be a sitting on Friday the sixteenth of this month in the week following the twelfth, and the whole of what I have to bring is a book, and in about six days a man is going to open that book in a room and go into it, and the third column is the boy's and it is not going to be read out because it is not filled, and the second column is mine and I am the only one who can read it and I cannot read one line of five.

"**And I would like it noticed that a man who cannot read his own second column out in a room is not in a worse position than a man who can, and that I have entered that in this column twice and I have not believed it either time.**"

The lamp was lit by about half past four. The tide made about a quarter of an inch in the sill and neither of them looked at it, and the far bench at the end of the shed was empty and had been empty at nine that morning and was empty at ten that night, which was two days this party has not put in the book since the seventh of September, and the reason it was put in on the seventh of September was a woman in a room saying a thing about a plate.

"**Now I am going to say the thing I have been not-saying since four o'clock,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and it is one question and you are not going to like it.**

"**I can read that line for you. I cannot tell you what it means. I do not know what happened on the fourteenth of August, I was on the boards with a slate, and I have never read the second column in this shed out loud, and I am not going to start on a Saturday night.

"**And that is not a refusal. That is the whole of what this column is, and if I read it out to you in a shed on a Saturday night to be kind then in about four years there is going to be a page in a city that says a woman of this harbor read a man's second column to him at his own table, and that is the end of it as a column and it will have been the person who was in the room that made it a column instead of the man who wrote in it.**"

"**Then it stays shut.**

"**And I am not going to say that that is a rule, because I wrote in this book on the tenth of September that I did not know whether the not-reading was a rule or an answer, and I am not going to improve on a line I wrote ten days ago because it has got uncomfortable.**"

"**Good,**" said Nessa Pike. "**Now sit down and look at the other one.**"

He looked at the other one.

*Second column, entered on Sunday the fourth day of October instant at about nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the third day of October instant that he was going to tell Mara Venn that he had written down in the second column, on the eighth day of September instant, a sentence she told him on the seventh day of August not to write down, and that he has entered that decision with the date and the hour against it, and that he did not tell her on the third, and that the reason he did not tell her is not a reason against the telling and is this: that on Saturday the third instant he sat down and read the whole of this book's first column and could not bring up one line of the second, and that a man who has found out this month that he cannot remember what he wrote in his own hand in his own book is not a man who is going to be able to tell a woman on a mole's steps that he kept something back from her for six weeks and be believed about it or be forgiven for it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man can be checked for what he did and cannot be checked for what he was going to do, and that the four lines above the named line of the twentieth day of July past are still four and are still nameless and are not struck out, and that a request to strike one out of this column is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

"**Read that back to me,**" he said, "**and then I am going to tell you the rest of the reason and you are not going to like that either.**"

She read it back. She read it the way she reads everything, from the top of the page, and she read the words *a man who has found out this month that he cannot remember what he wrote in his own hand* twice and did not look up.

"**Then that is the reason,**" she said, "**and the rest of it is this, and it is not going to be nicer for being said out loud.

"**If you tell her that you had it for six weeks, on a mole, in the first ten seconds, then everything you say after that is a thing she has to weigh, including the true things, and she will weigh them, and she will be right to. And the sentence she told you not to write down is going to be in your own book with a reason against it and no name on it, and it is the only page in four volumes where there is a thing of hers with no name on it, and that is either the finest thing in this book or the worst thing in it, and nobody in this harbor will ever be able to tell you which, and I am not going to be the person who settles it for you in a shed.

"**You are not going to ask her to be the woman who checks your page. I know you the way I know a board. You have had nothing but your own two columns since May and you have got so used to having nothing to put a thing against that you are about to go to a woman on a mole and ask her to hold one end of it.**"

"**Yes,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Then write that down, and put a reason against it, and date it, and do not go up that hill and ask her for anything at all in October.**"

He wrote it down. It took about eleven seconds and the read-back took longer.

*First column, entered on Sunday the fourth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that this party did not go to the harbor authority of this harbor on the Monday or the Tuesday instant, or on any day this month, and did not ask that office for anything, and has entered why, and the entry of the twelfth of September instant on the same thing stands and is not being entered twice, and the second season of the writ of the first of May instant is a named absence and nothing in this month touches it, grants it, refuses it or deals with it; and that this party considered on the Sunday instant whether to go to that office for one thing, being a record of the harbor's own ground for the week of the eighth of August instant, and did not go, and has entered why, which is that the ninth acknowledgement in that office's ledger is the last entry there is in it and a tenth may not be entered, and that the price of an instrument of that office which puts a person or a boat or a lead inside a closure falls on a man and not on that office, and that the price of the ninth fell on a man of the first lay out of a Monday and has not been paid and is not going to be thanked for, and that a yard which has been told in the open air by the officer of that office that his ledger is spent does not go back up the same steps with the same request; and that this is entered as a decision and is not a virtue, and no person in this yard is going to thank this party for it and this party is not asking.*

*And that the third column of this book was not filled on Saturday the third or on Sunday the fourth instant, and has not been filled since the first day of August, and that this party has not appointed anybody and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Sunday night, and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column; and that the two objects of nobody's were on the crown of that mole about a hundred and thirty-eight and about a hundred and thirty-nine days, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and that neither of them moved on either day and that this party went past both of them on the Sunday morning at about ten and touched neither.*

*And that the far bench at the end of that shed was empty on Saturday the third and on Sunday the fourth instant at nine in the morning and at ten at night on both days, and that this party entered that, and entered that the last time the far bench was empty for two days together was in September, and that this party has not asked why and is not going to, and that neither brother has said why and neither brother is going to.*

*And that the number sixty-two, being the number of a leaf of the file of the ASTER GANNET at the head of a copying job sheet of a woman of this harbor, entered in the first column on Thursday the first instant, is in the first column and not the second, and that the reason against that line is on that line and is the reason of the woman who brought it; and that the holder entered on the Sunday instant that the other five lines of the second column cannot be got by anybody, including him, and that there is no reason against those five lines that he can bring up, and that a second column wants a line and a reason and that he has got five lines and no reasons he can find, and that this is entered against himself with the hour against it and is not going to be softened and is not going to be put in the second column.*

**No money came into this yard on the Saturday and none on the Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and a mark and twelve shillings is in the bag, and that is three hundred and four pence, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the sixth of October and is the third Tuesday in a row that one has fallen due, and this party entered on the twelfth of September that it does not know what it is going to write on the sixth and has not improved on that entry since.**

**Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, three counts unmoved. Five marks of gland standing unpaid and useless. Nothing in a tin. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat to this harbor's bellwright, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor with her name on the line at her own asking. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and nobody has written to him and nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October. Twenty lines and eighty marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, fifteen envelopes on a bench, and two weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the eight-clause plan of the seventeenth of January is refused in a record with a date on it, and this party has not written a better letter and is not going to, and whatever closes that note will close it in an office and not in a shed. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out and is not going to go out inside four years. The cistern behind the engine house was charged by strangers at no figure on the first instant, and this party did not walk it, and it has held since the nineteenth of June.**
