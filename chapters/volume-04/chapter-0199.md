# Chapter 199

*Two Questions in a Market, and a Note That Is Not Re-Written*

Wednesday the second and Thursday the third of December, at the fish market, at the head of this dock, and in the shed.

**On Wednesday the second of December, in the market, with the boards down and about forty people in it, a man of the first lay asked this yard two questions in about twenty minutes, and he said at the start of it that he was the one asking, and that he had said in the month of October that he was not going to be talked about in a market, and that this was him doing the other thing and that the two are not the same.**

Wil Tranter, fifty-one, of the first lay, master of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons of his own, who came up this hill on the sixth day of October with a letter in a coat pocket and did not want his name on a page, and who has had it himself inside a month off a man out of a city.

"**First one, and it is short,**" he said. "**That form of the office, read out on Tuesday by a woman at a penny a sheet, says a line of a manifest was altered and does not say by whose hand. I am not asking whose hand. I am not asking who ordered it and I am not going to be asking anybody that in a market.

"**An alteration is not a thing that happens by itself. Somebody agreed to a thing, at some point, and I want to know which office agreed to it. And I am asking you and not the office, because an office will give me a form and a form will give me the same four paragraphs, and you put a page up on the fifteenth of August with the place of every copy of it on the face of it, and that is the only reason anybody in this harbor is talking about a manifest at all.**"

"**Then I am going to say no, and I am going to give you the whole of the reason in about four minutes, and you are not going to like it and I am not going to improve on it while you are standing there.**

"**The reason is a woman. Not her name, and not that she is in this harbor, and not that she knows anything about your question. The only person on this coast who can tell a man what a form of that office says is a woman of no office who kept a copying counter in a room off the fish market in this town until the twelfth day of October, and she is four days up this coast, and this party has written to her once in four months, and that letter went on the sixteenth of November and it is in the first column of this yard's own book with the date on it, and this party is not going up this hill tomorrow with a second errand.

"**And the reason this party is not going to do that is that this party was refused in a room open to anybody in this town in eleven people's hearing on the twenty-ninth of September, and a party which has been refused once and then comes back with a second errand is a party which has decided that a person is a door, and she told that room she was not a door, and this yard wrote that down and has not taken it out of it and is not going to.

"**And I am not going to stand in a market and say that is the whole of it, because it is not the whole of it. The other half is that on the sixth of May this yard entered in its own book that it does not put a name on a thing it cannot check, and a man of the first lay of about fifty-five stood at a wall outside this market office on the fifth of November and asked this yard for a name and was refused in about four minutes, and he was right to ask and we said no for the same reason, and it is going to be the same reason until one of us is in a room we cannot check out of.

"**So: no. Not to you, not to this market, and not to a man in a city who puts it to us in a form.**"

"**That is a no with a reason in it,**" said Wil Tranter, "**and it is the first no anybody in this market has heard that was not dressed up as a principle. I will take it.**"

"**And now the second one, and it is the one I actually came for, and I am going to say it in a market because a market is the only place on this coast where a question has to be answered in front of people.

"**Your form says a line of a manifest was altered. It does not say by whose hand. And an alteration is not a thing that happens by itself, and you have told me you are not going to tell me which office agreed to it, and I am not going to argue with you about that, because I have been on a page myself and I took my name off it and I know exactly what a page does to a name.

"**But here is what I have not been able to get past, and I have been carrying it since Monday morning.

"**This country has had an office keeping records for three hundred years. Every office that keeps records that long has had to decide, at some point, what it was allowed to lose. That is not an accusation, that is arithmetic. You cannot keep everything for three centuries. Somebody, in some room, in some year nobody is alive in, decided what this coast was going to be without.

"**And I am asking what they thought they were guarding against. I am not asking for a document. I have never asked you for a document in my life and I am not going to start on a Wednesday. I am asking this yard, in a market, what an office is for.**"

Calder Marr did not answer for about ten seconds and about forty people waited.

"**That is the second time a man has asked this yard that in a market in this harbor this year, and I am not going to stand here and tell you it is a different question. It is the same question with a different man holding it, and the answer has not moved and it is not going to.

"**The answer is that this yard does not know. This yard said that out loud in this market on the thirteenth day of August last in about forty people's hearing, and it is not going to say it a second time about the same thing, and anybody in this market who wants to know what the answer was then can go and read the sheet of the fifteenth of August and the reason is on the face of it in about nine words.

"**And the reason it has not moved is the reason I have just given you, and I am not going to say it again in a different way in the same street.

"**And I am going to add one thing, out of our own book and not out of a hole, because you would find it anyway and I would rather you had it from me.

"**There is a second-column line in that book. It is a fact and it has no name in it. It says that a line of places kept by more than one community, for longer than any book in that office goes back, cannot have been taken out of those communities' hands without somebody having had to decide to do it. There is a reason against that line and the reason does not go into a record and it is not going into this street either, and I am not going to say the reason.

"**That is the whole of what this yard has. It is a fact, it is on a wall, it names nobody, and it says nothing whatever about what anybody thought they were protecting. And I am not going to stand in a market and call a fact an answer.**"

Nobody in that market said anything about it, which is what about forty people say about a man who has told them the exact size of what he does not know.

---

**And at about half past eleven on the same Wednesday, the same market, a man of the third lay asked one more question and it was a different one and it was not a hard one.**

Denzil Hare, of the third lay, about fifty, with a boat of his own and no crew of his own since the spring.

"**Is the case coming up.**"

"**It is at about five and a half fathoms, inside four hundred yards off the outer marks,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it is not on this office's schedule and it is not on that office's schedule and it is not on anybody's schedule, and I have not got an instrument that reaches that water and I have not got money that reaches that water and neither has anybody else on this coast whose work it is.

"**And four people own it and they are alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and they wrote a line themselves and signed it, and I am not going to tell you in a market what the line says, and I have not asked them to take it off and I am not going to ask twice.**"

"**I did not expect you to tell me,**" said Denzil Hare. "**I have a boat and no crew and I am not the man who is going to say a thing about a weir. I asked because a man of the first lay has been standing about telling people that a yard is going to raise five bells out of the four hundred yards this winter, and I would rather somebody said the words out loud that it is not on anybody's book.**"

"**It is not on anybody's book,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it was never on mine, and I am not going to be a man who puts it on one in December to look busy.**"

---

**On Thursday the third of December, at the head of this dock, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, and this being a rota day, at about ten in the morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know and this party did not walk it, and this party has walked none of them, and the walk is nil and stays nil.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the Thursday from about ten until about four, and was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and he was not at that bench on the Wednesday at any hour.**

**And at about half past four on the Thursday afternoon, in the same street, the holder of the writ of the first of May said out loud, in about eleven people's hearing, that the chandler's note is not re-written, and why, and that this yard has now said that three times in a market and is not going to say it a fourth time.**

"**Thirty-one on it. Not re-written. Twenty-two weeks in his own count, thirteen in ours, and eleven is the last figure anybody ever wrote in that page, and none of the three has moved since the fourth of January and none of them is moved by anything a yard does with a pencil.

"**And the reason is the reason of the third of May and the fifteenth of August and it has not got better: re-writing a note with nothing in the bag is not a payment. It is a figure. And I would rather owe a man thirty-one on a page he has not re-written than owe him nineteen on a page we did together in a shop.**"

Nobody in that street said anything, which is what about eleven people say about a note.

*First column, entered on Thursday the third day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on Wednesday the second instant, in the market in this harbor, with the boards down and about forty people in it, a man of the first lay, who gave his name as Wil Tranter and who is fifty-one years old and is master of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons of his own, asked this party two questions, and said at the start that he was the one asking and that in the month of October last he had said he was not going to be talked about in a market and that this was him doing the other thing; and that the first question was which office agreed to an alteration of a line of a manifest, and that he said he was not asking whose hand wrote it and not asking who ordered it; and that the holder of the writ of the first of May refused that question in the market in about four minutes and gave the whole of the reason, and that the reason is a woman of no office who kept a copying counter in a room off the fish market in this town until the twelfth day of October past, that this party has written to her once in four months and that the said letter went on the sixteenth day of November instant and is in this book's first column with the date on it, and that this party is not going up this hill with a second errand; and that the reason this party is not going to do that is that it was refused in a room open to anybody in this town in eleven people's hearing on the twenty-ninth day of September past, and that a party which has been refused once and then comes back with a second errand is a party which has decided that a person is a door, and that she told that room she was not a door, and that this yard wrote that down and has not taken it out of it; and that the holder said that the other half of the reason is that on the sixth day of May last this yard entered in its own book that it does not put a name on a thing it cannot check, and that a man of the first lay of about fifty-five asked this yard for a name at a wall outside the market office of this town on the fifth day of November past and was refused in about four minutes, and that he was right to ask and that the said refusal is in this book and stands; and that the holder said in the market, no, not to the said man, not to that market, and not to a man in a city who puts it to this party in a form; and that the said man said that that was a no with a reason in it and that it was the first no anybody in that market had heard that was not dressed up as a principle, and that he said he would take it.*

*And that the said man then asked a second question in the said market, and said he was asking it in a market because a market is the only place on this coast where a question has to be answered in front of people; and that the said question was what an office that has kept records for three hundred years thought it was guarding against when it decided what it was allowed to lose, and that he said he was not asking for a document and had never asked this party for one; and that the holder answered in the market that this yard does not know, and that this yard said the same out loud in the said market on the thirteenth day of August last in about forty people's hearing, and that it is not going to say it a second time about the same thing, and that the sheet of the fifteenth of August past carries the answer and the reason on the face of it in about nine words; and that the holder said the reason it has not moved is the reason he had given the said man about four minutes earlier and that he was not going to say it again in a different way in the same street; and that the holder then said, out of this yard's own book and not out of a hole, that there is a second-column line in that book, being a fact with no name in it, which says that a line of places kept by more than one community, for longer than any book in that office goes back, cannot have been taken out of those communities' hands without somebody having had to decide to do it, and that there is a reason against that line and that the reason does not go into a record and was not said in the market; and that the holder said that that is the whole of what this yard has, that it is a fact, that it is on a wall, that it names nobody and that it says nothing whatever about what anybody thought they were protecting, and that he was not going to call a fact an answer in a market; and that about forty people in that market said nothing.*

*And that at about half past eleven on the said Wednesday, in the same market, a man of the third lay, who gave his name as Denzil Hare and who is about fifty and has a boat of his own and no crew of his own since the spring, asked this party whether the case of five bells is coming up; and that the holder said that it is at about five and a half fathoms inside four hundred yards off the outer marks and that it is not on the schedule of this harbor authority, nor of the circuit office, nor of anybody's, and that this yard has no instrument that reaches that water and no money that reaches that water; and that four people own it and are alive in this town with no boat and no wages and about nine marks between them, and that they wrote a line themselves and signed it, and that the holder was not going to say in a market what the line says and had not asked to have it taken off and is not going to ask twice; and that the said man said he had not expected to be told, and that he asked because a man of the first lay has been standing about in that market telling people that a yard is going to raise five bells out of the four hundred yards this winter, and that he would rather somebody said out loud that it is not on anybody's book.*

*And that on Thursday the third instant at about half past four in the afternoon, at the head of this dock, with the market gone in and about eleven people in the street, the holder of the writ of the first of May said out loud that the chandler's note is not re-written, and that the figure on it is thirty-one, and that the creditor's count of weeks is twenty-two, the yard's count is thirteen, and eleven is the last figure ever written in that page, and that none of the three has moved since the fourth of January and none of them is moved by anything a yard does with a pencil; and that the reason is the reason of the third of May and the fifteenth of August past and has not got better, which is that re-writing a note with nothing in the bag is not a payment but a figure, and that he would rather owe a man thirty-one on a page he has not re-written than owe him nineteen on a page the two of them did together in a shop; and that nobody in that street said anything.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Thursday the third day of December instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a question was asked in a market in this harbor on the second day of December instant, in about forty people's hearing, as to what an office in this country that has kept records for three hundred years thought it was guarding against at the point at which it decided what it was allowed to lose, and that this yard did not answer it and has entered why; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that the answer to a question about what an office is for is not a fact about a mole, and that what a man in a city is going to do with a question that was asked in a market and not answered in a market is not checkable from a mole either, and that this yard has not been given an answer to this question in four volumes and has not been shown a document in this harbor or in any other that would answer it, and does not know whether such a document exists; and that the question is entered here and not answered, and is not going to be answered by this yard, and that a question in a market that is not answered is a different animal from a question that is never asked, and this yard has the second of those in its own house and knows the difference.*

*And that the holder entered on the said day that a man of the first lay asked twice in a market and was refused twice with the same reason, and that the second refusal was in about ten seconds and the first was in about four minutes, and that the reason both of them is a person of no office and not an office, and that the yard entered in the first column above that it is not going to put a name on a thing it cannot check and that a refusal with a person at the end of it is the same refusal as a refusal with an office at the end of it and that the yard knows that and is not going to improve on it; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether a refusal in a market makes a person safer or only makes a person harder to reach is not checkable from a mole, and that four people of this coast who wrote a line in the month of August are the only people in this harbor who could answer it and are not going to be asked.*

**No money came into this yard on the Wednesday and none on the Thursday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and one mark and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, which is two hundred and thirty-seven pence. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright and it is not paid. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month, no digit of anything he said on the second of July is printed, the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots stand, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand, and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the whole of the answer that came up this hill on the twenty-eighth instant is that it is not paid and that she is not going to be asked again this winter. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, six weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November, the seventeenth of November and the first of December instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and seventy-three days up the crown of this mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved.
