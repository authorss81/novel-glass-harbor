# Chapter 167

*Thirty Marks, and Four Hundred Feet of a Woman's Warp*

Saturday the twenty-third and Sunday the twenty-fourth of September, in the shed, in a doorway, and at the head of the dock.

**There is a piece of paper folded in two in the back of the writ of salvage working granted at this harbor on the first of May, and it has been there since June, and it comes out about once a month, and it came out on Saturday the twenty-third of September because a bag with twelve shillings and fourpence in it and a wage of four marks on Tuesday is the only kind of afternoon that puts a man's hand on a thing he has not touched since the third of May.**

The front of it is a paper of somebody else's. The back of it is nine sentences in Calder Marr's own left hand, and the nine sentences are a refusal, and the refusal is of thirty marks in coin for a license to go and look at a road.

**He did not read it out as a document. He read it out the way a man reads out a thing he wrote a long time ago and has had to look at since, which is slowly, and twice, because it is nine sentences and it is in his own hand and he wanted to know whether it still said what he thought it said.**

It did. It said what it said. It had been refused in June, and the refusal stands and it is not a decision this yard is going to take up again, and the whole of what refusing it cost this yard is on a wall in about sixty boats and in a column, and neither of those is a bill, and the thirty marks are not in anybody's bag and were never in it and are not owed to anybody and cannot be applied to anything.

"**And I want the whole of that said out loud once,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and then I want it put away, because you have taken it out and I can see what your face is doing and I have seen that face twice this month and both times it was in a shed at nine at night and neither time it was about money.

"**You have not been thinking about the thirty marks. You have been thinking about the sentence. And I have read the same sheet twice and the sentence is nine words long and you are not going to get a better one out of it in September than you got in June.**"

"**No.**

"**Then say the number, because the number is what is in the drawer and the sentence is what is on the paper, and if we are going to have this out we are going to have the number out and not the sentence.**"

---

**And here is the number, and it is the only reason the thirty marks has ever been on this yard's books at all, and it is not a figure anybody in this shed made up.**

About four hundred feet of a woman's own warp came up off the bottom of this harbor on Friday the twenty-eighth of July, at a price of twenty-six marks and a day, and the man who took it was a man of Ashtide, and she was paid by him, and the day was his and the price was his and both of those are arrangements of another crew's and not this yard's.

**And the same four hundred feet of warp is the thing the thirty marks would have paid for.**

Nobody said anything for about a minute, and out on the boards a man was caulking something and the sound of it went on.

"**So that is the arithmetic,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I have never once said it out loud and I am saying it now because we are going to be poor for a long time and there is exactly one way to be poor well and that is to know what the thing you turned down was for.

"**Thirty marks refused in June would have paid for about four hundred feet of Sella Brigg's own warp on the bottom of this harbor, and Sella Brigg's warp came up on the twenty-eighth of July for twenty-six marks and a day off a man of Ashtide, and she was paid by him, and this yard owes her eight shillings and tenpence of that, and it has not paid it and it has not paid a shilling of it, and that figure has not moved since the twenty-eighth of July and it is not going to move this month.

"**And the refusal is a refusal and it stands and it is not a decision this yard is going to take up again. I am not going to argue it. I want to say one thing about it and then I am going to put it away.

"**We did not refuse thirty marks for the road. We refused thirty marks, and the road was the reason they were on the table, and there is a man in a city printing two figures that cannot check each other and a man of fifty-four sounding the same bar four times in nineteen years, and none of that is settled, and it is not going to be settled by us, and I have not read that sheet in a shed in twenty-four days and I am not going to read it again.

"**What the thirty marks were for is four hundred feet of a woman's warp, and it is at the bottom of this harbor now with a man of Ashtide's money on it, and she is paid, and we owe her eight shillings and tenpence, and the whole of what that refusal has cost this yard in nine weeks is in a column and a figure and it is not going to be in a column and a figure in October either. It is a cost this yard is still carrying. That is all a refusal is, once it is done. It is a thing you are carrying.**"

He put it back in the back of the writ and folded the writ in half and put it in the bag, and the bag is the crew's book bag and it has a mark and twelve shillings and fourpence's worth of nothing in it in the sense that matters, and the book in it is forty-seven weeks of this yard's own weather and its own shame and is going into a file in a city on Saturday week.

---

**And on Sunday the twenty-fourth, at about half past ten in the morning, Sella Brigg came up the hill, and she did not ask for anything, and she came because she had heard something in a market and had come to see whether it was true.**

She is a woman of this harbor and she is a weaver of warp on this coast and about four hundred feet of her own work came up out of this harbor in July off a man of Ashtide's boat at his own price, and she has not asked this yard for a shilling of it and has not asked anybody else either, and she had come up the hill on the Sunday to say one thing and she said it in a doorway in about four minutes.

"**There is a man in the market saying that this yard owes a woman eight shillings and tenpence and has not paid it since July, and he is saying my name and he is saying it loud, because he wants somebody to be angry at somebody in a market and I am the nearest name to hand.

"**And I have come up here to say three things and to ask for nothing.

"**One. It is true. I am owed that and you have not paid it and you are not going to pay it this month and I would rather you did not try to pay me a shilling of it to stop a man talking, because a man in a market who is owed nothing stops talking and a man in a market who is owed something is a man somebody will be angry at, and I would rather he was angry at you than at me.

"**Two. I am not going to be a name on your wall. I have read that wall four times. There is my warp on it in a block of type and there is no name on it and that is the correct way for my warp to be on it, and if a name goes on it I will take my warp off it in front of people, and I would rather say that to you on a Sunday than find it out on a Tuesday.

"**Three. I am not going to ask you to say anything about me in a market. There is a man in a market saying it and there is going to be one in October, and I have decided that the two of those are a matter between me and him and not between me and a yard that is being written about in a city at the same time as I am.

"**And that is all. I have not come up here for the eight shillings and tenpence. If I had come up here for the eight shillings and tenpence I would have come on a Tuesday when there is a market and I would have stood on the boards and said it out loud, and I did not, and you can ask the man at the market office on Friday morning whether I was in there or not, because I was in there, and I did not say it.**"

---

"**Then here is what goes in the book,**" said Calder Marr, "**and it goes in the first column and it goes in with your name on it, and I want to know that you want that.**

"**I want my name on the figure and not on the wall. Yes.**"

"**Then it goes in.**

"**And the reason it goes in the first column is that a man in this market can come up this hill and ask whether that figure has moved and can go and look, and there is no name of any kind in it but yours.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth of September instant at about eleven in the morning in this shed, at the request of the woman named: that eight shillings and tenpence is owing by this party to Sella Brigg of this harbor, undiminished, unpaid, and not reduced by any part of the figure of twenty-six marks and a day at which about four hundred feet of her own warp was taken off the bottom of this harbor on Friday the twenty-eighth of July past by a man of Ashtide, she being paid by him; and that this party has not paid it, will not pay a shilling of it, and will not pay a shilling of it in order to stop a man in a market saying her name; and that the said woman came up this hill on the twenty-fourth instant at about half past ten in the morning and said, unasked and in a doorway, that she is not going to be a name on this party's public sheet, that if a name goes on that sheet she will take her own warp off it in front of people, and that she is not asking this party to say anything about her in a market; and that this party entered the whole of it with the hour against it because a woman who says what she is owed and what she is not asking for has said two checkable things and the second one is the one that costs.*

*And that this party entered on the twenty-third instant, in the same column and with the same hour against it, that about four hundred feet of that warp is the thing that thirty marks refused in the month of June would have paid for, and that the refusal stands and is not to be taken up again, and that a refusal once made is a cost this yard carries and is not a decision it re-opens in a shed on a Saturday night; and that the thirty marks are not owed to anybody, are not in anybody's bag, and cannot be applied to anything.*

**No money moved on the Saturday and none on the Sunday. Twelve shillings and fourpence in the bag, and a wage of four on Tuesday the twenty-sixth. Thirty-one on the chandler's note, not re-written, three counts of weeks unmoved since the fourth of January. Five marks of compressor's gland standing on it, unpaid since the nineteenth of May, and of no use to this yard whatever because the thing after the gland is the hose. Eight marks and a day of a boat standing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unpaid and unargued, and the chest in that shed was not opened on either of these two days and is not going to be opened in a shed on a Sunday. Forty marks standing to a boy of twenty-two, whole. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the party has not offered her six marks and has not been asked to and has entered that it is going to say no if it ever is.**

**The cistern is charged on a rota and this party has not walked it since the third of August. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list in a city. And Neave Tarrant, nine winters, out since the twenty-eighth of February, is not found, and there is a line in that book saying there is nothing in it, and this party has not gone and is not going to.**

**And on Sunday the twenty-fourth Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about five with his back to the room, and he was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and it is one line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week.**

**And at about nine that night the ship's hand read the whole of the two days back from the top of the bench to the bottom and the holder read it back after her, and there was nobody else in that shed, and the second column was filled about half an hour later and it carries a reason against the line and no name in it, and it is the shortest line anybody in this shed has written since the second of May, and it is as follows.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth of September instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that this party is going to be poorer on the last day of this month than it was on the first, and that it entered that on the second day of this month when it could not pay a wage, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man can check what a yard has in its bag on any day of a week and cannot check what a harbor is worth, and that this party has got a figure for the money and has not got a figure for the other thing and is not going to invent one, and that the four people of the Sill have about nine marks between them and no boat and they have not asked this party for anything and this party has not offered them anything and has entered that it would like to and has had nothing, and that the four of them hold a limit in their own words with four names on it and the holder of it asked for it to be lifted on the tenth of August and could not lift it and did not ask twice and is not going to.*
