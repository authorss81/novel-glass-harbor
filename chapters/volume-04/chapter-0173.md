# Chapter 173

*The Twenty-Second Wall, and a Wage That Does Not Go In*

Monday the fifth and Tuesday the sixth of October, at the mole's steps, at the head of the dock, and in the shed.

**The wall behind the reeve's bench came down at eight on Monday morning and it came down bare, and that was twenty-two Mondays since the first of May, and the clerk kept his own page and did not mention it, and no sitting has been held in that room since the sixteenth of September and the next one is to be appointed for the week following the twelfth of October.**

**And at about ten on the Monday morning the cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by four men this yard does not know, and this party did not walk it, and that is the second rota day of this month and the fifteenth since the third of August, and this party has walked none of the fifteen.**

Alden Marr was at the far bench from about ten until about four on the Monday with his back to the room and the same net in his hands, and he was not asked for anything and asked nothing, and Calder Marr did not go over to him, and it is one line in the first column and either brother can check it on any day of a week.

---

**And on Tuesday the sixth of October the wage of four marks did not go in, and the envelope was not made, and it is the third Tuesday in a row on which one has fallen due and has not been paid.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the sixth of October instant at about four in the afternoon in this shed: that the wage of four marks for the week ending on Tuesday the sixth day of October instant was not paid and no envelope was made, that the bag held a mark and twelve shillings in it on the morning of that day, that the column of this book stands at twenty-one lines and eighty-four marks with the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, and that this is the third Tuesday in a row on which a wage has fallen due and has not been paid; and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is now THREE, being the week of Tuesday the fifteenth day of August past, the week of Tuesday the fifth day of September past and the week of Tuesday the sixth day of October instant, and that the three figures are three different figures and none of them is either of the others, and that all three are entered in the same breath in this column and not one after the other, because a yard which is asked how many weeks it has paid has to say all three at once rather than the one that is easier; and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is fifteen and has not moved since the twenty-sixth of September, and that an envelope that is not made is not a wage and this column is not the wage; and that the reason against the line is that a debt is a debt and a wage is a wage and a man is not paid a shilling of it and told it is a payment, and that this party entered it against itself on the twelfth of September instant with the hour against it rather than wait for a day it does not know is coming, and has entered it again today because it came.*

*And that on Friday the second day of October instant this party paid four pence at a counter in this town for the postage of a letter of its own into the ordinary post, and that the said four pence is not the seven pence owing to this party's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August past out of her own pocket; and that those are two different figures and neither of them is the other, and that the seven pence stands unpaid, and is not being paid out of the bag this month, and is not going to be paid out of the bag out of anything that has come into this yard this month, and that the shipwright said on the second instant, in front of two people, that if the two figures are ever put on one line in this book she will take the book off the bench.*

---

**And at about half past two on the Tuesday afternoon a man of fifty-one came up the hill with a letter in a coat pocket and had read it twice on the boat coming up and had not sent this party one word of it, and said so before he said anything else.**

Wil Tranter, of the first lay, master of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons of his own.

"**I wrote to him on the Friday night and I wrote to him again on Sunday night and I have brought you the second one and I have not sent you either of them, and I am going to tell you why I have not sent them before I tell you what is in it, because otherwise you will think I have been keeping something from you and I have only been keeping my own name out of it.**

"**And I have come up here before anybody else hears it, and I am not here to be thanked, and I am here because a man in a city has got four words of yours he did not write and my name would have gone under the line next to them, and I would rather you had it from me in your own doorway than have it in a paper in October with my name at the foot of it.**"

He took it out and put it on the bench face up and did not give it away and did not ask anybody to touch it.

> *Sir, —*
>
> *I have your second letter. I am in your debt for the trouble of it and I will come to the point at once because you have been plain with me and I have no figure to beat about with.*
>
> *You ask whether I will set my word under your finding that the ground of this harbor is barred to two and three-quarter fathoms at the middle of a spring tide.*
>
> *I will not, and I am going to give you the reason in full so that you are not left to guess at one.*
>
> *I have crossed that bar on twenty-two springs and I have put a hundred and ninety tons on it once, in my second year, and I came off. I know what that ground is like and I have not the least doubt in the world what you have found. But I did not make the figure and I cannot swear to a figure I did not make, and if I swear to it then in about four years there is a man in a city who can point at a line with my name under it and ask me a second question about a bar of gravel, and the second question is going to be about a mole, and the mole is not mine either.*
>
> *I have a boat and four men and I am fifty-one years old, and a man's name under a line about water is the only thing a man of the first lay has that a man of no lay does not have, and I am not going to spend it on somebody else's arithmetic.*
>
> *I am obliged to you for the trouble and I am sorry that the answer is no.*
>
> *I am, Sir, your obedient servant,*
> *Wil Tranter.*

Nessa Pike read it twice, standing, and then put her thumb on the last line of it and looked at the man.

"**You have written to a man in a city that you know the ground of this harbor, in a letter he is going to keep, in a hand he is going to be able to tell from any other hand on that coast.**"

"**I have.**

"**Do you know that.**"

"**I have had three days with it in my coat and I have known it the whole three days,**" said Wil Tranter, "**and I have not slept much and I am not sorry I came, and I have not come up here to be told I have been careful. I came up here because there is a paper going into a thing in a city in the week following the twelfth of October and I would rather you knew that the name is not on it because I took it off than because it was never near it.**"

"**Then say the other half of it,**" said Calder Marr, "**because there is one and you have been holding it since Saturday and you came up on the Friday and did not say it.**"

"**Then I will say the other half. Your letter came up the coast on Saturday and reached me on Monday morning and I read it in my own wheelhouse and it is four lines and it is in your own hand and it is the same four reasons you gave me in your doorway on the twenty-fifth of September, and one of them is the reason I have just given a man in a city in my own hand and put my name to.

"**I have never had a refusal in writing off anybody in nineteen years of being a man who signs things, and I have had four reasons with it, and I did not know what to do with it and I have been not-doing anything with it for four days.**"

Nobody in that yard improved on that.

"**Now the thing I have come to say,**" said Wil Tranter, "**and then I am going to go down the hill and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be talked about in a market, and I have not said a word to you about which of the two figures is right and I am not going to, now or to him, ever, and that is not a favor to you. If I say it, I have said it.

"**Here is what you have got and what you have not got. You have got a surveyor in a city with a street and a number who wrote down four words you did not say, and who told you in his own letter that he had not got them from you. You have got me, saying in my own hand that I know what that ground is like, and you have got a document of mine that says a man of the first lay will not swear to a figure of a man of no lay.

"**And you cannot put either of them in front of the man. Not one page of it. Because a copy of a record is a thing nobody in this harbor can check, and a letter off a man of the first lay is a thing about a man of the first lay and not about a bar, and the four words are in a file in a city and the yard of Morrow Quay cannot get at the file and will not be able to get at the file, and there is no instrument in this town that would make anybody hand it over.**"

"**Then it has cost you three days and a night's sleep to give me a document I cannot use,**" said Calder Marr.

"**Yes,**" said Wil Tranter.

"**And I cannot pay you for the three days and there is no figure in this yard that would be the right figure for them.**"

"**I did not sell you the three days and I am not going to let you turn them into a bill. I have been up this hill three times this month and I have not been paid for one of them and I have not asked to be, and I am not asking now, and I would rather it was said out loud that you offered and that I would not have it, because in about four years a man in a market is going to say the yard of Morrow Quay sent a man of the first lay to a city with a question and the man of the first lay went up on his own account and refused to swear to it, and both of those are going to be true and only one of them is going to be the whole of it.**"

*First column, entered on Tuesday the sixth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on the said day a man of the first lay, who gave his name as Wil Tranter and who is fifty-one years old and is master of a coaster of about a hundred and ninety tons of his own, came up this hill at about half past two in the afternoon with a letter in a coat pocket, which he said he had written to a marine surveyor of a city on the Sunday night and had not sent to this party, and which this party read out in this yard and did not take; and that the said letter states that the said man will not set his word under the said surveyor's finding as to the ground of this harbor, and gives his reason in his own hand, and that the reason is that he did not make the figure and that a man's name under a line about water is the only thing a man of the first lay has that a man of no lay does not have; and that this party did not thank him, offered him nothing, and paid him nothing, and that the said man said in this yard that he is not going to be thanked and did not ask to be, and that both halves of that are entered here on the date and are checkable by anybody who comes up this hill.*

*And that this party did not ask him and did not say to him which of the two figures for the said bar is right, and that the said man did not say and has entered that he does not know which of them is right, and that this volume does not decide it and this party is not going to decide it in a shed; and that the said man said in this yard that the said surveyor has a street and a number and wrote down four words this party did not say and said in his own letter that he had not got them from this party, and that the said man's letter and the said surveyor's report are two documents about two men and that this party cannot put either of them in front of anybody, and has entered that and the reason against it.*

*And that the said man read the letter of the second day of October instant, which this party put into the ordinary post on that day, in his own wheelhouse on Monday the fifth instant, and that this party entered that on the second instant in advance, and that it was read, and that it is four lines and is in the holder's own hand and is the same four reasons given in a doorway on the twenty-fifth of September past.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Tuesday the sixth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the second day of September instant that a party which has published itself is not a party that gets to be sorry in a street and mean it, and that on the sixth day of October instant a man of fifty-one has refused in his own hand, in a letter to a city, to put his name under a line about water, and has brought the letter up this hill unasked, and that the consequence of it is that the entry in the said surveyor's report stands unaltered and four words of it are still words this yard did not say; and that the holder entered on the sixteenth day of September instant that a record in a city is in a file and that a clerk will not say what is in it and will not say why, and that this party did not ask him twice and has entered why; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man in a boat can check what a man of the first lay said on a mole and cannot check what a man of fifty-one will think about in three days in a coat pocket on the way up from a city, and that the whole of the difference between the two documents this yard has been given this month is that one of them this yard can put in a book and the other one this yard cannot put in front of anybody, and that a party which has published itself cannot be given the second kind by being careful.*

**No money came into this yard on the Monday and none on the Tuesday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and a mark and twelve shillings is in the bag, and three hundred and four pence is the whole of it, and a wage of four marks did not go out of it on the Tuesday into an envelope, and that is a figure in a column and not a figure in a hand, and the yard is two marks and twelve shillings short of a wage for the third Tuesday running and is going to be short of it on the thirteenth as well unless the ninth of October pays. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, its three counts unmoved since the fourth of January at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of gland on it, unpaid, useless. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright, unpaid, and that is a second week in a month in which this yard has spent money on the ordinary post and has not paid a woman of this yard seven pence. Eight marks and a day of a boat to the bellwright, unargued. Eight shillings and tenpence to a woman of this harbor. Forty marks to a boy of twenty-two and forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October. The third column has not been filled since the first of August and the head of it keeps the boy's hand. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty days up the crown, one tarpaulin with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The hose of the fire party of this harbor is dead until the spring. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by an officer this yard emptied, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. A man in a city has four words in a file that this yard never said and a man of the first lay has a letter in a coat pocket that this yard cannot use, and the day is the sixth of October and the sitting is on the sixteenth or thereabouts, and this yard is not represented at it and has entered that it will not be represented at it.**
