# Chapter 177

*A Wage That Goes In, and a Man Who Came Up the Hill Asking the Wrong Yard*

Tuesday the thirteenth and Wednesday the fourteenth of October, at the head of the dock, in the market office, in the yard, and in the shed.

**On Tuesday the thirteenth of October at about eleven in the morning the wage of four marks went in, into an envelope, in the holder's own left hand, with the week written on the outside of it, and the whole of it went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about eleven people and took about two minutes.**

Twenty-two lines and eighty-four marks in the column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there. Sixteen envelopes on a bench in the chest. Three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them, being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September and the sixth of October, entered in the same breath, and the three figures are three different figures and none of them is either of the others.

The reading at the head of the dock is not a reading of the book. It is the shipwright, in about two minutes, standing on the boards, saying the figures out: what the bag holds, what the column holds, how many weeks have fallen due without an envelope and which weeks those are, how many envelopes are on the bench, and that a wage of four marks has gone in and that two have not and that the fifteenth of August and the fifth of September are the two that have not and the sixth of October was the third, and that this yard has entered the third one against itself with the hour on it rather than wait for a day it does not know is coming.

Nobody said anything about the third. About nine people in the street said nothing at all, which is what about nine people say about a wage in a hard month, and one man of the third lay said *that is the third in a row* to nobody in particular, and the shipwright heard him and did not answer him.

A mark and twelve shillings was in the bag on the Friday. Six marks and twelve shillings was in it on the Friday after the day of work. **Two marks and twelve shillings is in it now**, and that is four hundred and sixty-four pence, and it is one hundred and seventy-six pence short of another wage.

---

**And at about ten on that same Tuesday morning, in the market office of Morrow Quay, which is open to anybody from nine, a man of about fifty came in with a letter in his hand and asked the room a question, and about nine people were in it, and the counting was going on behind the long table, and the press was at the back of the floor under its stone slab.**

He was of the first lay. He gave his name as Oswin Rede and he had a boat of his own and a letter that had come on the Saturday boat in an envelope with a street and a number on it and nothing else, and the letter asked him to explain a form.

"**I am not asking anybody to write anything,**" he said. "**I am asking whether anybody in this room can tell me what it says. There is a line on it in a woman's hand and the woman calls herself a tide conservator and says she examined a thing and says it is a worked glass and not a lump, and the letter wants to know what a lump is and what a worked glass is and whether a man can have both.**

"**And I have had it four days and there is nobody in this town to ask, and a woman of the second lay told me on Sunday that there was a person in this room on a Friday morning who would know and I have come on a Tuesday because I did not want to miss her.**"

Somebody at the back of the room said that the market office was open to anybody and that the person he was asking about had not been in it since the Friday before last and had said at the counter that she was not coming on Tuesdays or Fridays any more, and that the reason was her own and was not going to be given, and that anybody who wanted it could ask her and she would not answer that either.

Oswin Rede stood there for a moment.

"**Then I will come back on the Friday,**" he said.

Nobody in that room could tell him anything, and about nine people in that room heard a man say out loud that a thing he had paid for was being explained to a city by a woman he had never met and could not pay, and nothing whatever was done about it, and the counting went on behind the long table.

---

**And on Wednesday the fourteenth of October Oswin Rede came up this hill at about ten in the morning with the letter in his coat, and he had not been asked and had not been sent, and he came up this hill for the second time in a week.**

"**I asked your man in the doorway on Saturday morning whether anybody knew, and he said you would know and he would not say more than that and he said it like a man who is afraid of saying it, and I have not come up here to make anybody say anything. I have come up here because there is nobody else in this town.

"**And I want to say first, before anybody decides to be helpful, that I am not after your boat and I am not after a job and I am not after anything of yours, and that if I have got this wrong I would rather be told so on a step than be helped at by a man in a doorway.**"

"**Then you have got it right,**" said Calder Marr, "**and I am going to tell you the whole of what I know in about nine sentences and it is that I know nothing at all, and I am not going to pretend to you that I know less than I do in order to look better, and I am not going to pretend to you that I know more than I do in order to look worse.**

"**That form is not ours. There is no form in this yard's book and no form in this yard's hand and nothing that any of us has ever written has anything to do with it. That woman is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is owed forty marks first after food by the work of this harbor since the fifteenth of October, and I have not paid her a shilling of it and am not going to this month.

"**And I am not going to tell you where she is, and I am going to tell you why, and the why is about her and not about you.

"**This party asked her for one thing on the twenty-ninth of September in a room open to anybody in this town, in eleven people's hearing, and she priced it before she answered it and the answer was no. The price was not money and she gave it to me and I could not pay it and did not offer it. And a party which has been refused once and then tells a man in a doorway where the woman lives is a party which has decided that a person is a door, and she stood in that room and told me she was not a door, and I wrote it down and I have not taken it out.

"**You have got a letter in a coat that wants a thing answered that is not about your boat. And I am the wrong yard and I have told you so, and I would rather you go down that hill and be told the same thing by four other people than have me be useful at you once.**"

Oswin Rede stood in the doorway with the letter in his hand and did not put it away and did not open it again.

"**Then I will say what I was going to say, and then I will go down the hill,**" he said. "**I did not know who she was when I asked in that room. I know now, because the woman at the back told me, and because I have been up this hill twice in six days and both times I have been told the same thing by a man who did not have to say it.

"**And a city is paying my underwriter's man to make a list of what a yard of two and a half people put in a book, and I am on the other side of that from about four hundred people who are not in this town, and I have never met the man who is writing the list and I am not going to meet him. And I have not got money. I have got a boat and a wife and a boy of nineteen who is on the second lay and has had four weeks this year.

"**And I would like it noticed that I have come up here twice and both times I have been told no, and that I am not the man who is owed anything by this yard, and that nobody in this yard has asked me for a thing, and that I am going to go down this hill now and I am not going to be a man who was helped.**"

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fourteenth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on the morning of Tuesday the thirteenth instant a man of the first lay, who gave his name as Oswin Rede, and who is about fifty years old and has a boat of his own, came into the market office of this town with a letter from a city and asked the room, in about nine people's hearing, whether anybody could tell him what a form says; and that a person of no office who had been in that room on a Tuesday and a Friday morning was not in it on the said morning, and had said at the counter that she was not coming on Tuesdays or Fridays any more, and that the reason was her own and was not going to be given, and that anybody who wanted it could ask her and she would not answer that either; and that this party was not in that room and was not told of the said question on the said day and did not arrange anything and has entered that.*

*And that on the morning of Wednesday the fourteenth instant the said man came up this hill at about ten o'clock with the said letter in his coat, unasked and unsent, and that this party told him in about nine sentences in the doorway that the said form is not this yard's and is not in this yard's book and is not in this yard's hand, and that the said woman is not on this crew and is not of this yard and is owed forty marks first after food since the fifteenth of October, and that this party has not paid her a shilling of it; and that this party did not tell him where the said woman is, and entered why, and the reason is that this party was refused by that woman in a room open to anybody in this town in eleven people's hearing on the twenty-ninth day of September past, and that a party which has been refused once and then gives a man in a doorway a direction to a woman is a party which has decided that a person is a door; and that this party did not thank the said man and did not offer him anything and did not ask him for anything, and that the said man said in this yard that he is not owed anything by this party and that nobody in this yard has asked him for a thing and that he was not going to be a man who was helped.*

*And that on Tuesday the thirteenth instant at about eleven in the morning the wage of four marks went in into an envelope in the holder's own left hand with the week on the outside of it, and that the column stands at twenty-two lines and eighty-four marks with the fifth of March the oldest, and that the count of envelopes on the bench in that chest is sixteen, and that the number of weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them is three, being the fifteenth of August past, the fifth of September past and the sixth of October instant, entered in the same breath and not one after the other; and that the said man of the first lay was not thanked for the day of the ninth and is not thanked for anything, and that this party has entered that it is not going to thank him because a man who is thanked for a thing he would have done anyway learns to do things he would not.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Wednesday the fourteenth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder entered on the fourth day of October instant that he had entered a decision to tell that woman a thing and had not told her, and on the twelfth instant she came up this hill and he did not tell her, and on the fourteenth instant a man came up this hill and asked this party where she was and this party did not tell him, and that the reason the same thing has happened twice in ten days and a different person each time is that a party which has published itself has to be able to say out loud what it will not do, and that a party which cannot say what it will not do is a party whose silence is going to be filled in by somebody else, and that this party has not decided that it is owed that and has entered that it is not asking to be thanked for it.*

*And that the holder entered on the thirteenth day of September instant that a poor crew does not get an inquiry's costs waived, and that on the fourteenth instant he entered that a yard of two and a half people is now the only party in this harbor that a man can come up a hill to and be told no by, and that this is not a thing it did and is not going to be a thing it is proud of, and that the reason it is in this column and not the first is that the number of people in this harbor who can be told no by a yard is checkable at the head of a dock and the reason this yard is one of them is not checkable from a mole.*

---

**And on the Wednesday the yard worked about four hours and fifty minutes on the face of the second seam, landward of the third seam's mark, on a hirer's own account, with nobody in the water, and a hand lead's line over the side and back four times.**

**Gross on the board fifteen.** The eighth, two. The share, two. The victuals, two. A hired hand, three. A second hired hand, three. Twelve out of fifteen at the mole and the yard's being three, and the three went up the mole's steps in daylight in front of about nine people beside the five of the ninth, and no figure was argued, and the eighth and the share came off the top of the day rate the way they have come off the top of every day rate since the third of May.

**Five marks and twelve shillings in a bag**, and a wage of four goes out of it on Tuesday the twentieth, and this is the third wage out of two days of work in four weeks, and the fifth of September and the sixth of October are still unpaid and the fifteenth of August is still unpaid, and the note is not re-written and its three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven as they were written on the fourth of January. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid, useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and is not going to be sent for. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August, unpaid, and that is not the four pence of the second of October. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and that man was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure at her own asking and not on any wall. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two who cannot do the thing he was kept for, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and nobody has written to him and nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and the head of the third column keeps his hand and the column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and she is not coming to the market office of this town on a Tuesday or a Friday morning any more and nobody in this yard arranged that and nobody in this yard is going to un-arrange it. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and forty-five days up the crown, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The hose of the fire party of this harbor is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The cistern behind the engine house was charged by strangers at no figure on the first, the fifth, the eighth and twelfth of October and this party walked none of the four. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list being made up in a city by an officer this yard emptied, and this yard's four returns are in her hands, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet and the note against it is open. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and no occasion was entered this month and no measurement was taken, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved and nobody in this harbor has ever said why and this party is not going to be the first. And a working harbor is poorer on the fourteenth of October than it was on the first of it, and a yard of two and a half people is poorer with five marks and twelve shillings in a bag than it was with one mark and twelve shillings and fourpence, and both of those are this yard's own figures and neither of them has been argued with.**
