# Chapter 182

*Three Shillings and Sixpence, and What a Clerk Can Enter*

Saturday the twenty-fourth and Sunday the twenty-fifth of October, in a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, and in the shed.

**At about ten in the afternoon on Saturday the twenty-fourth of October a bonesetter came up this hill on foot and looked at an arm in a shed for about eleven minutes and charged three shillings and sixpence for it, and the three shillings and sixpence went onto her own counter out of a bag that held four marks and twelve shillings, and after it the bag held four marks and eight shillings and six pence, and that is seven hundred and forty-two pence.**

Hessa Barlow, forty-four, of this harbor, with a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, and a name that has been in this town on a painted board over that door since before either brother in this shed was born.

"**There is a crack in the bone about two inches above the wrist,**" she said. "**And there is nothing in the joint out of place, and I want you to understand what those two sentences are worth to you, because they are the whole of what I can do and they are the whole of what anybody can do.

"**The joint is all right. Everything you can do with a joint, you can go on doing. The bone is not a joint and it is not a tendon and it is not the nerve, and a bone that is cracked wants to be held in one position and kept in that position for six weeks so that it knits in the shape it is going to be, and if you let it go at four weeks because a pump feels all right, then at six weeks it is a forearm that is a thing you cannot put a pump handle on, and at six weeks you will not be able to put it on at all.

"**So it is a thing that is tight for six weeks. And after six weeks it is a month before that arm is a hand that does work, and after the month it is a month of work in that arm again before anybody in this harbor should be asked to hold a pump on a wharf with it. That is a quarter of the year gone out of the left side of a shipwright and there is nothing in the world I can do about any part of it.**"

"**I have understood,**" said Nessa Pike. "**And I would like it in the book in my own hand and not in his, and I would like it in the book tonight, and I would like the word *quarter* in it because a quarter is a figure and a month is not.**"

"**I will do that,**" said Hessa Barlow, "**and I will do it for nothing, because you told me on the boards yesterday which of the two it was before I had asked you, and because a woman who can do that is worth a great deal more to a yard than a quarter is.**

"**And I am not going to be thanked for it, and if anybody in this shed thanks me I am going to be annoyed, and I have been annoyed twice this year and one of the times it was a man of the first lay with a bad plate.**"

She strapped it. It took about four minutes and she talked the whole time, in the flat voice of a woman who has done this a great many times and does not enjoy it and does it anyway, and she said one thing at the end of it that went into the book.

"**Come back on the fourteenth day and that is the seventh of November, and I will not charge you for coming back on the fourteenth day, and I will not be thanked for it either, and that is a thing I am telling you now so that neither of you can say afterward that a bonesetter put a bill on a yard that had a bad Friday on it.**"

---

**And on Sunday the twenty-fifth of October the two of them did a thing in that shed that neither of them had thought to do in nine weeks, and it was to go through the second column and find out how much of it could go into a record.**

There are two ruled columns and a third one at the head of a page, and the second column is the one a man writes in when a thing cannot be checked off a mole, and the standing practice of this yard since the second of May is that there is a reason against every line of it, and that both columns stand and neither is scratched out, and that a request to strike a line out of either is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in the other.

They did it on the Sunday with the lamp lit and the tide making about three inches in the sill, and it took about an hour and forty minutes, and the ship's hand did the counting and the holder did the reading, and neither of them said anything clever.

"**Go on from the fourth of May,**" she said.

He went on from the fourth of May.

"**A line, a date, an hour, and a reason. That is the shape of every one of them and it has been the shape of every one of them since the second of May and I have never once asked a clerk about it, because there has never been a clerk in a room with this book in it until the sixteenth of October and on that day not one line of it was read.

"**Now. What is a line? A line is a thing a man said or did or could not do, and it has a day on it and an hour on it, and that is three things, and any clerk in any office on this coast can enter three things.

"**What is a reason? A reason is a paragraph, and a paragraph is long, and there is no column for it in a record, and the man who wrote it cannot shorten it to fit, and if he shortens it to fit then it is a different reason and it is a reason with a lawyer's hand on it and not with mine.

"**And there is the second thing about it, which is worse, and it is not mine, it is the clerk's own trade and he gave it to us on Thursday in a doorway without being asked. Whatever goes into the record on those two days is what a clerk can enter and not what a clerk can be asked about afterwards.

"**So the reason is not going into the record. Not because anybody has decided it must not. Because it is long, and a record has no room for it, and the man who wrote it is not going to be in the room to defend the short version of it, and in about four years a man in a city is going to read five lines out of a record in this circuit and he is going to read them with no reason against any of them.**"

"**How many,**" said Calder Marr.

She went along the column with the back of her thumb and did not hurry.

"**Five that nobody in this harbor can check, and that is what this party entered on the fourth of October and has not improved on since. Four of them carry no name at all, and the count of the four is in the first column in a ship's hand and is not struck out, and a request to strike one of them out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in the first column.

"**And the fifth is the fourteenth of August, and you can bring up the first four words of it, and the four words are *a man in a boat*, and the rest of it is in your own left hand in this book and was written in this shed at about nine in the evening of that day by the man who wrote it, and he cannot remember writing it. And that is a fact and it is checkable against this page.

"**So. Five lines, in a room, on Wednesday, with a clerk and a pen. Five lines, four of them with nothing against them but a date, and one of them with a man's four remembered words on it and the rest of the line gone.

"**And not one reason. Not one of the five. Five bare things in a record in a city, and the whole of what makes them honest is on a page in this shed that nobody in a city is going to see.**"

Nobody in that shed improved on that either, and it is in the book.

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that on the said day this party went through the second column of this book from the fourth of May instant and found that it stands at five lines that no person in this harbor can check, of which four carry no name against them and the count of the four is entered in the first column in a ship's hand and is not struck out, and that the fifth is a line of the fourteenth day of August instant of which the holder can bring up the first four words and the rest of the line is in his own left hand in this book and he cannot remember writing it, and that every one of the five carries a reason in this book and that the reason is against the line in each case; and that a bonesetter of this harbor, being Hessa Barlow, forty-four, of a room at the back of a chandler's shop off the market, charged three shillings and sixpence on Saturday the twenty-fourth instant for the strapping of the left forearm of this yard's own shipwright and for a visit, and that the said figure was paid out of the bag on the said day at about ten past four in the afternoon in her own hand at her own counter, and that she said the bone is cracked about two inches above the wrist and that nothing in the joint is out of place, and that it wants to be in one position for six weeks, and that the forearm is not going to be a hand that takes a pump handle in a month and is not going to be one in a quarter of a year; and that she said that a return on the fourteenth day, being Saturday the seventh of November instant, would not be charged, and that she would not be thanked for it and would be annoyed if anybody tried.*

*And that on the said day the bag held four marks and eight shillings and six pence, and that is seven hundred and forty-two pence, and that the four pence of the postage of a letter of the second of October past is the whole of what this yard had spent on itself in the twenty days before that, and that this party entered on the twentieth of October past that it does not know what the fifth time is going to cost and is not going to find out by not spending it, and that entry stands, and that a bonesetter's bill is not one of the things that entry is about and is not going to be counted as one by anybody in this shed.*

*And that the third column of this book was not filled on Saturday the twenty-fourth or on Sunday the twenty-fifth instant, and has not been filled since the first day of August, and that this party has not appointed anybody and is not going to appoint anybody in a shed on a Sunday night, and that the head of that column keeps the hand of a boy of twenty-two and a request to scrape it out is refused in the same words it is refused for a line in any column.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of October instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that the holder and the ship's hand went through the five checkless lines of this column on the said day and entered, against themselves and with the hour against it, that a line and a date and an hour is what a clerk can enter, and that the reason against a line is a paragraph and is not going into a record, and that this party entered on the fourth day of October instant that it has five lines and no reasons it can find, and that on the twenty-fifth instant it worked out what that means, which is that on the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth instant five bare lines of this column go into a record in a city and the reason for every one of them stays in this shed; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check what a clerk wrote down and cannot check what is going to be read as a reason, and that a party which has spent four volumes saying that the second column is the only place a man in this harbor is allowed to say that he does not know and mean it has found out in a shed on a Sunday that the reason is the part of that column which does not travel.*

*And that the holder entered on the twenty-fifth instant that the said clerk gave this party a warning in a doorway on Thursday the twenty-second instant, and that the said warning was that whatever goes into the record on those two days is what a clerk can enter and not what a clerk can be asked about afterwards, and that this party had already worked out the other half of that on its own and did not know it had worked it out, and that the two halves of it together are the whole of what the last nine weeks of this book have been, and that neither half of it is a discovery and both halves of it are now on the page with the date against them.*

**No money came into this yard on the Saturday and none on the Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day except three shillings and sixpence, and four marks and eight shillings and six pence is in the bag, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the twenty-seventh instant and there is enough in the bag to pay it, and eight shillings and sixpence is what will be in the bag on the Wednesday morning after it, and this party entered on the twelfth of September past that it does not know what any given Tuesday is going to bring and has not improved on that entry since and is not going to now. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, its three counts of weeks unmoved since the fourth of January. Five marks of compressor's gland on that note, unpaid, and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose and this harbor has no hose. There has been nothing in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and nobody is going to be sent for him. Seven pence stands owing to this yard's own shipwright for the postage of a letter of the twentieth of August out of her own pocket, and it is not paid and is not going to be paid out of the bonesetter's three shillings and sixpence or out of anything else that has come into this yard, and she asked for that specifically on the Saturday and wrote it in her own hand. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month and is in no page of it. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure in the first column at her own asking and nothing on any wall. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and the head of the third column keeps his hand. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and the six marks and two days have not been offered, have not been reduced and are not going to be. Twenty-three lines and ninety-two marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest and staying there, seventeen envelopes on a bench in that chest, and three weeks fallen due this year with no envelope standing against them. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted and not narrowed and not re-served, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard was inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it is open, and this party wrote no better letter about it in October and is not going to write one in November. The cistern behind the engine house was charged at no figure by strangers on the twenty-second instant and this party walked none of the twenty charges there have been since the third of August. The two objects of nobody's were on the crown of that mole about a hundred and forty-five days on the Sunday, about nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one of them with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither of them has moved and this party did not go up and count them twice. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. Nineteen winters are in a naval file and in a list in a city and this yard's four returns are in the hand of the woman making it. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions, two of them with no call in them, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. The writ of the first of May instant runs to midnight on the first of March next and carries four names and no fifth and is not the Second Bell license and is not a charter and renews nothing, and the second season is a named absence and nothing in this shed touches it. The custody room was not asked for and no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book and the live question that no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody. The coast road is in a press in a city beside a pilot's figure, and neither has been withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them and the limit they wrote stands and nobody in this yard has asked to have it lifted.
