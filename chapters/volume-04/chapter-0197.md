# Chapter 197

*One Sheet in Her Hand, and an Office Saying a Thing About Itself*

Saturday the twenty-eighth and Sunday the twenty-ninth of November, in the shed.

**There was no day of work booked on the Saturday and the board was not written, and this party entered that, with the reason against the line, which is that a day on which nothing happens still happened, and which is a day on which two things happened and neither of them was work.**

---

**And at about ten in the morning on the Saturday one sheet came up this hill in the ordinary post, addressed in a hand that this yard knows from a bench in a doorway, and it was thin and it had been folded once and the fold had gone soft on it, and it is dated the twenty-seventh and it came in on the twenty-eighth because that is what the post does between here and four days up the coast.**

The ship's hand read it out in the shed, standing, and she did not sit down, and she read it once and then she put it down squared to the edge of the pump, which is what she does with a sheet, and then she read it a second time and neither of them said anything while she did it.

> *One question was asked of me in a letter dated the sixteenth of November and it reached me on the twentieth and I am answering it on the twenty-seventh, and I am putting the price first because I was once asked for a thing and priced it afterwards and I have not forgotten which order that was.*
>
> *The price is this. Keep the letter. Put what I have written in the second column, with a reason against the line, and the reason is the one I am giving here. And do not put one word of this in a market.*
>
> *Not because a sheet on a wall is a document. A sheet on a wall is a sheet, and I put one of my own up in July and it is still up and I would put another up.*
>
> *Because in about four years a man in a city is going to read that a yard of Morrow Quay went to a woman it owed forty marks to, and asked her whether it might pay her part of it, and was refused. And that reading is going to be true of a man like him. And it is not going to be true of him. And there is nothing whatever I can write from where I sit that will make the difference, because the difference is the four lines he has not written down and he is not going to write them down for me, and a reason that does not travel is a reason that does not travel whichever side of it you are standing on.*
>
> *So that is the price and here is the answer and the answer is no.*
>
> *A part payment is a receipt. A receipt is a document. And a document is how a person of no office becomes a thing a person with an office can use, and you have that in a book already, in your own hand, with the date on it, and I said it to you in a room in September and you did not argue with me then and I am not asking you to argue with me now.*
>
> *The forty marks stands first after food. It has not moved since the fifteenth of October. It is not going to move because a yard has had a fortnight. And the figure of six marks and two days has not moved either and is not being offered by anybody and is not going to be reduced by anybody, including by me, because it is my price and not a debt and the two of those have never been one thing and this is the first time anybody has tried to make them one.*
>
> *You asked me once in twenty days. Do not ask me again this winter. The reason is not that I am hard with you and it is not that I am kind to you either. It is that a price is a price and you asked for one.*
>
> *M. Venn, tide conservator, of no office. And I am not a door, and you knew that in September, and you did not go looking for a different woman to be one.*

Nessa Pike stood where she was for about a minute and then said, "**Right,**" and sat down on the bench, which is the only sound she made about it.

"**I am not going to be thanked,**" she said. "**And I am not going to be told you are sorry, because you have not said it and if you say it now it will be because you have come off worse and I have been in this business for nine years and I know what a man sounds like when he has come off worse.**

"**And I read it twice because a fold has gone soft on it and there is a crease about four inches down and I was not going to lose a word to a crease, and that is the same reason I gave a woman of fifty-eight in a room off the fish market in October, and I am not going to give it again.

"**And I want one thing in the first column tonight in your hand and not mine, and I am going to tell you what it is before you write it so that you cannot improve on it. It is this: the whole of what that sheet says is that the answer is no. Not one word of it is about the copy you applied for on Monday, and not one word of it is about a room over a tide-gang store on Thursday, and this yard did not tell her about either of them and is not going to, and if anybody in four years reads that letter next to a page about a copy then somebody has put them next to each other and it was not me and it was not her.**"

"**And the other one,**" said Calder Marr.

"**There is no other one,**" said Nessa Pike. "**You have a sixth occasion in a book and I know it and you are not going to put it in the same line as this. Write it on its own line, with the reason of the fourth of October, and do not put a new reason on it, and do not write that you did not tell her because you had just been refused a receipt. She has not refused you anything tonight. She has given you a price and you have paid it with a sheet of paper and it is the cheapest thing either of you has done in four volumes.**"

---

**And at about ten in the morning on Sunday the twenty-ninth of November another sheet came up this hill in the ordinary post, and it is half a sheet, and it is the form of an office and not of a man, and there is no signature on it anywhere, and at the foot, where a name would be if the thing had one, there is the name of an office.**

> *To any person, and to no particular person, —*
>
> *This office acknowledges that a manifest referred to in the record of the entry of one item of recovered transparent mineral, as that manifest stood when this office first entered the item and as that manifest stands now, does not agree with a copy of that manifest taken at the time. A line of it has been altered. The alteration bears a date.*
>
> *This office does not state by whose hand, at whose instance, or with what intent the alteration was made, and this acknowledgment names no person.*
>
> *This acknowledgment is made because a copy of that record was made, was released, and was read out in a room in Morrow Quay. A copy which has been made cannot be unmade. An office which has released a transcript containing a discrepancy and has not acknowledged it will be read in four years as an office which released it.*
>
> *This office does not find against anybody. This is not a finding of the inquiry. This office does not alter the record; the record stands as it stands and the line stands as it stands, and this acknowledgment is a thing about this office and not about the record.*
>
> *Entered on the twenty-ninth day of November instant. The date at the foot of this is the date of this acknowledgment and is not the date of the alteration.*
>
> *For the circuit office of this place.*

"**That is an office saying a thing about itself,**" said Calder Marr, "**and in four volumes the only thing this yard has wanted out of any office is one line of that.**

"**And it costs a penny a sheet,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and I know what that costs, because a woman of fifty-eight sat at a counter off the fish market in October and charged me a penny for a fair copy of a sheet of my own and I paid it out of my own pocket and it is not the forty marks and I have entered it as not the forty marks.**

"**So: it will have to be copied to be read in a market, and a copy is a penny a sheet, and we have two hundred and thirty-seven pence in a bag, and we are not going to spend a penny a sheet on about four hundred people, and we are not going to ask a copyist to do it for nothing, and we are not going to go up to that counter and say it is for the yard.

"**Then it will not be read in a market by this yard,**" said Calder Marr.

"**It will be read in a market by whoever pays a penny for a sheet,**" said Nessa Pike, "**and that is a different thing and it is a better thing and I am not going to be the one who stops it.**"

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-ninth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed: that one sheet came up this hill in the ordinary post on Saturday the twenty-eighth instant at about ten in the morning, addressed in a hand this yard knows, dated the twenty-seventh instant, thin, folded once with the fold gone soft on it, and that the ship's hand read it out standing up in this shed and then read it a second time, and that neither of them said anything while she did it; and that the said sheet puts its price first and its answer second, the price being that this party keep the letter, put what the said woman has written in the second column with a reason against the line, and put not one word of it in a market, and gives the reason, which is that a sheet on a wall is a sheet, and that in about four years a man in a city is going to read that a yard of Morrow Quay went to a woman it owed forty marks to and asked whether it might pay her part of it and was refused, and that the said reading is going to be true of a man like him and is not going to be true of him, and that there is nothing the said woman can write from where she sits that will make the difference because the difference is in lines the holder has not written down and is not going to write down for her; and that the answer in the said sheet is no, and that the said woman gives the reason, which is that a part payment is a receipt and a receipt is a document and a document is how a person of no office becomes a thing a person with an office can use, which the holder has in his own book with the date on it and which the said woman said to him in a room in the month of September last and which he did not argue with then; and that the said sheet says that the forty marks stands first after food and has not moved since the fifteenth of October past and is not going to move because a yard has had a fortnight, and that the figure of six marks and two days has not moved and is not being offered by anybody and is not going to be reduced by anybody including by the said woman, because it is her price and not a debt and the two have never been one thing and this is the first time anybody has tried to make them one; and that the said sheet says that this party asked her once in twenty days and is not to ask again this winter, and gives the reason, which is that a price is a price and the holder asked for one.*

*And that the said woman said in this shed on the said Saturday that she is not going to be thanked and is not going to be told he is sorry, and gave the reason, which is that he has not said it and that a man who says it after he has come off worse is a thing she has heard before; and that she said she read the said sheet twice because a fold had gone soft on it and a crease about four inches down, and gave the same reason she gave a woman of fifty-eight in a room off the fish market in the month of October last, and that this party agrees with that reason and did not improve on it; and that the said woman asked that one thing go in the first column in the holder's hand and told him what it was before he wrote it, which is that the whole of what the said sheet says is that the answer is no, and that not one word of it is about the copy this party applied for on Monday the sixteenth instant nor about a room over a tide-gang store on Thursday the twenty-sixth, and that this party told her about neither and is not going to; and that the holder entered in the first column on the said evening a sixth occasion not to tell her a thing he wrote in the second column on the eighth of September instant, and that he did not, and that the reason is the reason of the fourth of October instant and there is nothing new to add to it and he is not going to invent a second one, and that the said occasion is entered on a line of its own and is not entered on the same line as the answer to a question about a part payment, and that a price is not a confession and a refusal is not a repair and this party is not going to call either of them one.*

*And that one further sheet came up this hill in the ordinary post on Sunday the twenty-ninth instant at about ten in the morning, and that the said sheet is the form of an office and not of a man and carries no signature anywhere, and that at the foot of it, where a name would be if the thing had one, is the name of an office; and that the said sheet states that a manifest referred to in the record of the entry of one item of recovered transparent mineral, as that manifest stood when that office first entered the item and as it stands now, does not agree with a copy of that manifest taken at the time, and that a line of it has been altered and that the alteration bears a date; and that the said sheet states that that office does not state by whose hand, at whose instance or with what intent the alteration was made, and that the acknowledgment names no person; and that the said sheet states that the acknowledgment is made because a copy of the record was made, was released and was read out in a room in Morrow Quay, and gives the reason, which is that a copy which has been made cannot be unmade and an office which has released a transcript containing a discrepancy and has not acknowledged it will be read in four years as an office which released it; and that the said sheet states that the office does not find against anybody, that this is not a finding of the inquiry, and that the office does not alter the record, the record standing as it stands and the line standing as it stands, and that the acknowledgment is a thing about that office and not about the record; and that the said sheet is entered on the twenty-ninth instant and that the date at the foot of it is the date of the acknowledgment and is not the date of the alteration.*

*And that this party entered on the said evening that a copy of the said sheet is a penny a sheet, and that this yard holds two hundred and thirty-seven pence, and that it is not going to pay a penny a sheet on about four hundred people and is not going to ask a copyist of this harbor to make one for nothing, and has not been to the counter of that copyist and has not sent anybody and does not intend to; and that the said sheet will be read in a market by whoever pays a penny for a sheet of it, and that this yard did not arrange that and is not going to.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on Sunday the twenty-ninth day of November instant at about half past nine in the evening in this shed, and no name is against it: that a woman of no office of this coast, four days up the coast from this harbor, was written to on the sixteenth instant by the holder in his own left hand with one question in a letter and no errand in it, and that the whole of what she wrote back is that the answer is no; and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that a man on a mole can check that a letter went and that a letter came and what is on the outside of a letter, and cannot check what a woman said at her own table on a Sunday, and that the only person in this harbor who could check it is the woman herself and she has said she is not going to be checked and has put a price on the yard's keeping it; and that the price does not travel and cannot, and that this yard entered on the sixteenth that a reason does not travel and has now entered that a price does not either, and that both of those are facts about this yard's own book and neither of them is about her.*

*And that this party entered on the said evening that a person of no office priced a thing in three weeks and that the thing she priced was not the copy and was not the room and was not the water off the south end of this mole, and that it was a part payment, and that the answer was no, and that this party did not go into a street about it and is not going to, and that the reason this line is in this column and not the first is that whether a party is owed something or has been refused something in four years is not a figure anybody can check from a mole and is not anybody's business except the two of them and the one of them is four days up the coast and is not in this harbor and was not asked for anything.*

**No money came into this yard on the Saturday and none on the Sunday, and nothing went out of it on either day, and one mark and six shillings and five pence is in the bag, and that is two hundred and thirty-seven pence, and a wage of four marks falls on Tuesday the first day of December instant. Thirty-one on the note, not re-written, and the three counts of weeks stand at twenty-two and thirteen and eleven. Five marks of compressor's gland on that page, unpaid and useless, and the thing after the gland is the hose. Nothing in a tin since the twenty-eighth of April. Three marks owing to a man of Ashtide for the eighth of August, unmoved, and he is not named in that book again and has not been sent for. Seven pence owing to this yard's own shipwright, and it is not paid and is not being paid out of anything that has come in this month. Eight marks and a day of a boat owing to this harbor's own bellwright since the twenty-third of May, unargued, and he was not asked anything this month, and the chest was not opened and was not brought down the hill. Eight shillings and tenpence owing to a woman of this harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks standing whole to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots of the eighth of June stand, and nobody has written to him, nobody has gone to see him and nobody has asked anybody who has seen him what he said, and the head of the third column keeps the boy's hand and that column has not been filled since the first of August and nobody has been appointed to it. Forty marks to a person of no office, first after food, unpaid since the fifteenth of October, and not one mark of it paid, and not one shilling of it offered, and a part payment was asked about once on the sixteenth instant and refused in writing on the twenty-seventh instant, and the figure stands and has not been reduced. Twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks in the wage column, the fifth of March the oldest, twenty envelopes on a bench, five weeks fallen due with no envelope standing against them being the fifteenth of August, the fifth of September, the sixth of October, the third of November and the seventeenth of November instant. The two objects of nobody's are about a hundred and sixty-nine days up the crown of this mole, nine feet apart, under two tarpaulins, one with a corner off it since the seventeenth of June and not lashed back, and neither has moved. The notice of the thirty-first of October has its four clauses and has not been lifted, narrowed, re-served or altered, and no person, no boat and no lead of this yard went inside its first clause on either of these two days. The plate at the fifth seam is in eleven feet, seen and not measured and not touched, and the note against it stands open, and the only mechanism anybody has named for reaching it is a change of politics in an office. The mole-head light is not on. The north berth is out. The tide gang is four men short. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One herring berth of two is out. Nineteen winters are in a list being made up in a city, and this yard's four returns are in the hand of the woman making it, and nobody in Morrow Quay will ever be able to check what she writes with them. The *Sea Wren* is at the pile and this yard has not thanked him. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and nothing was entered in these two days and nothing was measured, and the delay is a hand's width of a second and has not moved, and this party is not going to be the first. The bell is off the south end in eight and a half feet and is not going out inside four years. The writ runs to midnight on the first of March next, four names and no fifth, and the second season is a named absence. The custody room was not asked for, nothing came out of it, no digit of any of the three numbers is in this book, and the question no paper in this harbor or in that office's copying room answers was not put to anybody in these two days. The coast road is in type in a city beside a pilot's figure and neither is withdrawn, and this volume does not decide which of them is right, and the Kettle was not looked at. The four of the Sill are alive with no boat and about nine marks between them, and the limit they wrote stands, and the holder did not ask in these two days to have it lifted.**
