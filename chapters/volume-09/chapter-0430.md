# Chapter 430

*Wednesday the Twenty-Second Day of September, a Leaf Read Back in Full on the Flat of the Bench Stone at That Mole Head at About Ten Past Five in the Evening in the Open in the Ship's Own Left Hand All Four Columns Including the Empty One With Both Definitions of the Four Figures on the Face of That Reading, a Fourth Column Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Five Where It Stood and Taking No Line and a Third Column None, the Bag at Five Hundred and Thirty-Two Pence Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty Pence, and a Harbor With No Flour in That Market and No Oil in It Since the Twenty-Ninth Day of August and a Light Out of a Mole Head and a Figure on a Board That Did Not Come Down*

Wednesday the twenty-second day of September, on that mole head, in that shed, in that market, and on that third lay.

**The said Wednesday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are five hundred and thirty-two pence in the bag, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what came into that yard in the ten days that ended on the said Wednesday is three hundred and thirty-six pence on two days, being the thirteenth and the seventeenth, at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and the whole of what went out of it is two hundred and twelve pence on three days, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon on the sixteenth to a caulker of about twenty-three at that same figure, and forty-four pence, being three shillings and eightpence, on the twenty-first at about half past ten in the morning for a loaf and a half, a candle and a pot, and four hundred and eight plus three hundred and thirty-six minus two hundred and twelve is five hundred and thirty-two, and the two sums are not the same figure and neither of them is the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, which has not been paid for forty-eight weeks.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Wednesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on that mole head from about five in the afternoon and stood four feet off the stone for the whole of the reading and said one thing afterwards. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he was on the ninth piece and at about half past three in the afternoon he got up off it and went home, which he has not done on any of the twenty days that this leaf has been adding to. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-eight days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and thirty days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eleventh week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-eight days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirty-fourth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty-two days, counted from the hour they were first seen on the evening of the first of August past. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for seventy days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-nine days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is twenty-two days old and has been answered by nobody, its fourth line ended on the twentieth, and it is still on that door.**

---

At about ten past five in the evening Nessa Pike read that leaf back on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head, in the open, in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, and the holder of that writ stood on the other side of it and did not touch it.

The mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past.

"The first column of that leaf has one hundred and sixty-two entries in it at this hour and one hundred and sixty-three at the close of the day," she said. "There were one hundred and forty-three of them at the close of Thursday the second day of September past and twenty have gone in since, one against each of the twenty days from Friday the third to this day, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own is the twentieth of the twenty and makes one hundred and sixty-three at the close. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has six hundred and eighty-seven lines in it at this hour and six hundred and eighty-nine at the close of the day," she said. "Five hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the second of September past and five have gone in on each of the twenty days since, three before the hour of that day's reading and two after it, and three of this day's went in before this reading and two have gone in in this afternoon after it, which is six hundred and eighty-seven at this hour and six hundred and eighty-nine at the close. **That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's one hundred and sixty-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five, at this hour, are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since and is not going to be said again in this harbor. He was not on the stone on any of these ten days and was put on nothing, and nobody in that crew has said one word to anybody about the three nots standing against the eighth of June past, and there is now an eleventh week in which that has not been said, and no day of any week before the first of July was entered against that line and none is going to be."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close," she said, "and it has taken no line since the twenty-third day of August past. It has taken no line on any of these twenty days and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to. The reason it has taken no line was entered in the second column at about ten past nine in the evening of the twenty-eighth of August, in that yard's own mouth, with the hour against it, and it is entered once and it is not entered again, and the reason it is in the second column and not in the fourth is that a man on a mole cannot check the second column and a man on a mole can check the fourth."

She shut it and put it under her own coat and neither of them said one word about the figure it gave.

---

Mara Venn said the one thing she said on that mole head, and she said it four feet off the stone with her hands at her sides.

"There is a sheet on a wall in that front street with a date at the top of it and a charge at the bottom of it, and four men in that market have said out loud that they were in a room, and a cold in the teeth is a real thing that four men have got and that a man of about thirty-four has got off a bell at eleven foot on the third lay, and there is not one figure in that harbor that puts the two of those together," she said. "And neither of those is going to be settled in this shed this month, and neither of them cancels the other, and I am not going to stand here on a Wednesday evening and pretend that one of them is a comfort."

The holder of that writ did not answer that and did not improve on it.

---

There is no flour in that market and there has been none since the spring and none is coming this month. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down and cannot be taken off. The sheet at the back of that end stall is on the same square of wall as that slate and has been on it for a week and nobody has taken it down. The paper at the top left of the door of the room over the joiner's shop in Fish Street is on that door and has been answered by nobody and nobody is going to answer it. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is on the front street wall for thirty-nine days. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and it is not re-written. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them. The mole-head light is out. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered since the ninth of November past and the three marks that stand against it are on an anchor date out of an earlier volume and are not going to be re-dated. The third lay fills on the first of April and one berth is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and forty-eight weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.

A man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers went out on the sixth clause at about half past six this evening and will come in past the outer marks at about five tomorrow morning. A man of about thirty of the third lay did not take his boat out yesterday and has not taken it out today. A man of about forty-four of the third lay is without eight fathoms of tarred rope. A man of about thirty-four off a screw-steam packet out of Bramble Strait has gone out on the tide and is not coming back to this harbor this month. Three pieces of worked glass went up-coast out of a crate this week for four shillings. A woman of about sixty-one of the third lay is not in a room at nine marks a year and has not been since the end of the quarter. A boy of about seventeen cannot account for an hour of this month and is not going to be asked.

This harbor is poorer on the twenty-second day of September than it was on the thirteenth day of September and poorer than it was on the fourteenth day of May, and a man with a berth and a rope and a day's work is not better off today than he was on Monday, and nothing that was taken in these ten days has been given back.

---

**The whole of what came into that yard in the ten days that ended on the said Wednesday is three hundred and thirty-six pence on two days and the whole of what went out of it is two hundred and twelve pence on three days, and four hundred and eight plus three hundred and thirty-six minus two hundred and twelve is five hundred and thirty-two pence, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and that is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, by one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings, and it is never zero and nobody is going to write one word under it saying anybody is going to pay it. A sheet of nine lines has been on a wall at the back of an end stall for seven days on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence that was not taken down first, and nobody has taken that sheet down and nobody has answered it, and a claim has been entered under it at a counter in an office off the west basin and no claim has been lodged in this harbor and nobody has paid the one hundred and sixty-eight pence at the foot of it. A room over a joiner's shop was let out three times in these ten days and about fifty-two people went up those stairs and a man of about forty-six read the head-notes out loud off the long table on the three Sundays of these ten days and wrote his own name and the hour under each of them in his own hand, and there was no claim in any of them. A woman of about sixty-one of that lay lost a room she has had for eleven years on the nineteenth and was not thanked for it and nobody in that harbor has been told that anything was done for her and nobody was charged and nobody was made whole. A berth was held on the eighteenth, a counter was shut to a man on the seventeenth, a claim window was held on a man who cannot read the paper on it, a boy lost a message in a street on the fourteenth and lost the hour he was going to be somewhere else in, and a man went down in a bell on the twentieth and came up at about twenty past eleven with a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron like a nail and could not tell which of two hatches he had come up out of. The four columns of that yard's leaf are at one hundred and sixty-three, six hundred and eighty-nine, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the close of the said day and one hundred and sixty-two, six hundred and eighty-seven, none and one hundred and twenty-five at the hour of that reading, and the fourth has taken no line in twenty days and the reason it has taken no line was entered once, in the second column, in that yard's own mouth, on the twenty-eighth day of August, and is entered again nowhere. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of any of the ten days.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-second day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about ten past five in the evening of the said day on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head, in the open, by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty one; and that both definitions of the four figures were stated on the face of that reading; and that the said leaf holds one hundred and sixty-three entries in its first column at the close of the said day, being one hundred and forty-three at the close of the second day of September past and one entry against each of the twenty days since, with no day in two of them, and one hundred and sixty-two of them at the hour of the reading; six hundred and eighty-nine lines in its second, being five hundred and eighty-nine at the close of the second and five a day since, three before the hour of each day's reading and two after it, and six hundred and eighty-seven at the hour of this reading; none in its third; and one hundred and twenty-five in its fourth, of which the hundred and twenty-fifth is the last thing in that book, and the column is not closed and nobody has closed it; and that the wall came down bare twice in the ten days, being the seventy-first on Monday the thirteenth day of September past and the seventy-second on the said Monday the twentieth, and it went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth on both of them and on the other eight days as well, and nobody put it back; and that the cistern was charged for the eighty-first time on the thirteenth, the eighty-second on the sixteenth and the eighty-third on the twentieth, at about eight in the morning, and walked on by none of it at about half past nine on each of them, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it, and that the fourteenth, fifteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth, twenty-first and twenty-second of September past are not rota days and no charge was placed on any of them; and that the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, was not paid on Tuesday the fourteenth or Tuesday the twenty-first and the column did not move on either, so that forty-six weeks stood fallen due at the open of these ten days and forty-eight stand at the close of them, and that the forty-ninth falls on Tuesday the twenty-eighth of September next and is not going to be paid either; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the thirteenth and on the seventeenth and nothing came in on any other day of the ten, and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it on the sixteenth and forty-four pence went out of it on the twenty-first, and that nothing else went out of it and nothing went into a tin in that shed; and that there are five hundred and thirty-two pence in the bag, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and that the figure short of that wage is one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings; and that a sheet of nine lines went up on a wall at the back of an end stall in that front street on the fifteenth day of September past at about ten in the morning, on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence that was not taken down first and cannot be taken off again, and that it has been on that wall for the seven days since and nobody in that harbor has taken it down; and that six lines of it were read out loud in the middle of that market on the sixteenth day of September past at about eleven in the morning by a man of about forty-six who wrote his own name and the hour under it in his own hand on the back of a crate, and that four men in that market said out loud that they had been in the room over the joiner's shop on the twelfth; and that a claim was entered under that sheet at a counter in the office off the west basin on the seventeenth day of September past at about four in the afternoon and that no claim has been lodged under it in that harbor and no claim has been read out in that room over the joiner's shop; and that a room over a joiner's shop was let out on the third, the twelfth and the nineteenth days of September past, and that about sixteen people went up those stairs on the first of them and about twenty on each of the other two, which is about fifty-six in all; and that the paper at the top left of that door is twenty-two days old and its fourth line ended on the twentieth day of September past and has been answered by nobody; and that the notice of the seventeenth day of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-eight days and has not been touched; and that the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and thirty days; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-eight days old and neither has moved and the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirty-fourth night and two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty-two days; and that the mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh day of May past; and that there is no flour in that market and there has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August past; and that the third column is none and nobody has been appointed to it and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that has not been said since the thirteenth of May past and is not on any page of these ten days.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the woman of no office said one thing on that mole head at about ten past five in the evening on the said day and that what she said is in the first column above and is not repeated here, and that the reason it goes on that page is that a man with a pencil can see it and a man in a shed cannot; and that the reason there is no figure in that harbor that puts a sheet on a wall and a cold in the teeth together is not anybody's reason and is not entered and this yard is not going to invent one; and that the reason the fourth column has taken no line in twenty days and the reason it has taken no line was entered once on the twenty-eighth day of August past is in the ship's own mouth and is that a man on a mole cannot check the second column and a man on a mole can check the fourth; and that the reason no name is against the man of about thirty-four who put a sheet up on a wall in that front street is that this yard did not put it up and has paid nothing to anybody to put it up and has not read the inside of another person's paper and is not going to; and that the reason no figure in any column stands against the loss of a room, a berth, a coil of rope, a day's work or a message is that all four of those are costs and not prices and that putting a figure on any of them would be this yard putting a price on a person; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Wednesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on any of these ten days.*
