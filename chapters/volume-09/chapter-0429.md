# Chapter 429

*Tuesday the Twenty-First Day of September, the Forty-Eighth Wage Tuesday and the Column Not Moved on It and Forty-Seven Weeks Standing Fallen Due at the Open of That Day and Forty-Eight Standing at the Close of These Ten Days, Forty-Four Pence Going Out of That Yard at About Half Past Ten in the Morning for a Loaf and a Half, a Candle and a Pot, a Boy of About Seventeen Telling a Man of About Thirty of the Third Lay Something at About Two in the Afternoon That He Was Told in Turn at a Counter in That Market and That Man Going Home and Not Taking His Boat Out, and a Holder of That Writ Saying Once Out Loud What He Wrote Down on the Twenty-Second Day of August Past and Why*

Tuesday the twenty-first day of September, in that market, in that shed, on that slip, and on that mole head.

**The said Tuesday was not a wipe and was not a rota day, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. It was the forty-eighth wage Tuesday and the column did not move on it. The column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and has not moved since the fourth of April past, and forty-seven weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of the said day and forty-eight stand at the close of these ten days, and the forty-ninth falls on Tuesday the twenty-eighth of September next. Forty-four pence went out of that yard on the said day at about half past ten in the morning, being three shillings and eightpence, for a loaf and a half, a candle and a pot. Nothing came into it, and there are five hundred and thirty-two pence in the bag, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on that third lay at about one in the afternoon and did not go down that slip. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he was sat on the second piece from ten until about one and then went and stood at the edge of that crown looking down the harbour side and came back and was on the second piece again from about two until four. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-seven days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and twenty-nine days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eleventh week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-seven days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirty-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty-one days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for sixty-nine days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-eight days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is twenty-one days old and has been answered by nobody, and the fourth line of it ended on the twentieth day of September past and nobody in that harbor has answered it.**

---

The forty-four pence went out at about half past ten in the morning to a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers, and he was going out on the sixth clause of the notice on that wall in a different street at about half past six that evening with about four days' victual in him and a loaf and a half and a candle and a pot, and it came out of that yard's own bag and not one penny of it came out of the owner of that boat.

He said that he had been told on the Saturday that his berth was held and that he had put his boat on the hard at the back of the lay for about three hours and had taken her off again and come back to the same berth in the evening when the paper was off the peg and the peg was still there.

"Nobody took it down," he said. "I do not know who took it down and I did not ask, and I am not going to stand at that office and ask a man with a sheet four days old why he wrote it. I have got a berth and I have got a tide and the tide is on the sixth clause and the sixth clause does not ask anybody anything."

---

At about two in the afternoon Dill Cardew came down that slip from the second lay to the third lay for the second time in eight days.

He had been sent by a man behind a counter in that fish market, who had said to him at about half past one that he was to go and find Emmet Slade and tell him two things, and the man had said the two things out loud to him and had not given him anything written to carry.

"Tell him his window is stopped."

"That is what you said."

"And tell him he is to hand in a third of anything landed in this harbor. A third, out of what has been landed, to the counter, and he will not be on glass again until he has done it."

Dill Cardew said those two things to Emmet Slade in about eight words in the middle of that slip at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and Emmet Slade cannot read.

"There is no third," said Emmet Slade, after a while. "There is no third of anything because there has been nothing landed in this harbor since June that anybody has got a claim on, and I have been in and out of that office all summer and I have never heard of a third."

"That is what the man at the counter told me to say to you."

"That is what the man at the counter told you to say to me."

Emmet Slade stood in the middle of that slip with his hands in his pockets.

"I was in there on Saturday," he said. "A man behind that counter read me the sheet out word for word and told me it says off, and I said that off is a different word to held, and he told me that off is the word that is on the paper. And now I have got it twice and both times a bit different, and I have been told to hand in a third of a thing that does not exist, and the market is tomorrow."

"What are you going to do."

"I am going home," said Emmet Slade, "and I am not taking my boat out tomorrow. I have got nine tons and I have got a market that I have not been to in nine days and if that window is gone then I am not going out in it."

He went home.

Nobody asked Dill Cardew where he was on Tuesday the fourteenth of this month at about ten in the morning. Nobody has asked him since and nobody in that yard is going to, and the reason is in the boy's own head and not in anybody's mouth, and it is that he has got an hour this month that he cannot account for, and a boy who cannot account for an hour is a boy who does not get sent anywhere again.

The man who was in that room on the twelfth was told on the Sunday after that the room had been let out and there had been about sixteen in it, and eleven of the sixteen could not read, and nobody went back to him and said whether he had been there.

---

At about half past nine in the evening Calder Marr was in that shed with the leaf under his coat and he took it out and put it on the bench stone and opened it.

"There are eleven lines in it," he said. "They are eleven dates in the order I read them, and there is no name against any of the eleven and no figure against any of the eleven, and there is a column ruled at the left of them with nothing in it yet. I wrote it out in a room over a fish market on the twenty-second day of August past with a pencil, in about a quarter of an hour, off a board about a yard wide that a man I do not know and have never spoken to kept under a sloping canvas on an after thwart, and I read the dates off that board and I did not read the two impressions at the foot of it and I did not ask."

"Why did you write them down."

"Because in July I had a thing in my head that only I had, and in August two other men had a thing in their heads that only they had, and it turned out that one of those things was a shape I had seen with my own eyes and the other one was a shape I had heard described in a room," said Calder Marr. "That is what you get when nobody keeps anything. So I wrote the dates down on my own hand, and I gave it to nobody, and I have not had a use for it since, and I am not going to have one this month."

"Are you going to tell anybody in that harbor what is on it."

"No," said Calder Marr. "And if I ever do, it is going to be in a room with about twenty people in it on a Sunday, and the man at the end of that table is going to write my name and the hour under it in his own hand, and not before. That is the arrangement I made on the Friday of this month when I put a pencil down two inches from that book, and I have not found a better one since and I am not going to go looking for one on a Tuesday night."

Nessa Pike did not ask him to read it out and did not ask to see it.

"The column at the left of it is the empty one and you have drawn it anyway."

"I drew it because on the tenth day of September I stood in that room and a man counted thirty-one heads on the back of his own hand and found one of them carrying three lines where the head of that book says four, and wrote it on the inside of the back cover in pencil with his own name and the date on it, and nobody has asked him who left the dash and nobody is going to," said Calder Marr. "I did not send that man. I want that said about that man before I say anything else about my own hand, because in about a month somebody is going to work out that the two things happened in the same ten days and start tying them together, and they are not tied and the only way they stay untied is if I say out loud now that I put a pencil down two inches from my own book and left it there."

"And is that why you have carried the other one for a month."

"That is why I have carried it," he said. "Not for a use. For a person who put something on paper without being sent and without being paid and who has not been asked one question about it."

She shut the ledger and did not write a line that night, and the leaf went back into the inside pocket of his coat and stayed there.

**The forty-eighth wage Tuesday came and the column did not move on it, and forty-seven weeks stood fallen due at the open of the said day and forty-eight stand at the close of these ten days, and the forty-ninth falls on the twenty-eighth of September next. Forty-four pence went out of that yard at about half past ten in the morning, being three shillings and eightpence, for a loaf and a half, a candle and a pot, to a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers, out of that yard's own bag and not one penny of it out of the owner of that boat. A boy of about seventeen called Dill Cardew was sent at about half past one in the afternoon by a man behind a counter in that fish market to tell a man of about thirty of the third lay two things, and told him in about eight words in the middle of that slip at about two o'clock, being that his window is stopped and that he is to hand in a third of anything landed in that harbor, and the man said out loud that there is no third of anything because there has been nothing landed in that harbor since June that anybody has a claim on; and that man had been told on the Saturday before by a man behind a counter in an office off the west basin that the word on his sheet is off, and had said that off is a different word to held and been told that off is the word on the paper, and said out loud that he had now got it twice and both times a bit different, and that the next market was the following morning; and he went home and did not take his boat out. Nobody asked that boy where he was on the fourteenth day of this month at about ten in the morning and nobody in that yard is going to. The holder of that writ said out loud in that shed at about half past nine in the evening that he wrote eleven dates on a leaf of his own in a room over a fish market on the twenty-second day of August past off a board about a yard wide, with no name and no figure against any of them, and gave his reason for writing them, being that in July he had a thing only he had and in August two other men had things only they had and one of them was a shape he had seen with his own eyes and one was a shape he had heard described in a room, and that he is not going to tell anybody in that harbor what is on it unless it is read out in that room on a Sunday with his name and the hour under it in the hand of the man at the end of that table. Nothing came into that yard on the said Tuesday, and the bag is five hundred and thirty-two pence, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and is short of the wage of four marks by one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is twenty-one days old and its fourth line ended on the twentieth and has been answered by nobody. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody in that yard asked a boy of about seventeen where he was on the fourteenth day of September past is in his own head and is not in anybody's mouth, and it is that he has got an hour this month that he cannot account for, and that a boy who cannot account for an hour is a boy who does not get sent anywhere again; and that the reason no figure in any column of that yard's leaf stands against a man's boat not going out on the twenty-second day of September past is that a man not going out is not a price and that the loss is a day and a market and nobody has a figure for a man's day; and that the reason a man of about thirty-four was sent by a man behind a counter to say a thing about a third of anything landed is not anybody's and is not entered, and that this yard has not asked him and is not going to; and that the reason the holder of that writ put a leaf with eleven dates on it into that shed on the said night and then put it back into his coat is in his own mouth and is that a thing told to about twenty people in a room on a Sunday with a name and an hour under it is a thing a stranger can check, and that a thing told to two people in a shed on a Tuesday night is not; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Tuesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
