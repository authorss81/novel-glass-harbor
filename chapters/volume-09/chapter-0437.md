# Chapter 437

*Wednesday the Twenty-Ninth Day of September, the Keel of a Boat of About Nineteen Units Rebuilt About the Shoe and Painted and Caulked in Company Between Her and the Next Tide, Seven Boats and About Thirty Men on the Water From About Ten Past Seven Until the Last of the Ebb, a Chain Weight Over a Rail at Twenty-One Minutes to Two in the Afternoon With the Reason Written Down, One Man in a Bell at a Quarter to Four in the Morning and Then Another Man in That Bell One at a Time With the Same Tender and the Same Cutoff and a Reason Against Each of Them Written Down With the Hour, a Bill for Damage Done in Company That Comes to This Yard, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Out at About Eleven in the Morning for Iron and a Plank and Roves and Copper, and the Bag at One Hundred and Ninety-Six Pence*

Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of September, on a bank about four hundred yards down that foul, at the head of the third lay, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about eleven in the morning for iron clamps and a plank of white oak for the garboard of a boat of about twenty-two tons and roves and copper, and there are one hundred and ninety-six pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and forty-four pence, being two marks ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing came into that yard on the said day.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on that bank from about a quarter to four in the morning until about eleven and did not go in the bell. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he got the eleventh piece up off that stone and turned it over twice and set it down again nine inches from where it had been. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and thirty-five days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and thirty-seven days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a twelfth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and seventy-five days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-first night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty-nine days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for seventy-seven days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the fourteenth day and the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the twenty-ninth day and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-six days.**

---

The new piece of keel went on the *Alewife* on the Tuesday and was faired and oiled with a hot iron and a mixture of tar and tallow and beeswax that a man of about thirty-four mixed in a pot on the after deck of a boat of about forty tons.

On the Wednesday it was caulked, and caulking it was a caulker's job, and the caulker was not asked for it by anybody.

Isaac Fell, of about twenty-three, of the third lay, of no boat, of no crew and of no office, was asked in that shed at about ten in the morning on the said Wednesday whether he would take the caulking of that joint on that afternoon, and he said the figure he worked to out loud before he was asked for the day, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and he was told that the day is paid on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon and that it is not paid until then and that nothing goes out of that yard before it is paid and that he is not on any sheet.

He caulked about eleven feet of that joint in about two hours with an iron and a maul and about forty ounces of oakum and he did it on the bank with the water about nine foot over her at the top of the tide and about seven foot and a half at the end, and Halvard Ness pulled his chain at about twenty-one minutes to two in the afternoon and Isaac Fell was out of that boat and on the tender's deck with his iron and his oakum and his maul in his hand before the weight had stopped swinging.

The reason was written down in the ship's left hand at about half past four in the afternoon with the hour against it, and it is the second of three such lines in that book.

---

The wreck has to be looked after between her and the next tide, and that is the whole of the trade and it is not a thing that can be done once.

At about a quarter to four in the morning on the said Wednesday, with the ebb at the bottom, Calder Marr went down in a bell off that boat's port quarter, because the port side was the high side and the soft plank was on the port side, and the tender lay on about eight foot of water about thirty feet off with the chain out and Halvard Ness standing on her after deck in the dark.

Nessa Pike put the first other man in that bell behind him, one at a time, and not two at a time.

Tam Sparre, of about twenty-nine, of the third lay, who crews in a boat of about eleven tons and who has been in a bell six times, went down at about half past five in the morning and was in there about twenty-five minutes and came up.

He came up and sat on the tender's deck and said one thing.

"There is a hole in her about two foot aft of where you say the shore is, and it is not open."

"It is not open."

"It is soft. I put my hand flat on it and it went in about the depth of a thumb and it came back with a smell off it that was not tar and was not oil." He wiped his hand on his thigh. "I have not been in her in June. I have got nothing to say about that plank except what my hand said and my hand said it is soft, and that is all I have got and I am not going to give you anything else."

Calder Marr said that he had put his thumb through the veneer of that plank on the Friday before last about the size of a shilling, at nine foot, and that they had found it then and had not stopped for it.

Two accounts of the same place in one morning that do not agree with each other, and both of them were written down, and neither of them was taken back and neither of them was settled before the end of the day, and that is how it stands.

---

The plank and the iron and the roves and the copper came out of that yard at about eleven in the morning at the figure of a caulker's day, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and the plank is white oak four inches by nine and eleven feet long and it is going into the lee rail of a boat of about twenty-two tons that struck a baulk of oak that that yard's own hands laid down at half past eight in the morning on the Tuesday with no light on it and no flag and nothing on it at all.

That is the first of the three things the work costs, and it is on the face of that bill and it is not charged to the owner of any of the seven boats, and the owner of that boat of about twenty-two tons is not asked to pay it and did not offer.

"The baulk was ours," he said. "It was ours at half past eight in the morning and it was ours to mark, and nobody marked it, and I have got a hole in my boat and there is a man of about thirty-one in that harbor who went over her side and held his own hat. That is what there is. I am not going to be told what a thing like that costs by a woman with a slate and I am not going to be thanked for saying so, and I am going to go and hold that baulk next time."

"You are going to hold the baulk next time," said Nessa Pike, "and the yard has written that down with the hour against it and there is a copy of it on the wall of that shed, and I am not going to tell you what it costs because I have not got a figure for a boat that strikes a baulk and I am not going to invent one and call it material."

---

At about four in the afternoon the woman of fifty-eight with the end stall was paid for a basket of biscuits and a coil of new manila out of the holder of that writ's own hand, being four pence for the biscuits and being eighteen pence, being one shilling and sixpence, for the coil, and neither figure came out of that bag and the four pence and the eighteen pence are not a figure of anything.

Isaac Fell was paid his day on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon in coin at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall on the Saturday, being asked for that day by name on the said Wednesday at about ten in the morning, and not before, and not on any other day.

---

There is no flour in that market and there has been none since the spring. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and a figure of four hundred and fifty pence stands on a board at an end stall with three hundred and seventy-eight pence visible above it and both dates on that board. The sheet of six lines is on the wall at the back of that stall for the fourteenth day on the outside of that figure and no claim has been lodged under it and nobody has paid the one hundred and sixty-eight pence at the foot of it. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-six days and is a different paper on a different wall. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the twenty-ninth day. The mole-head light is out. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One berth on the third lay is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven. Forty-nine weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.

**The keel of a boat of about nineteen tons named the *Alewife*, lying on a bank about four hundred yards down the foul off the south end of that mole, was rebuilt about the shoe on the said Wednesday and painted and caulked in company between her and the next tide by seven boats and about thirty men who were on the water from about ten past seven in the morning until the last of the ebb, and a caulker of about twenty-three who crews in no boat and holds no office and is on no sheet was asked in that shed at about ten in the morning whether he would take the caulking of that joint on that afternoon and said the figure he works to out loud before he was asked for the day, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, and was paid on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon on the Saturday next and not before. The chain weight came over a tender's rail at about twenty-one minutes to two in the afternoon and he was out of that boat and on the tender's deck with his iron and his oakum and his maul in his hand before the weight had stopped swinging, and the reason for it was written down at about half past four in the afternoon with the hour against it. At about a quarter to four in the morning a man of about twenty-nine of the third lay went down in a bell on the port quarter of that boat one at a time behind the first man and was in there about twenty-five minutes and came up and said out loud that there is a hole in her about two foot aft of where the shore is and that it is not open and that it is soft and that he put his hand flat on it and it went in about the depth of a thumb and came back with a smell off it that was not tar and was not oil, and said that he has not been in her in June and that he was not going to give anybody anything else; and the man who had shored her on the Friday before last said that he had put his thumb through the veneer of that plank about the size of a shilling at nine foot and that they had found it and had not stopped for it, and both accounts are in that book and neither of them agrees with the other and neither of them was taken back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about eleven in the morning for iron clamps and a plank of white oak four inches by nine and eleven feet long and roves and copper, and that plank is for the lee rail of a boat of about twenty-two tons that struck a baulk of oak that that yard's own hands laid down on the Tuesday with nothing on it, and the owner of that boat said out loud that the baulk was ours and that nobody marked it and that he has got a hole in his boat and a man of about thirty-one in that harbor who went over her side and held his own hat, and that he is not going to be told what that costs by a woman with a slate and is not going to be thanked for saying so, and that he is going to hold that baulk next time and the yard has written that down with the hour against it. One hundred and ninety-six pence is in that bag at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the keel of a boat of about nineteen tons lying on a bank about four hundred yards down the foul off the south end of that mole was rebuilt about the shoe on the said Wednesday, and that about eleven feet of the joint was caulked in about two hours by a caulker of about twenty-three who crews in no boat and holds no office and is on no sheet, and that he was asked in that shed at about ten in the morning whether he would take that caulking on that afternoon and said the figure he works to out loud before he was asked for the day, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence; and that the chain weight came over a tender's rail at about twenty-one minutes to two in the afternoon, being the second time in three days and the second such line written against it; and that a man of about twenty-nine of the third lay went down in a bell off the port quarter of that boat at about half past five in the morning on the said day, behind another man, one at a time and not two at a time, and was in there about twenty-five minutes; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about eleven in the morning for iron clamps and a plank of white oak four inches by nine and eleven feet long and roves and copper, and that nothing came into that yard on the said day, and that three hundred and sixty-four minus one hundred and sixty-eight is one hundred and ninety-six pence at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings, being short of the wage of four marks by four hundred and forty-four pence, being two marks ten shillings and fourpence; and that a caulker's day was asked for by name on the said day and is paid on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon on the Saturday next at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall and not before and on no other day; and that no charge was placed on that cistern on the said day and the said day was not a wipe and was not a wage Tuesday.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a boat of about nineteen tons is in nine foot of water on a bank four hundred yards down that foul and not on a hard where a man could get at her is in that yard's own mouth and is that no hard in that harbor will take a boat of about nineteen tons before the middle of November and the third lay fills on the first of April and one berth is short, and that this yard has been paying for the same thing since April and has not had the berth; and that the reason two accounts of the same place in that boat do not agree and are both kept is in the two men's own mouths and is that one of them had his thumb through the veneer of a plank about the size of a shilling at nine foot on the Friday before last and that the other one has not been in her in June and had his hand flat on it at about eight foot in the dark and it went in about the depth of a thumb and came back with a smell off it that was not tar and was not oil, and that this yard has entered both of them in that order and has not settled them and is not going to settle them; and that the reason no figure is entered against either account is that two men in a bell in eight foot of water in the dark are not checkable from a mole, and that a figure against one of them and not the other would be a price on one man's hand and not on the other's; and that the reason the bill for a baulk with nothing on it comes to that yard is in the owner's own mouth and is that the baulk was that yard's at half past eight in the morning and it was that yard's to mark and nobody marked it, and that the owner is not charged a penny and did not offer and is not asked to; and that the reason that caulker's day was asked for by name on the said Wednesday at about ten in the morning and is paid on the Saturday at about four in the afternoon and on no other day is in that shed's own mouth and is that a man of about twenty-three who has no boat and no crew is the only person in this harbor who cannot be sent for and must be asked, and a man who is asked on the day he is wanted is a man who has said no to something else that week and taken the smaller of the two; and that the reason four pence and eighteen pence went out of a man's own coat at about four in the afternoon for a basket of biscuits and a coil of new manila is that they were not a day and not a wage and did not come out of that bag and are not a figure of anything; and that the reason the owner of that boat is going to hold that baulk next time and has not been thanked for saying so is in his own mouth and is that a man does not want to be thanked for being hit by his own yard's timber; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Wednesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
