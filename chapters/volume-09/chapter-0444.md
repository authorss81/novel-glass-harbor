# Chapter 444

*Wednesday the Sixth Day of October, a Long Sheet of a Copyist's Hand About Four Feet Long With a Head at the Top of It and a Rule Under the Head and Nine Boundaries Printed on the Face of the Same Sheet and Not on a Separate Paper, Three Names Against the Head of Custody and Two More Against a Line That Says a Rotation and Not an Office, a Woman of About Thirty-One of No Lay and a Man of About Forty-Four Who Carried a Piece of Worked Glass Out of a Boat's Hold in June Having Asked to Be on That Rotation and Neither of Them in Any Column, a Sheet of Four Refusals With Four Names and Four Hours on It Put Into the Fourth Box on a Table in an Office off the West Basin Against the Word of a Man of About Fifty With Two Papers on That Table, a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Asked for by Name at About Ten in the Morning and Paid at About Two in the Afternoon, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Wednesday the sixth day of October, in that shed, in a room off that market, and at a counter in an office off the west basin.

**The said Wednesday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about two in the afternoon for a caulker's day at that same figure, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the fourth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he stopped the hook in the crack in the stone that a mast made in September and looked at it for about two minutes and went back to the eleventh piece. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-two days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-four days and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a thirteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-two days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-eighth night. Two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-six days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-four days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-first day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-sixth day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-third day.**

---

The sheet was four feet long because the account had that much on it and not because anybody wanted a long sheet, and it was copied at a counter in a room off that market at about a quarter past nine in the morning by a copyist of about fifty-nine who has been at that counter for twenty-two years and who did not ask what she was copying and was not asked and did not stop.

The penny for it came out of the holder of that writ's own coat and not out of that bag, and the penny is not a figure of anything and it is not in the second column of that leaf.

He wrote the head himself at about ten o'clock, and then he stopped, and then he said out loud that there were three people who would have their names against the head of it and that two of them had not asked to be on it and that this was the part where somebody had to say something in a room, so Mara Venn was in that shed at about half past ten and he said it to her, and she said the thing she had said on the Sunday about a person who asks to be taken off a piece of paper, and he wrote her name against the head of it.

The third name was Nessa Pike's, and she had asked to be on it, and she put it there in her own hand and gave no reason and was not thanked for it.

Then there were two more names, and they were under a line that did not say *office* and did not say *deputy* and did not say *member* and did not say anything at all except *for a turn of four weeks from the sixth day of October, asked for, and not asked for twice*.

Jens Ower, of about twenty-nine, of no lay, who lightered about eleven tons out of that harbor and did not crew in anybody's boat and had no boat of his own, had come up that slip at about a quarter to ten in the morning and said that he had heard there was going to be a sheet with three names on it and that he wanted to know who the other people on it were before he put his name under it, and having been told the two, had put his name under it.

Nate Pry, of about forty-four, of that lay, who had carried a piece of worked glass about two hands square out of a boat's hold in June on his own back for a shilling and a half and had not been near a piece since and whose counter was shut to him on the seventeenth, put his name under it too, and said one thing when he did it, which was that he had been in a chandler's shop four weeks ago and had walked out of it, and that a sheet that said three names was a sheet he could stand behind and a man behind a sheet is a different thing from a man standing in front of a counter.

There were four names on that line under the head, and two of them had not asked to be on the head and one of those two had not asked to be on anything at all, and a stranger with a pencil could count that on the face of the sheet and would not have to be told it by anybody.

---

The head-rule went on the top of the sheet in the ship's own hand, and it is the whole of what the house has been printing since a Tuesday in a market two volumes back, and it is four lines and it is on the face of the sheet and it does not shrink anything.

*What a thing is. What a thing is not. Where a thing came from. What a thing does not show.*

*This account is entered in a public record and is not a private proof and is not the proof of anybody's case in any court in any city, and no line of it settles any contested claim, and a person who does not agree with a line of it may put his own words against that line and have both printed, and the one who entered this does not get to strike his.*

*This account is not the book in the room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street and is not a page of it, and a line in this account is not a line in that volume and neither of them corrects the other.*

*The refusals are on this account too and are not smaller than the rest of it.*

Under that they printed the nine boundaries, and the nine boundaries are the reason the community can stand behind four names at all, and they are these.

One. **The copy is a line and nine soundings cut in lead about the size of a man's palm, made on the fifth day of October at about ten in the morning, and it is in a drawer in that office and it is not in this harbor and it does not come to it.**

Two. **The original is on a shelf in that office and has not moved and nothing about this account moves it, and a person who wants to see the original will have to ask that office and not this account and not this harbor.**

Three. **The thing does not show the place about the size of a thumb where the pressure on one side of it is not the pressure on the other, and it was left out on purpose, and a person with a pencil can see that it was left out on purpose, and this account does not say what was in that place and cannot.**

Four. **The thing is not a transcript and is not a copy of what happened and is a made thing, and every mark on it can be seen and none of them is a number.**

Five. **The thing may be used for a route and not for a scene, and this account authorizes a limited use of the route by the four people whose names are on it, and nothing else is authorized, and the person who cuts a scene out of that line is not using this account and is not covered by it.**

Six. **Four lines are missing from the twenty-second head of the public volume kept in a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street, being the clause that would say what that panel is not fit for, and that clause is not on that page and is not being put on it, and a person with a pencil has found the place it should be in and that finding is on this account because that page cannot carry it and will not be made to.**

Seven. **A panel in that harbor holds a scene that a living person was made to settle at about nineteen years old in a room with a prepared frame and an anchor in it. That person is alive and is not named on this account and is not on any other sheet in this harbor, and nobody in this harbor is to ask that person about it again.**

Eight. **A berth was held on a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons on the eighteenth day of September by a sheet tied to a peg, and a claim window is held on a man of about thirty who cannot read the paper it is on, and neither of those two holds is given back by anything on this account, and the word on that paper is the word on that paper and nobody in this harbor is going to read it to him.**

Nine. **A charge of one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, stands at the foot of a sheet of six printed lines on a wall at the back of an end stall in a front street, on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence that was not taken down first and cannot be taken off, and no claim has been lodged under that charge and no penny of it has been paid and this account does not ask anybody to pay it.**

And under the nine, in a smaller hand and not smaller in size, a line that says what a person may do about all of it.

*Any person in that harbor may put his own words against any line above, in the room where this account is read out, with his own name and the hour under it, and both are printed, and the person who entered this does not get to strike his, and the nine words that refused four requests in that harbor in September are the same nine words and are printed with this account at the same size as the rest of it and are the reason the fourth request was refused and not the first.*

---

At about half past three in the afternoon Mara Venn went down the west basin with a sheet of good paper in a fold and a penny's worth of copying in it, and at a counter in that office she gave it to the man of about fifty who works at a table with two papers on it and who had said, in that shed, in the last week of the month before, that there was a fourth box on that table and that he would like it left empty.

He did not ask her what it was. He did not ask her to leave it. He put his hand flat on it for a moment and then he lifted it and he did not lift it far.

"There is no name on that," he said.

"There is no name on that," she said. "There are four names on the top of it and an hour against each of them and four sets of the same nine words under them, and it went through a counter in a room off that market at a penny a sheet, and the penny is not off any of you and it is out of a man's coat, and I put it in that box because a refusal that stands on a wall in a shed is a thing that a person can be told did not happen, and if it is in that office it is a thing that has a place and a date and cannot be told out of the world by anybody who does not come here."

"And I said I wanted that box left empty."

"You said it once, in a shed, and I heard you, and you have not been asked about it since and you are not going to be asked about it now, and I have put it in anyway, and that is on the face of that sheet in the last line, which says who put it there and why it went in against a man's word, and the man is me, and the word is yours, and the date is the sixth day of October."

The last line of that sheet is in that office and says: *Entered in the fourth box on the sixth day of October by Mara Venn, of no office, against the word of the man at that table, who has not been asked about it, on the ground that a refusal which stands only on a wall in a shed is a thing that can be told not to have happened.*

He read that last line twice and put the sheet into the fourth box and shut it, and the fourth box on that table is not empty, and the first box on that table has not been empty since an earlier week, and neither of them is a matter for anybody in that harbor, and neither of them is on a page of this account.

---

**THE ACCOUNT THAT IS ENTERED IN A PUBLIC RECORD IN A BUILDING ON THAT HILL CARRIES NINE BOUNDARIES ON ITS OWN FACE, AND A STRANGER WITH A PENCIL CAN COUNT THEM, AND THE SEVENTH OF THEM NAMES A PERSON WHO IS ALIVE AND IS NOT NAMED, AND THE EIGHTH OF THEM SAYS THAT TWO HOLDS STAND AGAINST TWO MEN IN THAT HARBOR AND ARE NOT GIVEN BACK BY ANYTHING ON THAT SHEET, AND THE NINTH OF THEM IS A CHARGE OF ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING ONE MARK AND EIGHTPENCE, ON A WALL AT THE BACK OF AN END STALL, WHICH HAS BEEN ON THAT WALL FOR THE TWENTY-FIRST DAY AND IS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND THE FIGURE IT IS PUT UP ON IS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE AND DID NOT COME DOWN. THE REFUSALS ARE ON THAT SHEET AT THE SAME SIZE AS THE REST OF IT AND THE HEAD OF THAT SHEET SAYS SO. A THIRD BOX ON A TABLE IN AN OFFICE OFF THE WEST BASIN IS NOT EMPTY AND THE MAN AT THAT TABLE WANTS IT EMPTY AND HAS SAID SO ONCE AND HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AGAIN, AND THE PAGE SHOWS WHO PUT A SHEET IN IT AND WHY IT WENT IN AGAINST HIS WORD, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED. A CAULKER OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WAS ASKED FOR THAT DAY BY NAME AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AND WAS PAID ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS NOT PAID ON ANY OTHER DAY AND WAS NOT THANKED.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the sixth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about two in the afternoon for a caulker's day at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a sheet of good paper about four feet long was copied at a counter in a room off that market at about a quarter past nine in the morning at a penny out of the holder of that writ's own coat and not out of that bag, and that a head and a head-rule and nine boundaries were written on the face of it in that shed between about ten in the morning and about half past one in the afternoon; and that three names are against the head of that sheet and two of the three had not asked to be on it, and that four names more are under a line that does not say office and does not say deputy and does not say member and says only that they asked for a turn of four weeks from the sixth day of October, and that a man of about twenty-nine of no lay who lightered about eleven tons and a man of about forty-four who carried a piece of worked glass about two hands square out of a boat's hold in June are two of those four; and that the ninth boundary names a charge of one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, standing at the foot of a sheet of six printed lines on a wall at the back of an end stall in a front street on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, and that no claim has been lodged under that charge and no penny of it has been paid; and that a sheet carrying four refusals in the same nine words with four names and four hours against them was put into the fourth box on a table in an office off the west basin at about half past three in the afternoon on the said day by a woman of no office against the word of the man at that table, and that he was not asked about it; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall for one hundred and forty-two days, the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-four days, the sheet of six lines is on that wall for the twenty-first day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-sixth day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-third day; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-two days old, the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-eighth night, and two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-six days.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nine boundaries are printed on the face of that account and not on a separate paper is in the holder's own mouth and is that a person who cannot read is going to be told that account by a man with it in his own hand, and that if the limits of a thing are on a different piece of paper from the thing then the limits are the first thing that does not get read out; and that the reason a sheet with four refusals in it was put into a box that a man at a table has said once he would like left empty is in that woman's own mouth and is that a refusal which stands only on a wall in a shed is a thing a person can be told did not happen, and that the last line of that sheet names her and names his word and carries the date, and that he was not asked about it and is not going to be; and that the reason no name is against four people on a rotation in that column is that they are in no column of that yard's leaf and are not in an office and are not a deputy and have asked for a turn of four weeks and are not going to be asked a second time whether they want it; and that the reason the seventh boundary names a person and does not name that person is that the panel holds a scene that a living person was made to settle at about nineteen years old and that putting that person on a sheet in this harbor is a thing this harbor has no right to do, and that nobody in this harbor is going to ask that person about it again; and that the reason no figure is set against a sheet of about four feet of paper, a box in an office off the west basin, or two people going into a chandler's shop in the last week of the month before, is that none of it is checkable from a mole and that a figure against a man's counter being shut would be a price on a man; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Wednesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
