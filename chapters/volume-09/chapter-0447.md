# Chapter 447

*Saturday the Ninth Day of October, a Far Bench Occupied from About Ten in the Morning Until About Two in the Afternoon and Not Empty at Four o'Clock, and That Bench Not Having Been Emptied Before Four o'Clock on Any of the Days Before It, a Man of Sixty-One Coming Down a Slip and Saying Four Sentences Out Loud in His Own Mouth in a Shed and There Being No Fifth of Them, the Fact of His Own Signature, the Harbor and the Yard and the Hour, the Weather and Two Marks Nine Shillings and Fourpence in His Pocket and a Day's Pay in Front of Him, and What He Said to His Wife That Night and What He Has Said in That House Every Day for Twenty-Two Years, and Nobody Thanking Him and Nobody Forgiving Him and the Holder of That Writ Not Going Over to Him at Any Hour of Any of the Ten Days, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Eleven in the Morning, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Saturday the ninth day of October, in that shed, and on a far bench at the end of that floor.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about eleven in the morning for cordage and a swivel, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about two in the afternoon for the seventh day of ten, and at about two o'clock in the afternoon the man on it got up off it and came down that slip and went into that shed, and the bench was not empty again until after four o'clock, and a shoe that is not his was on the floor beside it at ten in the morning and was on the floor beside it at four o'clock and has been on the floor beside it since the third of May past, and that is the one thing about that bench that did not change on the said day. Nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon and was not told about any of it. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he did not go near the water and did not speak. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-five days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-seven days and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a thirteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-five days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-first night. Two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-nine days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-seven days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-fourth day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-ninth day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-sixth day.**

---

Nessa Pike went out to the head of the third lay at about ten past two on the said day because the caulked joint in a boat of about nineteen tons wanted looking at in daylight and she wanted to look at it herself, and she put her coat on the back of a chair and went down that slip, and that shed was shut from the front and open at the back and there were two people in it at about two o'clock in the afternoon.

The man who came down that slip at about two o'clock in the afternoon was sixty-one and had been on that far bench since about ten and had not asked for anything all morning, and he had come down it without anybody in that yard going up to him, and he came down it the way a man comes down a slip he has not come down in twenty-two years, which is that he puts his hands in front of him and looks at the steps.

He stood about four feet off the bench stone with his hands at his sides and he said four sentences, and they were these.

"I put my name to a paper at Ternwatch on the twenty-second of November, and it was my name, and I have never once said it was anybody else's."

"The harbor was Ternwatch and the yard was two hundred yards inside the third pier and I was at the gate of it at about eight in the morning."

"It was blowing hard off the land and I had two marks nine shillings and fourpence in my pocket and there was a day's pay in front of me and I took the job."

"I came home that night and told my wife it was a survey, and it was not a survey, and I have said that sentence in this house every day for twenty-two years."

And then he stopped, and that was the end of it, and there was no fifth of them, and he did not say another word in that shed for about four minutes.

The holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of any of the ten days, and on the said day he was standing at the bench stone when the four sentences were said and he did not take a step forward while they were being said, and afterwards he did not go over to him either, and there was nobody else in that shed and nobody came into it, and the door at the front was shut the whole time.

What he said, when he said anything, was about a minute after, and it was not a thank and it was not a forgiveness and it was not a question about a paper.

"I am not going to say anything about that here," he said, "and I am not going to say it tonight either, and you are going to sit on that bench tomorrow from ten until four like you have."

The man of sixty-one said that was all right and went back up the slip, and he got back onto that far bench at about ten past three and was on it at four o'clock, and he did not speak to anybody in that yard again that day, and nothing was put in his hands at any hour of that day.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody forgave him. A man of about thirty-two did not forgive a man of sixty-one on a Saturday afternoon in a shed and did not say that he had and did not say that he had not, and a woman of no office upstairs was not told any of it, and the second column of that leaf has no line in it about the twenty-second of November and never will, and neither brother has ever said why either of them went into that yard in April or why neither of them has said one word about it since.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the holder of that writ went up that slip and did not stop at that far bench, and that is the seventh day out of ten on which he has not stopped at it, and he does not stop at it on any of the three days after it either.

What was said in that shed on the ninth day of October was not written down by anybody in that harbor at any hour of that day or after it, and the reason is in that shed's own mouth and was given to the man who keeps that leaf at about a quarter to eight in the evening and it is this: that a man said four sentences in a shed and nobody asked him for a fifth and nobody asked him for the first of them again, and that this yard keeps what it did and who did it and in what order, and that what a man of sixty-one said once at about two o'clock in the afternoon is not something that yard did and is not something that yard is going to write down, and that if this yard wrote it down then a person in that harbor would find out one afternoon what a man said in a shed, and this yard is not doing that to anybody.

**A man of sixty-one said four sentences out loud in his own mouth in that shed at about two o'clock in the afternoon on the ninth day of October and there was no fifth of them, and they were the fact of his own signature, the harbor and the yard and the hour, the weather and two marks nine shillings and fourpence in his pocket and a day's pay in front of him, and what he said to his wife that night and what he has said in that house every day for twenty-two years, and they came out of his own mouth in the order they were in him, and nobody thanked him for them and nobody forgave him for them and nobody is going to. A SHOE THAT IS NOT HIS WAS ON THE FLOOR BESIDE THAT FAR BENCH AT FOUR o'CLOCK ON THAT DAY AND WAS THERE ON THE THIRD OF MAY PAST. THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND FOUR SENTENCES WERE SAID IN THAT SHED AT ABOUT TWO O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON, AND THE SLATE THAT WAS HANDED ROUND IT WAS SCRUBBED AT ABOUT ELEVEN o'CLOCK AND PUT BACK ON ITS HOOK, AND NOTHING CAME OFF THAT SHED IN ANYBODY'S HAND.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the ninth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about eleven in the morning, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a far bench at the end of that floor was occupied from about ten in the morning until about two in the afternoon and was not empty again until after four o'clock, and that a shoe that is not his was on the floor beside it at ten in the morning and at four o'clock, and that nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift; and that a man of sixty-one came down that slip at about two o'clock in the afternoon and went into that shed and said four sentences out loud in his own mouth and that there was no fifth of them, and that a woman of about twenty-seven was out of that shed at that hour at the head of the third lay and that the holder of that writ did not go over to him at any hour of any of the ten days and did not take a step forward while those four sentences were being said; and that nothing that was said in that shed on the said day was written down by anybody in that harbor at any hour of that day or after it, and that the reason is in that yard's own mouth and is that what a man said once is not a thing that yard did and is not a thing that yard is going to write down, and that if it were written down then a person in that harbor would find out one afternoon what a man said in a shed; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall for one hundred and forty-five days, the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-seven days, the sheet of six lines is on that wall for the twenty-fourth day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-ninth day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-sixth day; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-five days old, the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-first night, and two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-nine days.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason four sentences were said and no fifth of them is in the man of sixty-one's own mouth and is that he had come down that slip to say them and had said them, and that the reason a man of about thirty-two did not forgive him on the said day is in his own mouth at about three o'clock in the afternoon and is that he was not going to say anything about it there and was not going to say it that night either and that a man is going to sit on that bench tomorrow from ten until four like he has; and that the reason there is no line in that column and no line in that yard's first column about the twenty-second of November is in that yard's own mouth and is given above, and that this yard keeps what it did and who did it and in what order and that what a man of sixty-one said once in a shed is not something this yard did; and that the reason nobody in that harbor was told any of it is in that yard's own mouth and is that a woman of no office was not in that shed and a man of sixty-one has said one sentence about a survey in his own house every day for twenty-two years and a second sentence about it is not this harbor's to hand out; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
