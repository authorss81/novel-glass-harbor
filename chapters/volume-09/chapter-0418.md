# Chapter 418

*Friday the Tenth Day of September, a Barrel of Lamp Oil That Cannot Be Had at Any Figure in a Harbor Where a Figure of Four Hundred and Fifty Pence With Three Hundred and Seventy-Eight Above It and Both Dates on a Board Stands Visible on a Board, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence of a Day With a Boat and Two Men at a Berth Coming In, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Out in Coin on the Flat of the Bench Stone at About Four in the Afternoon to a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Who Had Been Asked for That Day by Name on the Wednesday Before, and a Man of About Thirty-One With a Chart in a Bag Who Came and Stood at the End of a Long Table for Ten Minutes and Said Nothing*

Friday the tenth day of September, in that shed, in a market, in Fish Street, and on that mole head.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, being one mark and eightpence, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon in coin on the flat of the bench stone to a caulker of about twenty-three for a seam on a pot-lid stock on the first lay, being one mark and eightpence, and he had been asked for that day by name at about five o'clock on the Wednesday before and told on the Thursday that he was wanted, and there are four hundred and eight pence in the bag, being two marks seven shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and thirty-two pence, being one mark six shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about two in the afternoon and did not go up to Fish Street. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he got the first piece out from the bottom of the eleven and stood it up in the sun on the stones with its edge on a stone so that it would not go over, and stood looking at it for about half an hour, and there is no sun to speak of on that coast on the tenth of September. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and sixteen days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and eighteen days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a tenth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and fifty-six days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and twenty-second night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in forty days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for fifty-eight days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for twenty-seven days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is ten days old. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month, and physic is at fourteen pence with none in that harbor.**

---

There was no oil in Morrow Quay on the said Friday and there was a board at an end stall saying there was.

The board is a slate, about two foot by three, ruled with a line under every figure, and the rule that governs it, and has governed it since before the holder of that writ came to this harbor, is that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again. There is a barrel of lamp oil on that board at four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted the first of June, and above it, still visible and not rubbed out, February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, and both dates are on that board: the first of June and the first of February.

A man of about thirty-three came to that stall at about half past ten in the morning with a tin can in his hand and put the can on the board.

"I want a quart for this."

"There is no oil in this harbor," said the woman of about fifty-eight. "There has been none since the twenty-ninth of August. I have had none for twelve days and the last four gallons went on the Sunday and a man of about forty with a boat of about six tons took them and he did not haggle and I did not make him and I would have taken less."

"Can you get any."

"I can get a man to go and ask at the office on the west basin and the answer is the same as the one I have just given you, and I have asked twice, and I am not going to ask a third time this month, because a woman who asks a third time is a woman who has nothing else to do, and I have got a market to stand in." She took the slate off the nail and turned it over so that the four hundred and fifty and the three hundred and seventy-eight and the two dates were showing again. "I am not taking that figure off. It is a figure for a thing that exists somewhere between here and four hundred miles away and it is the truest thing on this board, and when there is oil in this harbor I will sell it at that figure and I will make a mark under it, and if it is higher then I will make the mark higher and the four hundred and fifty will still be up there with the first of June against it. That is the rule and you cannot have it the other way, and you are not the first man to ask me for it and you will not be the last, and I have a mole head in this harbor with no light in it and a lens out of it since the seventh of May past, and that is not my board and I have not got a figure for that, and I could not read one out of a board if there was one."

The man of about thirty-three stood with the can in his hand for a moment.

"What is the light in the market at."

"Oil. In a lamp. At a figure that was three hundred and seventy-eight pence in February."

"Will you write one under that oil figure, so that people can see there is none."

"No," said the woman of about fifty-eight, "and I will tell you why, and then you can say what you like to anybody in this market. A line with one word on it under the oil figure goes up the street in about an hour and by Tuesday there is a queue at my stall, and by the end of next week every man in this market will be saying the oil has gone, and in a fortnight there will be men saying it was never here, and in a month there will be a man on that mole who tells you there has not been oil at that stall since the spring and he will believe it, because it is easier to believe than to remember a figure. So I am going to write it in chalk on the edge of this board, in my own hand, where I wrote it in August, and I am not going to rule a line under a figure for it."

She took a stub of chalk and wrote the one word on the edge of the slate, low down, in her own hand, and it can be rubbed out in about a second, and she has not let anybody rub it out since the twenty-ninth of August.

Nessa Pike, who had come down to the end stall for the slate figure at the figure in chalk for a day with a boat and two men, stood there while it happened and said nothing, and afterwards, at the bench stone in that shed, she wrote one line in the second column and gave the reason for it against the line, being that a figure on a board cannot be taken off again and that a figure is what it says and not what can be got.

A man of about thirty-one of that lay with a chart in a bag came up the stairs at about ten past four on the Friday afternoon and stood at the end of the long table for about ten minutes with his hands behind his back and did not read the volume and did not touch it, and everybody in that room carried on.

He left at about ten past five. At the bottom of the stairs he stopped and said one sentence to Hobb Lee, and then he went away down that street and did not come back.

"Somebody is going to read that thing out loud in a room and about eleven people who cannot read are going to be in it," he said, "and every one of them will be told it on somebody's word, and in about a fortnight one of the eleven will say in a market that a thing in that book is not so, and nobody will be able to put a hand on where he got it, and there is no arrangement that stops that."

"Then it will be a man with a mouth," said Hobb Lee. "That is not a fault in a book. A book that a man reads out loud in a room to people who cannot read is a book that is going to be a man's word by the end of a fortnight, and the only thing anybody can do about that is to make sure the word is the truth, and I cannot do that. I am a net-mender. I have got no way of knowing what is true and I have never claimed any."

The man of about thirty-one with a chart in a bag went down the street and has not been in that room since, and he is not on a sheet and he is not on a list, and nobody in that yard is to go and ask him about a chart in a bag.

The caulker came up the slip at about four in the afternoon and was paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, out of that yard's own figures, and counted it with his own thumb, and the holder of that writ said nothing at all to him, which is the fifth time in a month, and that is now the arrangement, and the man of about twenty-three put the coin in his pocket and said:

"You are going to have me on the Monday and you know you are going to have me on the Monday."

"You are not on the Monday until I ask you for the Monday, and I will ask you for the Monday on the Friday before, and if there is not a Monday in it then you are not on it," said Calder Marr, "and I have not got a board with your name on it and I am not going to put one up, and there is a place at the end of a table over a joiner's shop on Sunday where anybody can come and sit down and nobody writes a list of who came, and if you want to sit in that room on Sunday you will sit in that room on Sunday and you will not be on anything."

"I will not be in that room on Sunday," said Isaac Fell, "and I have not said why and you have not asked, and that is the way we have worked since the tenth of August and it is going to stay that way."

There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth of August and a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it, and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken off that board on the said day and is not going to be. There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month. A man of about thirty-one of that lay with a chart in a bag stood at the end of that long table for ten minutes on the said afternoon and said one sentence at the bottom of those stairs about a man with a mouth, and is not on a sheet and is not on a list. A caulker of about twenty-three was paid for the sixth time since the tenth of August past, and the six are the seventeenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-seventh and the thirty-first of August past and the sixth and the tenth of September, and the tenth of August past was the payment before those six and is not one of them, and he said out loud that the holder of that writ was going to have him on the Monday, and was told that he is not on the Monday until he is asked for the Monday by name on the Friday before, and said that he will not be in that room on Sunday and did not say why and was not asked. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

and that there has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August last and there was none on the said day, and that a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, with the first of June against it, and carries February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it with the first of February against it, and that the figure of four hundred and fifty pence was not taken off that board on the said day and cannot be taken off; and that a man of about thirty-three put a tin can on that board at about half past ten in the morning on the said day and was told that there is no oil in that harbor; and that a woman of about fifty-eight wrote one word in chalk on the edge of that board in her own hand, where she wrote it in August, and would not rule a line under the oil figure, and gave her reason, being that a line with one word on it under a figure goes up the street in about an hour and that by the end of next month men will be saying the oil was never here; and that there is no flour in that market and none is coming this month and physic is at fourteen pence and there is none of it in that harbor; and that the mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past; and that a man of about thirty-one of that lay with a chart in a bag stood at the end of the long table in a room over a joiner's shop for ten minutes at about ten past four in the afternoon and said one sentence at the bottom of those stairs, being that somebody is going to read that volume out loud and about eleven people who cannot read are going to be told it on somebody's word and that in about a fortnight one of the eleven will say in a market that a thing in that book is not so and that no arrangement stops that; and that a caulker of about twenty-three said on the said day that the holder of that writ was going to have him on the Monday and was told that he is not on the Monday until he is asked for that day by name on the Friday before, and that there is no board with a name on it in that yard and there is not going to be one.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the figure of four hundred and fifty pence was not taken off a board on the said day is that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again, and that a figure on a board is what a thing costs and not what can be got, and that both of those are true about the oil on the said day and neither of them cancels the other; and that the reason a man of about thirty-one with a chart in a bag was not asked one question after what he said at the bottom of a stair is that he is a person of no office with a chart in a bag and what he said there is his own, and that this yard has asked him nothing about that chart in nine months and is not going to start; and that the reason the holder of that writ said nothing at all to a caulker on the said day for the fifth time in a month is in the caulker's own mouth and is that being paid without a word said over it is the only arrangement either of them has, and that a man who thanks a man for a day at a bench stone makes that day into a favour, and that it is not a favour; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Friday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
