# Chapter 419

*Saturday the Eleventh Day of September, a Room over a Joiner's Shop Shut for the Week Day and a Joiner at the Bottom of the Stair and a Volume Lying at the End of a Table He Glues Battens Along From Six in the Morning, a Man of About Forty-Six Getting a Room Ready for the Second Sunday in About a Month of Sundays and Not Being Asked To, a Bowl Wiped Out and Put Back on the Top Stone of a Step, and a Man of About Thirty-One Called Arthur Sennett Who Writes Figures on a Slate for a Buyer at a Fish Stall and Who Came Up a Stair Nobody Had Told Him About and Read the Whole of a Book Nobody Had Asked Him To Read*

Saturday the eleventh day of September, in Fish Street, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are four hundred and eight pence in the bag, being two marks seven shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and thirty-two pence, being one mark six shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. A sheet of good paper from a copyist of about fifty-nine at a counter in a room off that market is a penny and was paid out of the holder of that writ's own hand on the said day and not out of that bag, and the penny is not a figure of anything.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she bought one sheet of good paper at a counter in a room off that market on the said morning for a penny out of her own hand, and the woman at that counter, who is about fifty-nine and has been at that counter for twenty-two years, did not ask what it was for and has never once asked what she is copying. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he got the sixth piece off the stones, turned it, and put it back the other way up, and left it there, and that is the whole of what he did with it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and seventeen days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and nineteen days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a tenth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and fifty-seven days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and twenty-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in forty-one days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for fifty-nine days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for twenty-eight days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is eleven days old and has been there eleven days and nobody has answered it.**

---

The joiner is a man of about thirty-four and he does not live over his shop and he was at the door of it at about ten in the morning on the said Saturday with a plane in one hand, and Hobb Lee had the room from ten and the door was off the latch, because it is a joiner's room on a week day and the joiner is in and out of it eight times a day and has never once locked it and there is nothing in that room to lock.

He cleaned the table, which took an hour, and it is a joiner's table and the glue is a week deep in the grain, and he did it with a scraper and then with his sleeve and then with a bucket and a cloth, and he did not do it because the volume needed a clean table. He did it because it is a table and he was in the room.

The benches came out from under the wall. There are four of them, two on each long side, and they are the joiner's benches and they are going to be back under the wall at six o'clock on a Sunday, and he wiped the two that had been against the wall and left the other two where they were.

The lamp is on a nail and it is not lit and it is not going to be lit, because the light on the mole head has not been lit since the seventh of May past and the lens has been out of it since then, and the room has one window that does not shut, and at half past four on a September afternoon there is enough light in that room to read a page by.

The bowl was on the step from two o'clock until six o'clock on the said Saturday, because Tom Barlas puts it out on a Saturday as well as on a Sunday, and the joiner had told him in August that if a man is going to come up a stair on a day there is no reason to make him knock on a door that is not shut. It was empty at six o'clock. There was ninepence in it on the Sunday before and there is no figure for the ninepence and none is going to be printed, and Tom Barlas carries it in at six and puts it in a drawer in a shop two doors down with other money in it, and the two people who have ever been in that shop are Tom Barlas and a boy of about sixteen who comes in on a Saturday and does not look in drawers.

"You have not asked me for anything," Tom Barlas said, at about half past three, from the bottom of the stairs.

"No."

"Everybody who wants a room asks for something. They ask for a window opened, or a lamp, or for the door to be shut, or they ask how much it is, and then they tell you they have not got it. I do not mind that. I mind being asked twice." He went back down. "It is out at two and in at six and I do not ask who comes and I have never written one of them down. If I wrote one of them down it would be a list, and a list in a room that anybody can pay half a penny to walk into is a list of everybody in this harbor who could not afford half a penny."

Hobb Lee brought the barrow up at about half past twelve, as he had said he would, and stood it at the top of the two flights with the two handles up, and it is a barrow with a wheel at the front and a flat platform, and it belongs to the joiner, and the joiner lent it on the Thursday and was not asked for it and is not going to be thanked for it and has a barrow to lend on a Saturday.

Then he came down to that shed at about four o'clock with the count of heads he meant to read on the Sunday, which is twenty-two of the thirty-one, and the nine he was not going to read were six pieces of glass off three wrecks and a lamp and a door plate and a box of tally sticks, and reading them would be nine minutes, and nine minutes in a room is a different thing from nothing.

"Twenty-two," he said. "Two o'clock to about half past four, and then the benches go back under the wall. And if a woman of about sixty-one comes up those stairs at one o'clock with a box on a barrow, then it is going to be half past one before that table is clear, and I am not going to stand at the top of that stair at half past one and tell a room of twenty people to wait. The barrow comes up at one, she puts it on that table herself, and I read."

---

Arthur Sennett is about thirty-one and he writes figures on a slate for a buyer at a fish stall, and he has done it for nine years, and his hand is the only thing about him that anybody in that market has ever noticed.

He came up that stair at about half past four on the Saturday afternoon because the door was off the latch and the bottom of the stair was open, and he had a slate under his arm and a stub of pencil behind his ear, and he stopped about two-thirds of the way up when he saw the volume lying open at the end of the table with about four inches of clean wood on either side of it, where the battens are not.

Hobb Lee did not stop him and did not speak to him. He went downstairs at about ten past five and left the room.

It took Arthur Sennett about fifty minutes. He turned the pages from the back to the front and then from the front to the back, and he read every one of them, and he did not write anything, and he did not go and get anybody, and when he had finished he shut the volume, squared it to the edge of the table, and put his slate under his arm and went down the stairs and out into Fish Street and went home.

Hobb Lee came back up at about ten past five and found the volume shut and squared and the benches where he had left them, and he did not know that a man had been in that room until the following afternoon, and that is how it happened, and nobody arranged it.

What Arthur Sennett said out loud on the Sunday the twelfth, at the bottom of those stairs, before he went up, was this, and it is put down here because it is the only thing he said in ten days, and it is not the finding and it is not the beginning of the finding:

"You have got a woman in there who would not have her oil entered, and you have put her in at the same size as a wreck, and somebody read that out on Sunday in a room with twenty people in it. I have been writing figures on a slate for a buyer for nine years and I know exactly what a figure does when it is read out in a room, and that one is going to be in about eleven markets by Tuesday. And I am not saying that is wrong. I am saying you have put a price on being a person and I do not know that anybody in that harbor has been asked whether they want that."

And Hobb Lee said, "No, they have not, and I have thought about it since the Friday, and it is too late to do anything about it now that it is in there, and I would do it again, and both of those are true and I am not going to pretend that they are comfortable."

**Nothing came into that yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and the bag is two marks seven shillings and fourpence and did not move on that day, and it is short of that wage by one mark six shillings. One sheet of good paper from a counter in a room off that market was bought on the said day for a penny out of the holder of that writ's own hand and not out of that bag, and the woman at that counter has been at that counter for twenty-two years and has never once asked what she is copying. A room over a joiner's shop was got ready on the said Saturday for the second Sunday in about a month of Sundays, and a man of about thirty-four who lets that room out put a bowl on the top stone of its step at two o'clock and carried it in at six, and it was empty, and he had not been asked for anything by anybody in that room. A man of about thirty-one called Arthur Sennett, who writes figures on a slate for a buyer at a fish stall and has done for nine years, came up that stair at about half past four on the said afternoon because the door was off the latch, and read the whole of that volume from the back to the front and from the front to the back, and wrote nothing, and shut it, and squared it to the edge of that table, and went home, and nobody knew he had been in the room until the following afternoon. A barrow with two handles was carried up two flights of stairs at about half past twelve on the said day and stood at the top of them. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

and that one sheet of good paper was bought at a counter in a room off that market for a penny out of the holder of that writ's own hand and not out of that bag; and that a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street was got ready on the said Saturday for the second Sunday, and that a man of about thirty-four who lets that room out put a bowl on the top stone of its step at two o'clock and carried it in at six and the bowl was empty, and that he has never written one of the people who came up that stair down; and that a man of about forty-six read twenty-two of the thirty-one heads of a public volume on the Sunday the twelfth of September next and is not reading the other nine, being six pieces of glass off three wrecks together with a lamp, a door plate and a box of tally sticks; and that a barrow with a wheel at the front and two handles was carried up two flights of stairs at about half past twelve on the said day and stood at the top of them, and belongs to the joiner, who lent it on the Thursday; and that a man of about thirty-one called Arthur Sennett, who writes figures on a slate for a buyer at a fish stall and has done for nine years, came up that stair at about half past four on the said afternoon because the door was off the latch, and read the whole of that volume from the back to the front and then from the front to the back, being about fifty minutes, and wrote nothing and asked nobody anything and shut that volume and squared it to the edge of that table and went down the stairs and out into Fish Street; and that it is not known in that yard that he was in that room until the afternoon of the Sunday the twelfth of September next, and that nobody in that yard sent for him or spoke to him or knew that he was coming; and that the paper at the top left of the door of that room is eleven days old on the said day and has been answered by nobody.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a joiner's table has the glue of a week in the grain and that cleaning it took the man of about forty-six an hour and that he did it because it is a table and not because a volume needed a clean table; and that the bowl on that step carried ninepence on the Sunday the fifth of September past and was empty on the said Saturday and there is no figure for the ninepence and none is going to be printed; and that a man who lets a room out has never written one of the people who came up that stair down, and has given his reason in his own mouth, being that a list in a room anybody can pay half a penny to walk into is a list of everybody in that harbor who could not afford half a penny; and that a man of about thirty-one said one sentence at the bottom of a stair on the Saturday the eleventh and that sentence is not the finding and is not the beginning of the finding, and that what he said was that a figure read out in a room is in about eleven markets by Tuesday, and that a refusal entered at the same size as a wreck is a price put on being a person, and that nobody in that harbor has been asked whether they want that; and that the reason the holder of that writ did not say a word to a man of about thirty-one on that stair is that nobody in that yard is to be asked about that volume and about who read it, and that a room is not a place where a thing is to be made known; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
