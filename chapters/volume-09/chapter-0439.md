# Chapter 439

*Friday the First Day of October, a Man of About Forty-Four Asking at About Eleven in the Morning for a Line to Be Struck Out of the Convoy's Own Sheet and a Woman of About Thirty-Four Asking at About Half Past Eleven for a Line of Hers to Be Struck Out of the Same Sheet and Two Men on the First Lay Asking at About Two in the Afternoon for a Line to Be Struck Out of That Yard's Own Leaf, and the Same Nine Words Given to All Three of Them, a Word Said Out Loud Once in a Shed and Not Taken by Being Said, a Fourth Man in That Bell With a Reason Written Down Before He Went Down and a Chain Weight Over a Rail at About Twenty Minutes to Two in the Afternoon, a Tender of About Forty Tons Whose Own Hours End at About Five in the Afternoon and Whose Own Rate Is His Tender's, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Out at About Eleven in the Morning for Canvas and Tar and a Spare Bight of Line and Copper, and the Bag at One Hundred and Ninety-Six Pence*

Friday the first day of October, at the head of the third lay, on a bank about four hundred yards down that foul, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about eleven in the morning for canvas and tar and a spare bight of line and copper, and there are one hundred and ninety-six pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and forty-four pence, being two marks ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing came into that yard on the said day.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about half past ten until about twelve and was in it for the whole of what is written below. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he put the eleventh piece down on its own edge where it had stood on the thirtieth and left it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and thirty-seven days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and thirty-nine days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a twelfth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and seventy-seven days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-third night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in sixty-one days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for seventy-nine days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the sixteenth day and the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the said day and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-eight days.**

---

Jos Lund came into that shed at about eleven in the morning and put his hand on the sheet of good paper that is on the wall of it.

"There is a line in here with my name against it that says I got to that bank eleven minutes late on the Tuesday," he said, "and it is true, and it is in a book that is in a shed that anybody can come into, and I would like it struck out."

"Why."

"Because I have got five men ashore and my boat is the one they are all looking at and I do not want to be a name on a page in a shed for the rest of my life over eleven minutes."

Nessa Pike took the slate and did not look at it.

"That line stays," she said. "Nothing comes out of this book once it is in, for any reason, to anybody."

---

Mina Kett came in behind him at about half past eleven and stood at the end of that bench.

"There is a line in that sheet with my name against it and it says I was paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence," she said. "That is not my figure. My figure is five shillings. I have been telling you since the Sunday that that is a caulker's figure and that it is not mine, and you put it on a wall in a shed with my name against it, and there is a man of about forty-six in this harbor who cannot read who could stand in front of that wall in about three weeks' time and be told that a bellwright is paid a mark and eightpence in Morrow Quay. Take my line out."

"That line stays," said Nessa Pike. "Nothing comes out of this book once it is in, for any reason, to anybody."

"Then put a second line under mine and let it stand."

"A second line goes in and the first one stays where it is, and I will tell you now that you are not going to like it, and here it is: *that she was paid at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, being the figure in that harbor for a day's work with a boat and two men and also the figure for a caulker's day, and being her own figure of five shillings, being sixty pence, before she came here, and she has said so once out loud and has been underpaid by it and has not stopped work and has not asked for it a second time.* That is in the second column, and there is no column in that yard's book for a thing like that and there is not going to be one."

"Then put it in the second column and let my line stand and let the sixpence stand there in the middle of it where anybody can find it."

"It stands there," said Nessa Pike. "And it is going to stand there after this yard is a hole in the ground."

Nobody thanked her and she did not ask.

---

Two men off the first lay came in at about two in the afternoon. They had been in the boat of about twenty-two tons on the Tuesday when she struck that baulk. They wanted a line out of that yard's own leaf, and the line they wanted out was the one in the first column with the baulk in it and about nine foot of garboard and the hole in the lee rail.

"That line stays," said Nessa Pike. "Nothing comes out of this book once it is in, for any reason, to anybody."

It is the same nine words. It went to a man of about forty-four with a boat on a bank, and it went to a woman of about thirty-four who had been underpaid by a hundred and eight pence a day for four days and had said so out loud once, and it went to two men off the first lay in the same hour, and the fourth of those people asked for it and got it in the same nine words, and there was no column in that book for which those nine words were any different from any other column, and that is the whole of it.

---

At about half past two in the afternoon Ormond Kettle, of about forty-four, master of a boat of about nineteen tons lying on the hard at the third lay with her heel off since June, said the word out loud for the first time in that harbor, and he said it to about six men in that shed.

"A keelwright," he said.

"No," said Mina Kett, who was at the end of that bench. "No, and I will tell you what it is on my coast, because you have not got it right and you have had four days to get it right. On my coast it means a man who keeps a keel right. It does not mean what it says here, which is a man who fits one, and there is a difference between those two that has taken me nine years to learn and it is not a small one. It means the man who comes back."

"It is the whole of that," said Calder Marr. "It is a trade and it is not a power. It does not settle anything and it does not price anything and it does not make anybody better off than he was on the Thursday before. It is a man, a chain, a weight, a tide and a day at a figure in chalk, and it stops when the four of them stop, and I am not going to stand in this shed and tell anybody they may put it on a sheet, and if anybody in this harbor puts it on a sheet they will be putting a thing on a sheet that this yard cannot answer for, and I have not got a thing I cannot answer for."

"Eleven masters were asked one at a time and one of them said no and was not asked again," said Sedge Rundle from the doorway, "and I will tell you what that is, because you have not counted it. That is a keelwright. The asking is not the work."

Nobody repeated it after him and nobody wrote it down in a shed and it is on no sheet in that harbor.

---

The fourth man went down in that bell at about half past five in the morning with the reason written against him before he went down, and he was a man of about fifty-eight who has swept out that room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street every Sunday since it was let out and who has never once been asked to be in anything, and what he said when he came up at about twenty past six in the morning was that he could not tell anything at all, and that he had gone down there to be able to say that he had been and could not tell anything, and that a man of his age in a bell in eight foot is a man who is going down there to be a second pair of eyes and not a pair of hands, and that if anybody wants a thing read out of that boat they will have to get somebody younger and that is not a thing this yard can fix with a figure.

The chain came over the rail at about twenty minutes to two in the afternoon, the fourth in five days, and the reason was written down with the hour against it.

Halvard Ness put his chain away at about five o'clock on the said day and went up that slip and his own tender's pay was eight o'clock on the Saturday and it came out of his own tender and there was no figure of it on any sheet in that harbor and he did not ask for one.

---

There is no flour in that market and there has been none since the spring. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and a figure of four hundred and fifty pence stands on a board at an end stall with three hundred and seventy-eight pence visible above it and both dates on that board, and that figure cannot be taken off. The sheet of six lines is on the wall at the back of that stall for the sixteenth day on the outside of that figure and no claim has been lodged under it and nobody has paid the one hundred and sixty-eight pence at the foot of it. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-eight days and is a different paper on a different wall. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the said day. The mole-head light is out. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One berth on the third lay is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven. Forty-nine weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.

**A man of about forty-four with a boat of about nineteen tons lying on a bank four hundred yards down that foul asked at about eleven in the morning on the said Friday for a line to be struck out of a sheet on the wall of that shed which carries his name and says that he got to that bank eleven minutes late on the Tuesday, and gave his reason in his own mouth, and was refused. A woman of about thirty-four who had been paid at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall for four days asked at about half past eleven for her line to be struck out of the same sheet on the ground that that figure is not hers and that a man of about forty-six in that harbor who cannot read could be told in three weeks that a bellwright is paid a mark and eightpence in Morrow Quay, and was refused, and asked for a second line under it and got one, and that line is in the second column of that yard's own leaf and says that she was paid at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, being the figure in that harbor for a day's work with a boat and two men and also for a caulker's day, and that her own figure is five shillings, being sixty pence, before she came here, and that she has said so once out loud and has been underpaid by it and has not stopped work and has not asked for it a second time. Two men off the first lay asked at about two in the afternoon for a line to be struck out of that yard's own leaf which carries about nine foot of garboard and a hole in a lee rail, and were refused. It is the same nine words in all three cases and there is no column in that book for which those nine words are different from any other column. A master of about forty-four said the word out loud for the first time in that harbor at about half past two in the afternoon and was told it is a trade and not a power and settles nothing and prices nothing and makes nobody better off than he was on the Thursday before, and a bellwright of about thirty-four told him that on her coast it means a man who keeps a keel right and not a man who fits one, and a master of about forty-six out of Lowfen standing in that doorway said that the asking is not the work and that eleven masters asked one at a time with one of them saying no and not being asked again is a keelwright. Nobody repeated it after him and it is on no sheet in that harbor. A man of about fifty-eight who sweeps out the room over a joiner's shop every Sunday went down in that bell at about half past five in the morning with his reason written down before he went down and came up at about twenty past six and said he could not tell anything at all and that a man of his age in a bell in eight foot goes down to be a second pair of eyes and not a pair of hands. The chain came over the rail at about twenty minutes to two in the afternoon, the fourth in five days, and the reason was written down with the hour against it, and a young man of about twenty-three put his chain away at about five o'clock and went up that slip and his own pay was eight o'clock on the Saturday and it came out of his own tender and there is no figure of it on any sheet in that harbor. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about eleven in the morning for canvas and tar and a spare bight of line and copper, and the bag stands at one hundred and ninety-six pence, being one mark and three shillings.**

*First column, entered on Friday the first day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that four people asked on the said day for a line to be struck out of a record and were refused in the same nine words, being a man of about forty-four at about eleven in the morning against a line in a sheet on the wall of that shed which carries his name and an hour and says that he got to that bank eleven minutes late on the Tuesday; a woman of about thirty-four at about half past eleven against a line in the same sheet which carries her name and an hour and the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall; and two men off the first lay at about two in the afternoon against a line in the first column of that yard's own leaf which carries about nine foot of garboard and a hole in a lee rail; and that the nine words are: that line stays, nothing comes out of this book once it is in, for any reason, to anybody; and that the same nine words were given to all four and there is no column in that book for which they are different from any other; and that a second line was entered under the second request in the second column of that leaf, with the hour against it, and the first line stands; and that a master of about forty-four of the third lay said a word out loud at about half past two in the afternoon for the first time in that harbor and a master of about forty-six out of Lowfen said in that doorway that the asking is not the work and that eleven masters asked one at a time with one of them saying no and not being asked again is a keelwright; and that nobody repeated it after him and it is on no sheet in that harbor; and that a man of about fifty-eight who sweeps out the room over a joiner's shop went down in that bell at about half past five in the morning with his reason written down before he went down and was in there about fifty minutes and came up at about twenty past six; and that the chain came over the rail at about twenty minutes to two in the afternoon, being the fourth such cutoff in five days; and that a young man of about twenty-three who is second hand on a steam tender of about forty tons put his chain away at about five o'clock in the afternoon and his own pay was at eight o'clock on the Saturday out of his own tender and no figure of it is on any sheet in that harbor; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about eleven in the morning for canvas and tar and a spare bight of line and copper, and nothing came into it on the said day, and that three hundred and sixty-four minus one hundred and sixty-eight is one hundred and ninety-six pence at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings, being short of the wage of four marks by four hundred and forty-four pence, being two marks ten shillings and fourpence; and that no charge was placed on that cistern on the said day and the said day was not a wipe and was not a wage Tuesday.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a request to strike a line out of any column is refused in the same words whatever the column is, is in the ship's own mouth and is that a line comes out of that book when it stops being what happened and not before, and that a yard with a rule about which lines may be taken out has a rule somebody will use; and that the reason the reason for that refusal is in this column and not in the first is that it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole, and that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count four requests and four refusals and cannot take the nine words out of it without reading them; and that the reason a man of about fifty-eight who sweeps out a room was put in that bell at all is in the ship's own mouth and is that he came and stood at the head of that slip and asked to be used and was asked for the day first, and that a man who asks to be used cannot afterwards say he was used; and that the reason what he said when he came up, which was that he could not tell anything at all, is entered in that book at the length it was spoken and not at the length it would read well, is that a record which improves a man's account of his own hands is not a record; and that the reason a bellwright of about thirty-four asked for her line to be struck rather than asking for the figure to be changed is in her own mouth and is that she was not underpaid on that line, she was paid the wrong figure on it and the line is what happened; and that the reason the hundred and eight pence a day she is under by is on the face of that second-column line and in no other place in that yard's book, and is that five hundred and thirty-two minus one hundred and sixty-eight is three hundred and sixty-four and that a mark and eightpence is what a caulker is paid in that harbor and is what a bellwright off the western reef line has been paid for nine years; and that the reason no word is on any sheet is in the holder's own mouth and is that this yard cannot answer for a word, and that a man of about forty-six who asked for one out loud on the Thursday before was told he would not have it in a shed and he said he would not ask again and he did not; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Friday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
