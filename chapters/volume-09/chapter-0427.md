# Chapter 427

*Sunday the Nineteenth Day of September, a Room over a Joiner's Shop Let Out for the Third Time With a Bowl on the Step at Two o'Clock and About Sixteen Going Up and Three Coming Down and Nine Heads Read Out Loud and No Claim in Any of Them, a Man of About Fifty-Eight Who Takes the Rents in Two Houses on the Third Lay Standing at a Door at About Eleven in the Morning Giving a Woman of About Sixty-One a Reason in His Own Mouth, a Box Carried Down a Slip on a Bad Knee Between One and Half Past Two in the Afternoon, and Nobody Charged and Nobody Made Whole and Nobody in That Harbor Told*

Sunday the nineteenth day of September, on that third lay, in Fish Street, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Sunday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are five hundred and seventy-six pence in the bag, being three marks eight shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Sunday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was not on that third lay at any hour of the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he was on the eleventh piece for the second time in ten days and did nothing to it at all between ten and four and put his hand flat on it once at about half past two. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-five days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and twenty-seven days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eleventh week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-five days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirty-first night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in forty-nine days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for sixty-seven days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-six days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is nineteen days old and has been answered by nobody, and the fourth line of it ends on the twentieth day of September next, which is tomorrow.**

---

On the Friday before last, at about two in the afternoon at the head of a quay, a man of about twenty-six who stacks empties said out loud, to about four people, that the woman who carried a box up two flights of stairs to the room over the joiner's shop on the Sunday before was a woman of about sixty off the third lay and had a rope walk in the back of her room.

He was not asked where he had got it. He said he had got it from a man who had been on the second lay and had seen a box go up the stairs, and he did not name the man, and the man has not been asked, and nobody put him up to saying it.

By Saturday there were people in that harbor who knew which lay she was on. By Sunday morning, when she opened her door at about half past ten, there were two men she had never spoken to standing on the stones opposite it who looked at her and then went and stood further off.

---

Ruben Priest is of about fifty-eight and he takes the rents in two houses on that slip and has taken this one for eleven years at nine marks a year, being one thousand four hundred and forty pence, paid out of rope in every one of those eleven years.

He was at her door at about eleven in the morning on the Sunday.

"Nell," he said. "I am going to say a thing once and I am going to say it straight and I am not coming back to it."

"Go on."

"There is a sheet on a wall at the back of an end stall that came up on the Wednesday and it says that anybody who has been in a room with a piece of worked glass with a scene in it is to be entered in a claim book at a counter and is not to be at work on glass until he has been. That sheet has been on that wall three days and every person in this harbor has read it or had it read to them, and there are people on this slip who were in that room, because there were about twenty people in that room and they have told anybody who stands still long enough."

"And who said so."

"That is not the half of it and you know it is not the half of it, and neither of us is going to find out who said it, and I am not going to waste the morning of either of us on it," said Ruben Priest. "The half of it is that I have a wife in that house and a girl of eleven and there is no way on this coast for me to go and stand in a room and ask a person face to face whether they have been in a room with a piece of glass, and that whatever you say to me in this doorway, you are telling me that you have not, and I have got no way of knowing whether you have, and that is the whole of my difficulty and it is a real one and it is not you that has put it there."

"You have not asked me."

"No."

"You have not asked me one question."

"No," said Ruben Priest, "and I am not going to, because if I ask you then you answer me and then I have made you say a thing out loud in a street that I will have heard and that I cannot get back out of my own head, and I would rather have asked you nothing and be wrong than have asked you and be right."

She put her hand on the door frame and left it there.

"When," she said.

"After the quarter. You have paid to the end of it and I will not have it out of you and I will not give it to anybody else either. After that the room is not yours and that is the end of it and I have not got any other room and I am not going to look for one for you, because if I looked for one for you then in a month somebody would stand at that door and ask me why I had put you in it."

That was the whole of what either of them said in that doorway.

---

The box is about three foot long and a foot wide and eight inches deep and it has a rope round it, and she cut the rope with the short knife out of her pocket at about one o'clock and lifted the thing out and put it back in and cut the rope again and put the knot in it the way she puts a knot in it.

She did not take the panel out of it. It has been in that box since the year she was nineteen and it went down two flights of stairs and up one in the middle of August and down two flights of stairs again on the Sunday the twelfth, and on the said Sunday it went down a slip and up the second lay in the same box.

Nobody on that third lay carried anything for her and nobody offered and there was nobody to offer.

Ruben Priest went in and shut his own door at about ten past twelve.

She went down that slip backwards with the wheel of the barrow against the step above her, the way she came down it on the twelfth, and it took her about an hour and a half, and at the top of the second lay she turned round and looked back at that slip for about a second and a half and then went on.

The room she has had for eleven years has a rope walk in the back of it about forty feet long and about five feet wide, with a handle at one end and a drum at the other, and it does not run, and there is not another rope walk in that harbor, and there is nobody in that harbor with the right to do anything about it and nobody who is going to.

Nobody was charged with anything on the said Sunday. Nobody was made whole. There is no figure in that harbor's own leaf against that room or that rope walk or those eleven years or that box or that knee. And nobody in that harbor has been told that anything was done for her, and nobody in that harbor has thanked her, and nobody in that yard went up that slip on the said day to say one word to her about the twelfth.

---

In Fish Street the bowl went on the top stone of the step at two o'clock and about sixteen people went up and three came down, and eleven of the sixteen could not read.

Hobb Lee stood at the end of the long table and read nine heads out of thirty-one, in the order they were in, and wrote his own name and the hour at which he finished each one under it in his own hand, and the first was at about two minutes past two and the last at about twenty-one minutes past three.

There was no claim in any of the nine and there was no claim on that table and nobody asked him to read one, and about a dozen people in that room had been told about the sheet at the back of the end stall before they came up those stairs and about nine of them had not.

The woman of about fifty-eight came up those stairs at about half past three on the said afternoon with her slate in her hand, which she has not done in three Sundays, and stood at the end of the table while a man of about thirty-one read a head about two doors off a house.

"That is not on that table," she said. "A sheet has been on a wall in that front street since Wednesday saying what is in the air of a room, and it has not been read out on that table and it has not been entered in that book, and I am not going to stand here and ask anybody to read it out in there."

"What would you like me to do about it."

"Nothing," said the woman of about fifty-eight. "I have come up these stairs to say it out loud in front of about twenty people so that it is a thing that was said and not a thing that was in somebody's head, and then I am going back down to my stall, because there is no barrel of lamp oil in this harbor and it is not coming and the price on my board is not going down."

Tom Barlas carried the bowl in at six and there was ninepence in it and he has never written one of the people who came up that stair down.

He did not ask where Nell Carrow had gone and he did not know and he was not going to go up a slip to find out.

**A woman of about sixty-one of that third lay was put out of a room she has had for eleven years at nine marks a year, being one thousand four hundred and forty pence, paid out of rope in every one of those eleven years, by a man of about fifty-eight who takes the rents in two houses on that slip, at about eleven in the morning on the said Sunday. He gave his reason in his own mouth: that a sheet has been on a wall at the back of an end stall since the Wednesday and that there are people on that slip who were in the room over the joiner's shop on the twelfth, and that there is no way on that coast for him to go and ask a person face to face whether they have been in a room with a piece of glass, and that he has a wife and a girl of eleven in that house. He asked her nothing, and said in his own mouth that he was not going to, because if he asked then she would answer him and he would never get it out of his own head, and that he would rather have asked nothing and be wrong than have asked and be right. She asked him one question, which was when, and was told it was after the quarter, and that she had paid to the end of it and that he would not have it out of her and would not give it to anybody else, and that he had no other room and was not going to look for one for her. That was the whole of what was said in that doorway. She cut the rope off her box at about one o'clock, lifted the panel out and put it back in and knotted the rope the way she knots it, and carried the box down a slip on a bad knee backwards with the barrow's wheel against the step above her, and it took her about an hour and a half, and nobody on that lay carried anything for her and nobody offered. The room has a rope walk in the back of it about forty feet long that does not run and there is not another rope walk in that harbor. Nobody was charged with anything on the said Sunday and nobody was made whole, and no figure in that yard's leaf stands against that room or that rope walk or those eleven years or that box or that knee, and nobody in that harbor has been told that anything was done for her and nobody in that harbor has thanked her and nobody in that yard went up that slip on the said day. A room over a joiner's shop was let out for the third time on the said Sunday and about sixteen went up and three came down and eleven of the sixteen could not read, and a man of about forty-six read nine heads out of thirty-one and wrote his own name and the hour under each in his own hand, and there was no claim in any of the nine and there was no claim on that table, and about nine of the sixteen had been told about the sheet at the back of the end stall before they came up those stairs. Nothing came into that yard on the said Sunday and nothing went out of it, and the bag is five hundred and seventy-six pence, being three marks eight shillings, and is short of the wage of four marks by sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is nineteen days old and its fourth line ends tomorrow and has been answered by nobody. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

and that he gave the reason in his own mouth, being that a sheet has been on a wall at the back of an end stall since the fifteenth day of September past and that there are people on that slip who were in the room over the joiner's shop on the twelfth, and that there is no way on that coast for him to stand in a room and ask a person face to face whether they have been in a room with a piece of glass; and that he asked her nothing and said in his own mouth that he was not going to, because an answer given in a street would stay in his own head; and that she asked him one question, being when, and was told it was after the quarter and that she had paid to the end of it and that he would not have it out of her and would not give it to anybody else, and that he had no other room and was not going to look for one; and that that was the whole of what was said in that doorway; and that she cut the rope off the box at about one o'clock, took the panel out of it and put it back in and knotted the rope, and did not take the panel out of the box; and that on the said Sunday a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street was let out for the third time with the bowl on the top stone of the step at two o'clock, and that about sixteen people went up and three came down and eleven of the sixteen could not read; and that a man of about forty-six read nine heads out of thirty-one in the order they were in, the first at about two minutes past two and the last at about twenty-one minutes past three, and wrote his own name and the hour at which he finished each one under it in his own hand; and that the paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is nineteen days old and its fourth line ends on the twentieth of September next, being the following day, and has been answered by nobody.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man of about fifty-eight who takes rents in two houses did not ask one question at a door is in his own mouth and is that an answer given in a street stays in the head of the man who asked for it, and that he would rather have asked nothing and be wrong than have asked and be right; and that the reason he did not go and find another room for a woman of about sixty-one is in his own mouth and is that a room found for her now would be a room somebody stood at in a month asking why she was in it; and that the reason no figure stands in any column of that yard's leaf against that room or that rope walk or those eleven years is that a woman who makes her own rent out of rope is not on a list and is not a figure and that this yard has never had a figure for it and is not going to invent one at the end of a bad week; and that the reason the man of about twenty-six at the quay is not named in the first column for what he said on the Friday is that what he said was said by him and about a woman and this yard did not hear him say it and has not asked him about it; and that the reason there is no entry in the second column for the man of about fifty-eight who let that room go is that he is not a man with a grievance against anybody and has not been paid anything and has not lost a figure, and that a person who is not owed a penny and has not had one is not a cost that a ledger has a column for; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Sunday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
