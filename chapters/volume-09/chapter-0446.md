# Chapter 446

*Friday the Eighth Day of October, a Line of Nine Soundings Taken Off a Sheet of Paper in a Room on a Hill and Carried Out in a Pocket by Hand, and Not the Lead, Which Did Not Come Down That Hill, Three Accounts of the Same Stone at the Fifth of Those Nine Soundings in About Four and a Half Fathoms That Do Not Agree With Each Other and Are Kept in That Order and Are Not Settled, a Man of About Forty-Four Going Against a Line of That Account With His Own Name and the Hour Under It and the Account Not Being Changed, a Diver Going Down Once on That Line at About Two in the Afternoon Under a Tender of About Twenty-Three and Fifteen Fathoms of Chain and a Weight on the Tail of It That Came Over the Rail at About Ten Past Three With an Hour and a Reason Written Against It, and What He Was Able to Put a Name to When He Came Up and What He Was Not, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Out at About Nine in the Morning and Coming Back at About Ten, and the Bag at One Hundred and Ninety-Six Pence*

Friday the eighth day of October, in that shed, in a room on that hill, and on about four and a half fathoms off that mole.

**The said Friday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon for iron clamps and a plank of white oak four inches by nine and eleven feet long, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the sixth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name stood on a public sheet for the fifth day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he did not go near the water. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-four days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-six days and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a thirteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-four days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fiftieth night. Two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-eight days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-six days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-third day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-eighth day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-fifth day.**

---

The line went out of that harbor in three pockets on the Friday morning and it was not the lead.

That is the whole of what a boundary is for. The lead is about the size of a man's palm and it is in a drawer in a building on that hill and it did not come down that hill on the fifth of October and it has not come down it since, and nobody in this harbor has held it, and nobody in this harbor is going to hold it. What came down was a sheet of about four feet of paper with a line of nine soundings written out on it in the plainest hand that a woman of about fifty-nine could produce at a counter in a room off that market, and a description of which three of the nine were shallow, and a note at the foot that said the lead has an office's mark and a hand cut on it and a person's own mark cut small and off to one side, and no figure at all.

Jens Ower, of about twenty-nine, of no lay, who lightered about eleven tons, had that sheet in a fold of oilcloth in the front of his jacket at about seven in the morning and had copied the nine soundings onto the back of his own hand with a bit of charcoal, and he said on the quay, to nobody, that he had done that because he could not read the numbers and could count nine of anything.

The line runs out of that harbor between the point and the north end of that mole and then about two miles and a quarter offshore, and the second, the fifth and the eighth of the nine are shallow, and the fifth is the shallow one and it is in about four and a half fathoms on the top of a stone.

---

Three accounts of one stone, in one afternoon, in the order they were said, and all three of them are in the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the reason against the line, and none of them has been taken back and none of them has been settled.

The first was the holder of that writ's, and he said it at about half past ten in the morning from the rail of a boat of about eleven tons with a lead line going down beside him, and the small steam tender of about forty tons was lying off with fifteen fathoms of chain off her quarter round a spurle and back with a weight on the tail of it, and Halvard Ness was on her after deck with his hand on it.

"There is a stone about the size of a kitchen table lying with its long side across the tide, and there is a piece of iron standing out of the middle of it about nine inches, and it is square and it is not rusted through, and I am saying that because I have seen it twice, once in June and once on the twenty-fourth day of August, and both of those times I was in a bell at about ten feet and looking down at it through the water, and a square thing nine inches out of a stone at four and a half fathoms does not grow."

Nate Pry, of about forty-four, of that lay, who had carried a piece of worked glass about two hands square out of a boat's hold in June on his own back for a shilling and a half, went out in his own boat of about nine tons with two men in her and took the same mark off the same sheet, and he was on the water at about one o'clock in the afternoon.

"There is a stone about the size of a kitchen table," he said. "There is nothing standing out of the middle of it. I lay off it and I looked at it for as long as it takes to boil an egg and there is nothing on that stone at all, and I have been in a lot of holds in this harbor in thirty years and I know what a piece of iron in a stone looks like from underneath, and there is not one."

Jens Ower, of about twenty-nine, went out in his lighter of about eleven tons at about a quarter past two in the afternoon and put his own hook over the side and got it on the stone and came up with it hooked in the rope.

"There is a stone," he said, "and there are two things on it, and one of them is a square piece of iron about the size of my hand standing up out of it, and the other one is a bottle with a cork in it, and the cork is not in and the bottle is not empty and there is a paper in the bottle and I have not read the paper and I have put the cork back in it as far as it will go and I have put the bottle back on the stone where it was because I do not know whose it is."

Nobody in those three boats could say the other one was wrong, and the three of them sat in that shed at about half past five in the afternoon with a slate between them and Nessa Pike wrote all three down in the order they were said and did not ask any of them a second question about any of it, and the three accounts of about two foot of soft plank in a boat of about nineteen tons that were entered in the last ten days stand in the same leaf in the same order and are not settled either, and there are now six accounts in that book that do not agree with each other and not one of the six has been settled and nobody in this harbor is going to settle any of them.

Nate Pry went back up the west basin at about six o'clock with the sheet of about four feet of paper in his hand and put his own words against the fifth line of that account, in his own hand, with his own name and the hour under it, and the line he went against is the line that says the thing may be used for a route, and what he wrote against it is that a route is a route if it is there twice and that he has been on it once and there was nothing on the stone.

That went under the account on the same day and both are printed, and the account was not changed, and it says at the head of it that the person who entered it does not get to strike a line out of it.

---

Calder Marr went down in a bell off the port quarter of a boat of about eleven tons at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and he went down once, and Halvard Ness had the chain taut and the weight on the tail of it, and the reason he was going down was written down before he went down and the hour was against the reason, and a man of about fifty-eight who has swept out the room over a joiner's shop in that harbor on every Sunday since it was let out and has been in a bell once and could tell nothing at all came along in the tender and stood on her after deck with a line in his hand and did not go down.

He was in the water about thirty-five minutes.

The chain came over that rail at about ten past three in the afternoon and the weight came up after it, and the reason was written down in the ship's left hand at about half past four with the hour against it, being that the bottom of that stone was not visible in about four and a half fathoms on a west wind and that no man was going to hold his breath for a third look at a piece of iron.

He sat on the tender's after deck with his coat off and his hands on his knees and he had a cold in his teeth and there was a taste of iron in his mouth like a nail, and he said the two of those things out loud to about four men on that deck because that is what a body says and a body says it to people and not to a paper, and the second of them had been said in that harbor before in the last month by a man in a bell and had been said twice by him and once by two men on a berth and once in a market, and it is an aftertaste and it is not a fever and it is not a complaint and it is not glass and it is not an illness and it does not tell anybody anything about anything.

Then he said the third thing, which was new, and he said it slowly.

"I can put a name to the afternoon," he said. "I cannot put a name to what was in the boat with me. The twenty-ninth of June, out of that harbor, on the ebb, and I know the day and I know the water and I know whose hand it was that I can see in my own head when I think about it, and there was a second man in that boat and I do not know which of two men it was, and I am not going to stand here and pick one out of the four that it could be, and I am not going to have anybody in this harbor pick one for me."

Nessa Pike wrote that into the second column of that leaf at about half past six in the evening with the hour against it and no name against it, and the reason she wrote it in the second column and not in the first is on the face of the ledger and in her own mouth, and it is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that a man was in the water on the eighth of October and cannot count what a man can put a name to about the twenty-ninth of June, and a figure in the first column against that would be a lie in a book that has never had one in it.

---

**THE LINE OF NINE SOUNDINGS WENT OUT OF THAT HARBOR ON PAPER AND THE LEAD DID NOT, AND THAT IS WHAT A BOUNDARY IS FOR AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME A THING HAS GONE OUT OF A DRAWER IN A BUILDING ON THAT HILL AS WRITTEN WORDS RATHER THAN AS A THING, AND NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR HAS THE LEAD AND NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR IS GOING TO HAVE IT. THREE ACCOUNTS OF ONE STONE IN ABOUT FOUR AND A HALF FATHOMS DO NOT AGREE AND ALL THREE ARE IN THAT LEAF IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SAID, AND A FOURTH ACCOUNT THAT AGREED WITH ONE OF THEM WOULD NOT HAVE BEEN ENTERED AND WAS NOT ENTERED, AND THERE ARE NOW SIX ACCOUNTS IN THAT LEAF THAT DO NOT AGREE WITH EACH OTHER AND NOT ONE OF THE SIX HAS BEEN SETTLED. A CLAIM ENTERED AGAINST THAT COPY ON THE SEVENTH IS STILL IN A BOOK AT A COUNTER OFF THE WEST BASIN AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN AND NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR HAS ANSWERED IT. A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND THE FIGURE ON THAT BOARD IS NOT THE PRICE OF ANYTHING THAT HAPPENED IN THE LAST TWO DAYS.**

*First column, entered on Friday the eighth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a line of nine soundings with the second, the fifth and the eighth of them shallow went out of that harbor on the said day in three pockets on paper and not in the hand of anybody who was given the lead, and that the lead is in a drawer in a building on that hill and did not come down that hill and has not come down it since; and that three accounts of a stone in about four and a half fathoms at the fifth of those nine were given on the said day, the first at about half past ten in the morning from a boat of about eleven tons, the second at about one o'clock in the afternoon from a boat of about nine tons, and the third at about a quarter past two in the afternoon from a lighter of about eleven tons, and that the three do not agree and all three are in that leaf in the order they were said and none has been taken back and none has been settled; and that a man of about forty-four put his own words against the fifth line of that account at about six o'clock in the evening on the said day with his own name and the hour under it, and that both are printed and that account was not changed and says at the head of it that the person who entered it does not get to strike a line out of it; and that a chain weight came over a rail at about ten past three in the afternoon with a reason written against it and the hour against the reason; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall for one hundred and forty-four days, the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-six days, the sheet of six lines is on that wall for the twenty-third day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-eighth day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-fifth day; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-four days old, the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fiftieth night, and two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-eight days.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason three accounts of one stone are all kept and none is settled is in that yard's own mouth and is that a man on a mole cannot check the bottom of four and a half fathoms and that a figure against one of three accounts would be a price on one man's eyes, and that this yard has six accounts in that leaf now that do not agree with each other and has settled none of them; and that the reason a man of about forty-four's words are printed under that account and that account was not changed is in the head of that account and is that the person who entered it does not get to strike a line out of it, and that is the same nine words that refused four requests in that harbor in September and they have not been softened and have not been lengthened; and that the reason a diver was put in the water once on that line and once only, with a reason written down before he went down, is in the reason itself and is that the bottom of a stone is not visible in four and a half fathoms on a west wind and that no man was going to hold his breath for a third look at a piece of iron; and that the reason a man can put a name to one afternoon in June and cannot put a name to what was in the boat with him is in his own mouth on the after deck of a steam tender at about half past four in the afternoon, and that he said he was not going to pick one of the four men it could be and was not going to have anybody in that harbor pick one for him, and that the reason that is entered here and not in the first column is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that a man was in the water on the eighth of October and cannot count what a man can put a name to about the twenty-ninth of June; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Friday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
