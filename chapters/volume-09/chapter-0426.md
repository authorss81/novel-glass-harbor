# Chapter 426

*Saturday the Eighteenth Day of September, a Berth Held on a Man of About Thirty-Eight With a Boat of About Thirteen Tons and No Papers at the Head of the Third Lay at About Six in the Morning by a Sheet of Paper Tied to the Peg, a Man of About Thirty of the Third Lay Called Emmet Slade Standing at a Counter in an Office off the West Basin at About Eleven o'Clock and Being Told Something by a Man Behind That Counter That He Cannot Read and Cannot Check, and a Man of About Thirty-One With a Chart in a Bag Who Says One Sentence at the Foot of a Slip and Is Not Asked About the Chart*

Saturday the eighteenth day of September, in that office off the west basin, at the head of that slip, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Saturday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are five hundred and seventy-six pence in the bag, being three marks eight shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Saturday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on the second lay at about noon and did not go into that office. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he took the third piece and the eighth piece and set them side by side on the stones and stood looking at the gap between them for most of the afternoon and did not close it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-four days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and twenty-six days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eleventh week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-four days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirtieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in forty-eight days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for sixty-six days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-five days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is eighteen days old and has been answered by nobody, and the fourth line of it ends on the twentieth day of September next, which is two days off.**

---

The man of about thirty-eight came down the third lay at about six in the morning with his boat of about thirteen tons behind him and no papers on her, because he has never had any and because the sixth clause of the notice on that wall in a different street lets anybody in free and has never once asked anybody anything.

There was a sheet of paper tied to the peg of his berth with a piece of line, and it was not his, and it said that the berth was held until further notice, and it had a date on it, and the date was the seventeenth of September past.

He stood there until about half past six and then he took his boat round to the hard at the back of that lay, which is a hard for boats nobody wants out of the way, and he lay there from about seven until about ten with about four other boats, and the tide came and went twice while he was on it.

"You have got the sixth clause," said a man of about twenty-nine who was standing at the head of that slip with a hod of lime.

"I have got the sixth clause," said the man of about thirty-eight. "The sixth clause lets me in. It does not give me a berth and it does not say anything about a sheet of paper on a peg."

The two other boats on that hard belonged to a man of about fifty-two whose engine had not turned over since the spring and to a boat of about six tons with nobody on her and nobody claiming her, and the two men on that hard did not ask him anything and he did not ask them anything, and about eight o'clock the man of about fifty-two put a kettle on his own stove and gave him a cup of it without saying why.

He went out on the sixth clause at about half past six that evening with the tide and he came in past the outer marks at about five the following morning, which was the Sunday, and he has a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers and he went out on it again on the Wednesday if the weather held.

Nobody charged him with anything and nothing of that is against him in any column of this yard's leaf, and a berth is not a price and there is no figure for a day on a hard at the back of a slip.

---

Emmet Slade was at that counter in the office off the west basin at about eleven in the morning on the Saturday.

He had been told on the Monday in that shed to go and ask for the paper and to take somebody with him who could read, and he had brought nobody, and he said afterwards that he had brought nobody because there is nobody in that harbor he would ask twice.

The sheet of the seventeenth was still on the counter, squared up to the edge of it, with the four lines of it face up, and there was a second sheet under it with his name written at the top of it in a hand that is not his.

He put his thumb on it.

"That is me," he said.

"That is you."

"What does it say."

The man behind the counter is not a name on anything and did not give one and has not been asked for one by anybody in this harbor.

"It says your window is off," he said. "It says you are not to be at work on glass in this harbor until you have been entered in the claim book, and that the entry is to be read out to you where you can hear it, and that when it is read out to you the window stands again. I have read it to you. You cannot read it. That is the whole of it."

Emmet Slade stood at that counter.

"It says off."

"It says off."

"That is a different word to held."

"It is the word on the paper and I am not going to give you another one," said the man behind the counter. "I have read you what it says and you can go and get somebody to read it to you and he will read you the same word I have read you, because it is the word that is on it."

"Is it against my boat."

The man behind the counter put his hand flat on the sheet.

"Your window is a paper that lets you work one wreck off the point and claim what comes up off it," he said. "Your boat is nine tons and it lies at the head of the third lay and it is not on this counter and it is not in this room. If what you are asking me is whether somebody is going to take your boat, then nobody has written that word down in front of me and I am not going to say a word that is not on a paper in front of me. That is the end of what I have got for you."

Emmet Slade went out of that office at about ten past eleven and walked down to the west basin and stood there for a while and then went home, and he did not take his boat out on the Saturday and he did not take it out on the Monday either.

At the head of the third lay he stopped for a moment with his hand on his own boat's stem and then let go of it.

"Nine tons," he said, to nobody. "And I have not lost the wreck yet and I have not lost her rails and I have not lost the window at the office. I have lost a word."

He was in his own house by about noon and he did not tell the woman he lived with where he had been or what had been said to him, and she did not ask him, and on the Tuesday he told her that the next market was the following morning and that he was not going to be out for it, and by that Wednesday she had heard about it from somebody else before she heard anything from him.

---

At about four in the afternoon a man of about thirty-one of that lay with a chart in a bag came down that slip from the second lay and stopped at the foot of it.

He had been in the room over the joiner's shop on the Sunday the twelfth, and on the Thursday he had heard six lines read out in a market and had not said a word in that market, and on the Friday he had not been in the market at all.

"I was in that room," he said. "I have had a cold in my teeth since about three o'clock on the Sunday and my hands have not been right since about three o'clock on the Sunday. I have not been near a piece of glass since the fourth of June and I have not carried one and I have not bought one and I have not been in a room with one since that Sunday, and I am not going to go into that market and say that a glass did it or did not do it, because there is a piece of glass in about nine boats in this harbor and eight of those boats went out this week with their owners in them."

He put his hand on the rail of that slip.

"You can write that down or you can leave it off," he said. "I am not on a sheet and I am not going to be."

A man of about fifty-eight who was carrying a basket of coal down that slip stopped and looked at him for a moment and then went on, and the man of about thirty-one watched him go and then said:

"That is the fifth one this week who has walked down past me and looked at me like that, and the fifth one I have not got the name of, and I am telling you that so that you know I have noticed it and not so that you do anything about it."

"Are you telling me so that somebody asks you about the chart."

"No," he said. "I have had a chart in that bag for six years and I have not opened it in that room and I am not going to be the thing that happens to that room on a Sunday. I am telling you so that if it comes to something then there is one man in this harbor who knows what it looked like on the four days it was there, and there is not going to be a sheet with it on."

Nobody in that yard asked him one word about that chart and nobody in that yard is going to.

**Nothing came into that yard on the said Saturday and nothing went out of it, and the bag is five hundred and seventy-six pence, being three marks eight shillings, and is short of the wage of four marks by sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence. A berth at the head of the third lay was held on a man of about thirty-eight with a boat of about thirteen tons and no papers by a sheet of paper tied to the peg with a piece of line and dated the seventeenth of September past, and he stood at it until about half past six in the morning and then took his boat to the hard at the back of that lay and lay there from about seven until about ten with about four other boats, and he went out on the sixth clause of the notice on that wall in a different street at about half past six that evening and came in past the outer marks at about five the following morning, and a berth is not a price and there is no figure for a day on a hard at the back of a slip. Emmet Slade, of about thirty, of that lay, who cannot read, was at a counter in the office off the west basin at about eleven in the morning on the said Saturday with his name written at the top of a sheet under the sheet of the seventeenth, and a man behind that counter read him the sheet out and told him in his own mouth that the word on it is off, and Emmet Slade said that is a different word to held, and was told that it is the word that is on the paper and that he was not going to be given another one; and that he asked whether it was against his boat and was told that his boat is not on that counter and is not in that room and that nobody has written a word about taking it down in front of that man and he is not going to say a word that is not on a paper in front of him; and that he went out of that office at about ten past eleven and did not take his boat out on the said Saturday and did not take it out on the Monday either. A man of about thirty-one of that lay with a chart in a bag said one sentence at the foot of that slip and said out loud that he was in that room on the Sunday, that he has had a cold in his teeth since about three o'clock and has not been near a piece of glass since the fourth of June, and that he is not going into that market to say that a glass did it or did not do it, and that he is not on a sheet and is not going to be; and nobody asked him one word about that chart. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is eighteen days old and its fourth line ends on the twentieth of September next and has been answered by nobody. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

and that he came down that lay at about six in the morning with his boat behind him and stood at the berth until about half past six and then took his boat to the hard at the back of that lay and lay there from about seven until about ten with about four other boats; and that he said out loud to a man of about twenty-nine standing at the head of that slip that he has got the sixth clause and that the sixth clause lets him in and does not give him a berth and does not say anything about a sheet of paper tied to a peg; and that he went out on the sixth clause at about half past six that evening and came in past the outer marks at about five the following morning; and that nobody charged him with anything; and that a man of about thirty of the third lay called Emmet Slade was at a counter in the office off the west basin at about eleven in the morning on the said day with his name written at the top of a sheet lying under the sheet of the seventeenth; and that he cannot read; and that a man behind that counter read the sheet out to him and told him in his own mouth that the word at the head of it is off and that he is not to be at work on glass in that harbor until he has been entered in the claim book and that the entry is to be read out to him where he can hear it and that the window stands again when it has been; and that Emmet Slade said out loud that off is a different word to held and was told that it is the word that is on the paper and that he was not going to be given another one, and that he could go and get somebody to read it and that the somebody would read him the same word because it is the word that is on it; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man of about thirty of the third lay who cannot read was told the word off on the said day and not held is not anybody's, and it is a word on a sheet and this yard has not read the sheet and is not going to guess at it; and that the reason no figure in this yard's leaf stands against a berth held at the head of a slip is that a berth is not a price and a day on a hard at the back of a slip is not a figure anybody in this harbor keeps, and that the man holding it went to sea on the same evening on the sixth clause of a notice in a different street, which is free and has not asked anybody anything; and that the reason a man of about thirty-one with a chart in a bag was not asked one question after what he said at the foot of that slip is that he is a person of no office with a chart in a bag and what he said there is his own, and that this yard has asked him nothing about that chart in nine months and is not going to start on the day a sheet went up on a wall; and that the reason there is no figure anywhere for what eight fathoms of tarred rope and a morning cost a man of about forty-four on the seventeenth, or what a week of a claim window costs a man of about thirty who cannot read on the eighteenth, is that both of those are costs and costs are not prices, and that putting a figure on either of them would be this yard pricing a man's morning; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
