# Chapter 448

*Sunday the Tenth Day of October, a Room over a Fish Market in a Different Building from the Room over a Joiner's Shop at the Top of That Street, About Twenty-Two People in It at About Ten in the Morning, a Long Sheet With No Name at the Head of It and Nobody Having Asked for One, Four Rule-Heads Written on It and Read Out From Eleven o'Clock Until About Ten Past Twelve, a Master of About Fifty Saying the Name of That Thing Once at About Ten Past Eleven in the Morning on the Day It Was Written and Writing It at the Head of That Sheet Himself, a Rotating Council, a Right for a Community to Withdraw From a Route, Two Standing Rules, a Claim Against a Copy Carried on It and Not Discharged, a Question Asked Once After the Four Heads Had Been Read and Not Answered by Anybody and Recorded in a Minute as Asked, a Remaining Question Asked Once and Carried by No Rule, and a Room over a Joiner's Shop Let Out for the Seventh Time on the Same Sunday*

Sunday the tenth day of October, in a room over a fish market, in that shed, and in a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street.

**The said Sunday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about half past eleven in the morning for canvas and nails, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the eighth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and a shoe that is not his was on the floor beside it. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name stood on a public sheet for the seventh day and nobody took it off it and nobody thanked her for it and nobody offered to. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he put the eleventh piece of a boat down on its own edge and left it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-six days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-eight days and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a thirteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-six days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-second night. Two lights off that point have not moved in seventy days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-eight days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-fifth day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the fortieth day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-seventh day.**

---

The room was over a fish market and it was not the room over a joiner's shop and it was not four hundred yards from it, and anybody in that harbor who has heard the two of them spoken about in the same week has heard the wrong one about the other.

There is a difference that matters and it is this: the room over a joiner's shop keeps a public volume of thirty-one heads with four lines under every head, and a claim is not a head and is not read at that table, and a man of about forty-six reads what is on a page in that room and will not read anything that is not a head in that volume. The room over a fish market has a long table in it, a stove that does not draw, and about twenty-two people in it at about ten in the morning on the tenth of October, and nothing in that room on that day was a claim.

The sheet was bought at a counter in a room off that market for a penny out of the holder of that writ's own coat, and it is about three feet long, and it was blank at the top.

That was the first thing anybody said about it and it was said at about half past nine in the morning by Ollum Trevask, of about thirty-four, of the second lay, who keeps the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book and has never shown it to anybody.

"There is a head of paper at the top of that thing and it is empty, and I am not going to be the one who writes on it, and anybody who wants to write on it can write on it and I will not help and I will not stop them."

Nobody wrote on it for about forty minutes.

---

At about ten past eleven in the morning a man of about fifty, who was master of a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay and whose boat opened about nine foot of garboard and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands on the twenty-eighth day of September by striking a baulk of oak that this yard's own hands had laid down with nothing on it, and who was not charged a penny for that bill and did not offer and was not thanked for saying he would hold that baulk next time and held it on the fourth day of October for about an hour and ten minutes and was not thanked for that either, picked up the pencil and said the name of the thing once.

"It is an open keel assembly," he said, and he wrote it at the head of that sheet himself, in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and put the pencil down.

He said it once and did not say it again that day, and the two people nearest him did not take it up, and the twenty-two people in that room were about eleven working people and their families and one bookbinder's apprentice of about seventeen and the holder of that writ and four people who have asked to be on the rotation, and that sheet went on the wall of that room over a fish market at about one o'clock in the afternoon and has hung there since with nothing written on it above those four heads, and eleven names are at the foot of it and there is no seat and there is no roll and there is no seal, and a person in that harbor who wants to know who belongs to that sheet can go up that stair and read the eleven names on it and count them.

About twenty-two people went up into that room over a fish market on the morning of the tenth of October and eleven of them put their names at the foot of that sheet and eleven of them did not, and the eleven who did not lost nothing and gained nothing by that and are not on it and are not spoken for on it, and about eleven of the twenty-two had been in that room on the hill on the Thursday and had heard the whole of that account, and there was no way for anybody to be in that room without having asked to be in it and there is no way for anybody's name to be on any part of that sheet or of any copy of it without their having asked for it in front of somebody else and in their own hand.

---

Four heads were written on that sheet under the name, and they were read out loud from about eleven o'clock until about ten past twelve, and the reader was Hobb Lee again, and he wrote his own name and the hour against each head as he read it, and about four people in that room could not read and heard all four.

**One. Claims.** A claim is a thing somebody wants and has not got, put in their own words with their own name and the hour on it, and it is not decided by anybody on that sheet and it is not decided by anybody off it either, and the person who wants it keeps it, and the person who is named in it is told what is said about them before anybody else is, and a person may be in a claim without being named in it and a name on a claim is not a finding.

**Two. Refusals.** A refusal is a thing this harbor has been given and is on this sheet at the same size as the rest of it and is not a footnote and is not a lesser item, and the same words are used for a refusal about a claim and a refusal about a panel and a refusal about a person, and a refusal given by anybody on that sheet may be put on it and cannot be argued down by anybody who was not there.

**Three. Restricted records.** Some things are closed and stay closed, and a thing that is closed is not a thing anybody has to explain, and the closing of a thing is written down in the same hand as the opening of it and both go on that sheet, and a person who puts something on that sheet as closed and then uncloses it in a room without a line about it has broken one of these four heads and everybody in that room will know which one.

**Four. Public correction.** When a thing on that sheet or on any sheet that is read out in that harbor turns out to be wrong, it is corrected in the same place it was printed, with the wrong thing left where it is and readable, with the hour and the name of the person who found it, and a correction is not a removal and a removal is not a correction, and anybody in that harbor may make one and nobody in that harbor may take one away.

Then four standing lines under the four heads, and they are the whole of what was added to that sheet on the tenth day of October and nothing has been added to it since.

**No route, and no panel, and no account, is represented by anybody who has not asked to be represented.** That is the first one and it is the reason a man of about thirty-four of the second lay could sit in that room with a book he has never shown anybody and not be asked to open it, and it is the reason a man of about fifty-one on the crown of that mole, who has a page of six names and a boat as a price against it and a stone in his own pocket that he put there himself, is not on that sheet and is not spoken for on it and has not been asked.

**The refusals are on it too and are not smaller than the rest.**

**A community may withdraw from a route.** Not a person. A community. And it takes one person of that community to say it and it does not take a vote and it does not take anybody to agree with him, and nobody on that sheet can ask a community why it wants out, and a route a community has withdrawn from is a route nobody on that sheet may put a boat on.

**The council turns.** Four names, asked for, for four weeks from the tenth day of October, and the turn goes round and no turn may be given to anybody who has not asked for it, and a person whose turn it is may be asked once and not twice.

---

And then there was a fifth thing on that sheet, and it is not a rule and it is not a head, and it is the only thing on that sheet that was not written on the tenth day of October.

A claim against the copy was entered in a book at a counter off the west basin on the seventh day of October by a man in a coat of an office in a city and it was not granted and it was not refused, and it is not withdrawn, and it is written on that sheet at the same size as the four heads and in the same hand, and that sheet does not discharge it, does not refuse it, does not answer it, and does not pretend to be able to.

And under that, four lines that say what that thing is not, which a stranger with a pencil can take and hold against the rest of the sheet and find that the sheet bears out.

*This is not a court. It decides no contested claim. It settles nothing. It has no head and no seat and no roll and nobody in it can be appealed to. It binds nobody who has not signed it, and about eleven people in that harbor did not sign it, and there is no rule on this sheet that can put a name on it that a person has not asked to have there.*

A stranger with a pencil can take that sheet and count the eleven and find that there is no mechanism on the whole of it for adding a twelfth.

---

The question was asked at about twenty past twelve in the afternoon and it was asked by a woman of thirty of no office, and she is Mara Venn, and she has forty marks first after food standing against her and has had it neither reduced nor paid and is not restored, and she asked it after the four heads had been written and read out, and she named no person, and nobody answered it, and three people began to and stopped.

"Can a thing like this speak for people who cannot speak safely, and for people whose own account of what happened to them is being held under a lie?"

A man of about thirty-three of that harbor said that it could not, because of the first of the four standing lines, and then he stopped. A woman of about forty-three who keeps a store on the second lay said that it could, because of the second, and then she stopped. Ollum Trevask said that he did not know, and that not knowing is a thing a man can put in a minute.

Nessa Pike wrote it into a minute on a sheet of good paper that cost a penny and that came out of the holder of that writ's own coat, and the minute says the question was asked at about twenty past twelve in the afternoon of the tenth of October in a room over a fish market, that nobody answered it, that three people began to and stopped, and that a man of about thirty-four said he did not know and that a minute is a document and a document says what happened. It does not say what the answer is, because there was not one, and nobody at that table wrote one, and the four heads on that sheet were not changed by the asking, and they would not have been changed by any answer.

The other question was asked at about half past twelve in the afternoon by a man of about forty-four who has a boat of about nine tons of his own and crews in nobody else's and holds no office, and it is a question and it is the whole of what he said.

"Who is it that gets into the big ones? The big wrecks that are the Crown's own. Is it the same for everybody, or is there a list?"

Nobody answered that either, and it was written into the same minute in the same hand at the same table, and no rule was written on that sheet about it, and no office was made for it, and no seal line was cut for it, and there is nothing in that room over a fish market or in that harbor or on that hill about it, and it is the whole of what that question got.

---

At about two o'clock in the afternoon the room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street was let out for the seventh time since the first of September, on the same Sunday as the room over the fish market and four hundred yards from it, and a bowl was put on the top stone of the step, and about twenty people went up those stairs over the next half hour and three came down, and eleven of them could not read, and Hobb Lee read the head-notes off the long table out loud until about half past three and wrote his own name and the hour under each in his own hand, and there was no claim in any of them and no claim on that table, and nothing that was written in a room over a fish market that morning was read out in that room that afternoon, and the eleven people on a paper of four lines at the top left of that door have been told nothing further by anybody in that harbor.

---

**THAT SHEET HAS FOUR HEADS ON IT AND NO JURISDICTION WHATEVER, IT BINDS NOBODY WHO HAS NOT SIGNED IT, IT DECIDES NO CONTESTED CLAIM, IT SETTLES NOTHING, IT IS NOT A BODY WITH A HEAD AND IT IS NOT A COURT AND IT DECIDES NOTHING AND IT IS THE FIRST DAY OF NOTHING, AND IT CARRIES A CLAIM AGAINST A COPY WHICH IT DOES NOT DISCHARGE, AND A STRANGER WITH A PENCIL CAN HOLD IT UP AND FIND THAT EVERY ONE OF THOSE SIX SENTENCES IS TRUE OF THE SHEET IN HIS OWN HAND. THE TITLE ON IT WAS SAID ONCE BY A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY AT ABOUT TEN PAST ELEVEN IN THE MORNING ON THE DAY IT WAS WRITTEN AND HE WROTE IT HIMSELF, AND IT IS ON NO OTHER SHEET IN THAT HARBOR AND NOBODY SAID IT AFTER HIM. THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND A BELT OF A COAST ROAD IS STILL UNLICENSED AND THE THIRTY MARKS ON IT WERE NOT PAID, AND THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET AND NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR.**
