# Chapter 445

*Thursday the Seventh Day of October, the Eighty-Eighth Time That Cistern Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It at About Half Past Nine, a Room on the Premises of That Office on the Hill With a Chair and a List at the Door and About Thirty People in It of Whom About Eleven Could Not Read, a Net-Mender of About Forty-Six Reading an Account of About Four Feet Aloud From Two o'Clock Until About Half Past Three With His Own Name and the Hour Under It in His Own Hand, the Foot of That Sheet Read Aloud Once in That Harbor and the Office Named Once in a Working Man's Mouth, a Man in a Coat of an Office in a City Entering a Claim Against the Copy in a Book at a Counter off the West Basin at About Four in the Afternoon and Neither Granting It Nor Refusing It, and the Original and the Copy Both Staying in the Building They Were In*

Thursday the seventh day of October, at the cistern at the back of that market, in a room on that hill, and at a counter off the west basin.

**The said Thursday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning for the eighty-eighth time and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon for canvas, tar and copper, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the fifth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and on the said day her name stood on a public sheet for the fourth day and nobody took it off it and nobody thanked her for it. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he worked at a seam in the ninth piece with the hook and said nothing. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-three days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-five days and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a thirteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-three days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-ninth night. Two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-seven days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-five days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-second day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-seventh day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-fourth day.**

---

The room was on the first floor, up a stair that turned, and it had been a counting house or a store or something else before and it had four tables in it and about thirty chairs and a window that looks at the back of a building.

There was a chair at the door and a woman of about forty-four sitting in it who keeps the key to that corridor and who had a list on a board and who wrote every name that went past her and the hour against it, and she did not read the list to anybody and she did not ask anybody what they had come for, and when the holder of that writ came up that stair at about ten past two he showed her the sheet and she read the head of it and then wrote his name and the hour and then wrote the name of every other person who came through that door until about twenty-eight of them were in the room.

Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, who mends nets on the third lay and can read, stood at the end of the middle table and read the sheet out loud from about a quarter past two until about half past three, and he wrote his own name and the hour under every page of it in his own hand as he came to it, and at the top of that sheet it says in four lines that any person in that room may stop him at any word and put his own words against it and have both printed, and nobody in that room stopped him for about an hour and a quarter, and then a man of about thirty-eight stood up at about half past three and said one word, and it was not a stoppage, and the word was *which*, and the reader said it was the seventh of the boundaries, and the man said that was the one he was asking about and sat down again.

About eleven of the people in that room could not read. There is a count of about eleven people in that harbor who cannot read a notice and it is a count of people and not a constituency and they are not named and they have not been sent for, and eleven of them were in that room on the seventh of October and every one of them heard the whole of that sheet from the middle of that table, and Hobb Lee did not turn a page round to face himself while he was reading it, and one of the eleven kept his own thumb on the line he was on until the reader had finished that line, and that is what that room did for the people in it that afternoon.

---

The foot of that sheet is the foot of a paper of that office, and a paper of that office has a seal line at the foot of it, and the first paper on the door of the custody room at the back of that corridor has carried the same seal line at its foot since the twenty-second day of August, and that seal line had been printed twice in a yard's own hand in the whole of that harbor and had gone into one working man's eye twice in a corridor, and nobody in that harbor had read it out loud until the seventh day of October, and the man whose hand is at the foot of the first paper had not read it out loud either.

Hobb Lee came to the foot of the sheet at about a quarter past three and read it out loud, and it is eleven words long, and the office at the end of it went into that room in a working man's mouth for the first time since the twenty-second day of August, and no minister was in that room and no minister appeared in it and nobody in that room was asked anything by anybody about it.

*Recovered accounts, circuit of this town. Magister Ilya Serr, Registrar of Recovered Accounts.*

There was a silence of about the length of two breaths, and then a woman of about forty who sells milk in that harbor and who has a boy of eleven said, in her own voice and to nobody, that she had been told since the summer that that name could take a harbor's records whole, and then she said that it is a name at the foot of a page and that she had been told a thing about a name and not about an office, and then she asked the man beside her whether the milk was still outside.

The reader said that the sheet went on the desk at the door on its way out and that anybody in that harbor could take it up and read the whole of it, and he said that out loud, and then he said that he was going to put his own name under the foot of it, which he did, with the hour, and that was the last thing he wrote on that day.

Nobody thanked him. He is a working man reading a paper out loud in a room and that is the whole of what he did and he is not a member of anything and is not an officer and is not on any list and is not going to be sent for.

---

The original did not move that afternoon and did not move on any of the ten days after it, and it is on a shelf in a building on that hill under a number that is on no page of this harbor, and the piece of worked lead cut on the fifth of October is in a drawer in the same building under a drawer of its own, and neither of them came to that harbor and neither of them went anywhere.

At about four in the afternoon a man in a coat of an office in a city came into that harbor at a counter off the west basin, which is not that building and is four hundred yards from it, and he was not named to anybody and was not asked anything and did not stop for more than a quarter of an hour.

He had a sheet with a mark on it that was not a seal and he put it into a book at that counter and the book keeps a register copy of what is given to it, and what he put in was a claim against the copy, being that a copy of a record of recovered accounts, made in that building, cut on the fifth of October, is a record of recovered accounts, and that a claim so made is to be held against it until somebody in authority has looked at it.

The man of about fifty who works at a table with two papers on it read it, and did not grant it and did not refuse it, and wrote in the register copy that it was entered at four o'clock and that it was not granted and not refused, and told the man in the coat that a claim entered is a claim entered and that a thing entered in a book in that office is a thing that can be pointed at by anybody who comes in with a pencil, and that is the whole of what this harbor has got to do about it and the whole of what it is going to do about it.

The man in the coat went out with the tide and is not in that harbor again.

That claim is not withdrawn. It stands in a book at a counter off the west basin and the fourth box on that table is not empty either, and the paper of four lines with no name on it and no seal at the foot of it is still on that counter where it was put on the seventeenth of September, and a man of about thirty who cannot read it came and stood at that counter on the eighteenth of September and was told that the word on it is off, and nothing in anything that happened on that hill on the seventh of October gives him his window back and nothing is going to.

---

**THE ACCOUNT WAS READ ALOUD IN A ROOM ON THAT HILL AT A STATED HOUR WITH A CUSTODIAN OF THAT OFFICE AT THE DOOR, AND THE COPY AND THE ORIGINAL ARE BOTH IN THAT BUILDING AND NEITHER OF THEM CAME TO THIS HARBOR, AND A CLAIM ENTERED AGAINST THE COPY AT A COUNTER FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY IS NOT GRANTED AND NOT REFUSED AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN. ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM COULD NOT READ AND EVERY ONE OF THEM HEARD THE WHOLE OF THAT SHEET, AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR HAS TOLD ANY OF THE ELEVEN ANYTHING ELSE AND NOBODY IS GOING TO. THE OFFICE AT THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET WENT INTO A WORKING MAN'S MOUTH AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON AND WENT IN ONCE, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY WITH A BOY OF ELEVEN SAID THAT SHE HAD BEEN TOLD SINCE THE SUMMER THAT THAT NAME COULD TAKE A HARBOR'S RECORDS WHOLE AND THAT SHE HAD BEEN TOLD A THING ABOUT A NAME AND NOT ABOUT AN OFFICE, AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM WAS AFRAID OF ANY PERSON, AND THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND THE CHARGE OF ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE AT THE FOOT OF THE SIX-LINE SHEET ON THE OUTSIDE OF THAT BOARD HAS BEEN ON THAT WALL FOR THE TWENTY-SECOND DAY AND HAS BEEN PAID BY NOBODY.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the seventh day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning for the eighty-eighth time and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it, and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a room on the first floor of that office on that hill was opened at about two o'clock in the afternoon and about thirty people went into it and about eleven of them could not read, and that a woman of about forty-four who keeps the key to that corridor sat at the door with a list and wrote a name and an hour against every person who went past her, and that a net-mender of about forty-six of the third lay read that account aloud from about a quarter past two until about half past three and wrote his own name and the hour under every page of it in his own hand, and that one man of about thirty-eight stood up at about half past three and said one word and it was not a stoppage; and that the seal line at the foot of that sheet, being the same seal line as the one at the foot of the first paper on that door, was read out loud at about a quarter past three in that room for the first time in that harbor since the twenty-second day of August, and that the office at the end of it went into a working man's mouth on that reading and is on no other page of these ten days; and that a claim against the copy was entered in a book at a counter off the west basin at about four in the afternoon on the said day by a man in a coat of an office in a city who was not named to anybody and was not asked anything, and that it was not granted and not refused and is not withdrawn; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall for one hundred and forty-three days, the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-five days, the sheet of six lines is on that wall for the twenty-second day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-seventh day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-fourth day; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-three days old, the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-ninth night, and two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-seven days.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a claim is entered against a copy in a book at a counter four hundred yards from the building the copy is in is in the man's own mouth at that counter and is that a claim entered is a claim entered and that a thing in a book in that office is a thing that can be pointed at by anybody who comes in with a pencil, and that the harbor has no answer to it and is not going to manufacture one; and that the reason a room was let out to anybody at a stated hour with a chair and a list at the door is in the woman in that chair's own mouth and is that a room where anybody can come in without asking a question is a room where a man who cannot read is not the only one who is out of place, and that she does not read the list to anybody and does not ask what anybody came for; and that the reason a net-mender of the third lay was the one who read it is that he reads out loud in a room over a joiner's shop in that harbor on a Sunday with his own name and the hour under it and that a person who can do that in one room can do it in another, and that he is not an officer and is not a member of anything and is not going to be sent for; and that the reason a woman of about forty with a boy of eleven said what she said out loud is that she had been told since the summer that that name could take a harbor's records whole, and that what is at the foot of a page is an office and that she was told a thing about a name; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Thursday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
