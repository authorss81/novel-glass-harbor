# Chapter 424

*Thursday the Sixteenth Day of September, the Eighty-Second Time the Cistern Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It at About Half Past Nine on a Rota of a Monday and a Thursday, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Out of That Yard in Coin on the Flat of the Bench Stone at About Four in the Afternoon to a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Asked for That Day by Name at About Five o'Clock on the Friday Before and Told on the Wednesday That He Was Wanted, a Man of About Forty-Six Reading Six Lines Out Loud in the Middle of That Market at About Eleven in the Morning With His Own Name and the Hour Against It in Front of About Thirty People, and a Man of About Thirty-Six Who Says Out Loud That He Has Stood in a Room With Glass off a Wreck and Come Out of It All Right and Is Not Answered*

Thursday the sixteenth day of September, in that market, in that shed, on that mole head, and at the head of that slip.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe and was a rota day, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the eighty-second time, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard on the said day, being a caulker's day in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon, being one mark and eightpence, and nothing came into it, and there are four hundred and eight pence in the bag, being two marks seven shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and thirty-two pence, being one mark six shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about one in the afternoon and did not go into the market. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he got the tenth piece out from under the eighth and stood it up on the stones and left it standing. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-two days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and twenty-four days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a tenth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-two days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and twenty-eighth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in forty-six days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for sixty-four days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-three days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is sixteen days old and has been answered by nobody, and the fourth line of it ends on the twentieth day of September next, which is four days off.**

---

At about eleven in the morning, in the middle of that market, on a crate that has been turned over for the purpose, Hobb Lee read the six lines out loud.

He did it because two men had asked him to do it separately on the morning before, in a chandler's and on a quay, and he said no to the first of them and no to the second of them and then he said that if a thing is going to be said in a market it is going to be said once and properly and not three times by three men who each of them have it slightly wrong.

He wrote his own name and the hour under it when he had finished, in his own hand, on the back of the crate, with a stub of pencil.

*Sixteenth day of September, eleven o'clock in the morning. Six lines off the sheet at the back of the end stall, read out whole in the middle aisle, about thirty people standing. Read by me and nobody helped me with it. — H. Lee, forty-six.*

There were about thirty people there and they stood in a half circle and about nine of them could not read, and every one of the nine heard the same six lines from the same mouth at the same hour, and not one of the nine was asked afterward what he thought of it and not one of the nine was asked to say whether he agreed.

---

Four men in that market said out loud on the said Thursday that they had been in the room over the joiner's shop on the Sunday the twelfth.

They said it in about the time it takes to say it, and then they went back to what they were doing, and one of them is a man who stacks empties at a quay and one of them is a man who sells fish off a barrow and one of them is a man who cuts weed off a mooring chain and the fourth is a man of about forty-one who was in that room from about half past two until about five and who has not been near a piece of glass since the fourth day of June.

There were about twenty people in that room on that Sunday. This yard is not going to count who was in it and is not going to write a list of them, and there is no sheet with their names on it.

---

A man of about thirty-six put his hand up at the back of that half circle and said the thing that about four other men had come to say before him and did not say.

"I have been in a room with glass," he said. "Off a wreck off the point in April, in a shed, with about six of us in it, and the man who carries it was in there and it was in a crate. I came out of that shed and I went home and I ate my supper. I have never had a cold in my teeth in my life and I am not going to have one over a crate of window glass."

The man of about forty-one said:

"I have not been near a piece since the fourth of June," he said. "I have had a cold in my teeth since about three o'clock on the Sunday and my hands have not been right since about three o'clock on the Sunday, and I was in that room and I was not looking at the thing on the table, I was looking at the door the whole time, and I have told a man with a book what it is and I have not told anybody else and I am not going to start."

The man of about thirty-six stood there for a while.

"You were in a room," he said.

"I was in a room," said the man of about forty-one. "You were in a shed with a crate in it. Those are two different things and neither of us is going to get the other one out of the other, and I am not going to stand in this market and say which of them is the one that does it, because I do not know, and a man that does not know ought to say so in a market and not after it."

---

At about half past one in the afternoon Hobb Lee was in that shed with the ship's hand and Calder Marr.

"That sheet wants a claim read out in that room on a Sunday," he said, "by the man at the end of the table, and I am the man at the end of the table."

"You are."

"And I am not going to do it," said Hobb Lee. "I will read a head off that table every Sunday until I am too old to climb the stair, and the head of that book says four lines under every head and says the refusals are on it and are not smaller than the rest, and a claim is not a head. It has no where it came from in it and it has no who stands where it is in it, and if I stand at that table in about a month's time with a claim in my hand I am a man reading something that is not in that book out in a room to about twenty people, and there is a line in the head of that volume that says a book is not a court, and the day I start doing that in there I have turned that table into a court with a net-mender at the head of it."

"Then who reads it."

"Nobody," said Hobb Lee. "If they want it read out in that room they can come and ask me in the street on a Monday like everybody else asks me for things, and I will tell them no there the same as I am telling you no here."

Nessa Pike had her hand on her slate and she did not write it down at once.

"Can I put it to you a different way," she said. "Suppose a man comes up that stair with a paper and a fee and asks you to read that paper out to the people in that room on a Sunday, and suppose there is no fee and it is a poor man with a sheet off the front of an office and no money at all. Is the answer different."

"Yes," said Hobb Lee. "It is different and it is the same, and there is no way to put that in a book that anybody in this harbor has got, and I have been thinking about it on and off for two days and I am not going to invent a rule about it on a Thursday afternoon in a shed."

"So what happens when it comes."

"It happens that a man in that harbor with a pencil in his hand stands at the top of that stair and says no to somebody with a piece of paper in his hand, one at a time, on a Monday, and that it is one man and not a court and not a rule and not a sheet," said Hobb Lee. "And it will be me or it will be Arthur Sennett or it will be somebody else who happens to be passing, and any one of us can be got at with a paper in a hand, and I know that is not a system and I am not going to stand in this shed and pretend to anybody that it is."

She wrote one line in the second column against a reason, and no name against it, and the reason is in Hobb Lee's own mouth and is that the head of that volume says four lines under every head and a claim is not a head.

---

Isaac Fell came up that slip at about four in the afternoon and was paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, out of that yard's own figures, and counted it with his own thumb, and the holder of that writ said nothing at all to him, which is the sixth time in a month, and that is the arrangement.

That was the seventh time he has been paid since the tenth of August past, and the seven are the seventeenth, twenty-first, twenty-seventh and thirty-first of August past, and the sixth, tenth and sixteenth of September, and the tenth of August past was the payment before those seven and is not one of them.

"You have not asked me for the Monday," he said.

"You are not on the Monday until I ask you for the Monday by name on the Friday before," said Calder Marr, "and there is not going to be a Monday asked for this week, and you will not be on the stone on the Monday and nobody in that yard is going to tell you that you will."

"That is all right," said Isaac Fell. "I have four days of my own account in the third week of September that nobody is going to ask me for and the arrangement has not changed since the tenth of August."

*First column, entered on Thursday the sixteenth day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine on the said Thursday, being the eighty-second time, and that the rota of that cistern is a Monday and a Thursday and that the top name on that board is still off it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon to a caulker of about twenty-three, being one mark and eightpence, and that he had been asked for that day by name at about five o'clock on the Friday before and told on the Wednesday that he was wanted; and that this is the seventh time he has been paid since the tenth of August past, the seven being the seventeenth, twenty-first, twenty-seventh and thirty-first of August past and the sixth, tenth and sixteenth of September; and that the holder of that writ said nothing to him, which is the sixth time in a month; and that the caulker said out loud that he has not been asked for the Monday and was told that he is not on the Monday until he is asked for it by name on the Friday before, and that the said caulker said that he has four days of his own account in the third week of September that nobody is going to ask him for; and that a man of about forty-six read the six lines off the sheet at the back of that end stall out loud in the middle of that market at about eleven in the morning on the said day in front of about thirty people of whom about nine could not read, and that he wrote his own name and the hour at which he finished on the back of a crate with a stub of pencil in his own hand and that no man helped him with it; and that four men in that market said out loud that they had been in the room over the joiner's shop on the twelfth day of September past, being a man who stacks empties at a quay, a woman who sells fish off a barrow, a man who cuts weed off a mooring chain, and a man of about forty-one who was in that room from about half past two until about five; and that there were about twenty people in that room on that Sunday and that this yard did not count who was in it afterwards and that no sheet carries the name of anybody who was in it; and that a man of about thirty-six said out loud that he had been in a shed with glass off a wreck off the point in April with about six men in it and had come out of it and eaten his supper; and that the paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is sixteen days old and its fourth line ends on the twentieth of September next, being four days off, and has been answered by nobody.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man of about forty-six read the six lines out loud in that market rather than let three men say them three times is in his own mouth and is that if a thing is going to be said in a market it is going to be said once and properly and not three times slightly wrong by three men; and that the reason his own name and the hour went under it on the back of a crate is that a person who cannot read cannot afterwards prove what was said to him, and that is the whole of why it is written there, and that nine people in that half circle could not read and every one of them heard the same six lines from the same mouth at the same hour; and that the reason no name is against a man of about thirty-four in this yard's leaf is that he did not put the sheet up out of anything he is owed to and no figure has been paid to anybody to put it up and he has said he was given it at the top of a gangway by a man behind a counter with a book open; and that the reason this yard is not going to count who was in that room on the Sunday is that a count of the people who went up that stair is a list, and that a yard that writes down the names of the people who went up a stair is a yard that has a list of everybody in that harbor who could not afford half a penny to walk in; and that the reason no figure stands against the four men who said out loud that they were in that room is that what they said is a fact about themselves and not a cost of this yard, and that the cost of that room is the four days that one man of about forty-one has had a cold in his teeth and has not got; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Thursday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
