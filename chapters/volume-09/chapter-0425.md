# Chapter 425

*Friday the Seventeenth Day of September, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence of a Day With a Boat and Two Men at a Berth Coming In at the Figure in Chalk on a Slate at an End Stall, a Chandler's Counter Shut to a Man Called Nate Pry of About Forty-Four Who Carried a Piece Out of a Boat's Hold in June and Was Not Asked Why He Was Carrying It, Four Shillings for Eight Fathoms of Tarred Rope That He Did Not Get, and a Paper Going Up on the Counter of the Office off the West Basin at About Four in the Afternoon With a Hand on It and No Name on the Paper*

Friday the seventeenth day of September, in that shed, in that market, in an office off the west basin, and on that mole head.

**The said Friday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, being one mark and eightpence, and nothing went out of it, and there are five hundred and seventy-six pence in the bag, being three marks eight shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Friday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about eleven in the morning and was not in that market at any hour of the said day. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he stood over the fifth piece with the hook in his hand and did not put it down on anything for about half an hour and then did not use it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-three days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and twenty-five days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a tenth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-three days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and twenty-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in forty-seven days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for sixty-five days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-four days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is seventeen days old and has been answered by nobody, and the fourth line of it ends on the twentieth day of September next, which is three days off.**

---

Nate Pry came to that chandler's counter at about eleven in the morning with eight fathoms of tarred rope on his arm and a week's account in his head.

He is of about forty-four and he is of that third lay and he took a piece of worked glass out of a boat's hold in the middle of June, a panel about the size of two hands with a border round it, and he carried it up a slip on his own back to a store on the second lay, and it went into a box, and the man that bought it gave him a shilling and a half and he has not been near a piece since and nobody has asked him to be.

"I want eight fathom of tarred rope," he said. "Four shillings, your chalk says."

The chandler is of about fifty and his wife keeps the count and had it done before he got to it. He looked at Nate Pry for about as long as it takes to look.

"No," he said.

"That is a new thing."

"It is a new thing since Wednesday," said the chandler. "There is a sheet on a wall at the back of that end stall and it says that every person who has been in a room with a piece of worked glass that has a scene in it is to be entered in a claim book at a counter and is not to be at work on glass until he has been entered. And I am not going to stand in this shop and ask you whether you have been in a room with a piece of glass, because you are not going to answer me and we both know it, and because I have got a woman and a girl of eleven in that house behind this counter and I am not going to have a thing in this shop that I cannot see in the person standing in front of me. I could be wrong about you. If I am wrong about you then I am wrong about me and about that girl, and it is my side of that that I have got."

"You could ask somebody else."

"I could ask somebody else," said the chandler, "and if I asked a man of about twenty-two standing in your place next week I would have to give him the same answer and I would have given you the same one, and you and he would work that out in about a minute and then nobody in this harbor could buy anything at this counter at all, and that is not a better shop."

He took the account he had already written and turned it round.

"Fourpence on the last one. Pay that and go."

Nate Pry put fourpence on the counter and took his rope back onto his arm and went out into the street with it, and he did not argue and he did not ask again, and nobody in that market said one word to him about it, and he was not charged with anything and there is no figure against him in any column of this yard's leaf.

At the end of that street a man he knows was mending a net on the stones and looked up at the coil on his arm and then at his face.

"Did he not have it?"

"He would not sell me it," said Nate Pry.

The man on the stones sat back on his heels.

"Nate," he said. "He would not sell you a candle. I have been buying candles off that counter since I was nineteen and I have never had him not sell me a candle."

"Then that is where we are," said Nate Pry, and went on down the slip with the rope on his arm.

There is no other rope seller in that harbor and no other counter that has ever sold him anything, and the eight fathoms went back onto that third lay on his own shoulder and lay in the bottom of his boat until the middle of October, and his seine was still open where it had been open since June.

---

At about four in the afternoon a man of about fifty, who works at a table with two papers on it in an office off the west basin, came out of that office with a sheet of paper in his hand and put it on the counter at the front of the room, and there was nobody else in the room.

There is a hand-written line at the top of that counter and it is in the hand of a man behind the counter and there is no name on the sheet and no seal at the foot of it and no office at the end of anything.

It is four lines and it says that a claim has been entered under a sheet that went up on a wall in the front street of this harbor on the fifteenth day of September past; that a person who has been in a room with a piece of worked glass that has a scene in it is to be entered in the claim book of this harbor under his own name; and that until he has been entered, a claim window held by him is held; and that the hour of this paper is a date and not an hour and that a claim window is not ownership and expires on the day it says it does.

The man of about fifty put it on the counter at about four o'clock, squared it up to the edge of the counter with two fingers, and went back in, and he did not say a word to anybody about it and nobody in that office said a word to him about it.

Behind that counter there is a line of men who have come and gone through that room since the fourth of April past and about half of them cannot read what is put in front of them at that counter either, and the man behind it has never once said out loud that he is sorry about it, and the reason is his own and he has given it twice and both times he said he had nothing to say about it.

A man of about twenty-six came in at about ten to five on the Friday, being the boy who stacks empties at a quay and who is about twenty-six and is not in that room for anything to do with glass, and he saw the paper and he asked the man of about fifty one question about it, being what a claim book was and where it was kept.

"It is a book," said the man of about fifty. "It is in this building. I am not going to tell you what room it is in and you did not ask me and if you had asked me I would not have told you."

That was the whole of it and the man of about twenty-six went out and did not come back to that counter and is not on a sheet and was not paid anything for asking.

Nobody in this yard asked the man of about fifty one question about it and this yard is not going to.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon Nessa Pike was in that shed and the holder of that writ was at the bench stone and they had it in front of them and neither of them had a paper in his hand.

"Nobody has lodged a claim," she said.

"Nobody has."

"And nobody has paid a penny of it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, for one claim, and that is the figure in chalk on that slate at the end stall for a day's work with a boat and two men, and there is not one man in this harbor who has that figure in his hand on any day of the week."

"Then it is cheap."

"It is cheap and it is nothing," said Nessa Pike. "I have had four days to think about that number and here is what I have. A man with nothing can put that sheet up on a wall. A man with nothing can pay a penny for a sheet of paper. And a man with nothing cannot pay a mark and eightpence for a claim, so a claim that costs a mark and eightpence is a claim that only a man with a mark and eightpence can make, and that is the first time in this harbor in about a year that a figure has been written that a poor man cannot get at, and the man it is written for is not a man in this market."

Calder Marr did not answer that.

"There is no name against it in the leaf," she said, "and there is no name against it in the first column either, because this yard did not put that sheet on that wall and has not paid a penny to anybody to put it there and is not going to argue with a paper it has not read the inside of. What goes in the second column is the number and the reason for it, and the reason is hers and not mine."

**One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said Friday at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall and nothing went out of it, and the bag is five hundred and seventy-six pence, being three marks eight shillings, and is short of the wage of four marks by sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence. A chandler of about fifty whose wife keeps the count would not sell eight fathoms of tarred rope at four shillings, being forty-eight pence, to a man of about forty-four of the third lay called Nate Pry, and gave his reason in his own mouth, being that there is a sheet on a wall at the back of an end stall since the fifteenth and that he is not going to stand in his shop and ask a man whether he has been in a room with a piece of glass, and that he has a woman and a girl of eleven behind that counter and would rather be wrong about himself than about her; he took fourpence on an old account, gave the rope back onto the man's arm, and did not argue, and the man was not charged with anything. A paper of four lines went up on the counter at the front of the office off the west basin at about four in the afternoon, put there by a man of about fifty who works at a table with two papers on it, with no name on it and no seal at the foot of it, saying that a claim has been entered under a sheet that went up in the front street on the fifteenth, that a person who has been in a room with a piece of worked glass that has a scene in it is to be entered in the claim book of that harbor under his own name, and that until he has been entered a claim window held by him is held, and that a claim window is not ownership and expires on the day it says it does; nobody in that yard asked him one question about it. Nobody in that harbor has lodged a claim and nobody has paid a penny of the charge at the foot of that sheet, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, which is the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall for a day's work with a boat and two men, and the ship's hand put that figure and her reason for it in the second column with no name against it. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is seventeen days old and its fourth line ends on the twentieth of September next and has been answered by nobody. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

*First column, entered on Friday the seventeenth day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, being one mark and eightpence, and that nothing went out of it, and that there are five hundred and seventy-six pence in the bag, being three marks eight shillings, and that the figure the bag is short of the wage of four marks, being six hundred and forty pence, is sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence; and that a chandler of about fifty with a wife who keeps the count refused at about eleven in the morning to sell eight fathoms of tarred rope at four shillings, being forty-eight pence, to a man of about forty-four of the third lay called Nate Pry, and took fourpence on an old account and gave the rope back onto his arm and did not argue; and that the said man carried a piece of worked glass about two hands square with a border round it out of a boat's hold in the middle of June past on his own back to a store on the second lay and was given a shilling and a half for it and has not been near a piece since and was not asked why he was carrying it; and that the chandler gave his reason in his own mouth, being that he is not going to stand in his shop and ask a man whether he has been in a room with a piece of glass, and that he has a wife and a girl of eleven behind that counter and would rather be wrong about himself than about her, and that asking any other man the same question would have produced the same answer and would have shut the counter to everybody; and that a sheet of paper of four lines went up on the counter at the front of the office off the west basin at about four in the afternoon, put there by a man of about fifty who works at a table with two papers on it, and that it has no name on it and no seal at the foot of it and no office at the end of anything, and that it says that a claim has been entered under a sheet that went up on a wall in the front street on the fifteenth day of September past, that a person who has been in a room with a piece of worked glass that has a scene in it is to be entered in the claim book of that harbor under his own name, and that until he has been entered a claim window held by him is held, and that a claim window is not ownership and expires on the day it says it does; and that nobody in that yard asked him one question about it; and that nobody in that harbor has lodged a claim under the sheet at the back of that end stall and nobody has paid a penny of the charge at the foot of it; and that the paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is seventeen days old and its fourth line ends on the twentieth of September next, being three days off, and has been answered by nobody.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the charge at the foot of the sheet at the back of that end stall is one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, for one claim, and that that figure is the same figure that is in chalk on a slate at an end stall for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth; and that the reason it goes in the second column and not in the first is in the ship's own mouth and is that a man with nothing can put a sheet up on a wall and can pay a penny for a sheet of paper and cannot pay a mark and eightpence for a claim, and that a claim that costs a mark and eightpence is a claim only a man with a mark and eightpence can make, and that in this harbor that is a figure a poor man cannot get at; and that no name is against any of it because this yard did not put that sheet up, has paid nothing to anybody to put it up, and is not going to argue with the inside of a paper it has not read; and that the reason a chandler's counter shutting to one man on the said day is not entered in the first column is that a counter shutting is a cost and not a figure, and that the cost is eight fathoms of tarred rope at four shillings and a morning, and that there is no figure in this yard for a man's morning; and that the reason no name is against the man of about fifty who put a paper on that counter is that this yard has asked him nothing about the box on that table and is not going to start on this one either; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Friday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
