# Chapter 428

*Monday the Twentieth Day of September, the Seventy-Second Time the Wall Behind the Reeve's Bench Came Down Bare and Went Up at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Nobody Put It Back, the Eighty-Third Time the Cistern Was Charged and Walked on by None of It, the Fourth Line of the Paper at the Top Left of That Door in Fish Street Ending on the Said Day and Being Answered by Nobody, a Man Going Down in a Bell at About Eleven Foot on a Berth at the Third Lay at Ten in the Morning Beside a Crate of Three Pieces of Recovered Glass and Coming Up at About Twenty Past Eleven With a Cold in His Teeth and a Taste of Iron Like a Nail, and a Ship's Hand Who Says Out Loud That She Has Got No Figure for It and Is Not Going to Make One Up*

Monday the twentieth day of September, at that berth on the third lay, in Fish Street, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, being the seventy-second time the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare, and it went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back, and it was a rota day and the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the eighty-third time, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and there are five hundred and seventy-six pence in the bag, being three marks eight shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed from about half past two in the afternoon and did not go down to that berth. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he scraped a lump of the old bedding off the sixth piece with the point of the hook and looked at it and put it in his pocket and went on working. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twenty-six days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and twenty-eight days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are an eleventh week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and sixty-six days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirty-second night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for sixty-eight days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for thirty-seven days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is twenty days old, its fourth line ends on the said day, and it has been answered by nobody.**

---

The fourth line of that paper says that anybody found to have been in that room on the night of the nineteenth of August is to take himself, or herself, off out of that harbor before the twentieth day of September next.

It is the twentieth day of September. The paper is on the door at the top left of it and it has been there twenty days and it has a drawing-pin under a fold of oiled paper so that it cannot be taken out in about a second, and nobody in that harbor has answered it.

The room was let out on the said Sunday at two o'clock with a bowl on the top stone of the step, and about sixteen people went up those stairs and three came down, and eleven of the sixteen could not read.

Whether one of the eleven was in that room on that Sunday is not a thing anybody in that room looked for.

---

At ten in the morning Calder Marr went down in a bell at a berth on the third lay.

The boat is of about eleven tons and her garboard was opened in June and the caulker had not been asked for her since the Friday and a seam of it had been let go. The owner had asked him on the Friday at that shed to go back and finish it and he went on the Monday because on the Friday he was doing a day at that berth and this was not a second day at a berth and nobody paid for it and nobody is going to.

The man of about twenty-nine who owns her was on the rope and a boy of about fifteen was on the pump and Calder Marr went down to about eleven foot with a lamp on his chest and a hand-lead in his belt.

Nine feet off his left shoulder, under the fore peak, there was a crate.

It is about four foot by two and it has boards across the top of it and it is stowed against the peak on the off side, and it has three pieces of worked glass in it under some straw. A border, a panel about the size of two hands, and a piece of a hatch cover with a hinge still on it. The owner bought them on the Friday off a man off a lighter, and he is sending them up-coast on the Tuesday, and he paid four shillings for the three of them and he thinks he got a good price.

Calder Marr was about eleven foot off the ground and nine foot from that crate for about fifty minutes.

The garboard was under his right knee and there was a seam of it about four foot long gone open and a cold draught coming through it into his left side, and he put the mend in and drove it home and paid it round with oakum and a marlinspike and did it twice over because the first lot went soft straight away.

He knew where the crate was the whole time without looking at it, the way a man knows where a ladder is in the dark, and at about half past ten the boy on the pump stopped pumping for a moment and started again and Calder Marr's hands went to the frame of the ladder before his head had told him anything, and he went on with the oakum.

He came up at about twenty past eleven.

---

He came up and he got his helmet off and he sat down on the stones at the head of that berth with his back against the stem, and he was there for about a quarter of an hour before he said anything.

"I am going to say this once," he said, "and I am saying it to the two of you and to nobody in this market, and I am not going to have it written anywhere with a name against it."

"Go on," said the man of about twenty-nine.

"There is a cold in my teeth that was not there at eight o'clock this morning, and there is a taste of iron in the back of my mouth like a nail, and my hands are not right, and I came up that ladder and I got to the bottom of it and I stood there and I could not tell you for about half a minute which of the two hatches in that boat I came up out of, and I had to put my hand on the frame of the near one to find out."

"That is a thing off a bell," said the man of about twenty-nine. "That happens. That is cold coming in off the water and a pump going and a man coming up fast. I have had it nine times."

"I have had it off a bell about four hundred times in nine months," said Calder Marr, "and I have had that taste of iron in my mouth four times in nine months and twice off that shelf on the second lay in June and once off the hill in August, and every one of those three times I was against a piece of worked glass within a foot of my face for about as long as it takes to boil an egg. This morning I was against a crate of three pieces of worked glass for fifty minutes at nine foot."

He sat on the stones.

"I have not got a reading out of it and I am not going to say that I have. I have got a cold in my teeth and a taste of iron and I could not tell you which hatch I came out of, and that is the whole of what I have got, and it has got nothing in it that anybody in this harbor has to take any notice of, and nobody is going to take any notice of it, and that includes me."

Nessa Pike came down that slip at about half past twelve and he told her the same three things in the same words and no more.

The man of about twenty-nine was standing at the head of that berth with the mend in it and a wet rope in his hand while she was there, and he said to her, not to Calder Marr:

"I have been in and out of bells for eleven years and I have had it nine times and it has been the cold coming in off the water every single time of the nine, and I have never once had a taste of anything out of my mouth. That is not a thing I am going to say out loud in a market, and I am saying it to you because you are the one who prices things."

"Noted," said Nessa Pike. "And not in that column, because a man saying what it is not is not a figure."

She stood and thought about it for a while.

"I have got no figure for that," she said. "I want you to hear me say that properly, because it is a thing I know how to do and I am not going to do it tonight. There is no figure in this harbor for a cold in a man's teeth on a Monday and I am not going to invent one, and if I put a mark against your name in the first column you would be a man on a page for the rest of his life over a bad lunch."

"Then leave me off the page."

"You are not going on the page, and I am going to write one line in the second column with the reason against it and with no name against it, because if I do not write it down then in a fortnight there will be two men in this harbor saying they had it on a Monday and nobody will know what day Monday was." She put her hand on her own slate. "The reason is that the holder of that writ has had a thing off a piece of worked glass three times in nine months and has told two people about it on the said day and has told nobody else anything, and that is the only reason there is a line in that book at all."

---

At about four in the afternoon the crate was still under that fore peak, and the three pieces of worked glass were still in it, and the owner was going to send them up-coast on the Tuesday.

Nobody in that harbor has told the eleven people anything about any of it and nobody in that market was told anything about it on the said day, and a man of about forty-six who read six lines off that sheet out loud in the middle of that market on the Thursday, and who said in a shed that same afternoon that he was not going to read a claim out at that table and gave his reason, read nothing out on the Monday.

The cistern was charged at about eight and walked on at about half past nine for the eighty-third time and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The fourth line of the paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street ended on the said day, being the twentieth day of September next, and that paper has been on that door twenty days and has been answered by nobody, and a room over a joiner's shop was let out on the Sunday before with about sixteen going up and three coming down, and whether one of the eleven was in that room on that Sunday is not a thing anybody in that room looked for. A man went down in a bell at about eleven foot at a berth on the third lay at ten in the morning with a man of about twenty-nine on the rope and a boy of about fifteen on the pump, and there was a crate of three pieces of worked glass under the fore peak about nine foot off his left shoulder, being a border, a panel about the size of two hands and a piece of a hatch cover with a hinge still on it, bought off a man off a lighter on the Friday for four shillings and going up-coast on the Tuesday, and he was beside that crate for about fifty minutes and came up at about twenty past eleven; and he sat down on the stones and said three things to the two men on that berth and to two people in a shed, being a cold in his teeth, a taste of iron in the back of his mouth like a nail, and not being able to tell for about half a minute which of two hatches in that boat he had come up out of, and saying that he has had that taste of iron three times in nine months and that on each of those three times he was within a foot of a piece of worked glass for about as long as it takes to boil an egg, and that he has got no reading out of it and is not going to say that he has; and the ship's hand said out loud that she has got no figure for that and is not going to invent one, and wrote one line in the second column against a reason with no name against it, and he is not on the page. Nothing came into that yard on the said day and nothing went out of it, and the bag is five hundred and seventy-six pence, being three marks eight shillings, and is short of the wage of four marks by sixty-four pence, being five shillings and fourpence. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the ship's hand wrote one line in that column on the said day is in her own mouth and is that if it is not written down then in a fortnight there will be two men in that harbor saying they had it on a Monday and nobody will know what Monday was; and that the reason no figure is set against a cold in a man's teeth is in her own mouth and is that there is no figure in that harbor for it and that inventing one would be a price put on a body by a woman with a slate; and that the reason the holder of that writ told two men on a berth and two people in a shed and has told nobody in that market anything is in his own mouth and is that he has got no reading out of it and is not going to say that he has, and that it has got nothing in it that anybody in that harbor has to take any notice of, and that nobody is going to take any notice of it and that includes him; and that the reason a crate of three pieces of worked glass is under the fore peak of a boat of about eleven tons at four o'clock on the said afternoon and is going up-coast on the Tuesday is that four shillings is a good price for three pieces and the owner is not a man with a grievance and has not been asked one question about what he bought; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
