# Chapter 436

*Tuesday the Twenty-Eighth Day of September, the Forty-Ninth Wage Tuesday and the Column Not Moved on It and Forty-Nine Weeks Standing Fallen Due at the Close of the Day, a Day on the Water Paid at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Out of That Yard's Own Figures at About Four in the Afternoon, a Bellwright's Day at the Same Figure, Seven Boats and About Thirty Men Out From About Seven in the Morning Until the Last of the Ebb, Fifteen Fathoms of Chain Off a Tender's Quarter With a Weight on the Tail of It Coming Up Over a Rail at Half Past Two With the Reason Written Down, a Boat of About Twenty-Two Tons Striking a Baulk of Oak That Was Laid Down Without a Light on It and Opening About Nine Foot of Her Garboard, and the Bag at Three Hundred and Sixty-Four Pence*

Tuesday the twenty-eighth day of September, on a bank about four hundred yards down that foul, in that shed, and on that mole head.

**The said Tuesday was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and it was the forty-ninth wage Tuesday, and the column at the front of that reeve's bench did not move on it, and the column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room, and forty-eight weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of these ten days and forty-nine stand at the close of the said day, and nobody is to write one word under any of it saying anybody is going to pay it. Two hundred and eighty-four pence, being one mark ten shillings and fourpence, went out of that yard on the said day, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence for the person on the water and one hundred and sixty-eight pence for the bellwright's day, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came back in at about two in the afternoon for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and there are three hundred and sixty-four pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two marks three shillings and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is two hundred and seventy-six pence, being one mark nine shillings and eightpence.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was at the head of that slip from about six in the morning until about five in the afternoon and did not go out in any of the seven boats. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he put the hook into the crack in the stone by the eleventh piece and left it standing there for the whole four hours with his hand on it and did not use it. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and thirty-four days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and thirty-six days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a twelfth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and seventy-four days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fortieth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty-eight days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for seventy-six days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the thirteenth day and the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the twenty-eighth day and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-five days.**

---

They were on that water at ten past seven in the morning.

Seven boats went out from the head of the third lay inside that harbor's own line with about thirty men in them, and the little steam tender of about forty tons came out ahead of them with fifteen fathoms of chain already made up, and Halvard Ness stood on that tender's after deck with his hand on nothing and watched a boat of about forty tons with no papers go past the outer marks and come back at about five on the Friday before and did not hail him and was not hailed by him.

The *Alewife* was in nine foot on a bank with a foot and a half of that bank under her, and the ebb started about half past five in the afternoon and would be at the bottom about a quarter to four the next morning.

They put two baulks of oak under her keel, one under the garboard strake and one forward of the shoe, and drove them up with wedges and a maul, and got the whole of that keel up about two feet clear of the mud by about eleven o'clock, and by about two o'clock in the afternoon the water over her had gone from nine foot to about seven foot and a half and the mud had begun to come up to her.

Mina Kett came down in the tender's boat at about eleven with her box and stood on that mud in about eight foot of water on the *Alewife*'s port bilge with the whole of the port rail out of the water, and she put the eleven battens down that keel one under another from the garboard forward to the stem with a mallet and a belaying pin and about eleven minutes, and then she held the two end ones while a man of about twenty-six with a saw took the new piece off them at the two ends and about six inches in from each end, and a man of about forty-four who has been planing forty-one years cut it out of two baulks of oak in about fifty minutes and it came off in three pieces and went up into that tender's boat.

It did not fit.

Nobody in that company said a word about it for about ten minutes. Then Mina Kett got her thumb into the end of the middle piece and said, out loud, to about thirty men in seven boats:

"That is a guess with a curve in it. I told you it was a guess with a curve in it. Take it up and cut it again and take about a sixteenth off the whole length and not off one end."

They took it up and cut it again and it went on in about two hours and it was a tight fit in the middle and a shade loose at the after end, and they faired the after end to the old with a chisel and a drawknife and a man of about twenty-six with a plane, and it was done at about twenty past two in the afternoon with the water down to about seven foot and a half over that shoe and still going and the mud coming up to her.

There is nine foot over that bank at the top of the water and about seven foot and a half at the bottom of it, and that foot and a half is the whole of what a man has got, and it comes back every twelve hours and a bit, and it is not improved by anybody coming to look at it.

Halvard Ness put the chain weight over that tender's rail at twenty-two minutes past two in the afternoon, before the water had made, and every man in that bank was in the boat in about nine minutes.

The reason was written down at about half past four in the evening in the ship's left hand, with the hour against it: *the chain came up at twenty-two minutes past two in the afternoon because the water was down to about seven foot and a half over that shoe and still going and the mud was beginning to come up to her, and there are nine foot on that bank at the top of the water and about seven foot and a half at the bottom of it, and that is a foot and a half and it comes back every twelve hours, and if a man had been in that mud at half past two he would have had about twenty minutes of it and no more, and twenty minutes is not enough to get a man off that bank on his own two feet.*

---

At about ten past two in the afternoon, about two hours before that, a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay was coming off the bank with her bow riding two feet high off a run of hard water, and there was a baulk of oak lying across about six feet of that run with about nine inches of it out of the water in a sea, laid down at about half past eight in the morning by two men of that yard's hands to sit under that keel, and there was no light on it and no flag and nothing on it at all.

Ham Birt took her straight at it because he had been coming in and out of that water all morning and had a way of it in his head, and the baulk was not there yesterday.

It opened about nine foot of her garboard forward and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands, and she took water and came round and he got her on her anchor, and about thirty men got a line to her in about four minutes and she was under about two feet by the time she was fast.

Nobody was hurt. A man of about thirty-one on the third lay who was on her forward thwart went over the side and came up holding his own hat.

Ham Birt sat on his own wheelhouse step for about ten minutes and said nothing at all, and then he said one sentence to the man of about fifty who owns that boat of about twenty-two tons of his own, and the sentence was: "Your baulk was lying on my ground and it had nothing on it."

The woman who prices things wrote it down in her own left hand on the back of the sheet of good paper that is on the wall of that shed, and the sheet of good paper has the four days on it and the figure in chalk on it, and the line she wrote is under both.

---

Nobody was charged for it.

The man of about fifty who owns that boat said in his own mouth that the baulk is that yard's and that the baulk was that yard's at half past eight in the morning and that the bill is that yard's, and that he is not going to charge it to the owner of any of the seven boats, and that he is not going to be asked to call seven boats anything at all in front of a reeve's bench, and that a man who has been hit by his own yard's timber once is not going to stand there and give it a name for them.

"Not one penny of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown," said Nessa Pike. "It comes out of this yard or it does not come, and if it does not come this is the day it did not come."

---

There is no flour in that market and there has been none since the spring. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August. A figure of four hundred and fifty pence is on a board at an end stall with three hundred and seventy-eight pence standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and that figure cannot be taken off. The sheet of six lines is on the wall at the back of that stall for the thirteenth day on the outside of that figure and no claim has been lodged under it and nobody has paid the one hundred and sixty-eight pence at the foot of it. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-five days and is a different paper on a different wall. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the twenty-eighth day. The mole-head light is out. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered. One berth on the third lay is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and is not re-written. Forty-nine weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them.

**Seven boats and about thirty men went out from the head of the third lay at ten past seven in the morning on the said Tuesday, inside that harbor's own line, with a small steam tender of about forty tons ahead of them and fifteen fathoms of chain made up off her quarter. They put two baulks of oak under the keel of a boat of about nineteen tons lying in nine foot on a bank four hundred yards down the foul, and got that keel about two feet clear of the mud by about eleven o'clock and the water over her went from nine foot to about seven foot and a half by about two o'clock in the afternoon. A bellwright of about thirty-four named Mina Kett came down in the tender's boat at about eleven and stood on that mud in about eight foot of water on that boat's port bilge and put eleven battens down that keel in about eleven minutes with a mallet and a belaying pin, and a man of about twenty-six with a saw took the new piece off the two ends and about six inches in from each end, and a man of about forty-four who has been planing forty-one years cut it out of two baulks of oak in about fifty minutes, and it came off in three pieces and it did not fit, and nobody in that company said a word about it for about ten minutes, and then she put her thumb into the end of the middle piece and said out loud to about thirty men in seven boats that it is a guess with a curve in it and that she had told them it was a guess with a curve in it, and they took it up and cut it again and took about a sixteenth off the whole length and not off one end, and it went on in about two hours and was a tight fit in the middle and a shade loose at the after end, and it was faired and done at about twenty past two in the afternoon with the water down to about seven foot and a half over that shoe and still going and the mud coming up to her, and there is a foot and a half between the top of the water on that bank and the bottom of it. The chain weight came over the tender's rail at twenty-two minutes past two in the afternoon, and every man in that bank was in the boat in about nine minutes, and the reason was written down at about half past four in the evening in the ship's left hand with the hour against it. At about ten past two in the afternoon a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay struck a baulk of oak that that yard's own hands had laid down at half past eight in the morning with about nine inches of it out of the water in a sea, with no light on it and no flag and nothing on it at all, and opened about nine foot of her garboard forward and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands, and about thirty men got a line to her in about four minutes and she was under about two feet by the time she was fast, and nobody was hurt. The owner of that boat said in his own mouth that the baulk is that yard's and that the bill is that yard's and that it is not going to be charged to the owner of any of the seven boats, and two hundred and eighty-four pence, being one mark ten shillings and fourpence, went out of that yard that day and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came in and the bag stands at three hundred and sixty-four pence, being two marks three shillings and eightpence, and the forty-ninth wage Tuesday did not move the column and forty-nine weeks stand fallen due.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-eighth day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the forty-ninth wage Tuesday and that the column did not move on it, and that forty-eight weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of these ten days and forty-nine stand at the close of the said day; and that seven boats and about thirty men went out from the head of the third lay at ten past seven in the morning inside that harbor's own line with a small steam tender of about forty tons ahead of them and fifteen fathoms of chain made up off her quarter, and that a keel of about nineteen feet was got about two feet clear of a mud bank about four hundred yards down that foul by about eleven o'clock, and that the water over her went from nine foot at the top of the water to about seven foot and a half by about two o'clock in the afternoon; and that eleven battens were put down that keel in about eleven minutes and a new piece of keel was taken off the two ends of them and about six inches in from each end and was cut out of two baulks of oak in about fifty minutes and came off in three pieces and did not fit the first time, and that a bellwright of about thirty-four said out loud to about thirty men that it is a guess with a curve in it and that she had told them so, and that about a sixteenth was taken off the whole length of it and not off one end and it went on in about two hours and was faired at the after end with a chisel and a drawknife and a plane; and that the chain weight came over the tender's rail at twenty-two minutes past two in the afternoon and every man in that bank was in the boat in about nine minutes; and that at about ten past two in the afternoon a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay struck a baulk of oak about six feet long laid down by two of that yard's hands at about half past eight in the morning with about nine inches of it out of the water in a sea and with no light on it and no flag and nothing on it, and opened about nine foot of her garboard forward and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands, and about thirty men got a line to her in about four minutes and she was under about two feet by the time she was fast, and nobody was hurt; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about four in the afternoon for the person on the water and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out at about four in the afternoon for the bellwright's day, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at about two in the afternoon for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, and that five hundred and thirty-two minus two hundred and eighty-four plus one hundred and sixty-eight is three hundred and sixty-four pence at the close of the said day, being two marks three shillings and eightpence, being short of the wage of four marks by two hundred and seventy-six pence, being one mark nine shillings and eightpence; and that no charge was placed on that cistern on the said day and the said day was not a wipe.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the reason for a cutoff being pulled is written down with the hour against it is in the ship's own mouth and is that a chain coming up over a rail at twenty-two minutes past two in the afternoon is worth nothing to anybody at four o'clock in the evening unless somebody wrote down that it was twenty-two minutes past two and why; and that the reason the reason is written in this column and not in the first is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count the first column and cannot take a reason out of it; and that the reason the new piece of keel came off in three pieces and did not fit is in the mouth of a woman of about thirty-four and is that it is a guess with a curve in it and not a drawing, and that this yard has entered it exactly as it was said and has not improved on it; and that the reason a bill for damage done in seven boats in company comes to that yard and is not charged to the owner of any of them is in the mouth of the woman who prices things and is that not one penny of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown, and that if it does not come out of that yard it does not come; and that the reason about ten minutes of that company's foot of water were lost on that Tuesday because a man of about forty-four with a boat on a bank got to it eleven minutes late is entered in the convoy's own sheet with a name and an hour against it and is not entered in this yard's book, and that this yard is not the owner of that sheet and does not have a copy of it and has not asked for one; and that the reason the second column of that yard's own leaf carries a line about a bellwright's own figure of five shillings on the thirtieth and not on the said Tuesday is that she did not say it on the said Tuesday and this yard does not enter a thing a person has not said; and that the reason no figure is entered against about thirty men and a holed hull and a chain and a re-cut piece of keel is that labour is not checkable from a mole and that a figure put against thirty men's Tuesday would be a price put on thirty men by a woman with a slate; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Tuesday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
