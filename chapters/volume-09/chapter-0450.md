# Chapter 450

*Tuesday the Twelfth Day of October, the Fifty-First Wage Tuesday, and the Wage Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With Twenty Envelopes in a Chest in a Front Room Did Not Move on It and Fifty-One Weeks Stand Fallen Due With No Envelope Against Any of Them at the Close of the Said Day, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and a Bellwright of About Thirty-Four Paid One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence in Coin on the Flat of the Bench Stone at About Four in the Afternoon, and Twenty-Eight Pence in the Bag, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty, a Mole Head at Ten Past Five in the Evening With the Light Out and Its Lens Out Since the Seventh of May, a Leaf Read Back in Full With Both Definitions on the Face of That Reading and the Fourth Column Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Five Where It Stood and Taking No Line, Three Statements About the Water and the Harbor and This Yard in Three Voices, the Whole of That Harbor's Standing Condition Once and Nowhere Else, and a Book on a Table in a Room With One Line in It Left Blank, Being a Place Where Nobody Was Asked*

Tuesday the twelfth day of October, on that mole head, in that shed, in a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street, and in a room over a fish market.

**The said Tuesday was the fifty-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon to a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line at that same figure, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence, being ten marks six shillings and eightpence, on ten days, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at the figure in chalk, and the whole of what went out of it is one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence, being eleven marks seven shillings and fourpence, in eleven payments on ten days, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence a payment, and one hundred and ninety-six plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight is twenty-eight. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown. The wage column did not move on the fifty-first, and fifty weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of the said day and fifty-one stand at the close of it.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the tenth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why, and a shoe that is not his was on the floor beside it. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name stood on a public sheet and on a sheet of four heads in a room over a fish market, and nobody took it off either and nobody thanked her and nobody offered. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he sat on the eleventh piece with his back to the water and got up at four and went home, which he has done four times in seventy days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-eight days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fifty days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page, and the bellwright of about thirty-four who was paid on the flat of that bench stone at about four in the afternoon on the said day is not that bellwright and is in a different place and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fourteenth week and nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of the third column with a name beside it that is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-eight days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-fourth night. Two lights off that point have not moved in seventy-two days, counted from the hour they were first seen on the evening of the first of August past. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety days and has been given nothing by anybody and has not been sent for. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-seventh day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-second day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-ninth day.**

---

At about ten past five in the evening, on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head, in the open, in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, Nessa Pike read that leaf back and the holder of that writ stood on the other side of it and did not touch it.

The mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past, and the oil in that lamp has been in that building since a day in August and there is none in that harbor to put in it.

"The first column of that leaf has one hundred and eighty-two entries in it at this hour and one hundred and eighty-three at the close of the day," she said. "There were one hundred and seventy-three of them at the close of Saturday the second day of October past and ten have gone in since, one against each of the ten days from Sunday the third to this day, and not one day in two of them, and this morning's own was the fortieth of the forty and makes one hundred and eighty-two, and this evening's own is the forty-first of the forty-one that have gone in across these ten days and makes one hundred and eighty-three at the close. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has seven hundred and eighty-seven lines in it at this hour and seven hundred and eighty-nine at the close of the day," she said. "Seven hundred and thirty-nine of them were in it at the close of the second of October past and five have gone in on each of the ten days since, three before the hour of that day's reading and two after it, and three of this day's went in before this reading and two went into it in this evening after it. Seven hundred and thirty-nine plus fifty is seven hundred and eighty-nine at the close, and seven hundred and thirty-seven plus fifty is seven hundred and eighty-seven at this hour. **That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's one hundred and eighty-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since. He was not on the stone on any of these ten days and was put on nothing, and nobody in that crew has said one word to anybody about the three nots standing against the eighth of June past, and there is a fourteenth week in which that has not been said, and no day of any week before the first of July was entered against that line and none is going to be."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close," she said, "and it has taken no line in these ten days and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to. The reason it has taken no line was entered in the second column at about ten past nine in the evening of the twenty-eighth day of August, in that yard's own mouth, with the hour against it, and it is entered once and it is not entered again, and the reason it is in the second column and not in the fourth is that a man on a mole cannot check the second column and a man on a mole can check the fourth."

She shut it and put it under her own coat and neither of them said one word about the figure it gave.

---

Three people said one thing each about that mole head at about ten past five in the evening on the said day, and they were said in that order, and none of them was said twice, and none of them was written down.

Jens Ower, of about twenty-nine, of no lay, who lightered about eleven tons, about the water: "The water is the only thing in this harbor that has not gone up since the first of June, and the reason is that nobody has ever managed to put a figure on it. There is nine foot on a bank four hundred yards down that foul and there is seven foot and a half at the bottom of it and there is a foot and a half between them, and a foot and a half is the whole of what a company of seven boats is worth, and that has not changed since the twenty-third of September and it is not going to change this winter."

The woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall and her slate, about the harbor: "This harbor is the cheapest thing in it. I have got a figure on a slate behind me that has been up since the first of June and it is the price of a barrel of oil and there is not a barrel of oil in this harbor to price, and that figure is not coming off, and I have been asked three times in a month to take it off and I am not going to take it off, and that is the whole of what I have to say about this harbor and I have said it."

The holder of that writ, about this yard: "This yard is a shed, a bench stone, a leaf with four columns in it, a hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at a figure in chalk on that slate, and twenty-eight pence in a bag at the close of today. Three of the four columns in that leaf are mine and the first of them is a count anybody can take with a pencil and the second of them is a count nobody can, and I have been printing that on the face of it for ten days so that a stranger knows which one they are holding. That is what this yard is and that is what it has been since the fourth of April past, and it is not an office and it decides nothing and it is not a member of anything, and a man of about thirty-four of that second lay told me on the fourth of October that the asking is not the work, and he was right about that before I was, and this yard is still the same yard."

---

Here is the whole of what that harbor was carrying on the twelfth day of October, and it is here once and nowhere else.

There is no flour in that market and none is coming this month and there has been no barley in that market since the middle of September. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August, and a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down and cannot be taken off. Physic is fourteen pence, being one shilling and twopence, and there is none of it in this harbor. The mole-head light is out and its lens has been out since the seventh of May. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide has been unanswered since the ninth of November past. The third lay fills on the first of April and one berth is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and is not re-written. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them, and the call in it is not named. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and fifty-one weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The cistern at the back of that market is charged on a Monday and a Thursday and was charged on the fourth, the seventh and the eleventh days of October and walked on by none of it on any of them, and the top name on that rota board is still off it, and the seven other days of these ten put no charge on it. The sheet of six printed lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-seventh day, on the outside of the figure of four hundred and fifty pence, and is answered by nobody, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is on a different square of a different wall in the same front street for the fifty-ninth day, names no person and no harbor, puts no figure on it, and is answered by nobody. The paper of four lines at the top left of the door of a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street is on that door for the forty-second day, its fourth line ended on the twentieth day of September past, and the eleven people it names have been told only that the paper is there. The notice of eight clauses of the seventeenth day of May past is on its own wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-eight days and has not been touched. The twenty-second head of the public volume in that room carries three lines and a dash, and there are four lines in pencil on the inside of the back cover of that volume with a name and a date against them, and it is not to be repaired, explained or named against. A ropemaker of about sixty-one of the third lay, with a bad knee and a stick and a rope walk about forty feet long that does not run, was put out of a room on the nineteenth day of September and is not back in it, and nobody has been thanked for that and nobody is to be, and nobody has been made whole and nobody has been charged with anything. The far bench is occupied from ten until four with a shoe on the floor beside it that is not his and has been there since the third of May past. The two objects of nobody's are four hundred and eighty-eight days old and about nine feet apart and one of them has had a corner off since the seventeenth of June past and has not been lashed back. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in seventy-two days. A man of about fifty-one on the crown of that mole has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for ninety days, has a stone in his own pocket that he put there himself, and has been given nothing. Three marks to a man of Ashtide stand on an anchor date out of an earlier volume and are ruled not repaired, not re-dated, not advanced, not reduced and not paid. Seven pence is owed to that yard's own shipwright. Eight marks and a day of a boat is owed to that harbor's own bellwright and the chest is shut and he has not been asked for a hundred and fifty days. Eight shillings and tenpence is owed to a woman of that harbor with her name on the figure. Forty marks first after food is owed to a person of no office. Forty marks whole is owed to a boy of twenty-two, and the three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fourteenth week unasked, and the third column is unfilled and unappointed and his hand is at the head of it. A municipal claim on the third of three things in a custody room on a hill has been in a book in that building since the December before last and is not withdrawn and no request for its withdrawal has been made by anybody. A box in an office off the west basin is not empty and a fourth box on the same table is not empty either, and a man of about fifty who works at that table wanted that fourth box left empty and has not been asked about it. A slip of paper with a figure on it and no name on it is in a drawer with a sheet in it and has not been looked at, priced or headed. A claim against a copy is in a book at a counter off the west basin and is not withdrawn, and a sheet of about three feet with four heads on it in a room over a fish market carries that claim and does not discharge it. A belt of a coast road is unlicensed and the thirty marks on it were not paid. A line cut in lead about the size of a man's palm is in a drawer in a building on that hill and did not come down that hill.

At about half past five in the evening, in the room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street, Hobb Lee put his hand on the long table and looked at the public volume on it and did not open it at the back cover, and then he turned back to the page he had left open and ruled a line under the last head on that page and left it empty, and he wrote his own name and the hour against it in his own hand.

The head at the top of that line is *A page of six names on a mole with a boat as a price and no figure against it.* The line under it is empty. The reason is written at the foot of that page in the same hand and it is: *There is nobody in this harbor I can ask for this line, and I am not going to write one and put a name against it, and I have left it ruled and empty so that anybody who comes in here can see that there is a place on this page where nobody was asked.*

The twenty-second head of that volume was on a different page and carries three lines and a dash, and it was not touched on that evening, and the four lines in pencil on the inside of the back cover were not looked at by anybody, and the bowl on the top stone of that step was still on the step where he had put it at two o'clock.

**THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET AND NO BARREL OF LAMP OIL IN THAT HARBOR AND A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS ON A BOARD THAT IS NOT CLEARED AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND THE LIGHT IS OUT, AND A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS NOT THE PRICE OF ANYTHING THAT HAPPENED IN THESE TEN DAYS. A CLAIM AGAINST A COPY IS IN A BOOK AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN, AND A SHEET OF ABOUT THREE FEET CARRIES IT AND DOES NOT DISCHARGE IT, AND A COPY AND AN ORIGINAL ARE BOTH IN A BUILDING ON A HILL, AND A WOMAN OF NO OFFICE HAS HER NAME ON A PUBLIC SHEET AND ON A SHEET OF FOUR HEADS AND NOBODY TOOK IT OFF EITHER AND NOBODY THANKED HER AND NOBODY OFFERED, AND A DIVER WENT DOWN ONCE AND CAME UP WITH AN AFTERTASTE AND WITH ONE AFTERNOON IN JUNE HE CAN PUT A NAME TO AND NOTHING ELSE, AND A ROPEMAKER OF ABOUT SIXTY-ONE IS NOT IN HER ROOM, AND NOTHING IN THAT HARBOR IS BETTER OFF THAN IT WAS ON THE THIRD DAY OF OCTOBER AND EVERY FIGURE ON THIS PAGE THAT IS A FIGURE OF MONEY IS A FIGURE OF A DAY'S WORK AT A FIGURE IN CHALK ON A SLATE AT AN END STALL.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twelfth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the fifty-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and that the wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and did not move on the fifty-first, so that fifty weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of the said day and fifty-one stand at the close of them; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings;e said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, and that the whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is one thousand six hundred and eighty pence, being ten marks six shillings and eightpence, on ten days at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, and the whole of what went out of it is one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight pence, being eleven marks seven shillings and fourpence, in eleven payments on ten days at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a payment, and that one hundred and ninety-six plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight is twenty-eight; and that the said leaf was read back in full at about ten past five in the evening of the said day on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head, in the open, by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, and that both definitions of the four figures were stated on the face of that reading; and that the said leaf holds one hundred and eighty-three entries in its first column at the close of the said day, being one hundred and seventy-three at the close of the second day of October past and one entry against each of the ten days since with no day in two of them, and one hundred and eighty-two of them at the hour of that reading; and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare twice in these ten days, being the seventy-fourth time on Monday the fourth day of October and the seventy-fifth time on Monday the eleventh day of October, and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth on those two days and on the other eight as well, and nobody put it back; and that a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line was asked for that day by name at about ten in the morning on the said day and was paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and on no other day of these ten, and was not thanked; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason three people said one thing each about that mole head at about ten past five in the evening is in the three of their own mouths and is that a thing said once in the open in October is a thing that can be repeated by a man who was not there, and that a man of about twenty-nine said that the water is the only thing in that harbor that has not gone up since the first of June, and that a woman of about fifty-eight said that her figure is not coming off, and that the man who keeps that yard said that a yard is a shed and a bench stone and a leaf with four columns and twenty-eight pence in a bag, and that none of those three said it twice; and that the reason a man of about thirty-four of the second lay who keeps the whole fouling ladder in that harbor in his own book and has never shown it to anybody is not on a sheet of four heads is the first standing line on that sheet, and that a man who has not asked to be represented is not represented and that is the whole of what that line is for; and that the reason no figure is set against a bellwright being paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence at four in the afternoon on the said day, or against her own figure of five shillings, being sixty pence, which she named once on the twenty-sixth day of September past and has not said again, is that a yard cannot pay a person what a person is worth and can only pay a person what a slate says, and that the hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings, a day she is under by is on the face of that leaf in a second line under her own first one and is in no other place in that yard's book;
