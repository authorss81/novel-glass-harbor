# Chapter 403

*Thursday the Twenty-Sixth of August, the Seventy-Sixth Time the Cistern Was Charged and Walked on None of It and the Top Name on That Rota Board Still Off It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In at a Berth at the Figure in Chalk on a Slate at an End Stall, and a Man of About Fifty-Three With a Bad Back Standing in Front of a Board in the Office on the West Basin Counting the First Month of a Return Line by Line With a Stub of Pencil on the Flat of His Own Hand Because a Figure in a Clerk's Hand Is a Figure in a Clerk's Hand*

Thursday the twenty-sixth of August, in that shed, at the office on the west basin, and at the end stall.

**The said Thursday was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and it was the day the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventy-sixth time, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth, at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and nothing went out of it, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of that day's figure came out of the owner of any boat or out of the man with two papers or out of anybody in that harbor.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Thursday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed at about one in the afternoon and read nothing and asked nothing. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and one days and has not been touched and has not been taken down. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and three days, the chest is shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

---

The berth work was done by a boat of about nineteen tons with two men in her and it took from about half past seven until about half past two, and Calder was not aboard and did not go near it and did not know until the slate came back at half past two that it had been done at all.

The figure in the chalk is hers and it is hers because she wrote it there on a Sunday in July with the day of the month beside it, and a figure put on a slate cannot be taken off it again, and this yard did not put it there and cannot take it off it.

---

The office on the west basin is a long low room with a counter across the front of it and a tide board on the wall behind the counter and about nine feet of wall on the other side of the door where things get nailed up.

Nailed to that nine feet of wall, inside the door and to the left of a man who has to come in sideways, is a sheet of ruled paper about two foot by three, folded once down the middle, and it is the return of recovered glass entered at that harbor since the first of April, and it is a return and not a decision, and it decides nothing, and it binds nobody, and it is not a schedule.

It has a head at the top. Then it is ruled in five blocks, one for each month, and every block has a line ruled for every day of that month from the first, and the last block is ruled for every day of August from the first to the twenty-third and stops there because the twenty-third is the last day anybody has had time to write on it. There is a column on the left with a day of the month in it and a column on the right with a figure in it, and where there is no figure on a line there is a single stroke drawn across the whole width of the line, which is that office's way of saying that a day went by and nothing came in on it.

At the foot of the sheet there is a ruled space two inches deep with a line and a half of a head above it, and the head says *carried*, and there is nothing in it.

Above each of the five blocks there is a figure in the hand of the clerk of about sixty, and the five figures are the number of lines in that block that carry a figure and not a stroke, and they are written small and up in the corner where they do not get in the way of a man coming in sideways, and they have been there since the first of April and they are the only figures on the sheet that anybody in that harbor has ever written about it.

---

The man of about fifty-three came in at about half past two on his way back from the third lay, where he works a winch that is heavier than it looks, and he has had the bad back since a night in the February before last when he got a chain out of a forward locker in a swell with a man of about twenty-six and the two of them got it out and he has not been right since and he has not been to anybody about it and there is nobody in this harbor he would go to.

He came in sideways. He read the tide board, because that is why he came in, and then he read the sheet, because it is nine feet of wall and nobody stands in front of nine feet of wall without reading it.

Then he stood there.

He had a stub of pencil in his right hand. He had had it in that hand since about ten o'clock in the morning, when he had sharpened a spike for the third lay and put the pencil behind his ear out of the way, and it was a stub about two inches long and it was blunt and it was his own and it is not the pencil that a master of about thirty-six made a mark with on a sheet in that shed in the last month, and it is not a mark of anything and it is on no page.

He put the flat of his left hand on the wall under the first block and started at the top of it and went down.

The clerk of about sixty watched him for about a minute and then went back to his own board, which is a list and is not a schedule and binds nobody in that harbor, and twenty-two years in that room and owed a quarter and with no book and no power.

The man of about fifty-three went down the first block, line by line, and his thumb stopped on every line that carried a figure and went past every line that carried a stroke, and at the bottom of the block he counted the twenty-six lines his thumb had stopped on, twice, and wrote the number on the flat of his own left hand in pencil, and then he did the same with the figures in the right-hand column, and he did that the way a man does it who has been adding up other men's figures on other men's boards for thirty years, which is out loud, in tens, under his breath, with his lips moving and nothing else moving.

Two marks. Three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten. One, one, one, one, one, one, one, one, one, one. Two, two, two, two, two, two, two, two, two, two. And so on, and then the last two, and then he said the answer to himself once, quietly, in the way a man says a thing he has not yet decided to say to anybody.

Then he stood there with his hands behind him and looked at the other four blocks for a while, and he worked out before he left the wall that the five small figures at the head of the five blocks were the number of lines in each block that carry a figure, and that if they were right he would not have to do the thumbs twice, and that he would set his own count against the small one at the head of each block as he came to it, and that he was not going to do four blocks in an afternoon at a wall with a back like his. He put the pencil behind his ear and went out sideways and did not say good afternoon to anybody in that room.

Calder came in at about ten to three and the man had gone and the sheet was on the wall and there was nothing on it that had not been on it at half past two.

---

"That is a return," he said to Nessa, in the shed, at about half past three. "It has a line for every day since the first of April and a figure on the days something came in and a stroke on the days nothing did, and there is a space at the foot of it with the word *carried* over it and nothing in it."

"It is a return," said Nessa. "It is a return and not a decision, and it decides nothing and it binds nobody, and the man who keeps that board is a clerk of about sixty who has been in that room twenty-two years and is owed a quarter of a pound and has no book and no power, and he is not going to be asked anything about it by anybody in this yard this year."

"How much work is it to take that off."

"It is five blocks and about a hundred and forty lines and a column of figures, and a man with a bad back standing up at a wall doing it twice, because the first time he will not trust his own count and the second time he will be checking the first one." She wrote something on the slate she had brought. "That is the bill. An hour and a half, maybe two the first time, at a wall, in a room he does not work in, on his own time, for nothing."

"Put it in with the hour against it."

"I will put it in the second column," she said, "because a man on a mole cannot check what a man counted at a wall, and the reason against the line is that he did it on his own account and nobody in this yard asked him to and nobody in this yard is going to pay him for it and nobody is going to thank him for it either, and if that is not a reason to keep a thing out of the first column then I do not know what is."

---

At about half past four the man of about fifty-three came past the shed with his hand over the small of his back and went by without stopping.

Calder went out after him to the corner and caught him about forty paces up the front street and said his name and the man's name, which was Peat Marrow, and which was the first time either of them had said the other's name.

"I am not going to ask you what you were doing at that board," Calder said.

"No," said Peat Marrow.

"I am going to say one thing and then I will let you go up the street. Whatever you counted there, if you ever finish it, you can say it to me on a day and I will write it down and I will not put your name against it. And I am not going to pay you for it and I am not going to thank you for it, and if you want paying for it you want the wrong yard, and the wrong yard's man has two papers on one table and he will want a paper out of you and you have not got one."

Peat Marrow stood in the street with his hand still over his back.

"I counted April," he said. "That is all I have counted. Twenty-six days out of that month had a figure on them and I have the twenty-six and I have the sum of them and I have got both of them on this hand and I have not washed it since two o'clock." He put his left hand up and showed the palm, and there were two figures in pencil on it and a thumb across the middle of them. "I am going to do May on Friday. I am doing it because a number in a clerk's hand is a number in a clerk's hand and I have been in that room enough times to know what a clerk does with a figure he has not counted, and I am not going to stand in there twice more being told a number."

"Friday," Calder said.

"Friday," said Peat Marrow, and went up the street with his hand over his back and did not look back and did not say anything else.

---

**Nothing went out of that yard on the said Thursday and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into it, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and there are three hundred and twenty-eight pence in the bag, being two marks and eightpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and twelve pence, being one mark twelve shillings and eightpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is going to write one word under it saying anybody is going to pay it. The cistern was charged for the seventy-sixth time and walked on by none of it and the top name on the rota board is still off it. A sheet of ruled paper about two foot by three is nailed to nine feet of wall inside the door of the office on the west basin and it is a return and not a decision, and it has a line ruled for every day since the first of April, a figure in the right-hand column on the days a thing came in and a single stroke across the line on the days nothing did, and a space at the foot of it with the word *carried* over it and nothing in that space. A man of about fifty-three of that harbor with a bad back stood in front of that wall on his own time and counted the first month of it line by line with a stub of pencil on the flat of his own hand, and said nothing to anybody in that room, and went out sideways, and named himself to the holder of that writ in the front street at about half past four in the afternoon and said he would do the second month on Friday. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-sixth day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Thursday was the day the cistern was charged for the seventy-sixth time at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about half past two in the afternoon, being a day's work with a boat and about nineteen tons in her and two men in her, at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall which is the woman's own figure and was put there by her on a Sunday in July with the day of the month beside it and cannot be taken off it again; and that the holder was not aboard that day and did not go near it; and that a sheet of ruled paper about two foot by three is nailed to a wall inside the door of the office on the west basin and is the return of recovered glass entered at that harbor since the first of April, and that it is ruled in five blocks, one for each month, with a line for every day of the month from the first, and the last block stops at the twenty-third of August; and that a line carries a figure in the right-hand column on a day a thing came in and a single stroke across the whole width of the line on a day nothing did; and that a man of about fifty-three of that harbor with a bad back came into that office at about half past two in the afternoon on his way back from the third lay, read the tide board, read the sheet, stood at it, and counted the first block line by line with a stub of pencil in his right hand and the flat of his own left, and that he counted the twenty-six lines in that block that carry a figure twice over and the figures in the right-hand column of those twenty-six lines out loud in tens under his breath; and that five small figures in the hand of the clerk of about sixty stand at the head of the five blocks and are the number of lines in each block that carry a figure, and that the man of about fifty-three worked that out standing at that wall on the first afternoon, and that he said he would set his own count against the small figure at the head of each block as he came to it, and that he did that on the four days after it, and that all five of the clerk's small figures were right when he came to them, and that he said none of that in that room; and that the foot of that return has a space two inches deep ruled for a total with the word *carried* over it and nothing in it; and that the man of about fifty-three gave his name to the holder of that writ in the front street at about half past four in the afternoon, being Peat Marrow, and said that he had counted April only, and that he had the count and the sum of it on the flat of his own hand, and that he would do the second month on Friday, and that the holder said nothing but the name of the day.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason against the counting of those five blocks is that a man on a mole cannot check what a man counted at a wall in a room he does not work in, and that the ship's hand put the time of it in, being an hour and a half and maybe two the first time, at a wall, on his own time, for nothing; and that a man counting a figure that a clerk of about sixty has written in the corner of a block is a man with a stub of pencil at a wall doing a sum in his own hand, and this yard did not send him and does not pay him and has no figure against him, and the stub of pencil is his own and is on no page; and that a return is a return and not a decision and the word *carried* over an empty space at the foot of one is a space and not a figure and is not a shortfall and is not anybody's debt; and that the man of about fifty-three is not to be paid for it and is not to be thanked for it and his name is not to go against it, and the reason is that the day this yard writes a man's name down against a number is the day this yard has a list, and a list is the boat and everybody not on it is not asked and everybody on it has a figure against him; and that a figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall is the woman's own figure and is not this yard's and was not put there by this yard and cannot be taken off it; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August is not entered here again and stands, and the whole of it stands unpaid and unmoved and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on the said day.*
