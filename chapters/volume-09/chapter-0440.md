# Chapter 440

*Saturday the Second Day of October, a Leaf Read Back in Full on the Flat of the Bench Stone at That Mole Head at About Ten Past Five in the Evening in the Open in the Ship's Own Left Hand All Four Columns Including the Empty One With Both Definitions of the Four Figures on the Face of That Reading, One Hundred and Seventy-Two and Seven Hundred and Thirty-Seven and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five at the Hour of That Reading and One Hundred and Seventy-Three and Seven Hundred and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five at the Close of the Day, a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Paid in Coin on the Flat of the Bench Stone at About Four in the Afternoon at the Figure in Chalk on a Slate at an End Stall, a Room over a Joiner's Shop Let Out for the Fifth Time With a Bowl on the Step at Two o'Clock, and a Harbor With No Flour in That Market and No Oil in It Since the Twenty-Ninth Day of August and a Light Out of a Mole Head and a Figure on a Board That Did Not Come Down*

Saturday the second day of October, on that mole head, in Fish Street, in that shed, at the head of the third lay, and on that bank four hundred yards down that foul.

**The said Saturday was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and no charge was placed on that cistern on it, and it is the tenth day of ten. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon to a caulker of about twenty-three at that same figure, and there are one hundred and ninety-six pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is four hundred and forty-four pence, being two marks ten shillings and fourpence, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is one thousand and eight pence, being six marks four shillings, on six days, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day at the figure in chalk, and the whole of what went out of it is one thousand three hundred and forty-four pence, being eight marks five shillings and fourpence, in eight payments on seven days, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence a payment, and five hundred and thirty-two plus one thousand and eight minus one thousand three hundred and forty-four is one hundred and ninety-six.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the tenth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why and they have not spoken across that shed in ten days. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on that mole head from about five in the afternoon. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he sat on the eleventh piece for about six hours and at about half past three he got up off it and went home, which he has done three times in sixty days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and thirty-eight days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a twelfth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and seventy-eight days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-fourth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in sixty-two days, counted from the hour they were first seen on the evening of the first of August past. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the seventeenth day and the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-second day and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-nine days.**

---

At about ten past five in the evening on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head, in the open, in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty one, Nessa Pike read that leaf back and the holder of that writ stood on the other side of it and did not touch it.

The mole-head light is not lit and its lens has been out since the seventh of May past.

"The first column of that leaf has one hundred and seventy-two entries in it at this hour and one hundred and seventy-three at the close of the day," she said. "There were one hundred and forty-three of them at the close of Thursday the second day of September past and thirty have gone in since, one against each of the thirty days from Friday the third to this day, and not one day in two of them, and this evening's own is the thirtieth of the thirty and makes one hundred and seventy-three at the close. A day of the first column carries one entry against it whatever else that day printed, so that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count it."

"The second column has seven hundred and thirty-seven lines in it at this hour and seven hundred and thirty-nine at the close of the day," she said. "Five hundred and eighty-nine of them were in it at the close of the second of September past and five have gone in on each of the thirty days since, three before the hour of that day's reading and two after it, and three of this day's went in before this reading and two have gone in in this afternoon after it. Six hundred and eighty-nine plus fifty is seven hundred and thirty-nine at the close, and six hundred and eighty-seven plus fifty is seven hundred and thirty-seven at this hour. **That figure is that book of that yard's own and it is not a count of anything anybody can check from a mole. The first column's one hundred and seventy-two and the fourth column's one hundred and twenty-five at this hour are a count of entries and a count of lines, and both of them can be taken with a pencil. Those are the two definitions and they are printed here so that a later writer with a pencil knows which of them they are counting and at which of the two moments.**"

"The third column is empty and nobody has been appointed to it, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is at the head of it with a name beside it that is not that boy's and is not the holder's, and that name was said out loud once in a boat on the thirteenth day of May past and has not been said since. He was not on the stone on any of these ten days and was put on nothing, and nobody in that crew has said one word to anybody about the three nots standing against the eighth of June past, and there is a twelfth week in which that has not been said, and no day of any week before the first of July was entered against that line and none is going to be."

"And the fourth column has one hundred and twenty-five lines in it at this hour and one hundred and twenty-five at the close," she said, "and it has taken no line in these ten days and there is no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. That is not a closure and it is not a strike-out and nobody has closed that column and nobody in this yard is going to. The reason it has taken no line was entered in the second column at about ten past nine in the evening of the twenty-eighth day of August, in that yard's own mouth, with the hour against it, and it is entered once and it is not entered again, and the reason it is in the second column and not in the fourth is that a man on a mole cannot check the second column and a man on a mole can check the fourth."

She shut it and put it under her own coat and neither of them said one word about the figure it gave.

---

Mara Venn said the one thing she said on that mole head on the tenth day, and she said it four feet off the stone with her hands at her sides and she did not look at the leaf.

"What is mine and what is anybody's is a thing you two have to write down and it is not a thing that yard's book is for," she said. "That yard's four columns and that yard's figure and that yard's method do not go on the table in Fish Street and are not going to on any wall in this harbor, and my name does not go on a sheet for any of this, and forty marks first after food is not reduced by a chain and a weight and a bellwright from off the reef line, and none of those three things has been paid and none of those three things is going to be paid in this harbor in my lifetime. That is the whole of what I have come up here to say and I am going home now."

The holder of that writ did not answer that and did not improve on it.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon Isaac Fell, a caulker of about twenty-three, of the third lay, of no boat, of no crew and of no office, on a day and a wage and an hour and on nothing else, was paid one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, in coin on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and he had been asked for that day by name at about ten in the morning on Wednesday the twenty-ninth of September past and was told the same day that he was wanted, and he was not paid before that hour on any day and is not paid on any day he is not asked for by name on, and nobody thanked him.

---

At about two o'clock in the afternoon the room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street was let out for the fifth time since the first of September.

A bowl was put on the top stone of the step at two o'clock and about seventeen people went up those stairs over the next half hour and three came down, and eleven of them could not read. Hobb Lee, of about forty-six, read the head-notes off the long table out loud from two o'clock until about half past three and wrote his own name and the hour under each in his own hand, and there was no claim in any of them and no claim on that table.

On the Sunday the twenty-sixth of September about twenty people went up those stairs and three came down, and on this day about seventeen went up and three came down, which is about thirty-seven across the two of them.

He did not read anything out loud in that room that is not a head in that book, and he was asked for nothing by anybody on the said day.

---

There is no flour in that market and there has been none since the spring and none is coming this month. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and a board at an end stall carries four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down and cannot be taken off. The sheet of six lines at the back of that stall is on the same square of wall as that slate and has been on it for the seventeenth day and nobody has taken it down and it has a charge of one hundred and sixty-eight pence at the foot of it and no claim has been lodged under it in this harbor and nobody has paid a penny of it, and it is a different paper from the nine-line sheet with a seal on it in the front street and it is on a different square of a different wall. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-second day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is on that wall for forty-nine days. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven and is not re-written. The delayed note stands at eleven logged occasions and two of them with no call in them. The mole-head light is out. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered since the ninth of November past and the three marks standing against it are on an anchor date that came in on the entry and are not going to be re-dated. The third lay fills on the first of April and one berth is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid in a list being made up in a city. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes in a chest in a front room and forty-nine weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. The cistern was charged for the eighty-fourth time on the twenty-third of September, the eighty-fifth on the twenty-seventh and the eighty-sixth on the thirtieth, and walked on by none of it on each of them, and the top name on that rota board is still off it.

**A boat of about nineteen tons named the *Alewife* lying on a bank about four hundred yards down the foul off the south end of that mole has a new piece on her keel and about eleven feet of caulked joint and is to be floated off that bank on the next tide that will take her. Seven boats and about thirty men went out on four days inside that harbor's own line and kept her in company and came home in company, and a small steam tender of about forty tons lay off that bank on all four days with fifteen fathoms of chain off her quarter round a spurle and back with a weight on the tail of it, and the weight came over the rail four times with an hour and a reason written against each of them, at about half past two on the twenty-eighth, at about a quarter to two on the twenty-ninth, at about nine minutes to three on the thirtieth and at about twenty minutes to two on the first of October. THREE THINGS THAT WORK COST ARE ON THE FACE OF IT AND NONE OF THEM IS A SUMMARY: A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-TONS OPENED ABOUT NINE FOOT OF HER GARBOARD AND PUT A HOLE IN HER LEE RAIL ABOUT THE SIZE OF TWO HANDS BY STRIKING A BAULK OF OAK THAT THAT YARD'S OWN HANDS HAD LAID DOWN WITH NOTHING ON IT, AND THAT BILL OF IRON CLAMPS AND A PLANK OF WHITE OAK FOUR INCHES BY NINE AND ELEVEN FEET LONG AND ROVES AND COPPER CAME TO THAT YARD AND IS NOT CHARGED TO THE OWNER OF ANY OF THE SEVEN BOATS, WHO WAS NOT ASKED TO PAY IT AND DID NOT OFFER; THE WHOLE OF THE MATERIAL AND WAGES OF TEN DAYS CAME OUT OF A BAG THAT STOOD AT FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO PENCE AT THE OPEN AND STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIX PENCE, BEING ONE MARK AND THREE SHILLINGS, AT THE CLOSE, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE LESS FOUR HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR PENCE, BEING TWO MARKS TEN SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND IT IS NOT ZERO ON ANY HOUR OF ANY OF THE TEN DAYS; AND FOUR PEOPLE ASKED ON FOUR OCCASIONS INSIDE THESE TEN DAYS FOR A LINE TO BE STRUCK OUT OF A RECORD AND WERE REFUSED IN THE SAME NINE WORDS EVERY TIME, THOSE NINE WORDS BEING THAT THAT LINE STAYS, THAT NOTHING COMES OUT OF THAT BOOK ONCE IT IS IN, FOR ANY REASON, TO ANYBODY, AND A LINE THAT SAYS A BELLWRIGHT OFF THE WESTERN REEF LINE WAS PAID AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE IN A HARBOR WHERE A BELLWRIGHT HAS BEEN PAID FIVE SHILLINGS, BEING SIXTY PENCE, FOR NINE YEARS, STANDS WHERE IT IS UNDER A SECOND LINE THAT SAYS SO. A woman of no office said one thing on that mole head and said that what is hers and what is anybody's has to be written down and is not that yard's book, and that the four columns and the figure and the method do not go on the table in Fish Street or on any wall in this harbor, and that her name does not go on a sheet for any of this, and that forty marks first after food is not reduced by any of it. And nothing that was taken in these ten days has been given back, and this harbor is poorer on the second day of October than it was on the twenty-third day of September and poorer than it was on the fourteenth day of May, and a man with a berth and a rope and a day's work is not better off today than he was on Thursday, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is on a wall in that front street for forty-nine days and is answered by nobody, and the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on a wall for the seventeenth day on the outside of a figure that did not come down, and there is no flour in that market, and the light is out.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the second day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said leaf ruled on the twenty-seventh day of March past was read back in full at about ten past five in the evening of the said day on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head, in the open, by the ship's hand in her own left hand, all four columns including the empty one; and that both definitions of the four figures were stated on the face of that reading; and that the said leaf holds one hundred and seventy-three entries in its first column at the close of the said day, being one hundred and forty-three at the close of the second day of September past and one entry against each of the thirty days since with no day in two of them, and one hundred and seventy-two of them at the hour of the reading; seven hundred and thirty-nine lines in its second, being five hundred and eighty-nine at the close of the second and five a day since, three before the hour of each day's reading and two after it, and seven hundred and thirty-seven at the hour of this reading; none in its third; and one hundred and twenty-five in its fourth, of which the hundred and twenty-fifth is the last thing in that book, and the column is not closed and nobody has closed it; and that of the ten days of this stretch the twenty-third of September, the twenty-seventh and the thirtieth were the only three on which the cistern was charged, being the eighty-fourth, the eighty-fifth and the eighty-sixth times, at about eight in the morning, and walked on by none of it at about half past nine on each of them, and that the twenty-fourth, twenty-fifth, twenty-sixth, twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth days of September and the first and second days of October, being seven of the ten, put no charge on it; and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare once in these ten days, being the seventy-third time, on Monday the twenty-seventh day of September, and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth on that day and on the other nine as well, and nobody put it back; and that one wage Tuesday fell in these ten days, being the forty-ninth on Tuesday the twenty-eighth, and the column did not move on it, and that forty-eight weeks stood fallen due with no envelope against any of them at the open of these ten days and forty-nine stand at the close of them; and that a sheet of six lines has been on the wall at the back of an end stall for the seventeenth day on the outside of a figure of four hundred and fifty pence that was not taken down first and is on a different square of a wall from the nine-line sheet with a seal on it, which is on that front street wall for the forty-ninth day; and that a caulker of about twenty-three was asked for the said day by name on Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of September past at about ten in the morning and was paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon of the said day at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and on no other day of these ten; and that a room over a joiner's shop at the top of Fish Street was let out on the twenty-sixth of September and on the said day, and that about twenty people went up those stairs on the first of them and about seventeen on the second and three came down on each, which is about thirty-seven across the two of them, and eleven of each of those could not read, and that there was no claim in any head read in that room in either of those Sundays and no claim on that table; and that the whole of what came into that yard in these ten days is one thousand and eight pence, being six marks four shillings, on six days at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day and the whole of what went out of it is one thousand three hundred and forty-four pence, being eight marks five shillings and fourpence, in eight payments on seven days at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a payment, and that five hundred and thirty-two plus one thousand and eight minus one thousand three hundred and forty-four is one hundred and ninety-six pence at the close of the said day, being one mark and three shillings, being short of the wage of four marks by four hundred and forty-four pence, being two marks ten shillings and fourpence; and that the seventh day lost to a thing that has no name in it stands lost and no second day was entered against that line in these ten; and that the second seam is about a foot and seven inches fouled on the weather side on the twentieth day of September last and it was not looked at on any of these ten days and the season of it is nine marks and neither has been paid.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason four requests to strike a line out of a record inside these ten days were all refused in the same nine words is in the ship's own mouth and is that a line comes out of that book when it stops being what happened and not before; and that the reason that is entered here and not in the first column is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count four requests and four refusals in the first column and cannot take the nine words out of it without reading them; and that the reason a bellwright of about thirty-four asked for her line to be struck rather than for the figure on it to be changed is in her own mouth and is that she was not underpaid on that line, she was paid the wrong figure on it, and the line is what happened; and that the reason the word for what a man does with a chain and a weight and a bell between two tides is on no sheet in this harbor is in the holder's own mouth and is that this yard cannot answer for a word, and that a man of about forty-six asked for one out loud on the thirtieth and was refused and said he would not ask again and did not; and that the reason no name is against the woman of no office is that she has not asked to be on anything and forty marks first after food stands whole against her name and is not reduced; and that the reason the reason the fourth column has taken no line is entered once on the twenty-eighth day of August past and not again is that it is already in this book and a yard that puts a reason in twice has two of them; and that the reason no figure is set against a boat of about nineteen tons on a bank, a chain, a weight, four cutoffs, four men put into a bell one at a time and three accounts of about two foot of plank that do not agree with each other is that labour is not checkable from a mole and that a figure against any of it would be a price put on about thirty men by a woman with a slate; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Saturday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on any of these ten days.*
