# Chapter 449

*Monday the Eleventh Day of October, the Seventy-Fifth Time That Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Came Down Bare and Went Up at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Nobody Put It Back, the Eighty-Ninth Time That Cistern Was Charged and the Last Charge Before the Last Day of the Ten, an Account of About Four Feet and a Man's Words Against One of Its Lines Read Out Aloud a Second Time in the Same Room at the Same Stated Hour With the Same Custodian at the Door and the Account Not Being Changed, Five Costs of Ten Days' Work Set Down One Under Another and Not One of Them a Payment and Not One of Them a Victory, a Man of About Thirty-Three Reading Six Printed Lines Aloud to Two Men Who Cannot Read on a Wall in a Front Street and Then Reading Them the Figure of Four Hundred and Fifty Pence Off a Slate Underneath, and Nobody Answering That Sheet and Nobody Knocking It Down, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Coming Into That Yard at About Ten in the Morning and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Going Out of It at About Four in the Afternoon, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence*

Monday the eleventh day of October, behind the reeve's bench, at the cistern at the back of that market, in a room on that hill, and in a front street.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning for the eighty-ninth time and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-fifth time at about eight in the morning and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon for a coil of marline, canvas and nails, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the ninth day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and a shoe that is not his was on the floor beside it. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and her name stood on a public sheet for the eighth day and on the sheet of four heads in a room over a fish market since the Sunday, and nobody took it off either and nobody thanked her for either. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he took the eleventh piece of that boat up off the stone and set it down on its other edge. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty-seven days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-nine days and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a fourteenth week, beginning on the said Sunday, and nobody in that crew has said one word about them. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-seven days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-third night. Two lights off that point have not moved in seventy-one days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-nine days and has been given nothing by anybody. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twenty-sixth day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-first day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-eighth day.**

---

The account was read out a second time at about a quarter past two in the afternoon of the said day, in the same room on the first floor of that office on that hill, with the same woman of about forty-four in the chair at the door and her list on the board and the same hour written on it.

There were about twenty-four people in that room and about eleven of them could not read, and Hobb Lee read it from the top again, and when he came to the fifth line he read the words that a man of about forty-four had put under it on the Friday evening, and he read them with his own name and the hour against them, the same as the rest.

Nobody stopped him for about an hour.

At about half past three a woman of about thirty-one of that harbor stood up and said that she had been in a room in that town in the summer of the year before last and had been asked to hold a sheet of paper flat on a table while a man copied off it, and that she was not going to say anything else about it in that room, and that she was saying it because that account is about a piece and because a man of about forty-four had put his words against a line of it on Friday, and a person who is not going to say anything else should at least be the kind of person who turns up.

Nobody asked her a question and nobody asked her to say it again and the reader wrote her name and the hour on the back of the sheet, and the sheet went back on the desk at the door on its way out.

The account was not changed. It says at the head of it that the person who entered it does not get to strike a line out of it, and four words have been added under the fifth line since the seventh of October and the head has not been touched, and it is on a desk at a door in a building on that hill and anybody in that harbor can go and take it up and count them.

---

There were five things that came out of the ten days, and none of them is a payment of anything that was owing before the ten days began, and none of them is a victory, and they are set down here one under another the way Nessa Pike read them off a slate at about five in the afternoon and not the way they would read on a wall.

**One.** The copy and the original are both in the building on that hill. The original is on a shelf and has not moved. The piece of worked lead cut on the fifth of October is in a drawer in the same building under a drawer of its own. Neither came to this harbor. Neither went anywhere. **Two.** The account was read out at a stated hour on the seventh and again at a stated hour on the eleventh, in a room on the premises of the office that holds the original, with a custodian of that office in a chair at the door and a list of names and hours on a board, and about eleven people in that room on both days could not read and heard the whole of it both times. **Three.** A claim against the copy is in a book at a counter off the west basin, entered on the seventh of October, not granted, not refused, not withdrawn, and carried at the same size as the four heads on a sheet of about three feet in a room over a fish market, and that sheet does not discharge it. **Four.** A woman of no office has her name on a public sheet and on that sheet of four heads, and the account says at the head of it that nobody is put on it who has not asked to be on it, and she did not ask to be on either of them and is on both of them, and nobody took her name off either, and nobody thanked her, and nobody has offered to take it off, and forty marks first after food stands whole against her. **Five.** A diver went down once on the eighth of October under a tender of about twenty-three and fifteen fathoms of chain and a weight on the tail of it, with the reason written down before he went down and the reason written down after the weight came over the rail, and he was in the water about thirty-five minutes, and he came up with a cold in his teeth and a taste of iron in his mouth like a nail, and he can put a name to one afternoon in June and cannot put a name to what was in the boat with him, and that afternoon is in the second column of that leaf in the yard's own mouth with the reason against the line and no name against it.

Beside those five, and not part of them and not smaller than them, a ropemaker of about sixty-one of the third lay is not in that room and does not get that room back at any page of anything, and no one has been made whole, and no one has been charged with anything, and a man of about fifty stood in six inches of water on the fourth of October holding a baulk of oak for about an hour and ten minutes and has not been thanked for it and did not ask, and a man of about thirty-eight whose berth was held on the eighteenth of September has not been given that berth back by anything that happened in these ten days, and a man of about thirty whose claim window is off cannot read the paper it is on, and the word on that paper was put into his own mouth once at a counter off the west basin in September and off is a different word to held, and he has not had it read to him since and nobody in that harbor is going to.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon a man of about thirty-three stood in a front street in front of a wall at the back of an end stall with two men beside him who cannot read, and he read the six printed lines on that sheet out loud to them, twice, and then one of the two asked him what the number under it was, and he turned round and read the figure off a slate on the same square of wall, and it is four hundred and fifty pence, being thirty-seven shillings and sixpence, quoted on the first of June, with February's three hundred and seventy-eight pence, being thirty-one shillings and sixpence, standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and the figure of the first of June was not taken down first and cannot be taken off.

The three of them stood there for about a quarter of an hour. The man of about thirty-three said that he could not say it was a lie and he did not say it was not, and one of the two men said that he had a brother in a boat that went for pieces in April and did not say anything else, and the other one said nothing at all, and they did not take the sheet off that wall and nobody in that street knocked it down and nobody wrote anything on it and nobody in that harbor has answered it in twenty-six days, and the charge at the foot of it of one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, has not been paid by anybody and no claim has been lodged under it.

**A CLAIM AGAINST A COPY IS IN A BOOK AT A COUNTER OFF THE WEST BASIN AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN. A SHEET OF ABOUT FOUR FEET THAT A STRANGER WITH A PENCIL CAN CHECK IS ON A DESK AT A DOOR IN A BUILDING ON THAT HILL WITH FOUR WORDS ADDED UNDER ONE OF ITS LINES AND NOTHING STRUCK OUT. A SHEET OF ABOUT THREE FEET WITH FOUR HEADS ON IT AND NO JURISDICTION ON IT IS ON A WALL IN A ROOM OVER A FISH MARKET AND CARRIES THAT CLAIM AND DOES NOT DISCHARGE IT. A FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND THE SIX-LINE SHEET IS ON THE OUTSIDE OF IT FOR THE TWENTY-SIXTH DAY, AND THE NINE-LINE SHEET WITH A SEAL ON IT IS ON A DIFFERENT SQUARE OF A DIFFERENT WALL IN THAT SAME FRONT STREET FOR THE FIFTY-EIGHTH DAY AND IS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND NEITHER OF THOSE TWO PAPERS HAS BEEN TOUCHED BY ANYBODY IN THESE TEN DAYS. A HARBOR IS POORER ON THE ELEVENTH DAY OF OCTOBER THAN IT WAS ON THE THIRD, AND NOTHING IN THAT STATEMENT IS A FIGURE OF ANYTHING AND NOBODY IN THAT HARBOR IS GOING TO ARGUE IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the eleventh day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning for the eighty-ninth time and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it, and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-fifth time at about eight in the morning and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that the three days of these ten on which a charge was placed on that cistern are the fourth, the seventh and the eleventh days of October, being the eighty-seventh, the eighty-eighth and the eighty-ninth times, and that the third, the fifth, the sixth, the eighth, the ninth, the tenth and the twelfth days of October are the seven of the ten on which no charge was placed on it and none may be placed; and that an account of about four feet was read out a second time at about a quarter past two in the afternoon on the said day in a room on the first floor of that office on that hill, with a custodian of that office in a chair at the door, and that the four words a man of about forty-four had put under the fifth of that account on the Friday were read out with a name and an hour, and that that account was not changed and its head was not touched; and that a woman of about thirty-one stood up in that room at about half past three and said she had been asked to hold a sheet of paper flat on a table in a room in that town in the summer before last and was not going to say anything else about it there, and that nobody asked her a question and nobody asked her to say it again; and that a man of about thirty-three read the six printed lines on a sheet at the back of an end stall in that front street aloud twice to two men who cannot read at about half past four in the afternoon and then read them the figure of four hundred and fifty pence off the slate on the same square of wall, and that nobody answered that sheet and nobody knocked it down and nobody wrote on it; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall for one hundred and forty-seven days, the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-nine days, the sheet of six lines is on that wall for the twenty-sixth day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the forty-first day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-eighth day; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty-seven days old, the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and fifty-third night, and two lights off that point have not moved in seventy-one days.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason five things are set down one under another on the said day is in that yard's own mouth and is that a set of five that reads as a victory is a set of five that has been arranged, and that this yard arranges them in the order they happened and reads them out of a slate at about five in the afternoon and does not put them on a wall; and that the reason a woman of no office is on a public sheet and on a sheet of four heads and did not ask to be on either is in her own mouth in that shed on the third day of October and is that a person who asks to be taken off a piece of paper is a person who has been on it, and that nobody in that harbor has thanked her and nobody has offered to take her name off; and that the reason a diver's account of the twenty-ninth of June is in this column and not in the first is that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil can count that a man was in the water on the eighth of October and cannot count what a man can put a name to about an afternoon in June, and that a figure in the first column against that would be a lie in a book that has never had one in it; and that the reason a woman of about thirty-one was not asked a question in that room on the said day is that she said she was not going to say anything else about it there and that a person who has said the half of a thing in a room of twenty-four is not to be asked for the other half by anybody in that harbor; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
