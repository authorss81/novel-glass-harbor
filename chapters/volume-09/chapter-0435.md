# Chapter 435

*Monday the Twenty-Seventh Day of September, the Seventy-Third Time the Wall Behind the Reeve's Bench Came Down Bare and Went Up at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Nobody Put It Back, the Eighty-Fifth Time the Cistern Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It at About Half Past Nine, a Rota Written on a Sheet of Good Paper From a Counter in a Room off That Market at a Penny Out of the Holder's Own Hand and Not Out of That Bag, a Young Man of About Twenty-Three off a Small Steam Tender of About Forty Tons Who Is Not a Cruiser and Not an Officer and Whose Own Hours Run From About Seven Until About Five and Whose Own Rate Is His Tender's and Not This Yard's, Fifteen Fathoms of Chain Off His Quarter Round a Spurle and Back With a Weight on It, and a Foot of Water on the Flood With About Two Hours of It in Every Twenty-Four*

Monday the twenty-seventh day of September, in that shed, at the head of the third lay, in that market, and on that mole head.

**The said Monday was a wipe and a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-third time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back, and the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the eighty-fifth time, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about two in the afternoon for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and there are five hundred and thirty-two pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed for about twenty minutes and read the rota off the wall and said one thing about it. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he took the eleventh piece and stood it up against the third piece and left the two standing with a gap of about nine inches between them and went home at four. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and thirty-three days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and thirty-five days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a twelfth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and seventy-three days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and thirty-ninth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in fifty-seven days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for seventy-five days. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the twelfth day and the paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the twenty-seventh day and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-four days.**

---

The sheet of good paper came from the counter in the room off that market at about nine in the morning and cost a penny and the penny came out of the holder of that writ's own coat and not out of that bag, and the woman of about fifty-nine at that counter asked him what it was for and was told that it was a list of days and was not asked one question further and copied it and did not stop.

The list of days was written in the ship's left hand and it had no figure on it against any day except one, and the one figure was the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall.

*Morrow Quay, from the twenty-eighth day of September next. One person on the water, in company, from about seven in the morning until the last of the ebb. One hundred and sixty-eight pence, out of this yard's own figures. Not out of any boat and not out of any owner of a boat. A person may refuse and will not be asked again.*

There were four days on it and the fourth was the first day of October.

Mara Venn read that off the wall of that shed where it was pinned and said one thing about it.

"That list has got a person in it," she said, "and that yard's book on that bench has four columns in it and three of them are about things and one of them is about things a stranger cannot check, and there is no column anywhere in that shed for a person who has been asked to turn up, and there is not going to be one, and I would put it in the second column with the hour against it and not in the first, because a man on a mole can count the first column and cannot count this."

Nobody thanked her for that and she was not thanked.

---

Halvard Ness came down that slip at about eleven in the morning and asked what the steam tender was wanted for, and he is twenty-three years old and is second hand on a small steam tender of about forty tons lying at the head of the third lay and has been in that harbor eleven weeks, and he is not a cruiser and does not carry anything that says so and does not call anybody sir and was not called anything by anybody.

"You want somebody to hold a line," he said. "And if you want somebody to hold a line and be told when to let it go, then you are describing the second hand of a steam tender, and that is me, and I am not being paid by you."

"You are paid by your tender."

"I am paid by my tender at eight o'clock on a Saturday and by nothing else, and you are not going to have any of my time at any figure you have, because I am not going to be able to tell whether you have been paid or not when I am sitting in a boat at four in the morning."

"Then the hours and the pay are yours and the working is ours," said Nessa Pike, "and this yard writes down what time you called the day and what time you called it off and does not pay you."

"That will do," said Halvard Ness.

He went down to his tender's quarter and brought up fifteen fathoms of chain with a swivel in it and put one end round that tender's quarter cleat and gave the other end to a man on the head of the *Alewife*'s bitt and made him round a spurle and back with a weight on the tail of it, and then he hauled it until the weight came up over the rail and it lay in coils on that tender's after deck.

"That is the whole of it," he said. "When that comes up, the working stops, and it does not matter what anybody in the boat thinks, and I will not ask anybody's leave to pull it and nobody in that boat has any standing to tell me not to pull it, and the only reason I can do that is that I am not being paid by any of them and they know it."

"Who says what time it comes up?"

"Nobody says. It comes up when I pull it, and I pull it, and if I pull it wrong that is a thing I have done and not a thing you have done, and I have done it on eleven boats in eleven weeks and I have never once been asked to wait."

---

A woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall and a slate came down to the head of that slip at about four in the afternoon with a basket of biscuits and tobacco and a coil of new manila and put it down on the third lay wall and stayed there.

Calder Marr looked at her slate for a while. It said one hundred and sixty-eight pence in chalk against a day's work with a boat and two men, and it had said that since the first of June.

"That is the same figure as the charge at the foot of the sheet on your wall," he said.

"It is the same figure," she said. "It has been the same figure since the first of June on that slate and somebody put the same figure in print on the fifteenth of September on a wall about four inches to the left of it, and I did not have anything to do with that and I am not going to be asked about it. If you are telling me that a claim costs what a day's work costs, then you are telling me something I worked out in June with a piece of chalk and a man from the harbor authority stood in front of my stall and looked at it for a minute and a half and wrote the figure of the first of June on a board above it and neither of them would take the other one off."

She turned the slate about and kept selling biscuits.

"That is two figures in this harbor that are the same figure and they are on two pieces of wood nine feet apart, and there is no more to it than that, and I would like a bag of your tobacco and I would like to be paid for it like anybody."

---

At about half past six in the evening seven boats went out from the head of that slip for a turn of the leg, not to the bank, not on the foul, and came in at about eight, and about thirty men were in them, and there was a foot of water on the flood over that bank and about two hours of it in every twenty-four and no more of it than that.

Nobody at that reeve's bench asked them for a paper and no paper went out with them and every one of them was inside that harbor's own line the whole time they were out.

---

There is no flour in that market and there has been none since the spring. There has been no barrel of lamp oil in that harbor since the twenty-ninth day of August and a figure of four hundred and fifty pence is on a board at an end stall with three hundred and seventy-eight pence standing visible above it and both dates on that board, and there has been no barley in that market either since the middle of the month. The sheet of six lines is on the wall at the back of that stall for the twelfth day on the outside of that figure. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for forty-four days and is a different paper. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on that door for the twenty-seventh day. The mole-head light is out. The north berth is out. The fire party's hose is dead until the spring. The tide gang is four men short. The hard at Ashtide is unanswered since the ninth of November past and the three marks that stand against it are on an anchor date that came in on the entry and are not going to be re-dated. One berth on the third lay is short. Nineteen winters are disputed and unpaid. The chandler's note is at thirty-one with its three counts at twenty-two, thirteen and eleven. Forty-eight weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them and the forty-ninth falls on tomorrow.

A sheet of good paper came from the counter in the room off that market at about nine in the morning and cost a penny out of the holder of that writ's own coat and not out of that bag, and four days were written on it, being the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth and thirtieth days of September and the first day of October, and the figure on it was the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall. A woman of no office read that off the wall of that shed and said that it has got a person in it and that there is no column in that yard's book for a person who has been asked to turn up and that she would put it in the second column with the hour against it and not in the first. A young man of about twenty-three who is second hand on a small steam tender of about forty tons lying at the head of the third lay brought up fifteen fathoms of chain with a swivel in it, put one end round his tender's quarter cleat, gave the other to the bitt of the boat on the bank, and had it round a spurle and back with a weight on the tail of it so that when it comes up the working stops, and said out loud that he would not ask anybody's leave to pull it and that nobody in that boat had any standing to tell him not to, and that the only reason he can do that is that he is not being paid by any of them, and that he is paid by his tender at eight o'clock on a Saturday and by nothing else and this yard is not going to have any of his time at any figure. A woman of about fifty-eight with the end stall and a slate came down to the head of that slip and put a basket of biscuits and tobacco and a coil of new manila on the third lay wall and stayed there, and was told that the figure on her slate is the same figure as the charge at the foot of the sheet on the wall of her stall, and said that it has been the same figure since the first of June and that she did not have anything to do with that sheet and was not going to be asked about it, and that two figures in that harbor are the same figure and are on two pieces of wood nine feet apart and there is no more to it than that, and asked for her tobacco to be paid for like anybody's. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at about two in the afternoon at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall and the bag stands at five hundred and thirty-two pence, being three marks four shillings and fourpence.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-seventh day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-third time on the said day and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back; and that the cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the eighty-fifth time, and the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that a sheet of good paper came from the counter in a room off that market at about nine in the morning and cost a penny out of the holder of that writ's own coat and not out of that bag, and that four days were written on it, being the twenty-eighth, twenty-ninth and thirtieth days of September and the first day of October, and that against the work on each of them stands one person on the water in company from about seven in the morning until the last of the ebb at one hundred and sixty-eight pence out of that yard's own figures and not out of any boat or any owner of a boat; and that a young man of about twenty-three who is second hand on a steam tender of about forty tons laid fifteen fathoms of chain with a swivel in it from his tender's quarter cleat to the bitt of a boat of about nineteen tons on the bank, round a spurle and back with a weight on the tail, so that the working stops when the weight comes up, and that he said in his own mouth that he would not ask anybody's leave to pull it and that nobody in that boat has any standing to tell him not to, and that his own hours are from about seven in the morning until about five in the afternoon and his own rate is his tender's and is paid at eight o'clock on a Saturday; and that a woman of about fifty-eight with an end stall and a slate came down to the head of that slip at about four in the afternoon and put a basket of biscuits and tobacco and a coil of new manila on the third lay wall; and that seven boats and about thirty men went out at about half past six in the evening for a turn of the leg and came in at about eight, and were inside that harbor's own line the whole time and no paper went out with them and nobody at that reeve's bench asked them for one; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about two in the afternoon at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and that three hundred and sixty-four plus one hundred and sixty-eight is five hundred and thirty-two pence at the close of the said day, being three marks four shillings and fourpence, and short of the wage of four marks by one hundred and eight pence, being nine shillings.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a list of four days was written on a sheet of good paper bought at a penny out of a man's own coat and not out of that bag is that a sheet of paper from that counter is a penny and is paid out of the holder's own hand, and that a penny is not a figure of anything; and that the reason the same words were used to ask eleven men one at a time and the same words are written at the head of that sheet is that this yard has two figures and no third one and that the shape of a thing has to be told to a person before they are asked for it; and that the reason a young man of twenty-three on a steam tender is the one who holds the cutoff is in his own mouth and is that he is paid by his tender and by nothing else, and that a man who is paid by nobody in the boat is the only man in the boat who can stop the working without asking anybody; and that the reason a penny's worth of good paper came out of the holder of that writ's own coat and not out of that bag is in his own mouth and is that a sheet of paper is a sheet of paper at a penny and the bag has two figures in it and neither of them is a sheet of paper; and that the reason the list of days is four days and not a standing thing is that a standing thing is a figure somebody has to be given and a list of days is a thing a man can read and come on, and that a man who has agreed to be on the water on the first of October and not on the second of October has been asked two questions and not one; and that the reason no figure is entered against the fifteen fathoms of chain or against the about thirty men in seven boats is that a chain is a length and a company is a number of men and a day, and that neither of them is checkable from a mole and that the second column is where a thing goes when a stranger with a pencil cannot take it; and that the reason the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands is that it was not paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day, and no part of it was.*
