# Chapter 408

*Tuesday the Thirty-First of August, the Forty-Fifth Wage Tuesday and the Column Not Moved on It and Forty-Five Weeks Standing Fallen Due With No Envelope Against Any of Them, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence In and One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Out on the Same Day at the Same Figure and the Bag Standing Where It Stood, a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Asked for by Name on the Saturday Before and Paid in Coin at About Four in the Afternoon on the Flat of the Bench Stone, a Man of About Fifty With Two Papers on One Table and a Fourth Box He Wants Left Empty, and a Nine-Line Sheet With a Seal on It in the Front Street Since the Fourteenth That Nobody in That Harbor Put Up and That Nobody in That Harbor Has Answered*

Tuesday the thirty-first of August, in that shed, in the office on the west basin, and on the flat of the bench stone at that mole head.

**The said Tuesday was a wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and the wall behind the reeve's bench came down and went up whole at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said day, being a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon to a caulker of about twenty-three, at that same figure, out of that yard's own money, and there are two hundred and eighty-four pence in the bag, being one mark ten shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and fifty-six pence, being two marks three shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. The wage column is at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with twenty envelopes on a bench in a chest, and the said Tuesday was the forty-fifth week, and it did not move on it, so that forty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and the forty-sixth falls on a Tuesday in September, and nobody in that harbor has any idea what this yard is going to do about the forty-sixth and that is the yard's own business. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Tuesday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was in that shed at about two in the afternoon and did not go up the hill. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and he was not sent for and not asked and not thanked, and he is not a teacher and not a door and not a sacrifice, and he is not the man on the far bench, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and six days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and eight days, the chest is shut, the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page.**

He had asked him for it on the Saturday.

"Isaac Fell. Tuesday the thirty-first. One day. A Tuesday, which is what you said you would do."

"And what is the Tuesday for."

"The same other end," Calder said. "You are three days into nine feet and I would like the whole of it done before the water gets in it again, and I am not asking you for a Wednesday and I am not asking you for a Thursday, and if the next Tuesday comes and I have not asked you for it then there is not one, and that is the arrangement and it has not changed since the tenth of August."

The caulker put his maul down on the plank he was standing on, which is the only time anybody in that yard has seen him put it down while anybody else was talking.

"You have said that before," he said.

"I have."

"You said it on the tenth and you said it on the seventeenth and you have not said it since the seventeenth, and I noticed, and I want you to know that I noticed and that it does not make me any more willing and it makes me a great deal less surprised." He picked the maul up again. "I will do the thirty-first. I am telling you now, on the Saturday, because you asked me on the Saturday, and if you had asked me on the Monday I would have done it and told you on the Tuesday, and that is the difference between the two of us and I do not expect you to be able to see it from where you are standing."

He was paid on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon on the Tuesday, in coin, out of that yard's own figures, at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, and he counted it on the stone with his own thumb and put it in his own pocket and was not thanked, and Calder Marr said nothing at all to him when he did it, which is the third time in a month and is the correct number of times.

---

The office on the west basin at about half past two in the afternoon on the said Tuesday.

The man of about fifty was at his own table with his two papers on it. The first box on the west basin paper had the twenty-seventh of July past in it in a man's own hand with his own hour against it and it has not been empty since. The fourth box on the other paper has been empty since the twenty-seventh of July past and it was empty at about half past two on the said day and it was empty at about six, and a man of about fifty wants it left empty, and nobody in that room asked him about it on that day or on any day, and this yard did not stop him and did not help him and did not ask him a single word about it.

"Two things," he said, before Calder was through the counter. "And then I am going back to my table and you can stand there or you can go."

"Go on."

"The first is that you gave this office a sheet on the seventh of July and this office keeps a register copy of what it is given. It is in. It is on the shelf behind you and it is yours and you can have it any day you like and you have not asked for it in fifty-five days, and I am telling you it is there because I would rather you knew that than have you go up that hill tonight and think it went in a fire."

"I will not ask for it."

"I know you will not," said the man of about fifty. "You have not asked for anything from this office since the seventh of July and you are the only person in this harbor who has ever given this office something and not come back for it, and I have noticed, and I am not going to say what I think about it because you would ask me what I think and I would have to refuse and we would both have had a wasted afternoon."

"The second."

"The second is that there is a sheet with a seal on the top of it in that front street that went up on the fourteenth of August, which was a Saturday, and it is still up, and I have read it four times." He put his thumb on the first of his two papers. "It proposes that every concern which takes work out of a hull and keeps no account of what it has taken be entered in one register, and that a concern not entered in that register is a concern whose accounts are not to be produced to anybody. It names no person. It names no harbor. It puts no figure on it. It has no price at the end of it. It asks nothing of anybody standing in that street."

"And you have not answered it."

"I have not answered it and I am not going to." He picked his pen up and did not look at Calder again. "Answering a thing like that takes a paper. I want you to hear that from a man in an office and not from a man in a market, because a man in a market will tell you that the answer is a word, and the answer is not a word, it is a piece of paper with a name at the foot of it, and the day I put a name at the foot of one is a day I have done something and cannot undo, and this office does not decide contested claims and neither do I, and I have been at that table for nineteen years and I have got a pension out of it and I am not going to spend that on the fourteenth of August of a year I cannot name."

He went back to his table and did not look up again.

---

At about four in the afternoon Calder Marr was on the flat of the bench stone and the caulker took his money and went down the slip, and at about half past four the woman of about fifty-eight came down the front street with her slate and did not come into that shed, and went on up the hill to her own door, and at about five a man of about thirty-one of that third lay came up the slip with his chart in a bag and sat down on the steps for about ten minutes and did not open the bag and then went back down.

At about six the office on the west basin closed and the return was still on the wall inside the door and the space at the foot of it with the word *carried* over it was still empty and the tide board was wiped and put up and the man who wiped it did not look at the return on his way past.

At about seven the market emptied and the end stall was shut and the slate was carried inside and the figure on it, being a mark and eightpence with the day of the month beside it, was still on it.

**One hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard on the said Tuesday and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it, at the same figure, on the same day, and there are two hundred and eighty-four pence in the bag, being one mark ten shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and fifty-six pence, being two marks three shillings, and the said Tuesday was the forty-fifth wage Tuesday and the column did not move on it, so that forty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them. A caulker of about twenty-three was asked for that Tuesday by name on the Saturday before, which is what he said he would do, and was paid in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon and was not thanked, and the holder of that writ said nothing to him at all, which is the third time in a month. A man of about fifty with two papers on one table told the holder of that writ that the register copy of what this yard gave that office on the seventh of July is on a shelf behind that counter and is his to have any day and that he has not asked for it in fifty-five days, and told him that a nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street since the fourteenth of August naming no person and no harbor and putting no figure on it, and that he has read it four times and has not answered it and is not going to, and that the answer to a thing like that is a piece of paper with a name at the foot of it and not a word said in a market. The fourth box on the other paper was empty at the end of that day as it has been since the twenty-seventh of July, and nobody in that room asked him about it. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the thirty-first day of August next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Tuesday was the forty-fifth wage Tuesday and the column did not move on it, and that forty-five weeks stand fallen due with no envelope against any of them, and that the forty-sixth falls on a Tuesday in September; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about half past two in the afternoon and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it in coin at about four in the afternoon, at the same figure, on the same day, out of that yard's own money in both directions, and that the bag stood where it stood on the Monday before; and that a caulker of about twenty-three was asked for that Tuesday by name at about ten in the morning on the Saturday before, and that the holder said that he was not asking for a Wednesday and was not asking for a Thursday and that the next Tuesday that comes without an asking is a Tuesday with nothing on it; and that the caulker put his maul down on the plank while that was being said, which is the only time anybody in that yard has seen him do it with another man talking; and that the caulker said that the arrangement had not changed since the tenth of August and that he would do the thirty-first because he was asked on the Saturday; and that he was paid on the said day on the said stone at one hundred and sixty-eight pence in coin and counted it with his own thumb and was not thanked, and that the holder of that writ said nothing to him at the time, being the third time in a month; and that a man of about fifty at his own table with two papers on it told the holder of that writ that this office keeps a register copy of what it is given, that the sheet this yard gave on the seventh of July is on a shelf behind that counter, that it is his to have any day, and that he has not asked for it in fifty-five days, and that he is not going to be asked for it; and that the same man said that a nine-line sheet with a seal on the top of it went up in that front street on the fourteenth of August, being a Saturday, and is still up, and that he has read it four times, and that it proposes that every concern which takes work out of a hull and keeps no account of what it has taken be entered in one register and that a concern not entered in that register is a concern whose accounts are not to be produced to anybody, and that it names no person and names no harbor and puts no figure on it and has no price at the end of it; and that the said man said he has not answered it and is not going to, and that answering a thing like that is a piece of paper with a name at the foot of it and not a word, and that this office decides no contested claim and neither does he.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a caulker who is asked for a day on the day before and paid at an hour on a stone has a day and a wage and an hour and on nothing else, and that the day a yard writes down who turns up that yard has a list, and a list is the boat, and everybody not on it is not asked and everybody on it has a figure against him; and that a man of about fifty who wants a fourth box left empty has not been asked about it by anybody in this yard and has not been helped and has not been stopped, and that this yard is not going to ask him and is not going to put anything in it and is not going to keep anything out of it, and the reason is that it is his table and not this yard's; and that a man who has read a nine-line sheet four times and has not answered it is a man who has done the counting and not the answering, and that not answering it is not agreeing with it and is not disagreeing with it either, and both of those are true and neither of them settles anything; and that a register copy of a thing given to a municipal office on the seventh of July is a real object and is a thing that office has kept and is not a claim and is not a debt and is not anybody's property until somebody asks for it; and that the reason against the fourth column entered on the twenty-eighth of August is entered once and is not entered again; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth day of August is not entered here again and stands, and the whole of it stands unpaid and unmoved and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on the said day.*
