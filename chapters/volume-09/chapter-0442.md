# Chapter 442

*Monday the Fourth Day of October, the Seventy-Fourth Time That Wall Behind a Reeve's Bench Came Down Bare and Went Up at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Nobody Put It Back, the Eighty-Seventh Time That Cistern Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It at About Half Past Nine With the Top Name on That Rota Board Still Off It, a Boat of About Nineteen Tons Off a Bank About Four Hundred Yards Down That Foul at About Half Past Two in the Afternoon With Eleven Feet of Caulked Joint in Her That Held, a Chain Weight Over a Rail at About Ten Past Three With an Hour and a Reason Written Against It, a Man of About Fifty Standing in Six Inches of Water on the End of a Baulk of Oak That the Same Hands of That Same Yard Laid Down a Week Before With Nothing on It, One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Going Out at About Nine in the Morning and Coming Back at About Ten and Going Out Again at About Four in the Afternoon, and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, Against a Wage of Six Hundred and Forty*

Monday the fourth day of October, behind the reeve's bench, at the cistern at the back of that market, at the head of the third lay, and on that bank about four hundred yards down that foul.

**The said Monday was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning for the eighty-seventh time and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-fourth time at about eight in the morning and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about nine in the morning for a pump leather, a coil of marline, canvas and nails, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into it at about ten in the morning for a day's work with a boat and two men at a berth at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it again at about four in the afternoon for iron straps, a length of three-inch stuff and a coil of new line, and there are twenty-eight pence in the bag at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. Nothing has been in a tin in that shed since the twenty-eighth day of April past, and not one penny of the whole of it comes out of the owner of any boat, out of the man with two papers on a table in an office off the west basin, or out of the Crown.**

**And the far bench was occupied from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon for the second day of ten and was not approached, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, and on the said day he sat on the eleventh piece with his back to the water and got up at four and went home, which he has done three times in sixty days. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and forty days. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-two days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and a bellwright of about thirty-four off the western reef line is in that harbor and is not asked one question about him and does not ask him one. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a thirteenth week, beginning on the said Sunday, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty days old. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-sixth night. Two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-four days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for eighty-two days and has been given nothing by anybody, and nobody in that harbor sent for him. The sheet of six lines at the back of that end stall is on that wall for the nineteenth day and the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-fourth day and has been answered by nobody. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-first day.**

---

The wall came down bare at about eight in the morning and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and the man who laid the last course of it on the Friday put the same course on the Monday and did not put it back the way it was, and has not done since the twelfth of August and is not going to.

The cistern was charged at about eight and a half of it went over the stone lip and the man who charged it wrote the date on the board in chalk and then looked at the top of that board for about as long as it takes to write a date, and the top name on that board has been off it since before the middle of the month, and it was off it at eight in the morning and it was off it at half past nine when he went back and put his boot on the stone and the water went over his boot.

Nobody said anything about that and nobody has said anything about that at any hour of any day, and the number of people who have walked on that cistern since it was put at the back of that market is a number that office does not keep and that this yard does not keep either.

---

The *Alewife* came off that bank at about half past two in the afternoon.

She came off the way a thing comes off that has been lying in nine foot with a hole in her for five days, which is that she came off all at once and about nine inches at a time and set up a hole in the water about four feet across, and seven boats and about thirty men were round her, and the small steam tender of about forty tons was lying off her with fifteen fathoms of chain off her quarter, and Halvard Ness had the chain taut and had his hand on the weight.

She came off on her own third attempt, on the ebb, with about two feet of water over her garboard where the new piece is and about seven foot and a half over the bank she had been lying on an hour before, and she came off upright, and her new piece of keel went into the water first and about four inches of it was under before the rest of her followed, and about eleven feet of caulked joint went under together and held.

Halvard Ness brought the chain over the rail at about ten past three with the weight coming up after it and the reason written down in the ship's left hand at about half past four in the afternoon with the hour against it, being that the joint had been wet twice and dry once and there was nothing more to hold her for and that if anybody wanted her held he could come out and say so on the record.

Nobody came out.

They took her up to the head of the third lay on the flood and put her alongside the stone between about four and six boats, and a caulker of about twenty-three came out and looked at about eleven feet of joint that had been in nine foot of water for five days and said that it was the only caulking he had ever seen that had been asked to do that and that he was not going to be paid for looking at it, and nobody paid him for looking at it.

Nine men went unstepped and got that mast in on her in about forty minutes and nobody was paid for that either and nobody was paid for any part of the day.

Jos Lund, of about forty-four, master of that boat of about nineteen tons, stood in his own boat at about five in the afternoon with the water at his knees and did not say anything for a while, and then he said the thing that everybody in that harbor was already saying about a boat that has been in nine foot of water for five days, which is that she had got off and that nobody had got her wetter and that this yard's new piece on her keel was still on her and about eleven feet of a caulker's work was still in her and that both of those things were still true at six o'clock on a Monday in October and would be true on Wednesday.

---

At about half past eight in the morning two of that yard's hands laid a baulk of oak down across the same run of hard water that the baulk of the twenty-eighth day of September had been laid across, and this time a man of about fifty stood in six inches of water at the end of it holding the top of it with both hands and did not let go of it until the tide had it and did not let go of it after that either.

Ham Birt, of about fifty, master of a boat of about twenty-two tons of the second lay, was the man whose boat opened about nine foot of garboard and put a hole in her lee rail about the size of two hands by striking the other baulk on the twenty-eighth, and he had said out loud in that shed on the Friday before last that the baulk was that yard's and that the baulk was that yard's to mark and that nobody marked it, and that he was going to hold that baulk next time, and the undertaking was written down with the hour against it, and the yard did not thank him for saying it and did not thank him for doing it.

He stood in six inches of water for about an hour and ten minutes holding a baulk of oak that is not his boat's baulk and is not his yard's baulk and that had nothing to do with his own hull, and about thirty men from seven boats went past him and about twenty of them said his name and about ten of them did not.

Nobody thanked him at six o'clock or at seven or at eight, and he did not ask, and at about ten past eight he took his hands off it and went home, and the baulk stayed where it was and was still there at dark and was still there on the Tuesday and was still there on the twelfth day of October.

**A BAULK OF OAK ABOUT SIX FEET LONG WITH NOTHING ON IT WAS LAID ACROSS A RUN OF HARD WATER ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH DAY OF SEPTEMBER AND OPENED ABOUT NINE FOOT OF THE GARBOARD OF A BOAT OF ABOUT TWENTY-TWO TONS AND PUT A HOLE IN HER LEE RAIL ABOUT THE SIZE OF TWO HANDS, AND THE BILL OF IRON CLAMPS AND A PLANK OF WHITE OAK FOUR INCHES BY NINE AND ELEVEN FEET LONG AND ROVES AND COPPER CAME TO THAT YARD AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING ONE MARK AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT WAS NOT CHARGED TO THE OWNER OF ANY OF THE SEVEN BOATS, WHO WAS NOT ASKED TO PAY IT AND DID NOT OFFER, AND ON THE FOURTH DAY OF OCTOBER A BAULK OF OAK WENT DOWN ON THE SAME RUN OF HARD WATER AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY HELD IT FOR ABOUT AN HOUR AND TEN MINUTES AND WAS NOT THANKED FOR IT AND DID NOT ASK, AND NEITHER OF THOSE TWO THINGS IS A FIGURE OF ANYTHING AND NEITHER OF THEM IS ON A SLATE. A BOAT OF ABOUT NINETEEN TONS IS ALONGSIDE THE STONE AT THE HEAD OF THE THIRD LAY WITH A NEW PIECE ON HER KEEL AND ABOUT ELEVEN FEET OF CAULKED JOINT IN HER, AND ABOUT THIRTY MEN AND A SMALL STEAM TENDER AND A CHAIN AND A WEIGHT AND A FOOT AND A HALF OF WATER BETWEEN THE TOP OF A TIDE AND THE BOTTOM OF ONE ARE THE WHOLE OF WHAT CAME OFF THAT BANK, AND NONE OF IT SETTLED ANYTHING, AND THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS STILL ON A BOARD AT AN END STALL AND DID NOT COME DOWN, AND THERE IS NO FLOUR IN THAT MARKET, AND A BARREL OF LAMP OIL AT FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE IS THE CHEAPEST THING THAT COULD BE BOUGHT IN THAT HARBOR BEFORE THE FIRST OF JUNE AND IT IS STILL THE CHEAPEST THING THAT CAN BE BOUGHT IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the fourth day of October next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning for the eighty-seventh time and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, and that the top name on that rota board is still off it; and that the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventy-fourth time at about eight in the morning and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard at about nine in the morning, one hundred and sixty-eight pence came into it at about ten in the morning, and one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of it again at about four in the afternoon, and that one hundred and ninety-six less one hundred and sixty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight pence at the close of the said day, being two shillings and fourpence, being short of the wage of four marks by six hundred and twelve pence, being three marks eleven shillings; and that a boat of about nineteen tons came off a bank about four hundred yards down that foul at about half past two in the afternoon on the said day with about eleven feet of caulked joint in her that had been wet twice, and that about thirty men and seven boats and a small steam tender of about forty tons were round her, and that the chain weight came over that rail at about ten past three in the afternoon with a reason written against it and the hour against the reason, and that a caulker of about twenty-three came out and looked at that joint and was not paid for looking at it, and that nine men got a mast in on her in about forty minutes and were not paid for that either; and that a baulk of oak about six feet long went down across the same run of hard water at about half past eight in the morning with a man of about fifty holding the end of it in six inches of water, and that he stood there about an hour and ten minutes and was not thanked and did not ask, and that baulk was still on that run of hard water at dark on the said day and on the last day of the ten; and that the notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall for one hundred and forty days, the bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and forty-two days, the sheet of six lines is on that wall for the nineteenth day, the paper of four lines at the top left of that door in Fish Street is on that door for the thirty-fourth day, and the nine-line sheet with a seal on it is in that front street for the fifty-first day; and that the two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and eighty days old, the bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and forty-sixth night, and two lights off that point have not moved in sixty-four days.*
*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a joint of oakum and tar has to be wet twice and dry once between before a boat will come off a bank is in a woman of about twenty-seven of that yard's own mouth and is that the second soak takes the hardness out of it and the dry puts it back, and that the reason that is entered here and not in the first column is that a man on a mole cannot check what a joint feels like and that a stranger with a leaf and a pencil is owed the dates and not the reasons; and that the reason the reason for a chain weight coming over a rail is written down before the boat comes in and not after is that a record which improves a man's account of his own hands is not a record, and that the reason it was written at about half past four in the afternoon for a chain that came over at about ten past three is that the two of them are in the same afternoon and the hour of the first of them is the hour it happened and the hour of the second of them is the hour it was written; and that the reason a man of about fifty stood in six inches of water holding the end of a baulk that was not his for about an hour and ten minutes is in his own mouth and is that the baulk was that yard's on the twenty-eighth and nobody marked it and that if it is that yard's to lay down then it is that yard's to be held, and that he was not thanked for saying so on the Friday and is not thanked for doing it on the Monday, and that a man does not want to be thanked for holding a thing that was put in his water; and that the reason no figure is set against a baulk of oak, or against about an hour and ten minutes of a man's legs, or against nine men and forty minutes of a mast, or against a caulker looking at a joint he was not asked to look at, is that none of it is checkable from a mole and that a figure against any of it would be a price on about thirty men; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
