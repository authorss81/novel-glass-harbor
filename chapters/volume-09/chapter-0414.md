# Chapter 414

*Monday the Sixth Day of September, the Seventieth Time the Wall Behind the Reeve's Bench Came Down Bare and Went Up Whole at the Angle It Has Been At Since the Twelfth and Nobody Put It Back, the Seventy-Ninth Time the Cistern Was Charged at About Eight in the Morning and Walked on by None of It at About Half Past Nine, a Caulker of About Twenty-Three Asked for This Day by Name on the Friday Before and Paid in Coin on the Flat of the Bench Stone at About Four in the Afternoon at One Hundred and Sixty-Eight Pence Out of That Yard's Own Figures, a Panel of Worked Glass About Eleven Inches by Eight on Shelf Four in a Store on the Second Lay With a Scene in It and a Note of Four Lines in Another Man's Hand Pinned to the Lid of the Box It Came In, and a Man of About Fifty-Five Called Ned Vaile Who Says in His Own Mouth What Happened in a Room on the Eighteenth Day of June*

Monday the sixth day of September, in that shed, on the second lay, and on that mole head.

**The said Monday was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe, being the seventieth time the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare, and it went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back, and it was a rota day and the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventy-ninth time, and the top name on the rota board is still off it. One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard on the said day, being a caulker's day in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon, being one mark and eightpence, and nothing came into it, and there are two hundred and eighty-four pence in the bag, being one mark ten shillings and fourpence, and the figure that bag is short of the wage of four marks is three hundred and fifty-six pence, being two marks three shillings, and it is not going to be paid and nobody is to write one word under it saying anybody is going to. A caulker of about twenty-three had been asked for that day by name at about ten past five on the Friday before, out loud, for the other end of a garboard on the second lay that has been open since June and that nobody wanted, and he had said yes on the Friday, which is the only way that arrangement works and always has been.**

**And the far bench was occupied on the said Monday from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon and was not approached on it, and the holder of that writ did not go over to him, and nothing was put on that bench and read as a gift, and neither brother has said why. Mara Venn was in that harbor and going nowhere, of no office, not on the crew, not restored, not forgiven, forty marks first after food, neither reduced nor paid, and she was on the third lay at about eleven in the morning and came down that slip at about two. Tobias Wren was on the crown of that mole from ten until four, being sixty-one, with eleven pieces of a boat behind him and a boat's hook in his right hand, and on the said day he got the eighth piece down off the parapet and set it on the stones flat and worked at the scarf of it with the hook for about an hour and then left the hook on the stones and did the rest of it with his thumb. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its wall in a different street for one hundred and twelve days and has not been touched. The bellwright of that harbor has not been asked for one hundred and fourteen days, the chest is shut, and the eight marks and a day of a boat have not been argued, and no digit of anything he ever said is on a page. The three nots standing against the eighth of June past are a tenth week in which nobody in that crew has said one word about them, and the hand of a boy of twenty-two is still at the head of the third column with a name beside it that has not been said out loud since the thirteenth of May past, and that name is not on any page of this harbor. The two objects of nobody's on that crown are four hundred and fifty-two days old and neither has moved. The bench under that pump is bare for the two hundred and eighteenth night. Two lights at about a mile off that point have not moved in thirty-six days. A man of about fifty-one has said nothing to anybody about a page of six names for fifty-four days. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it has been in that front street for twenty-three days and has been answered by nobody. The paper at the top left of that door in Fish Street is six days old.**

---

The wall came down bare at about ten past seven in the morning and a man of about sixty with a barrow of sand and stone put it back at about eleven at the angle it has been at since the twelfth, and he did it the same way he has done it sixty-nine times before, which is to say that the bottom course goes down a hand's width to the left of where it was and the coping is lifted and dropped and nobody looks at it and nobody says anything.

"Shed," said Nessa Pike, coming in behind him with the slate. "That wall has been down bare seventy times and there is not one figure in this harbor for what it costs to put a wall up twice a week."

"There is a figure and it is not ours," said Calder, "and the man on the barrow is paid out of a berth fund at a figure in chalk somewhere, and he knows it, and it is not the figure in chalk on the end stall's slate, and both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other, and we are not going to be the ones who put a figure on that wall."

---

A man of about thirty-four called Simeon Gale has the keys to two stores on the second lay and took Calder Marr up a stair that is outside the building and down a passage with a bucket in it to a room about nine feet by eleven feet with a window that does not shut.

Shelf four is on the far side, at about the height of a man's chest, and there are nine things on it and six of them are panels, and the fourth from the left is a panel of worked glass about eleven inches by eight in a shallow box with a lid.

"There is a note on the lid," said Simeon Gale. "It has been on that lid since the fourth of June, which is the day it came in, and it is not mine and it is not the harbour's and it is not anybody's, and I have not read it and I am not going to."

The note is four lines in a man's hand that has been folded twice and refolded many times, and it is signed at the foot with a name and not an office.

*Sound at the join. The join will take a mend and it will take a backing. Landed the fourth of June at the third lay out of a hull with her bottom opened and the panel out whole. A. Pell.*

That is all of it. Four lines, and the fourth of them is a man's name.

Calder Marr did not take the panel out of the box. He stood in front of it and looked at it for about as long as a man looks at a thing when he has been told there is nothing to be done about it, and what was in the panel was a room, and the room was a room with a door in it, and there was a figure standing a little way inside the door with its back to whoever was looking at it, and the figure was about four inches high and it was not a photograph of a person and it was not a drawing of a person, it was a person, it had a shoulder and a hip and a hand, and the hand was up at the height of its own chest with the fingers not shut, and there was light coming in from the left in a way that glass does when a thing is put into it under a weight and held.

"That is a person in that," said Simeon Gale, from the doorway.

"Yes."

"I have had the key to this store for six years and I have opened that box about once a year to look for a box under it, and I have never said anything about it, and I am saying it now because you are the first person has come and asked me for shelf four and I would rather said it in a week than said it in a year."

"Nobody is going to be charged for anything in that box," said Calder Marr. "Not you and not the man who marked it in. It came out of a hull on the fourth of June, and it is on a public shelf in a store in this harbor, and it is going to stay on that shelf, and nobody is going to be told they have to move it, and nobody is going to be told what it is for, and the man who put it in a box and pinned a note on the lid will not be asked about it, and he did not have to pin the note on the lid, and the note has been on the lid for eighty days and it is going to stay on the lid."

"Will you put it in that book."

"It is in that book," said Calder Marr. "It is the twenty-second entry and I read it out on the Friday from the first page to the last. What it is: a panel of worked glass about eleven inches by eight out of a hull. Where it came from: the fourth of June, landed at the third lay, with a note on it in a man's hand. Who stands where it is: the store on the second lay, shelf four, and no keeper is paid for that store. And then the fourth line ends in a dash."

He put his hand flat on the lid.

"And I have been in this harbor nine months and I have looked in about nine hundred places where something is kept, and I can stand here and tell you that a person is in that panel, and I have got a book on a table over a joiner's shop that says in four lines what every one of those nine hundred places does not show, and it does not say this one. And that is not a lie in it. Nobody has written a false thing about that panel. There are three lines on that page and one of them is true and one of them is true and one of them ends in a dash, and a dash is not a lie, a dash is a hole, and I have not got anybody in this harbor yet to put a name to the hand that made it and I am not going to go looking for one, and I want you to understand exactly what I have just told you and exactly what I have not, and I have not told you who put that person in that panel, and I am not going to, and I am not going to ask anybody."

---

Ned Vaile is about fifty-five and marks at the fish quay, and he has marked there for twenty-six years, and he has kept the marks of that volume since the twentieth of April past, and nobody pays him for it and he has never asked.

He is not a conservator. He has a bad chest and he is about as long in the hand as a man of fifty-five is and he is not the only man of his age in that harbor who could have been asked to do it and three of the others said no before he said yes.

"You are asking me about the twenty-second," he said. "I know it is the twenty-second because I entered it on the eighteenth of June and I have entered two since and neither of them is a piece of glass."

"Tell me about the eighteenth of June."

"There were four men in that room and two of them were men who had come off boats and one of them was the man who carries the glass." He did not look up. "I had a page in front of me with the head ruled on it and four lines ruled under the head, and I had the note, and I copied the note into the first line and I copied where it came from into the second and I put the shelf into the third, because the shelf was where the man who carries the glass said it was going, and the fourth line is what the thing does not show."

"Was anything said about the fourth line."

"I asked for it," said Ned Vaile. "I asked one man, in that room, in front of the other three, in words, what that panel is not fit for, and he said he did not know, and none of the other three said anything, and there was about a quarter of a minute in that room and then the man who carries the glass said we were holding him up. And I put a dash."

"Whose word was the dash."

"It was mine. I put a dash because I had nothing to put under it and I was not going to write a thing down that nobody had said to me, because that book has thirty-one heads in it and every one of them has a name against it or a day against it, and I am not going to be the man in that book who wrote down a thing about glass that nobody said to him. So I put a dash, and a dash is true, and a dash is a hole, and I have known that it was a hole since the eighteenth of June and I have not come back to it in eighty days, and you are asking me now for the first time and I am telling you."

"Why did you not come back to it."

"Because I have written the fourth line on page after page since the twentieth of April and I have never once gone down that volume with a pencil and counted what was in the fourth line of each head," said Ned Vaile. "I copy what I am given. That is the whole of what I do and I have been doing it since the twentieth of April and I have never gone down a volume and counted anything, and if you want to know the truth about that head then go and count it yourself, and I am not going to tell you what you will find because I have not looked, and a man who has not looked does not get to tell a man who has looked what is there."

Calder Marr sat down on the end of a bench in a shed at the quay and did not say anything for about a minute.

"You did not ask twice."

"I asked once," said Ned Vaile, "and I asked it in a room with three men in it, which is the worst place in the world to ask a question and the best place in the world to get an answer to, and I got nothing, and I went away, and I have not been asked to go back, and I have not offered to go back, and there is a difference between those two things and it is about eighty days wide."

---

The caulker was paid at about four in the afternoon on the flat of the bench stone, in coin, at one hundred and sixty-eight pence, being one mark and eightpence, out of that yard's own figures, and he counted it with his own thumb and put it away, and the holder of that writ said nothing to him at all, which is now the fourth time in a month, and Isaac Fell said, on his own account and to nobody:

"You have not asked me since the thirty-first."

"No."

"I told you I would do it if you asked on the Friday and tell you on the Tuesday, and you asked on the Friday and I am telling you on the Tuesday, and I am not going to ask you for anything, and you are not going to thank me, and I have got four days of my own account in the second week of September that nobody is going to ask me for either, and that is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement since the tenth of August."

**One hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard on the said Monday in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon to a caulker of about twenty-three at the figure in chalk on a slate at an end stall, being one mark and eightpence, and nothing came into it, and the bag is one mark ten shillings and fourpence and did not move up on that day, and it is short of that wage by two marks three shillings. The wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare for the seventieth time and went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth and nobody put it back. The cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventy-ninth time, and the top name on that rota board is still off it. The twenty-second head of a public volume was entered on the eighteenth day of June past, being a panel of worked glass about eleven inches by eight out of a hull landed at the third lay on the fourth day of June, standing on shelf four in a store on the second lay where no keeper is paid, and it carries three lines under the head and the third of the three ends in a dash, and the head of that volume says four lines go under every head. A man of about fifty-five who marks at the fish quay and keeps the marks of that volume and is not paid for it entered that head in a room with four men in it on the eighteenth of June past, copied a note of four lines off the lid of the box, asked one man in front of the other three what that panel was not fit for, got nothing, and put a dash, and said out loud on the said Monday that he has never gone down that volume with a pencil and counted anything and that a man who has not looked does not get to tell a man who has looked what is there. A person is in that panel, about four inches high, with a shoulder and a hip and a hand, standing a little way inside a door. Nobody is to be charged for that panel. The far bench was occupied from ten until four and was not approached, and neither brother went near the other at any hour of the said day.**

*First column, entered on Monday the sixth day of September next at about half past nine in the evening in that shed: that the said Monday was the seventieth time the wall behind the reeve's bench came down bare and that it went up at the angle it has been at since the twelfth; and that the cistern was charged at about eight in the morning and walked on by none of it at about half past nine, being the seventy-ninth time, and that the top name on the rota board is still off it; and that one hundred and sixty-eight pence went out of that yard in coin on the flat of the bench stone at about four in the afternoon to a caulker of about twenty-three for the other end of a garboard on the second lay that has been open since June, and that he was asked for that day by name at about ten past five on the Friday before and said yes on the Friday; and that the twenty-second head of a public volume was entered on the eighteenth day of June past and carries three lines under the head where the head of that volume says four, and that the third of those three lines ends in a dash; and that on the said Monday a panel of worked glass about eleven inches by eight was looked at in a store on the second lay on shelf four by a man who has the keys to two stores, standing up, without being taken out of the box, and that a note of four lines in a man's hand, signed with a name and not an office, has been pinned to the lid of that box since the fourth day of June last, being that the glass is sound at the join, that the join will take a mend and it will take a backing, and that it was landed at the third lay out of a hull with her bottom opened and the panel out whole; and that a person about four inches high with a shoulder and a hip and a hand, standing a little way inside a door in that panel, was seen by three people on the said Monday; and that the head of that volume says that the fourth line under every head is what the thing does not show, and that the twenty-second head does not show that a person is in it; and that a man of about fifty-five who marks at the fish quay and has kept the marks of that volume since the twentieth of April past, and who is not a conservator, entered that head on the eighteenth day of June past in a room with four men in it, of whom two had come off boats, and that he copied the first three lines off that note and put the shelf in against the third line because the man who carries the glass said where it was going, and that he asked one of those four men, in front of the other three, what that panel was not fit for, and was told he did not know, and that none of the other three said anything, and that he put a dash, and that he said out loud on the said Monday that a dash is true and a dash is a hole and that he has known it was a hole since the eighteenth of June past and has not come back to it in eighty days; and that the same man said out loud on the said Monday that he has written the fourth line on page after page since the twentieth of April and has never once gone down that volume with a pencil and counted what was in it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about half past nine in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the twenty-second head of that volume carries three lines where the head of that volume says four is that the man who entered it asked one question in a room on the eighteenth of June past and was given nothing, and that the reason he did not ask a second time is in his own mouth and is that he asked it in front of three other men and there was about a quarter of a minute and then a man said they were holding him up; and that the difference between a dash and a lie is that a dash is true, and that a hole on the face of a page is a hole and not a falsehood, and that the finding of a hole is not the same thing as the naming of a hand; and that a man of about thirty-four who has the keys to two stores on the second lay has had a key to that store for six years and has opened that box about once a year and has never said anything about the panel in it, and said on the said Monday that he would rather have said it in a week than in a year; and that on the said day nobody in that harbor laid a charge against that panel and nobody in that harbor asked the man who marked it in a question about it, and that he did not have to pin that note on the lid, and that the note is going to stay on the lid; and that the second column of that yard's own leaf has taken no figure out of that volume and none is going to be taken out of it, because a stranger with a pencil cannot check a thing in a room that is let out on a Sunday; and that the standing list entered in full on the fourteenth of August past is not entered in full on the said Monday and stands, and no part of it was paid, reduced, advanced or improved on that day.*
