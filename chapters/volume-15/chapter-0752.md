# Chapter 752

*Thursday the Eleventh Day of August, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Fourth Charge on That Cistern, Nine Route Delegates Sitting in That Shed From About Ten Until About Two and Rejecting One Authority Over Nine Places in Twenty-One Words Counted on the Face of a Page by a Stranger With a Pencil and Not a Figure of Money, Six Things Set Down on the Face of the Same Page and No Fifth Thing Added to That Wall, the Foot of That Money Sheet Stepping From Tomorrow to One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Two Pence Out and One Hundred and Eleven Thousand Two Hundred and Forty-Two Pence Short, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Thursday the eleventh day of August, with that market open from about nine and a grey morning with the wind in the north-east and a short sea under it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventy-fourth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Nine delegates of nine routes sat in that shed from about ten until about two and put on the face of a page twenty-one words refusing one authority over the other eight, and the twenty-one words were counted by a stranger with a pencil and the count is not a figure of money. No line went onto that money sheet and no figure went into its foot. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short today, and from tomorrow it stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand eight hundred and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand two hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine clocks of things not being done went on in that yard this morning and this is where they stood at noon. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and fiftieth day. A bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and fifty-two days without anybody asking him anything, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and ninetieth day of themselves. That bench beneath that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-sixth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventy-fourth days and no figure is set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-ninth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and forty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet carrying a seal in that front street has stood three hundred and sixty-first day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and ninety-two days of telling nobody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Nine delegates came into that shed at about ten and each of them came from a different place, and about four of that nine put a hand on that bell rope in their own harbor on their own bell on their own day in the last month and about four of that nine came down that coast road and about one of that nine had not left that harbor in six weeks.

They came on the strength of their own communities' own pages and this yard sent for none of them and paid for the carriage of none of them, and the keeper wrote the hour they came and the hour they went and wrote no reason against either hour.

---

At about half past ten a working man of about forty-four, who keeps a page in a room off the west basin and who had walked down that road on the Monday, laid a single sheet on the dry end of that bench stone in about nine people and did not read it and stood back from it.

He said he had brought a page and that it carried twenty-one words on it and that he had counted them twice and that he was not going to read them and would leave it to whoever wanted them, and that a man who reads a thing out loud here has made it a thing of that shed and he had not come to do that.

---

The woman of thirty of no office read it out then, at about eleven, once, in the yard's own mouth, and about four of that nine could read it and about five of that nine could not.

The twenty-one words are these. No one authority stands over nine routes. Each route is kept by its own people. Nine keep a thing, not one.

Nobody counted them out loud while she read them and nobody counted them afterward in a room, and the keeper wrote the twenty-one on the top corner of that sheet in his own hand with the hour against it.

---

A stranger counted them at about half past eleven. He is a chandler of about fifty-eight who was in that shed to buy a fathom of line and who said he counted anything written down and that he had counted that sheet twice and that it was twenty-one words and that he did not care what they meant.

That count is a count of words and is not a figure of money, and it went on the top corner of that sheet with the chandler's own two figures beside it, and the chandler's name is on no page of this harbor.

---

At about one the six things went on the face of the same sheet under the twenty-one words and were read out once by the keeper and not by the man who brought it.

They are that the keeping of a route turns and passes from one community to another and that no turn may be given to anybody who has not asked for it; that a record of a route is kept in the place the route is; that a bell, a buoy and a line are mended by all nine and by nobody standing over them; that where a thing came from is written where a stranger can read it; that a thing said in error is corrected in the same place and at the same size and the error is left showing under it; and that one person of a community may take that community off a route and that nobody may ask that person why.

---

Nobody put a fifth head on that wall. Those four are the four that wall has carried since the ninth and the twelfth of July, being claims, public correction, refusals and restricted records, and the refusals are on it at the same size as the contributions, and nothing was put up in that market today about a warning or a rescue or a dive or a claim or a seal or a refusal or a document or a victory.

---

The keeper named the four heads on that wall and the fitting-out sheet out loud and apart at about two, in that shed, and said they were two things and not one and that a claim may not be counted with a contribution.

The six claims that went up on the boards at the back of that market on the fifth of August are still up and are not in that shed and were not read out again today, and the central record was not in that shed and was not touched.

---

At about three the keeper wrote the hours at the door of that shed and the keeper wrote that this assembly decided no contested claim and bound nobody who had not signed and could not send anybody anywhere, and that the words on that sheet were the whole of what nine people agreed and not a beginning.

The man of about thirty-two said out loud at about half past three, in about nine people, that he was not going to say those twenty-one words were right and was not going to say they were wrong, and that he had put his hand to nothing in that shed today because he had been asked to put his hand to nothing.

---

Nobody in that shed said that anything had been settled by a page. The woman of thirty of no office said out loud that her book carries the twenty-one words and the six things and the hour and that it carries them as what nine people agreed and not as what nine people are now able to do to anybody, and the man of about thirty-two said that was the difference he had been asking about since June and she said she knew it and that it was not going to be settled by her saying so twice.

---

The ordinary work of that Thursday went on and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine and the bottom of a boat of about six tons washed down with a bucket from ten, and the fourth lay tied twice in that short sea. The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench stood empty from ten until about two and was then occupied by a man who wanted a dry seat and nothing put on it.

---

Nobody was thanked for the twenty-one words and nobody was thanked for a count a stranger made with a pencil, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**NINE DELEGATES REFUSED ONE AUTHORITY OVER THE OTHER EIGHT IN TWENTY-ONE WORDS ON THE FACE OF A PAGE, AND THE COUNT WAS MADE BY A STRANGER WITH A PENCIL AND IS A COUNT OF WORDS AND NOT A FIGURE OF MONEY. SIX THINGS WENT UNDER THOSE WORDS AND WERE READ OUT ONCE. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL ARE CLAIMS, PUBLIC CORRECTION, REFUSALS AND RESTRICTED RECORDS, ALL FOUR OF THEM ALREADY ON IT, ALL FOUR OF THEM EXERCISED, THE REFUSALS AT THE SAME SIZE AS THE CONTRIBUTIONS, AND NO FIFTH THING WAS ADDED. THIS ASSEMBLY DECIDED NO CONTESTED CLAIM AND BOUND NOBODY WHO HAD NOT SIGNED AND COULD NOT SEND ANYBODY ANYWHERE. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND FROM TOMORROW AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-TWO PENCE OUT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten the reason the man who brought that page did not read it is that a thing read out loud in a room is a thing that room has made public, and he brought a page and would not have it become a thing of that shed, and a man who would not read his own page out loud in nine people is not a man who is hiding it; that at about half past eleven the reason a count of those words was made by a stranger and not by anybody in that shed is that a count made by one of the party is a count the party has agreed to, and a page nine people agreed to is the thing this coast has been refusing since the twenty-first of June; that at about one the reason the six things went under the twenty-one words and not beside them in a second sheet is that six things on a sheet of their own are six claims, and six claims put up beside each other on one coast is what the four heads on that wall were made to stop; and that at about half past three the reason the man of about thirty-two said he would not say the words right and would not say them wrong is that a man who says a thing is right has begun to keep it, and a man who says it wrong has begun to argue it, and both of those are the middle of a record nine places keep and not the edge of one.*
