# Chapter 703

*Thursday the Twenty-Third Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Sixtieth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand Between About Eight and About Eleven and Walked on by None of It, a Hand-Bill of Eight Clauses Come Ashore in a Boat's Boots at About Ten and Read Out Once at About Eleven in Front of About Twenty People of Whom About Eleven Cannot Read It, Its First Clause About the Nine Lines and Its Fourth About One Controlled Mechanism Both True of the Sea and Said Wrong by Nobody, Line Three on That Money Sheet With the Day the Work Was Done Against It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand and Fifty-Four*

Thursday the twenty-third day of June, with that market open from about nine and the lane behind it damp from the morning cart, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixtieth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. A hand-bill of eight clauses came ashore in a boat's boots at about ten in the morning and was read out once at about eleven on the boards at the back of that market in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it. Nothing went onto the money sheet before four in the afternoon and line three went on it with the day the work was done against it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and first day. Four hundred and three days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and forty-first day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and seventh night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-fifth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and eightieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-fifth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and twelfth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and forty-three days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and one days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixtieth time, and the men who carry it set the covers on at about eleven and left them until about four. The water stood clear in the buckets and the stones at the bottom stayed covered, and about four people who came for water before noon were told to come back after four and came back. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the hour of the covering and the hour of the lifting on the page of that week, and he wrote the number of the charge against the day in the same hand as every other charge.

The paper came off the boat before the cistern men had finished. She was a small boat out of the west basin with two men aboard, and one of them stepped onto the boards with wet boots and a sheet folded inside the left boot. He drew it out on the boards at the back of that market and laid it flat with his palm, and about four people saw the seal at the foot of it before anybody read a word. The sheet is one page and eight clauses, with no figure on it and no address on it, and the seal is at the foot and pressed into wax.

---

The man of about thirty-one of the second lay was asked to read it, and he did not want to and did it. He stands on that step every Tuesday and reads a column in four minutes, and about eleven of the twenty in front of him cannot read a notice. He read slowly and he read each clause whole before going on to the next, and about four people in the twenty asked him to hold the page still while they looked at the seal.

The first clause says the pressure in the water off this coast since the ninth of June is coming from the nine independent lines and not from the old ground. The second says the lines are being held at separate hours by separate hands and cannot be checked from any one mole. The third says no single harbor can say when all nine are in contact. The fourth says a single controlled mechanism is the only arrangement known to hold a coast-wide pressure down, and that it can be put in place in about a fortnight. The fifth says each community will be asked to name the hours its own bell is rung. The sixth says boats that carry testimony will be entered at the port where they land. The seventh says every community which stops carrying a line before the fourteenth of July will be recorded in the next return as having withdrawn from a rescue arrangement. The eighth says the return will be read in that front street and a copy kept at the office on the west basin.

About four people in that twenty asked whether it was true and about four said it was, and the man who read it said he did not know and could not check it and had read it because he was asked. Nobody in that twenty said it was wrong, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote that down in the second column with the hour against it, being that no voice in that twenty said the sheet was wrong. The yard did not write that anybody said it was right either, because about four saying so among twenty is not a finding of twenty.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two stood on those boards with the sheet still lying flat under a stone. He said the fourth clause was true of the sea as far as this harbor knows, and that the first clause was true of the sea as well, and that a thing being true of the sea is not the same as a thing being ordered by anybody. He said this yard would not be answering the sheet, because the sheet asks for nothing and a yard that answers a thing that asks for nothing is making a promise nobody asked it to make. About four people heard the whole of that and about four heard part of it, and nobody argued with him on those boards.

Line three went onto the money sheet at about four with the day the work was done against it. The work was ninety yards of line at three pence the yard, being two hundred and seventy pence, and two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence, being thirty-six pence, and a watch-glass and an independent line for the tender at sixty pence, being sixty pence, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, being four times two times sixteen, which is one hundred and twenty-eight pence. Those four are two hundred and seventy and thirty-six and sixty and one hundred and twenty-eight, and they are four hundred and ninety-four pence, being three marks, one shilling and twopence. The line carries the twenty-third of June as the day the work was done and the twenty-fifth as the day it is settled, and both days are on the face of it.

---

Eight clauses came ashore in a boat's boots and at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and eight words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been on those boards at about eleven in the morning.

"**That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven this morning for the hundred and sixtieth time, and the covers went on at about eleven and will come off at about four has come, and nobody walked on the water after it. A sheet came ashore in a boat's boots at about ten and it is eight clauses on one page with a seal at the foot of it and no figure and no address, and it was read out once at about eleven in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it, by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay who reads a column every Tuesday and did not want to and did it.**

"**The first clause says the pressure since the ninth of June is coming from the nine independent lines and not from the old ground, and the fourth says a single controlled mechanism is the only arrangement known to hold a coast-wide pressure down and can be put in place in about a fortnight, and both of those are true of the sea as far as this harbor can check. About four in that twenty asked whether it was true and about four said it was, and the man who read it said he did not know and could not check it and read it anyway. Nobody in that twenty said it was wrong, and I have written that no voice said so, with the hour against it.**

"**Line three went onto that sheet this afternoon with the day the work was done against it, being ninety yards at three pence the yard for two hundred and seventy, and two seats at eighteen pence for thirty-six, and a glass and a line for sixty, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day for one hundred and twenty-eight, and those four are four hundred and ninety-four pence, being three marks, one shilling and twopence. The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out until tomorrow, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and sixteen days without one.**

"**And the last of it. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster and the plaster has not been whitewashed. The page of hands is not this yard's page this month and stands where it stood. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said one word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and one days today and nothing on that sheet touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a reading, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had five lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A HAND-BILL OF EIGHT CLAUSES CAME ASHORE IN A BOAT'S BOOTS AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING AND WAS READ OUT ONCE AT ABOUT ELEVEN ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET IN FRONT OF ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE OF WHOM ABOUT ELEVEN CANNOT READ IT, BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE OF THE SECOND LAY, AND ITS FIRST CLAUSE ABOUT THE NINE LINES AND ITS FOURTH ABOUT ONE CONTROLLED MECHANISM ARE BOTH TRUE OF THE SEA AND NOBODY IN THAT TWENTY SAID EITHER WAS WRONG. THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND SIXTIETH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. LINE THREE WENT ONTO THAT MONEY SHEET WITH THE DAY THE WORK WAS DONE AGAINST IT, BEING NINETY YARDS AT THREE PENCE THE YARD FOR TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE AND TWO SEATS AT EIGHTEEN PENCE FOR THIRTY-SIX PENCE AND A GLASS AND A LINE FOR SIXTY PENCE AND FOUR MEN AT TWO DAYS AT SIXTEEN PENCE A MAN-DAY FOR ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR PENCE, BEING THREE MARKS, ONE SHILLING AND TWOPENCE. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT UNTIL THE SETTLEMENT DAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-third day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixtieth charge, and that the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon; and that a hand-bill of eight clauses came ashore in a boat's boots at about ten in the morning, being one page with a seal at the foot and no figure and no address, and that it was read out once at about eleven on the boards at the back of that market by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read it; and that the first clause says the pressure since the ninth of June is coming from the nine independent lines and the fourth says a single controlled mechanism is the only arrangement known to hold it down and can be put in place in about a fortnight, and that about four in that twenty asked whether it was true and about four said it was, and that nobody in that twenty said it was wrong; and that line three went onto that money sheet with the day the work was done against it, being ninety yards at three pence the yard and two seats at eighteen pence and a glass and a line for sixty pence and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, being four hundred and ninety-four pence, being three marks, one shilling and twopence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason a man who did not want to read was asked to read is that a column read every Tuesday in four minutes is a voice about four people in that twenty trust with figures, and a stranger reading would have been heard as a claimant; that at about eleven the reason the yard wrote down that nobody said the sheet was wrong is that a sheet nobody argues with in twenty people is a sheet this coast will hear about for a fortnight, and a yard that writes down only what it liked about the hour would be making its own return; that at about two in the afternoon the reason the man of about thirty-two said two clauses were true of the sea is that a warning that is right about the water is not answered by calling it false, and this party has no answer that is not nine times given by nine communities; and that at about four in the afternoon the reason the line carries two days on its face is that the work day and the settlement day on this run of days are always two days apart, so that a stranger can see which day a figure arrived on.*
