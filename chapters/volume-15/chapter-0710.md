# Chapter 710

*Thursday the Thirtieth Day of June, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Sixty-Second Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, a Crown Boat With a Belt on a New Shackle Lying on the Inner Side of That Head Since About Ten in the Morning, an Account With No Boat's Name on It Brought by a Woman of About Twenty-Four, This Yard Not Going and Not Sending Anybody and Writing That It Did Not Go, Line Four on That Sheet With the Day the Work Was Done Against It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Forty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand and Eighty-Nine*

Thursday the thirtieth day of June, with a clear morning and a Crown boat lying where she was not lying last month, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-second charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. A Crown boat lies on the inner side of that head with a belt on a new shackle since about ten in the morning, seen by about four people at ten and about four more at two. This yard did not go out and did not send anybody and did not ask another boat to go and look. Line four went onto that money sheet this afternoon with the day the work was done against it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and eighth day. Four hundred and ten days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and forty-eighth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fourteenth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and thirty-second days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and eighty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and nineteenth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and fifty days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and eight days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixty-second time and the covers went on and came off at the same hours. The men who carry it worked steady and did not stop to look at the water, and about four people who came for water after four were served without waiting. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the charge number and the hours on the page of that week, and he wrote that the rota days of this movement are now two behind and one to come no more, because the thirtieth is the last rota day of the twelve.

The second mooring is on the inner side of that head and about four people saw the belt go on at about ten in the morning and about four more saw her lying there at about two. She is a Crown boat with a belt on a mooring that is not the mooring she took last month, and the shackle under the belt is bright and new. About nine people can see her from the mole, and the yard's own inclination is to go out and look, and the yard did not go and did not send anybody.

---

A woman of about twenty-four of the third lay brought the account in at about three and she would not give the name of the boat and she was not asked for it twice. What she did say is that the belt was new and the shackle under it was bright, and that the boat lay still without a guard boat alongside. She stood on the boards with her hands in her apron and she answered two questions and no more, and the man who keeps the leaf wrote what she said with the hour against it and did not write what she did not say.

The man of about thirty-two read her account and did not go out and did not send anybody and did not ask the boat of about fourteen tons to go and look, and the last of those is in the second column with the hour against it. He said out loud that a page that says this yard did not go is the first such page in nine runs of days, and that it should have been written long ago. About four people in that nine agreed and about four said nothing, and nobody said the yard should have gone.

---

Line four went onto that money sheet this afternoon with the day the work was done against it, and it is settled into the total on Friday, because on this run of days the work day and the settlement day are two days apart. The line is three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being three times one hundred and sixty-eight, which is five hundred and four pence, and coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, being sixty pence, and rope, forty yards, at three pence the yard, being forty times three, which is one hundred and twenty pence, and tin and sheet copper for the pump at seventy-two pence, being seventy-two pence, and four men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day, being four times three times sixteen, which is one hundred and ninety-two pence. Those five are five hundred and four and sixty and one hundred and twenty and seventy-two and one hundred and ninety-two, and they are nine hundred and forty-eight pence, being five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence. The multiplication is printed at every one of the five parts.

The turn of the day is a page that carries what this yard did not do. It writes on its own page that it did not go, which is the first time in nine runs of days that a page has carried a thing this yard did not do. A yard that only writes what it did will be taken to have done everything it did not write, and on a coast with a Crown boat on a new shackle that is a dangerous way to keep a page.

---

And at about half past five on that Thursday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about nine hundred words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had seen the belt from the mole at about two.

"**That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixty-second time and the covers went on and came off. A Crown boat lies on the inner side of that head with a belt on a new shackle since about ten, seen by about four at ten and about four more at two. A woman of about twenty-four brought the account with no boat's name on it and was not asked for it twice, and what she said is the belt was new and the shackle bright.**

"**This yard did not go and did not send anybody and did not ask another boat to go and look, and it writes on its own page that it did not go, which is the first time in nine runs of days that a page has carried a thing this yard did not do.**

"**Line four went onto that sheet this afternoon with the day the work was done against it, being three days of a boat at one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four, and coal and oil for sixty, and forty yards at three pence for one hundred and twenty, and tin and copper for seventy-two, and four men at three days at sixteen pence for one hundred and ninety-two, and those five are nine hundred and forty-eight pence, being five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence. It is settled tomorrow.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and thirteen thousand two hundred and ten pence out until tomorrow, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is one hundred and two thousand five hundred and seventy pence short. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and twenty-three days without one.**

"**And the last of it. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and eight days today and nothing on that mooring touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for watching, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twelve lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A CROWN BOAT HAS TAKEN UP A SECOND MOORING ON THE INNER SIDE OF THAT HEAD WITH A BELT ON A NEW SHACKLE SINCE ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING, AND THIS YARD DID NOT GO AND DID NOT SEND ANYBODY AND DID NOT ASK ANOTHER BOAT TO LOOK, AND WRITES ON ITS OWN PAGE THAT IT DID NOT GO. LINE FOUR WENT ONTO THAT SHEET WITH THE DAY THE WORK WAS DONE AGAINST IT, BEING THREE DAYS OF A BOAT AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHT FOR FIVE HUNDRED AND FOUR PENCE AND COAL AND OIL FOR SIXTY PENCE AND FORTY YARDS AT THREE PENCE FOR ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY PENCE AND TIN AND COPPER FOR SEVENTY-TWO PENCE AND FOUR MEN AT THREE DAYS AT SIXTEEN PENCE FOR ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO PENCE, BEING NINE HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING FIVE MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE OUT UNTIL FRIDAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the thirtieth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-second charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that a Crown boat lies on the inner side of that head with a belt on a new shackle since about ten in the morning, seen by about four at ten and about four more at two; and that a woman of about twenty-four of the third lay brought the account with no boat's name on it and was not asked for it twice, saying the belt was new and the shackle bright; and that this yard did not go and did not send anybody and did not ask another boat to go and look; and that line four went onto that sheet with the day the work was done against it, being three days of a boat at one hundred and sixty-eight pence and coal and oil for sixty pence and forty yards at three pence and tin and copper for seventy-two pence and four men at three days at sixteen pence, being nine hundred and forty-eight pence, being five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason four seeing a belt is entered as two pairs is that a mooring taken up is a thing seen by a harbor in parts, and a page that writes nine saw it would be claiming a muster that never stood; that at about three the reason no boat's name is entered is that a name not given is not this yard's to find, and asking twice for a name would make this yard the office that keeps names; that at about four the reason not asking another boat to look is entered last of the three nots is that it is the hardest of the three, and a yard that will not send its own hands will send a neighbour's if it does not write the line; and that at about half past five the reason a page of not going is praised is that a yard that only writes what it did will be taken to have done what it did not write.*
