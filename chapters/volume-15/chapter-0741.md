# Chapter 741

*Sunday the Thirty-First Day of July, a Wipe and the Hundred and Seventeenth Time at Thirty-Four Off and Twenty-One Bare and Thirty-Four Back Being Eighty-Nine the Whole of It, a Weather Code Rung on That Mole at About Eleven and the Same Code Rung at Eight Other Places on This Coast at the Same Hour, the Sheet at One Hundred and Eighteen Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Four Pence Out, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seventy-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Forty-Four*

Sunday the thirty-first day of July, with that market shut until nine and a hot morning lying still on the boards from ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and that wall behind that bench stood bare from about half past ten until about a quarter to twelve, being the hundred and seventeenth time at thirty-four off and twenty-one bare and thirty-four back and eighty-nine the whole of it. A weather code was rung on that mole at about eleven in the morning and the same code was rung at eight other places on this coast in the same hour, being the code that says a storm is on the coast at about six in the evening, and no one in that shed has found out who rang it at this mole. That boat was out on the second of her two hired days and came alongside at about one. The sheet stands at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and thirty-ninth day. Four hundred and forty-one days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and seventy-ninth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and forty-fifth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and sixty-third days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and eighteenth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and thirty-third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fiftieth day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and eighty-first days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-nine days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came down bare at about half past ten and the ladder went up at about a quarter to twelve, at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January, and it did not go up whole. The three figures were read off a slip before the ladder went up, being thirty-four and twenty-one and thirty-four and eighty-nine the whole of it, and the keeper read them twice because about five of that party cannot read a column.

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday went on around the bare plaster, being the pump closed up and the gaskets left to set and the bottom washed down with a bucket because the hose has been dead since the spring. About four men laid the coil of the day along the flat of that bench stone and about four sat on the far bench with nothing put on it, and nobody in that lane said why the work is getting slower.

---

At about eleven the weather was rung on that mole in the code this harbor has used since before this yard kept a page of days. The code says a storm is on the coast at about six in the evening and it is the oldest thing on that mole, and the keeper wrote the hour and the code and wrote that a code is a signal and a signal is not a document and nobody has asked it a question.

---

At about twenty past eleven a boy of that market's own came up the lane at a run and said that the same code was rung at Orison at eleven, and at about half past eleven a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a store in a town two days up that coast road came through and said the same code was rung there at eleven, and before noon two more had come in with the same hour and the same words.

---

By about one that shed had heard the same code reported from eight other places on that coast, all of them at about eleven, and the keeper wrote the eight hours on a sheet by themselves and wrote the name of no place against any hour and put the sheet on the end stall and not on the nail.

---

Then the yard went to its own instruments, because that is what a harbor has when a signal from elsewhere arrives. The sea lead was tried against the harbour side and the harbour side agreed with it, the swell on that bar was a foot and a half and no more, and the glass in the office on the west basin had not gone down a tenth since Friday.

---

Nobody in that shed said the code was wrong about the sea. A woman of about fifty-eight who had lost a roof in the blow of the spring said out loud that the glass and the water were right and the hour was not, and that a signal with a right hour in it empties a coast of boats in an afternoon, and about four agreed with her and about four said nothing.

---

What this harbor did was bring two small boats in off the boards at about twelve and stand a second watch on that mole from about one, and it sent nobody up that coast road and it asked no other place to do anything. The keeper wrote the two boats and the watch and the hour of each and wrote that this yard gave no order to any other place and was given none.

---

The boat came alongside at about one on the second of her two hired days with the pilot at the helm and the pump hand of about forty-four sitting down hard on the after thwart. He said one of the two had found ground where he meant her to go and had gone back out past the point at about four the afternoon before, and that the other had stood where it was until about ten that evening and after that was not in that water and nobody in his boat followed it.

---

He said the stones he used are not on the chart of about nine years standing and that the chart is a chart of a coast that has moved, and that in about thirty years of fishing that water he had never known it to be the way that chart has it. Nobody in that shed said the chart was wrong and nobody said the water was right, and nobody said either of those things out loud twice.

---

No person in that shed said those words to the man of about thirty-two about going down on that Sunday, and none of them said them to him at the end stall, and no hour went on the page of days for him. He went up the lane to the second watch on that mole at about one and stood two hours of it and came down at about three, and the quiet around that page settled nothing.

---

The disagreement between the two of them about what a page prints was in that shed again at about four in about nine people, and it is not settled. He said a signal with a right hour in it is the most dangerous thing a coast can be handed and that a page which prints who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing, and she said a page which prints nothing about who did not agree is a page that cannot be answered and that a signal nobody can answer is the thing that empties a coast.

---

He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and that she was still keeping the second book on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him and the matter is open and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it.

---

At about half past five on that Sunday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about nine hundred and twenty-two words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood in the lane that morning watching that wall stand bare.

"**That wall behind that bench came down bare at about half past ten this morning and stood bare until about a quarter to twelve, and it went up at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole. I read the three figures off a slip before the ladder went up, being thirty-four off and twenty-one bare and thirty-four back and eighty-nine the whole of it, and I read them twice because about five of this party cannot read a column. The pump was closed up and the gaskets left to set and the bottom washed down with a bucket.**"

"**At about eleven the weather was rung on that mole in the code this harbor has used since before this yard kept a page of days, and the code says a storm is on the coast at about six this evening. At about twenty past eleven a boy came up the lane at a run and said the same code was rung at Orison at eleven, and before noon two more came in with the same hour and the same words, and by about one that shed had heard it reported from eight other places on this coast, all of them at about eleven. I wrote the eight hours on a sheet by themselves and wrote the name of no place against any hour and put the sheet on the end stall and not on the nail. A code is a signal and a signal is not a document and nobody has asked it a question and nobody in this yard has found out who rang it at this mole.**"

"**Then we went to our own instruments, being the lead against the harbour side and the swell on the bar and the glass in the office, and the harbour side agreed with the lead and the swell was a foot and a half and the glass had not gone down a tenth since Friday. A woman of about fifty-eight who lost a roof in the blow of the spring said out loud that the glass and the water were right and the hour was not, and that a signal with a right hour in it empties a coast of boats in an afternoon, and about four agreed with her and about four said nothing. Nobody in this shed said that code was wrong about the sea.**"

"**We brought two small boats in off the boards at about twelve and stood a second watch on that mole from about one, and we sent nobody up that coast road and we asked no other place to do anything. Our boat came in at about one on the second hired day and the pilot said one of the two had found ground where he meant her to go and had gone back out past the point at about four yesterday afternoon, and the other stood where it was until about ten last night and was not in that water after, and nobody in his boat followed it. He said the stones he used are not on that chart of about nine years standing and that the chart is a chart of a coast that has moved.**"

"**Nobody put words to the man of about thirty-two about going down today and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and he went up to the second watch at about one and stood two hours of it. The disagreement was in this shed again at about four. He said a signal with a right hour in it is the most dangerous thing a coast can be handed, and that a page printing who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing, and she said a page printing nothing about who did not agree is a page that cannot be answered, and that a signal nobody can answer is what empties a coast. He said the June objection was the June objection, and she said she knew the shape of it by now and had stopped arguing the shape and gone on to the other thing, and about four agreed with her and about four with him, and this is not settled and no page here settles it.**"

"**And the last of it, and it is read here in the yard's own mouth. Twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four. The sheet stands at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fifty-four days without one. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-nine days today and nothing rung or rung against this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a code rung at eleven, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A WEATHER CODE WAS RUNG ON THAT MOLE AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND THE SAME CODE WAS RUNG AT EIGHT OTHER PLACES ON THIS COAST IN THE SAME HOUR, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED HAS FOUND OUT WHO RANG IT AT THIS MOLE, AND A SIGNAL IS NOT A DOCUMENT. THAT WALL STOOD BARE FROM ABOUT HALF PAST TEN UNTIL ABOUT A QUARTER TO TWELVE, BEING THE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEENTH TIME AT THIRTY-FOUR OFF AND TWENTY-ONE BARE AND THIRTY-FOUR BACK. NOBODY PUT THE SAME WORDS TO THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO ABOUT GOING DOWN THIS DAY. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the thirty-first day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that that wall behind that bench stood bare from about half past ten until about a quarter to twelve, being the hundred and seventeenth time at thirty-four off and twenty-one bare and thirty-four back, being eighty-nine the whole of it, the three figures having been read twice; and that a weather code was rung on that mole at about eleven in the morning, being the code that says a storm is on the coast at about six in the evening, and that the same code was reported as rung at eight other places on this coast in the same hour, the first report coming at about twenty past eleven from a boy of that market and the last before noon; and that the eight hours were written on a sheet by themselves with the name of no place against any hour, and that the sheet was put on the end stall and not on the nail; and that the yard's own instruments were tried afterwards, being the lead against the harbour side, the swell on that bar and the glass in the office, and that the harbour side agreed with the lead and the swell was a foot and a half and the glass had not gone down a tenth since Friday; and that two small boats were brought in off the boards at about twelve and a second watch was stood on that mole from about one, and that this yard sent nobody up that coast road and asked no other place to do anything; and that the boat came alongside at about one on the second of two hired days and the pilot said one of the two had found ground and gone back out past the point at about four the afternoon before and the other had stood until about ten that evening and was not in that water after; and that nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two on that day about going down, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the code was written as a code and not as a warning is that a signal carried on a bell has no author in it and cannot be answered, and a page that names an author it has not found is a page that invents one; that at about one the reason eight hours were put on a sheet by themselves is that a list of places is a list, and this yard does not keep lists of other communities and will not begin on a Sunday with the first one; that at about two the reason the two small boats were brought in and no one was sent up the coast road is that bringing a harbor's own boats in is a thing that harbor can do and sending a warning to a place that did not ask for one is a thing it cannot, and the difference is not courage but jurisdiction; and that at about four the reason the disagreement was put where nine people could hear it is that an argument kept between two people stops being an argument and becomes a marriage, and the house has enough of those.*
