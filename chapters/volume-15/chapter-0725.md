# Chapter 725

*Friday the Fifteenth Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the First of Two Books Left on the Flat of That Bench Stone at About Two With a Cart Wheel Over It and Two Leaves Spoiled and the Second on the Shelf Above the Butt Dry and Carrying the Same Figures, the Standing Disagreement Between Two People Put in the Open in About Nine and Not Settled and Not Settled on Any Page, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Friday the fifteenth day of July, with that market busy from nine and a hot afternoon with the wind still in the east, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. The first of the two books begun on the fifth of July was left on the flat of that bench stone at about two in the afternoon and a cart wheel went over the corner of it at about half past two and two leaves of it were spoiled beyond reading. The second of the two was on the shelf above that butt and was dry, and it carries the same figures in the same hand, and the woman of thirty of no office wrote the two spoiled leaves out again from it before five. The disagreement between two people about what a page prints was put in the open in about nine this afternoon and was not settled. The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and twenty-third day. Four hundred and twenty-five days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and sixty-third day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and twenty-ninth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and forty-seventh days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and seventeenth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and thirty-fourth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and sixty-fifth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-three days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work went on all morning, being the pump housing taken apart and the gaskets gone over again and the boat's bilge pumped out and her bottom washed down, and the sixty yards of rope off the rail from Wednesday went back on in a coil that a man of about forty checked twice before it went on the pin.

---

At about two the woman of thirty of no office put the first book down on the flat of that bench stone and went across to the boards with the second under her arm, because about nine people were standing at the end stall and she wanted the figures read off both. She was gone about twenty minutes.

At about half past two a cart came across the flat of that stone with its left wheel on the corner of the book and went on up the lane without anybody seeing it. About four people at the end stall saw the wheel go over and about four were talking and did not. The corner was wet through and two leaves were spoiled beyond reading, being the leaves of the twelfth and the thirteenth of July.

---

She came back at about half past two and looked at it and did not say anything about it for about a minute. Then she took the second book off the shelf above that butt and opened it on the dry end of the bench stone and it is dry inside and carries the twelfth and the thirteenth of July in the same hand with the same figures on them.

She said the thing that had been lost was one wet corner of paper and not one figure, and she said that before the second book was begun she had not known that this coast loses paper, and that she had begun it because she expected to lose arguments and not pages.

Nobody said anything about that and about four people in that nine wanted to know what had been on the two leaves and she read both of them out of the second book before five, twice, because about five of that nine cannot read a column. What was on them was the charge numbers and the hours and the four sentences of the closing and the eleven boats and the creek's name.

---

The disagreement was put in the open at about four in about nine people. He said the second book would print a disagreement twice if the same disagreement was written into both of them, and that a refusal entered twice is a refusal with a following. She said that what goes in both of them is what she was told and never a name against a person, and that if the same sentence is written twice by the same hand it is one sentence in two places and not a refusal with a following.

He said that was not what he had been objecting to in June and that he knew what he was objecting to. She said she knew that too and that she was still going to keep the second book.

About four people agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said the two of them were wrong, and the matter is open between them and is not settled and no page in this yard carries a resolution of it.

---

At about half past five the reading of that creek was quieter than it was on Wednesday. Two people who had come down that road on Wednesday did not come down it today and about nine people waited at the end stall until about ten past six and then went home, and the keeper wrote the hour they stopped waiting and no reason against it.

---

A cart wheel went over a book on that Friday and at about half past five on that Friday the shortfall was read out loud at that bench stone in about six hundred and fifty words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had seen a cart wheel go over a book at about half past two.

"**The pump housing came apart and the gaskets were gone over again and the bilge was pumped out and the rope off the rail went back on in a coil that a man checked twice. At about two the woman of thirty of no office put the first of the two books down on the flat of this bench stone and went across to the boards with the second under her arm. At about half past two a cart came across this stone with its left wheel on the corner of the book and went up the lane without anybody seeing it, and the corner was wet through and two leaves were spoiled beyond reading, being the twelfth and the thirteenth of July.**

"**The second of the two was on the shelf above that butt and is dry inside and carries those two days in the same hand with the same figures on them, and she read both spoiled leaves out of it before five, twice. She said what was lost was one wet corner of paper and not one figure, and that before the second book was begun she had not known that this coast loses paper and had begun it because she expected to lose arguments and not pages.**

"**At about four we put the disagreement in the open in front of about nine. He said the second book would print a disagreement twice if the same disagreement went into both, and that a refusal entered twice is a refusal with a following. She said what goes in both is what she was told and never a name against a person, and that the same sentence written twice by one hand is one sentence in two places. He said that was not the thing he had been objecting to in June and that he knew what he had been objecting to, and she said she knew that too and was still keeping the second book. About four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong, and this is not settled and no page of these days settles it. And the reading of that creek was quieter than it was on the thirteenth, and two people did not come down that road today, and we waited at the end stall until about ten past six and then went home, and I have written the hour we stopped waiting and no reason against it.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty-eight days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-three days today and nothing a cart did to a wet corner at about half past two touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a second book on a shelf, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-seven lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A CART WHEEL WENT OVER THE CORNER OF THE FIRST OF TWO BOOKS AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO AND TWO LEAVES WERE SPOILED BEYOND READING, AND THE SECOND WAS ON THE SHELF AND WAS DRY AND CARRIES THE SAME FIGURES, AND THE TWO SPOILED DAYS WERE READ OUT AGAIN FROM IT BEFORE FIVE. THE DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE WAS PUT IN THE OPEN IN ABOUT NINE THIS AFTERNOON AND IS NOT SETTLED. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the fifteenth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that at about two the first of the two books begun on the fifth of July was left on the flat of that bench stone and at about half past two a cart wheel went over the corner of it, and that two leaves were spoiled beyond reading, being the twelfth and the thirteenth of July; and that the second of the two was on the shelf above that butt and was dry and carried the same figures in the same hand, and that the two spoiled days were read out of it before five and read twice; and that at about four the disagreement between two people about what a page prints was put in the open in about nine and is not settled and is not settled on any page of these days; and that the reading of that creek was quieter than it was on the thirteenth and two people did not come down that road, and that about nine people waited at the end stall until about ten past six and then went home.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past two the reason no cart was stopped and nobody went after the driver is that a book lying on a public stone is a book in a public place, and a record that cannot lie in a public place has already been told that it is not one; that at about three the reason the two spoiled days were not copied fresh from memory is that a figure written out again from memory on the same afternoon is a figure nobody has checked, and copying from the second book is a figure two hands have now touched; that at about four the reason the same objection was answered and not met is that an answer moves a disagreement along and a meeting would end it, and a disagreement that ends in nine people on a Friday is a disagreement two people decided between themselves; and that at about half past five the reason the hour a reading stopped waiting is written with no reason against it is that the quietest way this coast loses a line is not an argument but a road that stops being walked, and a page that prints why a road was not walked is a page that keeps a roll.*
