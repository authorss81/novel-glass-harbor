# Chapter 719

*Saturday the Ninth Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Two of the Four Heads on That Wall Used for the First Time in Nine Runs of Days, a Claim That a Page Be Put Up With Its Seam Showing and a Correction of a Sentence Said at the End Stall on Friday at the Same Size as the Rest, Four Stones Cut and Faced and a Line Not Written Until Monday Because the Line Carries Two Days, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Saturday the ninth day of July, with that market open from about nine and the boards at the back of it busy by eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. Two of the four heads on that wall in the room over the market were used for the first time in nine runs of days, being a claim and a public correction, and both were put up at the same size as the rest of that wall. Four stones were cut and faced at the third lay this morning and the line that pays for them is not written until Monday, because the line carries two days on its face and one of them is not yet past. The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence out and one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence short. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and seventeenth day. Four hundred and nineteen days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and fifty-seventh day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and twenty-third night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and forty-first days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and ninety-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and eleventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and twenty-eighth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and fifty-nine days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and seventeen days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Four stones were cut and faced at the third lay between about seven and about eleven, and the line and the shackles for them were got out and tried on each and came off again. Six men worked two days on it and two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk stood against it, and the keeper did not write the line, and about four people asked him why at about eleven and he told them.

He said the line would carry two days on its face and that one of them was not yet past, and that a line written with a day on it that has not happened is a line anybody can check against a day that is wrong. About four people said that was right and about four said nothing. The stone and the shackles went under a cover at the third lay and the cover is off them at the same hours as every other cover in that yard.

---

At about half past eleven in the room over that market the four heads on that wall were used. The first of the two was a claim, and the woman of about fifty-eight of the Lowfen Reed put it up herself and it was in her own hand and it is on that wall in the same size as the rest.

The claim is that her community's page be put up on that wall with the seam showing and the flat stroke showing and no explanation attached to either of them. She said she had been three days deciding whether to put it up at all and that she had decided to put it up in that form because a page hung up clean is a page somebody can read as a verdict, and hers has been read that way twice already by two people she has never met.

The woman of thirty of no office wrote it out at the size of the rest and put it under the head it belongs to, and read it back to the woman of about fifty-eight before the pins went in, and the woman of about fifty-eight said it was right.

---

The second of the two was a public correction, and it corrects a sentence said in that market on Friday afternoon at that end stall. The sentence said that the Lowfen Reed had never given a time. The correction says they gave a time on the third of July and that the time has been taken back by them, and it is dated today and it is in the same size of hand as the rest of that wall.

About eleven people stood in that room while it went up and about five of them cannot read it. The man of about thirty-one who hires hands read it out twice, and he read it out twice because that is what a correction on that wall is for, and nobody in that room said the sentence had been meant kindly, because it had been meant kindly and that was not the point.

Nobody in that room said which other pages are good because of that correction, and the correction does not say so either.

---

One person went home from that room at about one and went down the road and said nothing to anybody in this party about it. She was about nineteen and she had come up the coast road with two others and she left with one page of her own and she did not leave the page.

---

At about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood in the room over the market at about half past eleven.

"**Four stones were cut and faced at the third lay between about seven and about eleven, and the line and the shackles for them were tried on each and came off again, and I did not write the line, because that line will carry two days on its face and one of them is not yet past, and a line written against a day that has not happened is a line a stranger can check and find wrong. The cover is off them the same hours as every other cover.**

"**Two of the four heads on that wall over the market were used today for the first time in nine runs of days, and neither of them was used by this yard. The first was a claim, put up by the woman of about fifty-eight of the Lowfen Reed in her own hand and in the same size as the rest of that wall, and it asks that her community's page go up with the seam showing and the flat stroke showing and no explanation attached to either. She said a page hung up clean is a page somebody reads as a verdict and hers has been read that way twice by two people she has never met.**

"**The second was a public correction, in the same size of hand, of a sentence said at that end stall on Friday afternoon, which said that the Lowfen Reed had never given a time, and it says they gave a time on the third and that they have taken it back. The man of about thirty-one read both out twice because about five of that room cannot read them. Nobody in that room said which other pages are good because of that, and the correction does not say so either.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence out and one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty-two days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and seventeen days today and nothing hung on a wall this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for cutting stone, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-one lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**TWO OF THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL WERE USED TODAY FOR THE FIRST TIME IN NINE RUNS OF DAYS, BEING A CLAIM AND A PUBLIC CORRECTION, BOTH IN THE SAME SIZE AS THE REST OF THAT WALL AND NEITHER OF THEM BY THIS YARD. FOUR STONES WERE CUT AND FACED AT THE THIRD LAY AND THE LINE THAT PAYS FOR THEM IS NOT WRITTEN UNTIL MONDAY BECAUSE THAT LINE CARRIES TWO DAYS. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND AND FOURTEEN PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIVE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the ninth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that four stones were cut and faced at the third lay between about seven and about eleven and that the line paying for them is not written until the eleventh because it carries two days on its face, one being the ninth and one being the eleventh; and that a claim was put up on that wall at about half past eleven by the woman of about fifty-eight of the Lowfen Reed, in her own hand and at the size of the rest, asking that that community's page go up with the seam showing and the flat stroke showing and no explanation attached to either; and that a public correction was put up on that wall at the same size of hand and at the same size of wall, correcting a sentence said at that end stall on Friday afternoon which said that the Lowfen Reed had never given a time, and saying they gave one on the third and have taken it back; and that both were read out twice in that room by a man of about thirty-one who hires hands; and that one person of about nineteen left that room at about one and went down the road with her own page and said nothing to anybody in this party.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason a line is not written against a day that has not happened is that the whole value of a line a stranger can check is that the day on it is a day that was; that at about half past eleven the reason a page goes up with its seam showing is that a claim about a page is a claim about a form and not about a truth, and a form is the one thing about a page a community can decide for itself and be right about; that at about one in the afternoon the reason a correction is put up at the same size as a claim is that a correction which is small says the thing that was said was small, and about eleven people in that room knew it was not small; and that at about four the reason no page says which other pages are good is that a correction spends a wrong sentence and a yard that spends a wrong sentence to buy a good name has bought the name with somebody else's word.*
