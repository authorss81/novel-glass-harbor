# Chapter 722

*Tuesday the Twelfth Day of July, the Eighty-Ninth Wage Tuesday, the Lid Read Out at About Ten in Four Minutes and Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moved, Two of the Four Heads on That Wall Used for the First Time, a Refusal and a Restricted Record and Both at the Same Size as the Rest, Line Seven Settled Into the Foot of That Sheet at About Four, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Tuesday the twelfth day of July, with that market open from about nine and rain coming in off the water after two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The eighty-ninth wage Tuesday and not a rota day and not a wipe, and the lid read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and standing at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and not moved, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it shut from ten until four, and the eighty-ninth week standing fallen due at the close of that day behind the eighty-eighth. The other two of the four heads on that wall were used for the first time since that wall was put up, being a refusal and a restricted record, and both were put up at the same size as the rest of that wall. Line seven was settled into the foot of that money sheet at about four in the afternoon and the total at the foot of that sheet is one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence from the morning. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and twentieth day. Four hundred and twenty-two days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and sixtieth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and twenty-sixth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and forty-fourth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and ninety-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and fourteenth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and thirty-first day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and sixty-second days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lid was read out at about ten in four minutes and did not move for the eighty-ninth time. The man of about thirty-one stood on that step with the lid open and read the twenty-six lines steady and about nine people listened without writing anything down. The chest with the twenty envelopes stood shut from ten until four, and the eighty-ninth week stands fallen due at the close of the day behind the eighty-eighth, and the column has not moved on any of the eighty-nine.

---

At about half past eleven in the room over that market the two remaining heads on that wall were used. The woman of about fifty-eight of the Lowfen Reed put up a refusal in her own hand and it is on that wall at the same size as the rest.

The refusal is that no hand of this yard shall go into that creek to raise stones, mend marks, read a page or do any work on that water, and that this yard shall not send a boat there for any reason at all, and that if a boat of theirs is ever wanted on that coast it will be asked for in words and paid for in chalk like any other boat and not sent because a person was asked.

She read it out herself to that room before it went up, and about eleven people were in the room and about five of them cannot read it, and the man of about thirty-one read it out again afterwards because that is what that wall is for.

---

The second of the two was a restricted record, and it was entered the same way and at the same size. That community's page with the seam showing goes into a folder on that wall and the folder is not opened in that room, and it comes out only when one person of that community says it may, and it is not copied into either of the woman's books, and it is not read out, and no part of it appears in the shed.

The keeper asked the one question that is a keeper's to ask, being how long it stands until somebody looks at it, and she said it stands until that community says otherwise and that it is not a year and not a season and is not anybody's business but theirs.

He wrote that on the wall face in her own words and under it in his own hand that this yard does not keep it and does not copy it and has not seen what is in the folder, and that the four lines it did see on Saturday stand on the page of that week and will stand there.

---

At about four in the afternoon the five parts of line six were read out again and the four parts of line seven were read out before the sum went into the foot of the sheet, being one hundred and twenty pence of stone, and eighty-four of line and shackles, and one hundred and ninety-two of men, and three hundred and thirty-six of boat. Those four are seven hundred and thirty-two pence, being four marks, seven shillings and eightpence.

The sheet stands this evening at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence out and one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence short, and goes into the foot of it in the morning at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, which is one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short.

About four people said that going into the foot of that sheet a line whose stones are gone was money well spent. Nobody in that shed said whether it was or was not, and the man of about thirty-two said the sheet is a record and does not know what well is.

---

The shortfall went out loud at that bench stone at about half past five on that Tuesday in about nine hundred words to about nine people, four of whom cannot read a column, and about four of those nine had stood in the room over the market at about half past eleven.

"**The twenty-six lines on that lid were read out this morning at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay, and the lid came down at ten past the hour carrying the same figure it has carried every Tuesday since the spring, being twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks. The chest with the twenty envelopes in it is shut from ten until four and the eighty-ninth week stands fallen due at the close of today behind the eighty-eighth.**

"**The other two heads on that wall were used today for the first time since that wall was put up, and neither of them was used by this yard. A refusal, in the hand of the woman of about fifty-eight of the Lowfen Reed, at the same size as the rest of that wall, saying that no hand of this yard shall go into that creek to raise stones, mend marks, read a page or do any work on that water, and that a boat will be asked for in words and paid for in chalk like any other boat and not sent because a person was asked. She read it out herself before it went up. And a restricted record, the same size of hand, being that community's page with the seam showing put into a folder that is not opened in that room and comes out only when one person of that community says it may, and not copied into either book and not read out. I asked one keeper's question, being how long it stands, and she said until that community says otherwise and it is not a year and not a season.**

"**Line seven was settled into the foot of that sheet at about four, being one hundred and twenty of stone and eighty-four of line and shackles and one hundred and ninety-two of men and three hundred and thirty-six of boat, and those four are seven hundred and thirty-two pence, being four marks, seven shillings and eightpence. The sheet stands this evening at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen out and one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four short, and goes into the foot of it in the morning at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six out against ten thousand six hundred and forty in, being one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six short. About four said going that line into the foot was money well spent and nobody in this shed said whether it was, because the sheet is a record and does not know what well is.**

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty-five days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty days today and nothing put up on a wall this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a refusal, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-four lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A REFUSAL AND A RESTRICTED RECORD WERE PUT UP ON THAT WALL TODAY, BOTH IN THE SAME SIZE AS THE REST AND NEITHER BY THIS YARD, AND ALL FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL HAVE NOW BEEN USED. THE REFUSAL STANDS AND NO HAND OF THIS YARD WILL GO INTO THAT CREEK. LINE SEVEN IS SETTLED INTO THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET AND THE TOTAL GOES IN IN THE MORNING AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twelfth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the eighty-ninth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that the lid was read out at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, with the chest with the twenty envelopes in it shut from ten until four, and the eighty-ninth week standing fallen due at the close of the day behind the eighty-eighth; and that a refusal was put up on that wall at about half past eleven in the hand of the woman of about fifty-eight of the Lowfen Reed and at the size of the rest of that wall, saying that no hand of this yard shall go into that creek to raise stones, mend marks, read a page or do any work on that water and that a boat will be asked for in words and paid for in chalk like any other boat; and that a restricted record was put up at the same size of hand, being that community's page with the seam showing in a folder that is not opened in that room and comes out only when one person of that community says it may, and is not copied into either of the woman's books and is not read out; and that line seven was settled into the foot of that sheet at about four, being one hundred and twenty pence of stone and eighty-four pence of line and shackles and one hundred and ninety-two pence of men and three hundred and thirty-six pence of boat, being seven hundred and thirty-two pence, being four marks, seven shillings and eightpence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past eleven the reason a refusal is the same size as a claim is that a claim is a thing a person has asked to be on a wall and a refusal is a thing a person has asked not to be done, and a wall that makes one large and the other small has decided which of the two counts; that at about twelve the reason a folder is not opened in the room it stands in is that the only thing a restricted record proves is that the people it belongs to have said it is theirs to give, and that proof is spent the moment it is read by anybody who was not asked; that at about half past three the reason the fourth of the four heads was used on the same wall and not on a second one is that a wall with four heads on it is one thing a man can learn in an afternoon and two walls are two things and a harbour learns the smaller one twice as often; and that at about four the reason no page settles whether a line whose stones are gone was worth entering is that this sheet exists so that a stranger can take a figure off it and see what was done, and a sheet that also carries judgements is a sheet nobody can take a figure off without also taking an argument.*
