# Chapter 760

*Friday the Nineteenth Day of August, the Sixtieth Day, a Boat of About Eleven Tons Out of That Quay Over the Bar at About Five in the Morning With Three Men in Her and No Escort and Nobody Following Her, Nine Buoys of Nine Different Shapes Answering One Another Across the Water Between About Six and About Seven and the Sound Not Merging Into One Note, the Read-Back of That Leaf at About Two in the Yard's Own Mouth and in the Open and All Four Columns Including the Empty One and Read Twice and Its Own Derivation on the Face of It, Line Thirteen Settled Into the Foot of That Money Sheet at About Four With the Four Parts Read Before the Sum Went Down and the Total Read After It, the Page of Routes Written for the Third and Last Time This Afternoon and the Sum Written on the Page and Not Said in a Room and Not in a Slip, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Friday the nineteenth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a hard bright morning with the wind in the north and the sea running clean over that bar, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. A boat of about eleven tons went out over that bar at about five this morning with three men in her and no escort and nobody following her, and between about six and about seven this morning nine buoys lying off that mole head answered one another in the order they always answer in and the sound did not merge into one note. The read-back of that leaf was made at about two in the yard's own mouth and in the open, all four columns including the empty one, and it was read twice because about five people cannot read a column, and its own derivation was on the face of it, being four hundred and ninety-two and two thousand three hundred and thirty-seven at the hour of that reading. Line thirteen settled into the foot of that money sheet at about four and the four parts were read with their multiplications before the sum went down, and the total at the foot of that sheet was read after that settlement and stands at one hundred and twenty-three thousand and seventy-two pence out and one hundred and twelve thousand four hundred and thirty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this run of days and this is where they stood at noon. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and fifty-eighth day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and sixty days with nobody asking him anything and his chest is shut and stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and ninety-eighth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and sixty-fourth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-second days and no figure is set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and thirty-seventh day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-second day. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and sixty-ninth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept four hundred days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water, and the forty marks whole that stand against him have not gone. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck and has no hundred and twenty-sixth line in it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-eight days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water, and the hearing he got back on the fourth of December is a different hearing from that one and is not set against it.**

That boat went out over that bar at about five this morning on the tide with three men in her and nobody on her quarter, and no boat went after her and none stood off that mole head to see her out, and about four of that yard's men stood on the stones and about four did not come out of that shed at all.

She was not going for the ninth section and was not going for anything off that landing, and the three men in her are two of the second lay and a man of about forty-three of that market, and what they had in her was coal and a kettle and two baskets, and nobody wrote down anywhere what that boat was for.

Between about six and about seven this morning the nine buoys lying off that mole head answered one another in the order they have always answered in and at the two hours a day those nine have kept, and the nine shapes of them are nine shapes and not eight.

There is a barrel, a cone, a spar, a triangle, a can, a spar with a flag on it, a double cone, a round and a square, and the sound of the nine of them came across that water nine separate times and stayed nine separate times and did not come out as one note and was not tried to be made into one note by anybody on that mole head that morning.

The ordinary work of that Friday went on and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine and the glass front of that case in its frame and lit at about eleven for the first time and the stove drawing, and a lighter going out of that quay at about two with three men in her.

The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it, and that man of about sixty-one was not in that yard.

At about eleven the woman of thirty of no office said out loud in about nine people that the entry was finished and had been finished since the Wednesday night, and that she was going to read it out at about two and that several people were going to stand and hear it and be named as having heard it, and that she had asked for them and had not appointed them.

About four of that nine said they would stand and about four said they would sit at the back and hear it, and the man of about thirty-two said he would stand and he would read it and that nobody was going to stop him reading it and nobody was going to let him put a word in it.

At about two that entry was read out at the flat of that stone, in about nine people, in the yard's own mouth, with five of those nine named as having heard it because about five of that party cannot read what is on that stone.

It carries what nine places agreed and what one man refused and what was asked for and was not granted, with the hour against each and no name against any hour, and it carries on its own face a statement of what is not known, which is that the ferry's manifest was altered and that it is not known by whose hand and at whose instance and with what intent, and that the ninth bell is in nine sections of which eight answer and none will take nine answers and make one answer out of them, and that a man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing out of the left side of his head since the fourth of December and that this is not improved by anything that has happened.

The man of about thirty-two read that entry at about two and read it as it was written and did not stop anywhere in it and did not put a word into it, and when he had finished he said out loud in about nine people that he could not put a word into it that would make the choice of the man who is in the ninth section read kinder than that entry reads it, and that he was not going to try, and that a record a man can improve is a record that has been improved.

About four of that nine agreed with him and about four said nothing, and nobody said either was wrong, and the woman of thirty of no office said out loud that her entry was her work and not his decision and that he was not to speak for her about it, and he said he would not.
