# Chapter 701

*Tuesday the Twenty-First Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, a Boat of About Thirty Tons Off That Mole at About Six in the Morning and Alongside It Again at About Two in the Afternoon with a Bell Seat Bolted Down Amidships That Was Not Bolted Down When She Left and Forty Yards of New Line in a Coil That Is Not in That Shed and Is Not on This Yard's Page, Two Men of the Only Route Anybody Is Holding Aboard Her and One of Them About Thirty-One With a Hand on That Rope at About Half Past One in the Afternoon Seen From the Mole Head, a Quarter Past Four in the Afternoon Written Down in That Route's Own Hand on That Route's Own Page With No Day of the Week on It and No Name Against It, Eleven Words Read Out on That Deck for the Fourteenth Time and Answered by Nobody and Written Down Again by Nobody, Nothing Read Out of a Gap and No Figure Moved by One, the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand and Forty-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-first day of June, with that market open from about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, a boat lying off that mole with her fenders in and nobody on her deck, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. A boat of about thirty tons went out off that mole at about six this morning with about nine people on her and came alongside it again at about two this afternoon, and she was hired by the only route anybody in this harbor is holding and not by this yard, and this yard sent a hand and no more. At about half past one this afternoon a man of about thirty-one of that route put a hand on that rope and the yard saw him do it from the mole head and wrote nothing on any page about it until the second column, where it is entered with the hour against it and the reason it is entered there. At about four in the afternoon a time went onto that route's own page in that route's own hand, being quarter past four in the afternoon and no day of the week on it. At about half past four this afternoon a man of about fifty-three said eleven words on that deck and nobody answered him and nobody wrote them down again. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its three hundred and ninety-ninth day. Four hundred and one days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and thirty-ninth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fifth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-third days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and tenth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and forty-one days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-nine days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

She was away before the market opened and this yard had nothing to do with it except the two hands it sent and a third man who carried the coil.

The coil was forty yards of new line, three-strand, wet, and it came off her in about four minutes and went straight up to the third lay and was laid on the boards there and the boards are that route's boards and not this yard's. The man who keeps the leaf stood at the top of the steps and watched it go up and did not write it into a page, because a line in chalk is a route's figure and a route's figure is on a route's page.

---

The bell seat is the thing worth the morning.

It was not there when she went out. It is a seat of ash about the width of a man's two hands, cut and faced, with a hole through the middle of it for a shackle and two side cheeks to hold a bell upright, and it was bolted down amidships on the port side with four bolts through a deck that somebody had painted over at some point in a winter nobody wrote down. He could see the new paint around the bolt holes from the mole head, at about half past one, in the flat light before the wind came round.

---

The man of about thirty-one had his hand on the rope for about eleven seconds and then took it off and coiled the slack the way a man coils slack who has done it ten thousand times.

He was one of two of that route aboard. The other was about fifty-four and did not come aft and stayed forward the whole crossing, and about four people in that shed who have been going down that coast road for years said afterwards that a man of fifty-four who stays forward on a day like that is a man who is being careful about something, and about four of them said nothing.

---

**What this yard is not.** The man of about thirty-two of that harbor said it out loud at about ten past three in the afternoon, in about nine people, and he said it in about forty-four words and he did not raise his voice and nobody wrote it down because it was said.

He said that this party does not hold that route and does not hold the bell and does not hold the rope, that the boat was hired by that route's own people at a figure in chalk that this yard does not know and has not asked for, and that the one thing this yard has done today was send two hands and carry somebody's coil up a ladder.

About four people in that nine said that was fair and about four said nothing.

---

At about four o'clock the man of about thirty-three came down the coast road from that route with a page in a coat and put it on the bench stone in that yard and did not stay.

On it was one line in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that shed, and the line was a time, and the time was quarter past four in the afternoon, and there was no day of the week against it and no name against it and nothing under it.

That route does not put days on times. Two of the nine said that aloud in that nine and one of them said that a day of the week is a thing a person puts on a thing that is going to be held to that person on a day, and that they had learned that in April from a page of somebody else's.

---

At about half past four this afternoon the man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons was still sitting on the after end of that deck with his knees up and his hands hanging over them.

He said his eleven words. He has said them since a Tuesday in a shed in the spring and he has said them fourteen times, and about four people on that deck heard the whole of it and about nine people in that market below did not hear any of it.

The eleven words were that this coast can be cut in one place at a time.

Nobody answered him. A man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm, who had the coil, said something to him about the paint on the deck that was not an answer, and the man of about fifty-three looked at the water and did not say it again.

---

Nobody wrote the eleven words on any page today and nobody put them against anything. The man who keeps the leaf wrote, in the second column, that a thing was said on that deck at about half past four and that this yard is not going to be the place it is written down, and he wrote the hour against that and no name.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote the Tuesday line on the page of that week and it says three things, being that a boat hired by a route came back with a bell seat bolted down that was not bolted down when she left, that a time went onto that route's own page in that route's own hand with no day of the week on it, and that eleven words were said on a deck at half past four and were not answered and were not written down again by this yard.

Nobody thanked anybody for that line and nobody argued with it.

---

And at about half past five on that Tuesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about eight hundred and sixty-seven words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been at the top of that mole at about half past one in the afternoon.

"**A boat went out of this mole at about six this morning and came alongside it again at about two this afternoon, and she was hired by the only route anybody in this harbor is holding and not by this yard. She went out with no bell seat on her and she came back with one bolted down amidships on the port side, four bolts through a deck somebody painted over in a winter, and I could see the new paint round the bolt holes from the mole head at about half past one this afternoon, and a man of about thirty-one of that route had his hand on the new rope for about eleven seconds and then coiled the slack the way a man coils slack who has done it ten thousand times, and the other one of that two stayed forward the whole crossing and I am not going to say why and nobody asked him.**

**What this yard did about it was send two hands and carry a coil up a ladder. That is the whole of it. This party does not hold that route and does not hold that bell and does not hold that rope, and the boat was hired at a figure in chalk by that route's own people and this yard does not know the figure and has not asked for it, and a line in chalk is that route's figure and it is on that route's page and not on mine.**

**At about four o'clock a man came down that road with a page in a coat and put it on that stone and went away again, and on it was one line in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in this shed, and the line was quarter past four in the afternoon and there was no day of the week against it and no name against it. Two of the nine said that route does not put days on times, and one of them said a day of the week is a thing a person puts on a thing that is going to be held to that person on a day, and that they learned that in April off a page belonging to somebody else. That page is that page and it is in that route's own hand and I have not copied it onto this one and I am not going to.**

**And at half past four this afternoon a man of about fifty-three said his eleven words on that deck, and they are on a page in this harbor and they have been there since a Tuesday in the spring, and nobody answered him and nobody wrote them down again and this yard is not going to be the place they get written down, because a page in this shed is a page that anybody can walk up and read and that is not a place for a man's eleven words that nobody answered.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third, and both of them are off a rehearsal in May and a bell seat cut in June and neither of them has a boat's hire in it, and a route's hire of a boat is a figure in chalk and not a line on that sheet. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fourteen days without one.**

**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four and nobody walked on the water. The page of the twenty-eighth of May is still on that wall with seven lines on it and about eleven inches of empty plaster under it, and nobody has written under any of them this week. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it and nothing read off it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls and there were nine on the twenty-first of January and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is one hundred and ninety-nine days and nothing that happened on that boat today touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a boat, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had one line on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A BOAT OF ABOUT THIRTY TONS WENT OUT OFF THAT MOLE AT ABOUT SIX IN THE MORNING AND CAME ALONGSIDE IT AGAIN AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON, HIRED BY THE ONLY ROUTE ANYBODY IN THIS HARBOR IS HOLDING AND NOT BY THIS YARD, AND SHE WENT OUT WITH NO BELL SEAT ON HER AND CAME BACK WITH ONE BOLTED DOWN AMIDSHIPS ON THE PORT SIDE, FOUR BOLTS THROUGH A DECK SOMEBODY PAINTED OVER IN A WINTER, THE NEW PAINT ROUND THE BOLT HOLES BEING VISIBLE FROM THAT MOLE HEAD AT ABOUT HALF PAST ONE IN THE AFTERNOON. FORTY YARDS OF NEW LINE CAME OFF HER IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES AND WENT UP TO THE THIRD LAY AND WERE LAID ON THAT ROUTE'S OWN BOARDS AND ARE NOT IN THAT SHED AND ARE NOT ON THIS YARD'S PAGE, A ROUTE'S FIGURE IN CHALK BEING THAT ROUTE'S FIGURE AND ON THAT ROUTE'S PAGE. TWO MEN OF THAT ROUTE WERE ABOARD AND ONE OF THEM, WHO IS ABOUT THIRTY-ONE, PUT A HAND ON THAT ROPE FOR ABOUT ELEVEN SECONDS AND COILED THE SLACK AFTERWARDS, AND THE OTHER, WHO IS ABOUT FIFTY-FOUR, STAYED FORWARD THE WHOLE CROSSING AND WAS NOT ASKED WHY. AT ABOUT TEN PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WHAT THIS YARD IS NOT, IN ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WORDS, AND IT WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN BECAUSE IT WAS SAID. AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON A MAN CAME DOWN THAT COAST ROAD WITH A PAGE IN A COAT AND PUT IT ON THAT STONE AND WENT AWAY AGAIN, AND ON IT WAS ONE LINE IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE HAND OF ANYBODY IN THAT SHED, BEING A QUARTER PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON WITH NO DAY OF THE WEEK AGAINST IT AND NO NAME AGAINST IT AND NOTHING UNDER IT. AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WITH A BOAT OF ABOUT FOURTEEN TONS SAID HIS ELEVEN WORDS ON THAT DECK, BEING THAT THIS COAST CAN BE CUT IN ONE PLACE AT A TIME, AND NOBODY ANSWERED HIM AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN SAID SOMETHING ABOUT PAINT THAT WAS NOT AN ANSWER AND HE LOOKED AT THE WATER AND DID NOT SAY IT AGAIN, AND THIS YARD DID NOT WRITE THOSE ELEVEN WORDS DOWN AGAIN ANYWHERE. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-first day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that that butt behind the market was covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either; and that a boat of about thirty tons went out off that mole at about six in the morning with about nine people on her and came alongside it again at about two in the afternoon, hired by the only route anybody in this harbor is holding and not by this yard, and that this yard sent two hands and a third man to carry a coil and did no more; and that a bell seat of ash about the width of a man's two hands was bolted down amidships on the port side with four bolts through a deck that had been painted over, and that the new paint round the bolt holes was visible from that mole head at about half past one in the afternoon, and that a man of about thirty-one of that route had his hand on the new rope for about eleven seconds and then coiled the slack, and that the second man of that route, who is about fifty-four, stayed forward the whole crossing and was not asked why; and that at about ten past three in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two of that harbor said out loud in about nine people what this yard is not, in about forty-four words, being that this party does not hold that route and does not hold the bell and does not hold the rope, and that the boat was hired at a figure in chalk by that route's own people and that this yard does not know the figure and has not asked for it, and that about four of the nine said that was fair and about four said nothing, and that it was not written down because it was said; and that at about four in the afternoon a man of about thirty-three came down that coast road with a page in a coat and put it on that bench stone and did not stay, and that on the page was one line in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that shed, being a quarter past four in the afternoon, with no day of the week against it and no name against it and nothing under it, and that two of the nine said aloud that that route does not put days on times, and that one of them said a day of the week is a thing a person puts on a thing that is going to be held to them on a day, and that they had learned that in April off a page belonging to somebody else; and that at about half past four in the afternoon a man of about fifty-three with a boat of about fourteen tons said his eleven words on that deck, being that this coast can be cut in one place at a time, and that about four people on that deck heard the whole of it and about nine people in that market heard none of it, and that a man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm said something about paint that was not an answer, and that the man of about fifty-three looked at the water and did not say it again, and that no page in this shed carries those eleven words and no page in this shed will; and that nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and that under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past one in the afternoon the fact of a hand on a rope is entered in this column and not in the first, and the reason is that a count of who rang what is a count that has to be taken off a page of days and written down once with the day and the hour against it, and a man seen putting a hand on a rope from a mole head is a thing this yard saw and not a thing the route's own people entered, and this yard does not put one over the other; that at about ten past three in the afternoon the reason what this yard is not was said and not written is that a sentence said out loud in nine people can be argued with by four of the nine before the hour is out, and a sentence on a page in this shed can only be argued with tomorrow and by whoever reads it, and there are about eleven people in this harbor who cannot read a column and about four of those eleven come into this yard; that at about four in the afternoon the reason that page was not copied onto this one is that a time is that community's own, and a copy of a time in this yard would be a second version of it, and two versions of a time is how a coast ends up with a schedule it never agreed to; that at about half past four in the afternoon the reason eleven words that nobody answered are not on a page in this shed is that this harbor has a page of days that anybody can walk up and read, and a man's eleven words with the hour against them and no name against them would be read by about nine people tomorrow as an accusation, and it was not an accusation, it was a thing a man said on a deck; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason the coil is not on any page in this shed is that forty yards of line in chalk belongs to the route that bought the line, and a route's figure belongs to that route's page, and the count of what this yard has spent today is two hands and a ladder and neither of those is worth a line.
