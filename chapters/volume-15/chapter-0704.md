# Chapter 704

*Friday the Twenty-Fourth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Eleven Households With Their Own Goods Going Up That Coast Road From Before Six in the Morning With Two Handcarts Among Them and the Market's Own People Carrying What Could Not Be Carried, a Man of About Thirty-Two Helping for About an Hour at the Top of That Road and Then Standing at the Bottom and Watching, No Figure Against What It Cost Them and the Yard Writing Down That It Was Not Asked and Did Not Go, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand and Fifty-Nine*

Friday the twenty-fourth day of June, with a spring tide at the top of the morning and the lane behind that market wet to the curb, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. Eleven households went up that coast road from before six in the morning with their own goods and two handcarts among them and the market's own people carrying what could not be carried. A man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood at the top of that road and helped for about an hour at about two in the afternoon and was told by a woman of about sixty that this was not his to do and went and stood at the bottom and watched the rest of it. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and second day. Four hundred and four days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and forty-second day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and eighth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-six days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and eighty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and thirteenth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and forty-four days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and two days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The lane behind that market floods at the top of a spring tide, and it flooded on Friday morning and the water stood halfway up the curb stones. The low end of it sits about nine feet below the lane it runs into, and the carts that went up had to be pushed at the bend where the stones are loose. Eleven households went with bedding and kettles and tools tied in cloth, and two households had a handcart and the other nine had nothing with wheels.

The market's own people had been carrying other people's goods up that road since before six in the morning. They took it in turns and they did not write anything down, and the woman who keeps the end stall stood at the corner and told each household where to set things down out of the damp. She had told them the water would be higher on Saturday, and she was right about Saturday when Saturday came, and nobody has said so since that Friday.

---

A man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm carried for about four hours and would not stop and would not be asked to. He took the heavy end of every load without being asked, and about four people told him to rest and he went on carrying. He drank water from a shared cup at about noon and went back up the road with a brass kettle in each hand, and one of the kettles belonged to a household with three small children.

At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two came to the top of that road and helped for about an hour. He lifted and carried and did not give orders, and about four people worked alongside him without speaking. A woman of about sixty put a hand on his arm and told him this was not his to do, and he went and stood at the bottom and watched the rest of it until about five. The man who keeps the leaf wrote both hours down, being the hour he helped and the hour he watched, and wrote against them that this yard was not asked and did not go as a yard.

---

The turn of the day is not a figure. The cost of the warning that came ashore on Thursday is eleven households' bedding and two brass kettles going up a hill in other people's hands, and the yard prints no figure against what it cost them. A figure against bedding carried up a road would be a price on people doing what people do when water comes, and this yard does not set such figures. The page of that week carries six lines after Friday, and the Friday line says eleven households went and this yard was not asked and went for an hour and then watched.

Nobody in that eleven asked this yard for anything, and this yard asked none of them for anything. That is written down whole in the first column, because a yard that helped for an hour without being asked is a yard that could be taken next week for a yard that goes where it is not asked. The distinction matters on a coast where nine communities are being asked when they can be in contact, and where a hand offered without asking can be read as a hand claiming a right.

---

Eleven households went up that coast road before six and at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and thirty-nine words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been at the bottom of that road in the afternoon.

"**Eleven households went up that coast road from before six this morning with their own goods and two handcarts among them and the market's own people carrying what could not be carried. The lane behind that market flooded at the top of the spring tide and the low end of it sits about nine feet below the lane it runs into, and the carts had to be pushed at the bend where the stones are loose.**

**A man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm carried for about four hours and would not stop and would not be asked to, and he took the heavy end of every load and drank from a shared cup at about noon and went back up with a brass kettle in each hand. At about two the man of about thirty-two stood at the top of that road and helped for about an hour and was told by a woman of about sixty that this was not his to do, and he went and stood at the bottom and watched the rest of it until about five.**

**The cost of yesterday's sheet is not a figure on a page in this shed. It is eleven households' bedding and two brass kettles going up a hill in other people's hands, and this yard prints no figure against what it cost them, and that is the only honest line available. This yard was not asked and it did not go as a yard, and one man helped for an hour and then watched, and both hours are on the page.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and seventeen days without one.**

**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four and nobody walked on the water. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and two days today and nothing on that road touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for carrying, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had six lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**ELEVEN HOUSEHOLDS WENT UP THAT COAST ROAD FROM BEFORE SIX IN THE MORNING WITH THEIR OWN GOODS AND TWO HANDCARTS AMONG THEM AND THE MARKET'S OWN PEOPLE CARRYING WHAT COULD NOT BE CARRIED, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO HELPED FOR ABOUT AN HOUR AT THE TOP OF THAT ROAD AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND THEN STOOD AT THE BOTTOM AND WATCHED. THE YARD WAS NOT ASKED AND DID NOT GO AS A YARD, AND IT PRINTS NO FIGURE AGAINST WHAT IT COST THEM. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-fourth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that eleven households went up that coast road from before six in the morning with their own goods, being bedding and kettles and tools tied in cloth, with two handcarts among them and the market's own people carrying what could not be carried; and that a man of about twenty-seven with a burn scar across one forearm carried for about four hours and would not stop and would not be asked to; and that a man of about thirty-two of that harbor stood at the top of that road and helped for about an hour at about two in the afternoon and was told by a woman of about sixty that this was not his to do and went and stood at the bottom and watched the rest of it until about five; and that this yard was not asked and did not go as a yard, and that it prints no figure against what it cost them.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about noon the reason the yard did not count the loads is that bedding in other people's hands is not a line on a money sheet, and a figure against it would be read next week as a debt; that at about two in the afternoon the reason one man helping for an hour is entered with both hours is that an hour worked and an hour watched are two different things, and a yard that writes only the first will be taken to have worked the whole afternoon; that at about five the reason the woman of about sixty is entered without more is that a refusal given kindly is still a refusal, and this yard keeps refusals at the same size as the rest; and that at about half past five the reason the slip ends on the nine hulls is that a day of people going up a road is a day this harbor will remember, and the hulls that did not move are the thing that did not happen while it happened.*
