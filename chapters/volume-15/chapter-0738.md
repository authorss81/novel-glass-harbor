# Chapter 738

*Thursday the Twenty-Eighth Day of July, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventieth Charge on That Cistern, No Line on That Sheet, the Total Stepped to One Hundred and Eighteen Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Four Pence From This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seventy-One and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Thursday the twenty-eighth day of July, with that market open from about nine and a flat heat lying over the boards by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventieth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. No line went onto that sheet and no figure was written into its foot, and the keeper said out loud at about four that the day carries no line and is not one of any form. The sheet steps from this day to one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, yesterday having stood at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past, this yard now one hundred and fifty-one days without one.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and thirty-sixth day. Four hundred and thirty-eight days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and seventy-sixth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and forty-second night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and sixtieth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and fifteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and thirtieth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and forty-seventh day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and seventy-eighth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-six days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and seventieth time and the covers went on at the same hour as yesterday. About five people came for water after four and were served without waiting, and the keeper wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that no line goes on the sheet today.

---

The pump housing came apart in the morning and the gaskets were gone over and the bilge was pumped out, and sixty yards of rope off the rail went back on in a coil that a man of about forty checked twice before it went on the pin. About four people worked the housing and about four carried water for the washing down, and the boat was back on her mooring by noon with her bottom clean.

---

No person put the same words to the man of about thirty-two in that shed all day and none put them to him at the end stall. He worked the pump housing until noon and sat on the far bench from two until four with the second book open on his knees, and the page of days carries no hour of an asking against this day and no name and no sum is on any sheet here.

---

The quiet in that shed at about two was the quiet of ordinary work and not of an agreement. About four people mended sailcloth at the far bench and about four sorted line on the flat of the bench stone, and nobody said the quiet meant anything and the keeper wrote nothing about it except the hours of the work.

---

The disagreement was put in the open again at about three in about nine people. She said the second book stays on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book, and he said two books with one disagreement in both is one refusal read twice. She said a mark put yesterday on an outside by two hands is one custody and not two owners, and he said a custody that two people hold is a custody neither can lift alone.

---

He said a refusal entered twice gains a weight no single entry carries, and she said what gains weight is the hand that writes it twice and not the refusal. About four people agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open between them and is not settled and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it.

---

The woman of thirty of no office read out the two spoiled leaves of the twelfth and the thirteenth again at about four because about five of that nine cannot read a column. She read them out of the second book and closed both books and put the first on the flat of the stone and the second back on the shelf, and nobody said the reading was needed and nobody said it was not.

---

At about four the keeper stood at the flat of that bench stone and said out loud that the day carries no line. He said yesterday settled line nine with two days on its face and the foot of the sheet steps from today, and that today writes nothing into it and measures nothing for it, and about nine people heard him and nobody asked him for a figure.

---

The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one. About four people stopped at it on the way to the boards and about four passed it without stopping, and the keeper did not go to it and did not send anybody to it.

---

The register copy of yesterday lies on a table at the west basin office with the hour against it. The office listed it and entered it and paid a day at the figure in chalk and bound nobody, and no name under any mark was read out in any room and none is in any mouth in that shed.

---

The water came up by hand all morning and the reading went out at that bench stone at about half past five on that Thursday in about five hundred and seventy words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had carried water for the washing down.

"**That butt at the back of that market was filled again this morning for the hundred and seventieth time in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven, and was walked on by none of it, and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four. About five people came for water after four and were served without waiting, and I wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that no line goes on the sheet today.**

"**The pump housing came apart and the gaskets were gone over and the bilge was pumped out and sixty yards of rope went back on in a coil a man checked twice. No person put words to the man of about thirty-two in this shed all day and none put them to him at the end stall, and he worked the housing until noon and sat on the far bench from two until four with the second book open, and the page of days carries no hour of an asking against this day and no name against it.**

"**At about three the disagreement was in the open again. She said the second book stays on the shelf because one wet road takes one book, and he said two books with one disagreement is one refusal read twice. She said the mark put yesterday by two hands is one custody and he said a custody two people hold is a custody neither can lift alone. About four agreed with her and about four with him, and this is not settled and no page here settles it.**

"**At about four I stood at the flat of this stone and said this day carries no line and is not one of any form, and today writes nothing into it. The sheet steps from this day to one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, with a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. Under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fifty-one days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That sheet with fourteen gaps stood all day with no figure moved. The register copy of yesterday lies on a table at the west basin office with the hour against it and no name in any mouth here. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with mending and sorting and nothing put on it to be read. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-six days today and nothing charged or mended this afternoon touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a day with no line, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THIS DAY CARRIES NO LINE AND IS NOT ONE OF ANY FORM, AND THE SHEET STEPS FROM THIS DAY TO ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHT THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT. THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED FOR THE HUNDRED AND SEVENTIETH TIME IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE WAS PUT IN THE OPEN IN ABOUT NINE AND IS NOT SETTLED. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-eighth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventieth charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that no line went onto that sheet and no figure was written into its foot, and that the keeper said out loud at about four that the day carries no line and is not one of any form; and that the sheet steps from this day to one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short; and that no person put words to the man of about thirty-two in that shed or at the end stall all day; and that the disagreement between two people was put in the open in about nine at about three and is not settled.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the covers were left lying until four is that water drawn through an open butt after a charge is water nobody has watched, and a yard that watches its charge watches it until the hour it stops watching is written; that at about two the reason the quiet of ordinary work was not written as anything is that a quiet page invites a reader to hear an agreement, and this yard keeps hours and not meanings; that at about three the reason a custody held by two was left standing as one custody is that two hands that cannot lift alone are not two owners but one lock with two marks, and a shed that calls it two owners has turned a working act into a married act; and that at about four the reason the keeper said no line out loud is that a day with nothing on it invites a later hand to write something small into it, and a day said empty out loud stays empty.*
