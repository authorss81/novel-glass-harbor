# Chapter 717

*Thursday the Seventh Day of July, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Sixty-Fourth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, Line Six Settled Into the Foot of That Sheet at About Four, the Total Going Into the Foot of It in the Morning for One Hundred and Sixteen Thousand and Fourteen Pence, Nine People in a Room at the Lowfen Reed With the Yard Not in It and Not Sending Anybody, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Thursday the seventh day of July, with that market open from about nine and a hard bright morning off the water, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-fourth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Line six was settled into the foot of that money sheet at about four in the afternoon and the total at the foot of that sheet is one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence from the morning, being one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence short. Nine people met in a room at the Lowfen Reed in the morning and this yard was not in that room and did not send anybody to it, and two people left that line and went home in the afternoon without saying why to anybody in this party. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and fifteenth day. Four hundred and seventeen days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and fifty-fifth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and twenty-first night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and thirty-ninth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and ninety-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and twenty-sixth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and fifty-seven days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifteen days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixty-fourth time and the covers went on and came off at the same hours. About four people who came for water after four were served without waiting. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the charge number and the hours, and he wrote that a room at the Lowfen Reed met this morning without anybody from this yard in it, because that is where a thing of that kind is done and not here.

---

The five parts of line six were read out loud at about four before the sum went into the foot of the sheet, being four times one hundred and sixty-eight for six hundred and seventy-two pence, and eighty pence, and sixty times three for one hundred and eighty pence, and ninety-six pence, and five times four times sixteen for three hundred and twenty pence. Those five are one thousand three hundred and forty-eight pence, being eight marks, five shillings and eightpence.

The keeper wrote the day against the line in two places, being the fifth of July as the day the work was done and the seventh of July as the day it was settled into the foot, so that a stranger can tell which day a figure arrived on. The total at the foot of that sheet stands at one hundred and fourteen thousand six hundred and sixty-six pence this evening and goes into the foot of it in the morning at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, which is one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence short.

---

About nine people were in a room at the Lowfen Reed from about ten in the morning and the woman of about fifty-eight read their own page out loud in that room twice. Nobody from this yard was there and nobody from this yard sent anybody, and the man of about thirty-two was asked in the morning whether he was going and said he was not, and about four people in that nine said that was right.

What they did in that room is their own and this yard wrote nothing about it until the woman of about fifty-eight came down the road at about three and said one thing, which was that they had agreed to take the time back. She said they had not agreed to say anything about why and would not be asked why, and about four people asked her to be sure and she said she was sure.

---

In the afternoon two people of that line went home. One of them is a man of about twenty-six and one of them is a woman of about thirty-one, and they went by different roads and at different hours, and neither of them said a word to anybody in this party. The keeper wrote the hour each of them went at and no reason against either, and about four people saw the man go and about four saw neither.

Nothing was said about that in the shed. The man of about thirty-two was told at about four and asked nothing about the two of them, and he said that they were theirs and not this yard's, and about four people agreed and about four said nothing.

---

The man who keeps the leaf read out the shortfall at that bench stone at about half past five on that Thursday, in about nine hundred words, to about nine people, four of whom cannot read a column, and about four of those nine had heard one sentence from the woman of about fifty-eight at about three.

"**The covers went on that cistern at about eleven and came off at about four, and about four people who came for water after four were served without waiting, and that was the hundred and sixty-fourth filling of it. Nine people met in a room at the Lowfen Reed at about ten this morning and read their own page out loud twice, and this yard was not in that room and did not send anybody, and the man of about thirty-two was asked whether he was going and said he was not.**

"**At about three the woman of about fifty-eight came down the road and said one thing, which is that they have agreed to take the time back, and that they have not agreed to say why and will not be asked why. Two people of that line went home this afternoon, a man of about twenty-six by one road and a woman of about thirty-one by another, and neither of them said a word to anybody in this party, and I have written the hour each went at and no reason against either.**

"**Line six was settled into the foot of the sheet at about four, being four times one hundred and sixty-eight for six hundred and seventy-two, and eighty for coal and oil, and sixty times three for one hundred and eighty of rope, and ninety-six for tin and copper and gaskets, and five times four times sixteen for three hundred and twenty of men, and those five are one thousand three hundred and forty-eight pence, being eight marks, five shillings and eightpence. The day the work was done is on the line and the day it was settled is on the line, and the sheet stands this evening at one hundred and fourteen thousand six hundred and sixty-six and goes into the foot of it in the morning at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence out against ten thousand six hundred and forty in, which is one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four short. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street and did not move today.**

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty days without one.**

"**And the last of it. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifteen days today and nothing settled this afternoon touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a room that was kept, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had nineteen lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THAT CISTERN WAS CHARGED IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS THE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOURTH CHARGE. NINE PEOPLE MET IN A ROOM AT THE LOWFEN REED AND READ THEIR OWN PAGE OUT LOUD TWICE AND THIS YARD WAS NOT IN THAT ROOM AND SENT NOBODY TO IT. TWO PEOPLE OF THAT LINE WENT HOME WITHOUT SAYING WHY TO ANYBODY IN THIS PARTY AND ONLY THE HOUR OF EACH IS WRITTEN. LINE SIX IS SETTLED INTO THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET AND THE TOTAL GOES IN IN THE MORNING AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND AND FOURTEEN PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIVE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the seventh day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-fourth charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that about nine people met in a room at the Lowfen Reed from about ten in the morning and read that community's own page out loud twice, and that this yard was not in that room and did not send anybody to it and was not asked to; and that at about three the woman of about fifty-eight said that they have agreed to take the time back and have not agreed to say why and will not be asked why; and that a man of about twenty-six and a woman of about thirty-one of that line went home in the afternoon by different roads, with the hour of each written and no reason against either; and that line six was settled into the foot of that sheet at about four, being four times one hundred and sixty-eight pence for six hundred and seventy-two pence and eighty pence for coal and oil and sixty times three pence for one hundred and eighty pence of rope and ninety-six pence for tin and copper and gaskets and five times four times sixteen pence for three hundred and twenty pence of men, being one thousand three hundred and forty-eight pence, being eight marks, five shillings and eightpence, with the fifth of July against it as the day the work was done and the seventh against it as the day of the settlement.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason this yard wrote nothing about that room is that a page of the yard saying what a room decided is the first step toward a yard deciding it, and nine communities cannot hold a time that this shed has already written about; that at about three the reason the woman of about fifty-eight was not asked twice is that the second time a yard asks is the time the answer stops being hers and starts being theirs; that at about four the reason the hour and nothing else is entered for two people who went home is that the hour is what this shed saw and the reason is not and a yard that writes the reason is a yard that keeps a roll; and that at about half past four the reason the settlement carries both days on the line is that a stranger counting these days has to be able to tell a day of work from a day of entry without asking anybody who works here.*
