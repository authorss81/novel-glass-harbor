# Chapter 753

*Friday the Twelfth Day of August, an Ordinary Working Day and Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Public Copy Carried Down That Lane at About Two in an Open Cart With a Man of About Thirty-Six Driving and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Walking Beside Him and the Copy Going Up on the Wall of the Witness House at About Four With What Is Not Known Written on a Board Beside It in the Same Hand and the Same Size, That House Being One Room Where a Visitor Can Hear the Scene and Read the Record Around It, the Sheet That Describes a Made Thing Not Carried and Not Used and the Sealed Record Under Two Seals and Not Opened, the Far Bench Occupied From Ten Until Four and Nothing Put on It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Four*

Friday the twelfth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a fine morning with the wind gone into the west and a smooth sea under it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. The public copy went up on the wall of the witness house at about four in the afternoon, with what is not known written on a board beside it in the same hand and the same size, and the sealed record stayed under two seals and was not opened. That sheet was not carried down that lane and was not used to decide where any boat goes, and it is in the inside pocket of a coat in a room over a fish market and it says on its own face that it is not the thing it describes. No line went onto that money sheet and no figure went into its foot. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand eight hundred and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand two hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine of the standing things were carried a day further and stood where this morning left them. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and fifty-first day. Four hundred and fifty-three days have gone by with nobody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown stand in the seven hundred and ninety-first day. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-seventh night running. Those two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventy-fifth days with nothing set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and thirtieth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and forty-fifth. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and sixty-second day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and ninety-third days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-one days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cart went down that lane at about two with a man of about thirty-six driving it and the woman of thirty of no office walking at the near wheel, and about four of that nine walked at the far wheel because there is one handle on that cart and it is the wrong side for two.

The copy went in three pieces and tied in oilcloth and one of the three pieces was the ferry's first minutes as anybody could read them, and the other two were the hours of nine places in nine hands, and the keeper went with that cart and wrote the hour it went out and the hour it came back and wrote no reason against either hour.

---

The witness house is one room over a chandler's store at the head of that lane with a door that stands open and a bench along the wall and nothing on that bench but a board.

A man of about thirty-three who keeps that room said out loud at about half past three that the reason that room is where it is and not a room over a fish market is that a person can come in off the lane without saying anything to anybody and sit down, and that from that bench a person can hear what is said in the room and can read what is on the wall of it without asking a single one of the nine people who keep it.

---

The woman of thirty of no office put the copy up herself at about four, and she used four nails and no more, and she put it up level and she stepped back and looked at it from the door and then came back and took one nail out and moved the left edge up a quarter of an inch and put the nail back.

She said out loud that she had put it up at the height a person of about five foot four could read without standing on anything, and that she had not asked any of the nine people who keep that room where they wanted it, because a record of theirs hung in a room of theirs and not in a room of hers.

---

Beside the copy on the same wall she put up a second board, and she wrote on it herself, in her own hand and in the same size as the copy's own heading, what is not known and what is not to be taken as known.

It says that the ferry's manifest was altered in the spring of a year that is not written on that board, and that it is not known whose hand altered it, and that it is not known at whose instance, and that it is not known with what intent, and that no person has been charged with it and that no person has been cleared of it.

---

That board also says that this room is not the whole of what is known, and that the room is what a person can hear and read and touch on a day, and that a person who comes in here is not to take the wall for the coast.

Nobody in that room at about half past four told anybody standing at that door that the scene was the whole truth and nobody said it was not the whole truth, and the keeper wrote the words on that board out at the door of that shed in the evening and wrote them again in the first column of that day with the hour against them.

---

The sealed record was in its case in a room on a hill and was not opened and was not moved and was not named, and two marks were on the outside of that case where two people put them there on the twenty-seventh of July, and no third mark went on it and no person went up that hill.

The two who put those marks are not asked about them in any chapter of this harbor and are not asked about them today, and one of them is the woman of thirty of no office and the other is a woman of about thirty-three with a watch on her own arm who did not come down that road today.

---

At about eleven the keeper read the hours at the door of that shed and read nothing out of that leaf today and read it no day of this harbor's run of days except the last one, and wrote on the face of that page that the read-back of that leaf is for the last day of this run and not for any other.

The third column of that page was empty and unappointed when it was read at about eleven and was empty and unappointed when it was read at half past five and the boy's hand of twenty-two was at the head of it and he was off the water and his surname is on no page.

---

The far bench was occupied from ten until four this day by a man of about sixty-one with his hands black to the second joint at the nails, and nothing was put on it and read as a gift, and nobody sent for him and nobody thanked him and he asked for nothing.

The man of about thirty-two did not go over to that bench at any hour of that day, and a man of about sixty-one came down to that shed on a Wednesday and not on this day, and nothing about that Wednesday was known to anybody in that yard this morning.

---

At about one the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine people that he had put no hand to a sheet in this yard in two days and had been asked to put his hand to nothing, and that he did not know what nine people agreeing with each other in one room was going to do to a coast, and the woman of thirty of no office said that was not a thing a page could be asked and that she had not asked it.

About four agreed with her and about four said nothing at all, and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open and no page in that shed carries a resolution of it.

---

The ordinary work of that Friday went on and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine, sixty yards of new rope going out on the west slip and coming back wet, a boat of about six tons hauled up on the east slip with her garboard off and two men under her from about one, and the fourth lay tied twice in that smooth sea.

The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the ladder went up that wall behind that bench at about a quarter to twelve and came down at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, that wall not being due to come down bare before next Sunday.

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two the reason that copy went down that lane in an open cart and not in a closed one is that a copy carried shut is a copy being kept from somebody, and the whole of what that copy is for is that a person in a room with no office can put her hand flat on it; that at about four the reason the board of what is not known is put beside that copy and not kept in a book is that a board on a wall is read by a person who came in off a lane and has not asked anybody anything, and that the day a thing stops being said out loud is the day it begins to be the whole of what is there; that at about four the reason she took a nail out and moved that copy up a quarter of an inch is that a record put up by the person who keeps it says something about the person who keeps it, and a record is the only thing in this harbor that gets to say that; and that at about one the reason the man of about thirty-two said he did not know what nine people agreeing would do to a coast is that a man who has spent a month refusing one voice for nine has no answer ready for nine voices agreeing, and a man with no answer ready says so in front of people and does not go and find one afterwards in a room with two in it.*
