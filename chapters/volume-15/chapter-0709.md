# Chapter 709

*Wednesday the Twenty-Ninth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, About Two Minutes on Two of the Nine Bells at About Half Past Four, the Morrow Line Ringing Though Dormant as Its Own Right, a Young Man's Voice With Water in It and a Bell Going Elsewhere, Two Mismatches Against the Account in That Front Street Written Down and Nothing About Meaning, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Forty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand and Eighty-Four*

Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of June, with a wet afternoon and the mole shining from rain, a boat's day cut short by weather, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. About two minutes came in on two of the nine bells at about half past four in the afternoon, and about four people on that mole heard it and about two of those four have heard the public copy before and about two have not. Nothing went onto the money sheet before Friday and line four carries the day the work was done against it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and seventh day. Four hundred and nine days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and forty-seventh day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and thirteenth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and thirty-first days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and eighty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and first. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eighteenth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and forty-nine days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and seven days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The two bells are the Morrow Line's and the Saltmarch Weatherway's, and the Morrow Line is dormant and is ringing anyway, which is that route's own right and not this yard's business. The keeper entered it in the second column as what it is, with the hour against it, and he did not call it a reopening and did not call it a count. About four people on that mole heard the two minutes at about half past four, and the sound carried through rain and stopped without fading the way a bell note stops when a hand leaves a rope.

It is a young man's voice and there is water in it and a bell going somewhere else, and it is not a warning and it is not a message and it does not ask anybody for anything. It runs about two minutes on each of the two bells, and the two soundings overlap without matching, the way two boats signalling in fog overlap without matching. About two of the four who heard it have heard the public copy before in a room over a fish market, and about two have not, and all four agreed afterwards on the length and on nothing else.

---

The woman of thirty of no office had the account that has been in that front street since the fourteenth of August open on the boards behind her. She read three lines of it against what about four people said they had heard, and the order does not match and the place does not match. The account puts the bell before the water, and what came in puts water round the voice before any bell is heard. The account puts the scene at the head of the mole, and what came in has wind and distance in it that belong further out.

She wrote the two mismatches down in her own book with the hour against them and wrote nothing about what the voice had been doing or why. About four people wanted somebody to say what it meant, and nobody in that shed said he understood it. The man of about thirty-two stood at the back through the whole of it and said nothing at all, and the first column carries that he was there and nothing about what he thought. A meaning put on two minutes by a yard that did not ring either bell would be a claim on another community's page, and this yard makes no such claim.

---

At about five the rain stopped and the mole dried in patches. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the Wednesday line of the page of that week with the two bells and the two mismatches against it, and he wrote that nobody said the man in that voice knew what he was doing. That last is entered because about four people wanted somebody to say it, and a page that leaves out what people wanted said is a page that hides the shape of the wanting.

Nobody thanked anybody for listening, and nobody thanked anybody for writing. The wooden tally slate lay flat through all of it, and the page of that week had eleven lines by the close of the day.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about nine hundred words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been on that mole at about half past four.

"**About two minutes came in on two bells at about half past four, being the Morrow Line's and the Saltmarch Weatherway's, and the Morrow Line is dormant and is ringing anyway, which is that route's own right and not this yard's business. About four on that mole heard it and about two of those four have heard the public copy before and about two have not. It is a young man's voice and there is water in it and a bell going elsewhere, and it is not a warning and it is not a message and it does not ask anybody for anything.**

"**The woman of thirty of no office had the account from that front street open on the boards and she read three lines of it against what about four people said they had heard, and the order does not match and the place does not match. She wrote the two mismatches down in her own book with the hour against them and wrote nothing about what the voice had been doing or why. Nobody in that shed said he understood it and about four people wanted somebody to.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and thirteen thousand two hundred and ten pence out, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is one hundred and two thousand five hundred and seventy pence short. Line four carries the day the work was done against it and is settled on Friday. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and twenty-two days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four and nobody walked on the water. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and seven days today and nothing on those bells touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for hearing, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had eleven lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**ABOUT TWO MINUTES CAME IN ON TWO OF THE NINE BELLS AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR, BEING THE MORROW LINE'S THOUGH DORMANT AND THE SALTMARCH WEATHERWAY'S, AND ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE HEARD IT. IT IS A YOUNG MAN'S VOICE WITH WATER IN IT AND A BELL GOING ELSEWHERE, NOT A WARNING AND NOT A MESSAGE. TWO MISMATCHES AGAINST THE ACCOUNT IN THAT FRONT STREET ARE WRITTEN DOWN WITH THE HOUR AGAINST THEM, AND NOTHING ABOUT MEANING IS WRITTEN AND NOBODY SAID THE MAN IN THAT VOICE KNEW WHAT HE WAS DOING. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-ninth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that about two minutes came in on two of the nine bells at about half past four, being the Morrow Line's and the Saltmarch Weatherway's, with the Morrow Line dormant and ringing as its own right, heard by about four people on that mole; and that it is a young man's voice with water in it and a bell going elsewhere, not a warning and not a message and asking nothing of anybody; and that the woman of thirty of no office read three lines of the account in that front street against what was heard, and that the order does not match and the place does not match, and that she wrote the two mismatches in her own book with the hour and wrote nothing of meaning; and that nobody in that shed said he understood it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past four the reason a dormant bell ringing is entered as a right is that a route's own bell rung by its own people is that route's to ring, and this party calling it a reopening would be putting a state on another community's page; that at about five the reason two mismatches are written and no meaning is that timing and point of view can be checked by four people who heard, and meaning cannot be checked by anybody who did not ring; that at about five the reason the silence of the man of about thirty-two is entered is that a man who says nothing while four want meaning is holding a line, and a page that leaves him out would read as if he were not there; and that at about half past five the reason the slip ends on the nine hulls is that two minutes on two bells is a thing this harbor will talk about, and the hulls that made no sound are the thing that stayed silent while it talked.*
