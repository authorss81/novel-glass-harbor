# Chapter 736

*Tuesday the Twenty-Sixth Day of July, the Ninety-First Wage Tuesday With the Column Unmoved at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks, a Held Line Pulled at About Eleven Minutes Against Twenty-Five by the Woman With the Watch Before Full Synchronization and He Surfaced and Was Not Fine, One Entry Into a Stored Testimony and Not a Living Person in About Nineteen Fathoms, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Sixty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Nineteen*

Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of July, with that market open from about nine and a pale morning with the flood running late by the landing, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The ninety-first wage Tuesday and not a rota day and not a wipe, and the column on the lid of that step read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and standing at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and not moved on that day, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it shut from ten until four, and a further week standing fallen due at the close of it. A held line was pulled at about eleven minutes against a fixed air limit of twenty-five minutes worth of capacity by the woman with the watch on the surface, before the route reached full synchronization, and he surfaced and was not fine. The reason was written down with an hour against it before anybody went down, and the tender stood on the surface with an independent line and a cutoff she can pull without asking. Line nine carries two days on its face, being the twenty-fourth for the work and this day for the day the descent was stopped, and it is not settled this day. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine tallies of work left undone moved a day forward on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and thirty-fourth day. Four hundred and thirty-six days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and seventy-fourth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fortieth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and fifty-eighth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and thirteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and twenty-eighth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and forty-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and seventy-sixth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-four days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The column was read at about ten in four minutes and the chest stayed shut as it stays shut on every wage Tuesday. The man of about thirty-one of the second lay read the twenty-six lines and the one hundred and four marks and shut the lid again, and a further week stood fallen due at the close of the reading.

---

The reason went on the page at about eight in the morning with an hour against it before anybody went down. The keeper wrote that one person would go down at the slack before the flood turned, held by the woman of about thirty-three with a watch, against twenty-five minutes worth of capacity, and that she holds the cutoff and pulls it without asking. One person at a time went on the page, and nobody twice in a day, and nobody at the top of a flood, and an ascent asked for not argued with, and no money figure against it.

---

Nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two twice this day about going down, and nobody put them once, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for this Tuesday. What was written at about eight was written because he had said on the Monday before that he would go, and not because anybody asked him a second time, and about nine people saw the reason go down and none of them spoke the words again. The keeper wrote no hour for an asking because no asking was made, and the quiet around the page settled nothing.

---

The boat left the landing at about half past nine with nine men aboard beside the diver and the tender gear made fast amidships. The woman of about thirty-three stood on the surface deck with the independent line in her own hand and the watch where she can see it, and the spare glass lay in its wrap beside her hand.

---

He went down on the line at about ten and the tender paid the line out arm over arm and kept her other hand on the cutoff. About eleven minutes against the twenty-five passed with the bell on the buoy answering the bell on the landing, and before the route reached full synchronization the tender pulled the cutoff without asking anybody, and the independent line took the weight and nobody argued with it.

---

What came up was stored testimony and not a living person, and no person on that boat spoke inside it. The diver heard the first minutes as a record carries them, with timing and with perspective and without any command laid upon him, and the tender kept hauling until his head broke the surface. He surfaced and was not fine, and the cost came in the same hour on the same deck, being pressure pain across the shoulders and a shared aftertaste and a losing of personal certainty that held him on the rail with both hands, and no figure of money stands against any part of it and it was not softened. On the deck he sat with his back against the rail for the better part of an hour while the woman of about thirty-three stayed beside him, and about four men heard the pulls had changed and about four were busy with the gear and none said the pull had been early or late.

---

It settled nothing and it proved two things only, and no third thing was proved on that deck. It proved that a conscious person held on an independent line can be withdrawn before full synchronization by a tender acting alone, and it proved that such a withdrawal leaves the diver not fine with the cost in the same hour.

---

The disagreement was in that shed again at about four in about nine people, and it is open between the two people and no page carries a resolution of it. He said the second book would print a refusal twice and that a pull taken alone would be printed twice if it went in both, and she said what goes in both books is what she was told and never a name against a person and that a pull by one hand is one pull in two places when it is written twice. He said he knew what he had been objecting to in June and that he had not moved from it, and she said she knew that too and that she was still keeping the second book and still holding one of the two marks to come.

---

That held line was pulled before the route came together and at about half past five on that Tuesday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about six hundred and seventy-two words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had watched a boat come back to the landing at about noon with a man holding the rail with both hands.

"**The coil on that pin lay ready from first light with the independent line flaked beside it, and the column on the lid of that step was read at about ten in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and the chest stayed shut from ten until four and a further week stood fallen due at the close of it. Nobody asked for the reading a second time, and I wrote the day and the hour and that the column had not moved.**

"**The reason went on the page at about eight before anybody went down, being one person at the slack before the flood turned, held by the woman of about thirty-three with a watch on the surface against twenty-five minutes worth of capacity, with a cutoff she pulls without asking and without waiting for any other hand, and one person at a time and nobody twice in a day and nobody at the top of a flood and an ascent asked for not argued with, and no figure of money against any part of it. Nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two twice this day about going down, and nobody put them once, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for it.**

"**The boat left at about half past nine and he went over the side at about ten in about nineteen fathoms off that place with the flood not yet turned, and the tender paid the line out arm over arm with her other hand on the cutoff and the spare glass beside her hand. At about eleven minutes against the twenty-five she pulled the cutoff without asking anybody before the route reached full synchronization, and the independent line took the weight and the ascent began at once and nobody argued with it. What came up was stored testimony and not a living person, and nobody was put inside a signal and no command was laid upon him.**

"**He surfaced and was not fine, and the cost came in the same scene and in the same hour, being pressure pain and a shared aftertaste and a losing of personal certainty that held him on the rail with both hands for the better part of an hour, and no figure of money stands against any part of it and nobody was thanked for it and it was not softened. It settled nothing and proved two things only, being that a tender acting alone can withdraw a conscious person before full synchronization, and that such a withdrawal leaves the diver not fine with the cost in the same hour carried by nobody else, and no third thing was proved on that deck.**

"**And the last of it. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and forty-nine days without one. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-four days today and nothing pulled or surfaced this Tuesday touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a pull taken alone, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A HELD LINE PULLED AT ABOUT ELEVEN MINUTES AGAINST TWENTY-FIVE BY THE WOMAN WITH THE WATCH BEFORE FULL SYNCHRONIZATION, AND HE SURFACED AND WAS NOT FINE WITH THE COST IN THE SAME SCENE. IT SETTLED NOTHING AND PROVED TWO THINGS ONLY. NOBODY PUT THE SAME WORDS TO THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO TWICE THIS DAY. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVEN THOUSAND AND NINETY-SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-sixth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was the ninety-first wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe, and that the column on the lid of that step was read at about ten in four minutes at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and was not moved, with the chest shut from ten until four and a further week fallen due; and that a held line was pulled at about eleven minutes against twenty-five by the woman with the watch before full synchronization, and he surfaced and was not fine with the cost in the same scene; and that what came up was stored testimony and not a living person in about nineteen fathoms, with one person at a time and nobody twice in a day and nobody at the top of a flood and an ascent not argued with; and that nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two twice on that day about going down, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for it; and that the disagreement between two people about what a page prints was in that shed at about four and is not settled and no page carries a resolution of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eight the reason the reason was written before anybody went down is that a descent without a written reason is a descent nobody can answer for after, and the hour has to stand before the line goes out; that at about ten the reason the tender kept one hand on the cutoff is that a cutoff a tender must find in haste is a cutoff lost, and a hand that stays on it pulls without asking; that at about eleven the reason the ascent was not argued with is that an ascent argued with is an ascent held down, and a line held down after a pull is a line nobody is tending; and that at about noon the reason he sat with his back against the rail is that a diver who is not fine and stands too soon falls against gear, and a deck that lets him sit keeps the cost where it fell.*
