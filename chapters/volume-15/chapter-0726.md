# Chapter 726

*Saturday the Sixteenth Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Count of Those Who Went Home Taken Off a Page of Days at About Half Past Three and Written Down Once and Said Nowhere, Being Zero and Zero and One and Zero and Two and Zero and One and Zero and Three and Zero and Zero and One and Zero and Three, Being Eleven in Sum, the Sheet at One Hundred and Sixteen Thousand Seven Hundred and Forty-Six Pence Out and One Hundred and Six Thousand One Hundred and Six Pence Short, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Saturday the sixteenth day of July, with that market open from about nine and the boards at the back of it busy by eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. The count of those who went home was taken off a page of days at about half past three and written down once and said nowhere, being zero and zero and one and zero and two and zero and one and zero and three and zero and zero and one and zero and three, and eleven is the sum of those fourteen figures. It is a count of departures and not a count of refusals and not a count of disagreements and not a count of people who were wrong, and it is not said in a room and it is not in this slip. The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and twenty-fourth day. Four hundred and twenty-six days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and sixty-fourth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and thirtieth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and forty-eighth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and eighteenth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and thirty-fifth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and sixty-sixth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-four days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The work was ordinary and it went on all day. The boat's sail was bent on and unbent again in the wind and the sea lead was tried against the harbour side and found to agree with the harbour side. About four people from the boards at the back of that market came down about eleven and stood at the end stall until about one, and none of them came into the shed.

The bell off that mole answered the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road at about half past ten this morning and answered again at about three in the afternoon, and the two answers were in hours and not in names.

---

At about half past three the man who keeps the leaf took the page of days off its nail. On that page the hands that rang stand down the side in the order they are always read in, and that column has not been touched since the second of July and stands with thirty against it, and it was not taken again today and is not taken again in the next nine runs of days.

Beside it there is a column headed WENT HOME, and it has been running at nothing against every day since the last week of the winter when that page was begun, and this afternoon he set the fourteen days of this fortnight down whole against the fourteen days.

He put the day and the hour against the sum and did not say the sum out loud, and nobody in that shed asked him for it, and it is not in this slip and it is not in the first column in words.

---

The figures against the fourteen days are zero and zero and one and zero and two and zero and one and zero and three and zero and zero and one and zero and three, and eleven is the sum of them. One of those figures is against the fifth of this month, two against the seventh, one against the ninth, three against the eleventh, one against the fourteenth and three against the sixteenth, and the other eight days stand at nothing against them.

About nine people were in that shed at half past three because they come at that hour, and about four of them can read a column and about four of them cannot, and the figures were not read out, and the reason they were not read out is in the second column.

---

A person who put a hand on that page and went home is on this column once and on no other sheet of this yard. The one who went down the road on the fifth is one figure, and the two who went on the seventh are two more and no day carries one of them twice, and the one who left that room on the ninth, the three who went on the eleventh, the woman who went up the road on the fourteenth and three of the people who came down off those boards this afternoon are the other eight, and eleven is the whole of the column. None of them is on any other paper here, and the one who wrote the hours of their going did not write one word about why.

The woman of thirty of no office looked at the column at about four and said out loud that eleven is a small number of people to have lost off nine lines in a fortnight and that nobody in that shed should try to make it a smaller one or a larger one by telling the story twice. About four people agreed and about four said nothing, and the man of about thirty-two said nothing at all about it, and nobody thanked anybody for the taking of it.

---

A column headed WENT HOME was set down whole and at about half past five on that Saturday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone, in about five hundred and ninety-two words, to about nine people, four of whom cannot read a column, and about four of those nine had been standing at the flat of that stone at about half past three.

"**The sail was bent on and unbent again in that wind and the sea lead was tried against the harbour side and found to agree with the harbour side, and that bell off that mole answered the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road at about half past ten and again at about three, and the two answers are in hours and not in names. About four people came down from the boards at about eleven and stood at the end stall until about one and none of them came into the shed.**

"**At about half past three I took the page of days off its nail. The column of the hands that rang stands down the side with thirty against it and has not been touched since the second of July, and it was not taken again today and is not taken again in the next nine runs of days. Beside it is a column headed WENT HOME, which has stood at nothing against every day since the end of the winter, and this afternoon I set the fourteen days of this fortnight down whole. I put the day and the hour against the sum and did not say the sum out loud and nobody asked me for it, and it is not in this slip and not in the first column in words. The reason I did not say it is that a thing said a second time in a room stops being a count and becomes a sentence nine people own, and this is a count and it is taken once.**

"**The woman of thirty of no office looked at the column at about four and said out loud that eleven is a small number of people to have lost off nine lines in a fortnight and that nobody in that shed should try to make it a smaller one or a larger one by telling the story twice. About four agreed and about four said nothing, and the man of about thirty-two said nothing at all about it, and nobody was thanked for the taking of it.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty-nine days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-four days today and nothing counted this afternoon touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a column of departures, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-eight lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THE COUNT OF THOSE WHO WENT HOME WAS TAKEN OFF A PAGE OF DAYS AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE AND WRITTEN DOWN ONCE AND SAID NOWHERE, BEING ZERO AND ZERO AND ONE AND ZERO AND TWO AND ZERO AND ONE AND ZERO AND THREE AND ZERO AND ZERO AND ONE AND ZERO AND THREE, AND ELEVEN IS THE SUM. IT IS A COUNT OF DEPARTURES AND NOT A COUNT OF REFUSALS AND NOT A COUNT OF DISAGREEMENTS AND NOT A COUNT OF PEOPLE WHO WERE WRONG. THE COLUMN OF THE HANDS THAT RANG STANDS AT THIRTY AND WAS NOT TAKEN AGAIN. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the sixteenth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that that bell off the mole answered the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road at about half past ten in the morning and again at about three in the afternoon, with the two answers in hours and not in names; and that at about half past three the page of days was taken off its nail and the column headed went home was set down whole against the fourteen days of this fortnight, being zero and zero and one and zero and two and zero and one and zero and three and zero and zero and one and zero and three, and that eleven is the sum of those fourteen figures; and that the day and the hour were put against the sum and that the sum was not said out loud and is not in this slip and not in this column in words; and that the column of the hands that rang stands with thirty against it and was not taken again on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past three the reason the sum was written and not said is that a count said twice in a room stops being a count and becomes a sentence nine people own, and that this is the second of the three counts this yard has made since the winter and the first of them has been written once and not said; that at about four the reason nobody was thanked for the taking of it is that a column of departures is not a thing anybody did well, and a yard that says a man ought to be thanked for counting the people who left has turned a count into an account that could be argued with; that at about half past four the reason no sheet carries a name beside any of the fourteen figures is that a person who put a hand on this page and went home is on this column once and on no other paper here, and the keeper of this leaf writes the hour of a thing he saw and not the reason of a thing he was not told; and that at about five the reason the three columns of the page of routes were not counted today is that their count is written on the last of the sixty days and on thirteen days after this one, and this fortnight is not one of them.*
