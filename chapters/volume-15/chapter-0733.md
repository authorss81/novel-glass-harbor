# Chapter 733

*Saturday the Twenty-Third Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Finding About a Moment and Not About a Man Paid Out of a Sheet of Figures in a Working Place to About Nine People, the Plan Changed in the Open So That No Testimony Stands as the Centre and Every Route Enters With Its Own Provenance and Limits, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Sixty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Four*

Saturday the twenty-third day of July, with that market open from about nine and a close morning with the wind standing light off the water, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. The finding of the week was paid out of a sheet of figures in a working place to about nine people, and not out of any speech, and the sheet says the finding is about a moment and not about a man. The plan was changed in the open on the same day so that no testimony stands as the centre of the braid and every route enters with its own provenance and its own limits. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine tallies of matters put by ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past comes onto its four hundred and thirty-first day. Four hundred and thirty-three days have moved by with nobody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown move onto their seven hundred and seventy-first day. That bench under that pump has lain bare for the five hundred and thirty-seventh night. Two lights about a mile off that point have kept three hundred and fifty-five days with no figure entered against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has arrived at its three hundred and tenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street comes onto its three hundred and twenty-fifth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has advanced to its three hundred and forty-second day. A man of about fifty-one has held three hundred and seventy-three days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-one days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The morning work was short because the sheets were wanted on the bench stone by ten. About four men swept the yard and wiped the flat of the stone dry. The public copy of first minutes was laid on the left and the sheet off the independent pressure bell on the right. By about half past nine the stones were on the corners and about nine people were standing within hearing.

---

At about ten a man of about sixty with rope marks across his palms came down from the boards and stood at the flat of that bench stone beside the man of about thirty-two. He put to him the same words that had been put in the week, being whether he will go down. The man of about thirty-two said he had heard the words and that he keeps no hour for an answer in that shed. The keeper wrote the hour and no name against it, and the older man went back to the boards without more speech.

---

At about eleven the keeper laid a rule of wood along the two sheets so that hour stood beside hour down the middle weeks. He read the hour of the public evacuation bell and then the mark of the live keel-line at the cut, and about nine followed the rule with their eyes. He read the mark of the second rescue line beside the cut, and the mark showed still active at that hour. About four people breathed out at the sight and about four stood still, and nobody moved the stones.

---

He read the gloved hand next, being a turn toward that line entered on the public copy before the cut. He laid a finger beside the entry without touching the paper and read the words exactly as written. The two together show the turn came before the cut and after the public bell had sounded.

---

At about noon a woman of about fifty of that row came to the end stall with mending in her hands. She waited until the man of about thirty-two came up for water and she put to him the same words, being whether he will go down. He said he had heard the words and that he keeps no hour for an answer in that shed. She said she had spoken so that her own people would know she had spoken, and she went back to her mending. The keeper wrote the hour and no name when he was told at about half past noon.

---

The keeper said in about nine people that the trapped workers below were brought clear through the cut, and that the same cut may also have blocked another chance for others. He said the record shows the saving and shows the blocking may stand beside it, and that the record cannot establish whether he understood the cost. He said no paper in that shed makes the turn into a command, and that an uncertain choice bound to its hour cannot be laid as a centre. About nine heard it and nobody asked for it twice.

---

At about one the woman of thirty of no office stood at the bench stone and said the sheet itself says the finding is about a moment and not about a man. She said the moment holds a sounded bell and an active line and a turned hand and a cut. The keeper wrote her words on a slip beside the sheets and not on either sheet.

---

At about two a man of about thirty of the third lay came to the shed mouth and put to the man of about thirty-two the same words. The man of about thirty-two said he had heard and keeps no hour for an answer. The keeper wrote the hour and no name, and each putting stood on its own hour.

---

At about three one more person in that shed was asked by about four to put the same words a second time, and refused. The person said there had been hearing enough for one day and that another putting would turn hearing into urging. The keeper wrote the hour of the refusal and no name against it on the page of days, and about nine heard the writing done. About four said the refusal was right and about four said nothing, and the refusal stands with its hour and no name.

---

At about half past three the plan was changed in the open in about nine people. The man of about thirty-two said no testimony shall stand as the centre of the braid from this day, and that every route shall enter with its own provenance and limits. The keeper wrote the change on a slip and read it back twice for about five who cannot read a column.

---

The bell off that mole answered the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road at about half past ten and again at about three, and both answers were in hours and not in names. About nine people waited at the end stall until about ten past six and then went home, and the keeper wrote the hour they stopped waiting and no reason against it. The stones stayed on the corners until about five and then the sheets were lifted and put on the shelf above that butt.

---

At about half past five on that Saturday the finding and the change were read out at that bench stone in about nine hundred words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood at that stone since about half past nine in the morning.

"**A rule of wood lay along two sheets from half past nine until five, with the public copy on the left and the pressure bell sheet on the right. I read the hour of the public evacuation bell and then the mark of the live keel-line at the cut. The bell had already sounded at that hour. I read the mark of the second rescue line beside the cut, and it showed still active at that hour. I read the turn of a gloved hand toward that line before the cut, exactly as written, without touching the paper.**

"**The trapped workers below were brought clear through the cut, and the same cut may also have blocked another chance for others. The record shows the saving and shows the blocking may stand beside it. The record cannot establish whether he understood the cost. No paper here makes that turn into a command, and an uncertain choice bound to its hour cannot be laid as a centre. The sheet beside the rule says the finding is about a moment and not about a man, and I wrote that saying on a slip beside the sheets and not on either sheet.**

"**The plan was changed in the open at about half past three in about nine people. No testimony shall stand as the centre of the braid from this day. Every route shall enter with its own provenance and its own limits, and her books shall carry both beside each entry from this day. I wrote the change on a slip and read it back twice for about five who cannot read a column, and about four repeated it back, and the public copy stayed on the left and the pressure sheet on the right and neither was marked.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and forty-six days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-one days today and nothing found on two sheets touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a rule laid straight between two sheets, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THE FINDING WAS PAID OUT OF A SHEET OF FIGURES TO ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND NOT OUT OF ANY SPEECH. THE PUBLIC BELL HAD SOUNDED AND THE SECOND LINE SHOWED STILL ACTIVE WHEN THE LIVE LINE WAS CUT, AND A GLOVED HAND TURNED TOWARD THAT LINE BEFORE THE CUT, AND THE RECORD CANNOT ESTABLISH WHETHER THE COST WAS UNDERSTOOD. HE SAVED TRAPPED WORKERS AND MAY ALSO HAVE BLOCKED ANOTHER CHANCE. THE FINDING IS ABOUT A MOMENT AND NOT ABOUT A MAN. NO TESTIMONY STANDS AS THE CENTRE FROM THIS DAY AND EVERY ROUTE ENTERS WITH ITS OWN PROVENANCE AND LIMITS. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVEN THOUSAND AND NINETY-SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-third day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the public copy of first minutes and the sheet off an independent pressure bell lay side by side under stones from about half past nine until about five, and that the hour of the public evacuation bell and the mark of the live line at the cut and the mark of the second rescue line were read hour beside hour; and that the public bell had already sounded and the second line showed still active at the hour of the cut, and that a gloved hand turned toward that line before the cut, and that the record cannot establish whether the cost was understood; and that the trapped workers were brought clear and another chance may have been blocked beside it; and that at about half past three the plan was changed in the open so that no testimony stands as the centre and every route enters with its own provenance and limits; and that at about three a refusal to put the same words a second time was written with the hour and no name.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason a rule of wood was laid along the two sheets is that hours set edge to edge show early and late without a hand between them, and a finger kept off the paper keeps the paper as it was found; that at about noon the reason the saving and the blocking were said together is that a cut that clears one way and shuts another is one cut with two burdens, and saying one without the other would make a moment into a man; that at about one the reason the saying about a moment was written on a slip beside the sheets is that a finding written on either sheet would mark that sheet as holding it, and a slip beside holds without marking; and that at about three the reason the refusal stands with its hour and no name is that a person who will not urge a second time has done work for this yard, and work without a name is still work the page can keep.*
