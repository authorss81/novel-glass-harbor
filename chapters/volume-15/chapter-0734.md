# Chapter 734

*Sunday the Twenty-Fourth Day of July, a Wipe and the Hundred and Sixteenth Time at Thirty-Three Off and Twenty Bare and Thirty-Three Back Being Eighty-Six the Whole of It, Line Nine Worked This Day With Tuesday the Twenty-Sixth on Its Face as the Day the Descent Was Stopped and Named Out Loud Before Any Figure Went Down and Not Settled This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Sixty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Nine*

Sunday the twenty-fourth day of July, with that market shut until nine and a soft grey morning coming off the road by ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and that wall behind that bench stood bare from about half past ten until the covers went back at the angle they have held since winter, being the hundred and sixteenth time at thirty-three off and twenty bare and thirty-three back and eighty-six the whole of it. Line nine was worked this day for one descent, carrying two days on its face, being Sunday the twenty-fourth day of July for the work and Tuesday the twenty-sixth for the day the descent was stopped, and the keeper named the twenty-sixth out loud in about nine people before he wrote any figure, and the line is not settled this day and the settlement stands for Wednesday the twenty-seventh. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine tallies of work left undone moved a day forward on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and thirty-second day. Four hundred and thirty-four days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and seventy-second day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and thirty-eighth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and fifty-sixth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and eleventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and twenty-sixth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and forty-third day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and seventy-fourth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-two days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came down bare at about half past ten with the same three minutes off and back that the ladder has kept since winter, and about nine people stood in that lane and watched the plaster show and said nothing about why the work is getting slower. The keeper read the three figures and the sum off a slip before the ladder went up, being thirty-three and twenty and thirty-three and eighty-six, and he wrote them with the hour and the day and no reason against the slowness. About four people of that nine can read a column and about four cannot, and the figures were read twice because the reading is for hands and not for paper.

---

The ordinary work went on around the bare plaster, being the pump leathers greased and the wash of a boat that had taken sand into her intake on Saturday. About four men laid sixty yards of line along the flat of that bench stone and worked the kinks out of it hand over hand, and the coil went back on the pin before noon.

---

At about noon the keeper took the money sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of that bench stone in front of about nine people. He said out loud that the work was done on Sunday the twenty-fourth day of July and that the descent was stopped on Tuesday the twenty-sixth, and that both days are on the face of the line, and that the twenty-sixth is the day the descent was stopped and not the day the work was done. He wrote the two days on the face before he wrote any figure, and about four people heard the second day named and about four heard it read back, and the reading was in hours and not in names.

---

What went on the face in parts is air and coal and pumping hours for one descent at two hundred and forty pence and a spare glass and a gasket at forty pence and nine men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at nine times three times sixteen for four hundred and thirty-two pence and two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, and two hundred and forty plus forty plus four hundred and thirty-two plus three hundred and thirty-six is one thousand and forty-eight, being six marks, seven shillings and fourpence, at six times one hundred and sixty for nine hundred and sixty and seven times twelve for eighty-four and four. The keeper read the four parts with the multiplications and then the sum, and the sum stayed on the face of the line and did not go into the foot of the sheet, and the foot stands as it stood yesterday at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out.

---

Nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two twice this day about going down, and nobody put them to him once, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for this Sunday. The man of about thirty-two worked the pump leathers beside the other men all morning and stood at the end stall at about two with the rest, and about four people spoke with him about rope and about four about the bare plaster, and none of them spoke about a descent. The keeper wrote no hour for an asking because no asking was made, and the disagreement about what a page prints was not settled by the quiet.

---

The disagreement was in that shed again at about three in about nine people, and it is open between the two people and no page carries a resolution of it. He said a refusal written into two books is a refusal with a following and that a line kept in two places is a line kept twice, and she said what goes in both books is what she was told and never a name against a person and that the same sentence in two places is one sentence and not a following. He said he knew what he had been objecting to in June and that he had not moved from it, and she said she knew that too and that she was still keeping the second book on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book.

---

At about four the woman of thirty of no office read the two leaves of the twelfth and the thirteenth out of the second book again for about five of that nine who cannot read a column. About four people agreed with her that paper is lost on this coast and about four agreed with him that a refusal ought not to be printed twice, and nobody said either was wrong.

---

At about half past five on that Sunday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about nine hundred words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had watched that wall stand bare at about half past ten.

"**That wall behind that bench stood bare this morning from about half past ten, and the ladder was three minutes off and back as it has been since winter, being thirty-three off and twenty bare and thirty-three back and eighty-six the whole of it, and I read the three figures and the sum twice because about five of this party cannot read a column. The pump leathers were greased and sixty yards of line were laid along this stone and the kinks worked out hand over hand, and a man checked the splices twice and put the coil back on the pin before noon.**

"**At about noon I took the money sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of this stone in front of about nine. I said out loud that the work was done on the twenty-fourth and that the descent was stopped on the twenty-sixth and that both days are on the face of the line, and I named the twenty-sixth as the day the descent was stopped before I wrote any figure. The four parts are air and coal and pumping hours for one descent at two hundred and forty pence and a spare glass and a gasket at forty pence and nine men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at nine times three times sixteen for four hundred and thirty-two pence and two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, and the sum is one thousand and forty-eight, being six marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and the sum stayed on the face and did not go into the foot, and the foot stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six.**

"**Nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two twice this day about going down, and nobody put them once, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for this Sunday. He worked the pump leathers all morning and stood at the end stall at about two, and about four spoke with him about rope and about four about the bare plaster, and none spoke about a descent, and the quiet settled nothing and proved nothing.**

"**The disagreement was in this shed again at about three. He said a refusal in two books is a refusal with a following and she said the same sentence in two places is one sentence and never a name against a person, and he said he had not moved from June and she said she was still keeping the second book on the shelf because one wet road takes one book, and about four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open and no page carries a resolution of it.**

"**And the last of it. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and forty-seven days without one. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-two days today and nothing bare plaster did this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for standing through a bare plaster morning, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A WIPE AND THE HUNDRED AND SIXTEENTH TIME AT THIRTY-THREE OFF AND TWENTY BARE AND THIRTY-THREE BACK BEING EIGHTY-SIX THE WHOLE OF IT, AND LINE NINE WORKED WITH THE TWENTY-SIXTH ON ITS FACE AS THE DAY THE DESCENT WAS STOPPED AND NAMED OUT LOUD BEFORE ANY FIGURE AND NOT SETTLED THIS DAY. NOBODY PUT THE SAME WORDS TO THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO TWICE THIS DAY. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVEN THOUSAND AND NINETY-SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-fourth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that that wall behind that bench stood bare from about half past ten, being the hundred and sixteenth time at thirty-three off and twenty bare and thirty-three back, being eighty-six the whole of it; and that line nine was worked on that day for one descent, carrying two days on its face, being the twenty-fourth for the work and the twenty-sixth for the day the descent was stopped, and that the keeper named the twenty-sixth out loud before he wrote any figure and the line was not settled on that day; and that nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two twice on that day about going down, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for it; and that the disagreement between two people about what a page prints was in that shed at about three and is not settled and no page carries a resolution of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten the reason the three figures were read twice is that about five of that party cannot read a column, and a figure read once in a lane is a figure for hands and not for paper; that at about noon the reason the twenty-sixth was named before any figure is that a day that is not the day the work was done has to be spoken before it is written, and a line that carries two days has to show which is which in front of nine people; that at about two the reason no hour went on the page of days is that no asking was made on that Sunday, and a page that prints an hour nobody heard is a page that keeps a roll; and that at about three the reason the disagreement was answered and not met is that an answer moves a refusal along and a meeting would end it, and a refusal ended in nine people on a Sunday is a refusal two people decided between themselves.*
