# Chapter 746

*Friday the Fifth Day of August, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, the Foot of That Money Sheet Standing From This Day at One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand One Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence Out and One Hundred and Ten Thousand Five Hundred and Fourteen Pence Short, Six Claims With Their Own Provenance and Their Own Limits Put Up on the Boards at the Back of That Market Beside the Central Record and Not Into It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seventy-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Friday the fifth day of August, with that market busy from nine and a flat sea and a hard sky that did not come to anything, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. No line went onto that money sheet and no figure was written into its foot, and the foot of that sheet steps from this day to one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, yesterday having stood at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out. Six claims were put up on the boards at the back of that market between about eleven and about one, each with its own provenance and its own limits on the face of it, and each was put up by a person of the place it came from and none of the six by this yard. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and forty-fourth day. Four hundred and forty-six days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and eighty-fourth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fiftieth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and sixty-eighth days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-third day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and thirty-eighth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fifty-fifth day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and eighty-sixth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-four days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The boat lay against the landing below that market from about ten with nine men in her and the gear still made fast, and the man of about thirty-three who stands in the tender said what the Ninth Bell had done in the day before she came away from that headland two days of water and a night down the coast, in hours and in about nine. It was run at ten and at two and at four, and the man on the line was there for all three, and the chain above it had not moved, and nobody had been able to say what it would do next.

---

At about eleven a woman of about thirty-four of a town four days up that coast road put the first of the six up on the boards at the back of that market with three things on the face of it. She said out loud that the three things were where the thing came from, who in that place stands behind it, and what it will not carry, and that the six on that wall would be six pages and not one.

---

The first claim is that a code rung at a mole at about eleven is a signal and not an instruction, and the place that rang it keeps the hour and does not give it up. The limits on the face of it are that the claim says nothing about any other mole on that coast, and says nothing at all about who rang it here.

---

The second was put up at about half past eleven by a man of about fifty-six of a creek, and it is that the boats of that creek are the boats of that creek and that no person off this coast may take a mooring in it. The limits on the face of it are that it does not say who holds those moorings and does not speak for anybody but the person who stood there and put it up.

---

The third was put up at about noon by a man of about thirty-four who keeps a page of days in a harbor he does not live in, and it is that two ships standing off that mole head on the thirtieth of July are on no chart that harbor holds, and that a chart of about nine years standing is a chart of a coast that has moved. The limits on the face of it are that it does not say where the two went and it does not say they were doing anything.

---

The fourth was put up at about half past twelve by the pilot of about forty-one in his own hand, and it is that the stones he used between the shoals on the thirtieth are not on the Crown chart and that a pilot may use them. The limits on the face of it are that this is one pilot's water in one pilot's words and it binds no other pilot and no other water, and that he was wrong once this summer and will say so if anybody asks him here.

---

The fifth was put up at about one by the man of about thirty-eight who keeps the line of that bell at Orison, and it is that the bell off that mole and the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road answer one another at two hours a day, and that the two hours are the record and not the words. The limits on the face of it are that the claim says nothing whatever about what is carried in the note between the two answers.

---

And the sixth was put up at about one by the woman of thirty of no office, and it is that the sheet of money on the nail in a yard and the four heads on that wall are two things and not one thing, and that a claim may not be counted with a contribution and a contribution may not be counted with a claim. The limits on the face of it are that it binds nobody, it decides nothing, and it says nothing about any figure on any sheet.

---

The central record was not touched and was not in that shed and is not in that market, and what went up beside the four heads went up beside a plain notice saying where the central record is kept, who keeps it, what it is for, and that this yard has put nothing into it and has taken nothing out of it and has not been asked to.

---

At about two the woman of thirty of no office read the six out once on those boards in front of about twenty people of whom about eleven cannot read a column, reading the three things on the face of each in the same three words each time. She read them once and did not read them again, and about four of that twenty said out loud that the six did not agree with one another, and that this was the first time anybody could remember the six of a thing being put up where they could all be read together.

---

The man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine people at the flat of that bench stone at about three that he was not going to decide which of the six was right about what the central record can carry, and that this yard's name goes on none of them, and that a claim put up by a person of a place is a thing that place can take down and this yard would not ask it why.

---

The disagreement between the two of them about what a page prints came into that shed at about four in about nine people, and it is not settled. He said the six claims print the people who did not agree and that a refusal printed with the names of the people who refused it beside it is a refusal with a following, and she said a claim that prints nothing about who did not agree is a claim nobody can answer, and that a wall where every claim says who disagreed is the only place a stranger can find out what is being held.

---

He said the position he had held in June was the position he was holding now, with nothing added to it and nothing taken off, and she said she had stopped expecting him to move and had started keeping a count of the weeks instead, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it.

---

Not one person in that shed put those words to the man of about thirty-two about going down on that Friday, and none put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour against his name. He read all six of the claims himself at about half past two without being asked and said nothing about them, and the quiet around that page settled nothing.

---

The four heads on that wall and the sheet of money on the nail in that shed were named out loud and apart at about five, in front of about nine people, and neither was put in the same sentence as the other, and the keeper wrote the two names on one line of the page of that week with a space between them and no figure against either. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it and the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at the end stall with no figure moved by one.

---

Six claims were put up on the boards at the back of that market and at about half past five on that Friday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about one thousand and sixty-two words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had carried a claim up that ladder at about eleven or at about one.

"**Six claims went up on the boards at the back of that market between about eleven and about one this morning, and each of them was put up by a person of the place it came from and none of the six was put up by this yard. Each of the six carries three things on the face of it, and the woman of about thirty-four who put up the first one said out loud that the three things are where the thing came from, who in that place stands behind it, and what it will not carry, and that the six on that wall are six pages and not one. The first is that a code rung at a mole at about eleven is a signal and not an instruction, and the place that rang it keeps the hour, and it says nothing about any other mole and nothing about who rang it here. The second is that the boats of a creek are the boats of that creek and no person off this coast may take a mooring in it, and it does not say who holds them. The third is that two ships standing off that mole head on the thirtieth are on no chart that harbor holds and that a chart of about nine years standing is a chart of a coast that has moved, and it does not say where the two went or say they were doing anything. The fourth is the pilot's own, in his own hand, that the stones he used between the shoals on the thirtieth are not on the Crown chart and that a pilot may use them, and that it is one pilot's water in one pilot's words and binds no other pilot, and that he was wrong once this summer and will say so here if anybody asks him. The fifth is that the bell off that mole and the bell off a shoal four days down that road answer one another at two hours a day and the two hours are the record and not the words, and it says nothing about what is carried in the note between the two answers. The sixth is that the sheet of money on the nail in this yard and the four heads on that wall are two things and not one, and that a claim may not be counted with a contribution, and it binds nobody and decides nothing and says nothing about any figure on any sheet.**"

"**The central record was not touched and was not in that market, and what went up went up beside a plain notice saying where that record is kept, who keeps it, what it is for, and that this yard has put nothing into it and has taken nothing out of it and has not been asked to. At about two the woman of thirty of no office read the six out once in front of about twenty of us, of whom about eleven cannot read a column, reading the three things in the same three words each time, and about four of that twenty said out loud that the six did not agree with one another.**"

"**The man of about thirty-two said at about three, in about nine of us at the flat of this stone, that he was not going to decide which of the six was right about what the central record can carry, and that this yard's name goes on none of them, and that a claim put up by a person of a place is a thing that place can take down and this yard will not ask it why. He read all six himself at about half past two without being asked and said nothing about them.**"

"**The foot of the money sheet steps from today to one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and no line went onto that sheet and no figure went into its foot today. The four heads on that wall and the sheet of money on the nail in this shed are two things and were named out loud and apart at about five in front of about nine, and neither was put in the same sentence as the other.**"

"**Nobody put words to the man of about thirty-two about going down today and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for this Friday. At about four the two of them had it in the open again. He said a refusal printed with the names of the people who refused it beside it is a refusal with a following, and she said a claim printing nothing about who did not agree is a claim nobody can answer, and that a wall where every claim says who disagreed is the only place a stranger can find out what is being held. He said he had not moved from June and she was still keeping the second book, and about four agreed with her and about four with him, and this is not settled and no page here settles it.**"

"**And the last of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fifty-nine days without one. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-four days today and nothing was claimed this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for six claims, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**SIX CLAIMS WERE PUT UP ON THE BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THAT MARKET BETWEEN ABOUT ELEVEN AND ABOUT ONE, EACH WITH ITS OWN PROVENANCE AND ITS OWN LIMITS ON THE FACE OF IT, AND NONE OF THE SIX WAS PUT UP BY THIS YARD, AND THEY WERE READ OUT ONCE AND ARE SIX PAGES AND NOT ONE. THE CENTRAL RECORD WAS NOT TOUCHED AND WAS NOT IN THAT MARKET. THE FOOT OF THAT MONEY SHEET STEPS FROM THIS DAY TO ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TEN THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that no line went onto that money sheet and no figure was written into its foot, and that the foot stands from this day at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, yesterday having stood at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out; and that six claims were put up on the boards at the back of that market between about eleven and about one, each carrying on its face where the thing came from, who in that place stands behind it, and what it will not carry; and that the first is that a code rung at a mole is a signal and not an instruction and that the place which rang it keeps the hour; and that the second is that the boats of a creek are the boats of that creek and that no person off this coast may take a mooring in it; and that the sixth is that the money sheet and the four heads are two things and not one and that a claim may not be counted with a contribution; and that each of the six carries its own limits on its face and that none of the six was put up by this yard; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the three things were printed on the face of every claim in the same three words is that a limit written in six different shapes is a limit that can be read six ways, and a stranger with a pencil has to be able to take the same three things off six pages without asking anybody what the words mean; that at about one the reason the central record was not brought into that market is that a record brought into a room of about twenty people is a record that room will now hold, and nobody asked to hold it; that at about three the reason the man of about thirty-two put his own yard's name on none of the six is that a yard that puts its name on a claim has claimed it, and a claim this yard has claimed is a claim this yard would one day want back; and that at about four the reason the disagreement about printing who did not agree was not carried to the boards is that the six claims say who disagreed in the limits and say it as a limit, and a limit is not an argument, and the argument is between two people in this shed and is theirs.*
