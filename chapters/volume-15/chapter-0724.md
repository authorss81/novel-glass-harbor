# Chapter 724

*Thursday the Fourteenth Day of July, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Sixty-Sixth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, Being the Last Rota Day of This Movement, a Woman of About Thirty-Four of That Creek Coming Down the Road at About Two and Withdrawing Herself From That Line and One Person Being Enough and Nobody Asked Her Why and Not Asked for Her Name, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Thursday the fourteenth day of July, with that market open from about nine and a low cloud coming up the coast road by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-sixth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. It is the last rota day of this movement and the three charges behind it were the hundred and sixty-third on the fourth, the hundred and sixty-fourth on the seventh and the hundred and sixty-fifth on the eleventh. A woman of about thirty-four of that creek came down the coast road at about two in the afternoon and withdrew herself from that line, and one person of a community is enough to do that and nobody asked her why and nobody asked for her name. The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and twenty-second day. Four hundred and twenty-four days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and sixty-second day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and twenty-eighth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and forty-sixth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and sixteenth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and thirty-third day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and sixty-fourth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-two days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixty-sixth time and the covers went on at the same hour as the other three this fortnight. About four people came for water after four and were served without waiting, and the keeper wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that this was the last rota day of this movement and that the next charge falls on a Monday.

---

The woman came in at about two and stood at the flat of that bench stone without taking her coat off. She said in about nine people that she is of that creek and that she withdraws herself from the Lowfen Reed line from today, and that she has told her own people before she came down the road and that she is not asking anybody to do anything about it.

The keeper asked her nothing. He wrote the day, the hour and the words she used, and he wrote the creek's name and the name of the line and no name against the person, and he read the three lines back to her and she said they were right and she went up the road again at about half past two.

About four people in that nine said nothing at all while she was standing there, and about four said afterwards that it did not take much, and nobody in that shed said the line was now smaller or that it should have been larger.

---

One person is enough. That is on that wall in that room over the market and it has been since before this yard began writing a page of days, and this yard did not check it and did not write a second time that it holds. What was written is that one person of a community said it on the fourteenth of July at about two, and the three columns of the page of routes were not counted today.

The keeper wrote one reason in the second column, which is that a second person agreeing would have made it a count, and a count of one community's warmth is a thing this yard keeps no apparatus for.

---

The tin under that bell seat off the mole is still there and the man of about thirty-three read it out yesterday and nobody read it again. About four people asked the keeper at about four whether the yard was going to take it down, and he said that the yard put it up on its own hook and the yard could take it down on its own hook, and that no community was asked about it either way, and that he had put it up because a figure that lives in one office's book is a figure that can be altered in one afternoon.

About four people said that was right and about four said a thing on a bell that can be altered in one afternoon is not worth the rivets. Nobody altered it.

---

The shortfall was taken out loud at that bench stone at about half past five on that Thursday in about nine hundred words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had watched a woman of about thirty-four stand at that bench stone at about two.

"**That cistern was filled in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven this morning, being the hundred and sixty-sixth filling of it, and the covers lay on it from about eleven until about four, and it is the last rota day of this fortnight and the next charge on that rota falls on a Monday. About four people came for water after four and were served without waiting.**

"**A woman of about thirty-four came down that coast road at about two, stood at the flat of this bench stone without taking her coat off, and said in about nine people that she is of that creek and that she withdraws herself from the Lowfen Reed line from today, and that she has told her own people before she came and that she is not asking anybody to do anything. I asked her nothing. I wrote the day, the hour, the creek's name, the name of the line and no name against the person, and I read the three lines back to her and she said they were right and went up the road at about half past two. One person is enough and that has been on that wall since before this yard began writing a page of days and I did not write it a second time, and I wrote one reason in the second column for not asking a second person, which is that two answers is a count and a count of one community's warmth is a thing this yard keeps no apparatus for. The three columns of the page of routes were not counted today.**

"**The tin under that bell seat is still there and nobody read it again after yesterday's reading. About four asked me at about four whether the yard was going to take it down and I said the yard put it up on its own hook and can take it down on its own hook and that no community was asked about it either way, and that I put it up because a figure that lives in one office's book is a figure that can be altered in one afternoon. Nobody altered it.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty-seven days without one.**

"**And the last of it. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-two days today and nothing said at about two this afternoon touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for standing at a bench stone without taking a coat off, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-six lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR OF THAT CREEK WITHDREW HERSELF FROM THE LOWFEN REED LINE AT ABOUT TWO THIS AFTERNOON IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND ONE PERSON OF A COMMUNITY IS ENOUGH, AND NOBODY ASKED HER WHY AND NOBODY ASKED FOR HER NAME AND NO SECOND PERSON WAS ASKED. THE THREE COLUMNS OF THE PAGE OF ROUTES WERE NOT COUNTED TODAY. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Thursday the fourteenth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-sixth charge and the last rota day of this movement, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that a woman of about thirty-four of that creek came down the coast road at about two in the afternoon and in about nine people withdrew herself from the Lowfen Reed line from that day, having told her own people before she came and not asking anybody to do anything; and that the keeper asked her nothing and wrote the day, the hour, the creek's name and the name of the line and no name against the person, and read the three lines back to her and she said they were right; and that one person of a community is enough and that was not written a second time; and that the three columns of the page of routes were not counted on that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two in the afternoon the reason a second person was not asked is that two answers is a count and a count of one community's warmth is a thing this yard keeps no apparatus for and no column for and no word for; that at about half past two the reason her coat stayed on is that a person who has come to withdraw from a line is not here to be argued with and a yard that makes her take her coat off is a yard that has made this a sitting; that at about four the reason the tin under the bell seat is this yard's to take down is that this yard riveted it up on its own hook out of a book it cannot reach, and a thing put up out of a reach has to be the kind of thing this yard can put down again without asking a community to sign for the removal; and that at about half past four the reason nobody altered the tin is that altering it would have meant this yard had an opinion about the figures on it, and this yard has not got one and is not going to get one this fortnight.*
