# Chapter 737

*Wednesday the Twenty-Seventh Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Line Nine Settled at About Four With Two Days on Its Face, Two Marks on an Unopened Case in a Room on a Hill at About Eleven, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seventy and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Wednesday the twenty-seventh day of July, with that market open from about nine and a pale wind coming off the road by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. Line nine was settled into the book at about four in the afternoon, carrying two days on its face, being Sunday the twenty-fourth day of July for the work and Tuesday the twenty-sixth for the day the descent was stopped, and the keeper named the second day out loud before he wrote a figure, being one thousand and forty-eight pence, being six marks, seven shillings and fourpence, in four parts, being air and coal and pumping hours for one descent at two hundred and forty pence and a spare glass and a gasket at forty pence and nine men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at nine times three times sixteen for four hundred and thirty-two pence and two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, and two hundred and forty plus forty plus four hundred and thirty-two plus three hundred and thirty-six is one thousand and forty-eight. Two marks went on the outside of a case that was not opened in a room on a hill at about eleven in the morning, under the oversight of the turning council, by two people and nobody else. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and thirty-fifth day. Four hundred and thirty-seven days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and seventy-fifth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and forty-first night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and fifty-ninth days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and fourteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and twenty-ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and forty-sixth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and seventy-seventh days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-five days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

A man came down from the boards at about ten and stood at the flat of that bench stone and put the same words to the man of about thirty-two that one other had put to him on the Monday before. The man of about thirty-two said he had heard the words, and the keeper wrote the hour and no name against it.

---

At about eleven two women went up the hill behind that quay with a paper from the turning council folded inside a coat. One of them was the woman of thirty of no office and the other was the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender, and no other person went up that hill with them and no other person went into that room that day.

---

The room is a small square room with one high pane facing the water and a case standing against the far wall under a cloth. The door stood to for the hour they were inside and nobody else crossed it.

---

One of the two took the cloth back and the other held the council paper flat on the lid without opening the lid. What went on the outside was a mark of release and not an exercising and not a reading, and nothing inside was named or numbered or measured.

---

The woman of thirty of no office put the first mark on the outside at about half past eleven and the woman of about thirty-three put the second beside it before noon, and both marks were put under the oversight of the turning council and neither of the three speaks for the other two. The cloth went back over the case and the lid stayed shut from the hour they came in until the hour they went out, and the drawer in that building stayed shut and the worked lead in that building did not come down that hill.

---

They came down the hill at about noon and went their separate ways without stopping at the end stall. Neither of them said in that shed what had been put on the outside of that case and neither of them said it in any slip.

---

At about two a second person put the same words to the man of about thirty-two at the end stall. About four people heard it and he said he had heard the words, and the keeper wrote the hour and no name against it, and no sum is set against either hour on any sheet in that yard.

---

The disagreement between the two people about what a page prints was in that shed again at about three. He said a refusal written into two books is a refusal with a following and that a mark put on by two hands is a mark with two owners, and she said what goes in both books is what she was told and never a name against a person and that two hands on one outside is one custody in two marks and not two owners.

---

He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and was still keeping the second book. About four agreed with her and about four with him, and the matter is open and no page here carries a resolution of it.

---

At about four the keeper took the money sheet off its nail and named the twenty-sixth of July out loud in about nine people as the day the descent was stopped. He said the work was done on the twenty-fourth and both days are on the face of the line, and then he read the four parts with the multiplications and the sum went down after the reading and not before it.

---

The mark went on in the hillside room before noon and the reading went out at the bench stone at about half past five on that Wednesday in about nine hundred words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had heard the twenty-sixth named out loud at about four.

"**At about ten a man stood at the flat of this bench stone and put words to the man of about thirty-two and I wrote the hour and no name, and at about two a second person put the same words to him at the end stall and I wrote the hour and no name, and no sum is on any sheet here. At about eleven two women went up the hill with a paper from the turning council, and no other person went into that room, and two marks went on the outside of a case that was not opened, under the oversight of the turning council, with the lid shut and nothing inside named or numbered or measured, and it is not an opening and not an exercising and not a reading.**

"**At about three the disagreement was in this shed again. He said a refusal in two books is a refusal with a following and a mark by two hands is a mark with two owners, and she said what goes in both is what she was told and never a name and that two hands on one outside is one custody in two marks. He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and was still keeping the second book and still holding one of the two marks, and about four agreed with her and about four with him, and this is not settled and no page here settles it.**

"**At about four I took the money sheet off its nail and named the twenty-sixth of July out loud as the day the descent was stopped, and said the work was on the twenty-fourth and both days are on the face of the line, and read the four parts with the multiplications, being air and coal and pumping hours for one descent at two hundred and forty pence and a spare glass and a gasket at forty pence and nine men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at nine times three times sixteen for four hundred and thirty-two pence and two days of a boat at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, and two hundred and forty plus forty plus four hundred and thirty-two plus three hundred and thirty-six is one thousand and forty-eight, being six marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and the sum went down after the reading.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fifty days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-five days today and nothing marked or settled this afternoon touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for two marks on an unopened case, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**TWO MARKS WENT ON THE OUTSIDE OF A CASE THAT WAS NOT OPENED IN A ROOM ON A HILL AT ABOUT ELEVEN THIS MORNING BY TWO PEOPLE AND NOBODY ELSE, UNDER THE OVERSIGHT OF THE TURNING COUNCIL, BEING THE WOMAN OF THIRTY OF NO OFFICE AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE, AND IT IS NOT AN OPENING AND NOT AN EXERCISING AND NOT A READING AND NOTHING INSIDE WAS NAMED OR NUMBERED OR MEASURED. LINE NINE WAS SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR CARRYING TWO DAYS ON ITS FACE BEING THE TWENTY-FOURTH FOR THE WORK AND THE TWENTY-SIXTH FOR THE DAY THE DESCENT WAS STOPPED, BEING ONE THOUSAND AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING SIX MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVEN THOUSAND AND NINETY-SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-seventh day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that at about eleven two women went up the hill with a paper from the turning council, being the woman of thirty of no office and the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender, and no other person went up and no other person went into that room; and that two marks went on the outside of a case that was not opened, the first at about half past eleven and the second before noon, under the oversight of the turning council, with the lid shut throughout and the cloth put back and nothing inside named or numbered or measured; and that at about ten and at about two the same words were put to the man of about thirty-two in two places and the hours were written with no names and no sum on any sheet; and that at about four line nine was settled with two days on its face, being the twenty-fourth for the work and the twenty-sixth for the day the descent was stopped, the second day having been named out loud before any figure was written, being two hundred and forty pence and forty pence and nine times three times sixteen for four hundred and thirty-two pence and two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being one thousand and forty-eight pence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten the reason the first asking was met with hearing and no hour for an answer is that a shed that gives an hour for an answer has turned a asking into a sitting, and a man who keeps no hour keeps the asking where it was put; that at about noon the reason the cloth went back over the case before the room was left is that a mark on an outside that is left bare invites a hand to test whether the lid will lift, and a cloth restores the case to furniture; that at about three the reason the disagreement was left open in front of nine is that an open disagreement can be carried by nine without being owned by any of them, and a settled one on a Wednesday would have been owned by the two who settled it; and that at about four the reason the twenty-sixth was named out loud before any figure went down is that a line carrying two days invites a reader to choose the earlier one, and a keeper who names the later day first leaves no room for the mistake.*
