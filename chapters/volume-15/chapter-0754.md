# Chapter 754

*Saturday the Thirteenth Day of August, Line Twelve Worked Today at About Two Carrying Monday the Fifteenth on Its Face for the Day It Settles and Not Settled Today, the Four Heads on That Wall All Four of Them Put to Use in About Nine People at About Three With the Refusals at the Same Size as the Contributions and No Fifth Thing Added, the Foot of That Money Sheet at One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Two Pence Out, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Nine*

Saturday the thirteenth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a warm morning with the wind gone slack and the water going glassy off that mole head by about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. Line twelve was worked today and went onto that fitting-out sheet at about two, carrying Monday the fifteenth of August on its face for the day it settles, and the keeper said the fifteenth out loud in about nine people before he wrote a single figure, being six hundred and sixty-six pence, being four marks, two shillings and twopence, in four parts. The line is not settled today and settles at about four on Monday the fifteenth. The four heads on that wall were all four put to use this afternoon at about three, being claims and public correction and refusals and restricted records, the refusals written at the same size as the contributions, and no fifth thing was added to that wall. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand eight hundred and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand two hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine of the things that are not being done were carried a day further this morning. The notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and fifty-second day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and fifty-four days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects that belong to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and ninety-second day of themselves. The bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-eighth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventy-six days and nothing is set against them. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and thirty-first day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and forty-sixth. The nine-line sheet carrying a seal in that front street has stood three hundred and sixty-third day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and ninety-fourth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-two days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That Saturday's work was the case for a glass front and the two seats and the bench and the stove, and nine men were on it in two shifts and four of the second lay were learning the glass work off a man of about forty-five who has cut glass in that yard for nineteen years.

The man of about thirty-two was ashore all day and went on no water, and he said out loud at about ten that he had not been asked to go down by anybody and that if he went down today it would be because he had chosen to and not because a page had it in a column.

---

At about two the keeper took that fitting-out sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of that stone in front of about nine people and said Monday the fifteenth out loud, twice, and then wrote it.

Glass and frames for a case at two hundred and forty pence. Two seats and a bench at fifty pence. A stove and its pipe at one hundred and twenty pence. Four men at four days at sixteen pence a man-day, at four times four times sixteen for two hundred and fifty-six pence. Two hundred and forty plus fifty plus one hundred and twenty plus two hundred and fifty-six is six hundred and sixty-six pence, being four marks, two shillings and twopence.

---

The four heads on that wall were put to use this afternoon at about three, and each of them was used by a person of the place the thing came from and none of the four was used by this yard.

A woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch brought up a claim about a bell's own hours and put her name and where she came from and what it will not carry on the face of it in the same three words. A man of about forty-nine of the Morrow Line brought up a public correction of a figure that a person of his place had got wrong in the spring and the correction went up beside the wrong figure and the wrong figure was left showing under it.

---

The refusals were the third head and they were written at the same size as the claim and the same size as the correction and the same size as the record that was restricted.

A woman of about thirty of the Ashtide Run refused to have her hours read out in that shed and said out loud in about nine that she would keep them and that nobody in that nine was to be given her reason, and about four of that nine said she was right and about four said nothing and nobody said she was wrong.

The restricted record was the fourth head and it is a record of a thing that is not shown and the notice of it is on that wall at the same size as everything else on that wall, being what it is, where it came from, who stands behind it, and what it will not carry.

---

Nobody put a fifth head on that wall and nothing went up in that market this afternoon on account of a warning, a rescue, a dive, a claim, a seal, a refusal, a document or a victory.

The keeper named the four heads on that wall and the fitting-out sheet out loud and apart at about half past three, and said they were two things and not one, and said that a claim may not be counted with a contribution and that a refusal may not be counted with either.

---

At about four the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine people that a line which carries the day it settles on its face is a line a stranger can check, and that the day it settles is not the day the work was done and that anybody who reads the face of that line will see two different days and have to ask which is which.

The keeper said that was the whole reason for it and that it had been that way since the twenty-third day of June, and the woman of thirty of no office said that a page which carries one day and lets a reader guess at the other is a page that has decided what a reader is allowed to know.

---

Nobody said either of them was wrong and about four of that nine agreed with the man of about thirty-two and about four agreed with the woman of thirty of no office, and the matter is open and no page in that shed carries a resolution of it.

At about half past four he said out loud that he had read the entry the woman of thirty of no office wrote on Tuesday about a request made on the sixth and a refusal given on the sixth, and that he was not going to ask her to soften one word of it, and that a man who asks a person who keeps a record to make that record kinder has already begun to keep it.

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday went on and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine and the two lay at the north quay caulking the stem of a lighter, and the fourth lay tied twice in that flat water. The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it.

---

About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. Nobody was thanked for the glass work and nobody was thanked for a refusal, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**LINE TWELVE WENT ONTO THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET AT ABOUT TWO CARRYING MONDAY THE FIFTEENTH ON ITS FACE FOR THE DAY IT SETTLES, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIX PENCE IN FOUR PARTS, AND IT IS NOT SETTLED TODAY. THE FOUR HEADS ON THAT WALL ARE CLAIMS, PUBLIC CORRECTION, REFUSALS AND RESTRICTED RECORDS, ALL FOUR OF THEM PUT TO USE THIS AFTERNOON, THE REFUSALS AT THE SAME SIZE AS THE CONTRIBUTIONS, AND NO FIFTH THING WAS ADDED. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirteenth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was put on that cistern and its covers lay on it from nine until four; and that the work of that day was the case for a glass front and two seats and a bench and a stove, with nine men on it in two shifts and four of the second lay learning the glass work off a man of about forty-five who has cut glass in that yard for nineteen years; and that at about two the keeper took that fitting-out sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of that stone in about nine people and said Monday the fifteenth out loud twice before he wrote it, and that line twelve went onto that sheet being glass and frames for a case at two hundred and forty pence, two seats and a bench at fifty pence, a stove and its pipe at one hundred and twenty pence, and four men at four days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times four times sixteen for two hundred and fifty-six pence, being six hundred and sixty-six pence, and that the line is not settled today and settles at about four on the fifteenth; and that the four heads on that wall were put to use at about three, a claim brought up by a woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch carrying her name and where she came from and what it will not carry in the same three words, a public correction brought up by a man of about forty-nine of the Morrow Line of a figure a person of his place got wrong in the spring with the wrong figure left showing under the correction, a refusal by a woman of about thirty of the Ashtide Run to have her hours read out in that shed with nobody to be given her reason, and a restricted record being a record of a thing not shown with the notice of it at the same size as everything else on that wall; and that the refusals were written at the same size as the claim and the correction and that nobody in that nine said that woman was wrong; and that nobody put a fifth head on that wall and nothing went up in that market on account of a warning, a rescue, a dive, a claim, a seal, a refusal, a document or a victory; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about two the reason the fifteenth was said out loud before any figure was written is that a settlement day named after the fact is a day the keeper chose, and a keeper who chooses the day a sum lands on chooses the day the work looks like it happened; that at about three the reason a refusal went up on that wall at the same size as a claim is that a wall where a refusal is smaller than a claim is a wall where a person has to be braver to refuse than to claim, and the only people who can afford to refuse are the ones who were never going to; that at about four the reason the day it settles is written on the face of the line beside the day the work was done is that a reader with a pencil can check one and cannot check the other, and this yard has spent a month finding that out; and that at about half past four the reason he said he would not ask her to soften one word of that entry is that softening is the whole of what a record is asked for by the person it is about and refused by the person who keeps it, and a man who asks for it once has asked for it.*
