# Chapter 702

*Wednesday the Twenty-Second Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, That Cistern Not Charged and Its Covers Lying on It From Nine in the Morning Until Four in the Afternoon, a Second Time Down That Coast Road in a Coat at About Ten in the Morning Being Half Past Seven in the Morning in a Hand That Is Not the Hand of Anybody in That Shed, Nine Words on a Third Page With No Time on It at All and the Reason Not Copied Onto This Yard's Page and Only the Fact of It Entered With the Hour Against It, Four People Who Cannot Read a Column Told That the Reason Was Nine Words Long and Was on a Page Four Days Up That Road, Nobody Sent Up That Road and No Second Letter Written and the Money Sheet Carrying Two Lines and No Third and the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand and Forty-Nine*

Wednesday the twenty-second day of June, with that market shut for the morning and standing open from about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. A second time came down that coast road in the inside of a coat at about ten this morning, being half past seven in the morning, in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that yard. A third page came down the same road at about two this afternoon with nine words on it and no time on it at all. At about half past three this afternoon the man who keeps the leaf entered the fact of a reason having been given, and by whom, and at what hour, and did not enter the reason, and about four people in that nine who cannot read a column asked him what the reason was and were told that it was nine words long and that it was on a page four days up that road. Nothing went onto the money sheet and nothing came off it, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done went on a day today. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundredth day. Four hundred and two days have gone by without a bellwright of this harbor being asked for anything, and the chest under his bench stays shut. Two objects of nobody's up on that crown are into their seven hundred and fortieth day. That bench under that pump has lain bare for the five hundred and sixth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-four days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and seventy-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-fourth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and eleventh day. A man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and forty-two days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column stands empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. Two of the five papers on that board at the head of that mole have never been opened by anybody in this harbor and nothing has gone up on that board this month. That basket in that boathouse holds a paper and nothing else and nobody has put a hand in it. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The coat came down the road on Monday evening and lay on the third lay overnight, and the man it belonged to came back for it at about ten on Wednesday morning with a page out of the inside pocket that had been folded twice and once more.

It is a page of about nine inches by six, the same size as the first one, and it came off the same road from the same direction, and it is not the same paper.

---

**Half past seven in the morning**, in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in that shed.

The hand is small and it presses hard and it leans back, and about four people in that yard have been shown the page and all four said it is not the man of about thirty-three's hand and not the man of about thirty-four's and not the man who keeps the leaf's.

Nobody in that nine asked whose hand it was. Two of them asked and were told that it is that community's own and that this yard is not going to sit here and name the person whose hand that is, and about four people agreed with that and about four thought it was a great deal of ceremony.

---

The third page came at about two in the afternoon and it is thinner than the first two and it has been folded the other way, from the bottom up, which is a thing people do when they are used to a different order to this one.

There is no time on it. There are nine words on it and nothing under them and nothing over them and no signature.

The nine words say that that community's own people are the only ones who know when that community's own buoys are in the water, and that a time written down anywhere else is a time somebody will hold them to on a morning when they are somewhere else.

---

At about half past three this afternoon the man who keeps the leaf wrote four lines on the page of that week and the fourth of them is the shortest line he has written on that page in nine runs of days.

The fourth line says that a reason was given, and by whom, and at what hour, and that the reason is nine words long and is on a page four days up that coast road in a hand that is not his.

He did not write the nine words down. He said out loud, to about nine people, that he is not going to, and he gave the reason in about nineteen words, and the reason was that the moment a reason stops being on the page of the community it belongs to, it stops being a reason and becomes a position, and a position with a person's hand on it is a thing that gets argued with by neighbours.

---

**About four people in that nine cannot read a column.** That is not a guess; that is the count the man who keeps the leaf has been keeping of the people who come into that yard and cannot read what is on the front of a thing.

All four of them asked him what the reason was. He told all four of them the same three things, being that it was nine words long, and that it was on a page four days up that road, and that it was not going to be in a book in this market where anybody could walk up and read it.

One of the four said that was about nine words of reason for not writing nine words down. He said that was fair and did not argue with it.

---

At about four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two of that harbor asked the man who keeps the leaf one question and got one answer, and the question is not written in the first column and the answer is.

He asked whether anybody had been sent up that road. He was told that nobody had been sent, and that a second letter had not been written, and that the yard did not go up that road in April either, and that the page had come down on Monday night inside somebody's coat because somebody on that road decided to bring it down and not because anybody here asked for it.

He said that was right and went up the steps by the rail.

---

Nobody thanked anybody for that page. The page of that week had four lines on it and the shortest of them was the fourth.

---

And at about half past five on that Wednesday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about eight hundred and eighty-eight words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had asked him a question about a reason between half past three and four o'clock.

"**Two times now, and one of them came down that coast road in the inside of a coat on Monday night and the other one came in the same coat this morning, and the second is half past seven in the morning in a hand that is not the hand of anybody in this shed, and nobody in that nine asked whose hand it is, because it is that community's hand and it is not this yard's business to name a person whose hand is on a page that came down a road on its own.**

**The third page came at about two this afternoon and it is thinner than the other two and it was folded from the bottom up, which is what people do when they are used to a different order to this one, and there is no time on it at all. There are nine words on it and nothing under them and nothing over them and no signature, and what the nine words say is that that community's own people are the only ones who know when their own buoys are in the water, and that a time written down anywhere else is a time somebody will hold them to on a morning when they are somewhere else.**

**And at about half past three this afternoon I wrote four lines on the page of this week and the fourth one is the shortest line I have put on that page in nine runs of days. It says that a reason was given, and by whom, and at what hour, and that the reason is nine words long and is on a page four days up that road in a hand that is not mine. I did not write the nine words down and I said out loud that I was not going to, and I gave the reason in about nineteen words, and the reason is that the moment a reason stops being on the page of the community it belongs to it stops being a reason and becomes a position, and a position with somebody's hand on it is a thing that gets argued with by neighbours.**

**About four people in that nine asked me what the reason was. That is not a guess; that is the count of the people who come into this yard and cannot read the front of a thing, and all four of them asked, and I told all four of them the same three things, being that it was nine words long, and that it was on a page four days up that road, and that it is not going into a book in this market where anybody can walk up and read it. One of the four said that was about nine words of reason for not writing nine words down, and I said it was fair, and I did not argue with it.**

**Nobody was sent up that road today and no second letter was written and this yard did not go up that road in April either. The page came down because somebody on that road decided to bring it down. The man of about thirty-two asked me that one question at about four this afternoon and got one answer and the question is not in the first column and the answer is.**

**The sheet stands at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that is one hundred and two thousand and seventy-six pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and thirteen shillings. Two lines on it and no third. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street and did not come down today.**

**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fifteen days without one.**

**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four and nobody walked on the water. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster and the plaster has not been whitewashed. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing on it. The page of hands is not this yard's page this month and stands where it stood. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said one word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred days today and nothing that came down that road in a coat touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a page, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had four lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A SECOND TIME CAME DOWN THAT COAST ROAD IN THE INSIDE OF A COAT AT ABOUT TEN IN THE MORNING, THE COAT HAVING CARRIED IT SINCE MONDAY NIGHT, AND IT IS HALF PAST SEVEN IN THE MORNING IN A SMALL HAND THAT PRESSES HARD AND LEANS BACK, AND ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT NINE WERE SHOWN THE PAGE AND ALL FOUR SAID IT IS NOT THE HAND OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE AND NOT THE HAND OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR AND NOT THE HAND OF THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF, AND NOBODY IN THAT NINE ASKED WHOSE HAND IT WAS. A THIRD PAGE CAME DOWN THE SAME ROAD AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON, THINNER THAN THE FIRST TWO AND FOLDED FROM THE BOTTOM UP, WITH NINE WORDS ON IT AND NO TIME ON IT AT ALL AND NOTHING UNDER IT AND NOTHING OVER IT AND NO SIGNATURE, AND THE NINE WORDS SAY THAT THAT COMMUNITY'S OWN PEOPLE ARE THE ONLY ONES WHO KNOW WHEN THAT COMMUNITY'S OWN BUOYS ARE IN THE WATER, AND THAT A TIME WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE ELSE IS A TIME SOMEBODY WILL HOLD THEM TO ON A MORNING WHEN THEY ARE SOMEWHERE ELSE. AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE LEAF WROTE FOUR LINES ON THE PAGE OF THAT WEEK AND THE FOURTH IS THE SHORTEST LINE ON THAT PAGE IN NINE RUNS OF DAYS, BEING THAT A REASON WAS GIVEN AND BY WHOM AND AT WHAT HOUR AND THAT THE REASON IS NINE WORDS LONG AND IS ON A PAGE FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST ROAD IN A HAND THAT IS NOT HIS, AND HE DID NOT WRITE THE NINE WORDS DOWN AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO AND GAVE THE REASON IN ABOUT NINETEEN WORDS. ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT NINE ASKED HIM WHAT THE REASON WAS AND WERE TOLD THE SAME THREE THINGS. AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO ASKED WHETHER ANYBODY HAD BEEN SENT UP THAT ROAD AND WAS TOLD THAT NOBODY HAD BEEN SENT AND THAT NO SECOND LETTER HAD BEEN WRITTEN AND THAT THE YARD DID NOT GO UP THAT ROAD IN APRIL EITHER. THE MONEY SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, WITH TEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE IN, BEING SIXTY-SIX MARKS, SIX SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IT IS ONE HUNDRED AND TWO THOUSAND AND SEVENTY-SIX PENCE SHORT, BEING SIX HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN MARKS AND THIRTEEN SHILLINGS, AND IT CARRIES TWO LINES AND NO THIRD. NOTHING WAS READ OUT OF A GAP AND NO FIGURE ON THE SHEET OF THE FOURTEEN WENT UP OR DOWN BY ONE. UNDER THAT SLATE IS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason the hand was not named is that a page that comes down a road in a coat is that community's page until somebody on that road says otherwise, and this yard naming the person whose hand is on it would be this yard putting a name on another community's paper, and there is exactly one name this yard is entitled to put on another community's paper and it is a man of about thirty-three with two papers in a room over that market and that is a different thing; that at about half past three in the afternoon the reason the fourth line on that week's page is the shortest line on it is that a page of days in this shed takes what was done and this yard did nine words of nothing today, and a line that says a man did not do something for a stated reason is longer than a line that says a man did something, and this house has learned that the hard way on a page of seven lines that is not a page of days; that at about four in the afternoon the reason the question is not in the first column and the answer is, is that a question this yard asked its own keeper is a thing about this yard and a thing the nine outside can hear, and a question somebody asks from the rail on the way out of a shed is a thing between two people and it is not nine people's business; and that at about half past five in the afternoon the reason that slip ends on the nine hulls is that the count of the people who cannot read the front of a thing has been the same eleven in this harbor for two runs of days and it is a count of people and not a count of anybody's ability, and there is no number against a man who cannot read a column in this market and there is not going to be one.
