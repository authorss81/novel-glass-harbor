# Chapter 749

*Monday the Eighth Day of August, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Third Charge on That Cistern, Line Eleven on That Sheet at About Two Carrying Two Days on Its Face and the Ninth of August Named Out Loud Before Any Figure Went Down as the Day the Mechanism Broke and Not Settled Today, the Ninth Bell at Orison Not Answering at Ten This Morning, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Monday the eighth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a grey morning with a hard swell coming in from the south-west that put spray over the mole head twice before eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventy-third charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Line eleven went onto that money sheet at about two in the afternoon carrying two days on its face, being Monday the eighth day of August for the work and Tuesday the ninth for the day the mechanism broke, and the keeper named the ninth out loud in about nine people before he wrote any figure, and the line is not settled today and the settlement stands for Wednesday the tenth. The ninth bell at Orison did not answer at ten this morning. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and forty-seventh day. Four hundred and forty-nine days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and eighty-seventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-third night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventy-first days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-sixth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and forty-first. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fifty-eighth day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and eighty-ninth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-seven days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and seventy-third time and the covers went on at the same hour and lay until four, and about five people came for water after four and were served without waiting. The keeper wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that no settlement goes into the foot of that sheet today.

---

A boat of the Orison landing came in over the bar at about ten with the wind on her quarter and one man in her, and he brought a sheet of hours in the hand of the man of about thirty-eight. The ninth bell was run at ten on the Saturday and at ten on the Sunday and at ten this morning, and it has answered nothing since the chain above it came apart at about half past twelve on the Saturday, and the eighth section came off the frame on the Saturday with the seventh and the sixth before it, and the ninth has not come off and nobody at that landing has been able to reach the man on the line.

---

At about eleven one person put the same words to the man of about thirty-two at the flat of that bench stone, and about four people heard it, and he said he had heard the words. The keeper wrote the hour and no name against it and did not add it to the column headed with the asking, because that column was summed off the page of days on Friday the twenty-ninth of July and is not summed again on any day that follows it.

---

At about two the keeper took the money sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of that stone in front of about nine people, and he named the ninth day of August out loud before he wrote a single figure. He said the work was done on Monday the eighth and that the ninth is the day the mechanism breaks, and that both days are on the face of the line, and that the ninth is not the day the work was done and he was not writing it as though it were.

---

The people doing that work said at Orison on Saturday that the ninth section would let go on the ninth and not before, and the keeper wrote down a day a working man gave him and not a day this yard chose. The second column carries the reason and no name against it, and about four of that nine heard the ninth named and about four heard it read back, and the reading was in hours and not in names.

---

What went on the face in parts is two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence and rope and shackles at sixty pence and nine men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at nine times two times sixteen for two hundred and eighty-eight pence and sounding line and oil at forty-four pence, and three hundred and thirty-six plus sixty plus two hundred and eighty-eight plus forty-four is seven hundred and twenty-eight pence, being four marks, seven shillings and fourpence.

---

The keeper read the four parts with the multiplications and then the sum, and the sum stayed on the face of the line and did not go into the foot, and the foot stands as it stood yesterday at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short. The boat leaves that landing in the morning for two days at the figure in chalk and this yard wrote that figure on the face of the line and not into its own book.

---

The two of them put their disagreement about what a page prints in the open in that shed at about four in about nine people, and it is not settled. He said a wall of six claims each printing its own limits and not printing the ninth section is a wall a stranger will read as the whole of what is held, and she said that is what a wall is and that a stranger who is shown the whole of it should be shown the part of it that is not held too, and that there is no place on that wall to put the ninth section without a person's name on it.

---

He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and that she was still keeping the second book on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it and nobody in that nine was thanked.

---

The swell came over the mole head twice before eleven and once more at about three, and the fourth lay was tied twice and the boom was got in and got out again. The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it to be read.

---

The mechanism broke at about half past twelve and its ninth section came off the frame and at about half past five on that Monday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about nine hundred and eighty-five words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had heard the ninth of August named out loud at about two in front of about nine.

"**A sheet of hours came in over that bar at about ten this morning in the hand of a man who keeps a line of bell two days of water from here, and it said the ninth bell at Orison was run at ten this morning and answered nothing, and that the chain above it came apart into nine pieces at about half past twelve on the Saturday. The hundred and seventy-third charge went up that butt between eight and about eleven and the covers lay on it until four, and I wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that no settlement goes into the foot of that sheet today.**"

"**A boat of the Orison landing came in over the bar at about ten with the wind on her quarter and one man in her, and he brought a sheet of hours in the hand of the man of about thirty-eight. The ninth bell was run at ten on the Saturday and at ten on the Sunday and at ten this morning, and it has answered nothing since the chain above it came apart at about half past twelve on the Saturday, and the eighth section came off the frame on the Saturday with the seventh and the sixth before it, and the ninth has not come off, and nobody at that landing has been able to reach the man on the line.**"

"**At about eleven one person put words to the man of about thirty-two at the flat of this stone, and about four of us heard it, and he said he had heard the words. I wrote the hour and no name against it and did not add it to the column headed with the asking, because that column was summed off the page of days on the twenty-ninth of July and is not summed again on any day after that.**"

"**At about two I took the money sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of this stone in front of about nine, and I named the ninth of August out loud before I wrote a single figure. I said the work was done on the eighth and that the ninth is the day the mechanism breaks, and that both days are on the face of the line, and that the ninth is not the day the work was done and I was not writing it as though it were. The people doing that work said at Orison on the Saturday that the ninth section would let go on the ninth and not before, and I wrote down a day a working man gave me and not a day this yard chose. About four of that nine heard the ninth named and about four heard it read back, and the reading was in hours and not in names.**"

"**The four parts are two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence of boat hired at the figure in chalk, sixty pence of rope and shackles, nine times two times sixteen for two hundred and eighty-eight pence of nine men at two days, and forty-four pence of sounding line and oil. Three hundred and thirty-six plus sixty plus two hundred and eighty-eight plus forty-four is seven hundred and twenty-eight pence, being four marks, seven shillings and fourpence. I read the four parts with the multiplications and then the sum, and the sum stayed on the face of the line and did not go into the foot, and the foot stands as it stood yesterday. The boat goes out in the morning for two days at the figure in chalk and I wrote that figure on the face of the line and not into this yard's book.**"

"**At about four the disagreement came into the open in this shed. He said a wall of six claims each printing its own limits and not printing the ninth section is a wall a stranger will read as the whole of what is held, and she said that is what a wall is, and that a stranger shown the whole of it should be shown the part of it that is not held too, and that there is no place on that wall to put the ninth section without a person's name on it. He said he had not moved from June and she was still keeping the second book, and about four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong, and this is not settled and no page here settles it.**"

"**And the last of it. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and sixty-two days without one. That swell came over the mole head twice before eleven and once more at about three. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings, and a hundred and sixty-eight pence came in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out at about four. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-seven days today and nothing named or read this afternoon touched that figure by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a day named before a figure, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**LINE ELEVEN WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT TWO CARRYING TWO DAYS ON ITS FACE, BEING THE EIGHTH FOR THE WORK AND THE NINTH FOR THE DAY THE MECHANISM BREAKS, THE NINTH HAVING BEEN NAMED OUT LOUD BEFORE ANY FIGURE WENT DOWN, BEING SEVEN HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING FOUR MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, IN FOUR PARTS, AND IT IS NOT SETTLED TODAY AND THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET HAS NOT MOVED. THE NINTH BELL AT ORISON DID NOT ANSWER AT TEN THIS MORNING. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Monday the eighth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventy-third charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that a boat of the Orison landing came in over the bar at about ten with a sheet of hours in the hand of the man of about thirty-eight, being that the ninth bell was run at ten on the Saturday and at ten on the Sunday and at ten on the Monday morning and has answered nothing since the chain above it came apart at about half past twelve on the Saturday, and that the eighth section came off the frame on the Saturday with the seventh and the sixth before it, and that the ninth has not come off and that nobody at that landing has been able to reach the man on the line; and that at about eleven the same words were put to the man of about thirty-two at the flat of that bench stone and the hour was written with no name against it and was not added to the column headed with the asking, that column having been summed on the twenty-ninth of July and not summed again on any day after it; and that line eleven went onto the money sheet at about two carrying two days on its face, being the eighth for the work and the ninth for the day the mechanism breaks, the ninth having been named out loud before any figure was written, being two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence of boat hired at the figure in chalk and sixty pence of rope and shackles and nine times two times sixteen for two hundred and eighty-eight pence and forty-four pence of sounding line and oil, being seven hundred and twenty-eight pence; and that the ninth was a day a working man at Orison gave and not a day this yard chose, and that the sum stayed on the face of the line and the foot was not written into and stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out; and that at about four the disagreement between two people about what a page prints was put in the open in about nine and is not settled; and that the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at the end stall with no figure moved by one.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the hour was written and the column was not added to is that a column summed twice is a roll, and a roll of who asked a man a thing is a thing this harbor will be asked to produce on a bad morning; that at about two the reason the ninth of August was named out loud before any figure was written is that a line carrying a day which is not the day the work was done gives a reader a choice between the two, and a keeper who names the second day first leaves no room for the choice; that at about two the reason the ninth was written because a working man gave it and not because this yard chose it is that a yard which chooses the day a thing stopped decides the day it stopped, and a page that says so is a page nobody can check; and that at about four the reason the question about the ninth section on that wall was left where it was is that there is no place on that wall to put a man without a name on it, and this yard is not the wall's clerk and does not put a name on that wall.*
