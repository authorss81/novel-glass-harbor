# Chapter 745

*Thursday the Fourth Day of August, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Second Charge on That Cistern, Line Ten Settled into the Foot of That Sheet at About Four and the Foot Stepping to One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand One Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence From Tomorrow, the Answer to One Question Written Up That Coast Road in Another Hand and Not Settled, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Seventy-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Thursday the fourth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a hard wind out of the north-east that made the doors hard to hold, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventy-second charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Line ten was settled into the foot of that money sheet at about four in the afternoon, carrying two days on its face, being Tuesday the second day of August for the work and this day for the day it settles, being two thousand three hundred and seventy pence, being fourteen marks, ten shillings and tenpence, in five parts, and the foot of that sheet is read at the close of this day as it stood before the sum went down and stands at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out, and it will stand at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out from tomorrow, being one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and forty-third day. Four hundred and forty-five days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and eighty-third day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and forty-ninth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and sixty-seventh days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-second day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and thirty-seventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fifty-fourth day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and eighty-fifth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-three days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and seventy-second time and the covers went on at the same hour and lay until four, and about five people came for water after four and were served without waiting. The keeper wrote the charge number and the hours, and the four men who carry that water were not asked what it is for and have not been asked since the ninth of June.

---

At about one the man of about thirty-eight came down that lane on foot with a page in his hand and a coat on this time, and he gave it to the man of about thirty-two at the door of that shed and said it had come up that coast road in the morning in a hand he knew and that he had carried it down himself. The man of about thirty-two was ashore all of that day and went on no water until about six in the evening.

---

The page carried one answer and nothing else, and the hour of it was against it, and there was no name on it and no name of the man on the line. The woman of thirty of no office wrote that a person who has put himself into a thing without being asked is not to be taken out of it by the people standing round it, and that a thing taken out of by the people round it is a thing the people round it are holding, and that a person is not a bell.

---

She wrote under that, in the same hand, that a page which prints the question and the answer and the names of the people who did not agree with either is the only page that can be answered, and the man of about thirty-two said out loud in that shed, with about four of that nine standing at the flat of the stone, that a page which prints who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing and that he had said so in June and had not been answered and was saying it again.

---

About four agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said either was wrong. He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and that she was still keeping the second book on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book, and the matter is open and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it and the answer came up a coast road and was not an answer to him.

---

At about three the man of about thirty-eight said what the bell had done in the day, and he said it in about nine and he said it in hours. The bell was run at ten and at two and at four, and the man of about fifty-six was on the line the whole of it, and he was asked nothing all day and gave nothing, and at about two he asked one thing of the man of about thirty-eight, being whether the chain above that bell had moved at all, and the man of about thirty-eight said it had not.

---

At about four the keeper took the money sheet off its nail and named the fourth day of August out loud in about nine people as the day line ten settles, and he said the work was done on the second and both days are on the face of the line. He read the five parts with the multiplications and then the sum, and the sum went into the foot of that sheet after the reading and not before it.

---

The five parts are six days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at six times one hundred and sixty-eight for one thousand and eight pence and coal and oil for six days at one hundred and twenty pence and ninety yards of rope at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence and six seats and seals at one hundred and eight pence and nine men at six days at sixteen pence a man-day at nine times six times sixteen for eight hundred and sixty-four pence, and one thousand and eight plus one hundred and twenty plus two hundred and seventy plus one hundred and eight plus eight hundred and sixty-four is two thousand three hundred and seventy pence, being fourteen marks, ten shillings and tenpence.

---

The foot of that sheet is read at the close of this day as it stood before the sum went down and stands at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short, and from tomorrow it stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and the keeper wrote both figures on the face of the line and wrote the day each of them begins on.

---

Nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two this day about going down, and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour against his name and no sum is set against it. He worked the seams of a tarpaulin at the third lay from about ten until two and read the answer twice and folded it into the inside of the book on the shelf, and nobody asked him what he was going to do with it.

---

At about six in the evening the boat came out of the water at the landing below that yard with nine men in her, the man of about thirty-two and the woman of about thirty-three who stands in the tender and the man of about thirty-eight in her, and the man who keeps the leaf went aboard with the register copy in a bag and the second book was not in the bag. The keeper wrote the hour and wrote that the boat was going out again in the morning, and the reason given for that was the work on the seats, and no reason was written against the going.

---

At about half past five on that Thursday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about one thousand and eleven words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had read the answer off that page at about one in the morning's words.

"**The eleventh trip of that water went up at about eleven this morning, which was the hundred and seventy-second charge, and the covers were put on at that hour and lay there until four. Nobody has asked the four men who carry that water what it is for since the ninth of June and nobody asked them again today, and about five people drew from it after four without waiting on anybody. I wrote the charge number and the hours.**"

"**At about one the man of about thirty-eight came down this lane on foot with a page in his hand and gave it to the man of about thirty-two at the door of this shed, and the man of about thirty-two was ashore all day and went on no water until about six in the evening, and said, and said it had come up that coast road in the morning in a hand he knew and that he had carried it down himself. The page carried one answer and the hour of it and nothing else, and no name on it and no name of the man on the line. The woman of thirty of no office wrote that a person who has put himself into a thing without being asked is not to be taken out of it by the people standing round it, and that a thing taken out of by the people round it is a thing the people round it are holding, and that a person is not a bell. She wrote under that that a page printing the question and the answer and the names of the people who did not agree with either is the only page that can be answered, and he said out loud in about nine of us that a page printing who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing and that he said so in June and was not answered and is saying it again. About four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong. That is not settled and no page in this yard settles it, and an answer that came up a coast road is not an answer to him.**"

"**At about three the man of about thirty-eight said what that bell had done in the day, in hours and in about nine. It was run at ten and at two and at four and the man of about fifty-six was on the line the whole of it, and he was asked nothing and gave nothing, and at about two he asked one thing, being whether the chain above that bell had moved at all, and the man of about thirty-eight said it had not.**"

"**At about four I took the money sheet off its nail and named this fourth day of August out loud in about nine as the day line ten settles, and I said the work was done on the second and that both days are on the face of the line. The five parts are six times one hundred and sixty-eight for one thousand and eight pence of boat hired at the figure in chalk, one hundred and twenty pence of coal and oil for six days, ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence of rope, one hundred and eight pence for six seats and seals, and nine times six times sixteen for eight hundred and sixty-four pence of nine men at six days. One thousand and eight plus one hundred and twenty plus two hundred and seventy plus one hundred and eight plus eight hundred and sixty-four is two thousand three hundred and seventy pence, being fourteen marks, ten shillings and tenpence. I read the five parts with the multiplications and then the sum, and the sum went into the foot after the reading and not before it.**"

"**The foot of that sheet is read at the close of today as it stood before that sum went down, and stands at one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and eight thousand one hundred and forty-four pence short, and from tomorrow it stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. I wrote both figures on the face of the line and I wrote the day each of them begins on.**"

"**Nobody put words to the man of about thirty-two about going down today and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for this Thursday. At about six the boat came out of the water at the landing below this yard with nine men in her and the man of about thirty-two and the woman of about thirty-three and the man of about thirty-eight in her, and I went aboard with the register copy in a bag, and the second book was not in the bag.**"

"**And the last of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and fifty-eight days without one. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-three days today and nothing settled or answered this afternoon touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a settlement at about four, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**LINE TEN WAS SETTLED INTO THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET AT ABOUT FOUR CARRYING TWO DAYS ON ITS FACE, BEING THE SECOND FOR THE WORK AND THE FOURTH FOR THE DAY IT SETTLES, BEING TWO THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE, BEING FOURTEEN MARKS, TEN SHILLINGS AND TENPENCE, IN FIVE PARTS, AND THE FOOT STEPS TOMORROW TO ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TEN THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN PENCE SHORT. THE ANSWER TO ONE QUESTION CAME UP THAT COAST ROAD IN ANOTHER HAND AND IS NOT SETTLED. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the fourth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventy-second charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that at about one the answer to one question put up that coast road on the Wednesday came down with the man of about thirty-eight in the hand of the woman of thirty of no office, with the hour against it and no name on it, being that a person who has put himself into a thing without being asked is not to be taken out of it by the people standing round it, and that a person is not a bell, and under that that a page printing the question and the answer and the names of those who did not agree is the only page that can be answered; and that the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine that a page printing who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing, and that he said so in June and was not answered and is saying it again, and that about four agreed with each and the matter is open; and that at about three the man of about thirty-eight said that the Ninth Bell was run at ten and at two and at four and that the man of about fifty-six was on the line the whole of it and was asked nothing and gave nothing; and that line ten was settled into the foot of the money sheet at about four, being the tenth line on that sheet and not a second, the fourth day having been named out loud before any figure was written, being six times one hundred and sixty-eight for one thousand and eight pence and one hundred and twenty pence for coal and oil and ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence of rope and one hundred and eight pence for six seats and seals and nine times six times sixteen for eight hundred and sixty-four pence, being two thousand three hundred and seventy pence; and that the foot was read at the close of that day as it stood before the sum went down, being one hundred and eighteen thousand seven hundred and eighty-four pence out, and stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short from the fifth, both figures being written on the face of the line with the day each begins on; and that the boat came out of the water at the landing below that shed at about six with nine men in her, the man of about thirty-two having been ashore all day until that hour, and went out again in the morning; and that nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two on that day about going down, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about one the reason the answer was folded into the book on the shelf and not kept loose is that a loose page in a coat is a page that can be shown to one person at a time, and a page in a book is a page that anybody standing at that stone can take out and read; that at about three the reason the man of about thirty-eight said the chain had not moved is that a man who keeps a line for four months will say what his line has done before he will say what he hopes it will do, and this yard took the hours and wrote them and wrote no reason against them; that at about four the reason the settlement was read out in its parts before the sum went down is that a foot is a total and a total is a thing a stranger cannot argue with, and the only defence a total has is that the five parts it is made of were said out loud where about five people who cannot read a column could hear them; and that at about four the reason the second book was not put in the bag is that this yard's boat is not this yard's record, and a record that travels in a boat is a record that arrives wet.*
