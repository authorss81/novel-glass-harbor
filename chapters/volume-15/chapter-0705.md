# Chapter 705

*Saturday the Twenty-Fifth Day of June, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Line Three Settled Into the Foot of That Money Sheet at About Four in the Afternoon and the Total Moved From One Hundred and Twelve Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixteen Pence Out to One Hundred and Thirteen Thousand Two Hundred and Ten, the Multiplication Said Out Loud and Written Under the Line as Well, a Man Who Was on That Water for About Eight Hours Going Up His Own Steps at About Five With a Hand on the Rail, No Figure of Money and No Figure of Time Against Any Part of That, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Thirty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand and Sixty-Four*

Saturday the twenty-fifth day of June, with that market open from about nine and the bench stone in shadow until noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. Line three was settled into the foot of that money sheet at about four in the afternoon, and the total moved from one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out to one hundred and thirteen thousand two hundred and ten pence out. A man of about thirty-two of that harbor went up his own steps at about five in the afternoon with a hand on the rail and he was not asked to go up them and he did not say that he could not. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten in the morning and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four in the afternoon, and under that slate at the close of the day is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and third day. Four hundred and five days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and forty-third day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and ninth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and twenty-seven days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and eighty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its two hundred and ninety-seventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fourteenth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and forty-five days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and three days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Line three went onto the sheet on Thursday with the day the work was done against it, and it is settled into the total on Saturday afternoon because the work day and the settlement day on this run of days are always two days apart. The man who keeps the leaf laid the sheet flat on the bench stone at about four and read each part out loud before he wrote the total under it. About nine people stood round the stone, and about four of them cannot read a column and listened to the multiplication the way people listen to a bell.

The line is ninety yards of line at three pence the yard, being two hundred and seventy pence, and two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence, being thirty-six pence, and a watch-glass and an independent line for the tender at sixty pence, being sixty pence, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, being four times two times sixteen, which is one hundred and twenty-eight pence. Those four are two hundred and seventy and thirty-six and sixty and one hundred and twenty-eight, and they are four hundred and ninety-four pence, being three marks, one shilling and twopence. The multiplication is printed at every one of the four parts and not only at the total, and the yard said it out loud and wrote it under the line as well.

---

The sheet stands at one hundred and thirteen thousand two hundred and ten pence out from about four in the afternoon and stood at one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen before that. What goes in is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is one hundred and two thousand five hundred and seventy pence short, being six hundred and thirty-seven marks and fourteen shillings. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the new total under the old one with the hour against it, and about four people watched the figures go down and said nothing.

The man who was on that water for about eight hours on Friday is the man who cannot get up his own steps without the rail on Saturday. He went up at about five in the afternoon with a hand on the rail and he stopped twice on the way, and nobody helped him and nobody offered to help him. He has an aftertaste on top of one he had already, and his left knee is stiff from standing in a boat, and there is no figure of money against that and no figure of time against it either. The cost of eight hours goes into the second column with hours against it and no figure of money against any part of it, because a body made tired by work is not a line on a sheet.

---

At about half past three a woman of thirty of no office stood at the bench stone and read the Friday line of the page of that week out loud. She read that eleven households went and this yard was not asked, and she said the Saturday line should carry the settlement of line three with both days on its face, so that a stranger can see which day a figure arrived on. The man who keeps the leaf wrote it as she said, and the Saturday line says the line was worked on the twenty-third and settled on the twenty-fifth, with the multiplication at every part.

About four people in that nine said the reading of figures out loud was a new thing and about four said nothing. The man who keeps the leaf said it was a thing he should have done in May, when the sheet carried two lines and nobody who cannot read a column could check either of them. He said a total that four of the nine cannot check is a total that five of the nine own, and that is not a way to keep a sheet on this coast.

---

Line three was settled into the foot of that sheet on that Friday and at about half past five on that Saturday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and eighty-two words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had watched the total go down at about four.

"**Line three was worked on Thursday and is settled this afternoon, because the work day and the settlement day on this run of days are always two days apart. The line is ninety yards at three pence the yard for two hundred and seventy, and two seats at eighteen pence for thirty-six, and a glass and a line for sixty, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day for one hundred and twenty-eight, and those four are four hundred and ninety-four pence, being three marks, one shilling and twopence. The multiplication is printed at every one of the four parts and not only at the total, and I have said it out loud and written it under the line as well.**

"**The sheet is one hundred and thirteen thousand two hundred and ten pence out from about four this afternoon and was one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen before that, being seven hundred and four marks, six shillings and fourpence before and more now, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence, and it is one hundred and two thousand five hundred and seventy pence short. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street.**

"**The man who was on that water for about eight hours went up his own steps at about five with a hand on the rail and he was not asked to go up them and he did not say that he could not, and there is no figure of money against that and no figure of time against it either. The cost of the eight hours goes into the second column with hours against it and nothing else.**

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and eighteen days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four and nobody walked on the water. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and three days today and nothing settled on that sheet touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for eight hours, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seven lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**LINE THREE WAS SETTLED INTO THE FOOT OF THAT MONEY SHEET AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, BEING NINETY YARDS AT THREE PENCE THE YARD FOR TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY PENCE AND TWO SEATS AT EIGHTEEN PENCE FOR THIRTY-SIX PENCE AND A GLASS AND A LINE FOR SIXTY PENCE AND FOUR MEN AT TWO DAYS AT SIXTEEN PENCE A MAN-DAY FOR ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR PENCE, BEING THREE MARKS, ONE SHILLING AND TWOPENCE. THE TOTAL MOVED FROM ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN PENCE OUT TO ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE OUT. A MAN WHO WAS ON THAT WATER FOR ABOUT EIGHT HOURS WENT UP HIS OWN STEPS AT ABOUT FIVE WITH A HAND ON THE RAIL, WITH NO FIGURE OF MONEY AND NO FIGURE OF TIME AGAINST ANY PART OF THAT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-fifth day of June at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that line three was settled into the foot of that money sheet at about four in the afternoon, being ninety yards of line at three pence the yard for two hundred and seventy pence and two bell seats at eighteen pence for thirty-six pence and a watch-glass and an independent line for sixty pence and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day for one hundred and twenty-eight pence, being four hundred and ninety-four pence, being three marks, one shilling and twopence, with the multiplication printed at every one of the four parts and said out loud as well; and that the total moved from one hundred and twelve thousand seven hundred and sixteen pence out to one hundred and thirteen thousand two hundred and ten pence out; and that a man of about thirty-two went up his own steps at about five with a hand on the rail and was not asked to go up them and did not say that he could not, with no figure of money and no figure of time against any part of that.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about four in the afternoon the reason the multiplication was said out loud is that a total four of the nine cannot check is a total five of the nine own, and this yard has kept such totals since May and is done with it; that at about five the reason a man on a rail is entered with hours and no figure is that eight hours on the water leaves a body that cannot climb steps, and a figure against tiredness would be read as a price for going again; that at about half past three the reason the Saturday line carries both days on its face is that a line with one day on it hides which day a figure arrived on, and this run of days settles two days after the work; and that at about half past five the reason the slip ends on the nine hulls is that a settlement day is a day figures move, and the hulls that did not move are the thing that stayed while figures moved.*
