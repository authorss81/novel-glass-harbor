# Chapter 730

*Wednesday the Twentieth Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Two Separate Puttings of the Same Words to the Man of About Thirty-Two at Different Hours and No Answer Kept for Either, the Public Copy of First Minutes Laid Beside an Independent Pressure Bell in That Shed, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Sixty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Wednesday the twentieth day of July, with that market open from about nine and a grey morning giving way to a dry afternoon with the wind out of the west, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon. The ordinary work went on from about eight, being the pump housing gone over again and sixty yards of line coiled and checked and a boat washed down and bailed out. The public copy of one brother's first minutes lay on the dry end of that bench stone beside a sheet of figures from an independent pressure bell, and about nine people came and looked at the two and touched neither. The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine tallies of matters left standing ran a day forward on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and twenty-eighth day. Four hundred and thirty days have run on without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest remains shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are on their seven hundred and sixty-eighth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and thirty-fourth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have run three hundred and fifty-two days with no figure laid against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on its three hundred and seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and twenty-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has run to its three hundred and thirty-ninth day. A man of about fifty-one has held three hundred and seventieth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-eight days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The pump housing came apart before eight and the gaskets were laid out on sacking and gone over one by one for cracks at the rims. About four men did the work while about four more hauled sixty yards of line across the yard and coiled it down. The boat alongside the harbour side was bailed out and washed down. By about eleven the housing was back together and the coil was on the pin.

---

At about ten a man of about forty of the second lay came to the flat of that bench stone and stood beside the man of about thirty-two. He said in about nine people that he had walked that coast road in all weathers and that he puts the same words that others have put, being whether the man of about thirty-two will go down. The man of about thirty-two said he had heard the words and that he keeps no hour for an answer in that shed. The keeper wrote the hour and no name against it and the two men went back to the coil.

---

The public copy lay on the dry end of the bench stone all morning with a stone on each corner to hold it against the draught from the door. Beside it lay a sheet of figures taken off an independent pressure bell, with hours against each fall and rise and no hand but the keeper's on it. About nine people came by ones and twos and read the two across from one another without moving either stone. About four of that nine cannot read a column and the keeper read the hours out for them twice, once at about eleven and once after noon, and he did not read any meaning out of them.

---

What showed was narrow and plain and it stopped short of any meaning. The hours on the pressure sheet ran a little ahead of the words on the public copy in the early part and ran even with them toward the middle. About four people said the difference was the difference between a hand writing after and a bell writing during, and about four said a bell can be early and still be right. Nobody in that shed said the public copy was wrong and nobody said the pressure sheet was wrong, and the keeper wrote that both sheets stayed on the stone and that neither was altered.

---

At about two a woman of about forty of that creek came down the lane with a bundle of mending and stood at the end stall. She waited until the man of about thirty-two came up for water and then she put to him the same words that had been put in the morning, being whether he will go down. He said he had heard the words and that he keeps no hour for an answer in that shed, and she said she had not come for an answer and had come so that her own people would know she had spoken. The keeper wrote the hour and no name against it when he was told of it at about half past two, and the mending went on as before.

---

The two puttings were not laid together on any page in that shed. One was at the flat of the bench stone at about ten and the other was at the end stall at about two, and the page of days carries the hours and no names. About four people at the boards spoke of the morning and about four spoke of the afternoon and nobody in that nine laid the two side by side to make a sum. The man of about thirty-two went back to the pump gear after each and his hands stayed on the work, and nobody asked him for more than the hearing of the words.

---

The disagreement between the two people about what a page prints was in that shed again at about three and was not moved. He said a second book that prints a refusal twice makes a small refusal look followed, and that a page that prints who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing. She said what goes in both books is what she was told and never a name against a person, and that the same sentence in two places by one hand is one sentence and not a following. About four people agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open between them and is not settled and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it.

---

At about four the keeper took the hours off both sheets once more for about five of that nine who cannot read a column. He read the fall of the public evacuation bell and the mark of the live keel-line and the mark of the second rescue line, and he read the hours against each without adding meaning. About four people said the second rescue line looked still active at the hour of the cut and about four said a mark on paper is not a rope in a hand.

---

The reading of that creek at the end stall was steady all afternoon and about nine people waited there until about ten past six. A boat's bell off that mole answered a bell off a shoal four days down that coast road at about half past ten and again at about three, and both answers were in hours and not in names. The wash water had dried off the stones by about five and the coil was still on the pin and the housing held tight when it was tried at about half past four.

---

A page went down that coast road with an hour on it and at about half past five on that Wednesday the shortfall was laid out at that bench stone in about five hundred and thirty-one words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood by that bench stone at about ten in the morning.

"**The housing came apart before eight and the gaskets were laid on sacking and gone over for cracks. Sixty yards of line were hauled across and coiled and checked hand over hand. The boat was bailed and washed and scrubbed back to clean timber, and by eleven the housing was together and the coil was on the pin.**

"**The public copy of first minutes lay on the dry end of this stone all morning with a stone on each corner. Beside it lay a sheet off an independent pressure bell with hours against each fall and rise, and about nine came by ones and twos and read the two across. I read the hours out twice for about five who cannot read a column, once at eleven and once after noon, and I read no meaning out of them. What showed was that the bell runs a little ahead of the hand in the early part and even toward the middle, and nobody here said either sheet was wrong and neither was altered.**

"**At about ten a man of the second lay stood at the flat of this stone beside the man of about thirty-two and put the same words others have put, being whether he will go down. He said he had heard and keeps no hour for an answer in this shed. At about two a woman of that creek at the end stall put the same words when he came for water, and he gave the same hearing, and she said she had not come for an answer. The keeper wrote both hours and no names, and the two puttings were not laid together on any page and no sum was set against them on any sheet in this yard.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand seven hundred and forty-six pence out and one hundred and six thousand one hundred and six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and forty-three days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and twenty-eight days today and nothing laid on this stone this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for laying two sheets side by side without moving a stone, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THE ORDINARY WORK WENT ON FROM EIGHT AND THE PUBLIC COPY LAY BESIDE AN INDEPENDENT PRESSURE SHEET ALL DAY AND NEITHER WAS ALTERED. TWO SEPARATE PUTTINGS OF THE SAME WORDS WERE MADE AT ABOUT TEN AND ABOUT TWO AND NO HOUR FOR AN ANSWER IS KEPT IN THAT SHED. THE DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE IS OPEN AND IS NOT SETTLED. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twentieth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that the ordinary work of pump housing and line and boat washing went on from about eight until about eleven; and that the public copy of first minutes lay on the dry end of that bench stone beside a sheet of figures off an independent pressure bell with hours against each fall and rise, and that about nine came and read the two across and neither was altered; and that at about ten at the flat of the bench stone and at about two at the end stall the same words were put to the man of about thirty-two, being whether he will go down, and that he said he had heard and keeps no hour for an answer, and that the hours were written and no names; and that at about three the disagreement between two people was in that shed again and is not settled.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the hours were read twice is that about five of that party cannot read a column and a reading that leaves them out is a reading half that shed did not hear; that at about noon the reason neither sheet was moved is that two sheets laid for comparing have to stay where hands can find them again, and a stone moved for tidiness is a figure lost for the afternoon; that at about half past two the reason the two puttings were kept apart on the page is that hours with no names are not a roll, and laying morning beside afternoon to make a weight would turn hearing into urging; and that at about four the reason the marks of three lines were read without meaning is that a keeper who adds meaning to hours has taken a side, and this yard has not got a side on those sheets and is not going to get one before the finding.*
