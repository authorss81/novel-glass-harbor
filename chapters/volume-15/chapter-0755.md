# Chapter 755

*Sunday the Fourteenth Day of August, a Wipe and the Hundred and Nineteenth Time at Thirty-Six Off and Twenty-Three Bare and Thirty-Six Back Being Ninety-Five the Whole of It and the Last Wipe Before That Wall Comes Down Bare Again, the Hand of the Man of About Twenty-Nine Cold and Blue at About Four in the Afternoon and No Figure Set Against It and No Question Asked of Him, No Line on That Sheet and No Figure Written Into Its Foot, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Fourteen*

Sunday the fourteenth day of August, with that market shut until nine and a soft morning with no wind at all and a heavy swell still running in from the south-west, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and that wall behind a reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and stood bare for twenty-three minutes until about half past twelve and went back up at about a quarter to one, at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January, and it did not go up whole. That was the hundred and nineteenth time and the figures were read off a slip before the ladder went up, being thirty-six and twenty-three and thirty-six and ninety-five the whole of it, and it is a minute longer each way and a minute longer bare than the one before it and nobody has said why and nobody has asked. No line went onto that money sheet and the keeper said out loud at about four that the day carries no line and is not one of any form. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand eight hundred and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand two hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine standing things went into the second week of this run of days and stood where the morning left them. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and fifty-third day. Four hundred and fifty-five days have gone by with nobody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and ninety-third day. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-ninth night running. Those two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventy-seventh days with nothing set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and thirty-second day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and forty-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and sixty-fourth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and ninety-fifth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-three days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came off bare at about half past ten this morning and the plaster of it was the colour it has been since the last Sunday, and the ladder went up at about a quarter to one with a man's weight on it and a second man on the foot of it, and about five people stood in that lane and watched and did not offer.

Thirty-six off and twenty-three bare and thirty-six back is ninety-five the whole of it, and the keeper read the three figures twice before the ladder went up because about five of that party cannot read a column, and he wrote the hour and the day and wrote beside them no reason at all.

---

The man of about thirty-two was on the stones at about eleven for the whole of the time that wall stood bare and did not go inside and did not go up a ladder and did not go near the water.

He said out loud at about half past eleven that he had not asked to be asked and was not going to be asked, and that if a man stands on a Sunday in front of bare plaster and waits for somebody to come and ask him whether he will go down then the man who comes and asks has moved the thing and not the person.

---

At about half past twelve the man of about twenty-nine put his left hand flat on that bench stone and found it cold and found it wet and took it away again and put it in his coat.

Nobody in that lane asked him why and nobody asked him to do it again and nobody said his name out loud at any hour of that day, and the keeper wrote the hour and wrote that the hand was cold and wrote that nobody asked and wrote no figure of money against any part of it.

He is not on the water and he has not been on the water in this harbor since the twentieth day of June and nothing said this morning brought him nearer it.

---

At about one the woman of thirty of no office read the entry she wrote on the Tuesday out loud at the flat of that stone, in about nine people, once, being the request that was made on the sixth and the refusal that was given on the sixth and the hour of each.

She said out loud that she was not going to read it a second time because a thing read twice in one week is a thing the room has begun to own, and about four of that nine agreed with her and about four said nothing.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine people that he had read the same entry at half past four yesterday and had not asked her to change one word of it then and was not going to ask her to change one word of it now, and that a man who reads a thing to find the sentence he wants has not read it.

The woman of thirty of no office said that was the first thing he had said since June that she did not have an answer ready for, and that she was not going to say whether that was a compliment, and about four of that nine said nothing and nobody said either was wrong.

---

At about three a boat of about twenty tons went out of that quay with four men in her and a man of about thirty-eight of that landing in her, and the ninth bell at Orison was not rung and was not rung on any day since about half past two in the morning of the ninth, and that was said out loud in about nine and no figure was set against any hour of it.

The boat was going for the two sections of that bell that came down on Wednesday and was not going for the man and was not going to bring anything back, and about four of that nine asked where she was going and about four did not ask.

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday went on at a Sunday's pace and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine and a boy of that yard sweeping that slip at about two because there was nothing else for him and being told to sweep and nothing more.

The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it, and that man of about sixty-one did not come down to that shed today.

---

The boat was going for two sections of that bell and not for the man and at about half past five on that Sunday the whole of that day was put out loud at that stone, in about one thousand and forty-three words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood in that lane at about half past ten watching that wall stand bare for twenty-three minutes.

Thirty-six off and twenty-three bare and thirty-six back is ninety-five the whole of it, and I read the three figures twice before the ladder went up because about five of that party cannot read a column, and I wrote the hour and the day and wrote beside them no reason at all. "**And the rest of it. The pump ran clean from about nine and a boy of this yard swept this slip at about two because there was nothing else for him and was told to sweep and nothing more. The sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at that end stall all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it, and that man of about sixty-one did not come down to this shed today. No line went onto that money sheet and I said out loud at about four that the day carries no line and is not one of any form. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand eight hundred and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand two hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. No tin in this shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and sixty-eight days without one. Twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings, with a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-three days today and nothing put on a stone or a page today touched that figure by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the wall and nobody was thanked for the entry that was read out once, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THAT WALL CAME OFF BARE AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND STOOD BARE FOR TWENTY-THREE MINUTES AND WENT BACK UP AT THE ANGLE IT HAS HELD SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, BEING THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH TIME AT THIRTY-SIX OFF, TWENTY-THREE BARE AND THIRTY-SIX BACK AND NINETY-FIVE THE WHOLE OF IT. NO LINE WENT ONTO THAT MONEY SHEET. THE HEARING IN THE LEFT SIDE OF A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THREE DAYS AND IS NOT IMPROVED BY ANYTHING THAT HAPPENED TODAY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the fourteenth day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was the hundred and nineteenth time and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday; and that the wall behind a reeve's bench came off bare at about half past ten and stood bare for twenty-three minutes until about half past twelve and went back up at about a quarter to one at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January and did not go up whole, the three figures being read off a slip before the ladder went up and read twice because about five of that party cannot read a column, and no reason being written against the slowness and nobody having asked for one; and that the man of about thirty-two was on the stones at about eleven for the whole of that time and went on no water and said out loud at about half past eleven that he had not asked to be asked and was not going to be asked; and that at about half past twelve the man of about twenty-nine put his left hand flat on that bench stone and found it cold and found it wet and took it away and put it in his coat, that nobody asked him why and nobody asked him to do it again and nobody said his name out loud at any hour of that day, and that he is not on the water and has not been on the water in that harbor since the twentieth day of June; and that at about one the woman of thirty of no office read the entry she wrote on the Tuesday out loud once in about nine, being the request made on the sixth and the refusal given on the sixth and the hour of each, and said she was not going to read it a second time because a thing read twice in one week is a thing the room has begun to own; and that at about two the man of about thirty-two said out loud that he had read that entry at half past four the day before and was not going to ask her to change one word of it then or now and that a man who reads a thing to find the sentence he wants has not read it, and that she said that was the first thing he had said since June that she did not have an answer ready for and that she was not going to say whether that was a compliment; and that at about three a boat of about twenty tons went out of that quay with four men in her and a man of about thirty-eight of that landing in her, being going for the two sections of that bell that came down on Wednesday and not going for the man and not going to bring anything back; and that the ninth bell at Orison was not rung and has not been rung on any day since about half past two in the morning of the ninth and no figure was set against any hour of that; and that the keeper said out loud at about four that that day carries no line and is not one of any form.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about one the reason the entry was read out once and not twice is that a second reading is not a courtesy to a room that did not hear it the first time, it is the beginning of a habit, and a thing read on a Sunday and again on the Wednesday is a thing read on the Wednesday by a room that was told on the Sunday that it had already had it; that at about half past eleven the reason the man of about thirty-two stood on the stones in front of bare plaster instead of inside is that a wall with nothing on it is the only thing in that harbor a man can stand in front of for an hour without having to be anything, and a man who waits in a doorway is waiting for somebody; that at about two the reason he read that entry and asked for no change is that a record which is softened once for a living person is a record which will be softened twice, and the second softening is always for a person who is not alive and the first softening is what taught him to ask; and that at about four the reason no line went onto that sheet on a Sunday is that a line that is worked and settled on the same page and in the same week is a line a reader checks, and a line put on a day a yard is shut is a line nobody can check.*
