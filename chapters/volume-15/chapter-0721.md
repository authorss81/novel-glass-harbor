# Chapter 721

*Monday the Eleventh Day of July, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Sixty-Fifth Charge on That Cistern, Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked on by None of It, Line Seven on That Sheet Carrying Two Days on Its Face, the Ninth for the Work and the Eleventh for the Day Four Stones of That Creek Were Let Go, Being Seven Hundred and Thirty-Two Pence in Four Parts and Settled on Tuesday, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Fifty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand One Hundred and Forty-Four*

Monday the eleventh day of July, with that market open from about nine and a hot noon off the water, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-fifth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Line seven went onto that money sheet at about two in the afternoon carrying two days on its face, being the ninth of July as the day the work was done and the eleventh of July as the day four stones of that creek were let go, and it is seven hundred and thirty-two pence in four parts and it is settled into the foot of the sheet on Tuesday. Three people of the Lowfen Reed went home in the course of that day without saying why to anybody in this party. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and nineteenth day. Four hundred and twenty-one days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and fifty-ninth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and twenty-fifth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and forty-third days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and ninety-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and thirteenth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and thirtieth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and sixty-first days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and nineteen days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixty-fifth time and the covers went on at the same hour as last Thursday. About four people came for water after four and were served without waiting. The man who keeps the leaf wrote the charge number and the hours on the page of that week and did not write anything else against the morning.

---

At about ten in the morning a man of about twenty-two of the Lowfen Reed came down that coast road alone, and he is not the boy whose hand is at the head of the third column of that leaf, and nobody in that nine said anything about that and nobody asked him a question.

He said four stones had been let go at that creek before eleven and that his community had done it and that it was done. He said the stones were the two at the mouth and the two inside the bend and that they had gone on a slack tide with a barge line under them and that the water had taken them out of sight inside about ten minutes, and that the channel is narrower this morning and everybody in that creek knows it.

He said nobody told him to do it and no paper came about it and that the man of about forty-seven who laid the stroke laid it on a page and not on a stone, and that this is a different thing and he wanted that said in a shed where about nine people were standing.

---

The keeper wrote it down with the hour and wrote the four stones down and wrote that they went on a slack tide with a barge line under them and that the water had them out of sight in about ten minutes. He asked one question, being whether the channel being narrower this morning would want a new page from that community in the spring, and the man said it would and that they would give it or not give it as they chose.

Nobody in that shed said that was a loss, and nobody in that shed said it was right, and the man of about thirty-two said one thing, which is that a line's own people letting go of their own stones is the one thing on this coast this yard has no standing to have an opinion about, and about four people agreed and about four said nothing.

---

At about two the keeper wrote line seven onto that money sheet with two days on its face. It is four times thirty for one hundred and twenty pence of stone cut and faced, and eighty-four pence of line and shackles for the four, and six times two times sixteen for one hundred and ninety-two pence of men, and two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence of boat. Those four are one hundred and twenty and eighty-four and one hundred and ninety-two and three hundred and thirty-six, and they are seven hundred and thirty-two pence, being four marks, seven shillings and eightpence, and it is settled into the foot of the sheet on Tuesday.

The ninth of July stands on that line as the day the work was done and the eleventh of July stands on it as the day four stones of that creek were let go, and the keeper wrote the second of those days in its own words and said the words out loud before he wrote the figure, because a line that carries a day has to say what the day is.

Nobody said the line ought to come off the sheet, and it is not struck and it is not cut back, and the work was done and the stone was cut and the men were there.

---

Three people of that line went home in the course of the day. The man of about twenty-two went up the road at about eleven. A woman of about forty-nine went at about one and was seen getting into a cart. A man of about sixty went at about four and walked as far as the top of the hill. The keeper wrote the hour of each and no reason against any of them.

---

At about half past five on that Monday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about six hundred and thirteen words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had heard a man of about twenty-two say at about ten that no paper came about four stones.

"**Eleven trips went up that cistern this morning and the covers went on at about eleven, the same hour they went on last Thursday, and that was the hundred and sixty-fifth filling of it. A man of about twenty-two came down that road alone at about ten and said four stones had been let go at that creek before eleven, and that his community did it and it was done, and that no paper came about it and no man told him to, and that the man who laid the stroke laid it on a page and not on a stone, and he wanted that said in front of about nine people. The two at the mouth and the two inside the bend went on a slack tide with a barge line under them and the water had them out of sight inside about ten minutes, and the channel is narrower this morning.**

"**The man of about thirty-two said that a line's own people letting go of their own stones is the one thing on this coast this yard has no standing to have an opinion about, and about four agreed and about four said nothing. He was asked one thing, being whether that channel would want a new page in the spring, and the answer was that it would and that they would give it or not give it as they chose.**

"**Line seven went onto that sheet at about two carrying two days on its face, being four times thirty for one hundred and twenty of stone, and eighty-four of line and shackles, and six times two times sixteen for one hundred and ninety-two of men, and two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six of boat, and those four are seven hundred and thirty-two pence, being four marks, seven shillings and eightpence, and it is settled on Tuesday. The ninth stands on it as the day the work was done and the eleventh stands on it as the day four stones of that creek were let go, and I said that second day out loud before I wrote the figure. The line is not struck and it is not cut back and no one said it ought to come off, because the work was done and the stone was cut and the men were there.**

"**The sheet stands at one hundred and sixteen thousand and fourteen pence out and one hundred and five thousand three hundred and seventy-four pence short, and what goes into it is ten thousand six hundred and forty pence. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and thirty-four days without one.**

"**And the last of it. The far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and nineteen days today and nothing let go of four stones this morning touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for four stones that went out of sight in ten minutes, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-three lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**FOUR STONES OF THE LOWFEN REED WERE LET GO BEFORE ELEVEN ON THAT MORNING BY THAT COMMUNITY'S OWN PEOPLE, ON A SLACK TIDE, WITH NO PAPER AND NO MAN TOLD, AND THIS YARD DID NOT ASK AND DID NOT TAKE THEM BACK AND SAID NOTHING EITHER WAY. LINE SEVEN CARRIES THE NINTH AND THE ELEVENTH ON ITS FACE AND IS SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-TWO PENCE, BEING FOUR MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND IS SETTLED ON TUESDAY. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN THOUSAND AND FOURTEEN PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIVE THOUSAND THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the eleventh day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-fifth charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that a man of about twenty-two of the Lowfen Reed came down that road at about ten and said four stones of that creek were let go before eleven by that community's own people on a slack tide with a barge line under them, and that the water had them out of sight inside about ten minutes and that no paper came about it and no man told him to; and that he said the man who laid the stroke laid it on a page and not on a stone, and that nobody asked him a question about anything else; and that the man of about thirty-two said a line's own people letting go of their own stones is the one thing this yard has no standing to have an opinion about; and that line seven went onto that sheet at about two carrying the ninth of July as the day the work was done and the eleventh of July as the day four stones of that creek were let go, being four times thirty pence for one hundred and twenty pence and eighty-four pence for line and shackles and six times two times sixteen pence for one hundred and ninety-two pence of men and two times one hundred and sixty-eight pence for three hundred and thirty-six pence of boat, being seven hundred and thirty-two pence, being four marks, seven shillings and eightpence, and is settled on the twelfth.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about ten in the morning the reason no boat was offered and no hand was sent after four stones is that a line's own people letting go of their own stones is the one act on this coast that cannot be undone by this yard, and a yard that undoes it has taken the line; that at about two in the afternoon the reason the day of the letting go is named in its own words before the figure is written is that a line which carries a day that is not the day the work was done is a line whose second day has to be legible, or a stranger will think the arithmetic is wrong; that at about half past two the reason the line is not struck is that the work was done and the stone was cut and the men were there on the ninth, and a sheet that stops carrying work done because the reason for it has gone is a sheet that stops being a record and becomes an opinion; and that at about four the reason three people going home are entered by the hour only is that the keeper of this leaf is the only man in that shed who writes things down and a reason written by him about a man he did not ask is a thing that man will read back in nine runs of days.*
