# Chapter 735

*Monday the Twenty-Fifth Day of July, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Sixty-Ninth Charge on That Cistern in Eleven Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, One Person Putting the Same Words to the Man of About Thirty-Two About Going Down and No Second Person Putting Them, Line Nine Carrying Two Days on Its Face and Not Settled This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Sixty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Fourteen*

Monday the twenty-fifth day of July, with that market open from about nine and a hard bright morning with the wind out of the east by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-ninth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. One person put the same words to the man of about thirty-two in that shed about going down, and no second person put them to him on that day, and the keeper wrote the hour and no name against it. Line nine carries two days on its face, being the twenty-fourth for the work and the twenty-sixth for the day the descent was stopped, and it is not settled this day. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, being sixty-six marks, six shillings and eightpence. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine tallies of work left undone moved a day forward on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and thirty-third day. Four hundred and thirty-five days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and seventy-third day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and thirty-ninth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and fifty-seventh days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its three hundred and twelfth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and twenty-seventh. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and forty-fourth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and seventy-fifth days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-three days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and sixty-ninth time and the covers went on at the same hour as the other charges this fortnight. About four people came for water after four and were served without waiting, and the keeper wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that the next charge falls on a Thursday. The boards at the back of that market were busy by eleven with the ordinary trade, and none of the trade touched the water in that butt except to draw from it.

---

The ordinary work went on all morning, being the wash of the shed floor and the tarring of a chafed strop and the going over of the tender gear on the landing. About four men spread the independent line along the flat of that bench stone and checked it arm over arm for chafe and for soft places, and a woman of about thirty-three with a watch on her wrist held the glass to the light and wiped it and put it back in its wrap. Nobody set a figure against the morning, and nobody wrote a second line about the gear.

---

At about two one person stood at the flat of that bench stone and put the same words to the man of about thirty-two in about nine people, being whether he will go down. The man of about thirty-two said he had heard the words and that he keeps no hour for an answer in that shed, and he said it without heat and without moving from where he stood by the pump. About four people heard the words plainly and about four were at the end stall and heard them from across the yard, and nobody repeated them after.

---

The keeper wrote the hour and no name against the asking, and he did not put the asking together with any other asking on any page. No second person put the same words to him on that Monday, and the keeper wrote no second hour and no sum was set against the day. The page of days carries the one hour and no name, and the hour is in the hand that keeps the leaf and not in any other hand. About four people asked afterwards what the hour had been, and the keeper said the hour is on the page and the page is not read out in that shed.

---

The woman of about thirty-three with the watch stood by the tender gear all afternoon and went over the cutoff with the man of about thirty-two beside her. She showed him the shackle and the independent line and the place where her hand falls on it, and she said she pulls it without asking anybody and without waiting for any other hand. He said he had seen it and that the reason is on the page with an hour against it, and about four people watched and none of them touched the gear.

---

The disagreement was in that shed again at about four in about nine people, and it is open between the two people and no page carries a resolution of it. He said a mark put on by two hands is a mark with two owners and that a book kept in two places is a book kept against loss and also against him, and she said what goes in both books is what she was told and never a name against a person and that two marks on one outside is one custody and not two owners. He said he knew what he had been objecting to in June and that he had not moved from it, and she said she knew that too and that she was still keeping the second book.

---

At about half past four the bell off that mole answered the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road, and the two answers were in hours and not in names. About nine people waited at the end stall until the second answer came, and the keeper wrote the hours and no reason against them. The reading of that creek was much as it had been yesterday, and nobody from that creek came down that road with a time, and nobody in that shed said the line was now smaller or larger.

---

That cistern took its eleventh trip at about eleven and at about half past five on that Monday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about five hundred and ninety words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had watched that cistern take its eleventh trip at about eleven.

"**That water in that butt lay still from nine until four under its covers, and it was drawn in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven for the hundred and sixty-ninth time and walked on by none of it, and about four came for water after four and were served without waiting. The boards at the back of that market were busy by eleven with the ordinary trade, and none of the trade touched the water except to draw from it.**

"**At about two one person stood at the flat of this stone and put the same words to the man of about thirty-two in about nine people, being whether he will go down, and he said he had heard the words and that he keeps no hour for an answer in this shed. I wrote the hour and no name against the asking, and no second person put the same words to him on this Monday, and I wrote no second hour and no sum is set against the day on any sheet in this yard.**

"**The woman of about thirty-three with the watch stood by the tender gear all afternoon and went over the cutoff beside him, and she showed the shackle and the independent line and the place where her hand falls, and she pulls it without asking anybody and without waiting for any other hand. About four watched and none touched the gear, and the reason is on the page with an hour against it and no figure of money stands against any part of it.**

"**The disagreement was in this shed again at about four, and he said a mark by two hands is a mark with two owners and she said two marks on one outside is one custody and not two owners, and he said he had not moved from June and she said she was still keeping the second book, and about four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open and no page carries a resolution of it. At about half past four that bell off that mole answered the bell off a shoal four days down that coast road, and the two answers were in hours and not in names.**

"**And the last of it. The sheet stands at one hundred and seventeen thousand seven hundred and thirty-six pence out and one hundred and seven thousand and ninety-six pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. Four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one less two thousand one hundred and eighty-one is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and forty-eight days without one. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and thirty-three days today and nothing charged or asked this Monday touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for standing through a rota morning, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**A ROTA DAY AND THE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-NINTH CHARGE IN ELEVEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND ONE PERSON PUT THE SAME WORDS TO THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO ABOUT GOING DOWN AND NO SECOND PERSON PUT THEM. LINE NINE CARRIES TWO DAYS ON ITS FACE AND IS NOT SETTLED THIS DAY. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVEN THOUSAND AND NINETY-SIX PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

---

---

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-fifth day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that that cistern was charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight and about eleven and was walked on by none of it, being the hundred and sixty-ninth charge, with the covers lying on it from about eleven until about four; and that at about two one person put the same words to the man of about thirty-two in about nine people about going down, and no second person put them to him on that day, and the keeper wrote the hour and no name against it and no sum was set against the day; and that line nine carries two days on its face, being the twenty-fourth for the work and the twenty-sixth for the day the descent was stopped, and it was not settled on that day; and that the disagreement between two people about what a page prints was in that shed at about four and is not settled and no page carries a resolution of it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven the reason the charge number went beside the hours is that a charge that is not written beside its hours is a charge nobody can take off the page after, and the next charge falls on a Thursday; that at about two the reason only one hour went on the page of days is that only one person spoke the words, and a second hour nobody heard is a second asking nobody made; that at about three the reason the cutoff was gone over without any other hand touching it is that gear a tender must pull without asking has to be gear nobody else has taken in hand that day; and that at about four the reason the two answers of the bells were written in hours and not in names is that an answer in names is an answer somebody owns, and this yard owns no bell and no answer.*
