# Chapter 748

*Sunday the Seventh Day of August, a Wipe and the Hundred and Eighteenth Time at Thirty-Five Off and Twenty-Two Bare and Thirty-Five Back Being Ninety-Two the Whole of It, the Mechanism at Orison Found in Sections at About Half Past Twelve on the Saturday That Cannot Be Used as a Whole, No Line on That Sheet and No Figure Written Into Its Foot, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Sunday the seventh day of August, with that market shut until nine and a warm morning with the water flat and no wind in it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A wipe and not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday, and that wall behind that bench stood bare from about five to twelve until about half past twelve and was back at about a quarter to one, being the hundred and eighteenth time at thirty-five off and twenty-two bare and thirty-five back and ninety-two the whole of it. No line went onto that money sheet and no figure was written into its foot, and the keeper said out loud at about four that the day carries no line and is not one of any form. The mechanism at Orison was found in sections at about half past twelve yesterday afternoon and nine sections cannot be used as a whole, and eight of them answer and will not do the work the whole was doing, and the ninth has not come off. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and forty-sixth day. Four hundred and forty-eight days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and eighty-sixth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-second night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventieth days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-fifth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fortieth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fifty-seventh day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and eighty-eighth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-six days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came down bare at about half past ten and the ladder went up at about a quarter to one, at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January, and it did not go up whole. The three figures were read off a slip before the ladder went up, being thirty-five and twenty-two and thirty-five and ninety-two the whole of it, and the keeper read them twice because about five of that party cannot read a column, and he wrote them with the hour and the day and no reason against the slowness.

---

The boat came in over the bar at about ten that morning with nine men in her, and the man of about thirty-two came down the ramp with his hand on the rail and did not take his hand off it until he was on the stones, and the woman of about thirty-three came down beside him and said nothing about that and told nobody else about it either. About four of that nine noticed and about four did not, and the keeper wrote the hour and wrote the rail and wrote no reason.

---

At about one the woman of about thirty-three gave her account of what happened in that room at Orison yesterday afternoon, in about nine people, and she gave it once and she gave it in hours. The chain above that bell came apart at about half past twelve into nine pieces, and the man of about thirty-eight took the nine off the frame in about twenty minutes and laid them along the bench in that room in the order they came off.

---

Eight of the nine answer when they are rung, and the man of about thirty-eight said that he rang each of the eight on Saturday evening and each of the eight gave back the answer that section has always given, and that not one of the eight will do the work the whole was doing, and that the work of the whole was taking nine answers and making one answer out of them and that this is the only thing any of the nine can do.

---

The ninth of the nine has not come off. The man of about thirty-eight said the ninth is the section that holds the bell's own answer and that the ninth is the section with a man on a line in about nineteen fathoms behind it, and that he is not going to name what he thinks is holding it together in that room with nine people standing in it.

---

The man of about thirty-two said out loud at about two at the flat of that bench stone, in about nine people, that the man of about fifty-six is the ninth section, and that when the ninth section goes the man goes with it, and that he was not going to be pulled out of it, and that he was not going to ask anybody else to pull him out of it either. Nobody in that shed called that anything and nobody in that shed said it was right and nobody was thanked for it and the keeper wrote the words and the hour and no reason.

---

The woman of thirty of no office said out loud, at the same hour, in the same nine people, that a person is not a bell, and that she wrote those words up a coast road on Thursday and that the man who wrote the question has just agreed with them in his own mouth and does not know that he has. He said that was not the same thing, and she said it was, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him and nobody said either was wrong.

---

He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and that she was still keeping the second book on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him and the matter is open and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it, and nobody in that nine settled anything and nobody in that nine was thanked.

---

No one in that shed said those words to the man of about thirty-two about going down on that Sunday, and no one said them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour against his name and no sum is set against it on any sheet there. He sat on the far bench from about two until about four with his back against the wall and could not hear out of the left side of his head, and the quiet around that page settled nothing and proved nothing.

---

At about four the keeper stood at the flat of that bench stone and said out loud that the day carries no line. He said yesterday settled nothing into the foot of that sheet and today writes nothing into it and measures nothing for it, and about nine people heard him and nobody asked him for a figure, and the sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at the end stall with no figure moved by one.

---

At about half past five on that Sunday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about nine hundred words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood in that lane that morning watching that wall stand bare from about five to twelve.

"**The ladder went to that wall at about half past ten this morning and the plaster stood bare behind the reeve's bench until about half past twelve, and the wall was back at about a quarter to one at the angle it has held since the twelfth of January and it did not go up whole. I read the three figures off a slip before the ladder went up, being thirty-five off and twenty-two bare and thirty-five back and ninety-two the whole of it, and I read them twice because about five of this party cannot read a column. I wrote the day and the hour and no reason against the slowness.**"

"**The boat came in over the bar at about ten and the man of about thirty-two came down the ramp with his hand on the rail and did not take it off until he was on the stones, and the woman of about thirty-three came down beside him and said nothing about that and told nobody else. About four of that nine noticed and about four did not, and I wrote the hour and wrote the rail and wrote no reason. He sat on the far bench from about two until about four with his back against the wall and could not hear out of the left side of his head, and nobody put words to him about going down today and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for this Sunday.**"

"**At about one the woman of about thirty-three gave her account of that room at Orison once, in about nine of us, in hours. The chain above that bell came apart at about half past twelve yesterday into nine pieces, and the man of about thirty-eight took the nine off the frame in about twenty minutes and laid them along the bench in the order they came off. Eight of the nine answer when they are rung, and he rang each of the eight on Saturday evening and each gave back the answer that section has always given, and not one of the eight will do the work the whole was doing, and the work of the whole was taking nine answers and making one answer out of them, and that is the only thing any of the nine can do. The ninth has not come off, and the man of about thirty-eight said the ninth is the section that holds the bell's own answer and that the ninth is the section with a man on a line in about nineteen fathoms behind it, and that he was not going to name what he thinks is holding it together with nine people standing in that room.**"

"**At about two the man of about thirty-two said out loud, in about nine of us at the flat of this stone, that the man of about fifty-six is the ninth section, and that when the ninth section goes the man goes with it, and that he was not going to pull him out of it and was not going to ask anybody else to pull him out of it either. Nobody in that shed called that anything and nobody said it was right and nobody was thanked for it and I wrote the words and the hour and no reason. The woman of thirty of no office said at the same hour in the same nine that a person is not a bell, and that she wrote those words up that coast road on Thursday, and that the man who wrote the question has just agreed with them in his own mouth and does not know it. He said that was not the same thing and she said it was, and about four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong, and this is not settled and no page here settles it.**"

"**At about four I stood at the flat of this stone and said out loud that the day carries no line, and that yesterday settled nothing into the foot of that sheet and today writes nothing into it and measures nothing for it. The sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at the end stall with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it to be read.**"

"**And the last of it. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and sixty-one days without one. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-six days today and nothing bare plaster or broken this morning touched that figure by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a mechanism that came apart, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**THE MECHANISM AT ORISON WAS FOUND IN SECTIONS AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE YESTERDAY AND NINE SECTIONS CANNOT BE USED AS A WHOLE, AND EIGHT OF THEM ANSWER AND WILL NOT DO THE WORK THE WHOLE WAS DOING, AND THE NINTH HAS NOT COME OFF. THIS DAY CARRIES NO LINE AND IS NOT ONE OF ANY FORM. THE WALL STOOD BARE FROM ABOUT FIVE TO TWELVE UNTIL ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE, BEING THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEENTH TIME AT THIRTY-FIVE OFF AND TWENTY-TWO BARE AND THIRTY-FIVE BACK. THE SHEET STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOUR PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TEN THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN PENCE SHORT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the seventh day of August at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was a wipe and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that that wall behind that bench stood bare from about five to twelve until about half past twelve and was back at about a quarter to one, being the hundred and eighteenth time at thirty-five off and twenty-two bare and thirty-five back, being ninety-two the whole of it, the three figures having been read twice; and that the boat came in over the bar at about ten with nine men in her and the man of about thirty-two came down the ramp with his hand on the rail and did not take it off until he was on the stones, and the hour and the rail were written and no reason was written against either; and that at about one the woman of about thirty-three gave her account of the room at Orison once, in about nine people and in hours, being that the chain above that bell came apart at about half past twelve on the Saturday into nine pieces, that the man of about thirty-eight took the nine off the frame in about twenty minutes and laid them along the bench in the order they came off, that eight of the nine answer and were each rung on the Saturday evening and each gave back the answer that section has always given, and that not one of the eight will take nine answers and make one answer out of them, and that the ninth has not come off and is the section with a man on a line in about nineteen fathoms behind it; and that at about two the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine that the man of about fifty-six is the ninth section and that when the ninth section goes the man goes with it and that he was not going to pull him out of it and was not going to ask anybody else to, and that nobody in that shed called that anything and nobody said it was right and nobody was thanked for it; and that the woman of thirty of no office said at the same hour that a person is not a bell and that the man who wrote the question has agreed with it in his own mouth, and that about four agreed with each and the matter is open; and that at about four the keeper said out loud that the day carries no line and that nothing was written into the foot of that sheet and nothing measured for it; and that nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two on that day about going down, and no hour and no name went on the page of days for it.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about one the reason the account was given once and in hours is that a mechanism taken apart is not an opinion and has no author, and an account of it that were given twice in a Sunday evening would be two accounts by Thursday; that at about two the reason the words about the ninth section were written down and nothing else was written against them is that a man saying out loud in nine people that he will not pull a man out of a thing is a thing said, and a thing said can be read back to him, and a thing said has no figure on it and no figure will be set; that at about two the reason the woman of thirty of no office answered him in the same nine and in the same hour is that an answer to a thing said in a shed is a thing said in a shed, and she had written the same words up a coast road three days before and a page going up a road is a slower thing than a mouth; and that at about four the reason the day was said empty out loud is that a day with nothing on it invites a later hand to write something small into it, and a day said empty in nine people stays empty.*
