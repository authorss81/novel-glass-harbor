# Chapter 750

*Tuesday the Ninth Day of August, the Ninety-Third Wage Tuesday With the Column Unmoved at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks, the Mechanism at Orison Having Lost Its Living Custodian and No Body Named and No Figure of Any Kind Printed Against It, One Request Refused on the Saturday and the Choice Going to the Surrounding People and the Independent Records and the Person Who Made It Still Inside, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Tuesday the ninth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a heavy sky with the swell still running in from the south-west and the wind gone round to the north in the night, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**The ninety-third wage Tuesday and not a rota day and not a wipe, and the column on the lid of that step read out at about ten in the morning in four minutes by a man of about thirty-one of the second lay and standing at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and not moved on that day, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it shut from ten until four, and a further week standing fallen due at the close of it. The mechanism at Orison lost its living custodian at about half past two in the morning of this day, being the ninth section coming off the frame, and a man of about fifty-six died with that section in that hour, and the ninth bell has not been rung since that hour. No body is named on any page of that harbor and no figure of any kind is printed against it. One request was made on the Saturday and refused on the Saturday and the refusal went to the surrounding people and to the independent records, and the person who made it is still inside. No line settled into the foot of that money sheet today and line eleven settles on Wednesday the tenth. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and forty-eighth day. Four hundred and fifty days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and eighty-eighth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-fourth night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and seventy-second days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-seventh day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and forty-second. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fifty-ninth day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and ninetieth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-eight days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

A boat of the Orison landing of about twelve tons came in over the bar at about six this morning with the north wind behind her and one man in her, and he had been at sea all night. The man of about thirty-eight had come off that headland on foot at about four and walked down that road with a sheet in his own hand, and the sheet carried the hours and nothing else, and the keeper read the hours at the door of that shed and wrote them with the day against them.

---

The hours say that the ninth bell was run at ten on the Monday and at two in the afternoon on the Monday and that it answered nothing on either hour, and that the ninth section came off the frame at about half past two in the morning of this day, and that the bell has not been rung since that hour, and that the eight sections are laid along the bench in the room beside that bell and answer when they are rung and will not do the work the whole was doing.

---

The hours say one more thing and it is on the same sheet in the same hand. On the Saturday the man on the line said out loud that he wanted what he had put into that bell read out into the record, in the hands of the nine places and not in a bell, and the man of about thirty-eight wrote the words out in full and did not shorten them and carried the sheet down himself.

---

The column on the lid of that step was read at about ten in four minutes and came to twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the chest stayed shut from ten until four as it stays shut on every wage Tuesday. The man of about thirty-one of the second lay read it out and a further week stood fallen due at the close of the reading and nobody asked for the reading a second time.

---

The keeper said out loud at about eleven at the flat of that bench stone, in about nine people, that nobody was going for that landing today, and that a boat goes tomorrow morning and takes nine men and what is asked for, and that he wrote that down with the hour against it and wrote no reason against it. About four of that nine agreed with him and about four said nothing and nobody asked him why nobody was going.

---

At about one the man of about thirty-two said out loud in that shed what the answer had been on the Saturday, in about nine people, and he said it in his own words and not out of a page. He said a man had asked to be read out of a mechanism into the record of nine places, and that the answer was no, and that the answer had not gone to him and had not gone to the man who asked, and that it had gone to the surrounding people and to the independent records, being the six claims on the boards at the back of that market and the hours nine places have given in their own hands.

---

He said that was his choice and not a rule, and that he was not going to make it into a rule, and that a rule about who may be read out of a record is the thing this coast has been refusing since June and that he would not be the one to write it down. He said he was not going to say the choice was right and he was not going to say it was wrong, and that nobody in that shed was to make it either for him.

---

Nobody in that shed called that anything. Nobody in that shed was thanked for it and nobody in that shed said the man on that line had done anything at all, and the keeper wrote the words and the hour and the fact that no body was named and no figure was printed, and wrote that the mechanism had lost its living custodian at about half past two in the morning, and wrote that a man of about fifty-six died with the ninth section of that bell in that hour, and wrote that this is what that phrase means when a person is in it, and wrote no name of a body and no figure of any kind against it.

---

The woman of thirty of no office said at about two, in the same nine people, that her record would carry the request and the refusal and both their hands and the hour of each, and that this was her work and not his decision and that he was not to speak for her about it. He said he would not, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him, and nobody said either was wrong, and the matter is open and no page in that yard carries a resolution of it.

---

She said a man who asks to be read out and is not read out is a man who has been asked to be a bell, and he said a man who is read out is one voice at the middle of a record that nine places keep, and that this is the thing the whole of these days has been about, and about four agreed with her and about four agreed with him. He said he had not moved from June and she said she knew that and that she was still keeping the second book on the shelf above that butt because one wet road takes one book.

---

Nobody put those words to the man of about thirty-two about going down on that Tuesday, and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for that Tuesday, and no sum is set against it on any sheet. The column headed with the asking was summed off that page on Friday the twenty-ninth of July and is not summed again on any day that follows it, and no figure is set against that column today, and the three columns of the page of routes were not counted today and are not counted on any day before the last.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday went on and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine and the bottom washed down with a bucket and the fourth lay tied twice in the swell. The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it to be read.

---

The mechanism at Orison lost its living custodian at about half past two and at about half past five on that Tuesday the shortfall went out loud at that bench stone in about eleven hundred and thirty-five words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood at that flat stone at about one and heard a man say what his own answer had been.

"**A boat of the Orison landing of about twelve tons came in over the bar at about six this morning with the north wind behind her, and the man in her had been at sea all night. The man of about thirty-eight had come off that headland on foot at about four with a sheet in his own hand, and the sheet carried hours and nothing else. The hours say the ninth bell was run at ten on the Monday and at two in the afternoon on the Monday and that it answered nothing on either hour, and that the ninth section came off the frame at about half past two in the morning of this day, and that the bell has not been rung since that hour, and that the eight sections are laid along the bench in the room beside that bell and answer when they are rung and will not do the work the whole was doing. The hours say one more thing in the same hand, being that on the Saturday the man on the line said out loud that he wanted what he had put into that bell read out into the record, in the hands of the nine places and not in a bell.**"

"**The lid on that step was read at about ten this morning in four minutes and came to twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move, and the chest stayed shut from ten until four, and a further week stood fallen due at the close of the reading. At about eleven I said out loud at the flat of this stone, in about nine of us, that nobody was going for that landing today, and that a boat goes in the morning and takes nine men and what is asked for, and I wrote that with the hour against it and no reason against it. About four agreed with me and about four said nothing and nobody asked me why nobody was going.**"

"**At about one the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about nine of us what the answer had been on the Saturday, in his own words and not out of a page. A man had asked to be read out of a mechanism into the record of nine places, and the answer was no, and the answer did not go to him and did not go to the man who asked, and it went to the surrounding people and to the independent records, being the six claims on the boards at the back of that market and the hours nine places have given in their own hands. He said that was his choice and not a rule and that he was not going to make it into a rule, and that a rule about who may be read out of a record is the thing this coast has been refusing since June and he would not be the one to write it down. He said he was not going to say the choice was right and he was not going to say it was wrong, and nobody in that shed was to make it either for him.**"

"**Nobody in that shed called that anything and nobody in that shed was thanked for it and nobody in that shed said the man on that line had done anything at all. I wrote the words and the hour and the fact that no body is named and no figure is printed, and I wrote that the mechanism had lost its living custodian at about half past two in the morning, and I wrote that a man of about fifty-six died with the ninth section of that bell in that hour, and I wrote that this is what that phrase means when a person is in it, and I wrote no name of a body and no figure of any kind against it. The woman of thirty of no office said at about two that her record would carry the request and the refusal and both their hands and the hour of each, and that this is her work and not his decision and that he was not to speak for her about it, and he said he would not. She said a man who asks to be read out and is not read out is a man who has been asked to be a bell, and he said a man who is read out is one voice at the middle of a record nine places keep, and about four agreed with her and about four with him and nobody said either was wrong, and this is not settled and no page in that shed settles it.**"

"**Nobody put words to the man of about thirty-two about going down today and nobody put them to him at the end stall, and the page of days carries no hour and no name for this Tuesday, and that column headed with the asking was summed on the twenty-ninth of July and is not summed again on any day after it, and the three columns of the page of routes were not counted today and are not counted on any day before the last. No line settled into the foot of that money sheet today and line eleven settles on Wednesday.**"

"**And the last of it. The pump ran clean from about nine and the bottom was washed down with a bucket and the fourth lay was tied twice in that swell, and nobody in that shed stopped working because of what was said at about one. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and sixty-three days without one. Twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings, with a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-eight days today and nothing written or refused this afternoon touched that figure by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a mechanism that lost its custodian, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-one lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**THE MECHANISM AT ORISON LOST ITS LIVING CUSTODIAN AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO THIS MORNING, BEING THE NINTH SECTION COMING OFF THE FRAME, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX DIED WITH THAT SECTION IN THAT HOUR, AND THE NINTH BELL HAS NOT BEEN RUNG SINCE THAT HOUR. NO BODY IS NAMED AND NO FIGURE OF ANY KIND IS PRINTED AGAINST IT AND NOBODY WAS CALLED ANYTHING. ONE REQUEST WAS MADE ON THE SATURDAY AND REFUSED ON THE SATURDAY AND THE REFUSAL WENT TO THE SURROUNDING PEOPLE AND TO THE INDEPENDENT RECORDS, AND THE PERSON WHO MADE IT IS STILL INSIDE. THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP DID NOT MOVE ON THE NINETY-THIRD OF THOSE TUESDAYS. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about six in the morning the reason the sheet of hours was taken in at the door rather than carried to the boards is that a sheet of hours is not a document and does not need a reading, and a thing read out is a thing that has been made public, and this yard did not make it public on the way in; that at about eleven the reason nobody was going for that landing on that day was written down without a reason is that this yard is a yard and not a rescue, and a yard that goes out on the morning after is a yard that has been decided about by the people in it; that at about one the reason the answer was said out loud in nine people and not written into a page first is that a refusal which is written first and said second has already been agreed to by the man writing it, and this one was not agreed to by anybody before it was said; and that at about two the reason the woman of thirty of no office told him not to speak for her is that two people who keep two records and are married cannot have one of them answer for the other one, and a hand that answers for another hand is a hand that will be asked to answer for it again.*
