# Chapter 759

*Thursday the Eighteenth Day of August, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Seventy-Sixth Charge on That Cistern, Line Thirteen Worked Today at About Two Carrying Friday the Nineteenth on Its Face for the Day It Settles and the Nineteenth Named Out Loud in About Nine People Before Any Figure Went Down and Not Settled Today, the Ninth Section of That Bell Cut in Two at Orison on the Friday and Neither Piece Rung, No Figure of Money Set Against the Hearing in the Left Side of a Man of About Thirty-Two or Against the Taste That Does Not Lift or Against the Joint in the Knee or Against the Nights He Does Not Sleep or Against the Aftertaste That Sits on Top of One He Already Had, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Ninety-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Three Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Thursday the eighteenth day of August, with that market open from about nine and a bright morning with the wind gone into the north and the sea gone flat by about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and that cistern charged in eleven trips by hand between about eight in the morning and about eleven and walked on by none of it, being the hundred and seventy-sixth charge, and the covers left lying on it from about eleven until about four in the afternoon. Line thirteen was worked today and went onto that fitting-out sheet at about two, carrying Friday the nineteenth of August on its face for the day it settles, and the keeper named the nineteenth out loud in about nine people before he wrote a single figure, being five hundred and twenty-four pence, being three marks, three shillings and eightpence, in four parts. The line is not settled today and settles at about four on Friday the nineteenth, and when it settles the foot of that sheet is read after that settlement. No figure of money is set against the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two, or against the taste that does not lift, or against the joint in that knee, or against the nights he does not sleep, or against the aftertaste that sits on top of one he already had, and none will be set. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand nine hundred and eight pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were kept in that shed through that morning and this is where they stood at noon. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and fifty-seventh day. That bellwright of this harbor has now gone four hundred and fifty-nine days with nobody asking him anything and his chest stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and ninety-seventh day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and sixty-third night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and eighty-first days with nothing set against them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and thirty-sixth day of its standing. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and fifty-first day. The nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and sixty-eighth day. And a man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and ninety-ninth days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-seven days today, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and seventy-sixth time and the covers lay on it until about four, and a boy of that yard carried the last two trips up it and was not asked to do the other nine and was not asked why he carried two.

The keeper wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that a line was worked today and was not settled today and that the day it settles is named on the face of it.

---

At about half past ten the man of about thirty-two sat down on that bench stone at about half past ten and said out loud in about nine people that he had been on no water since the sixth of August and was not going on any today, and that his hearing in the left side was the same as it had been every day since and that he was not going to say it out loud twice.

About four of that nine heard him and about four did not, and nobody asked him about going down and nobody asked him about the hearing, and the keeper wrote the hour and wrote the words and wrote no figure of money against any part of them.

---

At about one the woman of thirty of no office said out loud in about nine people that she had begun the final entry in that second book on the Monday and that it would be finished and read on Friday, and that it would carry what was agreed and what was refused and what was not known, and that it would not be finished early for anybody.

The man of about thirty-two said out loud that he was not going to ask to see it before Friday and that if he read it on Friday he would not be allowed to put a word in it, and that he had known that when he said it and that she had not had to make him say it.

---

At about two the keeper took that fitting-out sheet off its nail and laid it flat on the dry end of that stone in front of about nine people and said Friday the nineteenth out loud, twice, and then wrote it.

Bell metal and a seat at one hundred and eight pence. Line, forty yards, at three pence the yard, at forty times three for one hundred and twenty pence. Coal for four days at forty pence. Four men at four days at sixteen pence a man-day, at four times four times sixteen for two hundred and fifty-six pence. One hundred and eight plus one hundred and twenty plus forty plus two hundred and fifty-six is five hundred and twenty-four pence.

---

At about half past three a boat of about nine tons came in over the bar with a man of about fifty of that landing in her and a piece of bell metal wrapped in sacking in the boat's foot, and the man of about fifty said out loud in that shed that the ninth section of that bell had been cut in two on the Friday with a saw and had not been rung since and that nobody at that landing had asked for it.

About four of that nine asked him what the two pieces were for and he said one of them was going into a new seat and one of them was going into a fire, and about four of that nine said nothing.

---

The ordinary work of that Thursday went on and was not stopped by any of it, being the pump run clean from about nine, four men in that glass case at the corner of that shed, and the fourth lay tied twice in that flat sea.

The sheet with the fourteen gaps at the end stall stood all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it, and that man of about sixty-one was not in that yard and did not come down that lane and was not sent for.

---

At about half past five on that Thursday the whole of that day was put out loud at that stone, in about nine hundred and twenty-four words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had stood at that flat stone at about two and watched a line go onto that sheet carrying a day that has not come yet.

"**That cistern went its eleventh trip at about eleven for the hundred and seventy-sixth time and the covers lay on it until about four, and a boy of this yard carried the last two trips up it and was not asked to do the other nine and was not asked why he carried two. I wrote the charge number and the hours and wrote beside them that a line was worked today and was not settled today and that the day it settles is named on the face of it.**"

"**At about half past ten the man of about thirty-two sat down on that bench stone and said out loud in about nine of us that he had been on no water since the sixth of August and was not going on any today, and that his hearing in the left side was the same as it had been every day since and that he was not going to say it out loud twice. About four of that nine heard him and about four did not, and nobody asked him about going down and nobody asked him about the hearing, and I wrote the hour and wrote the words and wrote no figure of money against any part of them.**"

The man of about thirty-two said out loud that he was not going to ask to see it before Friday and that if he read it on Friday he would not be allowed to put a word in it, and that he had known that when he said it and that she had not had to make him say it.**"

Bell metal and a seat at one hundred and eight pence. Line, forty yards, at three pence the yard, at forty times three for one hundred and twenty pence. Coal for four days at forty pence. "**At about half past three a boat of about nine tons came in over the bar with a man of about fifty of that landing in her and a piece of bell metal wrapped in sacking in that boat's foot, and that man said out loud in this shed that the ninth section of that bell had been cut in two on the Friday with a saw and had not been rung since, and that nobody at that landing had asked for it. About four of that nine asked him what the two pieces were for and he said one of them was going into a new seat and one of them was going into a fire, and about four of that nine said nothing.**"

"**And the rest of it. The pump ran clean from about nine and four men were in that glass case at the corner of this shed and the fourth lay was tied twice in that flat sea, and nobody in this shed stopped work because of what was said at about two. The sheet with the fourteen gaps stood at that end stall all day with no figure moved by one, and the far bench was occupied from ten until four with nothing put on it, and that man of about sixty-one was not in that yard and did not come down that lane and was not sent for. The line is not settled today and settles at about four tomorrow, and when it does the foot of that sheet is read after that settlement. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence out and one hundred and eleven thousand nine hundred and eight pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. No tin in this shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and seventy-two days without one. Twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings, with a hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this evening and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and fifty-seven days today and nothing said at about half past ten today touched that figure by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for a line that was worked and not settled and nobody was thanked for four sentences said on Wednesday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**LINE THIRTEEN WENT ONTO THAT FITTING-OUT SHEET AT ABOUT TWO CARRYING FRIDAY THE NINETEENTH ON ITS FACE FOR THE DAY IT SETTLES, BEING FIVE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOUR PENCE IN FOUR PARTS, AND IT IS NOT SETTLED TODAY. THE NINTH SECTION OF THAT BELL WAS CUT IN TWO AT ORISON ON THE FRIDAY AND NEITHER PIECE HAS BEEN RUNG. NO FIGURE OF MONEY STANDS AGAINST THE HEARING IN THE LEFT SIDE OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO, OR AGAINST THE TASTE THAT DOES NOT LIFT, OR AGAINST THE JOINT IN THAT KNEE, OR AGAINST THE NIGHTS HE DOES NOT SLEEP, OR AGAINST THE AFTERTASTE THAT SITS ON TOP OF ONE HE ALREADY HAD, AND NONE WILL BE SET. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that at about one the woman of thirty of no office said out loud in about nine that she had begun the final entry in that second book on the Monday and that it would be finished and read on Friday, and that it would carry what was agreed and what was refused and what was not known, and that it would not be finished early for anybody, and that the man of about thirty-two said he would not ask to see it before Friday and that if he read it on Friday he would not be allowed to put a word in it and that he had known that when he said it and she had not had to make him say it; and that at about two line thirteen went onto that fitting-out sheet carrying the nineteenth for the day it settles, the nineteenth having been named out loud twice in about nine people before any figure was written, being bell metal and a seat at one hundred and eight pence, line of forty yards at three pence the yard at forty times three for one hundred and twenty pence, coal for four days at forty pence, and four men at four days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times four times sixteen for two hundred and fifty-six pence, being five hundred and twenty-four pence; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about half past ten the reason he said he would not say it out loud twice is that a thing said once in nine people is a fact and a thing said twice is a complaint, and the day a man begins repeating the cost of a thing to make the room carry it is the day the room is carrying it instead of him; that at about one the reason she said the entry would not be finished early for anybody is that a record finished in a hurry is a record finished before the argument has finished with it, and the argument about that day had not finished and would not finish because of the writing; that at about two the reason the day it settles was named out loud twice before any figure was written is that a line whose settlement day is written on it and spoken on the day it is written cannot afterwards be said to have been an accident of when the keeper had his pen; and that at about half past three the reason nobody in that nine asked the man of about fifty anything about the two pieces is that one of those two pieces went into a fire and a room that has just heard four sentences on Wednesday does not ask a man where the other half of a thing went.*
