# Chapter 747

*Saturday the Sixth Day of August, the Second Held Line Taken Off the Orison Landing at About Eleven in the Morning and Held Until the Distributed Routes Held and Not Pulled, the Same Limit of Twenty-Five Minutes' Worth of Capacity and the Same Tender and the Same Cutoff, He Surfaced and Was Not Fine and Was Not Fine Later and No Figure of Money Stands Against Any Part of It, the Sheet at One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand One Hundred and Fifty-Four Pence Out, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Eighty and the Second at Two Thousand Two Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Saturday the sixth day of August, with that market opening late in that town and a flat morning on the water off Orison with no wind in it at all, the yard's boat coming to the Orison landing at about ten in the morning with nine men in her, and about nine people in the room beside that bell at half past five that evening.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern in that harbor, whose covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon eleven hundred yards and one market away. A held line was taken off that landing at about eleven in the morning and was held until the distributed routes held, being about twenty-two minutes against a fixed air limit of twenty-five minutes' worth of capacity, with the same tender on the surface and the same independent line and the same cutoff, and it was not pulled. He surfaced and was not fine and was not fine later in the same day. The cost is permanent and no figure of money stands against any part of it. The chain above that bell lost its centre for about a minute at about half past eleven and nearly came apart. The reason was written down in the hand of the man of about thirty-two at about half past nine on the face of that landing with the hour against it. The sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and no line went onto it and no figure was written into its foot. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine days of things that are not being done went on a day in the morning, and this is what they stood at. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its four hundred and forty-fifth day. Four hundred and forty-seven days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of that harbor a question, and his chest is shut and stays shut. Two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the seven hundred and eighty-fifth day of themselves. That bench under that pump has now stood bare for the five hundred and fifty-first night running. Two lights about a mile off that point have gone three hundred and sixty-ninth days with nothing set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached the three hundred and twenty-fourth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the three hundred and thirty-ninth. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and fifty-sixth day. And a man of about fifty-one has now kept three hundred and eighty-seventh days of saying nothing to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed with a boy's hand of twenty-two at the head of it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head in that other harbor lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and that harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-five days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

The reason was written down at about half past nine on the face of that landing in the hand of the man of about thirty-two, with the hour against it, in the same words as the one written on the third of July. One person, held by the woman of about thirty-three with a watch on the surface, against twenty-five minutes' worth of capacity, with a cutoff she can pull without asking, one person at a time, nobody twice in a day, nobody at the top of a flood, and an ascent asked for not argued with.

---

He went down on the line at about eleven and the tender paid it out arm over arm with her other hand on the cutoff and the spare glass in its wrap beside her. The man of about thirty-eight stood in the room beside the bell with his hand on the rope where it comes through the wall, and the man of about fifty-six was on the other line in about nineteen fathoms and had not come off it since the third of July.

---

At about half past eleven the chain above that bell lost its centre. The answers under it came out in the wrong order and stayed in the wrong order, and for about a minute nothing in that room was answering in one voice, and the man of about thirty-eight said afterward that the worst of that minute was not the silence and was the noise, because nine places on that coast were all saying a different thing at the same hour and none of them was wrong.

---

And in that minute, out of the ninth bell on the headland above that landing, and not into the room and not towards it, a voice said one word. It was a name, and it was his own name, and the word was Ilya Serr, and the man of about thirty-eight wrote in the book in that room that a voice out of the bell said one word and would not write down which word, and that word is in no mouth in that yard and in no other mouth on that coast in nine runs of days and is on no sheet and is in no slip.

---

At about twenty-two minutes the distributed routes held. They did not hold in one voice, and they held for about a minute, and nine separate things agreed with nine separate things for the length of that minute, and the holding was over at the end of it, and the man on the line came up on the independent line by himself.

---

The tender did not pull the cutoff. The keeper wrote that she did not pull it and wrote why, being that a cutoff is a thing a tender pulls against a line that will not come up, and that this one came up, and that a hand pulled a moment after a man has come up is a hand that has decided something was over before the man in the water knew it was.

---

He surfaced at about twenty-five past eleven and was not fine. Pressure pain across both shoulders and a taste that was not his own and would not lift, and a losing of the telling of one hour from another, and he sat on the deck with his back against the rail for about an hour with the woman of about thirty-three beside him and her hand flat on his back between the shoulder blades.

---

About four men in that boat heard that the pulls had changed and about four were busy with the gear and none of them said whether the pulls had been early or late. He said nothing about the minute in which the chain had lost its centre except that he had been in it, and that what came up the line in that minute was nine things and not one thing, and he would not say which of the nine he had been able to tell was his.

---

About three he was still not fine, and at about four he was still not fine, and at about half past four he asked the man of about thirty-eight one thing, being whether a person could go into a thing that had been in him with other people standing round, and the man of about thirty-eight said that he had kept that line for about four months and had never once gone into a thing and did not know the answer to that, and that nobody else in that room did either.

---

The keeper wrote at about five what is now written on every page this yard keeps, being that the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two did not come back, and that it did not come back on that deck and it did not come back in that room and there is no figure against it and none will be set, and that he cannot go into a thing again without other people standing round, and that the taste does not lift.

---

He said out loud in about nine people in that room at about half past four that the argument he has been having with his wife since June is about how much of one person's account to put in front of strangers, and that he has not settled it and is not going to settle it at nineteen fathoms, and that what came up the line in that minute was nine accounts and that nobody in that room is going to pick one of them and print it as the account.

---

Nobody put the same words to the man of about thirty-two this day about going down, and nobody put them to him on that landing, and the page of days carries no hour against his name and no sum is set against it. The column headed with the asking was summed on the twenty-ninth of July and is not summed again on any day that follows it, and no figure is set against that column on this day.

---

At about half past five on that Saturday the reading went out in that room at Orison in about nine hundred words to about nine people, and about four of those nine had been in that room since about ten, and the money sheet was not in that room and was not read in it.

"**The reason was written down at about half past nine this morning on the face of this landing in the hand of the man of about thirty-two, with the hour against it, in the same words as the one written on the third of July, being one person held by the woman of about thirty-three with a watch on the surface against twenty-five minutes' worth of capacity, with a cutoff she can pull without asking, one person at a time and nobody twice in a day and nobody at the top of a flood and an ascent asked for not argued with. He went down at about eleven and the tender paid the line out arm over arm with her other hand on the cutoff and the spare glass in its wrap beside her. The man of about thirty-eight stood in this room with his hand on the rope where it comes through the wall, and the man of about fifty-six was on the other line in about nineteen fathoms and had not come off it since the third of July.**"

"**At about half past eleven the chain above that bell lost its centre. The answers under it came out in the wrong order and stayed in the wrong order, and for about a minute nothing in this room was answering in one voice, and the man of about thirty-eight said afterward that the worst of that minute was not the silence and was the noise, because nine places on that coast were all saying a different thing at the same hour and none of them was wrong. In that minute, out of that bell and not into this room, a voice said one word. It was a name and it was his own name, and the man of about thirty-eight wrote in the book in this room that a voice out of the bell said one word and would not write down which word, and that word is in no mouth in that yard and on no sheet and in no slip.**"

"**At about twenty-two minutes the distributed routes held, and they did not hold in one voice, and they held for about a minute, and nine separate things agreed with nine separate things for the length of that minute, and the holding was over at the end of it, and the man on the line came up on the independent line by himself. The tender did not pull the cutoff, and I wrote that she did not pull it and why, being that a cutoff is a thing a tender pulls against a line that will not come up, and this one came up, and a hand pulled a moment after a man has come up is a hand that has decided something was over before the man in the water knew it was.**"

"**He surfaced at about twenty-five past eleven and was not fine, and the cost came in the same hour on the same deck, being pressure pain across both shoulders and a taste that is not his own and will not lift and a losing of the telling of one hour from another, and he sat with his back against the rail for about an hour with the woman of about thirty-three beside him. At about three he was still not fine and at about four he was still not fine and at about half past four he asked the man of about thirty-eight one thing, being whether a person can go into a thing that has been in him with other people standing round, and the man of about thirty-eight said he had kept that line about four months and had never gone into a thing and did not know the answer and that nobody else in that room did.**"

"**What is written now on this page and will stay written is that the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two did not come back, and that it did not come back on that deck and did not come back in this room, and that there is no figure against it and none will be set, and that he cannot go into a thing again without other people standing round, and that the taste does not lift. Nobody was thanked for any of it and nobody in that boat was thanked and no figure of money stands against any hour of it. He said out loud in about nine of us at about half past four that the argument he has been having with his wife since June is about how much of one person's account to put in front of strangers and that he has not settled it and is not going to settle it at nineteen fathoms, and that what came up that line in that minute was nine accounts and nobody in that room is going to pick one and print it as the account.**"

"**And the last of it, and it is read here and not in that market. That sheet stands at one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and fifty-four pence out and one hundred and ten thousand five hundred and fourteen pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and no line went onto it and no figure went into its foot, and that sheet is not in this room. A hundred and sixty-eight pence went into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight came out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus two thousand one hundred and eighty-one plus one thousand eight hundred and forty-eight less four thousand and twenty-nine is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and that yard is now one hundred and sixty days without one. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and forty-five days today, and nothing that was done in this room touched that figure by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for a line that was held and came up, and that room stood empty by about six with its window open on the frame, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

**A HELD LINE WAS HELD UNTIL THE DISTRIBUTED ROUTES HELD, BEING ABOUT TWENTY-TWO MINUTES AGAINST TWENTY-FIVE, AND IT WAS NOT PULLED, AND HE SURFACED AND WAS NOT FINE AND WAS NOT FINE LATER IN THE SAME DAY. THE CHAIN ABOVE THAT BELL LOST ITS CENTRE FOR ABOUT A MINUTE AND NEARLY CAME APART. THE HEARING IN THE LEFT SIDE OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO DID NOT COME BACK, HE CANNOT GO INTO A THING AGAIN WITHOUT OTHER PEOPLE STANDING ROUND, THE TASTE DOES NOT LIFT, AND NO FIGURE OF MONEY STANDS AGAINST ANY PART OF IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

and that the reason for one descent was written down at about half past nine in the hand of the man of about thirty-two with the hour against it, in the same words as on the third of July, being one person held by the woman of about thirty-three with a watch on the surface against twenty-five minutes' worth of capacity with a cutoff she can pull without asking, one person at a time and nobody twice in a day and nobody at the top of a flood and an ascent not argued with; and that he went down on the line at about eleven and the tender paid it out arm over arm with her other hand on the cutoff; and that at about half past eleven the chain above that bell lost its centre and the answers under it came out in the wrong order for about a minute, and that nine places on that coast were saying a different thing at the same hour and none of them was wrong; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that room at Orison, and no name is against it: that at about half past nine the reason the same words were written a second time is that a reason rewritten is a reason tested, and a second writing that is a different reason is a man who has changed and a writing that is the same is a man who has not, and this yard wanted to know which of the two it was before the line went out; that at about half past eleven the reason the one word out of the bell was not written down is that a name written into the book of a place that keeps a page of days is a name in that book's mouth, and a book that has swallowed one name will be asked to swallow another; that at about half past twelve the reason the not pulling of the cutoff was written down with the reason for it is that the not pulling is the only part of a held line a reader can check, since the hand that held it is under water and the line is under water and the hour is on the page; and that at about half past four the reason the disagreement was said out loud in that room and not written down is that a page printing who did not agree is what the man of about thirty-two has been arguing against since June, and this yard does not hand him a page to print it on while he is nineteen fathoms down.*
