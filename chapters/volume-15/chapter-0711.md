# Chapter 711

*Friday the First Day of July, Not a Rota Day and Not a Wage Tuesday and Not a Wipe, Line Four Settled Into the Foot of That Sheet and Line Five on It With the Day the Work Was Done Against It, the Total at One Hundred and Fourteen Thousand One Hundred and Fifty-Eight Pence Out, a Fifth Time Down That Road in a Basket of Vegetables and a Sixth Page Back With Nothing on It, No Column of Those Without a Time and No Count of Them, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, Being Two Shillings and Fourpence, the First Column at Four Hundred and Forty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand and Ninety-Four*

Friday the first day of July, with that market open from about nine and the flat of that bench stone warm by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**Not a rota day and not a wage Tuesday and not a wipe, and no charge put on that cistern and its covers lying on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon, and that butt behind the market covered from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon with no charge on it either. Line four was settled into the foot of that sheet at about four in the afternoon and line five went onto it with the day the work was done against it. The total is one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence out and one hundred and three thousand five hundred and eighteen pence short. A fifth time came down that road in a basket with vegetables in it and a sixth page came back with nothing on it at all, not even a hand. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and no tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters about things that are not being done ran a day on this morning. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its four hundred and ninth day. Four hundred and eleven days have gone by without anybody asking a bellwright of this harbor, and his chest stays shut. Two objects of nobody's on that crown are into their seven hundred and forty-ninth day. That bench under that pump has stood bare for the five hundred and fifteenth night. Two lights about a mile off that point have spent three hundred and thirty-third days with no figure set against them. A six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has reached its two hundred and eighty-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on its three hundred and third. A nine-line sheet with a seal on it in that front street has stood three hundred and twentieth day. A man of about fifty-one has kept three hundred and fifty-one days of not saying anything to anybody about a page of six names. That third column is empty and unappointed and a boy's hand of twenty-two is at the head of it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water. That fourth column stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck. About two sea miles off that mole head lie nine hulls this afternoon and nine lay there on the twenty-first of January, and this harbor has not said a word about any of them easing. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and nine days, one more than the day before, no figure is set against it, and he is not on the water.**

---

Line four was settled into the foot of the sheet at about four in the afternoon, and the multiplication was read out again at every one of its five parts before the total went down. Three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being three times one hundred and sixty-eight, which is five hundred and four pence, and coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, being sixty pence, and rope, forty yards, at three pence the yard, being forty times three, which is one hundred and twenty pence, and tin and sheet copper for the pump at seventy-two pence, being seventy-two pence, and four men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day, being four times three times sixteen, which is one hundred and ninety-two pence. Those five are nine hundred and forty-eight pence, being five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, and the total is one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence out and one hundred and three thousand five hundred and eighteen pence short.

Line five went onto the sheet this afternoon with the day the work was done against it and is settled into the total tomorrow. It is two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence, being two times eighteen, which is thirty-six pence, and a shackle and a swivel for the lift at forty pence, being forty pence, and six men at one day at sixteen pence a man-day, being six times one times sixteen, which is ninety-six pence, and two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being two times one hundred and sixty-eight, which is three hundred and thirty-six pence. Those four are thirty-six and forty and ninety-six and three hundred and thirty-six, and they are five hundred and eight pence, being three marks, two shillings and fourpence.

---

The fifth time came down the road in a basket with vegetables in it at about eleven, folded under a cloth with greens over it. The hand is a new hand to this shed and the time is written plain, and the page went onto the bench stone with damp at one corner where the greens had lain. The sixth page came back at about two with nothing on it at all, not even a hand, carried by the same carrier who brought the fifth, and he said the household had read the fifth and had nothing to put on their own.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay who cannot read asked the man who keeps the leaf, in about nine people, whether there was a page for the places that had not answered, and was told that there is not one and is not going to be one. The reason goes in the second column, being that a page of names that have not answered is a page of people a route's own neighbours could be shown. About four people in that nine said that was right and about four said nothing, and the man of about thirty-four said he had asked because he wanted to know how the yard keeps track, and was told the yard keeps the times it has and does not keep the times it has not.

---

The man of about thirty-two went up the steps by the rail again on Friday and nobody said anything about it and nobody thanked anybody for the day's work. He carried a bucket of water up for the bench stone and set it down without spilling, and about four people watched him do it steady. The woman of thirty of no office wrote the Friday line of the page of that week with the settlement and the two pages against it, and she wrote that no column of the rest is kept and no number of them is said.

The turn of the day is stopping counting. The yard does not write a column of those without a time, and does not write a count of them, and does not say the number out loud, and the reason is on the page. Six times stand on six pages in six hands, and that is what the yard has, and what it has not is not the yard's to list.

---

Line four was settled into the foot of that sheet at about four and at about half past five on that Friday the man who keeps the leaf took the shortfall out loud at that bench stone, in about four hundred and seventy words, to about nine people, and about four of those nine had seen the basket opened at about eleven.

"**Line four was settled at about four, being three days of a boat at one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four, and coal and oil for sixty, and forty yards at three pence for one hundred and twenty, and tin and copper for seventy-two, and four men at three days at sixteen pence for one hundred and ninety-two, and those five are nine hundred and forty-eight pence, being five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence. The total is one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence out and one hundred and three thousand five hundred and eighteen pence short.**

"**Line five went on this afternoon with the day the work was done against it, being two seats at eighteen pence for thirty-six, and a shackle and a swivel for forty, and six men at one day at sixteen pence for ninety-six, and two days of a boat at one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six, and those four are five hundred and eight pence, being three marks, two shillings and fourpence. It is settled tomorrow.**

"**A fifth time came in a basket with vegetables at about eleven and a sixth page came back at about two with nothing on it at all, not even a hand. A man asked whether there is a page for the places that have not answered and was told there is not one and is not going to be one, because a page of names that have not answered is a page neighbours could be shown.**

"**The sheet carries two lines settled and one waiting, and four hundred and fifty pence is still on that board in the front street. A hundred and sixty-eight pence in at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight out at about four, and twenty-eight plus one hundred and sixty-eight less one hundred and sixty-eight is twenty-eight, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. No tin in that shed has been opened since the twenty-ninth of April past and this yard is now one hundred and twenty-four days without one.**

"**And the last of it. That cistern was not charged and its covers lay on it from nine until four and nobody walked on the water. The wall in that shed is still two lines and about eleven inches of empty plaster. About two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls this afternoon and there were nine there on the twenty-first of January. A man of about twenty-nine hears out of the left side of his head and it is two hundred and nine days today and nothing settled on that sheet touched it by a day.**"

Nobody was thanked for the arithmetic and nobody was thanked for greens, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirteen lines on it, and that wooden tally slate was still lying flat on that bench stone with nothing on it.

---

**LINE FOUR WAS SETTLED INTO THE TOTAL AT ABOUT FOUR, BEING NINE HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING FIVE MARKS, TWELVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THE TOTAL IS ONE HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND THREE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN PENCE SHORT. LINE FIVE WENT ON WITH THE DAY THE WORK WAS DONE AGAINST IT, BEING TWO SEATS FOR THIRTY-SIX PENCE AND A SHACKLE AND A SWIVEL FOR FORTY PENCE AND SIX MEN AT ONE DAY FOR NINETY-SIX PENCE AND TWO DAYS OF A BOAT FOR THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX PENCE, BEING FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHT PENCE, BEING THREE MARKS, TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. SIX TIMES STAND ON SIX PAGES, WITH NO COLUMN OF THE REST AND NO COUNT OF THEM. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE.**

*First column, entered on Friday the first day of July at about six in the evening in that shed: that the said day was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe, and that no charge was placed on that cistern and that the covers lay on it from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon; and that line four was settled into the foot of that sheet at about four in the afternoon, being three days of a boat at one hundred and sixty-eight pence for five hundred and four pence and coal and oil for sixty pence and forty yards at three pence for one hundred and twenty pence and tin and copper for seventy-two pence and four men at three days at sixteen pence for one hundred and ninety-two pence, being nine hundred and forty-eight pence, being five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, with the multiplication at every part; and that the total is one hundred and fourteen thousand one hundred and fifty-eight pence out and one hundred and three thousand five hundred and eighteen pence short; and that line five went on with the day the work was done against it, being two seats for thirty-six pence and a shackle and swivel for forty pence and six men at one day for ninety-six pence and two days of a boat for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being five hundred and eight pence, being three marks, two shillings and fourpence; and that a fifth time came in a basket with vegetables and a sixth page came back with nothing on it, and that no column of the rest is kept and no number of them is said.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that at about eleven in the morning the reason a time in a basket is entered with the basket is that a page carried with greens is a page carried as food is carried, and a yard that leaves the basket out will be taken to hide how pages travel; that at about two in the afternoon the reason a page with nothing on it is entered as nothing is that an empty page brought back is an answer and not a silence, and this yard does not fill it for the household; that at about three the reason no column of the rest is kept is that a page of names that have not answered is a page neighbours could be shown, and neighbours shown such a page would do the asking this yard refuses to do; and that at about four the reason the settlement carries all five parts out loud is that a line settled without its parts is a total nobody can take apart again.*
