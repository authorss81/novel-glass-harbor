# Chapter 821

*Wednesday the Nineteenth Day of October, the Eleventh Day, a Third Line Worked at About Eight With the Twenty-First on Its Face for the Day It Settles, a New Glass Going into the Lamp at That Mole Head at About Ten Past Four, Three Days of a Boat Hired Here at the Figure in Chalk That Bought Nothing Four Days up That Coast Road, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Forty-Four*

Wednesday the nineteenth day of October, with that market open from six and a clear cold morning with the wind getting into the north-east about eleven and the whole of that mole head standing up in sharp light off the water, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Wednesday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the twenty-third and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-fifth. A third line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet today at about eight with the twenty-first of October on its face for the day it settles, and nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today and the foot was not read today, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety pence out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this eleventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and nineteenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-first day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-ninth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-fifth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-third day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and thirteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirtieth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-first day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-second day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twentieth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the nineteenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and nineteen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The third line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight this morning in the keeper's hand, with the nineteenth on it as the day the work was done and the twenty-first on it as the day it settles, and both are on it because they are not the same day.

The first part of it is three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, three times one hundred and sixty-eight, for five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings. The second is coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, being five shillings. The third is a new glass for the lamp at that mole head at two marks, being three hundred and twenty pence. The fourth is sixty yards of line at three pence the yard, sixty times three, for one hundred and eighty pence, being one mark, one shilling and eightpence. The fifth is five men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day, five times three times sixteen, for two hundred and forty pence, being one mark, six shillings and eightpence.

Five hundred and four and sixty and three hundred and twenty and one hundred and eighty and two hundred and forty is one thousand three hundred and four pence, being eight marks and two shillings. Nothing settles into that foot today and the foot is not read today.

The new glass went up to that mole head at about half past three in the basket on the hand bar and came back down empty at about four. The lamp was lit at about ten past four and about four of that nine who were in that yard looked up at it and could see it from the steps of that fish market.

---

At about eleven, in that shed, in about nine people, one of that nine asked the keeper out loud whether the figure in chalk in the first part of that line was the same figure that is in chalk on a board four days up that coast road.

The keeper said out loud that it was the same figure and that it was right in both places, and that a boat for a day on this coast is one hundred and sixty-eight pence and has been that figure for longer than anybody in that shed has been standing in it. He said that the same figure went up a coast road in September on the back of a page and came down as what a crossing costs, and that on that figure four days of a boat and three days of a man came to seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that none of it was spent. He said that the figure was not wrong anywhere and that nobody had said one word that was not true, and that the only thing that differs between the two places is that one of them hired the boat and the other one did not.

About four of that nine said out loud that that was the whole of it in one sentence. Nobody said the keeper was right and nobody said the keeper was wrong.

The man of about thirty-two was in that yard at eleven with the page of routes under his arm and heard all of it and said nothing at all, and went to the far end of that shed and did about an hour of the day's work there without looking up.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a coil of line measured off against a gauge with the shed door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

On their thirty-first day those four new crews had the morning's work off in about two hours of it with the man of about thirty-two over them, and four of the nine said that the glass going up to that lamp was the first thing they had watched leave that yard and climb that mole head in a month.

---

From about ten until about one the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard with her own page under her arm, and she went off up that coast road on the one o'clock boat. He was in that shed from two until five and did not go into the end of that mole once in all that time. A room with the other one and a page in it did not come up on either of their days, neither of them put a name on the other's paper, and neither of them said one word about the two figures that are in the chalk.

That bench in the second lay was worked from about ten until about two by a man of about thirty-four who cannot read, and nobody put one question to him in that time, and that is the seventy-first day of that run of days.

Thirteen hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and not one of those thirteen belonged to whoever wrote the line it went against, and the page of days carries thirteen against this day.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand one hundred and nine words, and the worked line came first in it, the lamp glass came in the middle of it, and the two figures in the chalk came at the end of it, and there is no count of anything in this reading at all.

*First column, entered on Wednesday the nineteenth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the eleventh entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that a third line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight in the keeper's hand with the nineteenth on it as the day the work was done and the twenty-first on it as the day it settles; that the parts of that line are three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence, coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, a new glass for the lamp at that mole head at two marks for three hundred and twenty pence, sixty yards of line at three pence the yard at sixty times three for one hundred and eighty pence, and five men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at five times three times sixteen for two hundred and forty pence; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper answered the question about the two figures in the chalk by saying they were one figure and were right twice over, and that the reason he answered it that way and did not answer it the other way is that a person who has been told for six days that the figure is the trouble will say anything at all to get rid of the figure, and that the figure is not the trouble and he would not have said so a week ago; that the reason nobody in that nine said the keeper was right and nobody said the keeper was wrong is that the second half of what he said is a fact about a boat four days up a road and about a day of a man there, and that a fact about another place is not a thing about nine people standing in a shed, and that this harbor has been taught that lesson the hard way twice this month; that what kept him silent at eleven is that a person can hear the whole of a thing and still have nothing at all to add to it, and that a page-holder who answers a question like that is a page-holder telling the person holding the page what to write, and that he has done that before and knows what it comes to.*
