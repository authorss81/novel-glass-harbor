# Chapter 832

*Sunday the Thirtieth Day of October, the Twenty-Second Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirtieth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Four Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One, Forty-Seven Off and Forty-Seven Back and One Hundred and Twenty-Eight the Whole of It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Asking Four People the Same One Question at About Two in About Half an Hour and Getting Four Answers and Not One of Them a Name, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Sixty-Five and Two Thousand Six Hundred and Ninety-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Sunday the thirtieth day of October, with that market shut until seven and the lane wet from the night and a light east wind that came up about eleven and stopped dead, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Sunday, and a wipe falls on it and no charge falls on that cistern and no wage movement falls in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday, the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the first of that month, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the sixth of November. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and stood bare until a quarter to one and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at forty-seven minutes off and forty-seven back and thirty-four standing bare, one hundred and twenty-eight the whole of it and a minute longer at each end than the last time. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood five hundred and thirty days. Nobody has asked the bellwright of this harbor anything for five hundred and thirty-two days. On that crown, what belongs to nobody is into the eight hundred and seventieth day. The night under that pump came down bare on its six hundred and thirty-sixth. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-first day. The man of about fifty-one has now gone four hundred and seventy-two days without speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their sixty-third day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-first day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirtieth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-two days. Nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Behind the reeve's bench that wall came off the wall at about half past ten and there were about four people standing in front of it counting and about two of those four had a watch and about two did not.

It stood bare thirty-four minutes and it went back up at a quarter to one and it did not go up whole, and it is the hundred and thirtieth time that has happened and each of the two ends is a minute longer than it was the time before, and nobody in that lane said anything about either end and nobody has said anything about any of them since the fourteenth of August.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was a hatches out of the water by about half past two and a coil of line measured off against a gauge with that door propped on a brick, and nothing went onto that fitting-out sheet in the ordinary way and no money is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews came to their forty-second day and got the day's work off in about two hours of it with the man of about thirty-two over them, and about four of that nine said that they had begun to know which of the four men in this shed said something when a thing went wrong and which of the four men said nothing.

---

At about two, in that shed, with about nine people round it, the man of about thirty-two put one question to four different people over about half an hour, and he put it the same way each time.

The keeper was first and he said out loud that he did not know and that he was asked that in that same ten days in nine working places with a pencil and a list, and that a list and a pencil gets the same nothing nine times, and that he was not doing it a second time inside one month.

A man of about thirty-six of that market was second and he said out loud that he has, twice, on freight work up from that quay, and that both times the figure he worked off was off somebody else's sheet and he did not know whose sheet it was and would not have asked, and that he has never once been told what that figure was for.

The woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing was third, and she said out loud that she has, and that she would rather not be asked, and that being asked is not the point and that the point is that there is nowhere to put an answer down when a person does answer it.

A man of about forty-four of that market who mends nets there was fourth and he said out loud that he has not, and that he does not read, and that a thing he cannot read is not a thing he works off, and that about half of what the man of about thirty-two had just said went past him entirely.

Nobody repeated any one of those four answers back to the man who gave it. The man of about thirty-two wrote none of them down and said out loud that he was not going to, and that was the whole of what he did with the half hour.

About two in that afternoon the keeper said out loud that this is the same round that went round this yard in the first ten days and that it will come out the same, and that the man of about thirty-two was right to do it and would be bad at it, and that those two facts are not in tension with each other and that about four of that nine said he was going to be bad at it.

---

At about four the keeper went up to that room over the sailmaker and read the whole of one of those eleven sheets over again and went down again. At about half past four the man of about thirty-six read one of them a second time and did not say which and the keeper did not ask him.

Nobody in that harbor is keeping a list of who has read one of those eleven and nobody in that harbor is going to start.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard with her own page from ten until about one and took that coast road up at one. The man of about thirty-two sat with that page of routes open on the bench in front of him from two until five and did not mention the half hour at four or at five or at any point in between.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay had that bench from about ten until about two in that lane and not one person asked him a thing, being the eighty-second day of that run of days.

Eight hands went onto four sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of the four was written by anybody who put a hand on it, and the page of days carries eight against this day.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and three words, and the wall came first in it, the four answers came in the middle of it, and the two readings came at the end of it, and this reading counts nothing.

Nobody was thanked for four answers on a Sunday afternoon, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-nine lines on it.
