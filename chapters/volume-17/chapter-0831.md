# Chapter 831

*Saturday the Twenty-Ninth Day of October, the Twenty-First Day, a Line Worked at About Eight with the Twenty-Third of October on Its Face for the Day It Settles and Nothing Coming Into the Foot of It Today, an Ordinary Working Saturday with No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement in It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Asking at About One in About Nine People for One Name and the Keeper Saying Out Loud He Will Not Put One on Paper, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Sixty-Four and Two Thousand Six Hundred and Ninety-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Ninety-Two*

Saturday the twenty-ninth day of October, with that market open from six and the first of a hard east wind coming up the quay about nine and a sea getting up outside that mole, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Saturday, and no charge falls on that cistern in it and no wipe is in it and no wage movement is in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the second of November. A line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight this morning with the twenty-third day of October written on its face for the day it settles, which is two days off and is not today. Nothing came into the foot of that sheet and the foot stands at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-first day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands on its five hundred and twenty-ninth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and thirty-first day of nobody asking him anything. Whatever belongs to nobody on that crown is into the eight hundred and sixty-ninth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirty-fifth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-third day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and nineteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and fortieth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-first day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their sixty-second day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirtieth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-ninth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-one days. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-nine days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

At about eight this morning, in that east wind, six men went out on that line and it went onto that sheet with the day it was worked against it and the day it settles against that, and the two days on its face are two days apart

Its five parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence; coal and oil for two days at forty pence; thirty yards of line at three pence the yard at thirty times three for ninety pence; two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence at two times eighteen for thirty-six pence; and six men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at six times two times sixteen for one hundred and ninety-two pence. The whole of that line is six hundred and ninety-four pence, being four marks, four shillings and sixpence, and it does not touch the foot of that sheet until that yard settles it, which is at about four on Monday, and until then it is on the face of that line and nowhere else.

The sixth man out on that work was the man of about forty-six of the second lay and he cut those two bell seats at the bench at the wall end of that shed and faced them with a file in about half an hour and put the shavings into a bucket and the bucket into the fire at about eleven.

The ordinary work of that Saturday was three boats off that mole before noon in that rising east wind and a hatches out of the water by three, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews came to their forty-first day and had the day's work off in about two hours and ten minutes under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said that on the first of a wind like this one of the four learned to read it in about four minutes and the other three stood and watched him do it.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the man of about thirty-two said out loud that a thing cannot be put right to nobody.

He said out loud that this harbor has spent a month finding out a figure was wrong and has found nobody to put it right to, and that he wanted one name before the month was out. He said out loud that he was not asking who had spent a day and was not asking for anybody to be blamed for anything, and that what he wanted was the name of one person in this harbor who had worked a day off a page and had never been told in his life what that figure on the page was for, and that he would put that name in his coat and carry it up that coast road himself on Monday.

The keeper said out loud that he was not going to write a name on a piece of paper in that yard, and that he had been round nine working places with a list and a pencil in the first ten days of this month and had not asked one of them for a name and was not going to begin on a Saturday with nine people standing round him watching him do it.

He said out loud, to about four of that nine, that a name is not what is wanted either, and that this harbor has four pages it can put things on and not one has anywhere to put a person who read a thing, and that he was not going to invent a fifth kind of wall this month because nine people asked him for a name at one o'clock.

A man of about fifty-eight of that market said out loud from that doorway that the only list of anybody this harbor keeps is on the lid of that step and it is twenty-six lines and it is wages, and that there is not one figure on it against anybody who ever read anything in his life.

About four of that nine said nothing at all and the rest went back to what they were doing. Nobody repeated the request after him. The keeper put nothing on any page and the man of about thirty-two kept his hands in his coat pockets for the rest of that hour.

---

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road at one. From two until five the man of about thirty-two sat at one end of that bench with the page of routes in front of him and nothing else on the boards, and at about four he was told that he had asked for a name in the morning and that the keeper had said no, and he said nothing about it and turned a page over.

That bench from about ten until about two in that wind was worked by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay and no question was put to him by anybody in that lane, which is the eighty-first day of that run and he has not missed one of them.

At about half past four one of those four new crews went up the lane to the room over that sailmaker's and read one of those eleven sheets a second time, having read one in September, and told nobody in that lane which one it was and told nobody it was the second time.

Nine hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and not one of the four was written by anybody who put a hand on it, and the page of days carries nine against that day,

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the man of about thirty-two asked at about one for a name and it was the first time anybody in this harbor had asked anybody for a name since the first of October, and that asking for a name is asking for the one thing a page cannot carry, and that the reason the keeper would not write it on a sheet is that a sheet carrying a name is a list, and this yard worked out five weeks ago that a list of who did not agree turns a refusal into a private thing, and that a list of who read something turns a page into a private thing the same way and for a worse reason; that about four of that nine keeping silent is not the same as four of that nine having no name to hand, and that about four of them had worked off a page that month, and that nobody who works in a place like this says out loud in nine people that they have done the thing a page cannot be told about, and that they are not hiding it and they are not ashamed of it and there is no name of any of them on any page this harbor has; that the man of about fifty-eight put that sentence in from that doorway rather than out of that shed is that he keeps an end stall and a slate and that what he said about the lid of that step came out of him as a thing about wages and not as a thing about names, and that the lid of that step is the only piece of paper in this harbor with twenty-six human lines on it and it has carried no figure against a reader since before the first of October and is not going to; that the reason the man of about thirty-two put his hands back in his coat pockets and did not raise it again is that a request made once at about one on a Saturday in nine people is not refused twice, and that a second asking would have made it a thing this yard was being asked to do rather than a thing this yard had already answered, and that he went back to the page of routes at two because there is nothing on that page either.*
