# Chapter 829

*Thursday the Twenty-Seventh Day of October, the Nineteenth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Sixth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, a Man of About Thirty-Six of That Market Asking Out Loud at About Two at That End Stall Whether Anybody Four Days up That Coast Road Has Said Anything About That Board Since Thursday and Nobody Four Days up It Coming Down Here Since the Seventeenth, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Thursday the twenty-seventh day of October, with that market open from six and a fine clear morning with the first of that weather coming off the west and a squall in off that water at about two that went away by half past three, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Thursday, being a rota day and a day with no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the first of November and the next charge after this one is on the morning of Monday. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-sixth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this nineteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-seventh day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-ninth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-seventh day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirty-third night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-first day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-first day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-ninth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their sixtieth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-eighth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-seventh day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-seven days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That butt was filled at about nine for the hundred and ninety-sixth time, fourteen trips by hand, with about four people standing near it and not one of them on any of it, and at about one a man of about twenty-seven of that second lay put the hundred and ninety-sixth against the day on that rota slate and nothing was read out.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was five boats off that mole before noon in that fine clear light and a bell seat cut and faced at that bench with the shavings going into a bucket and the bucket going into the fire at about three, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

---

At about two, under that awning, in about four people, a man of about thirty-six of that market asked out loud, to nobody in particular, whether anybody four days up that coast road had said one word about that board since it went up on Thursday.

About four of that four said no and one of that four said that the woman of about thirty-four had gone back up on the seventeenth and had said out loud at that gate that she would come down again when there was a reason and not before. The keeper was two stalls along and came over and said out loud that nobody off that morning boat or any other has come down that road into this market since she went, and that no messenger has come either way, and that a letter has not come down it from up there either and there is a post twice a week on that coast.

He said out loud that a thing nobody has asked about for eight days and nobody has come down about is not being refused and is not being allowed and is being waited on, and that he does not know for how long and that nobody has ever come back down that coast road to say that they had stopped waiting.

Nobody in that four said anything after that. The man of about thirty-six of that market looked at the board on the stall and said out loud that the top of it has gone altogether now and that there is one figure left on it and one line of words, and then went and did his own work.

---

On their thirty-ninth day those four new crews got through the day's work in about an hour and three quarters with the man of about thirty-two over them, and about four of that nine have said in the last three days that they will be gone before this run of fifty days is out and about four of that nine have not said a word about when they go.

The woman of thirty of no office came into that yard at about three with her own page and was in it until about five, and the man of about thirty-two sat in that shed with the page of routes from two until about four. The two of them were in one yard for about an hour and no room with the other one and a page in it came up, and neither of them signed the other's page.

Neither of them said one word to the other about the board or about anybody four days up that coast road. About four of that nine noticed that neither of them said anything about it and about four of that nine noticed nothing at all.

The second lay's bench was occupied from ten until two by a man of about thirty-four and he was not asked one thing in all that time, and that makes seventy-nine days of that run.

Twelve hands went onto four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and not one of the twelve was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and forty-nine words, and the cistern came first in it, the question at that stall came in the middle of it, and the two of them in one yard came at the end of it, and this reading counts nothing at all.

Two hundred and forty-second days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in nineteen days and is neither shut nor struck. Sixty days of it, and the wrong figure is left showing underneath that correction and is not struck out, and the two of them are a hand's breadth apart at one size as each other. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its fifty-fifth day. The sheet of the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen. That wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare.**"

"**Nothing at all has been heard out of the left of that man of about twenty-nine's head for three hundred and twenty-seven days, no figure is set against that figure and he is not on the water and no day is set against that figure either. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Off that mole head at two sea miles sit nine hulls, and nobody in this harbor has said anything about any of them for nineteen days.**"

Nobody was thanked for nobody coming down a coast road, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-six lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIXTH CHARGE WAS MADE AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. NOBODY HAS COME DOWN THAT COAST ROAD INTO THIS MARKET SINCE THE SEVENTEENTH AND NO MESSENGER HAS COME EITHER WAY, AND A THING NOBODY HAS ASKED ABOUT FOR EIGHT DAYS IS NOT BEING REFUSED AND IS NOT BEING ALLOWED AND IS BEING WAITED ON. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-SECOND DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the twenty-seventh day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the nineteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Thursday and was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-sixth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it; that the ordinary work of that said Thursday was five boats off that mole before noon and a bell seat cut and faced at that bench, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that at about two under that awning in about four people a man of about thirty-six of that market asked out loud, to nobody in particular, whether anybody four days up that coast road had said one word about that board since it went up on Thursday, and that about four of that four said no; that the keeper said out loud that nobody off that morning boat or any other has come down that road into this market since the woman of about thirty-four went up on the seventeenth, and that no messenger has come either way and that a letter has not come down it from up there either and there is a post twice a week on that coast; that the keeper said out loud that a thing nobody has asked about for eight days and nobody has come down about is not being refused and is not being allowed and is being waited on, and that he does not know for how long; that the man of about thirty-six said out loud that the top of that board has gone altogether now and that there is one figure left on it and one line of words; that the four new crews were on their thirty-ninth day and got through the day's work in about an hour and three quarters; that the woman of thirty of no office came into that yard at about three with her own page and the man of about thirty-two had the page of routes in that shed from about two until about four, and that neither of them said one word to the other about that board or about anybody four days up that coast road, and that neither of them signed the other's page; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the seventy-ninth day of that run of days; that twelve hands went onto four sheets that left that harbor that day, and that no hand and no writer were the same person on any of the four; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; and that the two sheets at that end stall are on their sixtieth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper said the sentence about being waited on out loud in about four people at about two in the afternoon and it is the first sentence anybody has said in this harbor this month about what the other end of that coast road is doing, and that it was said by accident while he was answering a different thing; that the reason there was nothing said between the man of about thirty-two and the woman of thirty of no office about that board is not that they do not think about it, and that half of that nine has watched those two go a month without a word between them over a page and has stopped making anything of it either way, and that this is what a thing looks like when nobody in a lane thinks it is theirs to have; that the reason no messenger and no letter has come down that road is that four hundred people of that community four days up that coast road carry a figure written in chalk that any one of them can read and no reason anybody has given for coming down about it, and that a figure a stranger can read is also a figure a community can be quietly wrong about on its own for a long time before anybody is invited to look; that the reason four of that nine said nothing after the keeper had said it is that there is nothing in this harbor that could be done on Monday about what four hundred people four days up a coast road are doing or not doing on Monday.*
