# Chapter 825

*Sunday the Twenty-Third Day of October, the Fifteenth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Ninth, the Wall Behind That Reeves Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Three Minutes and Back at a Quarter to One and Not Whole, Being Forty-Six and Thirty-Three and Forty-Six and One Hundred and Twenty-Five the Whole of It, a Board in Chalk Standing on the Floor of the Room over That Fish Market Against the Bottom of That Wall from About Half Past Ten Until About Two and Not on That Wall at Any Point, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Sunday the twenty-third day of October, with that market shut until about seven and a rain coming in from the west at about ten that came heavier at about half past ten and stayed heavy until about one and then went off, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Sunday, being a wipe day and a day with no charge on that cistern and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-fifth. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-three minutes and went up again at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, and the whole of that was one hundred and twenty-five minutes, being forty-six off and forty-six back up and thirty-three standing bare, which is one minute longer at each end than the hundred and twenty-eighth time and three minutes longer in all. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fifteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-third day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-fifth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-third day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-ninth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-seventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and seventeenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-fifth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-sixth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-fourth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-third day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-three days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten the way it comes off every Sunday morning, and it was forty-six minutes off and thirty-three minutes standing bare and forty-six minutes back up, which is one hundred and twenty-five minutes the whole of it, and that is a minute longer at each end than the last one was and three minutes longer in all.

About four people were standing there when it came off and about four were standing there when it went up and nobody said why and nobody has said why on any Sunday this harbor can account for, and the ladder is a uniform step and it is a step longer than it was the time before.

The ordinary work of that Sunday in that rain was two boats off that mole while the water was coming over the wall of it and that hatches was out of the water by a little after two in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

---

At about half past ten, in that rain, the man of about fifty-eight of that market and a woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing carried the board of deal in off that stall and up the stairs of that fish market and stood it on the floor of the room against the bottom of the long wall, where the four things are, and where the rain coming off the awning at the bottom of the steps had got in.

It stood there from about half past ten until about two. It was on the floor and it was leaning on the bottom of that wall and it was not on that wall at any point in that time and nothing was written on that wall that day and nothing came off it. The four things on that wall were the four things that had been on it for fifty-six days at the same size, a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction.

About nine people came into that room in the course of that morning and every one of them looked at the board on the floor and not one of them said a single word about it. One of them, a man of about forty-five of that market, stood in front of it for about two minutes and then went out and said nothing to anybody at all, and he has been asked nothing about it since and has not asked anything.

The wall went up again at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, and the board was not on it and nothing was said about that either. At about two the rain went off and the two of them carried that board back down the steps and out to that end stall and stood it against the end of that stall again, and it has been standing in the weather since and the chalk on it is thinner than it was on Friday.

---

On their thirty-fifth day those four new crews had the morning's work off in about an hour and a half of it, which is the quickest they have gone in that shed at anything, and nobody said out loud why and the man of about thirty-two does not know either.

In that room over that fish market the woman of thirty of no office was from about ten until about eleven and again from one until about three, and the man of about thirty-two carried the page of routes in that shed between two and five. They did not say one word to each other about the board on that floor and neither of them signed the other's page.

The man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat on that bench from ten until two and was on it when the wall came off, and nobody asked him anything, and it is the seventy-fifth day of that run of days.

That Sunday put five hands onto the faces of two sheets that left buildings in that harbor, and not one of the five was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries five against this day.

---

The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August.**"

"**The left of the head of that man of about twenty-nine has been empty of any sound at all for three hundred and twenty-three days and no figure stands against that figure and he is not on the water at all. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. That mole head has nine hulls lying off it at about two sea miles and a fifteenth day of this run of days has gone without this harbor noticing them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a board that stood on a floor for three hours and a half and went back out into the weather, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-two lines on it.

A BOARD IN CHALK STOOD ON THE FLOOR OF THAT ROOM AGAINST THE BOTTOM OF THAT WALL FOR ABOUT THREE HOURS AND A HALF AND WAS NOT ON THAT WALL AT ANY POINT AND NOTHING CAME OFF IT AND NOTHING WENT ON IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the twenty-third day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the fifteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Sunday and was a wipe day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday; that the wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-three minutes and went up again at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, and that the whole of it was one hundred and twenty-five minutes, being forty-six off and forty-six back up and thirty-three bare, which is one minute longer at each end than the hundred and twenty-eighth time and three minutes longer in all, and that nobody said why and nobody asked; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that two people carried a board up a flight of stairs and stood it against a wall for three hours and a half and neither of them proposed anything, and that the reason nobody in that room proposed anything is that every person in it could see at once that the board fitted the bottom of that wall and did not fit anywhere on it, and that a thing which fits a wall and not on it is not asking to be put up, and that about nine people noticing that at once and none of them saying it is the ordinary way this harbor handles the things it cannot place; that the reason the wall was standing bare for thirty-three minutes is not a reason anybody has, and that if the board had been on that wall instead of on the floor of it the bare thirty-three minutes would have been the same thirty-three minutes and the board would have gone up with it and would have been down again at a quarter to one with a fifth thing on it; that the four new crews went the quickest they have ever gone in that shed on the day nothing was worked and nothing settled and nobody paid anybody, and that the man of about thirty-two does not know what that means and has not said, and that this yard has four weeks of that in it and would not be the same harbor if it were explained; that nobody in that lane was asked on that Sunday which way round that chalk meant, and that nobody asked a second time because two people had already said out loud in that stall that they did not know, and that a second asking would have turned two admissions into a demand.*
