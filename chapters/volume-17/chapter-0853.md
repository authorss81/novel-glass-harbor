# Chapter 853

*Sunday the Twentieth Day of November, the Forty-Third Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Third Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Seven Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One, Fifty Off and Fifty Back and One Hundred and Thirty-Seven the Whole of It, and Nothing on That Wall Being Explained and Nobody in That Lane Asking, the Sixth Column Down the Side of That Leaf Standing Without a Figure in It on This Day and No Zero Written in It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Six and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Sunday the twentieth day of November, with that market shut until seven and a wet morning out of the south-west with a hard fresh breeze behind it and spray coming over that mole head in gusts that reached the yard twice, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the twenty-first day of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-second day of November. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare until a quarter to one and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty minutes off and fifty back and thirty-seven standing bare, one hundred and thirty-seven the whole of it and a minute longer at each end than it ran the time before. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-first day and nobody has answered it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-third day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-first day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-seventh night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirtieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-second day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-third day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-four days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-second day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-first day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-three days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came down at about half past ten and about four people stood in front of it counting, and one of those four was the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, who has seen that wall come down three times now and had not said one word about it any of the three.

It stood bare thirty-seven minutes and went back up at a quarter to one without being made whole again, and it is the hundred and thirty-third time, and each of the two ends runs a minute longer than it ran the time before, and nobody in that lane said one word about either end of it.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was one boat off that mole before eleven with that fresh breeze on it and a coil of line measured off against a gauge with that door propped on a brick, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-third day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had spent that two hours watching spray come over that mole head and had got nothing at all out of it.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that there is a day down the side of that leaf with nothing at all in the sixth of those columns on it.

The keeper said out loud that he is not going to write a zero in that column for a day nothing was put back on, and that a day with no figure in it is not a day with nothing wrong with it, and that a zero in a column is a thing a man reads at four in the afternoon and believes without asking anybody else at all.

About four of that nine said out loud that this is the first day since that column was started that it has come out of a week empty, and that nobody in that shed can say why, and that the two days before it went one and three, and that the Saturday was the day a line was worked, and that anybody in that room could have told them on Friday what was going to be in that column on Saturday and did not ask.

Nobody asked him a second thing about it. Nobody said any of it twice out loud and nobody put one word of it on anything.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and third day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-two stood in front of that bare wall for the whole of those thirty-seven minutes and counted them on his own two hands without a watch and did not say one word out loud to anybody while he did it, and about four of that nine saw him doing it and said nothing about it then or afterwards.

Two hands went onto the faces of one sheet that left that harbor that day and not one of those two was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries two against this day.

---

That wall went back up at a quarter to one with the top two courses of it left short of where they were the week before, and about four people walked past it between one and two and did not look up at it.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about five hundred and ninety-seven words, and the wall came first in it and the empty column came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and of that Sunday this reading carries five blocks and omits four paragraphs, being the one about the ordinary work of it, the one about those four crews, the one about the two hands, and the one about the top two courses of that wall being left short.

It stood bare thirty-seven minutes and went back up at a quarter to one without being made whole again, and it is the hundred and thirty-third time, and each of the two ends runs a minute longer than it ran the time before, and nobody in that lane said one word about either end of it.**"

From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and third day of that run. The man of about thirty-two stood in front of that bare wall for the whole of those thirty-seven minutes and counted them on his own two hands and said nothing out loud about it.**"

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and sixty-sixth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-three days and is neither shut nor struck. Eighty-four days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its seventy-ninth day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare.**"

"**It is three hundred and fifty-first days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-three days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a wall coming down and going up and getting a minute longer at each end for the hundred and thirty-third time without one person in that lane opening their mouth, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THIRD WIPE WENT BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND STOOD BARE THIRTY-SEVEN MINUTES AND WENT BACK UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE, FIFTY AND THIRTY-SEVEN AND FIFTY AND ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN THE WHOLE OF IT, AND NOBODY SAID WHY AND NOBODY ASKED. THE SIXTH COLUMN DOWN THE SIDE OF THAT LEAF STANDS WITHOUT A FIGURE IN IT ON THIS DAY AND NO ZERO WENT INTO IT. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a wall which comes down on the same hour of the same day for a hundred and thirty-three weeks and takes a minute longer at each end every one of those weeks is the only thing in that lane with a shape in it, and that a shape is not an answer and that about four people stood in front of it and counted it and went to lunch; that the sixth column of that week came out empty and nobody in that room raised it, the cause being that the man of about thirty-two worked out on Saturday morning that this harbor had three figures going into it and could have told the room on Friday afternoon and did not, and that a question about it now would be a question about Friday; that the reason the keeper will not set a zero down is that this harbor has a page of days with a boy of twenty-two at the head of the middle of it with nothing under his hand, and that a column a man reads and believes is the thing this whole run of days has been about; that the man who carried water at about eleven has now stood in front of that wall three times over and opened his mouth on none of them is because he has been in that harbor nineteen days and has asked nobody for anything, and that a man who has counted a thing four times without speaking is not a man making a point.*
