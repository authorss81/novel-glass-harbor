# Chapter 839

*Sunday the Seventh Day of November, the Twenty-Ninth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-First Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Five Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One, Forty-Eight Off and Forty-Eight Back and One Hundred and Thirty-One the Whole of It, About Four of That Nine Saying Out Loud at About One in That Shed That They Have Been Up That Lane to That Room Over the Sailmaker and Have Each Read One of Those Eleven and That Two of Them Read the Same One and That Nobody Is Going to Write Any of It Down, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Seventy-Two and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-One and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Two*

Sunday the seventh day of November, with that market shut until seven and a sky the colour of a wet slate and no wind at all, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Sunday, and a wipe falls on it and there is no charge on that cistern in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe falls on Sunday the fourteenth of November and the next charge is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the second of November. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare until a quarter to one and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at forty-eight minutes off and forty-eight back and thirty-five standing bare, one hundred and thirty-one the whole of it and a minute longer at each end than the last time. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-seven days and nobody has answered it. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-ninth days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-seventh day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and forty-third night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and sixty-first day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and sixteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-ninth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy days together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-eighth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-seventh day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-nine days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-seven days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and there were about four people in front of it counting and one of those four was the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, who had never seen it come down before.

It stood bare thirty-five minutes and it went back up at a quarter to one and it did not go up whole, and it is the hundred and thirty-first time, and each of the two ends is one minute longer than it was the time before, and nobody in that lane said anything about either end and nobody has said anything about any of them since the fourteenth of August.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was a hatches out of the water by about two and a shackle turned at that bench stone, and that work went on no sheet of this yard.

Those four new crews reached their forty-ninth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said that one of them had asked the other three whether anybody was going to say out loud what the four of them were for and that nothing had been said.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that they had been up that lane to that room over the sailmaker during the morning and had each read one of those eleven.

They said out loud that two of them read the same one, and that nobody said which and that nobody minded, and that a count of those would be a count of readings and not of sheets, and that this harbor has never had a column for a reading and is not going to get one this month.

The keeper said out loud that he is not writing one, and that if anybody in this harbor writes the name of a person against one of those eleven he will find out who did it, and that a name against a sheet says the person was put the question and gave an answer, and that nobody has asked anybody anything.

About four of that nine said they had read one out of curiosity and about four said they had read one because they had worked a year off somebody's figures and wanted to see what a page looked like from the other side.

Nobody repeated any of that. Nobody wrote any of it down and about four of that nine went up that lane again at about two and read one of those eleven again, and nobody saw them go.

---

Between about ten and about two not one person in that lane asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, and it is the eighty-ninth day of that run.

The woman of thirty of no office came back down that coast road at about four and was in that yard for about half an hour with her own page and did not go up the coast road again on that Sunday.

The man of about thirty-two did not ask her where she had been and she did not tell him, and neither of them raised the room over that fish market or anything said in it on Saturday, and neither of them signed the other's page.

Five hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and the page of days carries five against this day.

---

The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and nobody here has said a word about them for twenty-nine days.**"

Nobody was thanked for four people going up a lane on a Sunday morning, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty-six lines on it.

ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE READ ONE OF THOSE ELEVEN IN THAT ROOM OVER THAT SAILMAKER AND TWO OF THEM READ THE SAME ONE, AND NO NAME OF ANY PERSON IS AGAINST ONE OF THOSE ELEVEN AND NONE IS GOING TO BE.
