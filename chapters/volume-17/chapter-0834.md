# Chapter 834

*Tuesday the First Day of November, the Twenty-Fourth Day, the Hundred and Fifth Wage Tuesday and the Column on the Lid of That Step Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moving and the Hundred and Sixth Week Fallen Due With No Envelope Against It and the Chest Shut From Ten Until Four, the Keeper Saying Out Loud at About One in About Nine People That He Read One of Those Eleven Sheets Over Again at About Eight This Morning and That Nobody Was Asked For It, a Man of About Fifty of That Market Refusing Out Loud to Be Counted, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Sixty-Seven and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Four*

Tuesday the first day of November, with that market open from six and a wind off that water that took the awnings down twice before nine and a bright cold morning after it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday carries a wage and there is no wipe in it and no charge on that cistern in it, and the next wipe falls on Sunday the sixth of November and the next charge is on the morning of Thursday. The column on the lid of that step stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it does not move today and it does not move on any wage Tuesday in this run, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the hundred and sixth, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-two days and has not been answered once in that time. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-four days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-second day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and thirty-eighth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and eleventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-third day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-fourth day of not speaking about six names. Sixty-five days now of that pair at that end stall standing together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-third day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-second day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-four days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-two days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That column was read out at about ten and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and it is the same figure it stands at on the hundred and fourth wage Tuesday and on the hundred and third and on every one of those before it. The chest was shut from ten until four, and the keeper keeps that key in his own pocket, and nobody came to the door of that office in that hour.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was four boats off that mole before noon in that bright cold light and a length of line cut and laid out on the boards at that shed, and not one of those two went near that fitting-out sheet, and no money at all is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their forty-fourth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said that a man who is paid nothing for four days comes back to a place like this faster than a man who is paid something for four days, and said it to nobody in particular.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper said out loud that he read one of those eleven sheets over again at about eight that morning.

He said out loud that he had read one in September and could not remember one figure on it, and that he went back up that lane and read the whole of it a second time and put it back on the shelf, and that nobody asked him to do that and that nobody was going to be told he had.

He said out loud that those eleven sheets are on a shelf in that room and are not an office, decide nothing, keep nothing, and have never kept anything, and that the only thing they are is a list, and that this harbor put them on a shelf and not on a wall because a wall takes four kinds of thing and a list is not one of the four.

A man of about fifty of that market who sells nothing but cord said out loud that he had read every one of those eleven when they were written and that a second reading of a thing he was in the room for is not a reading, and that he will not have his day counted for it.

Nobody argued with him about it. The keeper said out loud that he was right about it and that nothing in this harbor is going to count him.

---

From about ten until about two the bench in that lane was worked by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay and he was not asked one thing by anybody in that lane on that Tuesday, being the eighty-fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and was in the room over that fish market from two until about three. Between two and five the man of about thirty-two had that page of routes in front of him in that shed and did not say one word to her at about three when she came through that yard on her way up the road.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and seventy-six words, and the column came first in it, the shelf over the sailmaker came in the middle of it, and the two of them passing through that yard at three came at the end of it, and this reading takes no count of anything.

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and was in the room over that fish market from two until about three, and between two and five the man of about thirty-two had that page of routes in front of him in that shed and did not say one word to her at about three when she came through that yard on her way up the road.**"

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and forty-seventh days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-four days and is neither shut nor struck. That pair at that stall has now stood sixty-five days where it stands, at one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure still left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its sixtieth day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen, and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare.**"

"**Three hundred and thirty-two days is that left side with nothing coming out of it, and this harbor sets no figure and no day against it, and he has not been on the water at any point in that time. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor is on its twenty-fourth day without one word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for reading a sheet over again at eight in the morning, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty-one lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND FIFTH WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND WENT AND THAT COLUMN DID NOT MOVE, STANDING AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, WITH THE HUNDRED AND SIXTH WEEK FALLEN DUE AND NO ENVELOPE AGAINST IT AND THE CHEST SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR. THE KEEPER SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ONE THAT HE READ ONE OF THOSE ELEVEN SHEETS OVER AGAIN AT ABOUT EIGHT AND TOLD NOBODY. A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY OF THAT MARKET SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WILL NOT BE COUNTED. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a man of about fifty of that market who sells nothing but cord asked not to be counted is the only person in this harbor in this run of days who has said out loud that he does not want a figure put against his day, and that every other person who has read one of those eleven this month has said the opposite in the ordinary way without being asked; that the reason he said it is that a person who was in the room when a thing was written is not the same as a person who came to it afterwards, and that the count of a second reading is a count of how many people have come back to a thing, and that putting a figure against a man who was in the room would be saying he came back to it when he never left it; that the keeper read one of those eleven over again at about eight that morning and told nobody, and that a reading nobody knows about is the ordinary kind and that a reading nine people watch is the other kind, and that this harbor has had five weeks of practice at learning that the second kind is worth more on a page and less in a lane; that the reason nothing was read out in that shed on that Tuesday is that the sheet is a list of eleven and a list of eleven does not become a record of who has read it by being talked about in nine people, and that the first of those two eleven sheets with a fold in it that was not there in September is still folded.*
