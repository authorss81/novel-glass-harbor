# Chapter 812

*Monday the Tenth Day of October, the Second Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-First Charge on That Cistern and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Keeper Asked Out Loud at About Eleven in About Nine People Whether the Second of Those Five Lines Reaches a Person Who Worked From a Page and Spent Nothing on It and Answering That He Does Not Know, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Coming Off Again Tomorrow, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Monday the tenth day of October, with that market open from six and a grey morning with the wind in the south-west and the water working at eight and a lumpy sea outside the mole head, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Monday, being a rota day and a day with no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the sixteenth and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-first time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Monday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and tenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twelfth day of nobody asking him anything, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fiftieth day of themselves. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and sixteenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-ninth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-first day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-second day of not speaking about six names and passed that lane once today and looked at neither wall. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-third day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eleventh day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the tenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, his surname is on no page and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is not shut and is not struck. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that point. Three hundred and ten days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has now gone without hearing anything, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Monday was four boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it. The four new crews were on their twenty-second day and did the morning's work in about two hours and a quarter under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said the quarter of an hour was in the rigging and not in the man.

The cistern was charged before any of that, at about nine, from the butt behind that market and not from the main, with the last of the previous fill still standing in it and going flat at the bottom, and it was the hundred and ninety-first time and nobody in that market has counted it but this yard, and about four people were standing near it and none of them walked on any of it.

---

At about eleven, in that shed, in about nine people, a woman of about forty-four of that market who sells line and keeps no account of her own asked the keeper a question out loud.

She asked him whether the second of those five lines reaches a person who worked from a page and spent nothing on it. She said it twice, the second time more slowly, and she said that she was not asking about the five lines and that she had read them standing in that lane last week with her thumb on the door frame.

The keeper said out loud that he did not know. He said that he made the five lines and that he stood in that shed with about nine people and said them once and did not say them twice, and that if a person had come up to him that day and said that a line would have to say who says a correction is owed, he would have put it in and nobody did. He said that those five lines give something to a person of a place that has spent on a figure being corrected, and that they give nothing to anybody else at all, and that he knows that is a gap and that a gap with a name on it in nine people in a shed is a gap that will get filled by somebody in a hurry in about a year. He said that he was not going to read those five lines out again in that shed.

About four of that nine said out loud that it was a fair question and about four said nothing and nobody said out loud that it was a poor one. The woman of about forty-four put her thumb back on the door frame, which she has not done since she was a girl in that lane, and said that her own page carried no figure this month and that was all she had come with.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about two with her own page and went up that coast road at two with a sheet under her arm, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about five with the page of routes. Neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them said one word to the other about the question that had been asked in that shed an hour before either of them was in it.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-second day of that run of days.

Seven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those seven was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries seven against this day and no running figure is printed against any of them.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and fifty-one words, and the cistern came first in it and the question in the shed came in the middle of it, and no count of anything came in it.

"**That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks. Two hundred and twenty-fifth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns with nothing under it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare.**"

"**A man of about twenty-nine has now gone three hundred and ten days without hearing anything in the left side of his head, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has closed a second day without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a question asked with a thumb on a door frame or for an answer given in nine people, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-nine lines on it.

**THE CISTERN WAS CHARGED AND IT WAS THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIRST TIME AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE KEEPER SAID OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE THAT HE DOES NOT KNOW WHETHER THE SECOND OF THOSE FIVE LINES REACHES A PERSON WHO WORKED FROM A PAGE AND SPENT NOTHING ON IT, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO READ THOSE FIVE LINES OUT AGAIN IN THAT SHED. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIFTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-first time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it, with the last of the previous fill still standing in it and going flat at the bottom; that the ordinary work of that said Monday was four boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their twenty-second day and did the morning's work in about two hours and a quarter under the man of about thirty-two; that at about eleven in that shed in about nine people a woman of about forty-four of that market who sells line asked the keeper out loud whether the second of those five lines reaches a person who worked from a page and spent nothing on it, and that the keeper said out loud that he does not know and that he made the five lines and said them once and did not say them twice, and that he said those five lines give something to a person of a place that has spent on a figure being corrected and give nothing to anybody else at all, and that he knows that is a gap; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the sixty-second day of that run of days; that seven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day and that no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that the two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-third day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, that the rule in the inside of that door is on its eleventh day, and that the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the tenth day of being on that community's own page four days up that coast road.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper was asked in about nine people whether the second of those five lines reaches a person who worked from a page and spent nothing on it, and that the reason he answered that he does not know is that he can name the day a page went up and the day a page came off and the two hands that went onto the faces of either of them, and that he cannot name one person who has read one, and that a man who is asked a question his own pages cannot answer has two things he can do and both of them are bad; that the reason he did not read the five lines out again in that shed is that the second reading of a thing out loud in a working place is not a record of the thing and is a performance of having it, and that about four people in that market would have said that they had heard them, and that hearing them is not the same as having read them and is worth less; that the reason nobody said out loud that it was a poor question is that the woman of about forty-four sells line to four of the people in that shed and that a poor question said in front of a trade is a thing that costs somebody's week, and that this yard does not put anybody in front of that.*
