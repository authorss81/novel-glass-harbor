# Chapter 855

*Tuesday the Twenty-Second Day of November, the Forty-Fifth Day, the Hundred and Eighth Wage Tuesday and the Last Wage Tuesday Inside This Run of Days, the Chest with the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut from Ten Until Four and the Column on Its Lid Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moving and the Hundred and Ninth Week Fallen Due on Every One of Those Twenty-Six, the Keeper Saying Out Loud at About One in About Nine People That He Is Not Going to Add the Sixth Column Down the Side of That Leaf Up This Month and That He Will Do It Once in Writing on the Last Day of This Run, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Eight and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Fourteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Nine*

Tuesday the twenty-second day of November, with that market open from six and a wet morning out of the south with a lid of cloud at four hundred feet that never lifted, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and eighth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a page. It is the last wage Tuesday inside this run of fifty days and the hundred and ninth falls outside it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday the twenty-fourth day of November and the next wipe is on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-fifth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-third day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-fifth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-third day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-ninth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-seventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-fifth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-six days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-fourth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-third day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-five days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-third days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut on that step from ten until four for the eighth wage Tuesday running, and not one person in that yard so much as laid a hand on that lid, and the column on that lid stands where it stood a week ago at this same hour, at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and the hundred and ninth week is owing on every one of those twenty-six lines.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon under that low cloud and a rubbing strake on one of them planed and varnished where the sun does not reach, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-fifth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that a wet Tuesday with the lid down at four hundred feet is a day for doing nothing much and that they had done nothing much in it well.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper said out loud that he is not going to add that sixth column down the side of that leaf up this month.

He said out loud that four of that nine have now asked him three Tuesdays running what it comes to, and that a column gets added up once and in writing and on the last day of this run of days, and that anybody who comes back at about six on that day can read it off the side of that leaf with their own eyes and does not need a man with a slate at a stall to say it out loud for them.

About four of that nine said out loud that a wage column is not a page and that nothing which has ever been used for something it does not say has come off that lid, and that this is why there is nothing in that sixth column against a day when twenty-six men have been owed a week since the last Tuesday they were owed one.

Nobody put him a second question about that and not one word of it got said over twice in that room.

---

At about half past two the keeper said out loud that on Wednesday he is going to have to stand in about nine people and say a rule out loud, and that he has said out loud twice this month that he will not say a word about what a sixth of anything would be, and that a rule in the inside of that door has five lines on it and that by Wednesday it will have six or it will have five.

Nobody asked him which and he was not asked.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about one until about half past four. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about four and said nothing out loud at any point in it, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about Wednesday or about Saturday.

Four hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those four was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries four against this day.

---

That lid was shut at about four on that Tuesday with the hundred and ninth week owing on every one of twenty-six lines and not one of those twenty-six written anywhere else in that harbor, and about four of that nine said out loud at the door that they would be back for it, and would be back for it eight more times.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and six words, and the chest came first in it and the three Tuesdays running came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and no figure out of that sixth column is in this reading because the keeper is not adding that column up.

Four hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day.**"

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and sixty-eighth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-five days and is neither shut nor struck. Eighty-six days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction.**"

"**The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-first day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare. It is three hundred and fifty-third days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-five days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for eight Tuesdays of a shut lid and a hundred and ninth week owing on every one of twenty-six lines, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-two lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTH WAGE TUESDAY CAME AND WENT WITH THE CHEST SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR AND THE COLUMN ON ITS LID AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND NOT MOVING, AND THE HUNDRED AND NINTH WEEK FALLEN DUE ON EVERY ONE OF THOSE TWENTY-SIX, AND NO WAGE TUESDAY FALLS AGAIN INSIDE THIS RUN OF DAYS. NO FIGURE OUT OF THE SIXTH COLUMN WAS ADDED UP BY ANYBODY. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-second day of November at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-fifth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and eighth wage Tuesday and was the last wage Tuesday inside this run of fifty days, and that the hundred and ninth falls outside it, and that it was not a rota day and was not a wipe day, and that the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday the twenty-fourth day of November and the next wipe is on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November; that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut on that step from ten until four for the eighth wage Tuesday running and not one person in that yard laid a hand on that lid; that the column on that lid stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the hundred and ninth week fallen due on every one of those twenty-six and did not move; that the ordinary work of that said Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon and a rubbing strake planed and varnished where the sun does not reach, and that not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet; that those four new crews were on their sixty-fifth day; that at about one in that shed in about nine people the keeper said out loud that he is not going to add that sixth column down the side of that leaf up this month, and that four of that nine had asked him three Tuesdays running what it comes to, and that a column gets added up once and in writing on the last day of this run of days; that about four of that nine said out loud that a wage column is not a page and that nothing used for something it does not say has come off that lid; that at about half past two the keeper said out loud that on Wednesday he is going to have to stand in about nine people and say a rule out loud, and that a rule in the inside of that door has five lines on it and that by Wednesday it will have six or it will have five, and that nobody asked him which; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and fifth day of that run; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about one until about half past four, and that the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about four and said nothing out loud, and that neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about Wednesday or about Saturday; that four hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and that the page of days carries four against this day; and that that lid was shut at about four with the hundred and ninth week owing on every one of twenty-six lines and not one of those twenty-six written anywhere else in that harbor.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that three people asking a man what a column comes to on three consecutive Tuesdays is as near as this harbor has come to wanting an answer from anybody, and that he will not give it early and that wanting it is not a claim; that the reason he said out loud on Tuesday that a rule in the inside of that door has five lines and that by Wednesday it will have six or it will have five is that a rule he has not said anything about for five weeks cannot be said out loud on Wednesday in about nine people, and that the only honest way to make a rule is to stand in front of it first and let about four people try to break it; that the reason nothing which has been used for something it does not say has come off that wage lid is that a wage is not a figure anybody worked from, it is a figure somebody is paid, and that this harbor has two of those and has had them for six weeks and has not once confused them; that the reason twenty-six men have been owed a week for eight Tuesdays without anybody saying anything about it in a shed is that the man who would say it out loud is the man the lid belongs to, and that a man does not stand in a shed and read his own lid out.*
