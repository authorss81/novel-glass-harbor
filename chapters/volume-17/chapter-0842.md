# Chapter 842

*Wednesday the Ninth Day of November, the Thirty-Second Day, the Fifth Line Worked onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight in the Morning With the Eleventh Day of November on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, Three Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for Three Days and a Hose Coupling and Ninety Yards of Line and Six Men at Three Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of It One Thousand One Hundred and Sixty-Two Pence and Nothing Coming into the Foot of That Sheet Until It Settles, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud at About Two in About Nine People That a Page Which Carries What It Was Used For Is a Page Which Can Be Got At, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Seventy-Five and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Forty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Forty-Four*

Wednesday the ninth day of November, with that market open from six and the rain coming in off the water in long lines that came and went all morning without ever quite settling into weather, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next charge is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wipe is on Sunday the thirteenth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the fifteenth of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fortieth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-second day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eightieth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and forty-sixth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and sixty-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and nineteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and thirty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-first day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-second day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-three days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-first day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fortieth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-two days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eight this morning six men went out on that fifth line and it went onto that fitting-out sheet with the eleventh day of November written on the face of it for the day it settles.

Its five parts are three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence; coal and oil for three days at sixty pence; a hose coupling at forty pence; ninety yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence; and six men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at six times three times sixteen for two hundred and eighty-eight pence. The whole of that line is one thousand one hundred and sixty-two pence, being seven marks, three shillings and sixpence, and it does not touch the foot of that sheet until that yard settles it, which is at about four on Friday, and until then it is on the face of that line and nowhere else.

The hose coupling was got at in that lane and the man of about forty-six of the second lay fitted it with two men holding the hose while he worked, and about four of that nine said that the coupling has been dead since before the winter and that it had cost a day and a half and nobody put that on any sheet.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, the man of about thirty-two said out loud that a page which carries what it was used for is a page which can be got at.

He said it the way a man says a thing into a shed and not to one person in it. He said out loud that a figure with a use written under it is a figure anybody can go and get, and that the man who wrote the use under it is a man somebody can walk up to and ask about it, and that a page with nothing but a figure on it can be got at by nobody for anything.

He said out loud that there is a man in this harbor who cannot read the figures and can copy them and who has said out loud in a room in about four people that he put one of them in the wrong line in September, and that the only reason this harbor knows that is that he said it out loud in a room, and that if the thing had gone on a page instead of into a room there would be no word of it anywhere and there would be nothing to find him by.

He said out loud that a person who is ashamed of a figure is a safer person on a page that carries the figure and nothing else, and that he will not stand in a working place on a Wednesday and be the man who asks a page-holder in front of about nine people where she spent it and what she spent it on.

About four of those nine said nothing at all. Nobody said any of it back to anybody and nobody set a word of it on a sheet.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon in that rain and a coil of line measured off against a gauge, and neither of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-second day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that the man of about thirty-two had said a thing this afternoon that four of the four of them had heard and none of the four could put it in a shorter form than that.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-second day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and then went up that coast road, and she was not in that shed at any point between two and four and did not hear what he said at two.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those eleven was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and twenty-three words, and the line came first in it, the thing he said at two came in the middle of it, and the four columns came at the end of it, and nothing that anybody counts is in this reading.

Nobody was thanked for a man putting the other half of a case out loud in about nine people on a Wednesday and not asking anybody one question about it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty-nine lines on it.

A PAGE WHICH CARRIES WHAT IT WAS USED FOR IS A PAGE WHICH CAN BE GOT AT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the two of them put a case each out loud in about nine people on two consecutive days and neither of them was in the room when the other one said it is the ordinary shape of a thing that has been going on for a month and not the shape of anybody avoiding anything; that the reason she was not in that shed between two and four on the said Wednesday is that she goes up that coast road at one on most days of the week and that going up that road on the morning after a wipe is what she does with a day she does not want to be asked about; that the reason he told that shed out loud that he will not ask a page-holder in front of about nine people is that he held a sheet on Friday while a man wrote on it and has said in his own mouth that a man holding a sheet in his own hand tells whoever is writing on it what that sheet is for, and that a man asking a question across a shed floor in about nine people is the same telling with a different mouth on it; that the reason nothing of that argument was written down is the same reason nothing of the argument at one on Tuesday was written down, and that the keeper has said out loud twice in thirty-two days that nobody has to write anything and that this harbor has no practice at writing down a thing anybody said in there.*
