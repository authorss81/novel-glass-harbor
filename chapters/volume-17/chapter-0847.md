# Chapter 847

*Monday the Fourteenth Day of November, the Thirty-Seventh Day, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and First Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, About Four of That Nine Asking the Man of About Thirty-Two at About Two in About Nine People Whether the Method He Keeps Is What Caused It and the Man of About Thirty-Two Answering That the Page Did Not Say What It Was For Because Nobody Wrote It Down, the Woman of Thirty of No Office in That Shed Hearing All of It and Saying Nothing at All, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Seventy-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Monday the fourteenth day of November, with that market open from six and a raw clear morning with the first of that real cold in it and the water going down off that mole in a long grey roll that took two boats an hour to get out, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on Sunday the twentieth of November and the next charge is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the fifteenth of November. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the two hundred and first time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixtieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-fifth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-seventh day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-fifth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-first night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and sixty-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and thirty-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-seventh day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-eight days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-sixth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-fifth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-seven days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-fifth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt was charged for the two hundred and first time and it took fourteen trips by hand and nobody walked on a single one of them, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried two of them and went back up that lane without stopping.

The ordinary work of that Monday was two boats off that mole before noon in that cold and a mooring eye spliced at that bench with a fid pushed through it and the standing part whipped over its own eye, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-seventh day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped saying out loud what they had waited for, and that what they were doing instead was waiting with better manners.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine asked the man of about thirty-two out loud whether the method he keeps is what caused it.

They asked it four different ways and about four of those nine said all four of them out loud in about two minutes, and they asked it about the page of routes and not about anything else in that shed.

He said out loud that he has been asked that question four days running in four different words and that he is going to answer it the same way four days running if they keep asking it that often.

He said out loud that the figure went four days up that coast road on a page, and that the page did not say what it was for, because nobody wrote it down.

He said out loud that he has spent thirty-seven days being asked about his own two hands and that the figure that went up that road never touched them, and that a page goes out of this harbor with whoever is holding it and that is the method he keeps and that he has said in his own mouth that the person holding it is the page.

He said out loud that the page did not say what it was for because nobody wrote it down, and that he will not say it was written down and lost, and that nobody in this harbor is going to be able to prove that anybody lost anything, because there is nothing on any page of this harbor that anybody could have lost.

About four of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody repeated it after him in there and nobody set one word of it on anything.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-seventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about one until about half past four, and she heard all of it, and she said nothing at all.

At about half past four she went up that lane on her own and the man of about thirty-two did not follow her and did not ask her anything about what she had heard.

Six hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those six was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries six against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a man answering four people out loud in about nine people with the same answer he had given three days before, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty-four lines on it.

**ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE ASKED THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO AT ABOUT TWO WHETHER THE METHOD HE KEEPS IS WHAT CAUSED IT, AND HE SAID OUT LOUD THAT THE FIGURE WENT FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST ROAD ON A PAGE AND THAT THE PAGE DID NOT SAY WHAT IT WAS FOR BECAUSE NOBODY WROTE IT DOWN. THE WOMAN OF THIRTY, WHO HOLDS NO OFFICE, HEARD ALL OF IT AND SAID NOTHING. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTIETH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that four people asked a man four questions in about two minutes about the method he keeps and he answered all four with one sentence and the sentence was the answer he gave on Saturday to four other people, and that the reason he said he would answer it the same way four days running is that a man who changes his answer each time a person asks it a fourth way is answering the way the question was put and not the thing; that the reason the answer was that the page did not say what it was for because nobody wrote it down is that it is the only part of that whole run of thirty-seven days which is not a matter of opinion between two people, and that everything else in that shed for a week has been a matter of opinion between two people and about four of that nine have wanted it settled; that the reason he said he is not going to say it was written down and lost is that if he says that then somebody in this harbor has lost a thing and can go and be angry about it, and that there is nothing on any page here that anybody could have lost, and that the man of about thirty-one of that place walking four days down that coast road with a board the length of a hand in his coat is not a man anybody in this harbor did anything to; that the reason she said nothing at all in that shed is that he was asked about the page of routes and the page of routes is his, and that a woman who keeps a page saying nothing while nine people ask a man about his own is the only thing she could have done in that room without putting the other one in a sentence about the other one's work.*
