# Chapter 830

*Friday the Twenty-Eighth Day of October, the Twentieth Day, an Ordinary Working Friday With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement in It, a Man of About Forty-Four Who Keeps the Crossings Saying Out Loud at About Three in About Nine People a Thing That Is Not a Refusal and Nobody in That Shed Asking Him One Question About It, Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Sixty-Three and Two Thousand Six Hundred and Eighty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Friday the twenty-eighth day of October, with that market open from six and a fine clear morning with the light going off that water early and a frost on the iron rail of that mole by about eight that was gone by ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Friday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the first of November. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twentieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-eighth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and thirtieth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-eighth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirty-fourth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-second day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventieth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their sixty-first day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-ninth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-eighth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-eight days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

What that yard did in the ordinary way on that Friday was three boats off that mole before noon and a length of coil measured off against a gauge with that door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews came to their fortieth day and got the day's work off in about two hours of it under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said that this is the half of it and that nobody has said what the other half of it is for.

---

At about three, in that shed, in about nine people, the man of about forty-four who keeps the crossings for one of those crews said a thing out loud.

He said that he keeps the crossings whether they are made or not, and that a crossing that is not made is still a crossing he is at the end of on that day, and that the tide does not know anything about it either way. He said that he has been asked twice in his life what a day of that comes to and that he could not answer either time, and that he has never worked it out and that it is not in any of the sheets he has been handed and that there is no line on any of them for it and he is not going to be the one who draws one.

He said that he is not saying he would not make a crossing for a boat that had not paid for one, and that he is not saying he would make it either, and that the page he has is still in his coat where he put it in February and that he has not taken it out since and is not going to take it out now. He said that the only thing he has said out loud about it this afternoon is that it is his day and not this yard's, and that he has been keeping crossings for twenty-six years and a crossing is a crossing in October the same as it is in April.

About four of that nine said nothing at all. About four of that nine said something out loud that was not a question and did not name the thing again, being about the water and about a boat that was going out on the tide and about the price of coal.

Nobody in that shed asked him one question about it. Nobody has asked him one question about it since and nobody will. The keeper was at the far end of that shed and heard the whole of it and did not come over.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard between about ten and about one with her own page, and the man of about thirty-two sat in that shed with the page of routes from two until five. He was at that bench when the man of about forty-four was speaking and heard all of it and said nothing about it at any point in the rest of that day.

About four of that nine noticed that he said nothing and the other four were not in that shed at three at all.

At about four the man of about fifty-eight of that market stood in front of that board for a while without touching it. There is one figure left on it now and one line of words under it and about four inches of wet deal where the second figure used to be. He said out loud to nobody that he would put it up again when it was gone altogether and not before, and went and shut that stall.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the eightieth day of that run of days, and he has not missed one of them.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor on that Friday, and no hand on that page and no hand going onto it were ever the same hand, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

"**It is three hundred and twenty-eight days and still nothing in the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, no figure and no days of any kind against it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Two sea miles off that mole head there are nine hulls, and a whole twenty days of this run of days have gone by without a word from anybody in this harbor.**"

Nobody was thanked for a thing said out loud that is not a refusal, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-seven lines on it.

THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR SAID A THING OUT LOUD THAT IS NOT A REFUSAL AND WAS ASKED NOTHING ABOUT IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-THIRD DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the man of about forty-four said out loud that it is his day and not that yard's and that a man who says a thing like that in about nine people has put the whole shape of it where anybody may walk up and look at it, and that nobody put a question to him because one question would have turned it into an answer, and that four hundred people four days up that coast would hear the answer and not the thing; that the reason he was not pressed is that about four of that nine said something about the water and about coal instead, and that people in this harbor have got good at that in five weeks and it is the only new thing any of them has learned since the first of October; that nothing at all was said about it by the man of about thirty-two because a man in that shed who turns and looks at somebody when a page will not sit right has already been named out loud in this yard once this month, and that a second turn of the head at that man would have been noticed by the two people it was done in front of; that board stands with a single figure on it now because four hundred people who could read a pair of them in September can read one, and that nobody has put a copy of the second one anywhere and nobody is going to, and that the first figure is a boat's hire that nobody took and that a stranger with a pencil can take that one figure off a wet board in that lane and cannot take three days off it.*
