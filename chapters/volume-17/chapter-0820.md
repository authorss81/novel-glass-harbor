# Chapter 820

*Tuesday the Eighteenth Day of October, the Tenth Day, the Hundred and Third Wage Tuesday and the Column on the Lid of That Step Not Moving for the Second Time Running, the Count of the Oweable Taken Once at About Two in Writing and Not Read Out in That Shed or in the Slip, Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Fifty-Three and Two Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Tuesday the eighteenth day of October, with that market open from six and rain in from the north-east by seven and the whole of that mole head out of sight until about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Tuesday, being a wage Tuesday and a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday. The column on the lid of that step stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move today and it does not move on any wage Tuesday in this run, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the hundred and fourth week, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety pence out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this tenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and eighteenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twentieth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-eighth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-fourth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twelfth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixtieth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-first day together with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its nineteenth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the eighteenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page and nobody has asked him about it. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in ten days and is neither shut nor struck. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has said nothing whatever about them in ten days. Three hundred and eighteen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without and he is not on the water and no figure is set against that figure.**

---

The keeper read the column on the lid of that step at about ten and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and it is the same figure it was on the hundred and second wage Tuesday and on every one of those before it, and a further week stands fallen due at its close and the hundred and fourth of those weeks has now fallen due.

He read it and then he shut the chest at four and nobody asked him anything about it, because nobody asked him anything about it last Tuesday either.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon in rain and a hatches out of the water by half past three, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

The four new crews were on their thirtieth day and did the day's work in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine have not come back since the second week and about four of that nine are still there and one of those four is the man of about thirty-one who has been in that yard since he was a boy and who was the first one the man of about thirty-two taught anything to.

At about two the keeper took a sheet of his own and wrote the count out on it in his own hand. It is the count of the column headed OWEABLE, which is the number of figures on pages in this harbor on that day that a person in this harbor has worked from and cannot be told about, a page carrying the hand that wrote it and the second hand and nothing at all of the person who read it. The ten days of that column stand at one, two, one, three, one, two, four, one, two and one, which is eighteen.

He wrote it once and read it back to himself before he put the chalk down and about four of that nine saw him do that and nobody has asked him about it. It was not read out in that shed and it is not being read out now. It is on that sheet of his own and on the page of days, and that is the only time in these fifty days it will be written down. Eighteen is a count of figures and not a count of persons and not a count of mistakes and not a count of harm, and every one of the ten days of that column carries a figure and no day in the ten stands at nothing, and nobody has worked out what the eighteen are yet.

---

At about four the keeper said out loud, to about nine people, what he had written on that sheet, which was that a page carries the two hands that can go against it and nowhere to put the name of a person who has read it, and that he had gone round nine working places in that market with a list and a pencil on each of the ten days and had put a figure against a day where a figure on a page in this harbor had been used by somebody in this harbor for something that page did not say, and that he did not know a single name and had not asked for one and was not going to.

About four of that nine said nothing. One of that nine, a woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing, said out loud that she had been one of the eighteen for about a year without knowing it, and that she had counted that year off her own hands and not off his sheet, and that nobody in that market could put a page on a board telling her she had, because there was no fifth kind of thing for it and he had said so on Saturday and he was right.

Nobody said out loud that the count was a good count and nobody said out loud that it was a bad one and nobody asked the keeper what he thought eighteen was.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road at one. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about five with the page of routes and did not go into the end of that mole once. Neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them said one word to the other about the keeper's sheet of his own.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the seventieth day of that run of days, and he has been on that bench every day of it.

Nine hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those nine was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and one hundred and seventy-nine words, and the wage Tuesday came first in it and the count came in the middle of it and the four columns came last in it.

Nobody was thanked for a count written out once in a keeper's hand, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-seven lines on it.

**THE COUNT OF THE OWEABLE WAS TAKEN ONCE, IN WRITING, AND IS EIGHTEEN FIGURES AND EIGHTEEN PEOPLE IS NOT WHAT IT IS. THE FOUR COLUMNS CLOSE THESE TEN DAYS AT FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THREE AND TWO THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINE AND NONE AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE. THE COLUMN ON THE LID OF THAT STEP DID NOT MOVE FOR THE SECOND TIME RUNNING AND A FURTHER WEEK STANDS FALLEN DUE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THIRD DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the eighteenth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the tenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and third wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day; that the column on the lid of that step stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and did not move and that the hundred and fourth week stands fallen due with no envelope against it, and that the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and was shut at four; that the ordinary work of that said Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon in rain and a hatches out of the water by half past three, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their thirtieth day and did the day's work in about two hours, and that about four of that nine have not come back since the second week; that at about two the keeper took a sheet of his own and wrote out once the count of the column headed OWEABLE, being the number of figures on pages in this harbor on that day that a person in this harbor has worked from and cannot be told about, because a page records who wrote it and who put a second hand on it and does not record who read it; that the ten days of that column stand at one, two, one, three, one, two, four, one, two and one, which is eighteen, and that a figure may be written in figures only and a day with no figure is not zero and a figure that a person is in is not a count of people, and that eighteen is a count of figures and not of persons and not of mistakes and not of harm; that the keeper wrote it once and read it back to himself before he put the chalk down and about four of that nine saw him do that and nobody has asked him about it, and that it was not read out in that shed and is not in the half-past-five slip; that at about four the keeper said out loud to about nine people that a page carries the two hands that can go against it and nowhere to put the name of a person who has read it, and that he had gone round nine working places in that market with a list and a pencil on each of the ten days and had not asked for one name and was not going to; that a woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing said out loud that she had been one of the eighteen for about a year without knowing it; that nobody said out loud that the count was a good count and nobody said out loud that it was a bad one and nobody asked the keeper what he thought eighteen was; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day and that no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety pence out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper wrote the count of the column headed OWEABLE in his own hand on a sheet of his own and did not read it out, and that the reason a count of figures is written and not said is that a column read out loud becomes nine people's memory and a column written down stays one person's hand, and that a figure nobody can take off that page afterwards with a pencil is worth less to this harbor than a figure four people heard and did not write down; that the reason he did not ask for a name against any of the eighteen is that the whole of what that column is for is that a figure can be counted without being given away, and that a column with names on it is a list, and that this yard has three lists already and every one of them is a thing somebody can be hurt with; that the reason a woman of about twenty-nine of that market said out loud that she had been one of the eighteen for about a year is that she worked it out from the number of what she has been handed and not from the page, and that a person can count herself, and that this yard has no column for that either.*
