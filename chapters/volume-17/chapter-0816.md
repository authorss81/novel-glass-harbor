# Chapter 816

*Friday the Fourteenth Day of October, the Sixth Day, the Line of the Twelfth Settling at About Four and the Foot of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Seven Thousand and Seven Hundred and Sixty-Eight Out and One Hundred and Seventeen Thousand One Hundred and Twenty-Eight Short, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud at About Three to About Four People That He Has Been the Person a Page-Holder Looks At When a Page Has a Gap in It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Nineteen*

Friday the fourteenth day of October, with that market open from six and a hard bright morning with a swell getting up outside that mole head and the spray coming over it about every fourth sea, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Friday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday. The line worked on Wednesday the twelfth settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four today, and the total at that foot is read after the settlement and not before it, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-eight marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-two marks and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and fourteenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and sixteenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-fourth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twentieth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-sixth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-seventh day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifteenth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the fourteenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fourteen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Friday was three boats off that mole before noon with the swell running and a hatches out of the water by three, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

The four new crews were on their twenty-sixth day and did the day's work in about two hours and a quarter under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said nothing at all today and about two of that nine said out loud that the swell was nothing.

The line settled at about four. The keeper wrote the figure into the foot of that sheet in front of about four people, being six hundred and eighty-six pence, being four marks, three shillings and tenpence, and read the two figures of the foot afterwards and not before, and one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two added to six hundred and eighty-six is one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight, and one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight less ten thousand six hundred and forty is one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight, and both of those are on the face of that sheet and both of those were said out loud.

---

At about three, in that yard, in about four people, the man of about thirty-two said a thing out loud about himself and about four of that four heard all of it.

He said that he has been, for about two years, the person a page-holder looks at when a page has a gap in it. He said that when a page in this harbor has a figure on it that somebody cannot account for, the head goes round and it comes to him, and that he has never written any of those figures and has never been asked to and could not have said where any of them came from. He said that this is because he was the one who knew what the routes were, and that knowing what the routes were made him the obvious person to be asked about a figure, and that there is no more to it than that and that he would not have said it if there had been.

He said that the trouble with that is that when he has stood there and been looked at, the person holding the page has usually gone on writing and not taken what he said. He said that a person looking at him is doing two things at once, one of which is a question and the other of which is a place to put a thing that does not fit, and that he can watch which of the two is happening and cannot stop it.

About four of that four said nothing and one of that four, the keeper, said out loud that it was the same thing the keeper has been doing to the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch and to the man of about fifty-eight of that market and to himself, and that he did not know until that moment that he did it, and that about four of that nine in that shed had come to look at the keeper this month rather than at the sheets.

The man of about thirty-two said nothing to that and went out to the mole and worked until five.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about two and was not in that yard at three, and the man of about thirty-two was in that yard at three and about half an hour before it, and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them signed the other's page.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-sixth day of that run of days.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those twelve was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a thing a person said about himself in about four people, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-three lines on it.

*First column, entered on Friday the fourteenth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the sixth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Friday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the ordinary work of that said Friday was three boats off that mole before noon with the swell running and a hatches out of the water by three, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their twenty-sixth day and did the day's work in about two hours and a quarter under the man of about thirty-two; that the line worked on the twelfth settled into the foot of that sheet at about four and that the total at that foot was read after the settlement, and that one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two added to six hundred and eighty-six is one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight out, being seven hundred and ninety-eight marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight short, being seven hundred and thirty-two marks and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that at about three in that yard in about four people the man of about thirty-two said out loud that he has been for about two years the person a page-holder looks at when a page has a gap in it, and that he said he has never written any of those figures and could not have said where any of them came from, and that the trouble with it is that the person holding the page has usually gone on writing and not taken what he said; that the keeper said out loud that it was the same thing the keeper had been doing all month and that about four of that nine had come to look at the keeper rather than at the sheets; that the man of about thirty-two said nothing to that and went out to the mole and worked until five; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about two and was not in it at three, and that neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them signed the other's page; that the two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-seventh day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out; and that the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the fourteenth day of being on that community's own page four days up that coast road.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the man of about thirty-two said out loud in about four people that he has been the person a page-holder looks at when a page has a gap in it, and that the reason it came out on that Friday and not a month earlier is that a woman had come four days down a coast road and a man had put a slate down face down and both of those had happened without him being in the room, and that a man who has just found out that people go on without him is not yet able to hear himself clearly; that the reason the keeper said out loud that he had been doing the same thing is that in the last week or so about four of that nine came into that shed to look at him rather than at the sheets, and that a keeper who is looked at instead of at his own pages is a page with nobody holding it, and that this yard has three of those and has never once called them by that name; that nobody in that shed put one question to that man about the thing he said he got wrong in September, and that the reason nobody asked is that is not settled on this day and is owed to a later run of days.*
