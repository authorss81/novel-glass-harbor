# Chapter 818

*Sunday the Sixteenth Day of October, the Eighth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Eighth of Them at Forty-Five Off and Thirty-Two Standing Bare and Forty-Five Back Up and One Hundred and Twenty-Two the Whole of It, a Woman of About Thirty-Six of That Market Reading Both Sheets at That End Stall in the Bare Thirty-Two Minutes and Saying One Word and Nobody Asking Her One Question About It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Sunday the sixteenth day of October, with that market open from six and a fine hard morning with the swell gone down in the night and the water flat as a floor at eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary Sunday, being a wipe day and a day with no charge on that cistern and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-eight marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-two marks and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and sixteenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and eighteenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-sixth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-second night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fortieth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and tenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-eighth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-ninth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out and nobody in this harbor has said which of the two of them is right. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventeenth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the sixteenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and sixteen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and it was forty-five minutes off and thirty-two minutes standing bare and forty-five minutes back up, which is a hundred and twenty-two minutes the whole of it, which is three minutes more than the last one was and one minute more at each end than the one before that. Nobody said why and nobody asked.

The man of about thirty-two was not at that bench at ten and was at the end of that mole from about eleven with the four new crews and the book of hours, and they were on their twenty-eighth day and did the morning's work in about an hour and three quarters, which is the quickest they have done and which is not because of him, and about four of that nine said so.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by two, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

---

At about twenty past ten, in the bare thirty-two minutes, a woman of about thirty-six of that market came round the back of that reeve's bench and then down to that end stall, which is about forty paces from where the wall is standing bare with nothing on it.

She had been behind another person at that stall seven days before and had said out loud that there was a person behind her, and she is not a keeper of anything and has no page and no slate and no account of her own.

She read the correction first and then the refusal, standing under that awning, and she did not touch either of them and she did not put her hand out to them. She read the wrong figure that is left showing underneath the correction and she read it twice. She stood there about three minutes in the bare thirty-two minutes with about nine people walking round that market and none of them stopping.

Then she said one word out loud.

The word was both.

Nobody asked her one question about it. Nobody repeated it back to her. Nobody said out loud that it was right and nobody said out loud that it was wrong. A man of about fifty-two who was buying nothing at a stall two along said that it was the first sensible thing he had heard said in that lane in about a month, and a woman of about forty-one said nothing, and the keeper, who was not there and heard about it on Monday, said on Monday that he thought a word was a good deal to get out of a person who had come and looked at two sheets.

She went back to her own two boards and sold until the light went and she has not been asked anything about that word since and has not said anything about it.

---

The line worked on Saturday the fifteenth still stands on that fitting-out sheet with the seventeenth on its face for the day it settles, and nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today and the foot was not read today.

The woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch was still in that market and slept at the back of that shed and went out and looked at the water from the end of that mole at about two for about half an hour and did not speak to anybody, and about four people saw her do it.

The woman of thirty of no office was not in that harbor and was three days up that coast road. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-eighth day of that run of days, and he was not there when the wall came off and nobody looked for him.

Five hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those five was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries five against this day.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and thirty-six words, and the wipe came first in it and the one word came in the middle of it, and no count of anything came in it.

"**The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and it was forty-five minutes off and thirty-two minutes standing bare and forty-five minutes back up, which is a hundred and twenty-two minutes the whole of it. That is three minutes more than the last one was and one minute more at each end than the one before that. Nobody said why and nobody asked.**"

"**The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by two, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it. The four new crews were on their twenty-eighth day and did the morning's work in about an hour and three quarters under the man of about thirty-two, which is the quickest they have done, and which is not because of him, and about four of that nine said so.**"

She read the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and she read it twice. She stood there about three minutes with about nine people walking round that market and none of them stopping. Then she said one word out loud. The word was both.**"

"**And the rest of it. The line worked on Saturday the fifteenth still stands on that fitting-out sheet with the seventeenth on its face for the day it settles, and nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today and the foot was not read today. The woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch slept at the back of that shed and went out and looked at the water from the end of that mole at about two for about half an hour and did not speak to anybody, and about four people saw her do it.**"

"**The woman of thirty of no office was not in that harbor and was three days up that coast road. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything, and it is the sixty-eighth day of that run of days, and he was not there when the wall came off and nobody looked for him. Five hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, every one of them the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against.**"

"**That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight pence out and one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks. Two hundred and thirty-first days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-ninth day with the wrong figure showing underneath the correction and nobody in this harbor has said which of the two of them is right and neither has been taken down. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the leaf from that other community is on its forty-third day a step to the left of that refusal. The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. A man of about twenty-nine has now gone three hundred and sixteen days without hearing anything in the left side of his head and no figure is set against that figure. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has closed an eighth day without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a word, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-five lines on it.

---

**THE WIPE WAS THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHTH AND TOOK ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO MINUTES. ONE WORD WAS SAID UNDER THAT AWNING AND NOBODY ASKED HER ONE QUESTION ABOUT IT AND IT WAS NOT READ OUT IN THAT SLIP. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the sixteenth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the eighth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary Sunday and was a wipe day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday; that the wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-two minutes and went up again at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, and that the whole of it was one hundred and twenty-two minutes, being forty-five off and forty-five back up and thirty-two bare, which is one minute longer at each end than the hundred and twenty-seventh time and three minutes longer in all; that the ordinary work of that said Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by two, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their twenty-eighth day and did the morning's work in about an hour and three quarters under the man of about thirty-two, which is the quickest they have done and is not because of him; that at about twenty past ten, in the bare thirty-two minutes, a woman of about thirty-six of that market came round the back of that reeve's bench and then down to that end stall, about forty paces off, and read the correction and then the refusal and then read the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction twice; that she said one word out loud and that the word was both; that nobody asked her one question about it and nobody repeated it back to her and nobody said it was right and nobody said it was wrong, and that a man of about fifty-two buying nothing at a stall two along said it was the first sensible thing he had heard said in that lane in about a month; that the line worked on the fifteenth still stands on that sheet with the seventeenth on its face for the day it settles and nothing settled into the foot of that sheet on the said day and the foot was not read on it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight out, being seven hundred and ninety-eight marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight short, being seven hundred and thirty-two marks and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch slept at the back of that shed and looked at the water from the end of that mole at about two and did not speak to anybody; that the woman of thirty of no office was not in that harbor and was three days up that coast road; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything, and that it is the sixty-eighth day of that run of days, and that he was not there when the wall came off and nobody looked for him; that five hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day; and that the two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-ninth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a woman of about thirty-six of that market said one word under that awning and it was both, and that the reason she said that word and not either of the two longer ones is that both of the longer ones would have named a side and she does not know what either of them is for; that the reason nobody repeated the word back to her is that a word repeated back to a person in a market becomes an opinion they are said to hold, and about nine people walking round that market would then have known what she thinks about two sheets that have stood there forty-nine days; that nobody in that market thought to ask the man of about thirty-two to be standing at that bench at half past ten, and that the reason nobody thought of it is that a man who keeps a page of routes is not a man anybody thinks of when a wall comes off, and that this harbor has never once asked anybody to keep both.*
