# Chapter 840

*Monday the Eighth Day of November, the Thirtieth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Ninth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Keeper Writing at About Two in About Nine People on a Sheet of His Own the Count of the Column Headed SECOND READING, Once, and Reading It Back to Himself Before He Put the Chalk Down, Its Ten Days Standing at One, Two, None, One, Three, One, Two, One, Four and One, Which Is Sixteen, and It Not Read Out in That Shed and Not in the Slip, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Seventy-Three and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Seven*

Monday the eighth day of November, with that market open from six and a hard blue morning with the first of the coming winter in the way that light comes off that water before the weather turns, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday carries a charge on that cistern and carries no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe falls on Sunday the fourteenth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of tomorrow. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-ninth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirtieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-eight days and nobody has answered it. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and fortieth day. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-eighth day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and forty-fourth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and sixty-second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and seventeenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and eightieth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-one days together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-ninth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-eighth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-eight days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

By about nine that butt had been filled for the hundred and ninety-ninth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and nobody walked on any of them, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried two of them and did not make a thing of it.

The ordinary work of that Monday was two boats off that mole before noon and a coil of line measured off against a gauge, and that work belongs to no sheet here.

Those four new crews reached their fiftieth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that a fifty-day run is a thing that has been said out loud this month and that nobody in that shed has said anything about where they go after it.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper wrote the count of a column headed SECOND READING on a sheet of his own, once.

He wrote it in chalk and he read it back to himself before he put the chalk down, and the ten days of that column stand at one, two, none, one, three, one, two, one, four and one, which is sixteen.

He did not read it out in that shed. It is not in the half-past-five slip. Nobody in that shed has been asked what sixteen is and nobody has said out loud what it is for, and about four of that nine have said out loud that they think he has taken a count of something and have not asked him of what.

He said out loud, to nobody in particular, that a day with no figure on it is not a zero and that he left that day blank and wrote nothing on that sheet for it, and that the ten days therefore add to sixteen and that anybody who wants to check that can check it.

He said out loud that the sheet with that count on it goes into a drawer in the corner of that room up the lane at about half past three on the last day of this run with the day it went in written on the outside of it, and that nobody is going to read it out before then and nobody is going to read it out after then either unless he is asked by somebody.

At about half past three a man of about thirty-six of that market read one of those eleven over again on that shelf and had read one on Friday, and neither of them was told which and neither of them was asked.

---

From about ten until about two that bench was worked by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay and nobody asked him anything, and it is the ninetieth day of that run of days he has not missed one of.

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road at one. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from two until five and was told about the count at about half past four and said nothing about it and read the page of routes he had in front of him until five.

Nine hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor that day and the page of days carries nine against this day.

The first column of that leaf stood at five hundred and seventy-two at half past five and the thirtieth entry went into it at about six, and three of that day's five in the second column had gone in before the reading began and two went in after it, and the four columns close these thirty days at five hundred and seventy-three and two thousand seven hundred and thirty-nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five.

"**It is three hundred and thirty-eight days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, and nothing of any kind is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and thirty days have gone by in which nobody in this harbor said anything about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for writing a figure down once and saying nothing about it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty-seven lines on it.

THE COUNT OF THE OWEABLE STANDS AT EIGHTEEN AND WAS TAKEN ONCE ON THE TWENTIETH DAY AND IS NOT TAKEN AGAIN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THIRD DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the eighth day of November at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirtieth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Monday and was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe falls on Sunday the fourteenth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of tomorrow; that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-ninth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it, and that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried two of those trips and did not make a thing of it; that the ordinary work of that said Monday was two boats off that mole before noon and a coil of line measured off against a gauge, and that not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet; that those four new crews were on their fiftieth day and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that shed has said anything about where they go after it; that at about two in that shed in about nine people the keeper wrote the count of a column headed SECOND READING on a sheet of his own, once, in chalk, and read it back to himself before he put the chalk down; that the keeper said out loud that a day with no figure on it is not a zero and that he left that day blank and wrote nothing for it; that the keeper said out loud that the sheet goes into a drawer in the corner of that room up the lane at about half past three on the last day of this run with the day it went in written on the outside of it, and that nobody is going to read it out before then or after then unless he is asked; that at about half past three a man of about thirty-six of that market read one of those eleven over again on that shelf and had read one on Friday and neither was told which; that between about ten and about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the ninetieth day of that run; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road at one, and that the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from two until five and was told about the count at about half past four and said nothing about it; that nine hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor, and that the page of days carries nine against this day; that the four columns close these thirty days at five hundred and seventy-three and two thousand seven hundred and thirty-nine and none and one hundred and twenty-five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the count of the column headed SECOND READING is a count of readings and not of sheets and not of people and not of understandings, and that two of the four who read one on the Sunday read the same one, and that this harbor had no column for a reading at all until today and has now written one down once on a sheet of the keeper's own and is not going to say out loud in about nine people what it comes to; that the reason it was taken once and on the last day of a movement is that a count taken every day is a thing this yard looks at every day and this yard has had five weeks of practice at looking at things once and then letting them alone; that the reason the third day of this movement carries no figure at all is that a blank is not a zero and that a count which goes down on a day because nobody happened to want to read anything is a count of what people wanted and not a count of what was available; that the reason the man of about thirty-two said nothing when he was told at about half past four is that he has spent ten days asking to be given a person to put a figure right to and has been given a figure instead, and that on Friday a man said out loud, about a sheet, that one in his hands makes him that sheet does not take up another one on the Monday, whatever is written on it.*
