# Chapter 846

*Sunday the Thirteenth Day of November, the Thirty-Sixth Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Second Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Six Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One, Forty-Nine Off and Forty-Nine Back and One Hundred and Thirty-Four the Whole of It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Putting His Case at About Two in About Nine People in the Form It Takes When It Cannot Be Made Any Easier and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Answering It in the Form It Takes When She Cannot Be Made Smaller, and Neither of Them Giving Way in Anything, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Seventy-Nine and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Sunday the thirteenth day of November, with that market shut until seven and a hard grey morning with that wind back in the north-west and the ice gone off the puddles in that lane in the night, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on Sunday the twentieth of November and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the fourteenth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the fifteenth of November. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare until a quarter to one and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at forty-nine minutes off and forty-nine back and thirty-six standing bare, one hundred and thirty-four the whole of it and a minute longer at each end than the last time. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-fourth day and nobody has answered it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-sixth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-fourth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fiftieth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and sixty-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and thirty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-sixth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-seven days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-fifth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-fourth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-six days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-fourth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and about four people stood in front of it counting and one of those four was the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, who had seen it come down twice now and had not said a word about it either time.

It stood bare thirty-six minutes and went back up at a quarter to one without being made whole again, and it is the hundred and thirty-second time, and each of the two ends runs a minute longer than it ran the time before, and nobody in that lane said one word about either end of it.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was one boat off that mole before eleven with that north-west wind still on it and a coil of line measured off against a gauge with that door propped on a brick, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-sixth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that whatever was going to be said in that shed today would be said in the wrong weather for it.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, the man of about thirty-two said out loud the whole of his case in the form it takes when it cannot be made any easier.

He said out loud that the person the use was made against is a man four days up that coast road whose name nobody in this harbor has and never will have, and that a use written at the foot of a page does not make that man's page safer but makes it longer, and that the name of the person who spent the money goes under the figure in the hand of the person who keeps the page.

He said out loud that a man whose name is under a figure in about nine people is the safest person in that harbor and is gone by Monday morning, and that the man of about thirty-one of that place came down four days of that road on his own two feet on Wednesday and would not have come at all if the page had carried his name in the hand of the person who keeps it.

He said out loud that he is not afraid of a page being wrong and has said in his own mouth on Friday that a page being wrong is not a reason to stop, and that what he is afraid of is a page being right in a way that costs a person the rest of his life.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, was in that room and she heard all of it, and four of those nine heard the whole of it, and about four of the rest of them were outside the door and two of the rest had gone up that lane.

---

At about half past two, in that shed, the woman of thirty, who holds no office, answered that in the form it takes when it cannot be made smaller.

She said out loud that a man asking for one page to be safe for the person who spent the money and findable for the person it was spent on is asking for two pages at once, and that one page cannot be both of those things at once, and that thirty-six days of two married people not managing that is not a failure of anybody in that shed.

She said out loud that the man of about thirty-one of that place did not come down that road because a page carried his name and he came because nobody in four days of coast road had told him that a figure was wrong, and that a page in this harbor cannot put a hand on a man at a gate four days away, and that the whole of what a page can do is be the thing a person was already holding when they made the decision.

She said out loud that safety is what the page is for and has been what the page is for since before either of them was keeping one, and that she is not going to keep a page that is safe for the people who spend money on it, and that she has said that out loud in about nine people twice now and is not going to stop saying it.

Neither of them gave way in anything. About four of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody said any of it twice out loud and nobody wrote one word of it down either.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-sixth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Two hands went onto the faces of one sheet that left that harbor that day and not one of those two was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries two against this day.
