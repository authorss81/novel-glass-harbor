# Chapter 857

*Thursday the Twenty-Fourth Day of November, the Forty-Seventh Day, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Fourth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, About Four of That Nine Saying Out Loud at About One in About Nine People That a Rule With Six Lines on It Is the Only Thing Made in This Harbor in Six Weeks That Anybody Agreed To and That Nobody Here Can Be Made to Work Under It, a Template for the Lamp at the Head of That Mole Cut at That Bench and No Glass in That Lamp and Nobody in That Yard Saying Out Loud Whose Glass It Is Going to Be, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Twenty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Nineteen*

Thursday the twenty-fourth day of November, with that market open from six and a clear morning with that first frost standing hard in the shade of everything that does not get the sun until about ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and no charge falls on that cistern again inside this run of fifty days and the next wipe is on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November, and no wage Tuesday falls again inside this run of fifty days. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the two hundred and fourth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-fifth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-seventh day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-fifth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-first night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-seventh day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-eight days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-sixth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-fifth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-seven days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-fifth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That butt was charged at about nine for the two hundred and fourth time and it took fourteen trips by hand, and not one of them was walked on, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place took two of the pails up himself and then stood at that door with the water running off him, and that is the twenty-third day of that run of days he has been in this harbor.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was three boats off that mole before noon with the sun on that hard frost and a template cut at that bench out of a strip of sheet copper for the lamp at the head of that mole, whose glass has been out since the middle of the summer and whose lamp has not been asked for by anybody, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-seventh day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that a copper template is the only thing made in that yard this week that anybody has said nothing about.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that a rule with six lines on it is the only thing made in this harbor in six weeks that anybody agreed to.

They said it in about four different words and about four of that nine said all four of them out loud inside about two minutes. They said out loud that nobody in this harbor can be made to work under it, and that a rule nobody has to work under is a rule about nothing at all, and that about four of that nine agreed to six lines on Wednesday for no reason that will survive a Tuesday.

The keeper said out loud that he is not going to argue that in a shed on a Thursday, and that it will have to be worked under by somebody before this run of days is over, and that he is not going to be the man who says by whom.

About four of that nine said out loud that the six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has now got a seventh line onto it, and he said out loud that it has not, and that that sheet has stood at the back of that stall for four hundred and thirty-four days with six lines printed on it and nothing written on it by anybody, and that a rule is not a sheet and a sheet is not a rule and that this harbor has been getting those two things mixed up for six weeks.

Nobody asked him which of the two was which any further than that.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and seventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about three and said nothing out loud, and at about half past two he thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that a thing this harbor agrees to is a thing this harbor has not wanted in six weeks, and that the first person in that shed to have to work under six lines is going to be a person who has asked this harbor for nothing for twenty-three days, and that he is not going to say that out loud either.

Six hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those six was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries six against this day.

---

That copper template lay on that bench stone at about five with the lamp at the head of that mole still out and no glass in it, and nobody in that yard had said out loud whose glass it was going to be.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about six hundred and seventy-one words, and the cistern came first in it and the six lines came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and this reading leaves out three paragraphs of that Thursday, being the one about those four new crews, the one about the man of about thirty-two at about half past two, and the one about that copper template lying on that bench stone at about five.

"**From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and seventh day of that run. Six hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings.**"

"**Two hundred and seventieth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-seven days and is neither shut nor struck. Eighty-eight days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-third day.**"

"**The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare. It is three hundred and fifty-fifth days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-seven days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for six lines nobody can be made to work under, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-four lines on it.

**ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ONE THAT A RULE WITH SIX LINES ON IT IS THE ONLY THING MADE IN THIS HARBOR IN SIX WEEKS THAT ANYBODY AGREED TO AND THAT NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR CAN BE MADE TO WORK UNDER IT. A COPPER TEMPLATE FOR THE LAMP AT THE HEAD OF THAT MOLE WAS CUT AT THAT BENCH AND THERE IS NO GLASS IN THAT LAMP. NO FIGURE WENT INTO THE SIXTH COLUMN AGAINST THIS DAY. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTIETH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason those four people put it out loud, that nobody in this harbor can be made to work under six lines, is that every one of those lines was agreed to on Wednesday with no stake in it, and that a rule a man agrees to for nothing costs him nothing and a rule a man has to live inside costs him everything he has, and that this harbor has found that out in six weeks at somebody else's expense; that the reason he would not say by whom it will be worked under is that the moment a keeper names a man in a shed that name is on a sheet in about nine people's heads and this harbor has spent six weeks finding out what a name under a figure costs; that the reason the confusion about the six-line sheet at that stall was allowed to stand for a minute before he corrected it is that four of those people had genuinely believed a rule had been printed there, and that a rule nobody can tell from a sheet is a rule two thirds of that harbor cannot use; that the reason the template was cut and left lying on that stone is that the lamp at the head of that mole has been out since the middle of the summer and nobody has been asked about it, because a light that everybody can see being wrong is a thing this harbor has never found a column for.*
