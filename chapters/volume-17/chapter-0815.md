# Chapter 815

*Thursday the Thirteenth Day of October, the Fifth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Second Charge on That Cistern and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, One Question Asked Out Loud of the Woman of About Thirty-Four of the Saltmarch by About Four People at About Eleven and Her Answer That the Page Did Not Say What the Figure Was For, the Line of the Twelfth Still Standing Unsettled Until Tomorrow, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Fourteen*

Thursday the thirteenth day of October, with that market open from six and a fine bright morning with the wind gone round to the north and the water glassy again at eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Thursday, being a rota day and a day with no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-second time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it. The line worked on Wednesday the twelfth is still standing unsettled on that fitting-out sheet with the fourteenth on its face for the day it settles, and nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today and the foot is not read today, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fifth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and thirteenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and fifteenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-third day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and nineteenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-seventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-fifth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-sixth day together with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fourteenth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirteenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirteen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The butt behind that market was charged at about nine for the hundred and ninety-second time and about four people stood near it and not one of them walked on any of it. A woman of about thirty-four of that market said out loud, without being asked, that a cistern was a thing this harbor had never once got wrong, and nobody replied to her and about four of them agreed with her later in the day.

At about eleven, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine asked the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch one question, and they asked it four different ways over about twenty minutes and she answered it once.

The question was what that page of the Lowfen Reed's own was for, and whether four hundred and six pence on it was the price of a crossing, and whether anybody had ever said on that page what it was a figure of.

She said out loud that the page did not say what the figure was for. She said that a page is a thing a figure is written on and that a figure of what a crossing cost on one day in August is not a figure of what a crossing costs, and that the two are the same size and one of them is a mistake waiting for somebody to make it. She said that in her own community nobody asked her what it was for and that she read it herself and spent it herself and that she is not going to say in this shed that somebody else should have stopped her, because there was nobody else in it.

About four of that nine said out loud that that was fair and about four said nothing. Nobody said her reason was wrong. Nobody said it was right either.

Then a man of about forty-six of the second lay who had been in that shed about an hour said out loud, to nobody in particular, that anybody could get that wrong, and that he had got something wrong himself in September and had never found out until a stranger told him, and then he said nothing more about it and about four of that nine looked at him and nobody asked him a single question.

---

The ordinary work of that Thursday was five boats off that mole before noon and a bell seat cut and faced at that bench, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either of those.

The four new crews were on their twenty-fifth day and did the day's work in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, who was in that shed from about two until about five with the page of routes, and he had not been in that shed at eleven when the question was asked and he has not been told what was said at eleven.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and did not come back into that shed after one.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-fifth day of that run of days.

Eight hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those eight was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries eight against this day.

---

Two hundred and twenty-eighth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. A man of about twenty-nine has now gone three hundred and thirteen days without hearing anything in the left side of his head, no figure is set against that figure, and he is not on the water. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has closed a fifth day without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for an answer given once in four different forms of a question, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-two lines on it.

**THE CISTERN WAS CHARGED AND IT WAS THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SECOND TIME AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE PAGE DID NOT SAY WHAT THE FIGURE WAS FOR AND NOBODY SAID THAT THE WOMAN WHO SPENT IT WAS WRONG. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch was asked one question four different ways and answered it once, and that the reason for the once is that four ways of asking a question are four chances for a person to answer a different one, and that a person who has understood a thing will give the same answer to all four or none of the four, and that this is the only test of understanding this yard has ever used and it costs nothing; that the reason she would not say in that shed that somebody else should have stopped her is that saying that would put nine people in a room where one of them is going to lose money and the other eight are not, and that is the shape of a room where nothing true gets said; that the reason a man who works from a page of his own got up in that shed and said a true thing about himself, and was then asked nothing at all about it is that a person who volunteers a fault in a working place is doing the one thing that cannot be done with him afterwards, and that about four of that nine understood that and the rest of that nine did not.*
