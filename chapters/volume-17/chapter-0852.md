# Chapter 852

*Saturday the Nineteenth Day of November, the Forty-Second Day, the Sixth Line Worked onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight in the Morning With the Twenty-Fourth Day of November on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, One Day of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for One Day and Forty Yards of Line and a Case and Four Nails and Six Men at One Day at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of It Four Hundred and Thirty-Four Pence and Nothing Coming into the Foot of That Sheet Until It Settles on Monday, Three Figures Entered at About Three Down the Side of That Leaf at the Figures They Were Given In, the Man of About Thirty-Two Not Being the One Who Puts It to That Room and Not One Thing in About Nine People on That Saturday Coming Out of His Mouth About It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Five and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Ninety-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Saturday the nineteenth day of November, with that market shut until seven on a Saturday and the rain coming in off the water in long lines until about two and then a hard blue sky behind it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Saturday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe falls on tomorrow, being Sunday the twentieth of November, and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the twenty-first day of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-second day of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it in the sense of settled on it, that foot standing before this morning's work at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and what was worked on it this morning stands on the face of that line and on nothing else in that yard. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fiftieth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-second day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninetieth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-sixth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-first day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-second day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-three days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-first day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fiftieth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-two days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fiftieth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eight this morning six men went out of that yard on that sixth line and it went onto that fitting-out sheet with the twenty-fourth day of November written on the face of it for the day it settles.

Its five parts are one day of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one times one hundred and sixty-eight for one hundred and sixty-eight pence; coal and oil for one day at twenty pence; forty yards of line at three pence the yard at forty times three for one hundred and twenty pence; a case and four nails at thirty pence; and six men at one day at sixteen pence a man-day at six times one times sixteen for ninety-six pence. The whole of that line is four hundred and thirty-four pence, being two marks, nine shillings and sixpence, and it does not touch the foot of that sheet until that yard settles it, which is at about four on Monday the twenty-first day of November.

The case and four nails were got at in that lane on Friday and about four of that nine said out loud that a case and four nails at thirty pence is the cheapest part of any line this yard has worked and that the boat's hire at one hundred and sixty-eight pence is the dearest and that both of those figures are the same two figures chalked on a board in that market four days up that coast road.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-second day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped waiting on Tuesday and had not started again and were not going to.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine put it to the keeper that a figure said out loud in a shed on Friday is not a figure anybody can get on Monday.

The keeper said out loud that he will not set a thing down in about nine people until that thing has been said out loud in about nine people and agreed to by about four of them, and that a rule in the inside of that door of his has five lines on it and that he is not going to say one word in this shed today about what a sixth of anything would be.

About four of that nine said out loud that there is nowhere in this harbor to set a thing down where a stranger could find it, because a wall in that room over the fish market takes four kinds of thing and a line is not one of the four, and because that room over the chandler's store has a bare wall and a drawer, and about four of that nine said the two of those out loud without being asked.

The man of about thirty-two was in that room the whole of that afternoon and not one thing about it came out of his mouth, and about four of that nine did not ask him one question about it at any point between one and four.

---

At about three the keeper set three figures down on the page of that week in his own hand at the foot of that day's entry, being one hundred and sixty-eight pence for a boat's hire for one day at the figure in chalk, being one mark and eightpence; and sixteen pence for a day of one man's work, being one shilling and fourpence; and three pence the yard for line off that bench.

He said out loud that all three of those are on the face of that sixth line this morning at the figures they were given in and that all three of them have been carried four days up that coast road as what a crossing costs, and that not one of the three of them says on its own face what it is for. Three figures went into the sixth of those columns down the side of that leaf and into no other.

About four of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody asked him what the three of them are worth and he was not asked.

---

About four of that nine turned to the woman of thirty, who holds no office, at about half past three and asked her in four different words whether a page of her own can carry a thing of that kind.

She said out loud that a rule is not hers, and that she keeps pages and does not keep rules, and that she put one thing at the foot of one page of her own this month and is not putting a second thing at the foot of a second one for anybody in that shed.

She said out loud that not one of the two people in this harbor who are married to each other has ever put a hand on the other's page, and that this afternoon is not the afternoon on which either of them starts. Nobody wrote one word of that down.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and second day of that run of days he has not missed.

Seven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those seven was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries seven against this day.

---

That sixth line lay on that bench stone with the twenty-fourth of November on the face of it until about five, and the figure in chalk on a board in that market four days up that coast road is one hundred and sixty-eight pence and is not the figure on the face of that line, and nobody in this harbor can get at that board to say so.

---

About half past five on that Saturday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and thirty-nine words, and the line came first in it and the four of that nine who wanted a thing set down came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and this reading carries six of the seven paragraphs of that day and leaves out the one about the man of about thirty-two at that bench.

Nobody was thanked for three figures being set down in the figures they were given in on a Saturday afternoon by a man who keeps a slate at a stall, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty-nine lines on it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that three figures going onto a leaf in the figures they were given in is the whole of what this harbor has been able to do about a use since the first day of October, and that three is fewer than a person expects and that a person expecting more is not being robbed; that the reason the keeper will not set anything down until about four of about nine people have agreed to it out loud is that he said on Wednesday that he will not copy a line onto anything and that a copy is not a witness, and that a sixth thing put up on Wednesday would have been that same copying with a different name on it; that the reason the woman of thirty said a rule is not hers is that a rule she agreed to would be a rule the keeper had to keep and a rule she had to answer for, and that two married people have already spent a fortnight over who holds the pen and she is not going to hand anybody a third pen; that the reason the man of about thirty-two put nothing to that room is that he said his case twice in about nine people last week and about four of that nine told a man of about thirty-six's version of it to his face on Saturday, and that a third time is not a case, it is a habit; that the reason that line lay on that bench stone until about five with its day on the face of it is that a line is the only thing in that harbor that carries what it cost and what it was for in the same breath, and that if that yard could do that for a crossing there would be nothing to argue about.*
