# Chapter 854

*Monday the Twenty-First Day of November, the Forty-Fourth Day, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Third Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Sixth Line Settling at About Four on That Fitting-Out Sheet and the Whole of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Not Before It, the Foot Standing at One Hundred and Thirty-Two Thousand One Hundred and Eighty-Four Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand Five Hundred and Forty-Four Pence Short, the Woman of About Twenty-Nine of the Lowfen Reed Coming Down That Lane for the Fourth Time in This Run of Days and Being Asked One Question at About One and Answering It, and Nobody Saying That Her Answer Is Wrong, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Seven and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Four*

Monday the twenty-first day of November, with that market open from six and a low grey morning out of the north with the wind gone in the night and a long smooth swell coming in under that mole that made no noise at all, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday the twenty-fourth day of November and the next wipe is on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-second day of November. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the two hundred and third time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. That sixth line settled at about four and the whole of that fitting-out sheet was read after that settlement and not before it, and the foot of that sheet stands at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-second day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-fourth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-second day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-eighth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-third day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-fourth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-five days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-third day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-second day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-four days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The butt was charged at about nine for the two hundred and third time and it took fourteen trips by hand and nobody walked on a single one of them, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried two of them and went back up that lane without stopping, and that is the twentieth day he has been in that harbor.

The ordinary work of that Monday was two boats off that mole before noon on that long smooth swell and a set of rigging screws gone through with a tap and then a smear of grease over each of them, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-fourth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had noticed that the sea had gone quiet and had not trusted it.

---

At about one the woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed came down that lane for the fourth time in this run of days and came into that shed and stood at the back of it.

The keeper asked her one question and he asked it in the ordinary voice and not to anybody in particular, and the question was whether a page is still a page once there is a use written at the foot of it.

She answered it out loud and in one run of sentences. She said out loud that a page is a price and that a price is what a thing cost and not what it was for, and that the page she keeps has never said what any figure on it was for and that this is not a fault in it and is not going to be put right. She said out loud that the day a page begins to carry what a thing was used for is the day a person can be angry with it, and that a thing a person can be angry with is not a thing a person can check, and that she would rather keep a page nobody can be angry with than be able to be angry with her own.

About four of that nine heard all of it. Nobody said one word against it and nobody asked her a second thing about it, and nobody set one word of it on anything.

---

At about four that sixth line settled and at about a quarter past four the keeper read the whole of the foot of that fitting-out sheet out loud in about nine people.

He said out loud that he reads the foot after the settlement and never in the morning before a figure has been agreed, and that he read it after the settlement in September and in October and in November and is not going to stop. He read out that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

About four of that nine said out loud, after he had finished, that a settlement is not a figure put back, and that a worked line carries what a thing cost and what it was for in the same five parts, and that that is why the sixth column down the side of that leaf stands empty on a Monday and will stand empty on every Monday this harbor has.

Nobody wrote that figure down out loud in that shed and nobody repeated it after him.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-two heard the whole of what the woman of about twenty-nine of the Lowfen Reed said and said nothing out loud at any point in it, and thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that she has said in four sentences the whole of what he has been trying to say to nine people for a fortnight, and that she said it about a page of her own keeping and not about him, and that he has no standing whatever to agree with her or to argue with her and is not going to get one this month.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those ten was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

That foot stood at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short at about a quarter past four on that Monday, and that sheet is not the sheet with the fifteen gaps in it at that end stall, and that sheet has not moved since the twenty-fifth day of September.

---

It is three hundred and fifty-second days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-four days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for one question asked of one woman at the back of a shed and one answer given in four sentences that nobody in that room argued with, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-one lines on it.

THE WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE OF THE LOWFEN REED CAME DOWN THAT LANE FOR THE FOURTH TIME AND WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION AND ANSWERED IT AND NOBODY SAID HER ANSWER WAS WRONG. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the fourth time that woman came down that lane she was asked one question and gave the same answer in fewer words than anybody in that room expected, and that nobody arguing with it is not agreement with it and is not a vote; that the reason the keeper asked her and not anybody else is that she has refused a correction in her own community's hand and has been right to refuse it, and that a person who has been right once about a page is the only person in that shed whose answer on a page is worth putting into a room; that the reason nobody said one word against it is that two married people have been saying the hardest version of their own case into that shed for a fortnight and a woman from four days up a coast road has just said the whole of it in four sentences and neither of them has been able to do it; that the reason a settlement is not a figure put back is that it is the one kind of record in that harbor which carries its own use in its own body, being five parts which each say what the thing is and what it cost together, and that if that were true of a crossing, nothing in that shed would be left to argue about at all.*
