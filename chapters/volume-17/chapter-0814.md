# Chapter 814

*Wednesday the Twelfth Day of October, the Fourth Day, a Line Worked This Morning on That Fitting-Out Sheet With the Fourteenth of October on Its Face for the Day It Settles, Four Days of Hire and Five Men and Forty Yards of Line Making Six Hundred and Eighty-Six Pence, a Woman of About Thirty-Four of the Saltmarch Coming Down That Coast Road in the Afternoon With What a Chalk Board Four Days Up It Carries, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Nine*

Wednesday the twelfth day of October, with that market open from six and the rain gone by nine and the boards of that yard drying in the wind off that lane, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Wednesday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday. A line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet today at about eight and it settles on Friday the fourteenth and the fourteenth is on the face of it, and where those two days are not the same day both are on the face of the line. Nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today and the foot is not read today because no settlement was made today, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twelfth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and fourteenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-second day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and eighteenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-third day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-fourth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-fifth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirteenth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twelfth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twelve days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight in the morning, in the keeper's hand, on the face of it, and the day the work was done was the twelfth and the day it settles is the fourteenth, and both are on it because they are not the same day.

The first part is two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, two times one hundred and sixty-eight, for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence. The second is coal and oil for two days at forty pence, being three shillings and fourpence. The third is forty yards of line at three pence the yard, forty times three, for one hundred and twenty pence, being ten shillings. The fourth is a case and four nails at thirty pence, being two shillings and sixpence. The fifth is five men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, five times two times sixteen, for one hundred and sixty pence, being one mark.

Three hundred and thirty-six and forty and one hundred and twenty and thirty and one hundred and sixty is six hundred and eighty-six pence, being four marks, three shillings and tenpence. Nothing settles into that foot today and the foot is not read today.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no figure of money is set against it.

The four new crews were on their twenty-fourth day and did the morning's work in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, who had a sheet of his own in his coat with that morning's hours on it and did not read it out to anybody, and about four of that nine have told two other people about that since and he does not know it.

---

At about half past two a woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch came into that market off the morning boat with a basket and a coat that had been four days on a road and one night in a chair, and she came down to that yard and stood at the door of that shed and did not go in for about a minute.

She is a person who was paid out of what a crossing was said to have cost in September, out of a figure that has since been found and corrected, and she has not been in this harbor since then and she did not come down to argue with anybody.

She said out loud, in about nine people, what a chalk board four days up that coast road carries. She said that on the Tuesday just gone, the hundred and second of them in this yard's count and the ninety-seventh in that community's, the hire of a boat for four days was not taken and the day of a man of about thirty-one of that community's own place was not worked and not paid. She said the board carries the figure of the boat's hire, four times one hundred and sixty-eight for six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and against it three days of that man at sixteen pence a man-day, three times sixteen for forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and that the two figures together are seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that none of it was spent.

She said that the board is in chalk and has no name on it and no second hand on it, and that it is a board and not a page, and that a person four days up that road can stand in front of it and look at it and that is all anybody will get off it. She said that she came down that road to say it out loud in a working place and not to ask anybody for anything, and about four of that nine said nothing and about four said that it should not have happened and nobody said it had been got right.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about one and then went out to the mole and was not in the shed when she was in it, and the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about one until about four with a page under her arm and heard about half of what that woman said and did not ask her one question about it.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-fourth day of that run of days.

Six hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those six was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries six against this day.

---

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twelfth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the fourth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that a line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight, in the keeper's hand, with the twelfth on it as the day the work was done and the fourteenth on it as the day it settles; that the parts of that line are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, forty yards of line at three pence the yard at forty times three for one hundred and twenty pence, a case and four nails at thirty pence, and five men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at five times two times sixteen for one hundred and sixty pence; that the whole of that line is six hundred and eighty-six pence, being four marks, three shillings and tenpence; that nothing settled into the foot of that sheet on the said day and the foot was not read on it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the woman of about thirty-four came four days down a coast road to say a figure out loud in a working place and not to ask anybody for anything, and that the reason she came down it and did not stay up there is that a board in chalk four days off can be looked at by the people who made it and by nobody else, and that a thing said out loud in about nine people in a shed is looked at by everybody in that shed including the ones who cannot read it; that the reason nobody said that it had been got right is that nobody in that shed knows whether it had been got right or not, and that the only person who does know is four days up that road and she did not come down here to be told; that the man of about thirty-two was out at the end of that mole at half past two and not in that shed at all, and that the reason nobody fetched him is that he was not told and would not have come, and that a person not being in a room when a thing is said in it is a thing this harbor has no way of knowing about.*
