# Chapter 813

*Tuesday the Eleventh Day of October, the Third Day, the Hundred and Second Wage Tuesday and the Column on the Lid of That Step Not Moving at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and a Further Week Fallen Due and the Chest With the Twenty Envelopes in It Shut From Ten Until Four, Four People Asking Out Loud at About Three Whether That Chest Should Be Opened on a Day When a Column Has Not Moved and the Keeper Saying No, the Next Charge on That Cistern on Thursday, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Four*

Tuesday the eleventh day of October, with that market open from six and rain coming in off the water in long patches after two and the boards of that yard wet by four, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Tuesday, being a wage Tuesday and a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday. The column on the lid of that step stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move today and it does not move on any wage Tuesday in this run, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the hundred and third week, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-six days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and eleventh day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and thirteenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-first day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and seventeenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninetieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-second day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-third day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-fourth day together with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twelfth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the eleventh day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five, unlined, unstruck and unshut. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head and nobody said anything about them. Three hundred and eleven days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water and no figure stands against that figure.**

---

The keeper read the column on the lid of that step at about ten, standing over it, with about four of that nine behind him and the rest of that nine going about their own work and not stopping for it.

The column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks. A hundred and four marks is one hundred and sixty-six pounds, being one hundred and sixty times one hundred and four, and it has stood at that figure since before this run of days began and it stands at that figure now, and the twenty-sixth line on that column is the twenty-sixth line and there is no twenty-seventh. A further week stands fallen due at its close and the hundred and third of those weeks has now fallen due, and there is no envelope against any of them and there has not been for a long time.

He read the figure and the count of lines and the further week and nothing else. Nobody asked him a question about it, because nobody asks him a question about it on a Tuesday, because it is the same figure every Tuesday, and about four of that nine have stopped hearing it and two of that nine never heard it at all.

The chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four and it was shut at four and it is shut now. The keeper keeps the key in his own pocket and the key is not on a hook and no copy of it exists that this yard knows of.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon in rain and a hatches out of the water by three, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it. The four new crews were on their twenty-third day and did about an hour and three quarters of it under the man of about thirty-two and then stood in the rain at the end of that mole for half an hour and then did another half hour, which was their own doing and not his.

At about three, in that shed, in about four people, one of the four asked the keeper out loud whether that chest should be opened on a day when the column has not moved, and said that there were twenty envelopes in it and that a column that had not moved in a hundred and three weeks was a column that would not move again.

The keeper said no. He said it once and he did not say it twice and about four people heard it. He said that the chest is not the column and the column is not the chest, and that a chest of envelopes opened on a day when a column has not moved teaches about twenty people that the two are the same thing, and that they would go home believing that money comes out of that chest when a thing goes wrong, and that on the first day it does not they will not believe the column again either. He said that the column is what a harbor owes and the chest is what a person is owed, and that a harbor that mixes the two ends up owing less of both.

About four of that four said nothing and nobody argued with him about it.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was not in that harbor today and was two days up that coast road, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about four with the page of routes in his hand and did not go into the end of that mole once in all that time, and nobody has said anything about where she was or where he was and nobody will.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-third day of that run of days, and he was on it in the rain the whole time and did not go in.

Nine hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those nine was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and twenty-two words, and the wage Tuesday came first in it and the chest came in the middle of it, and no count of anything came in it.

"**A man of about twenty-nine has now gone three hundred and eleven days without hearing anything in the left side of his head, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced and is a different thing from that one and is never set against it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns with nothing under it and his surname is on no page and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither shut nor struck. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has closed a third day without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a column read aloud at ten or for a chest kept shut until four, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty lines on it.

THE CHEST WAS SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR AND IT WAS SHUT AT FOUR. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**
