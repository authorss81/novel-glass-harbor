# Chapter 859

*Saturday the Twenty-Sixth Day of November, the Forty-Ninth Day, an Ordinary Working Saturday on Which Nobody Added Up the Sixth Column Down the Side of That Leaf and About Four of That Nine Saying Out Loud at About One in About Nine People That They Had Been Trying to Add It Up in Their Own Heads Since Friday and Being Told by the Keeper That He Is Not Going to Do It Until the Last Day of This Run, the Man of About Thirty-One of the Lowfen Reed's Own Place on His Twenty-Second Day Carrying Water and Asking Nobody for Anything, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Two and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Thirty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-One and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Saturday the twenty-sixth day of November, with that market shut until seven on a Saturday and a close grey morning with no wind in it at all and the water flat off that mole head and going out in long even rolls, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Saturday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on tomorrow, being Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November, and that is the last day of this run of fifty days, and no charge and no wage Tuesday falls again inside it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and one line is on the face of that sheet with tomorrow's date written on it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-seventh day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-ninth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-seventh day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-third night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-first day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-first day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-ninth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-eighth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-seventh day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-nine days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-seventh days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was two boats off that mole before noon on that flat water and a set of blocks strapped down for the winter where they usually hang, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-ninth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that on a flat sea like that they could hear themselves think and had spent the two hours not thinking about anything.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place put two pails over that cistern at about eleven and stood at that door afterwards with his hands behind him until the noon bell, and that is the twenty-fifth day he has been in that harbor and he has asked nobody for anything on any of the twenty-five.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that they had been trying to add that sixth column up in their own heads since Friday.

They said it in about four different ways and about four of that nine said all four of them out loud inside about two minutes, and the ways were that there was a one in it on Friday and three in it on Saturday and one in it on Monday, and that they had got as far as that and were not going to say the answer out loud in a shed on a Saturday.

The keeper said out loud that today is not the day either, and that one column is totalled once, in a keeper's own hand, on the last day of a run of days, and that he said exactly that on Tuesday and would say it again on Sunday for anybody who did not hear it on Tuesday.

Nobody said the answer out loud and nobody guessed out loud and nobody asked him a second thing about it.

---

At about half past two in that shed the keeper said out loud that one day of this run of fifty days is all that is left in it and that he has not the least idea what is going to be on that day.

About four of that nine said out loud that they had all said on Wednesday that they would be back for the last day, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had been trying all week to work out what a rule with six lines on it was for and had got nowhere, and about four of that nine said out loud that whatever is on tomorrow will be on tomorrow and that this harbor has spent six weeks correcting things it had already finished with.

Nobody said out loud what any of them thought was going to be on it.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and ninth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about one until about three, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about three, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about tomorrow.

Three hands went onto the faces of one sheet that left that harbor that day and not one of those three was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries three against this day.

---

That shed stood empty at about five on that Saturday with the date of tomorrow standing at the twenty-seventh day of November on every page of that week, and nobody in that yard said out loud what was going to be on it.

---

About half past five on that Saturday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about seven hundred and sixty-seven words, and the four people doing the adding up came first in it and the keeper not doing it came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and no figure out of that sixth column is in this reading and no answer to what it adds up to is in this reading either.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-fifth day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare.**"

"**It is three hundred and fifty-seventh days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-nine days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for four people not saying a number out loud in a shed for two minutes on a Saturday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-six lines on it.

**ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ONE THAT THEY HAD BEEN TRYING TO ADD THAT SIXTH COLUMN DOWN THE SIDE OF THAT LEAF UP IN THEIR OWN HEADS SINCE FRIDAY AND THE KEEPER SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE IS NOT ADDING IT UP BEFORE THE LAST DAY OF THIS RUN OF FIFTY DAYS AND THAT NOBODY SAID THE ANSWER OUT LOUD AND NOBODY GUESSED OUT LOUD. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SECOND DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that four people refusing to say a number out loud in a shed is this harbor learning the one thing six weeks of correcting figures has not taught it, being that a number a man says in a room is a thing he has agreed to in a room and a number a man writes on a leaf is a thing he has agreed to in public; that the reason they had got as far as a one and three and a one and were not going further is that there were two empty days in between and that a column with empty days in it cannot be added up by anybody who is not the keeper of it, and that is the whole of what an empty day in a column does; that the reason the keeper said he would say it again on Sunday is that four people asked him on Tuesday and two people asked him on Friday and the answer does not improve with repeating; that the reason nobody said out loud what they thought was going to be on the last day is that this harbor has a hundred and thirty-fourth wipe of a wall behind a reeve's bench coming at about half past ten tomorrow and a line with tomorrow's date on it coming off that sheet at about four tomorrow, and that those two are known, and that a room which guesses what it knows has already lost the habit the whole of this run of days was spent on.*
