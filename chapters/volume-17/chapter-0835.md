# Chapter 835

*Wednesday the Second Day of November, the Twenty-Fifth Day, an Ordinary Working Wednesday, a Man of About Thirty-One of the Lowfen Reed's Own Place Coming Down That Coast Road on Foot at About One and Saying Out Loud That the Decision Was Made on a Copy of a Copy, a Woman of About Forty-Five Four Days Up That Road Keeping a Tally and a Boy of About Nineteen Who Cannot Read Having Written What He Was Told, Nobody Lying and the Figure the Same Figure, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Sixty-Eight and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Fourteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Twelve*

Wednesday the second day of November, with that market open from six and a flat sea and no wind at all and the sound coming in off that water the way it does on a morning like this, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Wednesday, and no charge on that cistern in it and no wipe in it and nothing in it that moves on a wage, and the next charge is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wipe falls on Sunday the sixth of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-fifth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-three days. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-five days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-third day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and thirty-ninth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-seventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and twelfth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-fifth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood sixty-six days together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-fourth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-third day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-five days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-three days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about one that afternoon a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place came into that market from the top of that lane on foot and stood in the doorway of that shed.

He had left his own place on the twenty-seventh and had been held two days by water on that coast road and had not sent a word ahead of himself, and he said out loud that he was not going to arrive with a message and that he had walked down in his own boots.

About four of that nine did not know who he was and about four of that nine had heard him described in October by a woman of about thirty-four of another place who had come down that road herself.

He put his hand inside his coat and brought out a piece of board about the length of a hand and the width of a hand and put it on the flat of that bench stone.

On that board there is a figure in chalk, seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence. It is in two hands on that board and not in one. The first hand wrote it large and the second hand copied it small under it.

---

The keeper asked him one question and it was the only question anybody in that shed asked him about any of it. He asked him out loud whether the figure on that board is what his own community's page carries.

He said out loud that it is, and that the page is in this harbor, and that he has never seen that page and does not know that it exists, and that he has never been in this harbor and did not know until he was standing on that quay that the page was four days up that road from his own gate.

He said out loud that a woman of about forty-five four days up that road keeps the tally where he works, and that she wrote the figure on that board in a slate in October because a man off a boat had told her what a crossing costs in this harbor, and that a boy of about nineteen in that room cannot read and wrote it out again onto that board from what she said, and that she told him at the gate that the figure was right and that the boy was right and that he was not to check it.

He said out loud that the three days he did not get are on that board in the second hand, and that somebody four days up that road decided on that board in the first ten days of October not to hire a boat, and that he has never met the person who decided and that nobody up there is going to tell him.

---

About eight of that nine heard all of it. Nobody said anybody had lied.

The keeper said out loud that the figure on that board is the same figure that is on a community's own page in this harbor and is right in both places, and that what happened to it is that it was copied twice on its way up that road, and that a thing copied twice is still the same figure and is now something this harbor cannot get at.

He said out loud that the thing that would have to be corrected to somebody is the use, and that the use was made four days up a coast road by a person this harbor cannot name, and that the two people this harbor can name out there are a woman who kept a tally and a boy who cannot read, and that neither of them decided anything and neither of them is a person the use can be corrected to.

He said out loud that a page records the two hands that can go against it and nowhere to put the name of a person who has read it, and that this harbor has been asking for a name since Saturday and has now got two names and both of them are on the wrong side of the copying.

About four of that nine said nothing and the rest went back to what they were doing. Nobody wrote the name of the woman or the name of the boy on anything.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon on that flat water and a hatches out of the water by two, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and no money at all is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews stood at their forty-fifth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said that a man who has walked four days down a coast road in his own boots is a thing that has not happened in this yard before.

From about ten until about two that bench was worked by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay and nobody asked him one thing, which makes the eighty-fifth day of that run and he has not missed one of them.

At about four three of that nine went up that lane and read one of those eleven sheets over again on a shelf in that room over the sailmaker, and one of those three read the same one twice, and nobody kept a list of it and nobody said which.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road at one. The man of about thirty-two was at that bench from two until five and said out loud at about three that he has been up that road four times this month in his own head and has now been told the thing by the person it happened to.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor that day, none of the twelve from the person who wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

**THE DECISION WAS MADE ON A COPY OF A COPY FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST ROAD BY A PERSON THIS HARBOR CANNOT NAME, AND THE PERSON WHO MADE IT HAS NEVER SEEN THE PAGE AND DOES NOT KNOW THAT IT EXISTS, AND THE THING THAT HAS TO BE CORRECTED HAS NO ADDRESSEE. NOBODY LIED AND THE FIGURE ON THAT BOARD IS THE FIGURE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**
