# Chapter 851

*Friday the Eighteenth Day of November, the Forty-First Day, an Ordinary Working Friday on Which the Figure of What a Person Who Worked from a Page Is Owed Was Put into About Nine People at About One and About Four of That Nine Said Out Loud That the Figure Is Smaller Than What Was Asked For, the Man of About Thirty-One of the Lowfen Reed's Own Place Saying Out Loud That What He Lost Is Not Written on Any Page in This Harbor and That He Is Not Asking Anybody for It, and the Keeper Putting One Figure into the Sixth and Last Column Down the Side of That Leaf at About Two in the Figure It Was Given In, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Four and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Ninety-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Friday the eighteenth day of November, with that market open from six and a hard clear morning with the frost gone off that cobble lane by seven and the sun on that mole head by eight for the first time in a week, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the twenty-first day of November and the next wipe falls on tomorrow, being Sunday the twentieth of November, and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-second day of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-first day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-ninth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-first day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-ninth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-fifth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-third day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-third day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixtieth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-first day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-two days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fiftieth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-ninth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-one days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-ninth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Friday was four boats off that mole before noon with that sun on the stone of it and a shackle at that bench ground back with a file until the bright came off the metal, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-first day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had got a trade back out of that morning and that it was the first one since the middle of October.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried water at about eleven, said nothing out loud to anybody, asked nobody for anything, and that is the seventeenth day he has been in that harbor.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper put a figure of what a person who worked from a page is owed into that room.

He put it the way he puts a foot down onto a sheet, which is that he said it out loud twice over and did not look up while he said it. He said out loud that the figure on that community's own page is four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, and that it is that figure and not a figure of his own, and that it is the whole of what that page can put into a room.

He said out loud that he is not going to say out loud what any person is owed, and that nobody in that shed is going to make him say it, and that a page hands over a figure and nothing else and has handed this harbor a figure and nothing else for six weeks.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place said out loud that a day of his is sixteen pence, being one shilling and fourpence, and that four hundred and six pence is not what he lost, and that what he lost is not written down on any page in this harbor, and that he is not asking anybody for it and has not asked anybody for it.

About four of that nine said out loud, in four different words and inside about two minutes, that the figure is smaller than what was asked for. Nobody asked the keeper a second thing about it and nobody said that it was the whole of it.

---

At about two the keeper wrote one figure onto the page of that week in his own hand at the foot of that day's entry, being four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, and one figure went into that sixth column on that leaf and into no other column on it, and the head of that column is PUT BACK, and that is the last column down the side of that leaf and there is no seventh.

He said out loud that he set it down in the figure it was given in and not in one of his own, and that the sixth column is not read out at that stone standing up and is not going to be.

About four of that nine said nothing at all. The man of about thirty-two was in that room the whole of it and said nothing out loud at any point in it, and nobody turned round and looked at him while that figure was going down.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and first day of that run of days he has not missed.

At about half past three the man of about thirty-two sat down at that far end of that bench with the page of routes in front of him and shut it, and thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that a figure got into nine people's mouths at about one and onto a leaf at about two is two places and neither of them is a person, and that he said out loud in about nine people a fortnight ago that a page which carries what a figure was used for is a page which can be got at, and that this is the fifth day running of his not being asked whether that is a good thing to have said.

Five hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those five was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries five against this day.

---

About four of that nine went out of that shed at about four, and the two of them that are married went up that lane together at about ten past four and were in that house before the light went off that cobble, and neither of them said one word to the other one about Friday, and both of them are still married.

---

About half past five on that Friday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and twenty-nine words, and the ordinary work came first in it and the figure came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the sixth column down the side of that leaf is not read at that stone standing up because the keeper does not read a column out, and this reading carries nine of those blocks and leaves out three paragraphs of that day, being the one about the keeper setting it down in the figure it was given in, the one about about four of that nine saying nothing at all, and the one about the two of them that are married going up that lane at about ten past four.

Nobody was thanked for a figure of four hundred and six pence going into nine mouths and onto one leaf in about three hours, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty-eight lines on it.

**A FIGURE OF WHAT A PERSON WHO WORKED FROM A PAGE IS OWED WENT INTO ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AT ABOUT ONE AND ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE SAID OUT LOUD THAT IT IS SMALLER THAN WHAT WAS ASKED FOR, AND ONE FIGURE WENT INTO THE SIXTH COLUMN DOWN THE SIDE OF THAT LEAF AT ABOUT TWO AND NO TOTAL OF THAT COLUMN WAS ADDED UP BY ANYBODY. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOURTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a figure of four hundred and six pence went into nine mouths and onto a leaf in about three hours on a Friday is the most this harbor has managed in six weeks, and that it is also the whole of what any page in this harbor can do, and that those two facts are one fact; that the reason four of those people said it out loud, that the figure is smaller than what was asked for, is that what was asked for was never money at all, and that a day of one man at sixteen pence and four days of a coast road walked on two feet are not on any page in this harbor and cannot be got onto one by anybody who is not that man; that the one man in that room who lost anything off that figure put it out loud in his own voice and not the keeper who had the figure, and that about four of that nine had been carrying his loss for him all week without knowing they were doing it and that about four of that nine had been saying his loss for him all week without knowing they were doing it; that the keeper gave for refusing to name out loud what any person is owed is that the word is not his to put a figure on and that he has watched two married people in that shed spend a fortnight over who gets to hold the pen; that the reason nobody turned round and looked at the man of about thirty-two while that figure was going down is that this harbor knows by now that a person standing there gets looked at, and that a man who has said out loud that holding a sheet is a way of telling a person what a sheet is for does not get to hold anything this month.*
