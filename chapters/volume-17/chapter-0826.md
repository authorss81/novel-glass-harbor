# Chapter 826

*Monday the Twenty-Fourth Day of October, the Sixteenth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Fifth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Keeper Saying Out Loud at About One at That Cistern in About Four People That Three Days of One Man Is Four Shillings and That Four Shillings Is the Reason Nothing Will Be Done About It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Monday the twenty-fourth day of October, with that market open from six and a hard clean morning after the rain with the puddles at the back of that yard carrying a skin of ice at about ten that went before eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Monday, being a rota day and a day with no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of tomorrow as well and the next charge after this one is on the morning of Thursday. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-fifth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this sixteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-fourth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-sixth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-fourth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirtieth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and eighteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-sixth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-seventh day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-fifth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-fourth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-four days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt was filled for the hundred and ninety-fifth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on, and the last of the fill from Thursday had gone flat at the bottom of it with a skin on it by about one and nobody drank it and nobody said it was bad.

The ordinary work of that Monday was three boats off that mole before noon in that hard clean light and a length of line cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

---

At about one, at that cistern, in about four people, the keeper wrote the hundred and ninety-fifth on that rota slate and then did not put the slate down.

He said out loud that the wage of a day of a man's work in that yard is sixteen pence, being one shilling and fourpence, that three of them is forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and that forty-eight pence is the whole of what one man four days up that coast road is out of pocket for three days he did not get. He said out loud that a man who works a full week in that yard is paid about five marks and four shillings a week and that four shillings is a small part of a week's pay and that anybody who stands in that lane and looks at him knows it.

He said out loud that the reason nothing is going to be done about it is that it is small, and that he is not going to stand at that butt at about one on a Monday afternoon and make four shillings into a large thing, because it is not a large thing and it does not become one by nine people agreeing that it ought to be. He said that if the figure on that board were nine marks there would be nine people at that reeve's bench by Thursday and that if it were nine pence there would be nobody anywhere, and that four shillings is nearer the second of those than the first and that everybody in that four knows it and that is why the four of them are standing at a cistern and not at a bench.

One of that four, a man of about forty-four who works a boat of the second lay, said out loud that he has a day coming on Friday and that four shillings is four shillings and that his own father lost three days at that crossing in his time, and that he is not saying what ought to be done about it and he knows what it comes to. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody asked him a second thing about it.

The keeper put the slate down on the coping of the butt and said nothing else about it and went and did his own work.

---

Those four new crews came to their thirty-sixth day and had the day's work off in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said that the three who announced last Saturday that they would be gone by this time next week are still here, and said it to nobody in particular.

About ten until about one the woman of thirty of no office had that yard to herself with her own page, and she went up that coast road at one. The man of about thirty-two had the page of routes in that shed between two and five and was not near that cistern at one and heard not a word of any of it until about half past four. Four shillings was not said by either of them to the other and neither of them signed the other's page.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two in that hard clean light and no question was put to him by anybody in that lane, which is the seventy-sixth day of that run.

Nine hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and not one of the four was written by anybody who put a hand on it, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

There are nine hulls about two sea miles off that mole head, which is a sixteenth day of this run of days with nothing said about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for saying out loud at a butt that a thing is small, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-three lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIFTH CHARGE WAS MADE AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THREE DAYS OF ONE MAN FOUR DAYS UP THAT COAST ROAD COME TO FORTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING FOUR SHILLINGS, AND THE KEEPER SAID OUT LOUD AT THAT CISTERN THAT THE REASON NOTHING IS GOING TO BE DONE ABOUT IT IS THAT IT IS SMALL, AND THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO MAKE IT LARGER BY AGREEMENT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the twenty-fourth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the sixteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Monday and was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of tomorrow; that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-fifth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it; that the ordinary work of that said Monday was three boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that at about one at that cistern in about four people the keeper wrote the hundred and ninety-fifth on that rota slate and did not put the slate down and said out loud that a man-day in that yard is sixteen pence, being one shilling and fourpence, and that three of them is forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and that forty-eight pence is the whole of what one man four days up that coast road is out of pocket for three days he did not get; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper said the truest thing anybody has said about that board this month and it was said at a cistern by a man holding a slate he had not put down, and that what put those words at that butt and not inside that shed is that a thing said at about one on a Monday goes out of that yard by four, while a thing said at half past five in about nine people is still in nine heads the next morning, and that he wanted the first of those two and not the second; that the reason he would not put four shillings into a large thing is that this harbor has spent five weeks learning to say a true figure when it finds one, and that the next thing after that is learning not to make a small one large in a lane, and that the two habits are in the same place in the same man and only one of them has anybody behind it; that the man of about forty-four who works a boat of the second lay said the thing about his own father and was not asked a second question, and that nobody has asked him since and will not, and that a man who tells four people what his own family lost in the ordinary way in the middle of four other people is not making an argument and should not be treated as if he were; that about four of that nine said that the three of the new crews who said last Saturday that they would be gone by this time next week are still here and said it to nobody in particular, and that this harbor has never had a run of four new crews before and has no figure for what it does with them and is not going to have one inside fifty days.*
