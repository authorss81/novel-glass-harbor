# Chapter 843

*Thursday the Tenth Day of November, the Thirty-Third Day, a Rota Day and the Two Hundredth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Woman of Thirty of No Office Answering at About One in About Nine People What the Man of About Thirty-Two Said at Two Yesterday and the Man of About Thirty-Two Answering Her Back in the Same Room and Neither of Them Giving Way, a Man of About Fifty-Eight of That Market Saying Out Loud at About Half Past Two That the Two of Them Are Arguing About Two Different Pages, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Seventy-Six and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Thursday the tenth day of November, with that market open from six and a fine bright morning that came up hard out of the east over a sea that had gone flat in the night and would not stay flat, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on Sunday the thirteenth of November and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the fourteenth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the fifteenth of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-first day and nobody has answered it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-third day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-first day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and forty-seventh night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and sixty-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twentieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and thirty-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-second day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-third day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-four days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-second day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-first day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-three days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt was charged for the two hundredth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and nobody walked on a single one of them, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried three of them and did not make a thing of it, and that is his ninth day in that harbor.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was one boat off that mole before noon in that bright light and a shackle turned at that bench stone with the swarf coming off it in a bright ring, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-third day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that nothing was said in that shed for the first hour and that they had spent that hour not saying anything themselves.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the woman of thirty, who holds no office, answered out loud what the man of about thirty-two had said at two the day before.

She said out loud that a page with a use written under it can be got at by anybody who wants the figure, and that a page with a figure and nothing else can be got at by anybody who wants the figure too, and that the difference is not in the getting at it at all.

She said out loud that the only people who want to know what a figure was used for are the ones it has already been used on, and that the person four days up that coast road who did not hire a boat is one of them, and that he is the only one of them this harbor has ever come close to, and that a page which said what the figure was for would have been the only thing that could have walked up that road and got to him.

He said back, to nobody in particular, that the figure went up that road on the first of October carried in one hand and copied out again by a boy of about nineteen, and that it was decided on in the first ten days of that month on a slate by a person who had never seen the page and does not know it exists, and that a page carrying the use would have been no use at all because nobody up that road knew the figure had ever left this harbor.

He said back that she is right about who a page is for and wrong about what one can be got at for, and that a figure is the only part of a thing anybody can be got at for, and that the use is a thing two people have to be in a room about and there is no page in this harbor that can get two people into a room.

He said back that he is not saying that the use should not be written down, and that he wrote one on Friday with his own hand at the top of the page and it is wrong, and that a thing being wrong is not a reason to stop doing it in this harbor.

About four of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody repeated any of it back to anybody in there and nobody put a word of it on a sheet.

---

At about half past two, in that doorway, a man of about fifty-eight of that market who keeps the end stall and a slate said out loud that the two of them are arguing about two different pages.

He said it out loud with his back to that bench and not to either of them. He said out loud that that stall in that market is his own and has been a long time and that the two people saying things in that shed on Tuesday and Wednesday and Thursday are talking about two pages and not one, and that on one of those pages there is a figure and on the other one there is a person, and that neither of them can be got at by the other one's method.

About four of that nine said out loud that they had thought the same thing and had not said it. Nobody asked him one question about it and nobody asked him a second thing he said on the first day of this month.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-third day of that run of days he has not missed.

Five hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those five was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries five against this day.

---

"**It is three hundred and forty-first days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and thirty-three days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for two people answering each other in about nine people in a working place and giving way in nothing, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty lines on it.

THE COLUMN IS AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS AND IT DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that two people answered each other in about nine people in a working place on a Thursday and neither of them gave way is the ordinary shape of a marriage that has been arguing in the open for a month and it is not a scene out of a room with two people in it, which is not a shape this harbor has used once in thirty-three days; that the reason she said that the only people who want to know what a figure was used for are the ones it has already been used on is that a stranger cannot ask that question and a person who has been asked it by nobody can, and that the man four days up that coast road who did not hire a boat has had it asked of him by a slate; that the reason he said that the use is a thing two people have to be in a room about is that a use is not an attribute of a figure, it is a thing two persons did, and that putting it on a page turns two persons into a column, and that the man of about forty-six has two bell seats cut and faced at the top of his page of his own and neither of them belongs to him; that the reason a man of about fifty-eight said out loud that they are arguing about two different pages is that he asked a question in about nine people on the first day of this month and was not answered, and that saying out loud that two people are arguing about two different things is the nearest thing to an answer this harbor has had since, and that he did not answer it either.*
