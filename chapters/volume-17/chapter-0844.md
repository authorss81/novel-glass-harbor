# Chapter 844

*Friday the Eleventh Day of November, the Thirty-Fourth Day, the Fifth Line Settling at About Four on That Fitting-Out Sheet and the Whole of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Not Before It, the Foot Standing at One Hundred and Thirty-One Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-One Thousand One Hundred and Ten Pence Short, the Keeper Reading That Foot Out Loud at About a Quarter Past Four in About Nine People, and Four Different People Asking the Woman of Thirty of No Office the Same Question in Four Different Words at About Two and Getting the Same Answer Four Times, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Seventy-Seven and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Friday the eleventh day of November, with that market open from six and a wind that got up in the night and came round to the north-west and made that mole hard work by eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the fourteenth of November and the next wipe is on Sunday the thirteenth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the fifteenth of November. That fifth line settled at about four and the whole of that fitting-out sheet was read after that settlement and not before it, and the foot of that sheet stands at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-second day and no answer has ever come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-fourth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-second day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and forty-eighth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and sixty-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and thirty-sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-third day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-fourth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-five days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-third day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-second day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-four days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Friday was five boats off that mole before noon in that hard north-west wind and a boiler tube scraped at that bench with the scale coming off it in a grey flake, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-fourth day and took the day's work off in about two hours in the middle of all that wind, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had spent that two hours arguing about a wind and had got nowhere at all with it.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, four different people asked the woman of thirty, who holds no office, the same question in four different words.

The keeper asked her whether she had made up her mind. The man of about fifty-eight of that market asked her whether she was going to put the use on the page or not. The woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing asked her whether anybody was going to be told which it was. The man of about thirty-six of that market asked her what a person is supposed to do on a Friday.

She gave all four of them the same answer and she gave it in the ordinary voice and not to anybody in particular.

She said out loud that she has not made up her mind and that she is not going to make it up in front of about nine people, and that a thing which is going on a page for the rest of its life ought not to be settled in a shed on a Friday because four people happened to be standing in it.

About four of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody asked her a fifth question and nobody asked the man of about thirty-two anything about it.

---

At about four that line settled and at about a quarter past four the keeper read the whole of the foot of that fitting-out sheet out loud in about nine people.

He said out loud that he reads the foot after the settlement on every day there is a settlement in it, and that he read it after the settlement in September and in October and that he is not going to start reading it in the morning before a figure has been agreed.

He read out that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and that the whole of what that sheet has done in five weeks is go up and not come down.

Nobody wrote that figure down out loud in that shed and nobody repeated it after him. About four of that nine had it before he said it and about four of that nine did not.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about two until about half past four. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about five and said nothing out loud at any point in it.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those nine was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

About half past five on that Friday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred words, and the four questions came first in it, the settlement came in the middle of it, and the four columns came at the end of it, and the figure the keeper read out is in this reading because he said it out loud in about nine people and not because anybody counts it.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its seventieth day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare.**"

"**It is three hundred and forty-second days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and thirty-four days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for four people asking one woman the same thing four times and getting the same answer four times, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty-one lines on it.

**THE FIFTH LINE SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR AND THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET WAS READ AFTER THE SETTLEMENT AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST FOUR IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, AND IT STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-ONE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND ONE HUNDRED AND TEN PENCE SHORT. THE WOMAN OF THIRTY, WHO HOLDS NO OFFICE, WAS ASKED THE SAME QUESTION FOUR TIMES BY FOUR PEOPLE AND GAVE THE SAME ANSWER FOUR TIMES. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that the ordinary work of that said Friday was five boats off that mole before noon in a hard north-west wind and a boiler tube scraped at that bench, and that not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet; that she gave all four of them the same answer, that she has not made up her mind and is not going to make it up in front of about nine people, and that a thing which is going on a page for the rest of its life ought not to be settled in a shed on a Friday; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that four people asked one woman the same question in four different words on a Friday and she gave the same answer four times is the shape a thing takes when nobody in the room has standing to ask it and the only person it concerns has standing to decide it and has said out loud that she will not do it in about nine people; that the reason she said it that way is that three days earlier she said the other half of it out loud in that same shed in that same nine people, and that a person who has said a case out loud in about nine people and is then asked four times in an hour about what she is going to do about it has not been asked a new question and has been asked the same one four more times; that the reason the keeper read the foot out loud after the settlement and not before it is that a figure everybody has in their heads before it is settled is a figure that gets argued about instead of added up, and that he read it out so that about four of that nine who had it and about four who did not would be in the same place about it for once; that the reason nobody wrote it down is that the whole of the four columns of that leaf is written down twice and the whole of that foot is read out once, and that a figure read out in about nine people is a figure nine people have and no more than that.*
